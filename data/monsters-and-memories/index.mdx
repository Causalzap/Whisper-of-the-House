*Early Access · Last checked October 6, 2026*

Monsters & Memories does not lead a new character through a fixed chain of
quest markers. Progress comes from learning enough about your class, city, and
surrounding camps to decide what is worth doing next.

The first hours usually follow the same pattern: prepare in town, find an
efficient camp, return when supplies or inventory become the limit, then move
on when the current enemies stop giving enough experience or useful loot.

<section id="getting-started" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Start with your class setup before wandering far from town

The **Starter Note** gives the first important direction. Read it, find the
class guild, turn it in, take the opening class quest, and learn the starting
abilities that are available at your level.

Guards, NPC keywords, the Journal, item descriptions, food, light, merchants,
and the first containers all matter during this opening stretch. The
[beginner guide](/monsters-and-memories/beginner-guide)
covers the full first-hour setup and the mistakes that can leave a new
character underprepared outside town.

Once that setup is done, stop waiting for another quest chain to appear.
Early experience mostly comes from fighting suitable enemies and staying at a
camp while the kills remain efficient.

</section>

<section id="choosing-a-class" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Pick around how much time you expect to spend alone

Grouping is important, but some classes can make much better use of the time
between groups.

Necromancer and Elementalist have strong pet-based solo setups. Druid can make
progress alone while still filling a healer role in parties. Bard, Beastmaster,
Ranger, and Rogue can also solo, but they depend more on control, positioning,
terrain, or target choice.

The
[best solo classes guide](/monsters-and-memories/best-solo-classes)
compares those playstyles and the point where each one becomes harder to manage
alone.

</section>

<section id="leveling" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Move camps when recovery starts costing more than the kills are worth

A useful leveling camp needs more than high-level enemies.

Watch:

- how quickly normal targets die;
- how much health or mana each pull costs;
- whether enough enemies are available for the group;
- whether every party member is still receiving experience.

Wyrmsbane becomes an early group option, Blacktide Bay opens another route in
the low teens, and River Pirates provide a useful step through the low 20s
before Glass Flats.

The exact transition depends on how well the current camp is working. The
[leveling guide](/monsters-and-memories/leveling-guide)
covers the early level brackets, group XP checks, Rested XP, and when staying
put is better than moving to a harder area.

</section>

<section id="inventory-and-crafting" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Full bags are a progression problem, not just an inconvenience

Pelts, wood, resin, cloth, fish, and other materials begin connecting into
crafting chains very early.

One character can train multiple tradeskills, but trying to keep every material
at once quickly turns the bank and inventory into the limiting factor. Early
bag crafting is one of the clearest reasons to start working with Tanning,
Leatherworking, or Tailoring.

Use the
[tradeskills guide](/monsters-and-memories/tradeskills)
for backpack materials, Tailoring container costs, Fishing setup, and the
material chains worth saving while leveling.

</section>

<section id="corpse-recovery" className="scroll-mt-24">

## A bad death changes the next task immediately

Death returns the character to the bindpoint while equipment remains with the
corpse.

A nearby body may only require retracing the route. A corpse behind hostile
enemies, inside a dungeon, or somewhere in a large open zone may need help
from a Necromancer, Bard, or Shadow Knight, corpse dragging, or resurrection.

Spellcasters also need to account for the equipped spellbook staying on the
corpse.

The
[corpse recovery guide](/monsters-and-memories/corpse-recovery)
covers locating a lost body, dragging it somewhere safe, recovery spellbooks,
Cantrips, resurrection, and the expired-corpse fallback.

</section>

<section id="faelindral-ranger" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Faelindral Rangers have an early scouting quest with several easy failure points

Captain Relgen Greenblade sends new Rangers to investigate the ruined archway,
Spore Haven Karst, and Whispering Pools.

Simply reaching those places is not enough. The quest depends on contextual
commands, following environmental clues, recording the evidence, and combining
the completed Ranger's Log Book before the final turn-in.

The
[Faelindral Ranger quest guide](/monsters-and-memories/faelindral-ranger-quest)
covers the exact route, required interactions, Full Ranger's Log Book step, and
Initiate Keeper's Cloak reward.

</section>

<section id="what-to-do-next" className="scroll-mt-24">

## What should you do next?

Use the current blocker to decide:

- **Still learning the city or class setup:** finish the beginner route.
- **Mostly playing alone:** check whether the class has the tools to stay
  productive without a group.
- **Experience has slowed down:** compare the current camp against the next
  leveling option.
- **Bags keep filling:** solve inventory space before carrying more materials.
- **Your corpse is missing or unsafe:** recover it before resuming the camp.
- **Playing a Faelindral Ranger:** complete Greenblade's scouting assignment
  before moving into the follow-up collection task.

After that, the game becomes a repeating choice between camps, groups,
equipment, travel, class progression, and crafting. The best next move is the
one that removes the limitation currently slowing the character down.

</section>
