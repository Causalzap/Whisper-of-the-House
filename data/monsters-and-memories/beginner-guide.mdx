*Early Access · Last checked October 6, 2026*

The first hour in Monsters & Memories depends on information that many modern
MMOs would normally turn into map markers. Your starting items, NPC dialogue,
city guards, Journal, and the terrain itself all carry directions.

A smooth start comes from handling the class setup first, then leaving town
with enough abilities, supplies, bag space, and light to stay outside for more
than a few pulls.

<section id="starter-note" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Read the Starter Note before looking for your class guild

Open the inventory and inspect the **Starter Note** before leaving the starting
area.

The note gives class-specific directions to the guild or trainer you need.
Those directions can name a district, building, cave, garden, gate, or another
landmark rather than placing an objective marker on the screen.

![A Starter Note giving written directions to the character's class guild.](/images/monsters-and-memories/starter-note-class-guild.webp)

Use **right click** to inspect items. **Alt + left click** can also open an item
description quickly.

Read the location wording carefully and keep the note until it has been handed
to the correct guild master or trainer. Once there, place the Starter Note on
the cursor and turn it in.

The guild is where the opening class setup begins:

- class trainers become easy to find;
- the introductory class quest becomes available;
- additional abilities can be purchased or learned;
- the surrounding district starts making sense as a return point.

Do this before exploring far outside the city. Reaching level 2 is much less
useful if the character still has unlearned starting abilities or cannot find
the trainer again.

</section>

<section id="guards-and-journal" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Use guards and the Journal when city directions stop making sense

When written directions are not enough, find a city guard, target them, and
press **H** to hail.

NPC conversations use topics and keywords. Class names, districts, trainers,
banks, merchants, and gates can all become useful conversation paths. Guards
are especially valuable because they can point toward major city services
without requiring the entire settlement to be memorized first.

Press **J** to open the Journal when an NPC has already given the answer but
the exact wording has been forgotten. Previous conversations remain available
there, so a useful route is:

1. hail the NPC;
2. follow the relevant topic or keyword;
3. check the Journal if the destination is forgotten;
4. navigate by the landmarks mentioned in the response.

The Notes section can be used for information that needs to remain easy to
find outside a single conversation, such as an awkward route or a location
worth revisiting.

This is more reliable than circling the city looking for a glowing marker that
does not exist.

</section>

<section id="starter-quest" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Take the opening class quest before settling into a leveling camp

The first class quest is worth picking up even when the objective will not be
finished immediately.

Some early class quests provide a container as part of the task. When that
happens, the container is already useful before the quest is complete because
new characters begin with very limited storage.

A practical start is:

- turn in the Starter Note;
- speak to the class trainer or guild master;
- accept the opening class task;
- use any container the quest provides;
- learn the abilities currently available;
- leave for nearby enemies once the character is actually prepared.

Do not assume the first quest must be completed before anything else can
happen. Monsters & Memories does not use a continuous quest chain as its main
leveling path.

Crafted containers become another option once materials and silver are
available. The material costs and early bag choices are covered in the
[Tradeskills guide](/monsters-and-memories/tradeskills).

</section>

<section id="starting-abilities" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Learn starting scrolls before assuming an ability is ready

A scroll sitting in the inventory is not the same thing as an ability that is
ready to use.

Open the ability book with **B** and check the starting scrolls. When an
ability can be learned at the current level, right click the scroll to inscribe
it into the appropriate part of the book.

![The ability book after a starting spell has been learned and placed into the spellbook.](/images/monsters-and-memories/starting-ability-scroll-spellbook.webp)

Spells, Skills, and Innates do not all behave identically. An Innate can refuse
to scribe when the character has not yet reached its required level, so a
failed right click is not always an inventory or UI problem.

After learning an ability, prepare the ones needed for combat and place them on
the action bar. A character can have **10 prepared abilities**, which means the
bar eventually becomes a choice between useful tools rather than a place for
every ability the character knows.

For spellcasters, the equipped spellbook matters while learning and preparing
spells. Once a spell is memorized, the book does not need to remain equipped
simply for that memorized spell to cast.

</section>

<section id="first-combat" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Check the target before attacking anything near town

Enemies standing close to a starting city are not automatically safe for a
level 1 character.

Check the target's con before committing. The first few fights are useful for
learning how much health, mana, pet durability, or recovery time the class
needs for one ordinary kill.

Use **Q** to toggle auto attack once the target is appropriate.

Weapon and defensive skills improve through use, so these early fights are
also building the combat skills the character will rely on later.

Avoid searching for one exact starter monster unless a quest specifically asks
for it. A nearby camp with several manageable enemies is usually more useful
than walking across the zone to fight one particular species.

For level ranges, Wyrmsbane, Blacktide Bay, River Pirates, and the transition
toward later camps, use the
[leveling guide](/monsters-and-memories/leveling-guide).

</section>

<section id="quests-and-leveling" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Quests give useful rewards, but kills drive most early leveling

After the class introduction, progression does not become a chain of quest
hubs.

Quests can provide class progression, equipment, containers, access, and
information about the world. Take them when the reward matters, but do not
remain in town searching for the next quest marker when nothing points to one.

The normal early rhythm is simpler:

**find suitable enemies → stay while kills remain efficient → sell and
resupply → move when the camp stops working.**

That distinction matters because a character can spend a long time looking for
an NPC that is not supposed to carry the leveling process forward.

</section>

<section id="money-and-loot" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Spend early money on the ability that changes the next fight

Trainer inventories can contain more abilities than a new character can
comfortably afford.

Prioritize the purchase that immediately improves the current combat loop:
the attack being used every pull, a heal that reduces recovery, an important
pet ability, or control that makes a difficult enemy manageable.

Situational abilities can wait when buying them would leave too little money
for food, drink, travel, or another essential skill.

Loot also needs more judgment than simply selling everything to the first NPC.

Merchants specialize. A butcher wants different goods from a tailor, and other
merchants have their own categories. When one merchant refuses an item, that
does not automatically mean the item has no value.

Shady Merchants are useful when the priority is unloading mixed loot quickly.
Specialized merchants are worth finding when getting better value matters more
than convenience.

Stackable, light loot is especially useful during the first levels because one
inventory slot can hold much more value than a heavy low-value object that
forces an early return to town.

</section>

<section id="leave-town-prepared" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Carry food, drink, and light before going far from the city

Three simple problems can end an otherwise good leveling trip without an enemy
ever winning a fight.

### Food and drink

Running out hurts health and mana recovery. Carry enough for the time expected
outside town instead of discovering at the camp that normal downtime has
suddenly become much longer.

### Light

Night becomes dark enough to make unfamiliar routes difficult to read.

The starting candle provides temporary light. Torches are a more practical
early option, while a lantern becomes convenient later because it can sit on
the belt.

Do not consume the light source unnecessarily during daylight when the route
will be much harder after sunset.

### Weight

Encumbrance slows movement, and severe overload can interfere with travel
abilities such as gate or teleportation.

When bags are full of heavy low-value loot, return while the route is still
easy. Another pull is not worth much if carrying the result makes the trip
back dangerous.

</section>

<section id="death" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Remember where you are before a losing fight ends

Death returns the character to the bindpoint while equipment and belongings
remain with the corpse.

When a fight is clearly lost, identify a landmark before the character dies:
a gate, shoreline, tower, bridge, unusual tree, ruin, or dungeon junction.
That information can save much more time than watching the final few percent of
the enemy's health bar.

If the body cannot be reached safely, do not keep repeating the same naked run.
Locate Corpse abilities, corpse dragging, resurrection, spellbook recovery,
and expired-corpse recovery are covered in the
[corpse recovery guide](/monsters-and-memories/corpse-recovery).

</section>

<section id="first-trip" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Before the first real trip outside town

A new character is ready to stay out longer once these pieces are handled:

- the Starter Note has led to the correct class guild;
- the opening class quest has been accepted;
- starting scrolls and abilities have been learned;
- the action bar contains the abilities needed for the next fights;
- food and drink are stocked;
- a working light source is available;
- enough inventory space remains for loot;
- the character knows where to return for trainers and merchants.

From there, the next decision is no longer "which NPC continues the tutorial?"
It is which nearby enemies the character can kill repeatedly without excessive
recovery, travel, or risk.

</section>
