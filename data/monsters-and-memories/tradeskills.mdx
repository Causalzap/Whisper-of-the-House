*Early Access · Last checked October 6, 2026*

Tradeskills in Monsters & Memories overlap much more than they first appear
to. A backpack may begin with hides but still depend on Tanning, wood, resin,
and purchased materials. Fish can become food or move into Alchemy, while
products from that chain can feed later crafts.

Trying to raise every skill at once is usually less useful than choosing one
result you actually need and following its material chain.

For most new characters, the first practical result is more inventory space.

<section id="all-tradeskills" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Can one character learn every tradeskill?

Yes. Tradeskills are not permanent profession choices that lock the others
out.

The same character can develop Leatherworking, Tailoring, Tanning, Fishing,
Lumberjacking, Woodworking, Carpentry, and other crafting skills at different
rates.

![Several tradeskills trained on the same character, including Fishing, Leatherworking, and Lumberjacking.](/images/monsters-and-memories/tradeskills-skill-list.webp)

The important consequence is material ownership: a pelt does not become
irrelevant because Leatherworking is currently low, and wood is not useful
only to a Woodworker. Materials can cross into supporting skills before
reaching the final craft.

That freedom also creates the easiest way to fill every bag and bank slot with
unfinished projects. Keep materials for a result you expect to make soon.
When no reachable recipe needs them and inventory is becoming the limiting
resource, selling or discarding them can be more useful than maintaining a
large stockpile.

**Foraging is listed separately from the normal tradeskill progression**, so
its value should not be used to judge how far the crafting skills have
advanced.

</section>

<section id="what-to-level-first" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Which tradeskills should you start with?

Start from the problem the character needs to solve rather than from a
profession name.

For early inventory space, two useful paths are:

- **Tanning → Leatherworking** for backpacks;
- **Tailoring** for cloth pouches and satchels.

Fishing makes more sense once there is a reason to use the catch through
Cooking, Alchemy, or another connected craft.

Wood and resin are worth saving when Leatherworking is the current goal because
hide processing can consume both before the finished bag is made.

There is little value in opening another crafting chain just because the skill
is available. Early silver, weight, and bag space are limited. Finish a useful
container or another specific craft first, then expand into the next chain.

</section>

<section id="backpacks" className="scroll-mt-24">

## What materials do you need for an early backpack?

For the low-quality-pelt backpack route, prepare:

- **2 low-quality pelts**
- **4 wood**
- **4 resin**
- **1 silver**

The wood and resin are used while processing the hide materials. The silver
covers the purchased component needed to finish the bag.

For several backpacks, scale the same material package directly. For example,
**8 backpacks** require:

- **16 low-quality pelts**
- **32 wood**
- **32 resin**
- **8 silver**

This is more efficient than gathering one material in isolation. Two pelts
without enough wood and resin do not complete the chain, and a large stock of
wood does not solve the bag problem without hides.

There is also a much more material-heavy route using leather scraps. A
backpack can consume **42 leather scraps plus 1 silver**, which makes the
two-pelt route the more practical choice when low-quality pelts are available.

### When should you buy or commission the bag instead?

A first useful container can be worth paying for when the current bags are so
small that loot is forcing repeated trips back to town.

The calculation changes after the immediate shortage is solved. Each backpack
still consumes **1 silver** in purchased material before counting the gathered
resources. Spending the last available silver on another bag can leave no
money for combat abilities, food, drink, or other essentials.

Solve the inventory bottleneck first. Extra containers can wait until their
cost no longer blocks something the character needs more urgently.

</section>

<section id="tanning" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Why does Leatherworking need Tanning, wood, and resin?

Raw pelts are not automatically ready for Leatherworking.

For the low-quality-pelt chain, processing one pelt uses:

- **2 wood**
- **2 resin**

A backpack needs two of those pelts, which is why the full material target
becomes four wood and four resin.

This also changes how resin should be treated. Once Tanning becomes part of
the plan, resin is not only a lighting or miscellaneous resource; it is part
of the hide-processing chain.

Higher crafting tiers begin asking for other materials, including resources
such as **fine wood** and **limestone**. Do not consume scarcer materials only
to push the skill number upward when cheaper materials can still produce useful
results.

Leatherworking and Tanning should progress together closely enough that the
supporting skill can prepare the materials required by the next useful craft.

</section>

<section id="tailoring" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Should you use Leatherworking or Tailoring for bags?

Both can increase inventory space, but they consume different material pools.

**Leatherworking produces backpacks.**

**Tailoring produces pouches and satchels.**

That means the better choice often depends on what is already sitting in the
bank. Pelts, wood, and resin favor the Leatherworking path. A large supply of
cloth gives Tailoring a reason to advance instead.

Cloth satchels can become expensive quickly. One **8-slot satchel** uses
**6 bolts of cloth**, which represents **36 cloth scraps** before the other
parts of the craft are considered.

Smaller pouches cost less material and can be easier to produce repeatedly.
When the next satchel tier consumes too much cloth for one additional upgrade,
making several cheaper useful containers can be the better use of the same
stockpile.

Do not force the highest bag recipe available simply because it exists. The
right craft is the one that improves inventory without consuming materials
needed for more important progression.

</section>

<section id="fishing" className="scroll-mt-24">

## How do you start Fishing?

Fishing requires a few specific setup steps:

1. get a **fishing rod**;
2. obtain **grubs**;
3. equip the grubs in the **ammo slot**;
4. move to a valid fishing location;
5. press **B** and use Fishing from **Innates**.

A fisherman is the first NPC type to check when looking for a rod.

The location matters just as much as the equipment. Fishing cannot be used in
every body of water. When the ability refuses to work, move to another fishing
spot before assuming the skill level is too low.

### What should you keep from Fishing?

Fish have several possible uses.

They can feed directly into **Cooking**, or move through **Alchemy** to create
fish oil. That oil can then become an input for later crafting, including
wood-related recipes.

Fish parts can also remain useful after the main catch has been processed, so
selling everything immediately can break another material chain later.

Fishing is therefore most useful when there is a destination for the catch.
Learn the setup, keep the materials needed by an active Cooking, Alchemy, or
crafting goal, and avoid filling storage with fish that have no near-term use.

</section>

<section id="material-chains" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Which materials are worth keeping?

Think in short crafting chains.

For an early Leatherworking goal:

**pelts → Tanning → processed leather → backpack**

with **wood + resin** feeding the Tanning step and **silver** covering the
purchased component.

For Fishing:

**fish → Cooking**

or:

**fish → Alchemy → fish oil → later crafting**

For Tailoring:

**cloth scraps → bolts of cloth → pouch or satchel**

This makes storage decisions much easier. Keep the materials whose next step
is already known. A bank full of disconnected ingredients is less useful than
a smaller supply that completes an actual recipe.

The same rule helps while leveling. Wolves can provide hides during normal
combat, humanoid enemies can supply cloth, and wood or resin can be gathered
between camps. There is no need to stop character progression every time a
crafting skill needs materials.

The
[leveling guide](/monsters-and-memories/leveling-guide)
covers the early combat route; keep the drops that support the crafting chain
you are actively building and sell the rest when weight or bag space starts
shortening the trip.

</section>

<section id="when-to-stop" className="scroll-mt-24">

## When should you stop pushing a tradeskill?

Stop when the next craft consumes more than the result is worth.

For bag crafting, that point may arrive once the character already has enough
inventory space for a normal leveling trip. For Tailoring, it can happen when
an 8-slot satchel starts consuming cloth faster than it improves storage. For
Tanning or Leatherworking, the next tier may begin asking for scarcer materials
that are better saved.

Fishing has the same limit. Another stack of fish is not useful when there is
no current Cooking, Alchemy, or crafting use for it.

Tradeskills do not need to rise evenly. Fishing may advance while waiting for
another activity, Leatherworking may jump ahead while bag space is a problem,
and Carpentry may remain low until enough wood and fish oil are available for
something worth making.

Raise the skill that completes the next useful item, then redirect materials
and silver when that goal is finished.

</section>
