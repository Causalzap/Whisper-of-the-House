Monsters & Memories rewards grouping, but a strong solo class still matters.
It determines whether a short session can turn into useful experience, money,
travel, or exploration without spending most of that time assembling a party.

Raw damage is only part of the answer. Solo play becomes much easier when a
class can solve several problems at once: keep an enemy off the player,
recover between fights, control distance, survive a bad pull, or travel
through dangerous areas without fighting everything on the route.

<section id="best-solo-classes" className="scroll-mt-24">

## What are the best solo classes in Monsters & Memories?

<GuideTable
  caption="Best solo classes in Monsters & Memories by playstyle."
  columns={soloClassColumns}
  rows={soloClassRows}
/>

For the safest general recommendation, start with **Necromancer**.
Elementalist is easier to understand if the preferred loop is simply
companion in front, caster behind. Druid becomes more attractive when the same
character also needs to heal groups instead of being built around a permanent
pet.

The other choices are more dependent on playstyle. Beastmaster keeps melee
combat central, Bard rewards active control, Ranger favors outdoor
independence, and Rogue asks more from positioning and target selection.

</section>

<section id="necromancer" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Necromancer is the strongest all-around solo pick

Necromancer covers more solo problems with one kit than the other choices
here.

The summoned minion can hold the front line while the Necromancer attacks,
controls the target, or supports the pet from range. That immediately changes
the risk of ordinary solo combat because the character's own health bar is no
longer absorbing every melee hit.

![A Necromancer minion holding the target while the player fights behind it.](/images/monsters-and-memories/necromancer-pet-tanking.webp)

The pet is only one part of the class. Necromancer can also use tools such as:

- life-drain effects to turn damage into sustain;
- snares to control distance;
- fear for kiting suitable targets;
- pet healing and defensive support;
- invisibility and invisibility against undead;
- Feign Death and other emergency tools as the kit develops.

That combination matters outside combat too. A character that can cross a
dangerous area without clearing every enemy has access to more camps, routes,
and recovery options than a class that must win every encounter standing in
the way.

### Where Necromancer can still fail

A strong pet does not make every pull safe.

Fear becomes risky when the fleeing target can reach additional enemies.
Tight dungeon rooms reduce the room available for kiting. A pet that dies
during a difficult pull can leave the Necromancer exposed before the fight is
under control.

Pet-support effects can also involve a direct tradeoff rather than being free
power. Some Necromancer buffs strengthen the pet while reducing the caster's
own durability, which makes avoiding direct aggro even more important.

For ordinary solo progression and exploration, those limitations are usually
easier to manage than having no frontline at all.

</section>

<section id="elementalist" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Elementalist has the simplest strong pet-caster loop

Elementalist is the cleaner choice when the desired playstyle is easy to
understand:

**let the elemental servant engage, then cast from behind it.**

The class is built around fire, water, earth, and air magic combined with a
living elemental companion. The servant is not just an extra damage effect;
its actions are part of how the Elementalist creates space to cast.

![The Elementalist class selection describes destructive elemental magic and a living elemental servant.](/images/monsters-and-memories/elementalist-class-selection.webp)

For solo play, the priorities are straightforward:

- let the companion establish the fight before committing heavy mana;
- use enough spell damage to keep kills moving without emptying the mana bar;
- support the companion when doing so avoids a reset or resummon;
- stop adding expensive effects when normal enemies are already dying cleanly.

Mana is the limiting resource. A fight can be perfectly safe and still be a
bad leveling pull when every kill drains enough mana to force a long break.

Elementalist therefore feels strongest when the servant can stabilize the
fight while the caster uses only the amount of magic needed to finish it.

Harder named enemies and cramped dungeon pulls reduce that simplicity.
Necromancer has more ways to escape or reshape a bad engagement, which is the
main reason it remains the broader solo recommendation.

</section>

<section id="druid" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Druid is the better solo choice when you also want to heal groups

Druid does not solve solo combat by putting a permanent minion between the
character and the target.

Instead, the class combines offensive nature magic with healing and support.
That gives a Druid enough independence to make progress alone while still
filling a valuable healer role when a party becomes available.

This is especially useful for players who do not want their character choice
to force a decision between solo time and group time.

A Druid can spend part of a session grinding alone, then join a group without
changing into a different role or rerolling around party demand.

The tradeoff is that incoming pressure reaches the Druid directly. Health,
mana, positioning, recovery, and enemy abilities all matter more when there is
no pet continuously standing between the caster and the target.

Druid is therefore a flexible solo option rather than the lowest-risk one.

</section>

<section id="beastmaster" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Beastmaster is the solo option for players who want melee plus a pet

Beastmaster pairs a leather-armored melee fighter with a beast companion.

That creates a very different feel from Necromancer or Elementalist. The
companion contributes to survival and damage, but the Beastmaster is still
actively fighting beside it rather than treating the pet as a wall between
the player and the enemy.

The class fits players who want:

- direct melee combat;
- a companion sharing the workload;
- buffs and support built around the pair;
- more solo safety than fighting completely alone.

The main risk is proximity.

When a caster's pet controls the enemy, the caster still has distance
available. Beastmaster is already standing near the target, so a failed pull
or sudden aggro change gives less room to recover.

Target selection matters accordingly. Efficient blue or otherwise manageable
enemies are better than repeatedly forcing harder fights that leave both the
character and companion needing a long recovery.

</section>

<section id="bard" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Bard can solo extremely well when charm works

Bard soloing is powerful for a completely different reason.

Charm can turn one enemy into a temporary weapon against the others. With
reverse-charm tactics, several enemies attack the charmed target at full
strength while the charmed creature deals reduced damage back. Once that
target is worn down, charm can be broken and the surviving enemies cleaned up.

Against suitable normal enemies, this can turn a dangerous pack into its own
damage source.

The limitation is target type.

Charm does not work on everything. For example, undead are not valid targets
for the same beast and humanoid charm tools, and difficult named enemies may
also be immune to the crowd control that makes the strategy possible.

That creates a sharp difference between two camps:

- normal charmable enemies can be excellent solo targets;
- an immune named enemy can remove the core strategy immediately.

Bard also requires more active management than the pet-caster options.
Positioning, songs, charm state, extra enemies and escape room all have to be
tracked during the pull.

Choose Bard for active control rather than for the easiest possible solo
routine.

</section>

<section id="ranger" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Ranger is strongest when solo play includes exploration

Ranger is less about one overpowering solo mechanic and more about reducing
friction while moving through the world.

The class mixes bow and blade combat with fieldcraft, wound recovery,
navigation, and tracking. Those tools become valuable in a game where reaching
the camp can be almost as important as winning the fight once there.

Tracking is especially useful when a target is somewhere in a large outdoor
area rather than standing at a marked objective. Rangers can also recover
between encounters and operate away from town without depending entirely on
another player for basic field support.

That makes Ranger attractive for players who spend a lot of time:

- exploring outdoor zones;
- searching for specific enemies;
- moving between camps;
- gathering information for quests;
- fighting without immediately returning to town after every few pulls.

For pure solo combat safety, the strongest pet classes remain easier.
For moving through large outdoor areas while still being able to fight,
recover, and track targets, Ranger has a different advantage.

</section>

<section id="rogue" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Rogue can solo, but it is not an easy first solo class

Rogue has the tools to create favorable fights: stealth, ambushes, backstab,
stuns, poisons, throwing weapons, and other positional attacks.

The difficulty appears once the opening advantage ends.

A solo enemy normally turns toward the Rogue, which removes the ideal
positioning for repeated back attacks and places all incoming damage directly
on the character. Stealth and control can improve that situation, but the
margin for error is smaller than it is with a pet standing in front.

A clean Rogue solo pull relies on setup:

1. approach from stealth when possible;
2. use the strongest opening positional attack;
3. keep control effects available for the part of the fight when the target
   turns;
4. avoid enemies that require more recovery than the kill is worth.

Rogue becomes much more effective when another character controls the target.
The same positional damage that is awkward to maintain alone becomes easy to
apply from behind an enemy focused on a pet, tank, or other party member.

For players specifically looking for the least punishing solo start,
Necromancer or Elementalist is the safer choice.

</section>

<section id="when-to-group" className="scroll-mt-24">

## When should you stop forcing solo play?

Solo capability is most valuable when it prevents dead time. It lets a
character gain experience, earn money, travel, or finish safe objectives while
a group is unavailable.

Once ordinary kills start taking several minutes, every pull requires a full
recovery, or a dungeon layout removes the space needed for control and kiting,
grouping usually becomes the faster option.

That decision should be based on efficiency rather than whether a class is
technically capable of winning the fight.

For where those transitions happen during early progression, use the
[leveling guide](/monsters-and-memories/leveling-guide).

</section>
