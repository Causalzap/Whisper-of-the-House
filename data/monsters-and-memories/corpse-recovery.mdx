Death in Monsters & Memories separates the character from the equipment.

After dying, the character returns to the bindpoint while carried gear remains
on the corpse. For spellcasters, the equipped spellbook stays there too. A
corpse beside a familiar road may only require a short run back, but a death
inside a dungeon, behind hostile mobs, or somewhere in a large outdoor zone
needs a more careful recovery.

<section id="what-happens-when-you-die" className="scroll-mt-24">

## What happens to your gear when you die?

Your character respawns at the **bindpoint**. Equipment and belongings remain
with the corpse at the death location.

Before a losing fight ends, use the remaining seconds to identify something
that will still help after respawning:

- a gate or zone line;
- a shoreline or river;
- a tower or large ruin;
- a distinctive tree or rock formation;
- a dungeon doorway, staircase, or fork in the route.

There is no in-game map placing a death marker for you, so terrain matters.
Knowing that the corpse is west of a gate or below a particular bridge is much
more useful than remembering only the name of the zone.

When the return route is still recognizable, retrace it normally. Once the
terrain no longer matches what you remember, stop pushing farther into the zone
and switch to a locating ability instead.

</section>

<section id="find-lost-corpse" className="scroll-mt-24">

## How do you find a corpse when you lost the location?

**Necromancers, Bards, and Shadow Knights can help locate corpses.**

Give the helper the best location information available before they begin:
which gate you left, which camp you were near, which side of the river you
crossed, or which dungeon room caused the death. Locate abilities are much more
useful when the search has already been narrowed to the right part of the zone.

![A corpse targeted in Shaded Dunes after a locating ability narrowed down its position.](/images/monsters-and-memories/locate-corpse-shaded-dunes.webp)

A locate attempt that returns nothing does not necessarily mean the corpse is
gone. Position and distance can matter. Move closer to the last known area,
confirm the correct character is being searched for, and try again before
treating the body as unrecoverable.

Once the corpse is targeted, let the equipped player handle the dangerous part
of the route whenever possible. A character with no recovered gear should not
follow directly through the same enemies that caused the original death.

</section>

<section id="drag-corpse" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Drag the corpse out instead of repeating the dangerous run

Finding the body is only half the problem when it is sitting behind hostile
enemies.

Another player can drag the corpse after receiving permission. This is often
safer than asking the dead character to run through a dungeon room, narrow
corridor, hostile camp, or dangerous zone line without normal equipment.

Only give corpse access to someone you trust. A badly moved corpse can turn a
difficult recovery into a longer one.

Groups that play together regularly can prepare corpse-drag macros before a
wipe happens. A basic setup targets one character and then runs the corpse
command:

<pre>
  <code>{`/target CharacterName
/corpse`}</code>
</pre>

Create separate macro entries for party members when needed. That allows a
surviving character to start moving bodies immediately instead of rebuilding
commands during the recovery.

Dragging is most useful when the corpse can be moved:

- behind a doorway;
- down a cleared corridor;
- toward a zone line;
- away from a named enemy or dangerous pull;
- outside the aggro range of whatever is guarding it.

The goal is not merely to move the corpse. Move it to a place where the dead
character can safely recover gear without starting another fight.

</section>

<section id="spellbook" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Why is your spellbook missing after death?

The spellbook is equipped gear, so it remains on the corpse with the rest of
the equipment.

![The equipped spellbook slot shows why a caster can lose the normal spell setup after death.](/images/monsters-and-memories/spellbook-corpse-recovery.webp)

That makes caster recovery different from simply putting armor back on. Until
the spellbook is recovered, the character may not have access to the normal
spell setup needed to reach the body or resume the camp.

Two systems reduce that risk.

### Use a Cantrip for a spell you would need after death

Classes begin receiving **Cantrips at level 4**. A spell protected by a
Cantrip can remain available after death.

That makes recovery value more important than normal combat frequency when
choosing one. A movement, protection, healing, or escape spell may be more
useful at the bindpoint than another damage spell while the rest of the gear
is still on the corpse.

### Keep a second spellbook in the bank

A secondary spellbook costs about **1 silver** and can be left in the bank
with recovery spells prepared for a bad death.

Do not carry both books together. If both are on the character when death
happens, both can end up at the same corpse.

Early on, one silver can still compete with important spells, abilities,
supplies, and equipment. Buy the backup once that cost is no longer delaying a
more important upgrade.

Bards do not have the same spellbook problem for songs because their songs use
Innates rather than an equipped spellbook.

</section>

<section id="resurrection" className="scroll-mt-24">

## When is resurrection worth waiting for?

**Clerics and Paladins can resurrect**, which can return a character to the
corpse and recover some of the experience lost to death.

A nearby outdoor corpse usually does not justify waiting around for extra
help. Run back, recover the gear, and continue.

Resurrection becomes more valuable when the corpse is:

- deep inside a dungeon;
- far from the bindpoint;
- behind enemies the returning character cannot safely pass;
- part of a larger party wipe;
- attached to enough experience loss that recovering some of it matters.

The useful comparison is not only travel time. Include the risk of another
death, the time needed to rebuild the group, and whether somebody can already
locate or drag the corpse into a safer position.

</section>

<section id="after-recovering-corpse" className="scroll-mt-24">

## What should you do immediately after recovering the corpse?

Getting the equipment back does not mean the character is ready for another
pull.

For a caster, re-equip the spellbook and restore the spells needed for the next
fight. For a melee character, confirm weapons and armor are back in place.
Then restore health and mana before returning to the camp.

After a difficult recovery, check these four things:

- critical equipment is equipped again;
- required spells or abilities are ready;
- health and mana have recovered;
- the route that caused the death is now safe enough to continue.

When the same pull still blocks the party, change the situation before trying
again. Pull from another position, clear nearby enemies first, move the camp,
or wait for another player.

Repeating the same approach with freshly recovered gear often produces the same
corpse in the same place.

</section>

<section id="expired-corpse" className="scroll-mt-24">

## What happens if the corpse expires?

Expired equipment is not permanently deleted.

Once a corpse reaches its expired state, look for an
**Opportunistic Adventurer** in a tavern. This NPC can help summon the corpse
back so the equipment can still be recovered.

Treat this as the final fallback rather than a normal recovery method. Until
the corpse reaches that state, locating it, dragging it, or making the return
run keeps the equipment available much sooner.

</section>

<section id="recovery-order" className="scroll-mt-24">

## What is the safest corpse recovery order?

When the corpse is no longer a simple run back, use this order:

1. **Identify the last known area.** Use gates, water, roads, dungeon rooms,
   bridges, or other landmarks.
2. **Ask for Locate Corpse** when the body cannot be found quickly.
3. **Keep the unequipped character somewhere safe** while an equipped player
   approaches the body.
4. **Drag the corpse** when standing on it would immediately start another
   fight.
5. **Use resurrection** when the location, travel time, or experience loss
   makes it worthwhile.
6. **Re-equip and recover fully** before restarting combat.
7. **Find an Opportunistic Adventurer** if an unrecovered corpse has already
   expired.

The dangerous part of a corpse run is usually not the first death. It is
trying to solve the recovery with fewer tools than the character had before
dying. Use locating, dragging, and resurrection to change the situation before
taking the same route again.

</section>
