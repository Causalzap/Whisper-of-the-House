Leveling in Monsters & Memories is built around camps rather than a chain of
quest hubs. The important decision is not simply where a character of a
certain level can survive. A useful camp needs enough suitable enemies,
reasonable recovery between pulls, and enough spawns to keep experience
moving.

That makes some weaker enemies better leveling targets than harder ones. A
dark-green mob that dies quickly can be more productive than a stronger target
that drains the mana bar, forces a long rest, or puts the corpse at risk every
few pulls.

<section id="how-to-level" className="scroll-mt-24">

## How to choose a leveling camp

Before changing zones, judge the camp by four things:

- **Kill speed:** ordinary pulls should not drag on long enough to make every
  enemy feel like a mini-boss.
- **Recovery:** health and mana should recover before downtime becomes longer
  than the fights themselves.
- **Spawn supply:** the character or group needs enough targets to keep moving
  instead of waiting for respawns.
- **Risk:** one bad pull should not regularly turn into a corpse run.

Mob color helps, but it does not replace those checks. Dark-green enemies can
still give worthwhile experience when they die fast enough, while harder
enemies can be inefficient when every kill requires a full reset.

Rested experience is also worth taking before a long grind. Resting near a
campfire or inside an inn builds a reserve that gives a **15% experience
bonus** while it lasts. A usable rest point can refill that reserve within a
few minutes, so topping it off before settling into a camp is usually worth
the short delay.

Weather can interfere with campfire rest when the character is not sheltered,
so an inn is the more reliable option when conditions are bad.

Quests still matter for class progression, equipment, access, and specific
rewards. They are not the main source of experience carrying a character
through these early brackets.

## Levels 1–4: Learn what your character can kill efficiently

The first levels are better spent learning the limits of the class than
searching for one mandatory starter monster.

In Faelindral, the dense Dryad population gives level 1 characters a practical
option when nearby targets are being heavily contested.

![Level 1 characters can use the dense Dryad population around Faelindral as an early grinding option.](/images/monsters-and-memories/level-1-dryads-faelindral.webp)

Wolves and other easy enemies around the starting regions are also useful, but
proximity to town does not guarantee that every creature is meant for a level
1 character. Check the target's con before committing and pay attention to how
much health, mana, or pet durability one kill costs.

Night Harbor has more than one low-level hunting area. The **West Gate** and
**North Gate** both provide early targets, while additional enemies can be
found around the docks, the river running through the city, and the outskirts.
Rats, goblins, snakes, and crocodiles give alternatives when the obvious
newbie yard is crowded.

The best early camp is usually the place where several safe targets can be
killed in sequence without competing with a crowd.

### Return to town when inventory starts slowing the route

Early bags fill quickly. Once carrying capacity or encumbrance begins slowing
travel, finishing one more pull is rarely worth much.

Return while the trip is still easy, sell or bank what matters, then go back
out with enough free space for another full camp cycle. Bag crafting and
material choices are covered in the
[tradeskills guide](/monsters-and-memories/tradeskills).

</section>

## Levels 4–10: Wyrmsbane becomes available, but do not force it

Wyrmsbane can begin working for a group at roughly **level 4**, but being able
to enter a dungeon does not make it the best leveling choice immediately.

Around levels 4–6, compare the dungeon against whatever outdoor camp is already
working. Wyrmsbane becomes worthwhile when the group can keep pulls moving
without spending most of the session recovering, reorganizing, or waiting for
another role to arrive.

A useful early dungeon group normally needs enough of the following:

- somebody or something that can hold the target;
- enough healing or sustain to avoid leaving after every few pulls;
- enough damage to prevent fights from stretching out;
- enough nearby enemies to keep the group occupied.

At level 6, an outdoor camp with easy pulls and little downtime can still beat
a dungeon group that takes a long time to assemble or struggles through each
enemy.

Wyrmsbane also has equipment value, so slightly slower experience can still be
worthwhile when several party members need useful drops. Stay for that reason
only while the dungeon continues providing something the group actually needs.

Do not remain because the zone is supposed to be a leveling destination.
Leave when the group spends more time recovering or waiting than killing.

</section>

## Levels 10–15: Add Blacktide Bay to your options

**Blacktide Bay becomes a practical group destination around level 10.**

The bracket is less about replacing Wyrmsbane on a fixed schedule and more
about choosing whichever camp your group can actually use well. Characters in
the low teens can already make progress inside Blacktide Bay, while Wyrmsbane
can remain useful when its camps are open or its equipment still matters.

For characters leveling around Evershade Weald, **Keeper's Bight** also offers
higher-level drakes once the easier drake camps stop giving enough progress.
Treat the move as a difficulty increase rather than an automatic upgrade.

A good choice at this stage depends on availability:

- an open Blacktide Bay group can be better than a perfect camp with no space;
- a familiar Wyrmsbane room can outperform a harder camp with long recovery;
- higher-level drakes are useful only when they still die cleanly enough to
  justify the extra pressure.

Green enemies are not a reason to leave by themselves. If the group is
clearing continuously and experience is still arriving at a useful rate, the
camp can remain productive after some targets drop in con.

</section>

<section id="levels-15-20" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Levels 15–20: Check party XP when the level spread grows

Large level gaps can stop the lower-level character from receiving party
experience.

At **level 12**, grouping with a **level 18** character can produce the
message that the lower-level character is too low to receive party XP. At
**level 13**, XP can resume while the highest character remains level 18.

That does not make six levels a fixed cutoff. Whenever the highest or lowest
member of the party changes, kill one normal enemy and check the XP message
before settling into the camp.

If anyone receives no experience, fix the level spread before committing to a
long dungeon session.

Green enemies can still be worthwhile when the group clears them quickly. In
this bracket, losing XP entirely because of the party's level spread is a much
bigger problem than fighting mobs that have started to con green.

</section>

## Levels 20–25: Work through the River Pirate camps before Glass Flats

River Pirates provide a useful progression path through the low 20s because
the same area contains enemies at noticeably different difficulty levels.

Pirates near the easier part of the camp can be around **level 21**, while
enemies farther toward the back can reach roughly **level 25**.

![A low-20s River Pirate fight with a summoned skeleton holding the target for the group.](/images/monsters-and-memories/necromancer-pet-tanking.webp)

Start where pulls are stable rather than moving directly to the deepest part
of the camp.

The route can progress without changing zones:

- work the easier pirates while they still provide solid experience;
- push farther back once those pulls become routine;
- use the stronger pirates only when the group can recover without long
  breaks;
- begin looking toward **Glass Flats** once the useful pirate range is running
  out.

The level 25 enemies in the back are not automatically better targets than
level 21 enemies near the front. A clean pull that keeps the camp moving is
usually better than a stronger enemy that empties healer mana or repeatedly
kills the pet or tank.

This is also why exact zone charts become less useful as characters gain
levels. One camp can cover several brackets depending on where the group
stands and which enemies it chooses to pull.

</section>

## Should you solo or look for a group?

Solo when the character can already produce steady experience without long
recovery.

A rested character killing dark-green or other manageable enemies can keep
progressing while looking for players. That is especially useful during short
sessions when forming and travelling to a dungeon group would consume most of
the available time.

Grouping becomes better when it changes the pace of the camp:

- a tank or pet makes dangerous pulls stable;
- healing removes long recovery periods;
- crowd control allows the party to handle additional enemies;
- extra damage keeps enough mobs flowing to support the larger group;
- dungeon access opens equipment or experience that solo play cannot match.

Do not wait for a perfect six-character composition before doing anything.
Start with the players who make the camp safer or faster, then add people when
the available spawns can support them.

Class choice changes how productive the waiting period can be. The
[best solo classes guide](/monsters-and-memories/best-solo-classes)
covers which classes have the strongest tools for progressing alone.

</section>

<section id="when-to-leave" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Leave a camp when the bottleneck changes

A leveling location has run its course when the enemies are no longer the
thing limiting progress.

Move when one or more of these starts happening:

- kills take much longer than the experience justifies;
- health or mana recovery dominates the time between pulls;
- somebody in the party is no longer receiving XP;
- too many players are competing for too few spawns;
- the group has outgrown the useful enemies in the camp;
- the equipment available there no longer matters enough to justify slower XP.

Stay longer when the opposite is true. Fast dark-green kills, useful drops,
an easy return route, a nearby rest point, and uncontested spawns can make an
older camp more productive than travelling across the world for a theoretically
higher-level zone.

Through level 25, a practical route is therefore:

**starter camps → Wyrmsbane or efficient outdoor camps → Blacktide Bay and
higher-level regional camps → River Pirates → Glass Flats**

The transitions are flexible. Use the level ranges to find the next reasonable
option, then let kill speed, recovery, XP messages, and available spawns decide
when the move actually happens.

</section>
