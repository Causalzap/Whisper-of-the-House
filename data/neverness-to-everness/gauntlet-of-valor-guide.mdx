<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
Clear **Gauntlet of Valor Normal once** before deciding whether to farm it. Normal has **99 floors**; clearing Floor 99 opens **Infinity**. The first half goes quickly on a developed 999 Nights account, while the meaningful checks start around Floor 80 and become much harsher from Floor 90 onward.

For progression, bring one reliable sustain option and a way to group scattered enemies. If a late floor times out while the whole team is alive, fix damage or grouping. If characters die with plenty of time left, fix survival first.

The one-time Normal clear is worth doing: the mission set pays **5,500,000 Warren Gold Coins and 5,500 Mystery Buttons**, before counting enemy drops. Infinity is useful after the unlock, but it is not the route I would choose for pure Mystery Button farming if an efficient Crimson Castle farm is already available.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

<h2 id="unlock-gauntlet-of-valor">
  Where to Find Gauntlet of Valor
</h2>

Gauntlet of Valor is part of **999 Nights** on the Warren Continent.

Enter 999 Nights through the permanent event entry or the board in your apartment, then open the Warren Continent map.

The Gauntlet teleport point sits **east of Crimson Castle** and uses a pink beast-style icon.

Normal and Infinity serve different purposes.

**Normal** is the fixed progression climb with numbered floors and one-time mission rewards.

**Infinity** opens afterward and continues through waves rather than another numbered ladder.

That makes the first goal straightforward:

**finish Floor 99 once before worrying about an Infinity farming setup.**

<h2 id="normal-structure">
  Normal Changes More in the Last 20 Floors Than the First 79
</h2>

Normal has **99 floors**.

The climb follows a recognizable pattern for most of the run: smaller stages between regular boss checkpoints, with enemy level and durability rising as the floor number increases.

A complete 1–99 clear on a strong account takes roughly **an hour to an hour and a half**, depending on how quickly the early floors are rushed.

The difficulty does not rise evenly.

The run I checked broke down more like this:

- **Floors 1–20:** fast clears, little reason to adjust the team
- **Floors 21–50:** shorter timers begin to expose wasted movement
- **Floors 51–80:** enemies survive longer and cooldown timing starts to matter
- **Floors 81–89:** some floors leave roughly one minute for the clear
- **Floors 90–98:** boss-heavy stretch with much higher survival pressure
- **Floor 99:** long multi-boss finish
- **Infinity:** unlocked after the numbered tower is complete

So I save serious team decisions for the part of the tower that actually tests them.

<h2 id="floors-1-20">
  Floors 1–20 Should Be Cheap
</h2>

Several early stages can disappear in roughly **15–30 seconds** when the account is already geared for later 999 Nights content.

That is the benchmark I use before touching resources.

If normal enemies are dying in one short rotation, keep climbing.

Floor 20 is the first visually obvious checkpoint because it brings **two dragon bosses** together.

A slow Floor 20 does not automatically mean a new character is needed.

First look at what is actually happening.

If both dragons stay alive through several full rotations, the team may simply be underbuilt for the mode.

If one dies quickly and the other takes time because the rotation was spent on the first target, that is a sequencing problem.

There is little value in rebuilding a team that is still winning comfortably.

<h2 id="floors-21-50">
  Floors 21–50 Start Charging for Wasted Movement
</h2>

The timer becomes more noticeable around Floor 21.

A stage here can give about **1 minute 30 seconds**, even while the enemies themselves remain easy to kill.

That changes one priority immediately:

**how far the team has to move between targets.**

When enemies spawn on opposite sides of the arena, a grouping skill can save more time than adding a little single-target damage.

By the high 40s, the enemies begin surviving attacks that erased the early floors.

Floor 50 provides a cleaner damage checkpoint.

The run reached **two Lv.85 ice dragons** with roughly **four minutes** available.

A comfortable Floor 50 clear is enough reason to keep the same core lineup.

The tower becomes much more informative later. Spending heavily here can fix a problem that never becomes the real late-floor bottleneck.

<h2 id="floors-51-80">
  Floors 51–80 Punish Bad Cooldown Timing
</h2>

The middle climb is where enemies stop feeling disposable.

Dragon bosses around Floor 60 reach roughly **Lv.90**, and another double-dragon checkpoint appears around Floor 70.

By this point, dumping every Ultimate into an enemy with a sliver of HP starts hurting the next floor.

If a target is almost dead, finish it with cheaper skills and carry the stronger cooldown into the next spawn.

That habit matters more as Floor 80 approaches.

The Floor 80 run reached **two Lv.103 dragons**.

Reaching Floor 80 without repeated wipes is a strong sign that the basic team shell is working.

Before changing characters, check three things:

- whether the opening burst is ready when a floor starts
- whether one slot can restore HP without consuming the whole timer
- whether scattered enemies can be pulled into the same damage window

Those weaknesses become much easier to see in the 80s.

This is where slow setup starts losing clears.

On mob floors, grouping comes first.

A common failed run looks like this:

**one enemy survives far away → time is spent crossing the arena → the next group spawns somewhere else → the last few seconds disappear during movement**

More single-target damage does not fix that pattern as efficiently as bringing enemies together.

Floor 83 can create the same sub-one-minute pressure.

Boss stages in the same range give more time, so one lineup has to handle two different jobs:

**short mob stage:** immediate grouping and burst

**boss stage:** enough sustain to remain functional through the full fight

If one style works and the other repeatedly fails, the problem is already specific enough to fix without rebuilding the entire account.

<h2 id="inventory">
  Make Inventory Space Before Floor 90
</h2>

A late climb can stop at the reward screen because the inventory is full.

That happened before the final push.

Clearing space before Floor 90 avoids breaking momentum between the hardest fights.

Discard or process the low-value items that are already expendable, then leave enough room for the remaining drops and milestone equipment.

Waiting until a Floor 90+ reward cannot be claimed turns an inventory decision into an interruption between difficult stages.

  Floors 90–98 Tell You Exactly What the Team Is Missing

From this point upward, boss encounters become the normal problem.

Floor 91 already takes noticeably more damage to clear.

By Floor 93, a bad defensive response can turn into a one-shot.

Floor 95 punishes the same mistake harder, and Floor 98 includes a Crimson Dragon capable of deleting a character if its dangerous attack connects.

This is where I stop asking whether the team is “strong enough” and look at the failure itself.

### The timer expires while everyone is alive

The immediate problem is **damage uptime or grouping**.

Check:

- whether burst is being spent on the correct target
- how much time disappears while moving
- whether Ultimates sit unused waiting for a perfect window
- whether one enemy keeps surviving outside the main AoE

More healing will not solve a clean timeout.

### A character dies while plenty of time remains

The floor is exposing **survival**.

Healing, dodge timing, positioning, or defensive investment now has a clearer return than another damage increase.

A sustain slot is not wasted DPS when it prevents a full restart.

The late bosses can remove fragile characters in one clean hit, so entering the final ten floors with four offensive slots only works while mechanics are being avoided consistently.

### Damage is fine until the last few bosses

Do not rebuild around Floors 1–80.

Spend on the current late-floor weakness instead.

That is where the same resource produces the most visible progression.

<h2 id="floor-99">
  Floor 99 Has 10 Enemies, Not One Final Health Bar
</h2>

Floor 99 is a boss rush.

The stage begins with **10 enemies remaining**, and the bosses reach roughly **Lv.115**.

The available time is much longer than the one-minute floors. One clear starts around the four-minute range, giving enough room to stabilize between targets.

The extra time changes the resource pattern.

Burning every recovery tool into the first boss can leave the later targets with nothing available.

Keep enough healing and cooldown recovery to survive the handoff into the next encounter.

The lineup includes different boss types, including scorpion and dragon enemies, so one perfect opening burst does not finish the floor by itself.

If a DPS dies midway through but the remaining characters stabilize, continue.

The floor has more room for a slower recovery clear than Floor 81 or 83.

A strong run can finish Floor 99 well inside the full timer; one recorded clear showed a **1:30 clear time** after the stage was completed.

Infinity changes from fixed floors to continuing waves.

Enemy levels sit around **Lv.115 and above**, so the same team that barely survived Floor 99 can enter Infinity without being ready to farm it efficiently.

AoE becomes especially valuable because the mode keeps feeding enemies into the arena.

There are two things that stop Infinity from being truly hands-off.

Some enemies remain far enough from the active fight that AoE never reaches them, forcing you to move over and finish them.

Crimson Dragon waves are more dangerous. A passive team can fail to kill a high-level dragon before its lethal attack becomes a problem.

So I treat Infinity as **semi-AFK at best**, not a mode I start and ignore indefinitely.

Milestones are tied to the Normal climb, with rewards attached as progress moves through the tower.

The complete mission set adds up to:

- **5,500,000 Warren Gold Coins**
- **5,500 Mystery Buttons**

The exact total will move with individual drops, so I treat 20,000 as a useful run-scale number rather than a guaranteed fixed payout.

<h2 id="worth-it">
  Is Gauntlet of Valor Worth Clearing?
</h2>

**Normal: yes.**

The first clear gives permanent progression, unlocks Infinity, and adds the large one-time mission payout on top of the Mystery Buttons dropped during the climb.

There is little reason to leave 5.5 million Warren Gold Coins, 5,500 mission Mystery Buttons, and the Infinity unlock unfinished if the account can already push through the late tower.

The farming answer is different.

<h3 id="infinity-worth-farming">
  Infinity Is Not My First Choice for Mystery Button Farming
</h3>

A measured Infinity run reached about **50 waves in 45 minutes** and produced roughly **7,000 Mystery Buttons**.

That is a meaningful payout.

It is not especially efficient compared with a developed Crimson Castle farming route.

The difference becomes visible in the drops themselves. Infinity commonly pays smaller Mystery Button stacks, while an established Crimson Castle loop can produce **50- and 100-button drops per pull** alongside Warren Gold Coins.

So my order is:

**Need the one-time Gauntlet rewards or Infinity unlock:** clear Normal.

**Want to push waves because the challenge itself is the goal:** play Infinity.

**Only care about Mystery Buttons per minute and already have a good Crimson Castle farm:** use Crimson Castle.

Infinity still has value as a combat benchmark and a semi-AFK option. It simply does not need to replace a faster farming route.

<h2 id="when-to-stop-upgrading">
  Stop Spending When the Failure Is Gone
</h2>

Gauntlet can consume resources indefinitely if every slower floor is treated as a reason to add more stats.

I stop when the actual blocker disappears.

If the timer problem is solved, extra damage is optional.

If the team survives the attack that previously caused the wipe, more defensive investment can wait.

If Floor 99 clears and Infinity is not part of the current goal, there is no need to keep upgrading a Gauntlet-specific setup simply because another wave exists.

The tower is most useful when it tells you **why** the account failed.

Once that answer changes from a clear weakness to “the fight is just taking a little longer,” the next upgrade can wait.
