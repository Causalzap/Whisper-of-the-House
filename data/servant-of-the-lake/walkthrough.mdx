The **Servant of the Lake walkthrough** is easiest for me when I treat the
Vanderboom weekend as a chain of duties rather than a house full of unrelated
puzzles.

The main story runs through **24 core tasks**:

- **9 tasks on Day 1**
- **8 tasks on Day 2**
- **7 tasks on Day 3**

Most of the times I got stuck were not because I had missed an obscure secret.

I had either left a task before collecting its progression item, tried to solve
something the game was not ready to use yet, or kept searching the current room
after the Notebook had already moved me somewhere else.

So my first-playthrough rule is simple:

**finish the active duty → take the item or instruction it gives me → follow the
next Notebook task.**

<h2 id="how-i-use-this-walkthrough">
  How I know whether to continue or backtrack
</h2>

I use the active Notebook task as my main checkpoint.

If the current task is still active, I usually stay inside that room or the
small group of rooms connected to it.

If the Notebook has already advanced to a new task, I stop clicking old objects
just because they still look suspicious.

That distinction saves a lot of wandering.

There is one other situation where I do backtrack:

**I have reached the next room, but I am missing the item that should have come
from the previous duty.**

That happens most noticeably around Laundry, the Bathroom, and the Day 2 night
route.

When that happens, I do not search the whole estate.

I return to the immediately previous task and make sure I actually collected
its reward.

<h3 id="day-1-arrival">
  The Arrival — get inside and start using the Notebook
</h3>

The opening sequence starts before I am properly inside the estate.

I finish the coachman's interaction first, deal with the entrance sequence, and
only then move into my actual servant duties.

Once I meet Kate, I sign the employment contract and receive the **Servant's
Notebook**.

That Notebook is more important than it first looks.

It records:

- the current time of day;
- the location;
- the active task;
- documents I have found;
- and achievement progress.

From this point onward, I use it whenever I am unsure which room the game
actually wants me to work in.

<h3 id="day-1-afternoon">

</h3>

- **Opening the Reunion**

I finish Alexandra's tea sequence first.

Once the game moves everyone into the Living Room, I follow the reunion puzzle
instead of returning to old afternoon rooms.

recurring symbols.

I remember them, but I do not try to solve every future use immediately.

<h3 id="day-1-night">
  Day 1 Night — finish the Watch, then search the Attic
</h3>

The final Day 1 tasks are:

- **The Night Watch**
- **Searching the Attic**

The house becomes much less ordinary here.

opens.

The Attic is my final Day 1 checkpoint.

Once that task resolves, I stop trying to clean up every leftover Day 1
interaction and move into the next morning.

That keeps hidden achievement hunting from slowing down the main story.

<h3 id="day-2-william">
  Waking up William — stay in the room until he is actually awake
</h3>

William's morning sequence is easy to underestimate.

Simply bringing him Coffee does not complete the whole task.

If I am interacting with his bed, feet, or the objects immediately connected to
his room and the task is still active, I keep solving there rather than walking
back downstairs.

<h3 id="day-3-departure">

<h2 id="shortest-route-rule">
  The route I use when I only want to keep progressing
</h2>

When I feel lost, I reduce the whole game to four checks.

**Day 1:** follow the estate schedule and learn how the Notebook hands one duty
into the next.

**Day 2:** make sure each multi-step task gives me its progression item before I
leave, especially around Laundry, the Bathroom, and the night route.

**Day 3:** finish the Cat search, complete Alchemy as one full ingredient chain,
then follow the maintenance and dinner tasks into The Departure.

**Post-game:** stop using the main walkthrough and switch to chapter replay for
achievements or the hidden ending.

That is the distinction that keeps Servant of the Lake manageable for me:

**the walkthrough tells me where to go next; the puzzle guides tell me how to
solve the blocker once I get there.**
