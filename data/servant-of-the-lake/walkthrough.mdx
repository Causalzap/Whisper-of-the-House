The **Servant of the Lake walkthrough** is much easier for me when I treat the
Vanderboom weekend as one chain of duties instead of a house full of unrelated
puzzles.

The main story has **24 core tasks**:

- **9 on Day 1**
- **8 on Day 2**
- **7 on Day 3**

My main rule is:

**finish the active duty → collect the item or instruction it gives me → follow
the next Notebook task.**

Most of the times I got stuck, I had either left a room before taking its
progression item or kept searching an old room after the Notebook had already
moved on.

<h2 id="day-1-walkthrough">
  Day 1 walkthrough
</h2>

Day 1 teaches me how the estate hands one servant duty into the next.

I do not try to solve every strange symbol or suspicious object the first time
I see it. My goal is to follow the household schedule and reach the Attic
without turning optional clues into fake progression blockers.

<h3 id="day-1-arrival">
  The Arrival — enter the estate and get the Servant's Notebook
</h3>

I finish the coachman's interaction and the entrance sequence before treating
anything inside the house as a real servant task.

Once I meet Kate, I sign the employment contract and receive the
**Servant's Notebook**.

From that point onward, the Notebook is my main progression check. It tracks the
current time, location, task, collected documents, and achievement progress.

If I am unsure where the game wants me next, I check the active duty before
wandering through the estate.

<h3 id="day-1-breakfast">
  Preparing Breakfast — finish Aldous's meal first
</h3>

The first proper household job is **Preparing Breakfast**.

I collect what the breakfast instructions require and complete the meal before
trying to push Aldous's bedroom forward.

This is an easy point to get distracted because the house already contains
objects that look important.

I ignore those until the Notebook needs them.

My checkpoint is:

**breakfast completed → move to Waking up Aldous.**

<h3 id="day-1-reception">
  Preparing the Reception — finish the house setup before calling the guests
</h3>

Once Aldous's morning is complete, I shift from bedroom work to preparing the
estate for the family reunion.

I handle the reception-related setup and flower tasks while this duty is active.

I do not start searching the Attic or Cellar because those areas belong to much
later parts of the schedule.

My progression rule here is simple:

**finish the reception setup before trying to trigger the guests.**

When the Notebook changes to Welcoming the Guests, I stop checking reception
objects and follow the new task.

<h3 id="day-1-guests">
  Welcoming the Guests — let the reunion schedule move forward
</h3>

After the Reception is ready, the arriving Vanderbooms become the next focus.

I complete the guest-arrival interactions instead of returning to unfinished
visual clues around the house.

At this point the story begins establishing more of the family relationships
and recurring symbols.

I remember those details, but I do not try to solve their future meaning yet.

Once the arrival sequence finishes, I follow the Notebook into Serving Tea.

<h3 id="day-1-tea">
  Serving Tea — finish Alexandra's tea before leaving the room
</h3>

The next servant duty is Alexandra's tea sequence.

I finish the requested tea interactions first and wait for the Notebook to
advance before moving elsewhere.

This is another place where I avoid using unexplained clues too early.

If an object clearly belongs to a puzzle but the current task has not created a
reason to use it, I leave it alone.

My checkpoint is:

**tea duty resolved → follow the family into Opening the Reunion.**

<h3 id="day-1-reunion">
  Opening the Reunion — follow the family into the Living Room
</h3>

Once everyone moves into the Living Room, I treat the reunion as the active
route.

I stop returning to the reception rooms because their job is already done.

This section introduces more family relationships and repeated imagery that
will matter later.

I pay attention to them without trying to turn every symbol into an immediate
code.

When the reunion sequence resolves and the Notebook moves into the night, I
follow it instead of continuing to search the Living Room.

<h3 id="day-1-night-watch">
  The Night Watch — finish the night duties before going to the Attic
</h3>

The estate changes tone during **The Night Watch**, but the same progression
rule still works.

I complete the current night interactions first.

I do not treat the Attic as available just because I know it will matter later.

Once the route actually opens and the Notebook moves into Searching the Attic,
I leave the earlier night-duty area behind.

<h3 id="day-1-attic">
  Searching the Attic — use it as the final Day 1 checkpoint
</h3>

The Attic is the final main-story destination on Day 1.

I complete the search sequence until the duty properly resolves.

Once the Notebook closes out the Attic task, I stop trying to clean up every
optional interaction from the first day.

That is especially important on a first playthrough.

Achievement hunting can wait until the game gives me a much better replay
system after the normal ending.

<h3 id="day-2-night-duties">
  Night Time Duties — check the items earned earlier in the day
</h3>

The Day 2 night sequence brings earlier progression rewards back into use.

If I reach a mechanism that expects something I do not have, I do not search
random Day 1 rooms.

I check the Day 2 tasks I just finished.

Laundry and the Bathroom are especially important because both have clear

This is where I use backtracking narrowly:

**missing required item → return to the immediately previous duty that should
have given it.**

I do not search the whole estate.

Once the night sequence opens the way into the Cellar, I follow it down.

When my current tools cannot solve an obstacle, I use the available reset and
try a combination that fits that branch.

<h2 id="walkthrough-stuck">
  If I am stuck, I check these four things
</h2>

### 1. Has the Notebook already changed tasks?

If yes, I stop searching the previous room.

The game has usually already given me permission to leave.

### 2. Did the previous duty give me an item that I forgot to collect?

This is the first thing I check on Day 2.

The **Bathroom Key** after Laundry and **Alexandra's Bronze Time Piece** after
Helping with Bathing are good examples of progression items I do not want to
leave behind.

### 3. Is the current problem actually a puzzle rather than a route problem?

If I know I am in the correct room and only need a code, pattern, ingredient
combination, tool choice, or logic answer, I stop using the walkthrough and
switch to the relevant puzzle guide.

The walkthrough tells me **where progression goes next**.

The puzzle pages tell me **how to solve the blocker once I get there**.

### 4. Have I already finished The Departure?

If yes, I stop treating the estate like the first-playthrough route.

I use chapter replay for achievements and the hidden ending instead of repeating
all 24 main tasks from the beginning.
