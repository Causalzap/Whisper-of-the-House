My first run through **Servant of the Lake** is much easier when I treat the
Vanderboom weekend as a sequence of duties instead of trying to solve every
strange object the moment I find it.

If I am still progressing through the three days, I follow the current Notebook
task and move from one duty into the next.

If I already know which task I am in and one puzzle is stopping me, I go
straight to the relevant solution.

After The Departure, I switch priorities completely and use task replay for
achievement cleanup and the hidden story route.

<h2 id="day-1">
  Day 1 — learn how the estate works
</h2>

Day 1 teaches me the rule I use for the rest of the game:

**current duty first, future mystery later.**

The house constantly shows me symbols, locked objects, and strange interactions
that will matter again later.

I do not try to solve all of them immediately.

Instead, I move through breakfast, Aldous's morning, preparations for the
guests, Tea, the Reunion, the Night Watch, and the Attic in the order the
Notebook gives them to me.

<h2 id="roots-connection">
  Is Servant of the Lake connected to Rusty Lake: Roots?
</h2>

Yes.

**Servant of the Lake takes place decades before Rusty Lake: Roots** and returns
to the Vanderboom house during the era of Aldous and William Vanderboom.

That is why so many familiar family names, symbols, locations, and references to
the Elixir start appearing as I move through the three-day story.

I do not need to have finished Roots to follow the servant's weekend, but knowing
the Vanderboom family history makes several of the later scenes and Alchemy
references much easier to recognize.

<h2 id="how-i-avoid-wasting-time">
  The three rules that save me the most time
</h2>

**I follow the active task before chasing background mysteries.**

Servant of the Lake constantly shows me things before I need them. If the
Notebook has already moved me somewhere else, I normally move on too.

**I do not leave a multi-step task the moment the dialogue changes.**

Aldous, Laundry, the Bathroom, and the Cellar all have points where a task looks
nearly finished before I have actually collected or completed the step that
moves progression forward.

**I save optional cleanup for task replay.**

Once the normal ending is complete, revisiting individual memories is much
cleaner than turning the first playthrough into an exhaustive search of every
clickable object.

For a normal run, my route is simple:

**Walkthrough for progression → Puzzle Solutions when I know the blocker →
dedicated puzzle guides for the longer chains → Achievements and Secret Ending
after the main story.**
