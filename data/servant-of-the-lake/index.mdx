**Servant of the Lake** is much easier once I stop treating the Vanderboom
estate like one giant escape room.

The game is structured around **24 main Notebook tasks across three days**.
Each task gives me a specific duty, location, or progression handoff, and most
of the confusing objects around the house only become useful when the Notebook
actually sends me back to them.

My basic rule is:

**follow the current duty first → solve the blocker in front of me → collect
the task handoff → move when the Notebook moves.**

After **The Departure**, that structure changes. The Notebook becomes a replay
tool for hidden achievements and the larger post-game secret route.

<h2 id="day-1">
  Day 1 — learn when to ignore a mystery
</h2>

Day 1 teaches me the most important habit in the game:

**current duty first, future mystery later.**

The nine main tasks are:

**The Arrival → Preparing Breakfast → Waking up Aldous → Preparing the
Reception → Welcoming the Guests → Serving Tea → Opening the Reunion → The
Night Watch → Searching the Attic.**

The house starts showing me symbols, locked objects, unusual paintings,
family clues, and suspicious interactions almost immediately.

Most of them are real.

They are simply not all **current**.

That distinction saves me a lot of unnecessary room searching.

<h2 id="after-ending">
  What opens after The Departure
</h2>

The normal ending does not make the earlier tasks irrelevant.

It makes them easier to revisit.

After **The Departure**, the Notebook lets me return to individual memories
instead of replaying the entire three-day weekend.

That is when I switch from story progression to cleanup.

<h2 id="roots-connection">
  Is Servant of the Lake connected to Rusty Lake: Roots?
</h2>

Yes.

**Servant of the Lake takes place decades before Rusty Lake: Roots** and
returns to the Vanderboom family during the era of Aldous and William.

That is why familiar names, family relationships, the Elixir, recurring
symbols, and locations begin to feel increasingly important as the weekend
continues.

I do not need to know Roots to understand what the Notebook wants me to do.

Knowing the wider Vanderboom history mainly adds context to the later story and
Alchemy scenes rather than changing the puzzle solutions.

<h2 id="how-i-avoid-wasting-time">
  The three rules that save me the most time
</h2>

**1. I trust the current Notebook task more than a suspicious object.**

The estate deliberately shows me things before I need them. I remember them,
but I do not force them into the wrong duty.

**2. I distinguish a route problem from a puzzle problem.**

If I do not know where to go, I use the walkthrough.

If I know exactly which task and room I am in, I use the relevant puzzle page.

That prevents me from rereading a full story route when all I need is one code
or object sequence.

**3. I save exhaustive cleanup for Notebook replay.**

The first run is for understanding the three-day progression.

The post-game replay system is for hidden achievements and the secret route.

My full Servant of the Lake flow is:

**Walkthrough for progression → puzzle guide when I know the blocker →
Achievements after The Departure → Secret Ending when I start Kate's
investigation.**
