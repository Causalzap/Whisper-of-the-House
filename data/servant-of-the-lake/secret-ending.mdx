The **Servant of the Lake secret ending** does not come from choosing a
different answer during the normal three-day story.

I only start the real hidden route after finishing The Departure and reading
Kate's final note.

That message completely changes how I use the Servant's Notebook. Instead of
treating old tasks as finished chapters, I replay specific memories and use one
hidden clue to tell me where to look next.

The first instruction is:

**"The crow awakens at five past four."**

From there, the route sends me back through Aldous's bedroom, Alexandra's
Bathroom, the Day 3 Alchemy sequence, the Attic, and eventually a hidden chamber
before the Telescope leads me to Kate's missing brother.

<h2 id="when-secret-ending-starts">
  When I start the secret ending route
</h2>

I do not restart the entire game after the normal ending.

Once I finish the final night and leave the estate, Kate gives me a reason to
return.

Her message explains that her brother disappeared a year earlier and asks me to
use the Notebook to revisit the previous three days.

That is the important change.

The Notebook now lets me replay individual tasks, so I stop treating Day 1,
Day 2, and Day 3 as a straight timeline.

For the hidden route, I am looking for one thing:

**a clue in the current memory that tells me which old task to replay next.**

<h2 id="crow-awakens-five-past-four">
  The crow awakens at five past four — use 4:05
</h2>

The first line that sent me in circles was:

**"The crow awakens at five past four."**

I go back to **Day 1 — Waking up Aldous**.

That memory contains both the alarm clock and the crow near the window, which
is enough to tell me I am in the right place.

Instead of using Aldous's normal morning alarm, I set the clock to:

**4:05**

<h2 id="maze-symbols">
  The dark maze sends me back to Day 3 Alchemy
</h2>

The maze looked like a completely new cipher the first time I reached it.

It is not.

The directional language has already appeared during **Assisting in Alchemy**.

Once I recognize that, I reuse the Left and Right symbols instead of trying to
decode another alphabet from scratch.

I treat each input as one movement through the maze.

The route I use is:

**Right → Right → Right → Left → Right → Left → Right → Right → Left**

Or, written compactly:

**R → R → R → L → R → L → R → R → L**

<h2 id="attic-constellation">
  The Attic symbols are relationships, not another number code
</h2>

This was my next major dead end.

I initially tried to translate every symbol into a number or reuse the Alchemy
meanings again.

That got me nowhere.

I return to the **Attic** and look for objects that belong together.

One clear example is:

**boat → fish → water droplet**

Those three related objects form a triangle.

That gives me the rule for the rest of the puzzle.

I connect objects by their relationship first, then use the shapes created by
those connections.

<h2 id="secret-chamber-code">
  How I solve the secret chamber lock
</h2>

The chamber lock calls back to information I have already used elsewhere.

I do not brute-force it from `000`.

Two clue positions come from earlier puzzles.

The Bathroom-style X clue gives me:

**6**

The Alchemy symbol gives me:

**4**

That leaves one unresolved dial.

I set that remaining position to:

**2**

The important part is that I do **not** treat this as a simple `642` password.

I match **6, 4, and 2** to the clue positions shown on the chamber lock.

The Telescope is fine.

<h2 id="where-i-got-stuck">
  The places that wasted the most time for me
</h2>

The secret ending became much easier once I stopped treating every note as a
brand-new puzzle.

Most of the route is asking me to reuse something I already learned during the
three normal days.

**If "The crow awakens at five past four" is going nowhere**, I replay Waking
up Aldous and use **4:05** on the bedroom alarm.

**If the Bathroom clue feels too vague**, I follow the nouns from the previous
note in order:

**Alexandra → bathtub → suitcase → severed hand**

**If the maze route fails**, I restart that attempt and enter all nine
directions cleanly:

**R → R → R → L → R → L → R → R → L**

**If the Attic symbols look meaningless**, I stop translating them one by one
and connect objects that share a relationship.

**If the chamber lock is confusing**, I keep the three clue positions separate
instead of assuming `642` is a normal three-digit code.

**If the Telescope shows nothing**, I change viewpoints. I do not go searching
for another Telescope or another inventory item.

The rule I use for the entire post-game route is:

**read the new note → identify the old memory it describes → replay that task →
reuse the puzzle language I already learned.**

Once I started following that pattern, the secret ending stopped feeling like a
collection of disconnected codes and started feeling like one long puzzle
spread across all three days.
