The **Servant of the Lake Bathroom puzzle** is where Day 2 made me stop
treating every strange object as a separate problem.

The room looks like several unrelated puzzles at first: a sliding board, groups
of symbols, a mouse hole, stitches on Alexandra's shoulder, soap, bubbles, old
family notes, and a locked place hiding her Time Piece.

They are actually one dependency chain.

I get through **Helping with Bathing** much faster when I stop clicking
everything at once and follow this order:

**get the Bathroom Key → fill the bath → solve the category puzzle → get the
Scissors → finish the sliding puzzle → get the Soap → cut the stitches → take
the Maggot → use it at the mouse hole → get the Comb → finish Alexandra's bath
→ solve the number clue → enter 281 → take the Bronze Time Piece.**

this room before the game is ready for it.

I finish the earlier chores first.

The important handoff comes from **Doing the Washing**.

The hanging clothes eventually give me the Key that opens the Bathroom, so if I
am standing outside Alexandra's locked door without one, I leave instead of
searching the hallway for another hidden switch.

<h2 id="fill-bath-first">
  I fill the bath before chasing every clue
</h2>

Alexandra's first instruction is simple:

**fill the bath.**

Then she asks me to add **Soap**.

The problem is that I do not have the Soap yet.

That is the moment where I stop treating her dialogue as an instruction to
search my inventory and start looking at the two puzzles already inside the
room.

There are two useful rewards waiting for me:

- one puzzle gives the **Scissors**;
- the sliding puzzle gives the **Soap**.

Both matter before I can finish the bath.

<h2 id="category-puzzle">
  How I solve the Bathroom category puzzle
</h2>

The symbol puzzle confused me because I initially tried to read the pictures as
one sequence.

That is not what it wants.

I group the pictures by what they belong to.

The categories I use are things such as:

- **body parts / senses**;
- **family members / people**;
- **animals**;
- **flowers**;
- **furniture**;
- and other matching personal objects.

Once I recognize the grouping rule, I stop moving tiles randomly.

For example, eyes, ears, nose, and the other body-related symbols belong
together; animals form another set; flowers another.

Finishing the groups gives me the **Scissors**.

I keep them.

Trying the Scissors on the mouse or on random room objects does not move the
main route forward yet.

Their target only becomes obvious after Alexandra is in the bath.

<h2 id="sliding-puzzle">
  The sliding puzzle is easier if I solve the center first
</h2>

This was the part of the Bathroom that cost me the most time.

My first instinct was to place every corner correctly as soon as I saw where it
belonged.

That trapped the pieces I actually needed to move.

The method that worked for me was the opposite:

**leave the corners flexible and solve the three central pieces first.**

Once the center is right, the remaining outer pieces can be moved into place

The reward is the **Soap**.

At that point I finally return to Alexandra instead of continuing to
rearrange the room.

<h2 id="use-soap">

I put the Soap into the filled bath.

That advances Alexandra's instructions.

She then asks me to remove her hat and find a **Comb** for her hair.

I still do not have the Comb.

But removing the hat and examining her more closely exposes the detail that
makes the Scissors useful.

There are **stitches on her shoulder**.

<h2 id="bubble-puzzle">
  How I approach the Bubble puzzle
</h2>

After the hair and undressing steps, the bath produces another puzzle involving
the bubbles.

The bubbles are not just different sizes.

They contain **directional information**.

  <Image
    src="/images/servant-of-the-lake/servant-of-the-lake-bathroom-bubble-puzzle.webp"
    alt="Directional bubble puzzle during Helping with Bathing in Servant of the Lake"
    width={1280}
    height={720}
    className="h-auto w-full rounded-xl"
  />
  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">
    I choose the bubble whose direction can actually lead into the next valid
    bubble, then keep following that chain.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

I do not solve this by clicking from smallest to largest.

Instead, I look at the direction shown by the current bubble and ask:

**Which bubble can this one point into next?**

If I reach a dead end immediately, I chose the wrong starting bubble.

I reset mentally and start with the bubble whose direction creates a valid chain
through the others.

Once the full directional path is completed, Alexandra is satisfied and says
she needs to rest.

That finishes the bathing part of the task, but I do **not** leave the room
immediately.

There is still something I need from her.

<h2 id="bathroom-code-281">
  The Bathroom code is 281
</h2>

After Alexandra settles down, I keep inspecting the room.

The family notes and the X-mark clue finally become useful here.

One of Alexandra's family notes shows the digit string:

**92681375**

The difficult part is deciding which digits survive the elimination clue.

I tried reading the X marks too literally at first and ended up with plausible
but wrong combinations.

The code that actually opens the Bathroom lock is:

**281**

If a code derived from the note looks convincing but the lock rejects it, I do

I go back to the X clue.

Here, **281** is the accepted answer.

<h2 id="bathroom-stuck">
  What I check when Helping with Bathing stops progressing
</h2>

If Alexandra keeps asking for **Soap**, I have not finished the sliding puzzle.

I solve the middle first and leave the corners until the center is aligned.

If I have the **Scissors** but cannot see what to cut, I advance the bath until
Alexandra's stitched shoulder is accessible.

If she asks for a **Comb**, I do not search the rest of the estate.

I cut the stitches, take the Maggot, and return to the mouse hole.

If the **bubble puzzle** seems random, I stop sorting by size and follow the
directions printed on the bubbles.

If Alexandra says she is finished but I still do not have her **Time Piece**, I
do not leave.

I inspect the number clues, enter **281**, wait until she is asleep, and use her
pendant on the final compartment.

That is the point where I consider the Bathroom truly finished:

**not when Alexandra finishes bathing, but when the Bronze Time Piece is in my
inventory.**
