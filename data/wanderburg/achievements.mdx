<h2 id="achievement-roadmap">
  Most Wanderburg achievements overlap with normal progression
</h2>

The achievement list looks much grindier on day one than it feels during the early account.

Twenty of the 26 achievements belong to four cumulative counters. Driving adds distance, ordinary combat adds vehicle kills, moving through populated areas adds consumed units, and every boss kill contributes to the fourth chain. Those numbers continue moving while I am unlocking Lands, testing vehicles, earning Silver, and improving the permanent account.

That is why I treat the six progression achievements as the first priority:

- **Spiderburg unlocked**
- **Welcome to the Dark Forest!**
- **Tankenburg unlocked**
- **Welcome to the Desert!**
- **You won!**
- **All Lands won!**

I want those objectives producing useful account progress while the four large counters accumulate for free.

A dedicated farm only starts making sense once the map is largely finished and I can see which counters are actually lagging behind.

<h2 id="all-lands">
  All Lands won! should come before the long cleanup grind
</h2>

**All Lands won!** is different from simply revealing every location on the overworld.

An unlocked Land can still need its own win state, so this is the progression achievement I want finished before I settle into dedicated farming.

That order is efficient because an unfinished Land can still advance every cumulative chain during the same run:

- I add distance while moving;
- destroy vehicles through normal combat;
- consume units along useful routes;
- and add boss kills while completing the Land.

If I spend hours farming 10,000 vehicle kills first, those remaining progression runs will still contain thousands of vehicles afterward.

The same logic applies to distance, units, and bosses.

Harder Lands also tell me whether the permanent account is actually ready for long farming sessions. A setup that can survive only the easiest route may increase counters, but it still leaves **All Lands won!** unfinished.

I solve that progression problem first.

<h2 id="cumulative-achievements">
  The four cumulative achievement chains need different cleanup runs
</h2>

The first thresholds of all four chains should happen through ordinary play. The final thresholds are where I start optimizing deliberately.

### Distance: 10 to 10,000 km

The full chain is:

**10 → 100 → 1,000 → 5,000 → 10,000 km**

Distance is the simplest counter because every useful run contributes.

I already had the first milestone during early progression, so I see little value in driving circles while the permanent account still has Lands and unlocks left to complete.

Once distance becomes one of the last unfinished goals, the run changes. I care about staying alive and moving for a long time more than squeezing maximum damage out of every slot.

A stable run that continues travelling is better for this achievement than a spectacular build that dies quickly.

### Vehicles: 100 to 10,000 destroyed

The vehicle chain is:

**100 → 500 → 1,000 → 5,000 → 10,000**

Vehicle kills accumulate quickly during ordinary combat, which makes the final number less intimidating than it first appears.

The 100-kill achievement arrived naturally for me. I leave the larger milestones alone while progression is still sending thousands of enemies into the same counters.

During final cleanup, damage that continues working while I move becomes especially valuable because stopping to manually line up every ordinary vehicle wastes time across thousands of kills.

The exact builds that create that kind of persistent pressure are covered in the <Link href="/wanderburg/best-builds">Wanderburg best builds guide</Link>.

### Units: 1,000 to 100,000 consumed

The unit chain rises much higher:

**1,000 → 5,000 → 10,000 → 50,000 → 100,000**

I consume dense groups that already sit along the route and avoid turning one distant cluster into a major detour.

That distinction barely matters while working toward 1,000.

At 100,000, it becomes the difference between accumulating units during useful runs and constantly interrupting the route for tiny gains.

Once this chain becomes a genuine cleanup target, I want an account strong enough to pass through heavily populated sections without sacrificing the entire run every time I move toward another group.

### Bosses: 10 to 500 destroyed

The boss chain is:

**10 → 25 → 50 → 100 → 500**

This is the counter I expect to remain unfinished longest for many accounts.

A normal completed boss sequence contains four fights. If all 500 kills came only from complete four-boss sets, the final achievement represents the equivalent of **125 full boss sequences**.

That scale is exactly why I leave it late.

Land clears, failed runs that still kill early bosses, vehicle tests, and ordinary farming runs can all reduce the remaining total before I ever repeat content specifically for boss kills.

Once 500 bosses itself becomes the objective, consistency matters more than experimentation. A familiar run that repeatedly clears the complete boss sequence contributes faster over time than a more exciting setup that frequently dies before the last fight.

The boss-specific preparation and late-run failure checks are in the <Link href="/wanderburg/bosses">Wanderburg Boss Guide</Link>.

<h2 id="overtime-for-achievements">
  Overtime is useful when several achievement counters still need work
</h2>

Achievement cleanup is one of the clearest reasons to continue a strong run after the normal win.

The castle is already built, so additional time can keep adding:

- distance travelled;
- vehicle kills;
- units consumed;
- and more combat opportunities without rebuilding the account from level one.

That does not mean I continue every victory.

If the finished castle has already lost its ability to move safely, ending the run and starting another reliable one can produce better long-term achievement progress than forcing a short survival attempt.

For achievements, I value **repeatable productive time** more than one dramatic Overtime record.

The broader continue-versus-leave decision and the actual Silver value of Overtime belong in the <Link href="/wanderburg/progression-unlocks">Wanderburg progression and unlocks guide</Link>.

<h2 id="completion-order">
  My Wanderburg achievement completion order
</h2>

From a fresh account, this is the route I would use:

**1. Reach the first Game Won state.**  
Prove that the current permanent account can finish the normal boss sequence before worrying about large cumulative numbers.

**2. Continue unlocking Lands and vehicles.**  
Spiderburg, Dark Forest, Tankenburg, Desert, and the remaining map progression all advance the account while the four cumulative counters keep growing.

**3. Win every Land.**  
Finish **All Lands won!** before turning the account into a dedicated farming setup.

**4. Compare the four cumulative counters.**  
Check distance, vehicles, units, and bosses after progression is substantially complete. I do not assume the achievement with the largest number is automatically the slowest one for my account.

**5. Combine cleanup targets whenever possible.**  
A long mobile run can add distance, kill vehicles, consume units, and advance bosses at the same time. I avoid creating four completely separate farms unless the remaining counters genuinely require it.

**6. Finish the slowest remaining chain deliberately.**  
Distance favors long survivable runs, vehicle kills benefit from damage that keeps working while I travel, unit cleanup rewards populated routes, and boss completion favors reliable four-boss clears.

This order also prevents the most common waste: farming something early that normal progression was going to give me anyway.

I will take a short detour when a counter is already close to its next threshold. I will not reorganize the whole account around 10,000 km, 100,000 units, or 500 bosses while required Lands are still unfinished.

By the final step, there should be very little mystery left in the achievement list.

The problem is throughput: keeping each run productive enough that several counters move together without sacrificing the consistency that got the account through the map in the first place.
