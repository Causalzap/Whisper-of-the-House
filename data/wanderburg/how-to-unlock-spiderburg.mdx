<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
Clear **all four bosses in Golden Dunes** to unlock Spiderburg. After the unlock message appears, check the loadout before starting another run: Spiderburg uses **two Top Module slots, one Front slot, one Back slot, and no Side slot**. If the account only owns one usable Top Module, the vehicle can be unlocked and still remain unplayable.

<h2 id="unlock-spiderburg">
  Clear the full Golden Dunes boss objective to unlock Spiderburg
</h2>

Spiderburg is not hidden somewhere inside Golden Dunes, and there is no special vehicle drop to search for.

The unlock is tied directly to the Land objective:

**complete the full four-boss Golden Dunes clear.**

Golden Dunes itself comes after the opening Green Plains progression. If it has not opened yet, I finish that earlier map gate first instead of looking for a Spiderburg-specific trigger.

Once I enter Golden Dunes for the unlock, partial progress is not enough. Killing the first, second, or third boss does not create a partial vehicle unlock; the permanent reward arrives only after the complete objective is satisfied.

If the run consistently reaches the later fights but cannot finish them, the blocker is combat rather than another hidden Spiderburg condition. That creates the most important distinction on this route:

**Spiderburg unlocked does not necessarily mean Spiderburg is ready to use.**

<h2 id="two-top-modules">
  Spiderburg needs two usable Top Modules
</h2>

Spiderburg changes the starter vehicle's module layout substantially.

The chassis I unlocked showed:

- **2 Top Module slots**
- **1 Front Module slot**
- **1 Back Module slot**
- **no Side Module slot**

The game gave me the useful warning directly: because the chassis has two Top positions, I needed another usable Top Module before the vehicle could start.

That requirement is literal.

Captains and Crews do not fill it, and buying a Side or other incompatible module does not solve it either. I check the slot icon on the permanent loadout and look specifically for another item classified as **Top**.

<h2 id="what-to-buy">
  Save enough Silver to complete the second Top slot
</h2>

Spiderburg is one of the first unlocks where permanent spending can delay something the account has technically already earned.

By this point the shop contains plenty of attractive purchases, but a new Captain, Crew, artifact, or unrelated module does not help if Spiderburg still has an empty required position.

Compatible Top options I encountered included **Top Mortar, Archer Tower, and Fire Mage**, depending on which permanent unlocks and purchases were already available.

I do not need every Top Module. I only need enough usable equipment to fill both Top positions.

That changes how I spend Silver shortly before a Golden Dunes clear. If Spiderburg is the next permanent target and the account still owns only one usable Top piece, I prefer leaving some currency available rather than emptying the account on a sidegrade that does not make the new chassis playable.

The broader question of whether Silver should go into modules, Crews, or Captains first is covered in the <Link href="/wanderburg/progression-unlocks">Wanderburg progression and unlocks guide</Link>.

<h2 id="spiderburg-chassis">
  Spiderburg is genuinely nimble before the castle becomes huge
</h2>

Spiderburg's vehicle description calls it a **nimble vehicle on legs**, and that matched the early handling much better than I initially expected.

The legs are functional rather than cosmetic: **stomps deal damage**, and Spiderburg is **immune to terrain effects such as water**.

Its base turning also feels very different from a slow tank-style chassis. While the castle is still reasonably small, I could reverse direction sharply, avoid incoming Rams, and rotate through tight angles that would have taken much more room on a less agile vehicle.

That distinction matters.

When Spiderburg becomes difficult to move late in a run, I do not assume the vehicle itself suddenly has bad turning. Boss upgrades have made the castle larger, enemy density has reduced the usable route, and depleted Nitro can remove the extra space the chassis needs to exploit those tight turns.

A nimble vehicle can still be trapped when there is nowhere left to turn.

The general Movement-versus-Defense tradeoff for large late-run builds is covered in the <Link href="/wanderburg/best-builds">Wanderburg best builds guide</Link>.

<h2 id="double-top">
  Two Top slots change which upgrades scale efficiently
</h2>

The second Top position begins as a purchase requirement, but it becomes one of Spiderburg's defining advantages once both slots are filled.

An upgrade or modifier that affects **all Top Modules** now improves two equipped pieces at the same time.

That made shared Top damage and cooldown bonuses noticeably more attractive during my Spiderburg runs. The benefit does not require two identical weapons; the important part is having two useful Top Modules that can both profit from the same slot-wide scaling.

One can handle persistent or area pressure while the other provides more immediate ranged damage, and a shared Top bonus still helps both.

Once the two positions are legal, the remaining question is no longer how to unlock Spiderburg. It is whether those modules form a coherent damage engine, which is covered in the <Link href="/wanderburg/best-builds">Wanderburg best builds guide</Link>.

<GuideCallout title="Spiderburg is a sidegrade, not an automatic upgrade">
Two Top slots can make Top-wide bonuses much more efficient, but losing the Side slot can break a permanent setup built around Side Cannons, Side Ballista, or another Side weapon. I switch chassis when the account supports Spiderburg's layout, not simply because it was unlocked later.
</GuideCallout>

<h2 id="is-spiderburg-good">
  Spiderburg is good when the permanent loadout fits the chassis
</h2>

Spiderburg's value comes from changing the platform rather than simply adding more raw progression.

A permanent account with useful Top options can take immediate advantage of the double-Top structure, while the tight turning and terrain immunity give the chassis practical advantages during normal movement.

The tradeoff is just as important.

If most of my proven damage comes from a Side weapon, changing vehicles removes that piece before the next run even begins. In that situation, the starter vehicle can still be the better choice despite being unlocked earlier.

This is why I judge Spiderburg on two questions:

- can the account fill both Top positions with modules that actually contribute;
- and does losing the Side slot remove one of the build's strongest permanent pieces?

If the answer to both works in Spiderburg's favor, the chassis is a real upgrade for that account.

If not, I switch back. The vehicle remains permanently unlocked, so there is no penalty for waiting until the Top pool becomes stronger.

<h2 id="spiderburg-not-working">
  Spiderburg not working? Check which state you are actually in
</h2>

There are two different Spiderburg blockers.

**If the vehicle has never unlocked**, return to the Golden Dunes objective and finish its complete boss requirement. Silver purchases cannot bypass that progression gate.

**If Spiderburg is already unlocked but cannot start a run**, stop checking Golden Dunes. Open the permanent loadout and inspect the two Top positions. An empty one usually means the account still needs another usable Top Module.

That is the fastest way I have found to diagnose the problem:

**no unlock message → progression problem  
unlock message but no playable vehicle → loadout problem**

<h2 id="after-spiderburg">
  Test Spiderburg before rebuilding the account around it
</h2>

After the unlock, I avoid changing the Captain, buying several new modules, changing chassis, and jumping into harder content all at once.

Too many changes make a failed run difficult to read.

My first Spiderburg attempt is deliberately controlled. I fill the required Top positions, keep the rest of the permanent setup as familiar as the chassis allows, and use the vehicle somewhere I already understand.

That run gives me a much cleaner answer about what Spiderburg itself changed: how the steering feels, whether the missing Side position damaged the old setup, whether the two Top Modules work together, and how well the chassis still handles once it grows.

A good result tells me the permanent account is ready to invest further into Spiderburg.

A weak result does not undo anything. I return to the previous vehicle, continue earning Silver and permanent unlocks, then revisit Spiderburg once the Top options are better.

The Golden Dunes clear puts Spiderburg on the account permanently.

Making its new slot layout stronger than the setup I already had is the part that comes afterward.
