<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
Clear **all four bosses in Golden Dunes** to unlock Spiderburg. If the unlock appears but the vehicle still cannot start a run, check the loadout instead of repeating Golden Dunes: Spiderburg uses **two Top Module slots, one Front slot, one Back slot, and no Side slot**. An account with only one usable Top Module can own Spiderburg without yet having a legal starting loadout.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

<h2 id="unlock-spiderburg">
  How to unlock Spiderburg
</h2>

Spiderburg comes from the **Golden Dunes four-boss objective**. There is no separate vehicle pickup inside the Land and no hidden interaction to trigger after one of the individual fights.

I need to finish the complete Golden Dunes sequence.

Golden Dunes itself sits behind the earlier Green Plains progression, so if the Land is not available yet, the missing step is farther back in the overworld rather than somewhere inside the Spiderburg route.

The important word here is **complete**. Killing one, two, or three Golden Dunes bosses does not partially unlock the chassis. The permanent reward comes after the fourth boss finishes the Land objective.

If the run is already reaching that final stretch and dying there, I stop looking for another progression trigger. At that point the problem is the run itself. The <Link href="/wanderburg/bosses">Wanderburg Boss Guide</Link> covers the boss sequence, while the <Link href="/wanderburg/best-builds">best builds guide</Link> is the better place to fix a setup that loses damage or mobility before the clear is finished.

The confusing part can come on the loadout screen afterward. I unlocked Spiderburg, selected it, and still could not launch the next run.

The vehicle was not bugged behind another Golden Dunes requirement. Its chassis simply expected a different permanent module layout from the vehicle I had been using.

That distinction is the key to troubleshooting Spiderburg:

**the progression gate can be finished before the account owns enough compatible modules to use the vehicle.**

<h2 id="two-top-modules">
  Spiderburg needs two Top Modules before it can start
</h2>

Spiderburg's starting layout is:

- **2 Top Module slots**
- **1 Front Module slot**
- **1 Back Module slot**
- **no Side Module slot**

That is why another Golden Dunes clear is wasted effort once this warning appears.

<h2 id="what-to-buy">
  What to buy if Spiderburg is unlocked but unusable
</h2>

The account does not need every Top Module in the game. It needs **two compatible Top choices that are both permanently available for the starting loadout**.

Top options I encountered included **Top Mortar, Archer Tower, and Fire Mage**, depending on which conditions and Silver purchases had already been completed.

This makes the timing of Silver purchases important. If I am close to finishing Golden Dunes and Spiderburg is the next vehicle I actually intend to use, spending the account down to zero on a Captain or an unrelated Side upgrade can leave me staring at an unlocked chassis with an empty required slot.

I would rather secure the second usable Top piece first.

The broader purchase order changes depending on which modules, reroll milestones, Captains, and vehicles are already open, so that belongs in the <Link href="/wanderburg/progression-unlocks">Wanderburg progression and unlocks guide</Link> rather than turning this page into a general Silver guide.

<h2 id="spiderburg-chassis">
  Spiderburg feels much more agile than its growing size suggests
</h2>

Spiderburg is described as a **nimble vehicle on legs**, and the handling supports that identity. Its movement speed was also increased during Early Access, so the chassis now has more room to use those sharp direction changes in practice.

While the castle is still compact, I can reverse direction quickly, rotate through tight gaps, and avoid incoming Rams without needing the wide turning arc that becomes uncomfortable on heavier layouts. The legs are functional too: **stomps can deal damage**, and Spiderburg ignores terrain effects such as water.

The late run tells a slightly different story. Each boss upgrade makes the castle physically larger, enemies occupy more of the usable route, and the Nitro bar has more work to do whenever I need to cross a dangerous section quickly. The chassis can still turn well and yet have nowhere useful left to turn.

That is why I do not answer late Spiderburg movement problems by stacking Defense alone. A bigger health pool may buy another mistake; enough Movement and Nitro can prevent the castle from sitting inside the next attack at all.

<h2 id="double-top">
  The second Top slot is more than an unlock tax
</h2>

Needing two Top Modules initially feels like an extra purchase requirement. Once the loadout is legal, it becomes one of the reasons to use Spiderburg.

Bonuses that affect **Top Modules** can improve both equipped positions. That gives shared damage, cooldown, range, or other Top-wide upgrades more leverage than they would have on a chassis carrying only one Top weapon.

The modules do not need to be identical. I can use one Top slot for persistent or area pressure and another for more immediate ranged damage, then take an upgrade that benefits both positions.

This is also why Spiderburg works particularly well with a developed permanent account. A shallow Top pool turns the second position into a restriction; a strong Top pool turns it into another place for the same upgrade economy to compound.

The actual module pairings belong in the <Link href="/wanderburg/best-builds">Wanderburg best builds guide</Link>.

<GuideCallout title="Spiderburg changes the build, not just the vehicle">
Spiderburg gains a second Top slot but loses the Side slot. A permanent setup built around Side Cannons, Side Ballista, or another important Side Module cannot simply be copied onto the new chassis.
</GuideCallout>

<h2 id="is-spiderburg-good">
  Is Spiderburg worth using?
</h2>

Yes, **if the permanent account already has good Top Modules**.

Spiderburg's appeal is not a hidden stat bonus that makes every previous vehicle obsolete. It gives me a different platform: tighter handling, terrain immunity, stomp damage, and two Top positions that can benefit from the same upgrade family.

The cost is the missing Side slot.

That matters immediately if the build that has been carrying progression depends on Side Ballista or Side Cannons. Unlocking Spiderburg can therefore make the available chassis newer without making the current build stronger.

I look at the existing permanent loadout before switching. If I can fill both Top positions with modules I actually want to invest in, Spiderburg has a strong reason to exist. If replacing the Side slot removes the engine of my best build, I keep using the earlier chassis and come back later.

There is no penalty for doing that. Spiderburg remains permanently unlocked.

<h2 id="spiderburg-not-working">
  Spiderburg not working: progression problem or loadout problem?
</h2>

There are two states that look similar until I check the unlock itself.

**Spiderburg has never appeared:** Golden Dunes is not finished yet. I return to its objective and complete all four required bosses.

**Spiderburg appears in the loadout but cannot start:** Golden Dunes is already finished. I check whether both Top positions can be filled with permanently usable Top Modules.

The second case is especially easy to misread because the game has already given the vehicle while still preventing the run from launching.

One Early Access hotfix also added protection for a Spider player-vehicle loadout freeze. If the required slots are filled correctly but the loadout itself is freezing rather than showing a normal missing-module warning, I make sure the game is current before troubleshooting the progression route again.

<h2 id="after-spiderburg">
  The first Spiderburg run should answer one question
</h2>

I do not rebuild the whole account the moment Spiderburg becomes available.

For the first run, I fill the required Top positions and keep as much of the remaining setup familiar as the chassis allows. That gives me a clean comparison with the vehicle I was already using: whether the sharper handling helps, how much losing the Side slot costs, whether the two Top Modules actually support one another, and how the larger Spiderburg feels after several boss upgrades.

If that version already works, investing further makes sense.

If it feels weaker than the old vehicle, I do not treat the Golden Dunes clear as a mistake. The permanent unlock is finished; the account simply needs a better Top pool before Spiderburg can outperform the setup that came before it.

The important part is that I no longer have to unlock Spiderburg again.

From that point onward, the vehicle is waiting for the permanent loadout to catch up.
