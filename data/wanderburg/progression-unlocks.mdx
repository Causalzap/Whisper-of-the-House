<h2 id="how-progression-works">
  Clear the boss gate, then improve what survives between runs
</h2>

Wanderburg started making sense to me once I stopped treating every unlock as part of one progression bar. A run can advance the overworld, unlock a permanent module, earn enough Silver for a new starting piece, and still leave the next run looking surprisingly similar to the previous one.

Those systems move together, but they are not interchangeable. **Biome objectives open lands and vehicles. Silver buys permanent loadout options. Module and cumulative objectives expand what can appear in that permanent loadout.** A fresh account has almost nothing to buy anyway, so my first priority is simply getting Green Plains moving instead of spending time staring at locked shop entries.

That separation also explains one of the game's most confusing early moments: opening a new land does not automatically fill the upgrade pool with new weapons. If the map changed but the starting arsenal did not, the progression is probably waiting somewhere in the permanent unlock layer rather than on the overworld.

<h2 id="green-plains">
  Finish all four Green Plains bosses before looking for another route
</h2>

The Green Plains objective screen gives a much clearer roadmap than the opening minutes of the run itself. The important early objectives include:

- defeating all four Green Plains bosses to open **Golden Dunes**;
- defeating the Green Plains boss set to open **Meadows**;
- consuming **100 units in one run** for **500 Silver**;
- destroying **20 Rams overall** for **1,000 Silver**;
- destroying **5 Harvesters overall** for **2,500 Silver**.

The first boss is therefore a checkpoint, not the end of the progression objective. Each boss grows the castle and gives me another vehicle upgrade, but the map gate is tied to finishing the complete sequence.

If the later fights are what stop the clear, I no longer treat that as a map problem. The <Link href="/wanderburg/bosses">Wanderburg Boss Guide</Link> covers the four-fight sequence and what changes between bosses, while the <Link href="/wanderburg/best-builds">best builds guide</Link> covers the loadouts that scale through the full run.

<h2 id="where-to-go-next">
  Golden Dunes and Meadows open different kinds of progress
</h2>

After Green Plains, the overworld stops being a single obvious line. I had both **Golden Dunes** and **Meadows** available, so the next choice came down to what I wanted the run to change permanently.

Golden Dunes is the route I favor when vehicle progression is the goal because its full boss objective leads to **Spiderburg**. Meadows is not a dead-end alternative: its own four-boss clear continues the overworld by opening **Waving Fields**, and its side objectives add more permanent Silver opportunities.

That makes older lands useful without making them mandatory. I return to one when a specific objective is nearly finished, when I want to fund a known purchase, or when a new loadout needs a safer test environment. Replaying Green Plains simply because it is familiar usually leaves the account in almost the same place.

The map also becomes less intuitive later. I have seen later gates reference another complete boss set around **Dark Forest 2 / Somber Marsh**, which is why I trust the objective text more than the physical position of a land on the overworld.

<h2 id="silver">
  Silver is strongest when it fixes the next run before it starts
</h2>

A new account begins with essentially no Silver, so the first useful lesson is that the shop does not need to be solved immediately. The opening run itself creates the first permanent economy. Once Silver starts accumulating, though, the number of possible purchases expands much faster than the amount I can afford.

Captains, Crews, modules, artifacts, and later vehicle requirements all draw from the same pool. The best purchases for me have been the ones that remove a concrete starting limitation: a missing module type, the piece that completes a build, or a Captain whose drawback and bonus already make sense for the setup I am using.

My rough spending order is:

1. **a module that opens a real build or fills a required slot;**
2. **a Crew that directly strengthens that build;**
3. **a Captain whose effect solves a problem I am already having;**
4. **experimental artifacts and sidegrades once the starting loadout is functional.**

That order becomes more valuable as the shop grows. An expensive Captain or novelty purchase can delay several smaller pieces that would have made the next boss clear substantially easier.

Captain prices vary enough to make that trade meaningful, so I judge the purchase against what the starting loadout is still missing rather than buying strictly by tier. Current Captain prices, effects, drawbacks, and rankings are in the <Link href="/wanderburg/captains-tier-list">Wanderburg Captains Tier List</Link>.

<h2 id="overtime">
  Overtime pays best when the winning build still has room to survive
</h2>

A normal win gives me a clean choice: end the run or continue into **Overtime**. The decision is less about greed than about the condition of the castle at the moment the normal boss sequence ends.

One Meadows run lasted **nearly 20 minutes** and finished with **3,000+ Silver** in total. Overtime supplied **570 Silver** of that result, while Gold conversion, collected Silver, and quest rewards supplied the rest. Meadows also showed a **1.2x Silver factor**.

That is why I treat Overtime as an extension of a successful run rather than a separate money button. If the final boss dies while HP is healthy, Nitro is still usable, and the build clears dangerous targets without emptying every cooldown at once, the extra time is efficient because the expensive part — assembling the build — is already finished.

A barely surviving victory is different. Late Overtime enemies can erase a large health bar very quickly, and several runs showed how suddenly safe movement can turn into a wall of spinning hazards, missiles, or body-blocking enemies. If I already earned enough Silver for the purchase I entered the run to fund, leaving can be the faster progression decision.

<GuideCallout title="Gold and Silver advance different things">
Gold powers the current run. Silver remains after it and expands the permanent loadout. A huge castle can disappear at the end of a run; a new module, Crew, Captain, or unlocked starting option changes every run that follows.
</GuideCallout>

<h2 id="permanent-module-unlocks">
  Strong runs are the best time to finish permanent unlock conditions
</h2>

Some of the most valuable account upgrades do not come directly from the shop. They first require a module level or cumulative objective to be completed during play.

The unlocks I have confirmed include:

- **Archer Crew level 5 → Archer Tower**
- **Mine Layer level 10 → Turret Layer**
- **Side Cannons level 10 → Front Cannon**
- **destroy 100 Health Boxes overall → Repair Crew**
- **consume 300 Cows → Catapult**
- **Ram level 10 → Side Arms**

This is where a run can become more valuable than its final Silver total. If Archer Crew is one level away from Archer Tower and the current damage engine is already comfortably ahead of the boss timer, taking the Archer level creates a permanent option for every future run.

The same decision is much worse while the run is struggling. Forcing Mine Layer from level 7 toward level 10 while the current setup cannot survive the next boss may turn a useful attempt into a failed one before the unlock is reached. I wait until the run has enough margin to carry the milestone.

Unlocking also does not necessarily mean receiving the new item for free. Several unlock messages explicitly send the new option to the loadout screen, where it can then be purchased with Silver. The progression therefore has two steps: **meet the condition, then fund the permanent purchase**.

<h2 id="same-modules">
  A repetitive upgrade pool usually points back to permanent progression
</h2>

Several early runs kept feeding me the same small group of choices: Cannon, Ram, Top Mortar, Mine Layer, and the starter Crew. Moving onto another land did not suddenly replace them with a much larger arsenal.

The variety arrived as the account did more permanent work. Archer Tower, Turret Layer, Front Cannon, Barracks, Ballistas, Mages, Catapult, Side Arms, and other options begin to matter only after their unlock conditions and purchases have been handled.

So when a run starts feeling identical to the last one, I check the account before blaming the random upgrade system. The useful questions are not “Why did I get Mortar again?” but **“Which unlock condition is closest?”** and **“Which permanent purchase would change the next starting loadout?”**

That approach also keeps me from spreading levels too widely during a promising run. Mine Layer reaching level 10 has a permanent payoff. Mine Layer sitting at level 6 beside five other half-developed modules usually does not.

<h2 id="spiderburg">
  Golden Dunes is the first place where an unlock can create a second blocker
</h2>

Clearing the Golden Dunes boss objective opens **Spiderburg**, but seeing the vehicle unlock does not automatically mean the account is ready to start a Spiderburg run.

That is the point where I stop treating the biome as unfinished. Once the unlock has fired, repeating Golden Dunes will not solve a loadout requirement created by the new chassis.

Spiderburg also changes enough about the chassis that unlocking it and having a usable Spiderburg loadout are two different states.

The exact Golden Dunes requirement, post-unlock blocker, module-slot layout, and first-run handling are covered in the <Link href="/wanderburg/how-to-unlock-spiderburg">How to Unlock Spiderburg guide</Link>.

If the account is reaching the correct Land but the growing castle can no longer reposition safely, I treat that as a build problem rather than another progression gate. The broader Movement-versus-Defense tradeoff is covered in the <Link href="/wanderburg/best-builds">Wanderburg best builds guide</Link>.

<h2 id="later-progression">
  Later lands keep asking for complete runs, not isolated boss kills
</h2>

Green Plains is the first example of the four-boss progression structure, not the last. Meadows continues the same idea by using its complete boss clear to open **Waving Fields**, while later gates again reference full boss sets before the next section becomes available.

That makes the biome objective my source of truth for geography. Once the required Land is already open, another boss clear is only useful if the objective still asks for it; build difficulty and vehicle compatibility are separate problems rather than hidden map gates.

<h2 id="progression-stuck">
  If Wanderburg progression still feels stuck
</h2>

I first separate a genuine progression blocker from a combat or loadout problem. An unfinished biome objective and an unlocked item that still needs a permanent purchase belong to progression; a weak build, poor mobility, or an incompatible vehicle layout does not require another hidden map step.

The <Link href="/wanderburg">Wanderburg guide hub</Link> has the full stuck-player diagnosis for map gates, permanent unlocks, builds, movement, and vehicle compatibility. For a run that reaches the correct boss but cannot finish it, use the <Link href="/wanderburg/bosses">Boss Guide</Link> instead.

<h2 id="next-objective">
  I choose the next run by what will still be different afterward
</h2>

Before Green Plains is complete, the answer is straightforward: finish the boss gate.

After that, the best objective can change from run to run. Golden Dunes advances the Spiderburg route. A nearly completed Mine Layer or Archer Crew milestone can be more valuable than another random map clear. A vehicle that is already unlocked may turn Silver into the next real bottleneck. Meadows can continue the overworld toward Waving Fields while also offering its own permanent objectives.

This keeps the progression loop compact:

**clear a meaningful gate → expand the permanent pool → buy the missing starting piece → take the stronger account into the next land.**

The run itself is temporary. The reason I start it should not be.
