<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
For normal Wanderburg progression, **Racer Ruth and Tankbert are my S Tier Captains**. Tankbert is the safer choice when otherwise good runs keep dying before the full boss sequence is finished, while Racer Ruth is better when the castle has enough damage and health but keeps getting trapped. **Dieter the Drunk and Patchy are A Tier**, with Patchy becoming especially strong once Cannons are already the main damage plan.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

<GuideCallout title="Early Access">
Wanderburg is still in Early Access, so Captain balance can move between patches. These tiers are for normal solo progression and complete runs rather than one highly specialized damage setup. Castle choice and permanent unlocks can also move a specialist up or down.

<h2 id="tier-list">
  Wanderburg Captain Tier List
</h2>

The Captains with the biggest bonuses are not automatically the easiest ones to win with. Several of them attach that power to slower movement, awkward steering, a different healing loop, or a build that has to come together in a particular way.

For general progression, this is where I currently place them:

**S Tier — Racer Ruth, Tankbert**

**A Tier — Dieter the Drunk, Patchy**

**B Tier — Empress, The Count, Huntress, Lumberjack**

**C Tier — Pyromaniac, Kapitalistus Maximus, Time Witch**

**D Tier — Sire Jonah, Norbert the Normal**

**Unranked — Duelist**

Ruth and Tankbert are the two I trust most without knowing exactly how the rest of the run will develop. Ruth gives me a better exit when positioning goes wrong. Tankbert gives me enough health to survive more of those mistakes in the first place.

The lower tiers become much less rigid once the build is already decided. Patchy can be exceptional with Cannons, Empress gets much better with a developed Summon setup, and Huntress makes more sense when Auto Attacks are doing most of the work.

<h2 id="racer-ruth">
  Racer Ruth is my best Captain when movement decides the run
</h2>

Racer Ruth gives **50% more speed while boosting** in exchange for **15% less normal driving speed**.

The slower normal movement is noticeable on open ground, but that rarely decides my later runs. The dangerous moments come after the castle has grown, the route is filling with enemies and projectiles, and I need one boost to cross a firing lane or leave a bad approach before everything closes around me.

That is where Ruth earns S Tier.

Her effect fits Ram especially well because speed can become part of the damage, but I do not need a Ram build to want her. Mines need room behind the castle, ranged builds need distance, and cooldown-heavy setups need somewhere safe to wait for the next ability cycle. Extra boosted movement helps all of them.

The **15% normal-speed penalty** still matters once Nitro is empty, so I continue taking useful Max Nitro, regeneration, and Movement upgrades rather than assuming Ruth has already solved mobility.

When a run still has enough HP but dies because I cannot get the castle out of the wrong place, Ruth is usually the Captain I want. The movement-heavy setups that pair best with her are covered in the <Link href="/wanderburg/best-builds">Wanderburg best builds guide</Link>.

<h2 id="tankbert">
  Tankbert is my safest early Captain for full runs
</h2>

Tankbert increases maximum HP as I destroy vehicles. I like that form of scaling because it happens during normal play: I do not have to find a special resource or force the run into one weapon family before the Captain starts helping.

His Nitro economy is the tradeoff. That becomes more noticeable as the castle gets larger, because eventually a huge health pool does not help much if I cannot create enough space for the next attack.

The health scaling is still strong enough to make the exchange worthwhile for me.

One Tankbert run climbed past roughly **800 maximum HP**, and a longer run later pushed the health pool beyond **1,000**. Those are not his starting stats; they show what his effect can become when a run survives long enough to keep feeding it vehicle kills.

Percentage-based Health Boxes also become more valuable as that pool grows. This is one reason I prefer Tankbert to The Count when I simply want safer progression: Tankbert keeps the normal recovery system while expanding what those heals are restoring.

I still take good Movement, Max Nitro, and speed upgrades when they appear. Tankbert does not remove the need to drive well; he gives me more chances to survive the moments when I do not.

For an early Captain purchase, that forgiveness is hard to beat.

<h2 id="s-tier-choice">
  Racer Ruth or Tankbert?
</h2>

The choice becomes easier once I look at how the run is actually ending.

When **HP disappears first**, I take Tankbert. If boss three or four keeps killing an otherwise functional build, another layer of survivability gives me much more room to learn the fight and recover from mistakes.

When **space disappears first**, I prefer Racer Ruth. More maximum HP cannot fix a projectile field or crowded route that the castle cannot leave quickly enough.

Tankbert is usually easier while learning unfamiliar content. Ruth becomes more attractive once survival is already comfortable and bad positioning has replaced raw damage as the main reason I lose.

If neither Captain fixes the problem because the account is still missing important modules, I would spend Silver there first. The broader permanent purchase order is in the <Link href="/wanderburg/progression-unlocks">Wanderburg progression and unlocks guide</Link>.

<h2 id="a-tier">
  A Tier Captains have stronger conditions attached
</h2>

Dieter and Patchy can both produce runs that look better than A Tier. I keep them below Ruth and Tankbert because their drawbacks become much more sensitive to what I am driving and what the build is trying to do.

### Dieter the Drunk

Dieter gives **Super Lucky** at the cost of **wobbly controls**.

Luck has unusually broad value because it can improve a Cannon run, an Archer run, a Summon setup, or something that changes direction halfway through. I do not have to decide on one damage type before choosing him.

The steering is the part I cannot ignore.

Wide-area attacks, persistent effects, and more automated damage make the wobble easier to tolerate because I can spend more attention on keeping the castle safe. Front-facing attacks expose the problem much faster. If I need to hold one clean angle for a Ram or another directional hit, a small steering error can ruin the approach.

I still like Dieter in A because the upside remains useful across so many builds. I simply prefer predictable handling when I am learning a difficult Land for the first time.

### Patchy

Patchy is much narrower: I pick him because I want **Cannons**.

A developed Cannon build can already delete bosses surprisingly quickly. Strong Side Cannons, the right Crew, and supporting upgrades give Patchy exactly the kind of damage engine his Captain effect wants to amplify.

The danger is that Cannon exchanges become more important in both directions. I do not want to sit broadside to a dangerous enemy just because my own damage is high enough to tempt me into staying there.

That makes Patchy excellent once Cannons are already unlocked and I know the enemy firing patterns. In that situation he can play like an **S Tier specialist**. For a normal run where I still need the draft to tell me what the build is becoming, I keep him in **A Tier**.

The modules and upgrade priorities for that playstyle are in the <Link href="/wanderburg/best-builds">Wanderburg best builds guide</Link>.

<h2 id="b-tier">
  B Tier Captains get better when the build already fits them
</h2>

### Empress

Empress produces **100% more friendly units**, so she immediately becomes more interesting once the permanent account has a proper Summon setup.

Summons can deal enormous damage, especially after Front and Side Barracks or other friendly-unit modules start working together. The difficulty is that those units are not simply extra projectiles. Their positioning follows the way I move the castle, they do not always attack the target I most want removed, and a large army can make a crowded late run even busier.

Her speed drawback also becomes more expensive once damage is no longer the problem. A Summon build with enough units may already kill everything it touches; at that point I would often rather make the castle easier to reposition.

For a normal progression run I leave Empress in **B Tier**. With a developed Summon account, I would move her closer to **A Tier**.

### The Count

The Count replaces normal Health Box recovery with healing from consumed units.

The important change is that **Health Boxes stop spawning**. A large Health Box normally restores **25% HP**, while a small one restores **10%**, so losing them changes how I plan recovery after a bad fight.

The Count can feel excellent on routes where I am already consuming large numbers of units. Healing becomes part of the same action that grows the castle, and small amounts of damage can disappear while I continue moving.

The problem arrives when the castle needs recovery now and the map is not feeding me the right targets. Health Boxes are easier to remember and route back toward; consumed-unit healing depends on what is available in front of me.

That trade keeps The Count in **B Tier**, although population-heavy routes can make him considerably better.

### Huntress

Huntress is strongest when **Auto Attacks are already carrying most of the run**.

Faster automatic damage lets me concentrate on driving while the modules continue applying pressure. That can work extremely well with a setup that does not depend on pressing several active abilities together every time a boss becomes dangerous.

The opposite kind of build makes her harder to recommend. Lightning Mage, for example, gets a large part of its safety from the active ability: the stun controls enemies, the large area cleans up groups, and getting that button back matters. The same issue appears if Dash, Teleporter, or another active skill is an important escape tool rather than optional damage.

For an Auto Attack-focused setup, Huntress can outperform this placement. As a general progression Captain, I keep her in **B Tier**.

### Lumberjack

Lumberjack gains Nitro by consuming trees.

When trees are already along the route, that is a useful way to turn the map into extra movement. Another boost can cross a projectile lane, give ranged modules more room, or stop a growing group of enemies from surrounding the castle.

The weakness is that the resource is not always where I need it. Driving toward trees can become a detour, and a tree behind me does not solve the dangerous route ahead.

Racer Ruth carries her mobility advantage everywhere. Lumberjack has to be fed by the map, so I leave him in **B Tier** even though tree-heavy areas can make him feel much stronger.

<h2 id="c-tier">
  C Tier Captains work better as planned runs
</h2>

### Pyromaniac

Pyromaniac is always on fire, ignites enemies on contact, and has **no Luck**.

Fire itself is not the problem. A developed Fire build can be extremely strong. Fire Mage, Fire Crew, persistent burning areas, and other Magic or Fire effects can clear large groups without forcing the castle to stay in contact with every dangerous target.

That is where Pyromaniac becomes awkward for me. His personal effect encourages close contact at the same time the rest of a good Fire build is giving me safer ways to spread that damage from farther away.

The lack of Luck also makes the Captain less attractive outside a deliberately planned Fire run. I can have a very strong Fire setup without needing Pyromaniac to make it work.

For that reason he stays in **C Tier for general progression**, even though a dedicated Fire run can still be fun and effective.

### Kapitalistus Maximus

Kapitalistus Maximus trades some of the Gold available during the run for a larger payout afterward.

That timing matters more to me than the final total. Gold during a run becomes levels and upgrades before the next boss arrives. If I am already struggling to stabilize the opening, slowing that power curve makes the difficult part harder.

The Captain makes more sense once I can finish the normal route comfortably and permanent farming becomes the goal. At that point I can afford to trade some immediate power for a better reward after the run.

For progression I keep Kapitalistus in **C Tier**. For farming on an account that already clears consistently, he has a much clearer purpose.

### Time Witch

Time Witch makes the **entire game run at twice normal speed**.

Once I know the route, that can be useful. Familiar sections finish sooner, bosses arrive faster, and a strong build spends less real time waiting for the next event.

While learning, I want the opposite. Enemy attacks, turns, positioning mistakes, and ability decisions are all happening inside the same shortened reaction window.

So I treat Time Witch as two different Captains depending on the goal: **C Tier for normal progression**, but potentially much stronger when I am replaying content I already know and simply want to clear it faster.

<h2 id="d-tier">
  D Tier Captains do not improve the run enough
</h2>

### Is Sire Jonah worth buying?

**Not for progression.**

Sire Jonah plays techno music and only knows one song. That is the whole appeal: the castle gets its own moving soundtrack.

Once Silver is plentiful, he is a fun purchase. Earlier on, I would rather spend it on something that changes how the run actually behaves. Ruth helps movement, Tankbert adds survivability, Dieter improves upgrade quality, and Patchy can transform a Cannon setup. Jonah changes the music.

For combat value, he stays in **D Tier**.

### Norbert the Normal

Norbert is better during the first few runs than D Tier makes him sound.

There is no strange steering, alternate healing rule, Nitro drawback, or weapon condition to understand. That makes him a useful baseline while I learn how the castle turns, how bosses approach, and which modules I actually enjoy using.

Once those basics are familiar, being neutral becomes his weakness. Almost every other serious Captain purchase gives the run something it did not have before.

Norbert is a good learning Captain and a **D Tier long-term choice**.

<h2 id="duelist">
  Duelist
</h2>

Duelist stays **Unranked** for now. I have not had enough complete runs with him to decide whether his boost-related tradeoff remains worth it once the castle gets large and the later bosses start punishing aggressive movement.

<h2 id="which-captain-to-buy">
  Which Wanderburg Captain should I buy first?
</h2>

I choose the first Captain from the problem that keeps ending the run:

- **You keep reaching later bosses and dying on HP:** Tankbert.
- **You still have health but cannot escape crowded fights:** Racer Ruth.
- **The build already works and better upgrade quality would raise its ceiling:** Dieter the Drunk.
- **Cannons are already your main damage source:** Patchy.
- **Your permanent setup is built around friendly units:** Empress.
- **You already want to heal through consumption:** The Count.
- **Auto Attacks do most of the important damage:** Huntress.
- **Trees are plentiful and Nitro keeps becoming the bottleneck:** Lumberjack.
- **The route is solved and you want faster clears:** Time Witch.

I would not buy the roster from S Tier downward. A specialist can be the better purchase when the account already supports it, while even an S Tier Captain is poor value if an important starting module is still missing.
