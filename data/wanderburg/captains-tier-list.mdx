For normal Wanderburg progression, **Racer Ruth and Tankbert are my S Tier Captains**. Ruth is stronger when positioning and escape speed are ending otherwise healthy runs; Tankbert is the easier survival purchase when I still cannot finish the normal four-boss sequence reliably. Dieter the Drunk and Patchy sit in A because both can produce exceptional runs, but their drawbacks demand more from either the driver or the permanent build.

<h2 id="tier-list">
  Wanderburg Captain Tier List
</h2>

The roster has enough extreme tradeoffs that I do not treat this as a simple damage ranking. The strongest Captain is usually the one whose advantage attacks the reason my current runs are ending without creating a worse problem somewhere else.

My current general-use ranking is:

**S Tier — Racer Ruth, Tankbert**

**A Tier — Dieter the Drunk, Patchy**

**B Tier — Empress, The Count, Huntress, Lumberjack**

**C Tier — Pyromaniac, Kapitalistus Maximus, Time Witch**

**D Tier — Sire Jonah, Norbert the Normal**

**Unranked — Duelist**

Ruth and Tankbert reach S through opposite forms of survival. Ruth creates more distance at the moment I decide to boost. Tankbert turns routine vehicle destruction into a growing HP reserve, then asks me to compensate for his weaker Nitro economy as the castle gets larger.

Neither is universally better. The important question is whether the run is losing **health** first or losing **space** first.

<h2 id="racer-ruth">
  Racer Ruth is my best Captain when movement decides the run
</h2>

Racer Ruth gives **50% more speed while boosting** in exchange for **15% less normal driving speed**.

That trade gets better as Wanderburg becomes more crowded.

The normal-speed penalty is noticeable while crossing safe ground, but those stretches rarely decide a late run for me. The dangerous moments are when the castle has grown several times, enemies are closing both sides of a route, and I need one boost to cross a projectile lane or leave a crowded fight before it collapses around me.

Ruth creates more distance during exactly that window.

Her effect also connects naturally with Ram because that archetype already turns speed into offensive value. Outside a dedicated Ram setup, the advantage is broader: extra boosted distance gives almost any build more room before its weapons, summons, mines, or cooldowns need to solve the next threat.

The full Ram setup and other movement-dependent builds are covered in the <Link href="/wanderburg/best-builds">Wanderburg best builds guide</Link>.

The downside still matters after Nitro runs out. The **15% normal-speed penalty** makes an empty bar much more uncomfortable, so Max Nitro, regeneration, and general Movement upgrades remain valuable rather than becoming redundant.

Racer Ruth is my S Tier choice when the account can already take a hit and the main reason a run dies is **positioning**.

<h2 id="tankbert">
  Tankbert reached S Tier once his Nitro weakness proved repairable
</h2>

Tankbert costs **1,500 Silver**, making him unusually accessible for a Captain whose main advantage can keep scaling through the entire run.

Destroying vehicles increases his maximum HP. That is a particularly useful form of scaling because it happens during normal play; I do not need to search for a special resource, force one damage family, or wait for a rare interaction before the Captain begins getting stronger.

Percentage-based Health Box recovery becomes more valuable at the same time because Tankbert is increasing the HP pool those pickups refill. The exact healing percentages matter most when comparing him with The Count, whose sustain mechanic removes Health Boxes entirely.

Nitro was the reason I originally stopped Tankbert at A Tier. Once the castle became huge, slower regeneration could leave me with plenty of HP but no clean way to create another escape window.

The longer run changed that conclusion. Boss upgrades and normal run choices can add Movement, Max Nitro, and speed while Tankbert's health scaling keeps happening automatically in the background. His biggest strength continues compounding, while a meaningful part of his weakness can be repaired through upgrades I already value on large castles.

That does not make him immortal. Late Overtime pressure can still remove a four-digit health pool quickly if the castle is trapped in the wrong place.

What Tankbert gives me is more tolerance for one bad exchange and more opportunities to recover before that mistake becomes the end of the run. For an early Captain purchase, that is S Tier value.

<h2 id="s-tier-choice">
  Racer Ruth or Tankbert depends on what fails first
</h2>

I choose between the two S Tier Captains by looking at the first resource that disappears during a bad run.

When **HP disappears first**, Tankbert is the larger correction. He is the Captain I prefer when boss three or four keeps ending runs that otherwise have enough damage and movement to function.

When **space and Nitro disappear first**, Ruth becomes stronger. More health does not solve a projectile field or crowded route that the castle physically cannot leave.

There is a learning difference as well. Tankbert gives me more room to make mistakes while entering an unfamiliar Land. Ruth rewards cleaner boost timing and better route reading, then becomes increasingly attractive once raw survivability is no longer the main bottleneck.

The permanent purchase order around Captains, modules, and the rest of the account is covered in the <Link href="/wanderburg/progression-unlocks">Wanderburg progression and unlocks guide</Link>.

<h2 id="a-tier">
  A Tier Captains can become S Tier specialists
</h2>

Dieter and Patchy both have enormous upside. Their problem is that the downside is not theoretical; each changes how safely I can drive once enemy pressure becomes serious.

### Dieter the Drunk

Dieter costs **5,000 Silver** and gives **Super Lucky** at the cost of **wobbly controls**.

The benefit is unusually broad. Better upgrade quality can improve almost any run without forcing the account into Cannons, Fire, summons, or another specific damage family, and that advantage compounds as more upgrade choices appear.

The price is steering.

The wobble is much easier to tolerate when damage does not depend on holding one precise direction. I noticed the opposite immediately with positioning-sensitive frontal attacks: the weapon already wanted careful alignment, and Dieter made that alignment harder to maintain.

That interaction is enough to affect his rank. The module combinations that tolerate or punish this kind of steering are covered in the <Link href="/wanderburg/best-builds">Wanderburg best builds guide</Link>.

Dieter stays in A because Luck remains useful across many different runs, but I prefer learning a difficult new Land before voluntarily adding another source of steering uncertainty.

### Patchy

Patchy is narrower than Dieter and considerably more explosive.

His Captain effect gives **100% extra Cannon damage**, while **enemy Cannons also deal 100% extra damage**.

Once Cannons are already the run's main damage engine, Patchy can perform like an S Tier specialist. Boss health can fall extremely quickly.

The same multiplier makes unfamiliar enemy Cannon patterns much more dangerous. A broadside hit that was already costly can become the mistake that ends the run, so I want to understand the biome's firing threats before taking him into it.

I use Patchy to amplify a mature Cannon setup, not to rescue an unfinished one. The actual Cannon modules, firing geometry, and upgrade priorities are covered in the <Link href="/wanderburg/best-builds">Wanderburg best builds guide</Link>.

That leaves him in **A Tier overall and S Tier for a committed Cannon specialist**.

<h2 id="b-tier">
  B Tier Captains become good after the account supports their condition
</h2>

Empress, The Count, Huntress, and Lumberjack all solve real problems. What keeps them below A is dependency: each wants the build, route, or recovery system to behave in a particular way before the Captain consistently beats a safer generalist.

### Empress

Empress produces **100% more friendly units**, so her value rises sharply once the permanent account already supports a real Summon setup.

The ceiling is high, but two limitations appeared in long runs. Friendly units do not always attack the target I need removed immediately, and large summon counts can create a visible performance cost once enough units and effects fill the screen.

She also gives up speed. That penalty becomes harder to accept when the castle already has more than enough damage but needs better escape options.

For ordinary progression, Empress remains **B Tier**. With a mature Summon setup already available, I treat her as **A Tier**.

The Barracks and Turret combinations themselves are covered in the <Link href="/wanderburg/best-builds">best builds guide</Link>.

### The Count

The Count replaces normal Health Box recovery with healing from consumed units.

The critical downside is straightforward:

**Health Boxes stop spawning.**

A large Health Box normally restores **25% HP**, while a small one restores **10%**.

That makes losing them a meaningful change to how I recover after a bad fight. Unit consumption can provide frequent sustain when edible targets are already part of the route, but it does not guarantee that recovery will be available at the exact moment a boss leaves the castle critically low.

Tankbert keeps normal Health Boxes while increasing the maximum HP pool those percentage heals refill. That is the main reason I prefer Tankbert when I simply want reliable survival.

The Count stays **B Tier**, with a much stronger case on routes already built around consuming large populations.

### Huntress

Huntress gives **50% faster Auto Attacks** but makes **ability cooldown time 50% longer**.

That is an excellent trade when most of the build's real output already happens automatically. It is much worse when survival or boss damage depends on several active abilities returning together for the next dangerous window.

The bonus is strong enough to build around, but it asks me to choose the right damage model before the run begins rather than adapting comfortably to anything that drops.

That dependency keeps Huntress in **B Tier** for general progression.

### Lumberjack

Lumberjack gains Nitro by consuming trees.

The effect became more valuable as my castles got larger. Extra Nitro can create another escape window or enough distance for the rest of the build to keep functioning.

Its limitation is the map.

Trees have to exist along a useful route, and sometimes reaching them is already the movement problem I am trying to solve. Racer Ruth carries her mobility advantage everywhere; Lumberjack depends on having the resource nearby.

That keeps him in **B Tier**, although a tree-heavy route can raise his value considerably.

<h2 id="c-tier">
  C Tier Captains ask for too much consistency in normal progression
</h2>

These Captains have real niches. I simply prefer selecting them after I already know the route or have decided on a specific experiment rather than taking them into unfamiliar progression.

### Pyromaniac

Pyromaniac is always on fire, ignites enemies on contact, and has **no Luck**.

The Captain encourages close contact at the same time later enemies make close contact increasingly dangerous. A dedicated Fire setup can make the effect more meaningful, but applying damage by touching threatening vehicles demands much more from movement and positioning than ranged or persistent damage.

Giving up Luck removes another source of run quality.

For a planned Fire experiment, Pyromaniac can make sense. For a blind full clear, I leave him in **C Tier**.

### Kapitalistus Maximus

Kapitalistus Maximus changes when the economy pays out:

**+40% Gold after the run, -20% Gold during the run.**

The timing is the problem.

Gold during play creates levels and therefore more module upgrades before the next boss arrives. Losing part of that early and middle power curve can make a weak run even harder to stabilize.

A mature permanent account can tolerate the slower ramp much more comfortably and then benefit from the later economy. An account still struggling to finish normal progression usually needs immediate power instead.

That makes Kapitalistus **C Tier for progression**, with a legitimate niche once farming becomes more important than surviving the route.

### Time Witch

Time Witch makes the **entire game run at twice normal speed**.

For ordinary progression, I rank that in C.

For deliberately speedrunning content I already know, it can become S Tier.

The distinction matters because this is not Racer Ruth-style one-sided mobility. Enemy attacks, movement, reaction windows, target selection, and my own decisions all happen inside the faster game.

Once a route is solved and I want to compress the time required to clear it, that can become an advantage. While learning a new Land, I would rather keep the normal reaction window.

<h2 id="d-tier">
  D Tier gives up the Captain slot without enough combat value
</h2>

### Sire Jonah

Sire Jonah plays techno music and only knows one song.

The effect is immediately obvious, and as a novelty Captain it delivers exactly what the description promises.

For progression, the opportunity cost is difficult to ignore. Ruth changes mobility, Tankbert changes survival, Dieter changes upgrade quality, and Patchy changes Cannon damage. Jonah changes the soundtrack.

I buy him after the account is already functional and Silver no longer needs to fix an important progression problem.

For combat power, he remains **D Tier**.

### Norbert the Normal

Norbert is more useful during the opening runs than a D Tier label makes him sound.

He gives me a clean baseline. There is no wobbly steering to interpret, no missing Health Boxes, no Nitro penalty, no incoming Cannon multiplier, and no unusual resource loop changing the way the game feels.

That is valuable while learning how Wanderburg itself handles.

Once those basics are familiar, neutrality becomes the weakness. Almost every serious Captain purchase either amplifies something the build already does or corrects a recurring failure.

Norbert is a useful tutorial Captain and a **D Tier long-term Captain**.

<h2 id="duelist">
  Duelist stays unranked
</h2>

Duelist is the one Captain I am not forcing into a letter tier without enough clean run data.

Several other Captains looked simple from the selection card and changed significantly once their drawback met a real long run. Dieter's steering, Tankbert's Nitro economy, and Patchy's incoming Cannon multiplier all became easier to judge only after seeing where those penalties actually caused problems.

Until Duelist has the same quality of evidence, **Unranked** is more useful than filling the gap with a guessed B or C.

<h2 id="which-captain-to-buy">
  Which Wanderburg Captain should I buy first?
</h2>

I choose from the reason the current run is ending rather than purchasing the roster from S Tier downward:

- **HP disappears before the normal boss sequence is finished:** Tankbert.
- **The castle survives hits but gets trapped late:** Racer Ruth.
- **The build is already stable and better upgrade quality would raise its ceiling:** Dieter the Drunk.
- **Cannons already provide the main damage:** Patchy.
- **Friendly units are already the permanent plan:** Empress.
- **Consuming units is already part of the route:** The Count.
- **Auto Attacks are doing most of the real work:** Huntress.
- **Trees are plentiful and Nitro keeps becoming the bottleneck:** Lumberjack.
- **The route is solved and clear time matters more than safety:** Time Witch.

I do not buy Captains strictly from S Tier downward. A specialist with the correct permanent account can be more useful than a higher-ranked generalist, while even an S Tier Captain can be the wrong use of Silver if the starting loadout is still missing an essential module.

If the permanent account itself is the bottleneck, I fix that first. The broader Silver and permanent purchase order is covered in the <Link href="/wanderburg/progression-unlocks">Wanderburg progression and unlocks guide</Link>.
