<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
Treat a Wanderburg clear as a **four-boss endurance run**, not four isolated fights. Before each boss arrives, clear nearby threats, collect healing, let important cooldowns return, and keep enough Nitro to escape. The early fights can tolerate a rougher build, but by bosses three and four I want damage that keeps working while I reposition. In the full clears I could verify, **Dark Tower** appeared as the fourth boss; its Fire Tornado and later inward pull make saved Nitro especially valuable.

<h2 id="four-boss-run">
  Wanderburg bosses test the same run four times as the castle keeps changing
</h2>

The first boss can make a run look healthier than it really is.

At that point the castle is still relatively small, the screen has more open space, and even an uneven loadout can sometimes put enough damage into one target to survive. The important change happens after the fight: the vehicle grows, another upgrade is added, and the next boss arrives against a physically different castle.

By the third fight, I care much less about whether one button can produce an impressive burst. I want to know whether the run still deals useful damage while I move, whether something is available during the next dangerous window, and whether the enlarged chassis can still leave a bad position without spending its entire Nitro bar.

That gives the four-boss sequence a useful shape:

**the opening fight tests whether a damage engine exists → the middle fights expose downtime and positioning problems → the fourth fight asks whether the whole run still functions at its largest normal size**

The map reward is tied to completing the full boss requirement rather than one isolated kill. The exact Land gates and permanent unlocks belong in the <Link href="/wanderburg/progression-unlocks">Wanderburg progression and unlocks guide</Link>.

<h2 id="before-boss">
  I use the last part of the timer to prepare the fight, not squeeze out one more farm
</h2>

The boss timer makes it easy to keep collecting Gold until the final seconds, but that has repeatedly created worse fights for me.

Before the timer reaches zero, I start cleaning the route instead. Fast Rams, explosive vehicles, and other ordinary threats are much easier to remove before the boss begins controlling the same space. If healing is visible, I take it while the route is still predictable instead of assuming I can cross the screen safely after the boss appears.

Cooldowns matter for the same reason. A Mine Layer active, Mortar burst, or other important tool has very different value when it is ready as the boss enters. Even Ram can be worth preserving on a non-Ram build because the dash gives me a way to correct one bad position.

I also look for open ground before the fight starts. A large castle surrounded by terrain, normal enemies, and the boss is dealing with several problems at once; the same boss becomes much easier when I can still draw a wide route around it.

<GuideCallout title="I stop farming when the next boss is close">
A little more Gold is rarely worth starting the fight with half HP, empty Nitro, and every useful ability on cooldown. Near the timer, I value a clean spawn more than one last upgrade attempt.
</GuideCallout>

### My first-boss mistake was forcing contact before the run needed it

One of my roughest opening fights was not caused by weak damage. I drove a Ram directly into the boss, lost far more HP than the exchange justified, and then spent the rest of the fight trying to recover from a mistake I did not need to make.

The safer version of that fight is much less dramatic. I keep the castle moving, let passive or ranged damage work, and preserve the Ram unless the run already has enough speed and room to make the hit cleanly.

The first post-boss upgrade is useful because it gives immediate feedback. If the boss died quickly but the castle took too much unavoidable damage, Defense has a case. If the fight dragged on while I remained safe, the damage engine is the obvious problem.

### By the second fight, castle size can become the real warning

A later full-clear run exposed a different issue during boss two. The castle had plenty of Defense, but several moments became dangerous simply because I could not move through the space I wanted.

<h2 id="third-boss">
  By boss three, I want damage that keeps working after I leave the angle
</h2>

The third fight is where cooldown gaps and stationary damage become much easier to notice.

In one successful full clear, Mine Layer had already grown into a large field by this point. I could place the damage, continue moving, and let the boss travel through it instead of keeping the castle beside the target for the entire exchange.

This is also where a burst-heavy run starts showing its downtime. A powerful mine field is not useful during the seconds when no mines are available. A strong active attack can erase one window and still leave the castle with nothing meaningful to do during the next one.

By boss three, I want some form of pressure to survive those gaps: Auto Attacks, placed effects, summons, or active abilities returning quickly enough that the whole build never goes silent.

If the run reaches this point but the damage engine itself is still unclear, I stop trying to fix that inside the boss fight. The actual module combinations and upgrade priorities belong in the <Link href="/wanderburg/best-builds">Wanderburg best builds guide</Link>.

<h2 id="dark-tower">
  Dark Tower is the fourth boss in the full clears I could verify
</h2>

In the standard full clears I could verify, **Dark Tower** appeared as the fourth boss.

Its most obvious threat is the **Fire Tornado**.

The tornado is much less threatening when the castle still has enough speed to keep opening distance. The difficult version is a late-run chassis that has grown through three previous boss upgrades while Nitro or mobility has fallen behind.

I therefore avoid spending the entire Nitro bar immediately before Dark Tower appears. I want enough available for the moment when the tornado, surrounding enemies, and the boss begin closing the same route.

A later four-of-four Dark Tower fight added another problem: the encounter began **pulling the castle inward**. Normal driving speed was not creating enough separation, while boosting did.

That is a Dark Tower-specific reason to save Nitro rather than a general argument that every boss should be fought at maximum speed.

I also keep steering after the boss is effectively finished. The Fire Tornado can remain dangerous around the kill, so treating the HP bar reaching zero as permission to stop moving can still throw away health at the end of an otherwise successful clear.

When projectile-heavy enemies are also on the screen, I prefer a broad arc over one long straight retreat. The point is not to follow one universal boss route; it is to avoid presenting the same predictable line while the tornado and other threats keep removing space.

<h2 id="between-boss-upgrades">
  I choose the next boss upgrade from the way the previous fight almost failed
</h2>

The vehicle upgrade after a boss is one of the best opportunities to repair the run before the next difficulty step.

I do not decide all four upgrades in advance. I read the previous fight instead.

**The boss survived too long while I was otherwise safe:**  
the damage engine still needs help.

**The boss died, but unavoidable hits removed a huge part of the health bar:**  
Defense becomes more attractive.

**The castle had enough HP but could not rotate or leave the danger:**  
Movement is the correction I want.

**Every escape emptied Nitro and the next safe window took too long to arrive:**  
the mobility problem is still unresolved.

**The abilities hit hard but were unavailable during the moments that mattered:**  
I look for cooldown, Auto Attack, or another source of sustained pressure rather than another isolated burst number.

That framework is more useful than a fixed “always take damage first” rule because the castle reaching boss three is not the same vehicle that entered boss one.

<h2 id="boss-four-wall">
  If boss three or four keeps ending the run, I check four failure patterns
</h2>

Repeated late failures usually contain enough information to stop guessing.

### Normal enemies are already taking too long to kill

The run fell behind before the boss arrived. I stop spreading levels across unrelated damage families and fix the engine that is already producing the useful kills.

### The boss is manageable until every active ability is unavailable

The run has burst but no floor. Something needs to keep working during the cooldown gap.

### The castle still has HP but nowhere to go

The immediate problem is route, Nitro, or repositioning rather than another hidden boss mechanic.

### The fight begins with low HP, empty Nitro, or a crowded screen

The failure happened before the boss spawned. I use the timer to clear the route, collect recovery, and stop wasting the abilities I want available at the start.

When one of those patterns is obvious, repeating the same setup without changing the weak layer rarely gives me a different result.

<h2 id="after-final-boss">
  After boss four, the boss problem is finished
</h2>

Killing the fourth boss reaches the normal **Game Won** state.

At that point I stop diagnosing the run as a boss clear. The remaining choice is whether the finished castle is still strong enough to make Overtime worthwhile or whether the normal victory has already achieved the progression goal I entered for.

Overtime begins after that gate has already been cleared.**
