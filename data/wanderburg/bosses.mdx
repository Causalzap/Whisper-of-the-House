<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
Treat a Wanderburg clear as one **four-boss endurance run**. Before each fight, clear nearby threats, pick up healing, let important abilities come back, and keep enough Nitro to escape once the arena becomes crowded. Bosses one and two can forgive an uneven build; by bosses three and four, I want damage that keeps working while the castle moves. **Dark Tower closes the standard four-boss route**, and its Fire Tornado plus inward pull make saved Nitro far more useful than one last burst of farming.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

<h2 id="four-boss-run">
  The four bosses punish a different weakness each time the castle grows
</h2>

The first boss is generous enough to hide problems that become expensive later. The castle is still compact, there is more usable ground around it, and a build with awkward cooldowns or mediocre movement can sometimes brute-force one target before those weaknesses matter.

The fight changes the vehicle as much as it changes the run. After every boss, the castle becomes larger and another upgrade joins the build, so boss two is not simply a harder version of boss one fought with better numbers. Turning takes more room, the route through normal enemies gets narrower, and attacks that were easy to avoid early can begin trapping the chassis once its footprint expands.

By the third fight, I care less about the largest burst number on the screen and more about what happens **after that burst is gone**. If the castle can still damage the boss while repositioning, recover an important cooldown before the next dangerous window, and cross open ground without emptying the entire Nitro bar, the run usually has enough structure to reach the end.

That gives the sequence a useful progression:

**boss one checks whether the build has a working damage engine → bosses two and three expose movement and downtime → boss four tests whether that engine still works after the castle has reached its largest normal-run size.**

The Land reward belongs to the complete boss sequence, not to one isolated kill. The exact map gates and permanent rewards are covered in the <Link href="/wanderburg/progression-unlocks">Wanderburg progression and unlocks guide</Link>.

<h2 id="before-boss">
  The last seconds before a boss are preparation time
</h2>

I used to keep farming until the timer disappeared because one more group of enemies might mean another upgrade. That was often a bad trade. A little extra Gold did not compensate for starting the boss with half health, an empty Nitro bar, or the ability I needed still sitting on cooldown.

As the timer gets low, I start cleaning the route instead. Rams, explosive vehicles, and anything fast enough to interfere with turning are easier to remove before the boss occupies the same space. If healing is nearby, I take it while the path is still predictable rather than planning to cross the arena for it after the fight has already started.

Boss spawning is more consistent now, so I do not build the plan around manipulating a strange spawn or hoping terrain will interfere with the encounter. I would rather control the things the run actually gives me control over: open ground, current HP, Nitro, nearby enemies, and which abilities are ready when the boss arrives.

A Mine Layer field or Mortar burst is worth much more when available immediately. Even Ram can be worth saving on a build that is not centered on Ram damage because one dash can pull a large castle out of a position that ordinary steering cannot fix quickly enough.

<GuideCallout title="I stop farming before the timer reaches zero">
A clean boss start is worth more than squeezing one last upgrade out of the map. I would rather enter with open ground, usable Nitro and the important cooldowns ready than arrive slightly richer and already be recovering from a bad position.
</GuideCallout>

  Bosses one and two reveal whether I am wasting health or running out of room
</h2>

### The first boss does not require a risky contact trade

One of my worst early mistakes was turning Ram into an excuse to drive directly through the boss before the build had the speed or durability to make that exchange worthwhile. The hit landed, but so did everything coming back at the castle.

The safer fight is usually less dramatic. I keep driving, let passive damage or ranged attacks work, and use Ram only when the approach is clean or when the dash itself solves a positioning problem.

What happens to the health bar tells me more than the kill time alone. If the boss disappears quickly but the castle loses an unreasonable amount of HP in the process, another damage upgrade is not automatically the answer. If I remain safe but spend far too long finishing the fight, the offensive engine is the part falling behind.

### Boss two is where castle size starts becoming visible

The second fight can expose a completely different problem. I have reached it with plenty of Defense and still had dangerous moments because the enlarged vehicle could no longer travel through the route I wanted.

A castle that still turns and escapes comfortably has no reason to solve a mobility problem it does not have. A castle surviving only because the health pool is large enough to absorb repeated hits is already warning me that the next growth step may make the route much worse.

<h2 id="third-boss">
  Boss three exposes builds that only work while every ability is ready
</h2>

The third fight is where cooldown gaps become difficult to hide.

A successful Mine Layer run made the difference obvious. By boss three, the mine field had enough coverage that I could place it, leave the area, and continue steering while the boss moved through damage I had already created.

The opposite kind of build can look excellent for several seconds and then suddenly have nothing to contribute. A large Mine field may destroy one phase of the fight, but it cannot help during the time Mine Layer is unavailable. The same is true of a huge active shot that removes a chunk of HP and then leaves the castle waiting for its next meaningful action.

By this point I want a floor under the build: Auto Attacks, summons, placed effects, short enough cooldowns, or several abilities whose gaps do not all overlap.

This does not mean every slot needs constant DPS. Control is valuable too. A Force Mage that removes incoming projectiles can buy the space another weapon needs to come back, and summons can keep applying pressure while the chassis concentrates on movement. What matters is that the entire castle does not become harmless every time the main button is unavailable.

If boss three is the first place where the run consistently collapses, the <Link href="/wanderburg/best-builds">Wanderburg best builds guide</Link> is the better place to solve that damage engine.

<h2 id="dark-tower">
  Dark Tower makes the fourth fight a Nitro and movement check
</h2>

**Dark Tower** closes the standard four-boss run.

On Difficulty 1, its health is currently **10,000**, so a fourth fight that still takes an extremely long time is a useful warning that the build has slipped behind the damage curve during the previous boss upgrades rather than simply running into an oversized HP wall.

Its more dangerous problem is the **Fire Tornado**.

The tornado itself is manageable while the castle can still create distance. The harder version of the encounter is arriving after three previous growth steps with a large chassis, limited room, and a Nitro bar that was spent just before the fight.

I save Nitro for the point where several threats overlap rather than burning it simply because Dark Tower has appeared. The useful boost is the one that crosses the dangerous section when the tornado, nearby enemies, and the boss are all taking away the same escape route.

The later part of the encounter can also pull the castle inward. Normal driving may not create separation quickly enough, while boosting can. That is why Dark Tower gives Nitro a specific job: not permanent maximum-speed driving, but a reserve for the moment ordinary steering stops winning the distance battle.

I keep moving after the boss HP reaches zero as well. The Fire Tornado can remain relevant around the end of the fight, and giving away a large chunk of health after the kill is especially wasteful if I am considering Overtime.

When projectile-heavy enemies are present at the same time, I prefer curving away through open space rather than committing to one long straight retreat. A changing angle leaves more options when the tornado closes one route and another threat begins occupying the next.

<h2 id="between-boss-upgrades">
  I use the previous fight to choose the next boss upgrade
</h2>

I do not decide the four boss upgrades before the run begins. Each fight has already shown me something about the castle, so the next choice should answer what nearly went wrong rather than follow a fixed priority list.

If the boss stayed alive too long while the castle was otherwise comfortable, I strengthen the damage engine that is already doing the work.

If the boss died quickly but ordinary hits removed too much of the health bar, Defense has a stronger case.

If I had enough health but repeatedly failed to rotate, cross a gap, or leave an attack before it landed, I treat that as a Movement problem.

If every useful escape emptied Nitro and left the castle helpless during the next danger window, more mobility capacity matters more than adding another small amount of damage.

And if the abilities were excellent whenever they were ready but the run repeatedly went silent between them, I look for cooldown, useful Auto Attacks, summons, placed damage, or another source of pressure that fills the gap.

The important part is that the boss upgrade repairs a weakness that already appeared. By fight three, choosing from a generic “damage first, Defense second” rule ignores too much about what the castle has become.

<h2 id="boss-four-wall">
  If boss three or four keeps ending the run, the failure usually appears earlier
</h2>

Late-run deaths often look like a boss problem even when the deciding weakness was visible several minutes before the boss spawned.

### Normal enemies already take too long to kill

The damage curve has fallen behind. I stop distributing upgrades across several unrelated families and put the next useful levels into the engine that is already producing kills.

### The fight works until every active ability is unavailable

The build has burst but no reliable floor. Another giant damage number may improve the good window without fixing the seconds that are actually killing the run.

### The health bar is still healthy but the castle cannot leave

I treat this as route, turning, Nitro, or Movement before adding more Defense. Surviving longer inside the same bad position may only delay the loss.

### The boss arrives into low HP, empty Nitro, and a crowded screen

That fight was compromised before the boss appeared. I clear the route earlier, collect recovery while it is safe to reach, and stop spending the tools I expect to need during the opening exchange.

Once one of these patterns repeats, I change that layer before running the same setup again. Boss three and four are difficult enough without asking the castle to carry a weakness that was already visible during normal enemies.

<h2 id="after-final-boss">
  The fourth kill ends the boss gate, but do not leave the rewards behind
</h2>

Killing Dark Tower reaches the normal **Game Won** state.

Before moving on, I collect the boss tombstone rather than driving away immediately. Boss tombstones drop **Silver**, so the kill now contributes directly to permanent progression as well as completing the combat gate.

After that, the boss problem is over. The remaining choice is whether the finished castle has enough health, movement, and sustained damage to make Overtime worthwhile, or whether the normal victory has already achieved what I entered the Land to accomplish.

I do not let a good four-boss clear become a bad progression decision just because Overtime is available. A castle that barely reached the finish line may earn more by banking the run and starting again; one that finishes with a stable damage engine can keep converting an already-built setup into more rewards.

The Overtime decision, Silver economy, and permanent unlock route are covered in the <Link href="/wanderburg/progression-unlocks">Wanderburg progression and unlocks guide</Link>.

For the boss route itself, the boundary is simple:

**four bosses complete the combat gate. Overtime begins after that gate has already been won.**
