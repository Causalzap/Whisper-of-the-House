<h2 id="best-build">
  Archer Tower, Side Ballista and Turret Layer is my strongest complete core
</h2>

The best Wanderburg build I have played did not depend on one enormous active ability. Its advantage was that damage kept happening from several places while the castle was already moving toward the next safe position.

**Archer Tower** handles range well. Its attacks arrive quickly enough to punish distant threats without waiting for a long Mortar arc, and it gives me a useful way to reach enemies before they crowd the vehicle.

**Side Ballista** supplies the steady pressure. Once its Auto Attack has enough damage and speed behind it, enemies continue losing HP between active abilities instead of every fight alternating between burst and silence.

**Turret Layer** completes the shape of the build. I can leave Ballistas beside a boss, fortress, or dense fight and move away while the deployed damage stays behind.

That overlap is what makes the setup feel complete. One cooldown ending does not switch the whole castle off, and repositioning does not mean giving up all offensive pressure.

A late boss in one of these runs disappeared before the fight had much time to develop. The important part was not a single spectacular number; several already-upgraded systems were attacking at once.

<h2 id="arrow-upgrades">
  Arrow builds reward concentration more than variety
</h2>

Once arrows are clearly carrying, I stop treating every Rare upgrade as automatically valuable.

Side Ballista normally earns early investment because its passive shots are contributing throughout the fight. Damage and Auto Attack improvements raise output that is already active without requiring another button press or a new firing pattern.

Turret Layer wants a slightly different package. **Cooldown** determines how often another firing position can be created, **duration** determines how long that position remains useful, and damage or attack speed determines whether the deployed Ballistas are actually worth building around.

Archer Tower becomes more attractive as range starts solving targets that would otherwise force the castle into a worse position.

Artifacts are easier to evaluate after that core exists. Arrow damage, effects triggered by repeated arrow hits, Chain Lightning, and cooldown acceleration from arrow kills all reinforce something the run is already doing.

A strong upgrade from another family still has to justify stealing levels from that engine. A Fire artifact without a Fire build or a Cannon bonus without Cannons is potential rather than current power.

<GuideCallout title="One damage engine should be obvious by the middle of the run">
By the time the castle has passed the early bosses, I want to know what is actually removing dangerous enemies. Arrows, mines, Cannons, summons and Ram can all answer that question. Four half-developed answers usually leave the run weaker than one system receiving most of the useful upgrades.
</GuideCallout>

<h2 id="front-slot">
  The fourth slot should reinforce the build instead of restarting it
</h2>

The three-piece Arrow core leaves the Front position flexible on a compatible chassis.

**Front Barracks** has worked well because it adds pressure without demanding that I face the target perfectly. Friendly units continue fighting while Archer Tower and Turret Layer cycle, so the slot contributes without competing heavily for the same positioning.

If the first three modules are already comfortably ahead of the difficulty curve, the fourth slot does not have to become another primary weapon. Control, summons, or survival can be more valuable than introducing a new damage family that now needs its own upgrades.

<h2 id="vehicle-layout">
  Check the chassis before copying a successful build
</h2>

A build that works on one Wanderburg vehicle may not even fit another one.

That matters most once later chassis start changing module availability. A setup built around a Side weapon can lose one of its defining pieces simply by changing vehicles, so I treat chassis changes as a new loadout decision rather than an automatic upgrade.

Spiderburg is the first major example. Its exact slot structure, post-unlock requirement, and first usable setup are covered in the <Link href="/wanderburg/how-to-unlock-spiderburg">Spiderburg unlock guide</Link>.

<h2 id="early-game-build">
  Mine Layer and Top Mortar are the early combination I trust most
</h2>

Before the permanent account owns enough pieces for a mature Arrow setup, **Mine Layer + Top Mortar** solves many of the same combat problems with fewer prerequisites.

Several independent runs kept arriving at this pairing.

Mine Layer turns pursuit into damage. Vehicles, swarms, and bosses often follow the route the castle has just driven, so mines punish enemies without requiring me to turn a growing chassis back toward them.

Top Mortar handles the other half of the space. It reaches ahead or farther away, and additional charges let it keep contributing while movement is temporarily more important than lining up another weapon.

The interaction gets better when mines begin slowing targets. Delayed Mortar shots are easier to land on something that is moving less aggressively, so the modules start supporting each other's attack pattern instead of merely occupying different slots.

For Mine Layer, **cooldown** is one of my earliest priorities. Once existing mines already kill ordinary pursuers, having the next field ready sooner can matter more than making the previous field deal still more damage.

Extra mines, effect size and damage become easier choices after that cadence feels comfortable.

Top Mortar benefits immediately from projectile damage and additional charges. Its job is already clear; I mostly increase how often and how hard it can perform that job.

<h2 id="movement">
  Movement becomes part of build scaling once the castle gets large
</h2>

This is the biggest change in how I evaluate Wanderburg builds after longer runs:

**survivability is not only the amount of damage the castle can absorb; it is also how reliably the castle can leave a position where damage is about to happen.**

Every boss upgrade makes the vehicle physically harder to manage. Its footprint expands, gaps become narrower, turning requires more space, and attacks that were trivial to circle around at the start of a run become much more awkward once the castle occupies a large part of the screen.

I had heavily defensive runs with enormous HP pools that still became fragile because rotation and escape had fallen too far behind.

Movement fixes several systems at once.

More **Max Speed** increases the distance between the castle and anything chasing it. More **Nitro** extends the time available to cross a dangerous section instead of absorbing it. Better mobility gives mines and turrets more space to work after deployment and gives long-range weapons time to cycle before enemies close again.

The value also changes by archetype:

- **Mine/Mortar** uses Movement to stretch the safe window between ability cycles.
- **Ram** converts speed directly into offensive value.
- **Cannon** uses mobility to establish and then leave favorable firing angles.
- **Summoner** benefits because the castle can concentrate on survival while friendly units continue attacking.
- **Arrow setups** can preserve range instead of allowing enemies to collapse onto the chassis.

That is why a Movement upgrade can improve an entire build even when the card itself contains no weapon damage.

The broader boss-by-boss situations where this becomes most visible are covered in the <Link href="/wanderburg/bosses">Wanderburg Boss Guide</Link>.

<h2 id="mine-mortar-stall">
  Mine and Mortar builds stall when the next danger arrives before the cooldown
</h2>

The early setup has a very specific failure state.

A mine field can be powerful enough to delete anything crossing it and still fail the run if the next threat arrives while Mine Layer is unavailable. The same problem appears when a Mortar barrage clears one group and leaves the castle with nothing meaningful to use during the next several seconds.

At that point, pure damage starts losing value.

If existing mines already kill normal pursuers, making those mines even stronger does not help during a cooldown gap. A shorter cooldown creates another usable answer. More mobility can solve the same timing problem by giving the castle enough room to wait for the answer to return.

That is when **cooldown, Nitro and route control** begin outperforming another modest damage increase.

<h2 id="ram-build">
  Ram only becomes a real damage build after speed arrives
</h2>

Ram produced some of the widest swings of any module I tested.

On a slow defensive castle, the impact could feel almost irrelevant.

Once the run had **Movement, Max Nitro and meaningful Ram investment**, ordinary targets started disappearing on contact and the ability became useful for both offense and escape.

My priority becomes:

**Movement → Max Nitro → Ram damage → Ram cooldown or duration**

That order looks unusual compared with a conventional weapon build, but Ram needs a chassis capable of delivering the hit before improving the hit itself becomes worthwhile.

Artifacts that convert speed into additional melee value fit naturally.

Racer Ruth also has an obvious synergy because her Captain effect strengthens the boosted movement Ram already wants. Her exact bonus, drawback, and ranking remain in the <Link href="/wanderburg/captains-tier-list">Wanderburg Captains Tier List</Link>.

Approach angle matters as much as the stat sheet. Heavy enemies that punish frontal contact are much safer to strike from the side or rear after circling them. A fast Ram build still wastes its mobility advantage if every attack becomes a head-on damage trade.

If the run never receives enough speed, I stop forcing the archetype. Ram remains useful as an emergency dash instead of consuming half the upgrade economy while pretending to be the primary weapon.

<h2 id="summoner-build">
  Summoner trades direct control for constant independent pressure
</h2>

A Summoner setup changes what I spend attention on.

With **Side Barracks + Front Barracks + Turret Layer**, a large portion of the damage leaves the castle itself. Fighters chase enemies, deployed Ballistas hold another position, and I can spend more attention steering than manually maintaining every attack angle.

The best upgrades are the ones that keep that pressure present: more useful unit count, stronger spawned units, shorter Barracks cooldowns, and stronger or longer-lasting turrets.

Turret Layer is most valuable beside something that will remain relevant for a while. A boss route, fortress, or dense combat area gives deployed Ballistas time to earn their slot; placing them into empty ground simply because the ability is ready throws away much of their duration.

The tradeoff is control.

Friendly units do not always attack the target I care about most. In long runs I had groups following the castle while dangerous enemies remained alive, and pathing around large targets could turn extra unit count into less real damage than expected.

Performance also became a practical limit. Large summon counts combined with many effects caused a noticeable frame-rate drop during one extended run. Beyond that point, adding more bodies was no longer automatically beneficial.

Captains can amplify the archetype further, but Captain-specific rankings and tradeoffs belong in the <Link href="/wanderburg/captains-tier-list">Captains Tier List</Link>.

<h2 id="cannon-build">
  Cannon builds become reliable when the castle stops fighting from the wrong angle
</h2>

Side Cannons changed the way I drove.

The target usually belongs **beside** the castle rather than directly in front of it.

A broadside setup wants a roughly parallel line where the Side Cannons can keep working without forcing the vehicle into a frontal collision. Once I started preserving that geometry, Cannon runs became much more consistent.

My normal priorities are:

- Cannon damage;
- Side Module cooldown;
- useful projectile effects;
- Canoneer Crew;
- Front Cannon as additional coverage rather than the whole build.

The danger is holding a good broadside for too long. Enemy Cannon fire can remove a large section of HP very quickly, so the correct firing angle eventually has to be abandoned when staying there becomes more expensive than another volley is worth.

<h2 id="build-pivot">
  By the middle of the run, upgrades should be multiplying something that already works
</h2>

The opening minutes can tolerate experimentation because the run is still discovering what it has.

That freedom becomes expensive later.

I may start with Mine Layer and then receive unusually strong Arrow options. An early Side Cannon can also receive enough support that it clearly overtakes the original plan. Either can justify a pivot.

The mistake is continuing to fund both plans after one has already become better.

When Ballistas are removing the dangerous targets, another Arrow upgrade has a known return. When mines are clearing pursuit, cooldown and mine scaling strengthen an established engine. When a Cannon broadside is carrying, an unrelated damage family now has to justify taking levels away from it.

A coherent Wanderburg build is not four individually good modules.

It is a group of upgrades whose value increases because the other pieces are already succeeding.

<h2 id="builds-not-to-force">
  Magic and Fire have promising pieces, but I would not force either on a progression run yet
</h2>

Pure Magic has not produced a run strong enough for me to rank it beside the proven setups above.

Force Mage has useful control, and Magic effects can create valuable space, but the more directional offense demanded precise positioning while still falling behind the harder targets in the runs I tested.

Fire has more obvious internal connections. Fire Crew, Fire Mage, burning effects, and Fire artifacts already point toward a recognizable archetype.

The problem is consistency. The versions I played did not yet reach the reliability of Arrow, Mine/Mortar, Ram, Summon, or Cannon.

That makes both families reasonable experiments when the permanent account already owns the pieces, rather than the setups I would choose while trying to break through an unfamiliar progression wall.

Economy-heavy choices have a different timing problem. Extra Gold can become valuable over a long run, but an investment that weakens the current damage curve is costly when the next boss arrives before the return has paid for itself. I add economy once the combat engine can already survive long enough to realize the benefit.

<h2 id="which-build">
  Which Wanderburg build should I use?
</h2>

For a developed starter-vehicle account, **Archer Tower + Side Ballista + Turret Layer** gives me the most complete combination of range, passive damage, and pressure that continues after repositioning.

Before those permanent pieces are available, **Mine Layer + Top Mortar** is the easier foundation. It asks less from the account and repeatedly gave me enough damage and control to keep progressing.

**Ram** becomes worth committing to when speed and Nitro are already improving.

**Summoner** works once Barracks and Turret pieces can maintain useful pressure without pathing or performance becoming the limiting factor.

**Cannons** are strongest when Side Cannons can remain on target while the castle preserves a safe broadside line.

A later vehicle can change which of those setups is legal, so I check chassis compatibility before spending Silver around a build that worked on the previous vehicle. Permanent unlock conditions and purchase order are covered in the <Link href="/wanderburg/progression-unlocks">Wanderburg progression guide</Link>.

The strongest runs have not been the ones with the most impressive collection of unrelated modules.

They have been the ones where each new upgrade makes an already successful damage engine harder to interrupt.
