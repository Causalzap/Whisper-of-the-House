<h2 id="what-to-do-first">
  What to do first in Wanderburg
</h2>

Wanderburg makes the first few runs look more permanent than they really are. The castle grows, Gold arrives quickly, weapons gain levels, and the screen can be full of effects before the account itself has changed very much.

My first target is therefore simple: **finish the full Green Plains boss route**.

That clear matters more than building the largest possible early castle because it starts opening the overworld beyond the introductory route. Once several destinations and permanent objectives are available, Wanderburg stops behaving like a single straight campaign.

From there, the best run is not always the one with the highest Gold total or the biggest castle. A run can be valuable because it opens another Land, completes a permanent unlock condition, funds something the starting loadout actually needs, or proves that the current build is ready for a harder objective.

That is the shift that makes the rest of Wanderburg easier to read.

<h2 id="what-is-blocking-you">
  What is actually blocking your next run?
</h2>

Several different problems can look like “progression is stuck,” but they do not have the same solution.

**The next Land is still locked.**  
Start with the objective attached to the current route. A complete boss requirement matters more than an isolated boss kill, and later map gates are easier to understand from the objective text than from the physical position of a Land. The <Link href="/wanderburg/progression-unlocks">Wanderburg Progression & Unlocks Guide</Link> covers the route, permanent milestones, Silver, and what should open next.

**The map advanced but the starting choices still look almost identical.**  
The overworld and permanent Module pool do not grow automatically together. If the same small group of Modules keeps returning, check permanent unlock conditions and purchases before assuming another biome clear will refresh the entire pool.

**Normal enemies or later bosses are scaling faster than the castle.**  
That is usually no longer a map problem. The <Link href="/wanderburg/best-builds">Wanderburg Best Builds Guide</Link> covers the damage engines, upgrade priorities, Movement tradeoffs, and when a run should pivot rather than keep spreading levels across unrelated systems.

**The build kills well but the castle cannot leave danger.**  
Movement and Nitro have become part of survivability. A large castle can have excellent damage and a healthy HP bar while still losing because it no longer has enough room or speed to escape the next attack.

**A new vehicle is unlocked but cannot start, or the old build suddenly stops fitting.**  
Check the chassis before replaying the unlock route. Vehicle progression can change the available Module positions, so a permanent setup that works on one vehicle may be illegal or simply weaker on another.

Knowing which of those states I am in usually tells me what the next run is actually for.

<h2 id="how-wanderburg-progression-works">
  Wanderburg has several kinds of progress happening at once
</h2>

Four systems can move independently.

**The overworld** determines which Lands and major routes are available.

**The permanent loadout** determines what the account can bring into a run before random upgrades begin.

**The current build** determines whether this particular castle can survive the objective in front of it.

**The vehicle chassis** determines which of those permanent pieces can actually fit together.

That separation explains many moments that otherwise feel inconsistent. Opening a new Land does not automatically add new weapons to the starting pool. Unlocking more permanent pieces does not rescue a run whose upgrades have been spread across several unfinished damage engines. A newly unlocked vehicle can be permanently available while still demanding a different loadout from the one that worked before.

After a run, the question I care about most is therefore:

**what is permanently different now?**

Sometimes the answer is obvious. A Land opened, a vehicle appeared, or a new permanent option became available. Other runs are useful because they reveal that the account has reached the right destination but not yet reached the power, mobility, or compatibility needed to finish it.

<h2 id="bosses">
  If boss three or four keeps ending the run
</h2>

The four-boss sequence is one of the clearest tests of whether a build still functions after the castle has grown.

An early boss can hide weak cooldown coverage or poor movement because the vehicle is still small and there is plenty of space. By the later fights, those weaknesses become harder to ignore. If the same failure keeps repeating, the <Link href="/wanderburg/bosses">Boss Guide</Link> helps identify whether the fight itself is the problem, while the <Link href="/wanderburg/best-builds">build guide</Link> covers the damage or mobility changes that should happen between attempts.

I do not treat another identical run as the default answer once the failure pattern is already obvious.

<h2 id="captains">
  A Captain is easier to value after the problem is known
</h2>

Captain bonuses cover very different jobs. Some make survival easier, some change movement, some reward one damage family, and others are more useful for farming or faster progression. Several of the strongest bonuses also come with drawbacks that matter only after the build and vehicle are already understood.

That is why I buy for a known need rather than treating the Captain screen as a second progression ladder. Current rankings, specialist cases, and first-buy choices are in the <Link href="/wanderburg/captains-tier-list">Wanderburg Captains Tier List</Link>.

<h2 id="vehicles">
  A newer vehicle is not automatically a better vehicle
</h2>

Vehicle unlocks change the platform as well as the available power.

A different chassis can steer better, interact with terrain differently, or give more value to one Module family, but it can also remove a slot that the previous build depended on. I treat the first run with a new vehicle as a compatibility test rather than assuming later automatically means stronger.

Spiderburg is the first major example. It gains a second Top position while losing the Side slot, which can be excellent for one permanent account and awkward for another. Its Golden Dunes requirement, two-Top-Module blocker, and first-run considerations are in the <Link href="/wanderburg/how-to-unlock-spiderburg">Spiderburg guide</Link>.

Later vehicle decisions follow the same principle: I care about what the chassis does to the loadout I actually own, not only where that vehicle appeared in progression.

<h2 id="module-unlocks">
  Why do the same Modules keep appearing?
</h2>

Opening more of the overworld does not automatically expand the permanent Module pool.

If several runs keep offering the same small collection, I check which permanent requirements are unfinished and which unlocked pieces still need to be purchased. Changing Lands without changing that permanent layer often produces a new background with almost the same starting problem.

Collection growth also matters beyond simple variety now because permanent Module and Artifact unlocks feed the reroll system. The exact milestones, purchase order, and current reroll rules are kept in the <Link href="/wanderburg/progression-unlocks">progression and unlocks guide</Link>.

<h2 id="achievements">
  Leave most achievement cleanup until progression is already working
</h2>

A large part of the achievement list advances while I am doing useful things anyway. Distance accumulates while travelling, vehicle kills and consumed units rise during normal combat, bosses fall while opening Lands, and progression achievements arrive from routes I already need to complete.

That makes dedicated farming much more attractive near the end than near the beginning.

Once the account has finished most of its normal progression, the <Link href="/wanderburg/achievements">Wanderburg Achievements Guide</Link> has all 26 current achievements, the reduced cumulative requirements, and the order I use for the remaining cleanup.

<h2 id="after-first-win">
  What should I do after the first win?
</h2>

The first **Game Won** screen finishes a successful run, but it does not mean the permanent account has run out of things to do.

After that point, I want a reason to start the next run rather than clearing the same route simply because I already know it works. That reason might be another Land objective, a vehicle route, a permanent Module or Artifact milestone, a stronger starting loadout, a higher difficulty, or achievement progress that will happen naturally alongside something else.

This is also where Wanderburg begins to feel different from its opening hours.

Early runs often start with a loose collection of pieces and wait for random upgrades to reveal what the build is becoming. A developed account can enter with a real plan already present: a known damage engine, a Captain chosen for that plan, Modules that fit the vehicle, and enough permanent flexibility to recover from a poor set of upgrade offers.

Randomness still matters, but it changes the plan instead of having to invent the plan from nothing.

<h2 id="later-lands">
  Later Lands are objectives, not one straight campaign
</h2>

Once several routes are available, I no longer expect one universally correct next destination.

One Land may continue the overworld. Another can matter because of a vehicle unlock. An older area may still contain a permanent objective that is close enough to finish efficiently. A higher difficulty may be useful only after the current starting setup is strong enough to survive the larger jump.

That makes the objective text more useful than the shape of the map.

I do not assume that a node farther across the overworld is automatically the next mandatory step, and I do not replay an older Land simply because it feels safer. The route has a purpose when I can name what completing it is supposed to change.

<h2 id="next-run">
  Decide what the next run is supposed to accomplish
</h2>

Before starting again, I try to reduce the goal to one sentence.

Maybe I need the complete boss clear that opens the next Land. Maybe one permanent unlock condition is close enough to justify targeting during a strong run. Maybe the Silver balance can finally buy the piece that makes a vehicle legal. Maybe the route is already correct and the only useful experiment is replacing a build that keeps dying in exactly the same way.

Not every run needs to solve several systems at once.

The useful ones either leave the permanent account stronger or give me a concrete answer about what should change before I try again.
