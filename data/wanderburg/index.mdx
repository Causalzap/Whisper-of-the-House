<h2 id="what-to-do-first">
  What to do first in Wanderburg
</h2>

Wanderburg gives me plenty of activity before it gives me a clear definition of progress.

I can consume settlements, destroy vehicles, chase chests, stack upgrades, boost around the map, and grow the castle until the run looks dramatically stronger than it did five minutes earlier. Most of that power disappears when the run ends.

The more important question is what remains.

My first goal is therefore not to create the largest possible Green Plains castle. I want to complete the opening boss route and start moving the permanent account forward. That is where new Lands, starting modules, Captains, vehicles, and better loadout options begin changing the next run before it even starts.

The basic controls are easy to understand, but steering matters more as the castle grows. Boosting eventually stops being just a way to cross empty ground and becomes one of the resources that determines whether I can leave a dangerous position.

That change is a good preview of the whole game: something that looks simple during the first run can become a progression problem later for a completely different reason.

<h2 id="how-wanderburg-progression-works">
  Wanderburg progression has four layers
</h2>

I get much better results once I stop treating every failed run as the same kind of failure.

There are four structural layers moving at once.

**The overworld** controls which Lands and major progression routes are available.

**The permanent loadout** controls what the account can bring into a new run before random upgrades begin.

**The current run build** determines whether that particular castle is strong enough to survive the objective in front of it.

**The vehicle chassis** changes how those permanent pieces can actually be used.

Those layers interact, but they do not replace one another.

Opening a new Land does not automatically improve the starting loadout. Unlocking more modules does not help if the current run spreads every upgrade across unrelated damage systems. Unlocking a new vehicle does not guarantee the existing permanent collection fits it well.

That is why I ask one question after almost every run:

**what is permanently different now?**

Maybe the answer is a Land. Maybe a module condition progressed. Maybe the account can finally afford a missing piece. Sometimes the only useful result is learning that the current loadout cannot survive the next boss sequence yet.

<h2 id="build-or-progression">
  A failed run can be a build problem even when the map looks like the blocker
</h2>

The overworld is easy to blame when boss three or four keeps ending the run.

Sometimes the account really has pushed into a Land too early. More often, the new difficulty has simply exposed something the earlier route allowed me to ignore.

I look at what happens before the death.

If ordinary enemies survive long enough to surround the castle, the damage engine is behind.

If one module is clearly doing most of the work while the others keep consuming upgrade choices, the build is too scattered.

If damage is excellent but the castle keeps getting trapped, I stop asking for another damage roll and treat movement as the failing part of the run.

That distinction matters because Movement eventually becomes part of build reliability. A larger castle needs more room to turn and more time to leave bad ground, so a build can continue dealing perfectly respectable damage while becoming much less capable of surviving the screen around it.

<h2 id="bosses">
  The four-boss sequence is the clearest test of whether a run still works
</h2>

A Wanderburg boss sequence does more than check raw damage.

Each fight arrives after the castle has changed again, so a setup that feels effortless early can develop cooldown gaps, movement problems, or unreliable damage by the later fights.

That makes the four-fight sequence useful as a diagnostic. If the same failure keeps appearing, I change the weak layer before starting another identical run.

For preparation before each fight, late-run failure patterns, and the dangerous mechanics I encountered around Dark Tower, use the <Link href="/wanderburg/bosses">Wanderburg Boss Guide</Link>.

<h2 id="captains">
  I choose a Captain after I know what the account needs corrected
</h2>

Captain bonuses are much easier to judge after a recurring failure already exists.

A Captain can improve survival, movement, upgrade quality, a specific damage family, friendly units, or another part of the run, but many of the stronger effects also introduce a meaningful drawback.

That makes the best Captain less about buying the highest letter on a tier list and more about matching the Captain to a problem the current account actually has.

The full ranking, exact Captain effects, prices, specialist cases, and first-buy decisions are in the <Link href="/wanderburg/captains-tier-list">Wanderburg Captains Tier List</Link>.

<h2 id="vehicles">
  New vehicles are sidegrades until the permanent loadout proves otherwise
</h2>

Vehicle progression is where Wanderburg stops behaving like a simple ladder.

A newly unlocked chassis can handle differently and use a different module structure. That means an account that feels excellent on one vehicle can lose an important part of its best setup after switching.

Does the permanent collection still contain a coherent starting setup? Did the vehicle remove a slot the old build depended on? Does the different handling solve a problem, or simply create a new one?

Spiderburg is the clearest early example. Its unlock condition, post-unlock usability problem, slot structure, and chassis-specific strengths are covered in the <Link href="/wanderburg/how-to-unlock-spiderburg">Spiderburg unlock guide</Link>.

For later vehicles, I follow explicit objective text rather than guessing an unlock condition from map position alone.

<h2 id="silver-overtime">
  Silver and Overtime are useful only when they move the permanent account
</h2>

Silver is most valuable when I can name the problem the purchase will solve.

A larger collection is not automatically a stronger account. A module that completes a legal starting loadout, a Crew that reinforces an established plan, or a Captain that fixes a known failure can matter more than a more exciting purchase with no immediate role.

Overtime follows the same logic.

I continue a winning run when the castle still has enough capacity to turn the finished build into more useful account progress. If the normal clear ends with the vehicle barely surviving and unable to reposition safely, leaving can be the better progression decision.

The exact Silver priorities, Gold-versus-Silver distinction, Overtime reward evidence, and continue-versus-leave framework are in the <Link href="/wanderburg/progression-unlocks">Wanderburg progression and unlocks guide</Link>.

<h2 id="module-unlocks">
  If the same modules keep appearing, the permanent pool may be the real bottleneck
</h2>

One of the stranger early progression moments is opening more of the map while the available modules still feel almost unchanged.

Those systems do not advance together automatically.

The account can have a new Land available while the permanent module pool remains small. New module families can depend on their own milestones and may still need to become permanent starting options afterward.

That is why I check the unlock requirement before assuming another biome will somehow refresh the whole build pool.

For now, the confirmed module milestones remain in the <Link href="/wanderburg/progression-unlocks">progression and unlocks guide</Link>. This is also the part of Wanderburg most likely to deserve a dedicated module-unlocks page as the permanent pool becomes larger.

<h2 id="achievements">
  Achievements work best as a second layer on top of progression
</h2>

Most achievement progress overlaps naturally with useful runs.

Driving, destroying enemies, consuming units, defeating bosses, unlocking vehicles, opening Lands, and completing wins all happen while the account is already moving forward.

That makes early dedicated farming less attractive than simply finishing the progression still available.

Once the map is substantially complete, the <Link href="/wanderburg/achievements">Wanderburg achievements guide</Link> covers the full list, the remaining cumulative cleanup, and the order I use to finish it efficiently.

<h2 id="what-changes-later">
  Later Wanderburg becomes more about starting with a plan
</h2>

The opening hours reward spectacle.

A lucky run can grow quickly, stack several impressive effects, and make Green Plains look solved before the permanent account is actually very developed.

Later progression exposes the difference.

A stronger account enters a run with an intentional core rather than waiting for random upgrades to invent one. Vehicle choice becomes part of the loadout. Captain choice reinforces a known plan. Silver purchases close specific gaps. Movement is considered before the castle is already too large to leave danger.

That is the progression threshold I care about most:

**the run begins with a plan, and randomness modifies it — instead of the run beginning with randomness and waiting for a plan to appear.**

<h2 id="next-run">
  When progression stops, identify the layer that stopped first
</h2>

Most of the stuck states I have encountered fit into five categories.

**The map is not advancing.**  
Read the current Land objective and confirm that the full progression requirement has actually been completed.

**The map advanced but the starting pool still feels unchanged.**  
Check permanent module unlocks, purchases, and whether the account has actually expanded the loadout rather than only opening another destination.

**Bosses are scaling faster than the castle.**  
Treat that as a build problem before assuming the route itself is wrong.

**The build kills well but cannot leave danger.**  
Movement or Nitro has become the failing part of the run.

**A new vehicle is unlocked but feels wrong or cannot be used.**  
Check chassis compatibility and permanent module requirements before replaying the unlock route.

That diagnosis determines what the next run is for.

Sometimes I need another Land clear. Sometimes I need one module milestone, one permanent purchase, or one controlled test on familiar ground.

A useful Wanderburg run does not have to be spectacular.

It needs to give the next run a better starting point, a clearer objective, or a concrete answer about what is still holding the account back.
