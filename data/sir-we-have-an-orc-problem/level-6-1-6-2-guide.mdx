Levels 6.1 and 6.2 tested two different parts of my defense.

Level 6.1 exposed weak routes. I could have enough total damage and still lose
because the top-left or bottom-left did not receive enough reliable coverage.

Level 6.2 exposed bad timing. My first survival did not complete the final
progression because enemies escaped, and the main reason was not a missing
upgrade. I had spent my strongest active abilities before the last rush.

That distinction controls every decision in this guide:

- when a route fails early, I change coverage or buy one relevant breakpoint;
- when the defense reaches the final rush, I stop rebuilding and change ability
  timing;
- when I only need a survival achievement, I do not confuse it with the
  separate perfect-clear result required by my final completion run.

<h2 id="before-level-6-1">
  What I Prepare Before Level 6.1
</h2>

I do not wait until the entire upgrade tree is complete.

Before starting Level 6.1, I want:

- enough permanent damage to hold more than one route without manual help;
- one way to cover an outer route that the central towers cannot reach;
- enough late cleanup to stop one or two damaged stragglers;
- at least one emergency ability available for normal progression attempts;
- one stable farming stage in case the first test exposes a real damage wall.

I do not buy another expensive node only because it is available.

I first enter the stage and classify the failure:

- a full route with little or no damage means I need coverage;
- enemies leaving every route with low health means I need a combat breakpoint;
- a defense that reaches the last group means I should test timing before
  returning to a long farm.

<h2 id="level-6-1-first-run">
  My First Level 6.1 Test
</h2>

I do not try to create the final layout before pressing Start.

I cover the visible entrances, keep some flexibility for the outer routes, and
use the first run to answer three questions:

1. Which area releases the first full-health enemy?
2. Which area only releases damaged stragglers?
3. Which area already holds without manual missile fire or an active ability?

The most useful locations are not automatically the busiest-looking locations.

A central section can contain thousands of enemies and still be safe because
they remain inside the permanent damage area. An outer section can look quiet
and still ruin the run when one group leaves its final firing line.

That is why I watch exits first and total damage second.

<h2 id="level-6-1-routes">
  How I Fix the Top-Left and Bottom-Left Routes
</h2>

The top-left and bottom-left were the first places I treated as separate jobs.

I do not reinforce them in the same way.

### When the top-left releases a full group

I check whether the existing tower remains aimed at the route long enough to
finish the group.

If it keeps rotating toward another target, I give the top-left a more
dedicated firing angle. I do not add another upgrade to a safe central route
and hope the extra total damage reaches the top-left indirectly.

### When the bottom-left releases full-health enemies

This is missing permanent coverage.

I move or reserve one tower for the lower route before adding more central
power. A late cleanup tower is not supposed to stop a complete wave by itself.

### When only one damaged enemy reaches an exit

I keep the main layout.

I make one smaller correction:

- tighten the nearest firing angle;
- move one cleanup tower closer to the late route;
- redirect manual missile fire toward the first escape point;
- stop helping a central section that already holds by itself.

</h2>

I do not use a minute-by-minute enemy schedule for this stage.

The useful breakpoint in my run was positional: the defense could look stable
until the remaining enemies were concentrated near the outer exits.

The dangerous phase arrived with roughly **1,000 enemies remaining**.

The counter later dropped to roughly **10 enemies**, but that did not make the

My final sequence is:

1. let the permanent defense handle groups still inside its firing lines;
2. keep the cursor near the route that leaked during earlier attempts;
3. use emergency control only after a group crosses the last recoverable point;
4. target the first straggler instead of the largest remaining crowd;
5. continue watching until the final enemy is removed.

I do not use an ability only because the screen is crowded.

A crowded screen is acceptable while the enemies remain inside the planned
damage area.

<h2 id="level-6-2-pressure">
  The Level 6.2 Pressure Pattern I Watch
</h2>

I divide Level 6.2 into three practical phases.

### Early route check

The opening groups tell me whether any route lacks permanent coverage.

A full-health group escaping here is not an ability-timing problem. I correct
the layout before spending more attempts on the final wave.

### Stable middle

The middle of the battle can look overwhelming while still being controlled.

If the front of each crowd keeps dying inside the main firing area, I do not
spend Nuke or Cryo Beam only to make the screen look cleaner.

### Final acceleration

The final pressure moves through the remaining route faster than the earlier
groups.

This is the real completion check.

I stop watching the center of the map and watch the last permanent firing line.
The trigger for an emergency ability is not a fixed enemy count. It is the
moment the group crosses a point the towers can no longer recover from.

I do not claim an exact special-enemy schedule for 6.1 or 6.2 because the
materials I tested do not support a reliable wave-by-wave enemy list. The
repeatable information is the route pressure and the late acceleration.

<h2 id="ability-timing">
  The Ability Timing That Changed My Level 6.2 Result
</h2>

My first near-clear used too much active damage early.

The permanent towers were still controlling the middle of the battle, but I
spent emergency tools because the screen looked crowded. When the final group
accelerated, I had no comparable answer left.

I changed the timing rather than rebuilding the whole map.

My rule is:

> I keep one burst answer and one control answer until enemies begin leaving
> the final permanent firing line.

I use burst when a dense group has already broken through.

I use control when the existing towers can still kill the group if I stop its
movement long enough.

I do not use either one to compensate for a route that has no permanent tower
coverage from the beginning.

This is the main difference between my failed finish and the successful one.
The defense already had enough power to reach the end; the abilities needed to
be available at the correct moment.

At that point, I separate three outcomes.

### The stage fails early

I return to the layout or buy one upgrade that directly addresses the first
failing route.

### The stage survives with several late leaks

The defense has enough global power. I improve the final route, cleanup, or
ability timing.

### One or two enemies escape during the last rush

I do not replace the entire build.

I preserve the route that already reached the end and adjust the last ability,
manual target, or cleanup position.

This distinction prevents another long farming loop when the real problem is
only the final ten seconds of pressure.

<h2 id="level-6-2-perfect-clear">
  How I Completed the Level 6.2 Perfect Clear
</h2>

I repeated the same basic defense and changed when I used the emergency tools.

I did not need every remaining upgrade or a fully maxed Cryo Beam branch.

My successful sequence was:

1. let the permanent towers clear the opening groups;
2. leave the stable middle alone;
3. keep the strongest burst and control tools unused;
4. watch the routes where enemies leave the final firing line;
5. use the emergency answer during the last acceleration;
6. target the final stragglers until the enemy count reaches zero.

<h2 id="achievements">
  Against All Odds and Four-tified Are Different Goals
</h2>

The achievement descriptions and the final completion condition should not be
merged into one rule.

**Against All Odds** requires surviving Level 6.2.

It does not state that the run must be perfect.

**Four-tified** requires surviving Level 6.1 with only four tower types and no
abilities.

It does not state that the run must be a perfect clear, but it has stricter
build restrictions than the normal Level 6.1 route.

My tested Level 6.2 completion needed every enemy dead to finish the final
progression. That is a separate observation from the listed Against All Odds
achievement condition.

I keep those statements separate because an achievement popup, a survival
result, and final game completion are three different checks.

<h2 id="tower-only-route">
  How I Would Adapt the Route Without Abilities
</h2>

The normal Level 6.2 route on this page uses late emergency abilities.

I have not verified a pure tower-only perfect clear for Level 6.2, so I do not
present one as tested.

For a no-ability Level 6.1 attempt, I would change the preparation in four
ways:

1. convert every route that needed emergency control into permanent coverage;
2. keep more tower placements available for the outer routes;
3. add cleanup before the final test instead of relying on Cryo Beam or Nuke;
4. restart as soon as a full route proves that the four selected tower types
   cannot cover the map.

The key tradeoff is flexibility.

A normal run can recover from one temporary surge with an ability. A
Four-tified run must solve that surge with the selected four tower types before
pressing Start.

That is why a successful normal Level 6.1 layout is useful evidence but not a
complete Four-tified guide.

  What I Do After Clearing Level 6.2

After the perfect clear, I check the final completion state and any remaining
upgrade or achievement goals.

I do not assume that an Endless mode has unlocked.

I did not verify a documented Endless unlock in the current completion route,
and the official Steam description focuses on the short campaign,
meta-progression, turrets, and active abilities rather than advertising an

My reliable post-clear goals are:

- finish remaining upgrade-tree nodes for Fully Loaded;
- return to the restricted survival achievements;
- test Four-tified with a verified four-type, no-ability setup;
- improve a normal 6.2 route only when I want a more repeatable perfect clear.

I keep post-clear speculation out of the walkthrough until the game itself
shows the mode or the developer confirms it.

The listed achievement condition only requires surviving Level 6.2.

That is different from my final progression result, which completed only after
no enemy escaped.

### Does Four-tified allow active abilities?

No.

The listed condition requires surviving Level 6.1 with only four tower types
and no abilities.

### Does Four-tified require a perfect clear?

The achievement text says to survive Level 6.1. It does not explicitly require
killing every enemy.

I still wait for the achievement popup before treating a restricted run as
confirmed.

### Do passive upgrades invalidate Four-tified?

The listed condition restricts tower types and abilities, not the passive
upgrade tree.

I have not verified every passive branch against the trigger, so I would keep
the run within the explicit restrictions and confirm the result from the
achievement popup.

I did not verify an Endless unlock after the tested perfect clear.

I would not promise one until it appears in the current game build or is
