Level 3.2 was the first stage where my usual strategy of spreading damage
across the whole map stopped working.

The map presents **4K enemies** and divides them across three pressure areas.
Each area fails for a different reason:

- the bottom has an easy-to-miss entry and exit path;
- the middle becomes dangerous when large groups stack together;
- the top often looks safe early, then becomes the final source of leaks.

I cleared the stage by treating those areas as three separate tests.

I did not rebuild the entire map after every loss. I first stabilized the
bottom, confirmed that the middle could hold without constant help, and then
moved surplus damage toward the top.

My first priority is not maximum damage.

It is making sure every route receives some permanent coverage.

I reserve at least one placement when possible. The first run usually reveals
that one section needs far more help than it appeared to need during the build
phase.

If I spend every placement before seeing the pathing, I often end up with too
much damage in the center and no clean way to repair the outer route.

<h2 id="enemy-pressure">
  Level 3.2 Enemy and Wave Pressure
</h2>

I do not use a fixed wave-number script for this stage.

I read it in three pressure phases.

### Opening split

The first groups reveal whether all three areas are covered.

This is where the lower route can surprise me. Enemies can pass through a
section that looks separate from the obvious central path, so I watch the
bottom before assuming the initial layout is complete.

If full-health enemies cross the lower bend immediately, that is missing
coverage. I do not try to repair it with another global damage upgrade.

### Mid-battle compression

The middle becomes busier as groups begin stacking inside the same pocket.

This is where a layout can look much worse than it really is. A large number
of enemies may remain visible, but they are still inside the permanent damage
area.

I do not add another central tower only because the screen is crowded.

I add one when the front of the group is leaving the pocket faster than the
existing defense can remove it.

### Late stragglers

The final test is no longer the largest crowd.

It is the small number of enemies that leave the main damage areas with enough
health to escape.

In my later attempts, the middle and bottom became reliable while the top
continued releasing stragglers. That was the signal to stop improving the
center and redirect the next available damage toward the top.

### Explosive and Silver Orcs

Explosive Orc effects can change group spacing and make two attempts with the
same layout finish differently.

When one run suddenly performs much worse, I watch where the group was pushed
before replacing the build.

Silver Orcs serve a different purpose.

They appear through economy upgrades and improve the payout. They do not
require a separate Level 3.2 tower plan. I keep the route focused on survival
and treat a Silver Orc as an income event rather than a special wave that
needs its own defense.

I fix the bottom according to what reaches the exit.

### Full-health groups reach the bottom exit

This means the route lacks permanent coverage.

I dedicate one available direct-fire or short-range tower to the lower bend. I
do not add more power to the middle and hope splash damage eventually reaches
the bottom.

### Only damaged stragglers reach the bottom exit

This means the route is mostly working.

I make a smaller adjustment:

- rotate one nearby tower;
- move one late tower closer to the escape point;
- tighten an existing firing angle;
- use the manual missile on the first enemy leaving the main line.

I do not replace the entire lower setup when only one injured enemy escapes.

<h2 id="middle-route">
  Stop Adding Towers Once the Middle Holds
</h2>

The middle is visually loud.

Large groups stack together, projectiles overlap, and it can look like the
entire defense is about to collapse.

That led me to overinvest in the central pocket.

The correct test is not how many enemies I can see.

I ask whether they are leaving the pocket.

If the front of the group keeps dying before crossing the final central firing
line, the middle is already doing its job. Another central tower adds damage
to a route that was going to survive anyway.

I stop adding to the middle when:

- no full-health enemies leave the pocket;
- the remaining group keeps shrinking;
- I no longer need to aim the manual missile there;
- the bottom survives without help from the center;
- the next leaks appear somewhere else.

At that point, I treat every unused or movable tower as support for the top.

The top did not need an entirely new build.

It needed damage removed from a safer area and reassigned to the actual leak.

My adjustment order is:

1. confirm the bottom can survive one run without manual help;
2. confirm the middle group dies before leaving its pocket;
3. move one surplus central tower toward the top;
4. aim the moved tower through the route enemies actually follow;
5. run the stage again before making a second change.

Moving one tower at a time matters.

If I change three routes at once, I cannot tell which adjustment fixed the
problem or which one created a new leak.

<h2 id="farm-or-reposition">
  When I Farm and When I Reposition
</h2>

Several failed attempts returned about **900 resources**.

That made short farming loops useful, but only when I was close to an upgrade
that addressed the current problem.

I use this rule:

### Farm when all three areas are underpowered

If the top, middle, and bottom all collapse, the problem is broader than one
tower angle.

I repeat the stage briefly and buy affordable combat upgrades for towers
already hitting the correct routes.

In my run, a group of inexpensive upgrades changed the best result from about
**2K enemies defeated** to more than **3K**.

That was a real combat breakpoint, not a cosmetic improvement.

### Reposition when only one area fails

If the middle and bottom hold while the top leaks, another global upgrade is
not my first answer.

I move or redirect one tower toward the top.

<h2 id="final-clear-route">
  My Final Level 3.2 Clear Sequence
</h2>

The successful route was a sequence of smaller corrections rather than one
perfect starting layout.

### Step 1: cover every pressure area

I make sure the hidden bottom path receives permanent damage before investing
more in the central pocket.

### Step 2: let one run expose the real weakness

I watch where the first full-health enemies leave a damage zone.

I do not judge the build only by the total enemy count still visible.

### Step 3: stabilize the bottom

I give the bottom enough coverage to stop full groups.

If only one injured enemy escapes, I use a small rotation or cleanup
adjustment.

### Step 4: stop overbuilding the middle

Once enemies die inside the central pocket, I leave that section alone.

I treat the remaining tower placements as a reserve for the outer routes.

### Step 5: reinforce the top

The top became the final weak point.

I moved damage away from the stable middle and aimed it through the actual top
path.

### Step 6: buy one relevant breakpoint

When all areas still lacked damage, I bought several affordable combat nodes.

I returned as soon as those purchases improved the existing defense rather
than waiting for every expensive branch.

### Step 7: protect the final stragglers

During the last part of the battle, I watch the exits rather than the largest
crowd.

The main group is often already under control. One unnoticed straggler is more
likely to remove the clean-win result.

<h2 id="after-level-3-2">
  What I Do After Clearing Level 3.2
</h2>

I do not immediately spend every new resource after the clear.

Level 3.2 opens additional Cannon, penetration, burning-target, and active
ability choices. The best purchase depends on what the next stage reveals.

My transition into Level 4 is:

1. keep the successful Level 3.2 layout unchanged;
2. enter the next unlocked stage once;
3. identify whether the new problem is coverage, damage, or late control;
4. return to Level 3.2 only if it is the most efficient farm for the resource
   I need;
5. buy one upgrade that answers the new failure;
6. test the new stage again.

I do not recommend entering Level 4 with a fixed resource bank.

Upgrade prices can change, and two players may reach the transition with
different branches developed.

I prepare capabilities instead:

- one stable farming stage;
- enough direct damage to hold a complete route;
- one answer for a separate outer lane;
- Automatic Missile when repeated manual clicking is distracting;
- one active ability reserved for a group the permanent defense cannot
  recover from.

I also do not buy a newly unlocked Cannon or active node only because it looks
powerful.

I first confirm that it solves the next map.

That keeps Level 3.2 as a transition point rather than turning it into an
unnecessary full-tree grind.

<h2 id="fresh-save-route">
  What I Would Do Earlier on a Fresh Save
</h2>

On a new save, I would use this shorter route:

1. enter Level 3.2 with at least one placement left uncommitted;
2. identify the hidden lower path before judging the middle;
3. give each pressure area basic coverage;
4. watch one full attempt without rebuilding during every crowded moment;
5. stabilize the bottom first;
6. stop adding to the middle as soon as it holds;
7. direct the next available damage toward the top;
8. farm only when several routes fail at the same time;
9. return immediately after one relevant upgrade;
10. keep the completed layout for the transition into Level 4.

The biggest improvement is not a specific tower.

It is delaying the final placements until the stage shows me where they are
actually needed.

<h2 id="faq">
  Level 3.2 FAQ
</h2>

### Is Level 3.2 a good farming stage after I clear it?

It can be, but I compare it with Level 3.1 rather than assuming the newer stage
is better.

I use Level 3.2 when it produces the required resource consistently and the
longer enemy count does not reduce resources per minute. I return to a shorter
stage when spawn time makes Level 3.2 inefficient.

### Do Silver Orcs change the Level 3.2 build?

No.

Silver Orcs change the payout after the corresponding economy upgrade. They
do not create a new route or require a dedicated tower placement.

### Should I perfect clear Level 3.2 before moving into Level 4?

Only when I need the separate clean-win resource or want the unlock tied to
killing every enemy.

A survival result can still advance progression and produce normal rewards. I
do not delay the next stage only to chase a perfect result that does not fund
my planned purchase.

### Why can the same Level 3.2 layout perform differently?

Explosive effects and knockback can change the spacing of a group.

When one attempt is unusually bad, I repeat the layout once and watch where
the enemies were displaced before replacing several towers.

### Should I buy the first upgrade that appears after Level 3.2?

No.

I test the next stage first. If it exposes a missing firing angle, I consider
tower count or coverage. If the correct towers already attack the route but
leave enemies at low health, I buy a combat stat instead.
