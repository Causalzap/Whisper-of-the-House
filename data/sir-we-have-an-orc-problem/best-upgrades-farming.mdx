The fastest way I progressed in Sir, We Have an Orc Problem was not by
buying every new tower or economy node as soon as it appeared.

I progressed faster after separating every purchase into three questions:

1. Does this upgrade make the current defense strong enough to reach the next
   stage?
2. Does it increase the value of several future farming runs?
3. Does it solve a missing firing angle that no cheaper stat upgrade can fix?

My biggest upgrade mistake was saving for expensive tower-count nodes while
leaving affordable Fire Rate, Damage, Burn, and Penetration upgrades
untouched.

My biggest farming mistake was assuming that the newest stage had to be the
best source of resources.

A shorter stage can pay less per completion and still produce much more per
minute.

On the opening stage, I can receive progression for surviving even when some
of the **400 orcs** escape.

Killing all 400 provides the separate clean-win result.

That creates my first farming rule:

> I choose the farming stage after choosing the target upgrade, not the other
> way around.

Before repeating a stage, I check:

- which resource the target node requires;
- whether the run only needs to survive;
- whether every enemy must die;
- whether my layout can produce that result consistently;
- how long one complete attempt takes;
- whether one leak makes the entire run useless for the current target.

An imperfect survival can still be productive.

It becomes a wasted farming run only when it fails to produce the currency I
am currently trying to earn.

<h2 id="perfect-clear-bonus">
  When the Perfect Clear Bonus Is Worth Farming
</h2>

I do not chase a perfect clear on every farming run.

I chase it when:

- the next upgrade requires the clean-win resource;
- the stage is already close to perfect;
- one placement or small stat upgrade can remove the remaining leak;
- restarting after one escape saves more time than finishing the run.

I stop chasing it when the layout still loses entire groups.

If several routes collapse, I first farm the normal repeatable resource and
improve the defense. Trying to force a perfect result from an unstable layout
usually produces less progress per minute.

I also distinguish between one late straggler and a complete damage failure.

One straggler tells me to adjust cleanup, targeting, or one affordable
breakpoint.

A full wave reaching the exit tells me to return to broader combat upgrades.

<h2 id="early-upgrades">
  My Early Upgrade Order
</h2>

I begin with Gunner Fire Rate and Damage.

The first maps reward concentrated continuous fire more reliably than a wide
collection of partially upgraded branches.

My early order is:

1. enough Gunners to cover the actual route;
2. Fire Rate;
3. Damage;
4. enough Range to create a useful firing angle;
5. early missile improvements only when the manual shot is making a visible
   difference;
6. the first economy purchase after the stage becomes repeatable.

### Why I do not buy Range automatically

Range is valuable when it allows a tower to reach a corridor, corner, or
second section of the path.

It is not a general damage upgrade.

If the Gunner already reaches its assigned kill zone, Fire Rate and Damage
usually improve the current result more directly.

### Why I delay some economy nodes

Economy upgrades pay over several future runs.

That makes them strong when the current farm is stable and weak when the next
combat node would immediately unlock a better stage.

I buy economy when I expect to repeat the current route several times.

I delay it when one affordable combat purchase can convert the current wall
into a new farming option.

<h2 id="penetration-rapid-fire">
  Penetration and Rapid Fire Are My First Gunner Breakpoints
</h2>

Penetration matters once several enemies move through the same straight firing
line.

Without it, the front target absorbs the shot.

With it, the same attack can continue into the packed group behind that
target.

I buy Penetration after confirming that the Gunner remains aimed through a
crowded lane. I do not buy it to fix a tower that spends the battle rotating
between scattered targets.

Rapid Fire is the next clear breakpoint.

<h2 id="automatic-missile">
  When Automatic Missile Is Worth Buying
</h2>

Automatic Missile does more than remove repeated clicking.

It gives me more attention for:

- checking the first leaking route;
- watching whether knockback moves enemies forward;
- comparing one farming result with the previous run;
- deciding when a clean-win attempt has already failed;
- using active abilities during the dangerous part of the battle.

I buy it once the manual missile is already contributing useful damage and
repeated clicking is interfering with those decisions.

I delay it when an entire route lacks tower coverage.

Automation cannot replace a permanent firing line.

Automatic Missile also does not make the run fully unattended in the tested
build. The shot still follows the mouse position, so I leave the cursor aimed
at a useful route before looking away.

<h2 id="short-stage-farming">
  My Farming Efficiency Formula
</h2>

The highest unlocked stage is not automatically the best farm.

I compared a stage containing about **2,000 orcs** with a much shorter cleared
stage using an end-of-battle bonus.

The short stage produced only about half as much per run, but it finished
roughly five times faster.

  Example: 0.5 payout × 5 completions = approximately 2.5 times the resources
  in the same amount of time.
</p>

The reusable formula is:

<p className="my-4 font-semibold text-slate-900">
  Farming efficiency = useful payout per run ÷ total completion time.
</p>

“Useful payout” means the resource required by my target upgrade.

A stage that gives more total currency can still be worse when most of that
currency is not the type I currently need.

Once the defense kills enemies soon after they spawn, spawn timing becomes the
main limit.

At that point, more tower damage may shorten the final walk of one enemy, but
it does not make the complete enemy count appear five times faster.

That is when I stop optimizing the farming layout and prioritize restart speed.

### I buy cheap stats when

- the existing tower is already aimed at the correct route;
- the route survives most of the battle;
- the remaining enemies leave with low health;
- Fire Rate, Damage, Burn, or Penetration can improve several existing towers;
- the tower-count upgrade requires many more farming runs.

### I buy another tower when

- one entrance receives almost no permanent damage;
- moving an existing tower opens a different route;
- the map has more independent pressure zones than the current count can cover;
- cheap stats improve the safe lanes but cannot create a missing firing angle.

<h2 id="levels-4-5">
  My Upgrade Priorities Through Levels 4.x and 5.x
</h2>

After Level 3.2, I stop trying to develop every branch at the same speed.

I alternate between economy and combat.

My cycle is:

1. stabilize a repeatable stage;
2. buy one economy node that will affect several future runs;
3. complete another run;
4. buy combat upgrades for the towers already producing useful damage;
5. test the blocked stage again;
6. repeat only when the last purchase addressed a real weakness.

This is also the point where Automatic Missile becomes more valuable.

Repeated runs require less mechanical attention, so I can compare:

- which route begins leaking first;
- whether a new upgrade changed the result;
- how much the completion payout changed;

<h2 id="abilities-and-farming">
  How Active Abilities Affect Farming Efficiency
</h2>

I separate farming abilities from progression abilities.

On a stable short farm, permanent towers should handle almost every enemy.

Using Nuke, Cryo Beam, Strafing Run, or another active effect on every cycle
usually adds manual attention without changing the spawn speed that limits the
run.

I invest in an active ability when it does one of three things:

- converts an unstable stage into a repeatable survival;
- protects a required perfect-clear result;
- saves the final surge of a progression stage.

I delay the upgrade when the ability only makes an already safe middle section
look cleaner.

### When an ability improves farming

An active ability improves the farm when one predictable group is responsible
for most failures.

If one Cryo Beam or Nuke turns that stage into a consistent completion, the
ability can increase useful resources per minute by preventing restarts.

### When an ability does not improve farming

It does not improve the farm when:

- the permanent layout already clears every group;
- the completion time is limited by enemy spawning;
- several separate routes collapse;
- I must watch the entire battle to use the ability correctly;
- the upgrade currency would unlock a stronger permanent breakpoint instead.

I do not need every active branch maxed before finishing progression.

The final-stage route succeeded without a fully maxed Cryo Beam, so I do not
treat active completion as a prerequisite for Level 6.2.

<h2 id="when-to-return">
  When I Stop Farming and Return to the Blocked Stage
</h2>

I return when the new purchase matches the failure I observed.

My return conditions are:

- a missing route now has another tower available;
- a nearly successful Gunner line now has Penetration or Rapid Fire;
- a burn route now has enough Damage or Tick Rate to finish stragglers;
- Automatic Missile lets me watch the dangerous routes instead of clicking;
- one active improvement can cover the final surge;
- the next upgrade requires a resource the current farm cannot produce
  efficiently.

I do not wait until every affordable node is filled.

That creates overfarming: I spend several additional runs improving a layout
that was already strong enough to move forward.

<h2 id="troubleshooting">
  Upgrade and Farming Troubleshooting
</h2>

### The newest stage pays more, but progression feels slower

Time complete attempts from Start to the next restart.

A stage paying twice as much is worse when it takes five times longer and both
stages produce the same required resource.

### I bought economy and the current stage did not improve

Economy affects future purchases, not the enemies already reaching the exit.

Buy combat first when one affordable breakpoint can unlock a stronger farm.

### I bought Range and the lane still leaks

Check whether the tower already reached the enemy path.

Range creates an angle. It does not increase Fire Rate, Damage, Burn, or
Penetration.

### The next tower-count node is too expensive

Identify whether the problem is missing coverage or insufficient damage.

Buy the tower count when a route has no firing angle. Buy cheap stats when the
existing towers are already attacking the correct lane.

### My payout changed even though I repeated the same stage

Check the result breakdown.

Silver Orc spawns, percentage bonuses, regular kills, Level Bonus, survival,
and perfect-clear results can change the total.

<h2 id="faq">
  Upgrades and Farming FAQ
</h2>

### Can Automatic Missile make farming fully AFK?

Not completely in the build I tested.

Automatic Missile removed repeated clicking, but its shots still followed the
mouse position. I leave the cursor aimed at a useful section before looking
away.

### Is 40.7K guaranteed from the late-game farming stage?

No.

That result included 9.65K from Silver Orcs and an 8.88K percentage bonus.
Chance-based spawns and active modifiers can change the next result.

### Do I need about 1.36 million resources to finish the game?

No.

That total came from completing the entire upgrade tree in an older, harder
balance state. It is not the current price of finishing Level 6.2.

### Can a failed run be a better farm than a successful run?

Yes, when the failed run ends quickly and still produces the resource required
by the target upgrade.

It is worse when the missing survival or perfect-clear result removes the
specific currency I need.

### Should I keep farming after buying the planned upgrade?

Usually not.

I return to the blocked stage and test the purchase. Another farming loop is
only useful when the same failure still appears and the next upgrade addresses
that failure directly.
