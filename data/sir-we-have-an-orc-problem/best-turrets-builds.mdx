The best turret in Sir, We Have an Orc Problem is not always the one with the
highest damage total.

My most reliable builds work because every tower has a specific job:

- Flamethrowers apply burn before the main kill zone;
- Gunners provide continuous direct damage;
- Cannons penetrate packed lanes and punish dense groups;
- Lasers solve awkward wall geometry;
- Mortars disrupt large clusters when their knockback points safely;
- Tesla Coils stop the final damaged stragglers.

Most of my failed builds were not missing one universally stronger turret.

They failed because a Gunner kept rotating between distant lanes, a Laser
bounced through empty space, or a Cannon pushed enemies toward the exit.

This guide ranks the turrets for normal progression, then shows how I diagnose
those failures before buying more upgrades or rebuilding the whole map.

<h2 id="why-builds-fail">
  Why a Strong Build Can Still Keep Failing
</h2>

Before changing towers, I classify the failure.

That stops me from buying the wrong upgrade or rebuilding a section that was
already working.

### The whole map collapses early

When several routes fail before the battle reaches its later pressure, my
upgrade tree is usually behind the stage.

I buy broad combat improvements such as:

- Gunner Fire Rate;
- Gunner or Cannon Damage;
- Penetration;
- Burn Damage or Burn Tick;
- Mortar Fire Rate.

Moving one tower cannot fix every route lacking damage.

### One route collapses while the others remain safe

This is a geometry or coverage problem.

I do not add another damage upgrade to the strongest route. I redirect one
tower or add a new firing angle to the lane that is actually failing.

The replacement depends on the route:

- Gunner or Cannon for a straight firing line;
- Flamethrower for a tight corner;
- Laser for a wall-separated hallway;
- Tesla Coil for a late leak;
- Mortar for a packed merge point.

### Only one or two injured enemies escape

I keep the main build.

A late Tesla Coil, redirected Gunner, or extra burn zone is usually enough.
Replacing the entrance layout after losing to one straggler often creates a
new leak somewhere else.

### The same layout suddenly performs worse

I watch the knockback and explosive effects.

Cannon shots, Mortar impacts, and exploding enemies can move a group through
the kill zone faster than usual. I repeat the layout once before rebuilding so
I can see whether the failure is consistent.

### One tower has huge damage but the run still loses

I check where that damage happened.

A Laser can farm damage on a route that was already safe. A Mortar can damage
a large crowd while pushing the leading enemies toward the exit.

The best-performing tower is the one that stops the actual leak, not
automatically the one at the top of the damage list.

<h2 id="best-beginner-tower">
  Gunner Is the Best Beginner Tower
</h2>

Gunner is the safest tower to build around during early and mid-game
progression.

It fires continuously, works on simple routes, and has clear upgrade
breakpoints.

My normal Gunner order is:

1. enough Gunners to cover the important routes;
2. Fire Rate;
3. Damage;
4. Penetration;
5. Rapid Fire;
6. Range only when it creates a useful firing angle.

### Narrow firing arcs beat wide coverage

I try to aim each Gunner through one concentrated route.

A wide arc looks useful because the turret can technically reach more of the
map. In practice, it may spend too much time rotating between enemies.

When several Gunners remain locked onto one corner, their Fire Rate and
Penetration upgrades produce much more consistent results.

### Penetration is the first major breakpoint

Penetration becomes valuable when multiple orcs move through the same firing
line.

Without it, each burst stops at the front target. With it, the same Gunner can
damage enemies farther back in the group.

I do not rush Penetration when the Gunner is firing at scattered single
targets. I first create a lane where the projectiles can keep passing through
the crowd.

### When I stop buying more Gunners

One `More Gunners` node cost **8.17K** and increased the tower limit from
**12 to 13**.

At that price, I ask whether number thirteen solves a real coverage problem.

I buy it when:

- one route has no dedicated direct-fire tower;
- moving an existing Gunner opens a different leak;
- the map has more independent entrances than the current count can cover;
- the cheaper Gunner upgrades are already developed.

I delay it when the existing twelve Gunners are aimed correctly but still have
affordable Fire Rate, Damage, or Penetration nodes.

<h2 id="gunner-cannon-flamethrower">
  Gunner vs Cannon vs Flamethrower
</h2>

These three towers overlap enough that it is easy to ask which one replaces
the others.

I do not treat them as direct replacements.

They solve different parts of the same route.

### Gunner wins on consistency

I choose Gunner when I need continuous damage through a predictable lane.

It performs best when:

- the firing angle stays narrow;
- enemies remain in range for several seconds;
- Penetration can pass through a packed line;
- the route needs reliable damage rather than one large hit.

Gunner is my safest first purchase because it does not need a special corner,
wall, or timing window to contribute.

### Cannon wins on packed straight routes

Cannon has lower Fire Rate but higher damage and built-in penetration.

I use it where several enemies repeatedly line up.

The Level 3.2 clear unlocked `Power Shot`.

Power Shot is strongest when the Cannon keeps firing through the same crowded
route.

It does not fix:

- a Cannon aimed at empty space;
- a route where enemies arrive one at a time;
- harmful knockback toward the exit;
- a tower that spends too much time changing targets.

### Flamethrower wins on burn coverage

Flamethrower initially looked weak because its range was much shorter than I
expected.

It became much better once I stopped comparing it with a direct-fire tower.

I place Flamethrowers before the main Gunner and Cannon line.

That gives the burning enemies time to take periodic damage while they remain
inside the rest of the defense.

Burn can also support upgrades that increase damage or penetration against
burning enemies.

### Which one is actually strongest?

For a new save, I choose Gunner.

For a long packed corridor, Cannon can become stronger.

For a tight turn where nearly every enemy crosses the same flame, Flamethrower
can provide more total value than another direct-fire tower.

The route decides the winner.

<h2 id="laser-mortar-tesla">
  How I Use Laser, Mortar, and Tesla Coil
</h2>

These are my specialist towers.

They can solve a lane that Gunners cannot, but they are easier to place badly.

### Laser solves wall geometry

Laser fires a beam that bounces off walls.

  <Image

  />
  <figcaption className="mx-auto mt-3 max-w-3xl text-sm text-slate-400">
    I use Laser bounces to cover hallways and hidden angles, then verify the
    full beam path during the first run.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

A useful Laser position:

- turns a corner that direct fire cannot follow;
- remains inside a hallway for several bounces;
- reaches an otherwise uncovered route;
- crosses the actual point where enemies escape.

A poor Laser can still produce a high damage number while contributing very
little to the dangerous lane.

I keep one strange-looking bounce when it consistently removes an unreachable
group. I move it when most of the beam travels through empty space.

### Mortar controls packed merge points

Mortar is most valuable when several groups merge into one dense crowd.

An early Mortar can look weak because it fires slowly. After Fire Rate and
Damage upgrades, the same placement can clear a much larger section.

I place it where:

- enemies remain tightly grouped;
- splash reaches most of the crowd;
- the impact pushes enemies deeper into the defense;
- another tower continues firing after the displacement.

I do not keep adding Mortars to a center that is already safe. Once one merge
point holds, the next tower should reinforce the route that still leaks.

### Tesla Coil protects the final corner

Tesla Coil became more useful when I moved it behind the main defense.

Its job is not always to compete for the highest damage total.

A successful Tesla placement may attack only the few enemies that survive the
main build.

I place it:

- near the final escape route;
- after the Gunner and Cannon lines;
- where several damaged routes converge;
- far enough back that it does not become distracted by the first full-health
  group.

<h2 id="knockback-direction">
  Knockback Direction Can Ruin an Otherwise Strong Build
</h2>

This is the most important placement mistake I found.

Cannon and Mortar damage can look strong while their impacts move the leading
enemies out of the main damage area.

<figure className="my-8">
  <Image
    src="/images/sir-we-have-an-orc-problem/sir-we-have-an-orc-problem-knockback-pushes-orcs-forward.webp"

  />
  <figcaption className="mx-auto mt-3 max-w-3xl text-sm text-slate-400">
    More damage does not fix an impact angle that sends enemies toward the
    exit.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

When adding a Cannon or Mortar makes the next attempt worse, I watch one impact
instead of immediately farming another upgrade.

I want the hit to push enemies:

- back toward the entrance;
- across the Gunner firing line;
- through a Flamethrower for another burn application;
- toward another Cannon or Mortar;
- away from the final escape route.

I avoid placing the tower on the entrance side when that causes every hit to
launch enemies forward.

Two Cannons can work together when the first pushes enemies into the second
firing line.

Two Cannons facing the same harmful direction can accelerate the failure.

<h2 id="layered-build">
  My Best Layered Tower Build
</h2>

My strongest late-game layouts follow the order in which enemies move through
the stage.

  <figcaption className="mx-auto mt-3 max-w-3xl text-sm text-slate-400">
    I apply burn first, send the crowd through penetrating direct fire, and
    leave a final cleanup layer near the exit.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

### Layer one: burn

Flamethrowers cover the entrances or the first tight corners.

I want most of the group burning before it reaches the main kill zone.

### Layer two: sustained direct fire

Gunners cover the predictable route after the burn.

Cannons sit on longer packed lines where their penetration and Power Shot can
hit several targets.

### Layer three: geometry and crowd control

Lasers reach around walls or through hallways.

Mortars attack dense merge points, but only after I verify the knockback
direction.

### Layer four: cleanup

Tesla Coils and one late direct-fire tower cover the final leak.

This layer should not fight the full wave. It exists to kill injured enemies
that survive the first three layers.

I do not force every tower type into every stage.

A build with four clearly assigned roles is stronger than a build using every
available tower without a reason.

<h2 id="abilities">
  How Active Abilities Change My Tower Build
</h2>

Active abilities can save a run, but I do not use them as permanent coverage.

Strafing Run, Bombing Run, Orbital Laser, Cryo Beam, and Nuke should correct a
dangerous moment. They should not hide the fact that one route has no tower
aimed at it.

### Cryo Beam

Cryo Beam is my control option.

I use it when enemies have crossed the point where the permanent towers can
recover naturally.

I save it when the screen is crowded but every route is still holding.

### Nuke

Nuke is my burst answer for one dense breakthrough.

I avoid using it on an early group that is still inside the main Gunner and
Cannon lines.

On the final stage, using too much active damage early left no comparable
answer for the last acceleration. Saving the emergency abilities changed the
result without requiring a completely new tower layout. 

### Strafing Run, Bombing Run, and Orbital Laser

I use wide-area abilities when several related lanes are under pressure or
when a predictable section remains occupied long enough to justify the
ability.

I do not use them to compensate for:

- a Gunner facing the wrong route;
- a missing Flamethrower at a required burn point;
- harmful Cannon knockback;
- an uncovered final exit.

This page focuses on how abilities support the tower build. Exact cooldown,
damage, and upgrade comparisons belong in a dedicated ability breakdown
rather than being repeated inside every turret section.

<h2 id="build-controls">
  Controls That Matter When Building
</h2>

A few controls directly affect how well the build performs.

- **Space** starts the battle.
- **Left click** fires the manual missile.
- Tower direction should be checked during the build phase before starting the
  next wave.

I use the manual missile to catch a leak or soften a dangerous group.

I do not treat constant manual firing as the solution to a route that lacks
permanent tower coverage.

Once Automatic Missile is unlocked, I can spend more attention watching:

- which lane fails first;
- where knockback sends the enemies;
- whether the Laser bounce reaches the correct path;
- when to hold or use an active ability.

<h2 id="faq">
  Best Turrets and Builds FAQ
</h2>

### How many towers can I place?

There is not one universal tower cap.

Each tower type has its own count upgrades. For example, one `More Gunners`
node increased the Gunner limit from **12 to 13** for **8.17K**, while a
completed `More Cannons 3/3` branch showed a total of **four Cannons**.

The available count therefore depends on how far that tower branch has been
upgraded.

### Should I place every available tower before starting the wave?

No.

I often keep several towers unplaced during the first test.

Once I see which route leaks first, I can spend those remaining placements on
the actual weak point instead of overbuilding an area that was already safe.

### Is Nuke better than Cryo Beam?

They solve different failures.

I prefer Nuke when one dense group has already broken through and needs
immediate burst damage.

I prefer Cryo Beam when the permanent towers can still kill the group if I
stop its movement long enough.

Neither ability repairs a permanently uncovered route.

### Does a B-tier turret mean I should avoid it?

No.

Laser, Mortar, and Tesla Coil are in B tier because their value depends heavily
on route geometry and placement.

A Laser can be the best answer for one hallway. A Mortar can dominate one
merge point. A Tesla Coil can protect an entire perfect-clear attempt from one
late straggler.

### Can I clear late stages without using every tower type?

Yes.

I build around the jobs the map requires rather than filling the stage with
every unlocked tower.

The essential jobs are burn or preparation, sustained damage, route-specific
control, and late cleanup. Several different combinations can cover those
jobs.
