Sir, We Have an Orc Problem starts with a simple idea: place towers, aim them
into the route, start the battle, and stop hundreds or thousands of orcs from
reaching the exit.

The difficulty comes from everything that happens after that first setup.

A layout can fail because one path has no coverage, a tower spends too much
time rotating, an explosive effect changes the enemy spacing, or an active
ability is used before the group that actually threatens the run.

I use this Hub as the starting point for the whole progression route. It
covers the controls, abilities, reward conditions, enemy behavior and
achievement restrictions that do not need separate pages.

The detailed layouts, upgrade math and difficult stages remain in their
dedicated guides.

<h2 id="core-loop">
  How the Progression Loop Works
</h2>

Each run follows the same broad sequence:

1. choose a stage;
2. place and aim the available towers;
3. start the battle;
4. fire the manual missile and use active abilities when necessary;
5. survive, perfect clear or fail;
6. spend the earned resources;
7. replay a cleared stage or test the next wall.

The opening level contains **400 orcs**.

Later stages scale into thousands, then much larger crowds. The challenge is
not only producing more total damage. The map also asks whether that damage
reaches every route before enemies leave the final firing line.

<h2 id="controls">
  Controls and First-Run Checks
</h2>

The controls that matter most during progression are simple:

- **Space** starts the battle.
- **Left click** fires the manual missile.
- The mouse position controls where the missile is aimed.
- Tower direction should be checked before starting the battle.
- Automatic Missile removes repeated clicking, but the tested build still
  follows the cursor position.

Before pressing Space, I check:

1. whether every visible route has permanent coverage;
2. whether a direct-fire tower is aimed through the route rather than beside
   it;
3. whether I have filled every placement too early;
4. which exit I will watch first;
5. which ability I intend to save for an emergency.

The first attempt on a new map is not always a clear attempt.

I often use it to learn which path releases the first full-health enemy and
which path only needs cleanup.

<h2 id="abilities">
  Active Abilities at a Glance
</h2>

Abilities correct a dangerous moment.

They do not replace permanent coverage for a route that fails from the
beginning.

The important difference is between burst and control.

I use burst when a group has already broken through and needs to disappear
immediately.

I use control when the towers still have enough damage, but need more time to
apply it.

For upgrade spending, I do not max every ability automatically. I buy an
ability improvement when it changes a specific failed stage or protects a
perfect-clear attempt.

<h2 id="enemy-behavior">
  Enemy Behavior That Changes My Decisions
</h2>

I do not have a verified wave-by-wave list for every stage.

The repeatable behaviors are more useful than pretending every run follows an
exact enemy schedule.

### Crowds compress at corners and merge points

A route can contain a huge visible group without actually failing.

I watch whether the front enemies are still dying inside the planned damage
area.

If they are, I leave that section alone.

### Explosive effects can change spacing

Explosions can move enemies forward or separate a previously tight group.

That is why two attempts with the same layout may not produce an identical
result.

I repeat an unusually bad run once before rebuilding everything.

### Silver Orcs affect income

Silver Orcs appear after the corresponding economy upgrade and contribute
additional resources.

They do not require their own tower layout. I treat them as an income event,
not a separate route mechanic.

### Late pressure can be more dangerous than the largest crowd

The final threat is sometimes a small group that has already crossed the main
damage line.

On Level 6.2, the last acceleration mattered more than the visually crowded
middle of the battle.

<h2 id="achievements">
  Achievement Conditions Worth Knowing
</h2>

Four achievements come naturally from progression:

- **Gotta Start Somewhere:** survive Level 1.1;
- **The Real War Begins Now:** survive Level 3.1;
- **Against All Odds:** survive Level 6.2;
- **Fully Loaded:** buy every upgrade.

The remaining challenge achievements change the way the stage must be played.

These conditions are valuable even without a separate achievement page.

I do not claim a fixed successful build for a restricted achievement until I
have:

- a complete successful run;
- the exact tower or ability combination;
- the achievement popup;
- confirmation that no forbidden input was used.

That is especially important for `Four-tified`, where a normal Level 6.1 clear
does not prove that a four-type, no-ability setup works.

<h2 id="failure-check">
  The Four Checks I Make After a Failed Run
</h2>

### Every route collapses

I return to combat upgrades.

This is usually a global damage or progression problem rather than one bad
tower position.

### One route collapses

I fix coverage.

I move or reserve one tower for that route instead of improving an area that
already survives.

### Only one or two damaged enemies escape

I preserve the main layout.

I adjust cleanup, manual targeting or one late firing angle.

### The defense reaches the final rush

I stop rebuilding automatically.

I check ability timing, the last route and the first enemy crossing the final
permanent firing line.

This short diagnosis sends me to the correct Spoke instead of repeating the
same general advice on every page.

<h2 id="faq">
  Sir, We Have an Orc Problem FAQ
</h2>

### Does Automatic Missile aim independently?

Not completely in the tested build.

It removes the need to click repeatedly, but the missile still follows the
mouse position. I leave the cursor aimed at a useful route before looking away.

### Do passive upgrades invalidate Four-tified?

The listed condition restricts the run to four tower types and no abilities.

It does not explicitly prohibit passive upgrade-tree nodes. I have not
verified every passive branch against the trigger, so I rely on the
achievement popup rather than assuming an untested run counted.

### Do I need a fully upgraded Cryo Beam to finish Level 6.2?

No.

My completed final run did not have a fully maxed Cryo Beam. Reaching the final
rush with the emergency tools still available mattered more than filling every
remaining ability node.

### Does clearing Level 6.2 unlock an Endless mode?

I did not verify an Endless unlock after the tested perfect clear.

I would not promise one until it appears in the current game build or is
confirmed by the developer.
