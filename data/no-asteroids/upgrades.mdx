A faster mining tool is not always the best purchase in **No Asteroids**. A full Cargo Hold, an empty Air Tank, or rock that resists your current tool can each limit the value of your next shift. Spend money on the equipment that removes that limitation first.

## What Are the Best Upgrades to Buy First in No Asteroids?

Your first few purchases should help you complete more productive mining shifts rather than simply increase every available attribute.

Watch what happens during your next trip to the asteroid:

- If your oxygen runs out before your cargo fills, buy another **Air Tanks** upgrade.
- If your cargo fills with plenty of oxygen remaining, increase your **Cargo Hold**.
- If valuable minerals take too long to break, improve your mining tool.
- If you repeatedly struggle to return to the Lander, invest in a **Tether** or **Jetpack**.
- If your mining equipment already performs well, consider increasing your income through refining or adding a drone.

### Recommended Early Upgrade Order

A practical starting order is:

1. Upgrade Air Tanks to extend your productive mining time.
2. Expand the Cargo Hold when it starts filling too early.
3. Buy the Sledgehammer.
4. Improve shared tool attributes, particularly Hit Power.
5. Save for the Drill while maintaining enough oxygen and cargo capacity.
6. Improve your mining income and mobility as you gain access to deeper deposits.

This order is flexible. If you can already fill your cargo quickly, another oxygen upgrade may be less valuable than additional storage.

Do not spend all your money improving a single attribute while ignoring the equipment that determines how much ore you can actually sell.

## All Eight Mining Tools in No Asteroids

The handheld mining progression starts with the Pickaxe and continues through the Sledgehammer, Drill, Laser, Plasma Cutter, and Missile Swarm. Two additional late-game tools, the Lance and Slicer, become available after you purchase an Orbital Strike or Collapsar.

Each new tool changes how effectively you can break rock, reach mineral deposits, or destroy larger areas.

### Pickaxe

The Pickaxe is your starting tool. Use it to collect accessible minerals near the surface, complete early Daily Orders, and earn enough money for your first equipment purchases.

It works well enough for establishing an income, but harder deposits and deeper rock quickly expose its limitations.

Avoid spending an entire shift repeatedly hitting a material your current tool cannot break.

### Sledgehammer

The Sledgehammer is the first major step up from the Pickaxe.

Its stronger hits make it useful for breaking rock more efficiently during the early game, although its attack rhythm differs from the Pickaxe.

Buy it once you can afford the purchase without neglecting a serious oxygen or cargo problem.

### Drill

The Drill is a substantial improvement for sustained mining and digging through the asteroid.

It becomes especially useful when shallow deposits are no longer enough to support your equipment purchases and you need to spend more time mining underground.

Continue improving Hit Power if you encounter materials that remain too hard to break.

### Laser

The Laser makes it much easier to attack rock from a distance and reach deposits that were awkward to mine with earlier tools.

It is a valuable middle-stage upgrade because it improves both excavation and general asteroid destruction.

Once you can afford it, compare its performance against your current Drill before committing more money to incremental upgrades.

### Plasma Cutter

The Plasma Cutter provides another improvement in your ability to excavate the asteroid and attack tougher material.

It is useful when your existing equipment can already produce a good income but is becoming slow against harder deposits.

You do not need to stop buying shared tool upgrades while saving for the Plasma Cutter. Those upgrades continue to benefit your later equipment.

### Missile Swarm

The Missile Swarm is a powerful ranged mining tool that can attack large sections of the asteroid without requiring you to stand beside each target.

Right-click to switch between spread fire and Tunnel Mode. Use spread fire for broad excavation and Tunnel Mode when you need to concentrate your missiles on a deeper section of rock.

### Lance

Purchase an Orbital Strike or Collapsar through Engineering to unlock the Lance in the Workshop.

The Lance supports charged shots and continuous fire. Right-click to switch between the two firing modes.

Use charged attacks when you want to concentrate mining power on a particular target, or continuous fire when sustained excavation is more useful.

### Slicer

The Slicer becomes available after purchasing an Orbital Strike or Collapsar.

It provides another option for cutting through asteroid material during late-game excavation.

Compare its cutting performance with your existing equipment before investing further in shared tool attributes.

Once both tools are unlocked, selling either one does not lock it again in the Workshop.

## Should You Upgrade Your Current Tool or Buy a New One?

You do not lose your previous tool attribute upgrades when buying a stronger mining tool.

Upgrades such as **Hit Power, Tempo, Sweep, and Luck** apply across your handheld tools. Buying the Sledgehammer after upgrading the Pickaxe does not reset those attributes.

### Which Tool Attributes Should You Upgrade?

- **Hit Power:** Prioritize this when a deposit takes too many hits or your equipment struggles with harder rock.
- **Tempo:** Improves attack frequency, making it valuable for sustained mining.
- **Sweep:** Helps you affect a wider area while attacking.
- **Luck:** Increases the chance of delivering a more powerful hit.

Hit Power, Tempo, Sweep, and Luck each have 20 upgrade levels. You can continue improving these attributes after purchasing the Missile Swarm.


Hit Power is particularly important when you first encounter materials that your current equipment cannot break.

Tempo and Sweep become more attractive once your tool can already handle the material you are mining. At that point, attacking more frequently or affecting more rock can provide a better improvement than concentrating entirely on individual hit strength.

### When Should You Save for the Next Tool?

Save for a new tool when your existing equipment is becoming the main limitation on excavation, despite reasonable attribute upgrades.

A new tool can change your mining range, attack pattern, and ability to destroy rock. Shared upgrades improve the tools you already own, but they do not replace every advantage of purchasing more advanced equipment.

The choice is not permanent. Any shared attributes purchased while saving remain useful after you unlock the next tool.

### Why Is Some Rock Too Hard to Mine?

As you move deeper into the asteroid, you encounter tougher materials, including Deep Stone, Basalt, and Core Metal.

New campaigns and later years can also contain a Strange Matter asteroid core. Existing asteroids retain their previous core configuration.

If your attacks repeatedly fail to damage the rock:

1. Check whether the tool you are using is strong enough.
2. Increase Hit Power if an affordable upgrade is available.
3. Try a more advanced mining tool.
4. Use an appropriate Charge to help excavate resistant material.

Do not confuse the ability to break the surrounding rock with the ability to efficiently collect a mineral deposit inside it.

If a particular deposit is consuming most of your oxygen without producing useful results, return to easier material until you can afford stronger equipment.

## How Do You Make Money Faster in No Asteroids?

Your income depends on more than the amount of rock destroyed. The minerals you collect, your available cargo capacity, current market prices, and completed Daily Orders all contribute to how much money you earn.

A productive mining shift brings back valuable cargo without wasting too much time on inaccessible deposits.

### Can You Sell Ordinary Rock in No Asteroids?

Starting on Day 35, ordinary rock can fill unused cargo space and be sold for $2 per unit.

Minerals take priority over ordinary rock, so you can continue collecting valuable deposits without treating every piece of excavated material as equally profitable.

This provides another source of income when you are focusing on asteroid destruction rather than mining specific ore deposits.

### Cargo Hold vs Compactor

The **Cargo Hold** increases the Lander's storage capacity, while the **Compactor** reduces the storage space occupied by supported minerals in both the Lander and the Ship Hold.

  columns={landerUpgradeColumns}
  rows={landerUpgradeRows}
/>

The first Compactor upgrade increases Space Ice compression from ×1 to ×1.5. This allows you to collect more Space Ice using the same Cargo Hold.

Prioritize the Cargo Hold when several different minerals are filling your storage. Consider the Compactor when supported minerals account for a large portion of your regular cargo.

As your mining tools become faster, improving both systems helps prevent storage capacity from limiting your income.

### When Should You Upgrade the Lander's Navigation?

The **Landing Navigator** costs $1,500. Buy it when travelling from your current landing site regularly consumes time that could be spent mining.

Hop becomes a worthwhile investment when your oxygen supply supports multiple excavation stops in one shift. Check <Link href="/no-asteroids#landing-sites">how to change landing sites and use Hop</Link> before spending money on navigation upgrades.

### Should You Upgrade Refining or Buyers?

The Trade Terminal has two economic skills:

- **Refining:** Increases ore prices and has 20 upgrade levels.
- **Buyers:** Increases demand for ore and has 10 upgrade levels.

Refining improves the value of future mineral sales, while
Buyers improves demand. Both become more useful once your
mining operation consistently produces large shipments.

During the early game, prioritize equipment that allows
you to collect more minerals. Improving selling prices
offers limited value if you regularly return with very
little cargo.

### How Do Market Prices Affect Your Income?

Mineral prices fluctuate, so the same quantity of a mineral may sell for different amounts at different times.

Consider what the money allows you to purchase, not just the percentage shown beside the mineral price.

## Are Daily Orders Worth Completing?

Daily Orders provide additional income for completing specified tasks during a mining shift.

They can request particular minerals, rock excavation, travel distance, mining depth, or the destruction of loose pieces.

During the early and middle stages, completing achievable orders can provide a useful injection of money toward your next purchase.

Check the order before launching. If it requests a material you can already mine efficiently, make that material a priority instead of collecting whatever happens to be closest to the Lander.

### Why Are Your Daily Orders Not Progressing?

If you are breaking the requested mineral but the order is not advancing, check your Cargo Hold.

A full Cargo Hold can prevent additional mineral collection if no overflow storage is available. If you have installed the Tractor Beam, check your Ship Hold as well. See the <Link href="/no-asteroids/ship-guns#tractor-beam">Tractor Beam and ore recovery guide</Link> for how overflow storage works.

Also check the exact wording of the objective.

Minerals recovered by your ship weapons also contribute toward daily mineral delivery objectives.

An order requesting a particular mineral is different from one asking you to destroy a certain volume of rock. Continuing to excavate after filling your cargo may still help the asteroid destruction objective, but it may not help a mineral collection order.

If your storage fills before reaching the requested quantity, improve your carrying capacity when upgrades remain available. On the next attempt, focus more closely on the requested material instead of filling the hold with unrelated minerals.

### Should You Complete Every Order?

Not necessarily.

Some orders are much easier to finish than others. Large mineral requests can require extensive searching, particularly after substantial sections of the asteroid have already been destroyed.

An order may become a poor use of your time if:

- The requested mineral is difficult to locate.
- Your remaining cargo space is insufficient.
- You cannot break the required material efficiently.
- You are spending most of your shift searching rather than mining.
- Completing the asteroid has become more important than earning additional money.

Once your equipment is well developed, it can be better to prioritize large-scale destruction and complete orders opportunistically.

Orders are a source of income, not a requirement for finishing every asteroid.

### Which Minerals Should You Mine for Money?

The most valuable mineral is not automatically the best mining target.

A deposit that takes almost an entire shift to break may generate less income than several easier deposits collected with the same equipment.

Consider three factors before committing to a deposit:

- How quickly your current tool can break it.
- How much cargo space remains.
- Whether the mineral has a favorable selling price.

Early on, accessible Space Ice, Meteoric Iron, and other nearby deposits can provide a more reliable income than repeatedly attempting to mine resistant material.

As your equipment improves, harder minerals become more practical targets.

### How Do You Find Specific Minerals?

Search exposed terrain for accessible Space Ice before committing to deeper excavation. When several deposits overlap, check the target mineral's name and position so that you do not spend an entire shift digging toward the wrong material.

A larger **Mineral Scanner** range is worth buying when locating suitable deposits takes longer than extracting them. Press C to scan, or check the <Link href="/no-asteroids#first-shift">mineral scanner controls</Link> if you are unfamiliar with its depth indicators.

## Which Suit Upgrades Are Worth Buying?

Suit upgrades improve mining time, mineral detection, visibility, and movement across the asteroid.

  columns={suitUpgradeColumns}
  rows={suitUpgradeRows}

### Air Tanks

Longer shifts give you more time to reach deeper deposits and complete demanding Daily Orders.

Check your current oxygen capacity in the Suit diagnostics panel before purchasing another upgrade. Allow enough time to return to the Lander rather than treating your entire oxygen supply as mining time.

### Mineral Scanner and Searchlight

The Mineral Scanner helps locate deposits, while the Searchlight makes excavation easier in dark areas.

They become more useful as you move away from exposed surface deposits and spend longer mining underground.

Prioritize the scanner if you repeatedly waste time locating the correct mineral. Improve visibility if darkness makes it difficult to navigate tunnels or distinguish the material around you.

### Tether

The Tether provides a way to pull yourself back toward the Lander.

It becomes increasingly useful as your mining equipment allows you to travel deeper into the asteroid or farther from the landing site.

A successful mining trip is not just about reaching valuable ore. You also need enough time to return with the cargo you collected.

### Jetpack

The Jetpack improves movement across the asteroid, between exposed rock surfaces, and through excavated areas.

Its value increases later in the game when large sections of the asteroid have been removed and ordinary movement becomes less reliable.

The Jetpack can recharge while you are airborne, and later upgrades provide longer flight time.

Use it to move between separated asteroid surfaces and navigate large excavated areas. Continue watching both your fuel and oxygen supplies, particularly when returning to the Lander from a distant mining site.

## Which Drones Should You Buy in No Asteroids?

There are four types of drones. Each supports a different part of your mining operation, so buying the most expensive available upgrade is not always the best decision.

<GuideTable
  columns={droneColumns}
  rows={droneRows}
/>

### Digger or Prospector?

Choose the Digger when you primarily want more rock destroyed around you.

Choose the Prospector when you want assistance collecting minerals, especially deposits that are harder to reach manually.

Both can contribute to your mining efficiency, but they solve different problems. Increasing excavation is not necessarily the same as increasing the value of the cargo you bring back.

### Is the Oxygen Drone Worth Buying?

The Oxygen Drone periodically restores some of your suit's air.

It can extend a productive mining shift without relying entirely on Air Tanks upgrades.

It is particularly useful when you have enough carrying capacity and mining power to continue working, but your available oxygen repeatedly forces you to return early.

If the Cargo Hold is already filling very quickly, improving storage or another drone may be a better investment.

### When Should You Buy the Sapper?

The Sapper is useful once you are incorporating more Charges into your excavation and want additional automated destruction.

Its ability to place and detonate Charges makes it a different investment from the Digger or Prospector.

Consider it when you can already produce sufficient income and want to break larger sections of the asteroid while continuing your own mining.

### Should You Upgrade Drone Power, Speed or Swarm?

Drone upgrades are managed separately for each type.

The available attributes depend on the drone's function. For example, a Prospector can benefit from improved mining performance and search range, while the Sapper has upgrades related to its Charges.

**Swarm** increases the number of drones of the selected type.

The Digger has eight Power levels, eight Speed levels, and three Swarm upgrade levels. At Swarm level 2/3, it operates with three drones.

Check the actual drone count in the equipment panel rather than treating the Swarm upgrade level as the number of active drones.

## Are Charges and Mining Rigs Worth Using?

Charges allow you to excavate rock without relying exclusively on repeated tool attacks.

Different Charges provide different effects, making them useful for opening tunnels, breaking resistant material, and destroying unstable sections.

Some particularly useful options include:

- **Plain Charges:** General-purpose excavation.
- **Shaped Charges:** Useful for digging through material along a more focused path.
- **Shaker Charges:** Help break loose unstable sections of rock.
- **Singularity Charges:** Create a destructive effect capable of removing a substantial area of nearby material.

### Should You Upgrade Blast Radius or Stock?

Blast Radius increases the area affected by Charges, while Stock upgrades allow you to carry more.

Increase Blast Radius if your current Charges are not removing enough material. Increase Stock if you regularly use all available Charges early in a mining shift.

It is generally more useful to expand the supply of Charges you actually use than to buy large quantities of equipment that remains unused.

Remember that a powerful Charge can release a substantial amount of material very quickly. Check your remaining cargo capacity if you intend to use it on valuable deposits.

### How Do Mining Rigs Work?

A Mining Rig drills automatically after you deploy it.

Place it early in a shift where it can continue excavating while you mine elsewhere. This gives it more working time than deploying it immediately before returning to the ship.

Mining Rigs become less practical when little solid terrain remains and suitable placement locations are difficult to find.

They are an additional excavation tool rather than a replacement for your handheld equipment.

## What Are the Best Late-Game Upgrades?

Once you own the Missile Swarm, compare further handheld upgrades with the cost of unlocking the Lance and Slicer through late-game ship weapons.

### Should You Max Out Every Upgrade?

Compare the price of a high-level tool attribute with the cost of another drone or a cheaper upgrade elsewhere in the Workshop. Spending heavily on a nearly maxed-out attribute may provide less additional excavation than improving an underdeveloped system.

### What Should You Do When Your Cargo Hold Is Maxed Out?

Even a fully upgraded Cargo Hold can fill before your oxygen runs out, particularly when powerful tools and drones release large quantities of minerals.

Once your storage upgrades reach their limits, change how you spend the remaining shift instead of continuing to invest exclusively in mineral collection.

If your Lander is full, check whether additional minerals can still be recovered through the <Link href="/no-asteroids/ship-guns#tractor-beam">Tractor Beam</Link>.

When no further storage is available, use the remaining time for asteroid destruction or return with your collected minerals.
