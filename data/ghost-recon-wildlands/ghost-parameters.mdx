<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
For my normal 2026 Wildlands replay, I do **not** turn every new realism option
on at once.

I start with two changes:

**One Primary Weapon + Tactical Reload.**

One Primary leaves me with one main weapon and a sidearm. Tactical Reload makes
me lose the ammunition left in a partially used magazine when I reload.

I keep **normal revives, health regeneration, three AI teammates, alarms, and
jammers** at first.

Then I change the wider Difficulty, HUD, and World settings only when I know
what problem I am solving. SAM launchers are a good example: I leave them on
while they still make flying interesting, then disable them if normal travel
has turned into the same missile-evasion routine every trip.

My order is:

**loadout rules → ammo discipline → HUD/difficulty → world friction → harsher
survival rules.**

<h2 id="where-ghost-parameters-are">
  Where I change the new 2026 gameplay settings
</h2>

For the settings that directly change my character rules, I go to:

**Settings → Ghost Settings → Ghost Parameters**

That is where I deal with things such as weapon count, reload behavior, and
revive rules.

I separate those from two other layers.

**Difficulty and HUD options** change how much information I receive and how
punishing certain parts of combat are.

**World and Session settings** change Bolivia around me — squad composition,
alarms, jammers, SAMs, patrol behavior, time, weather, and similar systems.

That distinction matters.

If all I want is a more deliberate loadout, I do not need to change enemy
durability, strip away my HUD, remove my squad, and disable half the world at
the same time.

I change the smallest layer that solves the problem.

<h2 id="one-primary-weapon">
  One Primary Weapon is the first setting I keep
</h2>

This is the 2026 setting that changed my normal campaign habits the most.

With it enabled, I carry:

**one primary weapon + one sidearm**

instead of having two primary weapons ready for completely different ranges.

It makes my loadout more deliberate without increasing enemy health or making
every mistake lethal.

<h3 id="when-i-use-two-primaries">
  When I switch back to two primaries
</h3>

I do not treat One Primary as the “correct” way to play Wildlands.

If I already know a mission benefits from two very different tools, I am happy
to switch back.

A suppressed general-purpose rifle plus a dedicated long-range weapon is the
obvious example.

I would also keep two primaries while relearning the game after a long break.

There are already enough systems to remember without deliberately removing one
of my tools.

My test is simple:

**Is the restriction making me choose?**

I keep it.

**Is it only making me annoyed that I cannot carry the tool I need?**

I switch back.

<h2 id="tactical-reload">
  Tactical Reload makes my reload habit cost ammunition
</h2>

Tactical Reload changes something much smaller than weapon count, but I notice
it constantly.

If I reload with ammunition still inside the magazine, the remaining rounds are
**discarded**.

They do not return to my reserve.

My old habit was to reload almost automatically after a short burst.

With Tactical Reload enabled, that starts wasting ammunition.

So my rhythm changes to:

**check magazine → judge the next fight → reload only when a full magazine is
worth losing what remains.**

That is why I like the setting.

It does not make an enemy artificially harder to kill.

It makes **me** stop doing something careless.

<h3 id="when-i-turn-tactical-reload-off">
  I turn Tactical Reload off while I am still relearning Wildlands
</h3>

There is a point where a good restriction becomes unnecessary mental load.

If I am still trying to remember:

enemy marking,

squad orders,

drone controls,

suppressors,

rebel abilities,

and how Unidad escalation works,

I do not also need to punish every reflexive reload.

I use normal magazines until those systems feel automatic again.

Then I turn Tactical Reload back on.

<h2 id="one-primary-plus-tactical">
  One Primary + Tactical Reload is my normal replay baseline
</h2>

These two settings work well together because they change different decisions.

**One Primary** makes me choose which ranges my main weapon can cover.

**Tactical Reload** makes me think about how I use the ammunition inside that
weapon.

Together, they make the campaign noticeably more deliberate without turning it
into a no-revive or no-regeneration challenge.

That is the balance I want for a long replay.

I still have Wildlands' normal progression.

I still have squad support.

I still have room to recover from a bad fight.

I just cannot carry a perfect answer for every range and throw away half-used
magazines without thinking about it.

<h2 id="survival-settings">
  I leave Revive and health regeneration alone at first
</h2>

This is where I separate **realism that changes decisions** from **difficulty
that changes the cost of failure**.

Ghost Parameters can make being downed much harsher, including an Instant Death
option where I lose the chance to be revived.

I do not start there.

For a normal campaign replay, I keep **Revive on Default**.

One bad angle, unexpected reinforcement, or missed enemy should not automatically
turn into a reset while I am still relearning Bolivia.

Health regeneration gets the same treatment.

I leave it enabled initially.

Disabling regeneration changes almost every firefight because damage keeps
mattering after the immediate shooting stops.

That is a much larger change than carrying one fewer rifle.

So my progression is:

**One Primary and Tactical Reload first.**

Then, if I decide I genuinely want a hardcore run:

**harsher revive rules and reduced survival forgiveness.**

I do not turn them on simply because they sound more realistic.

<h2 id="difficulty-hud">
  I treat Difficulty and HUD as a second pass, not a starting preset
</h2>

The updated customization goes beyond Ghost Parameters.

Difficulty can be tuned more specifically, including things such as **health
regeneration delay, enemy endurance, and vehicle breakability**.

HUD controls also let me reduce information such as the **Intel HUD and input
reminders**, as well as some of the visual help around interactable objects and
throwables.

I do not change all of these just to create something that looks like a
“hardcore preset.”

I wait until I can name the problem.

If combat feels too forgiving, I look at **Difficulty** before randomly
disabling HUD information.

If the screen feels too busy or I already know the game well enough that I no
longer need constant assistance, I reduce **HUD guidance** without necessarily
making enemies stronger.

Those are two different goals.

That distinction is important to me because removing information and increasing
enemy punishment at the same time can make a run harder without telling me
which change actually improved it.

For a returning player, I keep both layers fairly familiar for the first few
missions.

Then I change one thing and see whether the campaign becomes more tactical or
simply more tedious.

<h2 id="ai-squad-size">
  I keep three AI teammates for a normal campaign replay
</h2>

The updated squad controls let me go from **zero AI teammates to the normal
three**.

For my standard campaign, I keep all three.

Wildlands already has systems I am used to planning around with them, especially
squad support and Sync Shot.

Removing the squad therefore changes more than the look of traveling alone.

It removes tools from my normal route through a compound.

I set it to zero only when I deliberately want a lone-Ghost challenge.

For me the decision is:

**normal replay → three AI teammates**

**solo-style challenge → zero**

I do not remove them just because the option is new.

Alarms and jammers are exactly the kind of friction I normally want from
Wildlands.

An alarm changes how I approach a base because I have a reason to identify the
reinforcement problem before opening fire.

A jammer stops me from solving every compound from safety with the drone.

That is useful friction.

So I leave both systems active until they stop producing meaningful choices.

If another base has become nothing more than:

**find jammer → remove jammer → repeat**

and I am doing the sequence mechanically rather than tactically, that is when I
consider reducing it.

<h2 id="sam-launchers">
  SAM launchers are the first world rule I change when flying becomes repetitive
</h2>

SAMs are different because they affect normal travel over a very large map.

I kept them initially.

Later in the campaign, I switched them off.

The difference was immediate: I could use helicopters for travel without every
journey turning into another missile-evasion sequence.

That obviously makes Bolivia less dangerous.

So I do not recommend disabling SAMs by default.

I use one question:

**Are SAM sites still making me choose a safer or smarter flight route?**

If yes, I keep them.

If the answer has become:

**No, I am just doing the same evasive maneuver again while trying to reach the
next mission**

then I turn them off.

That is exactly what I want from the new World settings.

I can remove one repetitive system without also making every ground firefight
easier.

Time and weather are the settings where I care least about a universal “best”

If I am:

learning a compound,

testing a route,

or trying to repeat an approach under consistent visibility,

I use a **fixed time of day**.

That removes one variable.

For normal campaign progression, I prefer **Dynamic**.

I like entering a mission in daylight and having the situation change as the
world moves toward evening.

Weather follows the same rule.

**Fixed weather** is useful when consistency matters.

**Dynamic weather** is what I keep when I am simply playing through Bolivia.

I only lock a condition when it solves a specific problem.

<h2 id="my-settings">
  The settings I would start with on a fresh 2026 replay
</h2>

If I started another campaign now, I would not build a giant realism preset
before the first mission.

I would enable:

**One Primary Weapon**

and:

**Tactical Reload**

I would keep:

**Revive — Default**

**health regeneration — on**

**three AI teammates**

**alarms and jammers — active**

**Difficulty — close to my familiar baseline**

**HUD — readable enough that I am not relearning the interface and the game at
the same time**

and most of the wider World settings unchanged.

Then I would play.

If flying starts to feel repetitive, I look at SAMs.

If the campaign feels too forgiving, I move into Difficulty.

If the screen is giving me information I no longer need, I reduce HUD help.

If I specifically want a punishing survival run, I move into harsher revive and
health rules.

That order matters more to me than any single “best settings” preset.

Changing everything at once can certainly make Wildlands harder.

It cannot tell me **which change made the campaign better**.

I get much more out of the new system by changing one layer at a time:

**loadout → ammunition → HUD/difficulty → world friction → survival.**

That gives me a replay that feels different while keeping the parts of
Wildlands I still enjoy.
