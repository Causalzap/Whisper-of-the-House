Caimanes can get visually busy when several special objectives are available,
so I do not assume every unusual marker is Predator.

The mission I want is:

**The Jungle Moved.**

Once I have the correct mission selected, I travel into the jungle and speak
with the girl.

<h2 id="investigate-three-areas">
  I complete all three investigation areas before looking for Predator
</h2>

Once the girl gives me the three investigation zones, I follow the markers.

I do not start wandering through Caimanes hoping Predator appears somewhere
between them.

The mission wants the clues first.

One of the clearest early signs is a group of **skulls and mutilated remains**
that does not look like an ordinary cartel attack.

**enter marked area → inspect the clue → wait for the objective to update →

The search phase is finished only when the objective itself changes.

<h2 id="thermal-vision">
  Thermal Vision is useful, but I did not need it
</h2>

This was one of my first concerns once it became obvious I was dealing with a
cloaked enemy.

I checked my gear.

I did **not** have Thermal Vision.

I considered leaving to unlock it.

I kept going instead.

That turned out to be fine.

Thermal Vision makes sense thematically and can make tracking a cloaked target
more comfortable, but I do **not** treat it as a requirement for completing
The Jungle Moved.

I finished the mission without it.

So if I have already reached the investigation areas and realize I do not have
Thermal Vision, I do not restart my entire route just for that.

The bigger question is whether I am ready for a long fight where Predator:

**appears**

**attacks**

**relocates**

and then forces me to find another damage window.

That is the behavior I prepare around.

<h2 id="kill-unknown-thing">
  When the objective says Kill the unknown thing, I stop searching
</h2>

After I finish the investigation sequence, the mission changes.

The important objective becomes:

**Kill the unknown thing.**

That is my signal that there is no fourth clue hiding somewhere in the jungle.

The investigation is over.

Now I need a fighting position.

**a loaded primary weapon**

**my AI squad nearby**

**solid terrain I can repeatedly use to break line of sight.**

If rebel support is available, I am happy to bring extra guns into the area.

I just do not rely on them to solve the fight for me.

<h2 id="how-i-fight-predator">
  I use a large rock as my anchor instead of chasing Predator
</h2>

My first instinct was to turn toward Predator every time he appeared and chase
the new angle.

That made the encounter worse.

The fight already has enough movement built into it.

If I respond by sprinting through open jungle after every relocation, I give up
the one thing I can control:

**my position.**

What worked better was finding a large piece of hard cover and building the
fight around it.

The rock does not make me safe.

It makes the encounter more readable.

Instead of watching every possible direction at once, I can keep part of the
arena blocked and react to a smaller number of angles.

My basic loop becomes:

**stay close to hard cover**

**watch for Predator**

**shoot when he gives me an opening**

**break line of sight when I need to**

**reposition around the same cover**

then repeat.

I still move constantly.

I just move with a reason.

<h3 id="full-auto">
  I use the short damage windows instead of waiting for a perfect shot
</h3>

Predator does not fight like a normal Wildlands enemy.

I do not get one clean, predictable exposure where a single careful shot ends
everything.

The fight gives me repeated short windows.

So once he becomes vulnerable and I have a usable angle, I want to put reliable
damage into that opportunity before he relocates again.

I found **full-auto** useful for that reason.

I am not trying to make every exchange elegant.

I am trying to make every safe exposure count.

Then I get back behind cover and wait for another one.

<h3 id="mines">
  Mines were too unreliable to become my main strategy
</h3>

I also tried setting mines around the arena.

I would not build a retry around them.

Predator did not reliably walk through the places where I wanted him to, and
the mines gave me another thing to manage while I was already tracking:

his position,

my cover,

my squad,

and my own health.

If a mine happens to add damage, I take it.

I do not stand around waiting for Predator to cooperate with a trap setup.

My repeatable strategy is still:

**cover → appearance → damage → reposition.**

<h3 id="ai-teammates">
  My AI teammates and rebels are extra guns, not a safety net
</h3>

More bodies in the arena help.

They do not make the fight safe.

I had stretches where several teammates were down at once.

At one point, essentially the whole squad was on the ground while I was still
trying to stay alive.

That changes how I use them.

I am happy for the squad and rebels to add pressure when Predator appears.

But I keep a route around my own hard cover that still works when nobody else
is standing.

I also avoid beginning the boss fight with an unrelated firefight already
raging around the area if I can help it.

Predator is enough to track by himself.

I do not need Santa Blanca shooting through the same space unless I have no
choice.

<h2 id="predator-death-explosion">
  Killing Predator is not the end of The Jungle Moved
</h2>

This is the part of the mission I would warn about before anything else.

I killed Predator.

I thought I had finally finished the hard part.

Then I died.

The objective changes to:

**Confirm the Predator's death.**

That wording makes the encounter feel finished.

It is not.

Predator still has a **self-destruct sequence**.

On my failed attempt, I completed the boss fight but did not create enough
distance before the explosion.

The blast killed me.

The painful part was what happened next:

**there was no checkpoint after the Predator kill.**

I had to repeat the whole boss encounter.

That completely changes how I treat the final seconds.

When Predator drops, I do not mentally switch into reward-screen mode.

I switch into:

**escape mode.**

<h2 id="escape-after-kill">
  After confirming the kill, I run immediately
</h2>

The second time I beat him, I knew exactly what had gone wrong before.

My boss strategy did **not** need replacing.

It had already worked.

The failure happened after the kill.

So once the confirmation objective appears, I do not:

wait beside the body,

revive everybody before moving,

look around for loot,

or assume the game has already saved my progress.

I finish the required confirmation.

Then I run.

I want as much distance between me and Predator as I can get before the
self-destruct finishes.

That is the whole priority.

If I died to the explosion but had already successfully killed Predator, I
would not start changing weapons, mines, or the entire combat plan on the next
attempt.

The fight was solved.

The escape was not.
