<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
Last Rites is a **six-mission story chain**:

**Operation: Lazarus → Deadly Obsession → And Your Enemies Closer → Vow of
Silence → With Extreme Prejudice → The Price of Freedom.**

My clean run from the opening briefing to the final debrief took about **1 hour**. A first run can take longer if I miss an intel trigger, fail the
villa upload defense, or alert one of the tripwire-heavy compounds.

I completed it solo with AI teammates.

The four places I pay the most attention are:

- **Deadly Obsession:** interacting with Vargas' Data Server starts a defense,
  so I reload and set up before beginning the transfer.
- **And Your Enemies Closer:** I do not grab the cultist. I stay hidden and use
  the **drone to listen to him**.
- **Vow of Silence:** triggering a tripwire does not automatically ruin the
  mission as long as the cultist I need to interrogate survives.
- **The Price of Freedom:** the order changes from **terminate** to
  **exfiltrate**, so I get Ines Cortez out alive instead of following the
  original kill order.

<h2 id="how-i-start-last-rites">
  How I approach Last Rites before Mission 1
</h2>

I do not approach Last Rites like another province-clearing session.

It is a contained six-mission chain.

The opening briefing gives me a target called **La Llorona**, but almost no
useful location information.

That tells me the shape of the operation immediately:

**find a lead → follow the intel → identify the next person or location → keep
moving.**

I do not need a special endgame loadout before starting.

What I want is much simpler:

**a suppressed primary I am comfortable with**

and:

**a working drone.**

Several important sections punish me more for misunderstanding stealth or intel
objectives than for bringing the wrong weapon.

I also do not delay Last Rites just to build a perfect setup.

If I am returning after a long break, I would rather begin with familiar
weapons and normal revive rules than turn on every new realism option at once.

If I want One Weapon, Tactical Reload, different squad rules, or harsher
survival settings, I set those deliberately through the{" "}
<Link href="/ghost-recon-wildlands/ghost-parameters">
  Ghost Parameters guide
</Link>
{" "}instead of changing them in the middle of the operation.

My clean run took roughly **1 Hour** from Price's opening briefing to the
final debrief.

That is not the time I would promise for a first run.

My first attempts took longer because the actual time losses came from:

- searching for an intel interaction after I had already cleared the enemies;
- misunderstanding the cultist objective;
- failing the Data Server defense;
- and hitting tripwires I thought I had already spotted.

<h2 id="operation-lazarus">
  Mission 1 — Operation: Lazarus
</h2>

Operation: Lazarus starts as an intel hunt.

My first stop is Unidad.

After I deal with enough resistance to reach the marked objective, I collect
the intel rather than treating the outpost itself as the mission.

That gives me the next lead:

an **off-the-books intelligence office / black site**.

Once the objective changes, I move.

I do not stay around the first outpost clearing every patrol.

That becomes a recurring Last Rites rule for me:

**when the mission gives me the next destination, the current location has
already done its job.**

<h3 id="operation-lazarus-black-site">
  I expect the drone to become unreliable around the black site
</h3>

At the black site, I run into one of the systems that comes up repeatedly in
Last Rites:

**a jammer.**

If my drone suddenly loses signal, I do not assume the controls are broken.

I start looking at the compound differently.

The black site itself is already a massacre when I arrive, but my objective is
not to explain every body or permanently clear the whole position.

I find the required intel.

The files identify:

**Victor Vargas**

and give me his location.

That is my exit condition.

Once I have Vargas' villa marked, I move on.

<h2 id="deadly-obsession">
  Mission 2 — Deadly Obsession
</h2>

Deadly Obsession takes me to Victor Vargas.

The villa approach can get messy because I am dealing with armed cultists,
restricted sight lines, and another situation where the drone may not give me
a perfect picture from outside.

I stay suppressed as long as I can.

My goal is not a perfect stealth clear.

My goal is reaching Vargas and advancing the investigation without creating
more work for myself.

Once Vargas is dealt with, his **bodycam/laptop** gives Price another piece of
the story.

The objective then changes to searching the villa for Vargas' hidden intel.

I do not keep guessing at rooms if I cannot find the interaction.

The exact stash route is in the{" "}
<Link href="/ghost-recon-wildlands/last-rites-intel-locations">
  Last Rites intel locations guide
</Link>

For the walkthrough, the important point is what happens **after** I find the
Data Server.

<h3 id="data-server-defense">
  I prepare before starting the Data Server upload
</h3>

My first mistake was treating the Data Server as the end of the search.

It is actually the beginning of a defense.

Interacting with it starts the transfer and brings enemies onto the villa very
quickly.

I learned that the expensive way.

On my first attempt, enemies were already pushing inside while the upload was
still early.

Two teammates went down, the room collapsed, and I eventually went down with
them.

So on the retry, I do four things before touching the server:

**reload**

**bring the squad close**

**pick the cover I want to fight from**

**check the entrances into the room**

Then I start the upload.

The transfer gives me visible progress checks.

I see it move through:

**20% → 50% → 75% → complete.**

That helps me resist the urge to leave the room and start chasing enemies.

I do not need to own the whole property.

I need the Data Server alive long enough to finish the transfer.

If enemies are outside but the room is secure, I let them come to me.

Once the upload completes and Price tells me to get out and meet her, I leave.

I do not stay to clean up another reinforcement wave.

<h2 id="ines-cortez">
  After Deadly Obsession, I finally understand who I am actually tracking
</h2>

The regroup with Price changes how I understand the rest of Last Rites.

She explains that **Ines Cortez was one of her former assets**.

Ines had been married to Marco Cortez and had been feeding intelligence before
she was exposed.

Price had been told Ines was dead.

She had not verified it herself.

Ines survived.

That is the point where I stop treating **Ines Cortez** as some unrelated
second target who suddenly appeared halfway through the story.

The investigation is revealing that Ines is the woman operating as **La
Llorona**, with the Penitentes building their cult around her.

Operationally, that gives me a much cleaner route for the remaining missions:

**follow Ines' fanatics → use them to find her.**

I do not need to solve the whole history before continuing.

I only need to understand that the cultists are now my trail to the original
target.

<h2 id="and-your-enemies-closer">
  Mission 3 — And Your Enemies Closer
</h2>

This mission gave me the clearest example of how literal I need to be with Last
Rites objectives.

I find the cultist.

I get physically close enough to interact with him.

That makes him look like a capture target.

So on my first approach, I treated him like one.

That was the wrong move.

The real objective is:

**Use the drone to listen to the cultist without being detected.**

There is another problem:

**the drone signal is being jammed.**

So my order becomes:

**stay hidden → restore a usable drone approach → get close enough with the
drone → let the conversation finish.**

I do not grab or knock out the target simply because the interaction is
available.

I wait.

The conversation reveals that La Llorona has a **shrine** and that the
Penitentes believe she is preparing for the end.

Once I get that new lead, I move on.

<h2 id="vow-of-silence">
  Mission 4 — Vow of Silence
</h2>

Vow of Silence changes the type of problem again.

Here my biggest concern is not finding the cultist.

It is reaching him without turning the whole approach into chaos.

The compound uses **multiple tripwires**.

I spotted several.

I still hit one I had missed.

That taught me to change pace as soon as I see the first trap.

I stop sprinting.

I check:

**narrow approaches**

**doorways**

**gaps between cover**

before moving through them.

But triggering one does not automatically make me restart.

My actual objective is reaching the **high-level cultist alive** so I can
interrogate him.

If the base is alerted but the target survives, I can recover.

I clear enough resistance to reach him and complete the interrogation.

His answers sound almost useless.

That is expected.

I do not stand there waiting for him to suddenly reveal Ines' exact coordinates.

Price continues working through the cultist intelligence and then identifies a:

**large buildup of Ines Cortez's forces.**

That new location is what sends me into the next mission.

<h2 id="with-extreme-prejudice">
  Mission 5 — With Extreme Prejudice
</h2>

By the time With Extreme Prejudice begins, I stop expecting another cultist to
cooperate.

The next lead is inside a stronger Penitentes position.

I approach this mission like a raid.

The first targets I care about are enemies with good sight lines over my route,
especially elevated threats that can keep the whole approach exposed.

I clear what is necessary, move into the base, and find the required intel.

I do not turn the search into another full-base cleanup.

The moment Price checks what I found and sends me a new waypoint, the mission
has given me what I came for.

That is when I leave.

The intel eventually confirms the clue:

**Alto Gracia**

and points Price toward an:

**old gold mine a few clicks north.**

That is where the final operation begins.

<h2 id="the-price-of-freedom">
  Mission 6 — The Price of Freedom
</h2>

The Price of Freedom is where the story and the objective finally collide.

Price meets me before the final push and confirms that the stronghold intel has
located Ines Cortez at the old gold mine.

The original plan is still:

**terminate the target.**

Then Price changes it.

Her reasoning is practical.

Killing Ines now would turn La Llorona into a martyr for the Penitentes.

The new order becomes:

**Exfiltrate.**

For gameplay, I take that literally.

I do not shoot Ines because the opening briefing told me La Llorona had to die.

The newest objective overrides the original plan.

<h3 id="final-mine-traps">
  I slow down again on the final mine approach
</h3>

The final compound brings the tripwire problem back.

The mine and sanctum are dark enough that I can focus on enemies and forget the
ground in front of me.

I hit another trap here even after Vow of Silence had already taught me what to
expect.

So I stop trying to save a few seconds.

My pace becomes:

**clear the immediate threat → inspect the route → move → repeat.**

I am especially careful around constrained approaches where I cannot easily
sidestep once I see the wire.

<h2 id="extract-ines-cortez">
  Once I reach Ines, I stop clearing and start extracting
</h2>

The confrontation eventually ends with a direct order:

**Get her up. Let's move.**

That is the clearest phase change in the final mission.

I am no longer hunting La Llorona.

I am getting **Ines Cortez** out alive.

I move with her.

I deal with enemies that directly block the route.

I get to the vehicle.

I do **not** stay behind trying to eliminate every remaining Penitentes fighter.

The objective is extraction.

Once Ines is out, the six-mission chain is effectively finished.

<h2 id="last-rites-ending">
  Why the final debrief says La Llorona is dead even though Ines survives
</h2>

The debrief can sound contradictory if I have been thinking of La Llorona and
Ines Cortez as two separate people.

They are not two separate targets.

Ines is the woman the operation has been tracking under the **La Llorona**
identity.

At the end, Price reports that:

**La Llorona is dead**

while also saying:

**Ines Cortez is no longer a threat.**

I read that as the operation ending La Llorona as the cult's active leader
while Ines herself survives the extraction.

For completing the mission, the practical point is simple:

**do not kill her in the final encounter.**

Once the objective changes to exfiltrate, get her out alive.

The game closes out the contained Last Rites operation and sends me back toward
the main menu before I continue with the normal Bolivia campaign.

So I treat the end of Last Rites as a clean stopping point:

**finish the extraction → collect the unlock → return to the main campaign if I
want to keep playing.**

The pattern that saved me the most time across all six missions was not a
particular weapon or build.

It was reading the **current objective** instead of continuing to solve the
previous one.

When the intel gives me a new destination, I leave.

When the Data Server starts uploading, I defend instead of searching.

When the cultist objective says listen, I use the drone instead of grabbing
him.

When the final order changes to exfiltrate, I stop trying to complete the
assassination I was originally sent to perform.
