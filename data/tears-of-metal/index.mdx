<h2 id="diagnose-the-failure">
  Diagnose why the Campaign failed
</h2>

The rule connecting every page is:

> Stop treating every failure as a damage problem.

A Campaign can end because the route added too much Threat, healing Coins were
spent too early, the Hero does not fit the encounter, the build lacks control,
the army lost key Commanders, or a boss punish started before recovery.

<h2 id="core-rules">
  Four rules that connect the guides
</h2>

### Route: stop after the build can clear Captains

Plan the line to the Great Gates before selecting the first node. Once one
attack loop works, health is falling, or the next battle crosses another Threat
threshold, stop adding optional nodes.

### Build: use setup, payoff, control, and a safe exit

A strong Emblem does not make a complete build by itself. Wallace and Brienne
both need a repeatable setup, a damage payoff, a way to control pressure, and a
way to leave before the counterattack.

### Village: upgrade the diagnosed bottleneck

A direct Hero failure points toward a Hero stat. Weak Ally Commands point toward
the Barracks. Poor merchant, Shrine, or event access points toward Market or
Cartographer. Route greed often means buying nothing and changing the next path.

### Co-op and bosses: confirm before committing

Co-op is shared routing with separate survival resources. Check both health
bars and both Coin balances before confirming.

Against Gilles, wait for recovery before the long punish. Against Iseult,
confirm the real target and safe lane before spending burst.

<h2 id="heroes-and-companions">
  Hero and Companion choices are progression decisions
</h2>

Wallace is my safer learning Hero, Brienne rewards clean Direct Hit chains, and
Ruadh exchanges speed for wide hammer pressure and Life Steal.

I do not buy permanent stats for all three at once. I test the base Hero against
the current problem, then invest in the one that actually improves the next
Campaign.

Companions are separate from Commanders. They have their own slots and event
logic, so increasing the Commander roster does not solve a Companion-capacity
problem.

<h2 id="faq">
  Tears of Metal guide FAQ
</h2>

### Where should a new player start?

Start with the Beginner Guide. It covers the Act 1 route, Threat, healing,
target priority, Wallace&apos;s first usable build, and Gilles preparation.

### Does health refill after every battle?

No. Sweep the battlefield for Chicken and unused recovery, then decide whether
the next Healing Font is worth the Coins.

### What carries over after a failed Campaign?

Permanent Hero and Barracks upgrades, Village investments, Monikers, and
permanent currencies remain. Coins and the current Emblem build do not carry
over.

### Is Tears of Metal co-op?

Yes. The detailed Host, loot, Coin, healing, and revive rules currently
documented here come from a tested two-player Campaign.

### What should I do after my first failed Campaign?

Identify whether the failure came from health, Block Stamina, Hero choice, army
output, route Threat, missing healing, or boss execution. Then open the matching
Beginner, Character, Village, Build, Co-op, or Boss guide.

### Should I always buy another permanent upgrade after dying?

No. When the build works and the Campaign ends because of route greed, maximum
Threat, or unused healing Coins, the next improvement is a decision change
rather than another stat.
