<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
Before your first fight, **equip your characters and assign their active skills**. Use **M** to check the map, collect Food for resting, and save before unfamiliar encounters. In combat, each character gets one action per round, but using a potion from the inventory does not consume that action.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

Underkeep can punish small oversights more than a poor character build. A hero may be carrying better armor without wearing it, a useful skill may not be selected, or the party may enter another fight with too little Food to recover afterward.

The game also gives you tools that are easy to overlook: manually marked maps, free-action potion use, and the ability to return later for locked chests. Understanding those systems early makes the dungeon exploration much easier to manage.

You do not need an optimized party to get started. If you are still choosing your four characters, the <Link href="/underkeep/races-classes">Underkeep classes and races guide</Link> explains the available roles, starting attributes, and team combinations.

<h2 id="first-steps">
  What Should You Do Before Your First Fight in Underkeep?
</h2>

Once character creation is complete, check the party before moving too far into the starting area.

The game provides a shared inventory, but **each hero has separate equipment slots**. Picking up a weapon, piece of armor, or accessory does not mean the character who needs it is already using it.

Start with these checks:

1. **Open your inventory and equip usable gear.** Put defensive equipment on characters likely to receive attacks, and check that each weapon is suitable for its intended user.
2. **Check the active skill slot.** Right-click the slot beside a character's basic attack to select an available active ability.
3. **Confirm your party positions.** Front-row characters should be prepared for direct attacks, while the rear positions normally rely on ranged attacks, spells, or Reach weapons.
4. **Look at your Food supply.** Resting consumes Food, so it is worth collecting even when everyone currently has full Health.
5. **Open the map with M.** Get used to checking your position before entering another passage or turning back.

Skills have another important distinction: passive abilities remain active without being placed in the active slot. Active abilities need to be selected before you can use them.

Some attacks also require a particular weapon type. If a skill is unavailable after changing equipment, check its description before assuming the character has lost access to it.

You gain a Skill Point when leveling up, but the best way to spend it depends on your class. The important early habit is to check for available upgrades and then make sure the abilities you intend to use are actually selected.

<h2 id="controls">
  Which Controls Should You Learn First?
</h2>

Underkeep uses grid-based movement. Each movement step advances the party by one tile, and turning changes the direction the entire group faces.

Explored parts of the current area are revealed on the map as you move. The map is particularly useful when the visible corridor makes it difficult to tell whether an enemy is directly ahead, to the side, or beyond an obstruction.

<h2 id="combat-turns">
  How Do Combat Turns and Actions Work in Underkeep?
</h2>

Combat is turn-based, even though exploration takes place from a first-person perspective.

**Each character can perform one attack or use one assigned special skill per round.** Once every character has acted, the round ends automatically.

You can also select the hourglass to finish the round early. Be careful: doing so skips any party members who still have an unused action.

### Why Can't You Move After Attacking?

One of the first confusing situations is when you attack an enemy and then try to move away.

The game can prevent party movement once a hero has taken an action during the current round. You must finish or skip the remainder of that round before moving again.

This makes movement a tactical decision rather than a free escape after attacking.

If an enemy is approaching, decide whether to reposition before committing a character's action. If you start attacking first, do not expect to step backward immediately afterward.

### Does Using a Potion Consume a Combat Action?

**No. Using a potion from the inventory does not consume a character's normal action.**

This is one of the most useful rules to remember during a difficult encounter.

A wounded hero can receive a suitable potion without automatically giving up their attack or active skill for that round.

That does not mean you should spend potions whenever someone takes minor damage. It means that when a potion is genuinely needed, you do not have to choose between using it and performing the character's normal attack.

For example, if an enemy has left your frontline character dangerously low on Health, you can use a healing potion from the inventory and still take the character's available combat action.

The practical limitation is your supply of potions, not the loss of that action.

### Can You Change Party Positions During Combat?

Yes, but swapping positions consumes an action.

Moving a vulnerable character away from direct pressure may be worthwhile, particularly when an enemy is repeatedly targeting them. However, the swap has an immediate cost, so it is better to begin most encounters with a sensible formation.

Remember that not every attack is limited to the front row. Certain dangerous enemies can hit rear characters or damage multiple party members at once.

<h2 id="early-combat">
  How Do You Survive Early Fights Without Wasting Supplies?
</h2>

The first fights are a good opportunity to learn which enemies can reach you, when you need healing, and how much damage your party can deal without expensive skills.

Start by checking the enemy's position.

An enemy may be visible but too far away for a melee attack. Another may be standing in a neighboring tile or around a corner, making its position unclear from the first-person view.

Use the map to check where the enemy is relative to the party. Do not repeatedly spend attacks on a target that is not within the required range.

When enemies are close enough to attack, use the entire party deliberately rather than immediately ending the round after one or two characters act.

### Save Limited Abilities for the Right Moment

Basic attacks can often handle weaker enemies without requiring every character to use a costly ability.

Skills that consume Mana or Stamina may become more valuable later in the same encounter, especially if several enemies appear or a stronger opponent arrives.

There is a difference between conserving resources and refusing to use them. If a dangerous enemy can inflict substantial damage next round, preventing that damage with a suitable skill may be more efficient than spending several potions afterward.

Watch what each action accomplishes. A control effect that fails may leave the enemy free to act, while an attack that also deals damage can still contribute even if its additional effect does not activate.

### What Should You Do About Poison, Bleed, and Burn?

Enemies can inflict harmful status effects in addition to ordinary attack damage.

Do not treat every status effect as identical.

- **Poison:** check the affected character's condition and use a suitable Cure Poison item when continuing damage becomes dangerous.
- **Bleed:** pay attention to the character's remaining Health and whether your party has an available way to remove the effect.
- **Burn:** consider both the immediate damage and the risk of taking additional damage while the condition remains active.

Some support abilities can remove negative effects while healing, but their availability depends on the characters you created.

Do not automatically spend your rarest recovery item on a minor effect that is nearly finished. Conversely, do not ignore an active condition on a character who is already close to dying.

The objective is to preserve enough Health and recovery supplies to survive the rest of the encounter, not simply to finish every round with everyone at maximum Health.

<h2 id="rest-food">
  How Does Rest Work, and Why Is Food Important?
</h2>

**Resting consumes Food and restores Health, Stamina, and Mana to your party.**

Use the sleep icon when you need to recover. You do not need to manually feed individual heroes before resting because Food is a shared party resource.

This creates an important difference between Food and potions.

Food supports recovery between encounters. Potions are useful when you need to recover a specific resource without waiting for another rest, especially during combat.

### When Should You Rest?

Rest before continuing into a dangerous area when several party members are injured or short on combat resources.

If only one hero has lost a small amount of Health and the rest of the party is ready to fight, spending Food immediately may be unnecessary.

As characters gain larger resource pools, recovering from more substantial losses can require more Food. This makes repeatedly resting after every minor encounter less attractive than recovering when there is a meaningful need.

Keep checking your Food supply as you explore. A party with strong abilities can still struggle if it has exhausted the resource needed to restore them.

### Should You Use Food or a Potion?

The answer depends on when recovery is needed.

**Use Food through Rest** when the party needs broad recovery after an encounter.

**Use a suitable potion** when a character urgently needs Health, Mana, or Stamina and resting is not the appropriate option.

For example, a Mana potion may be more useful than another healing potion when the party's main healer is healthy but unable to cast.

Likewise, entering another difficult battle with full Health but almost no Mana or Stamina can leave a character unable to perform their intended role.

Check the resources that power your selected abilities, not just the red Health bars.

<h2 id="inventory-equipment">
  How Do Inventory, Equipment, and Gold Work?
</h2>

The party shares **Inventory, Gold, and Food**, but each character equips their own weapons, armor, and accessories.

That means equipment decisions are not simply about finding an item with a higher number.

A newly acquired weapon may have better damage but require attributes the intended character does not have. An armor piece may increase Armor while reducing Evasion, or provide resistance that is useful against particular enemies.

Inspect equipment requirements before replacing something that is already working.

### Do You Need Strength to Carry More Items?

The shared inventory does not use a conventional weight limit, so increasing Strength will not give your party more room for loot. Attribute requirements matter when a character tries to equip a weapon or piece of armor, not when the party collects it.

### What Should You Spend Gold On Early?

Gold is shared across the party, so purchases for one character reduce the amount available to everyone else.

Early on, prioritize items that solve a current problem rather than buying every apparent upgrade.

A healing potion can be more useful than a marginal equipment improvement if the party has no other reliable way to recover during a fight. On the other hand, better defensive equipment may prevent repeated damage and reduce your future need for potions.

Keep in mind that reviving a fallen hero at a Revival Statue also costs Gold.

There is no need to commit all your money to one item merely because it is available at a trader.

If you are carrying equipment you cannot use, check whether selling it will help fund supplies or upgrades you actually need.

<h2 id="map-and-secrets">
  How Do You Avoid Getting Lost or Missing Important Items?
</h2>

Underkeep's handcrafted dungeons contain hidden passages, locked chests, unusual objects, and paths that may not be accessible on your first visit.

Not every blocked passage is something you are expected to open immediately.

A useful habit is to distinguish between a place you have fully explored and a place you need to revisit later.

### Mark Locked Chests and Unfinished Paths on the Map

You can add your own notes to the map.

Open the map, select the relevant location, and enter a reminder for whatever you could not complete.

Useful notes include:

- **Lockpick chest** for a container you cannot currently open.
- **Unexplored passage** for a branch you deliberately skipped.
- **Return with key** for a locked door whose requirement you have already identified.
- **Resource deposit** for a location that requires a tool or ability you do not yet have.

This matters because the map does not automatically preserve every reason you stopped at a particular location.

Once you find the missing item or requirement, you can return directly to the marked place instead of trying to remember where you saw it.

### Should You Use Every Lockpick Immediately?

Lockpicks are **single-use items**, and you do not need a Thief in the party to use one.

That makes them worth thinking about before spending.

If a chest is optional and you do not currently have a Lockpick, mark its location and continue exploring. You can return when you find another.

If you already have a Lockpick, decide whether the current container is worth opening or whether you would rather keep the item available for a later obstacle.

Do not assume that every locked chest prevents main-story progression.

### How Can You Recognize Hidden Passages?

Pay attention to environmental clues and objects that look different from their surroundings.

Some areas reward checking unusual walls, doors, or interactive scenery. Written clues may also hint that a secret passage exists near a particular room.

When a location appears suspicious, inspect it and try the available interaction before abandoning it.

However, do not spend an unlimited amount of time attacking random walls. A clue about an inaccessible area may refer to a different entrance or an item found later.

If you can leave a map note, use it. There is no reason to memorize every unusual object in a large dungeon.

<h2 id="death-and-saving">
  What Happens When a Character Dies in Underkeep?
</h2>

A fallen character does not automatically mean that the entire party must be abandoned.

Underkeep provides **Revival Statues**, where fallen heroes can be resurrected for a fee.

There are also **Potions of Resurrection**, which can restore a fallen ally during combat.

These options have different practical uses.

A Revival Statue is a recovery option when you can reach one and pay the required cost. A Potion of Resurrection is particularly valuable during a battle because it can return an important character to the fight without leaving the encounter.

Do not assume you will always have one available. Check your current supplies before entering an unfamiliar or dangerous area.

### When Should You Save?

Save regularly, especially before:

- Entering an area where you cannot easily judge the number or strength of enemies.
- Starting a difficult fight with limited Food, Mana, or potions.
- Testing an unfamiliar puzzle that might activate enemies or traps.
- Spending a scarce resource on an optional interaction.

Saving before a difficult encounter gives you a way to reconsider your preparation rather than repeating a long section of exploration.

It is also helpful to check your equipment and active skills after loading a save. A character may be using an older weapon or have a different skill selected than you remember.
