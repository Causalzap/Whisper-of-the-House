Your first fishing trip in Nivalis Nights costs nothing. Boardwalk Addy gives you a rod at the Docks, and the nearby waterfront is enough to catch your first fish without owning a boat.

The equipment becomes more interesting when you start looking for particular species. Some fish depend on the district, time of day, and weather. The Fish Database helps narrow those conditions, while the Putter lets you reach offshore fishing spots that cannot be approached from the pavement.

You will also recover objects that are not fish. Understanding what the boat scanner is showing prevents a lot of wasted casts, particularly when a fishing trip takes you into unfamiliar water.


<h2 id="get-fishing-rod" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Where to Get Your First Fishing Rod
</h2>

Head down to the **Docks** and find **Boardwalk Addy** near the waterfront. Ask him about fishing, and he offers you his spare rod.

It is an old, rusty piece of equipment, but it works. You can equip it immediately and start fishing from the shore rather than spending your early Ramen Noir earnings on a replacement.

Addy asks you to come back once you have caught something. Make that your first objective: take the rod, fish nearby, and return to him with a successful catch.

You may receive another old rod later from **Salt Pete**. That one arrives during his maritime treasure quest, so it is not necessary to wait for Pete or buy additional equipment before trying ordinary fishing.

As you collect more rods, compare their actual bonuses rather than replacing the first one automatically. Casting range and luck are useful considerations, but neither will compensate for searching the wrong district when a species has particular conditions.


<h2 id="how-to-fish" className="scroll-mt-24">
  How to Catch Fish From Shore or From Your Boat
</h2>

Open your inventory and equip the fishing rod. Move close enough to the water to cast, aim into the fishing area, and wait for a bite before reeling in the catch.

You can fish from the **shoreline** or while standing on your **boat**. The fishing action is the same; what changes is which water you can reach.

At the beginning, a convenient patch of water near the Docks is perfectly adequate. Once you are looking for a missing species, location matters much more. Repeatedly casting where you have already caught common fish will not necessarily reveal something new.

Another surprise is that a successful cast does not always produce a fish. You can pull up objects such as antique lamps, lost bracelets, old computer equipment, and other pieces of submerged salvage.

These finds are part of fishing, not evidence that your rod has stopped working. Some objects have resale value, while others may be useful for a quest. Check unfamiliar items before deciding what to keep.

The animated fish you can see swimming nearby are useful visual clues, but they do not tell you every condition governing a catch. A familiar fishing spot can still give different results as you explore additional species and locations.

For a first session, catching something and returning to Addy is enough. The more deliberate search begins once you start using the Fish Database.


<h2 id="get-the-boat" className="scroll-mt-24">
  How to Unlock the Putter for Offshore Fishing
</h2>

You do not need the Putter to start fishing, but shore access becomes restrictive once you want to investigate distant water, scanner signals, or locations separated from the mainland.

The boat is introduced through **Salt Pete at the Docks**. Speak to him about taking possession of the vessel and continue the maritime registration process. Ava handles the registration once your claim is established, allowing you to board the **Putter** as its captain.

Once the boat is available, you can travel between accessible waterways, approach offshore fishing spots, and make use of its scanner while searching.

Fuel is the practical limit on longer outings. Check your supply before crossing to a distant district, particularly when the route may require searching around obstacles or visiting more than one fishing area.

The Putter is worth unlocking before you start chasing specific offshore catches. It is not worth delaying all fishing progress until you own it: your starter rod already works from the Docks, and those early catches contribute to your Fishing skill and Fish Database.


<h2 id="fish-detector-database" className="scroll-mt-24">
  How to Find Specific Fish With the Detector and Database
</h2>

The Fish Detector and Fish Database solve different parts of the search.

The **Fish Detector** helps you locate fish activity. It is useful for deciding where to cast instead of guessing at empty stretches of water.

The **Fish Database** helps you decide *which water to visit*. Its entries reveal information about discovered species, including their locations and relevant time or weather conditions.

The Database becomes more useful as you catch different species. Fish are organized into tiers, and completing the available catches in a tier helps reveal information needed for later ones.

That creates a much more manageable progression than sailing around Nivalis hoping an unknown fish will appear.

When you want a particular species, first inspect its Database entry. Take note of the listed locations, then check any available time or weather details. Travel to one of those areas and use the Detector to identify where fish are concentrated.

A species appearing in the Database is not a promise that every cast in its district will catch it. The named location narrows the search; you still need an appropriate fishing spot and, where applicable, the right conditions.

For fish whose useful details remain unknown, continue catching the species you can already identify. Those catches improve your collection and can open up better information for what comes next.


<h2 id="boat-scanner" className="scroll-mt-24">
  What the Boat Scanner Colors and Yellow Pulses Mean
</h2>

Once you are offshore, the Putter's scanner provides more information than the fish models visible in the water.

Fishing activity appears through signals that help distinguish different types of fish and the density of their schools. These are the signals to follow when your goal is catching a species for the Database, your restaurant, or an achievement.

**Yellow pulses indicate something different: submerged objects with unusual signatures.**

A yellow signal is not automatically a valuable treasure. Some locations contain ordinary salvage, and a nearby fishing spot may produce fish rather than the object you are trying to recover.

For that reason, pay attention to both the scanner and your current objective. Finding a submerged item does not necessarily mean you have found the one requested by a character.

This becomes particularly important during Salt Pete's treasure hunt. His three missing android parts are recovered from separate waters near the Oil Rig, Calypso Island, and the Sewers. For ordinary fishing, there is no need to stop at every yellow pulse. Concentrate on fish signals when you are trying to discover another species, and investigate unusual objects when you have a reason to search for them.

**Stickleback** is a useful early species because its Database information identifies several locations:

- **Mountain Town**

**Pike** and **Burbot** can also appear during early fishing trips. You may encounter them while exploring the waterways opened through the first boat objectives.

When one of these fish appears, record the catch and inspect its Database information before planning the next trip. A fish found near one district does not mean that every species you have seen will share that location.

Pike also becomes useful if you want to prepare fish dishes at your restaurant. Catching enough to experiment with a recipe can be worthwhile, provided you can get the fish into storage before it spoils.

The early objective is to discover several species and learn where they occur. There is little benefit to filling your entire inventory with the same common fish while the remaining Database entries are still unexplored.


<h2 id="fishing-levels" className="scroll-mt-24">
  What Changes When Your Fishing Level Increases?
</h2>

Fishing experience comes from making catches, including the fish and submerged objects you recover while exploring.

An important early milestone is **Fishing Level 3**, which increases your scanner radius.

A wider scanning range means you can identify activity over a larger stretch of water without steering as close to every possible fishing location. This becomes useful around offshore districts, where a small change in direction can otherwise leave you searching the wrong section of sea.

Fishing progression also provides bonuses that improve your ability to locate and catch fish.

Early on, improving your Fishing skill through normal catches is a sensible use of time. It helps you reach later fishing spots with better search capabilities instead of relying entirely on new equipment.

When a particular species is missing, though, check the Database before assuming that another Fishing level or a better rod will solve it. A fish tied to a different district or set of conditions still requires you to go where it can be caught.


<h2 id="fishing-for-money" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Is Fishing Worth Doing for Money and Restaurant Ingredients?
</h2>

Fishing provides an activity outside Ramen Noir that can return both usable fish and sellable objects. Lost jewelry, old equipment, and other salvage may be worth collecting during a trip that you were already making for a new species.

The real financial advantage is that fishing does not require you to purchase another batch of restaurant ingredients before every catch.

Fish can also supply dishes on your menu. Pike is an early example: collecting it yourself can reduce how much of that ingredient you need to purchase, although the benefit depends on whether the restaurant is actually selling the relevant dish.

**Do not overlook spoilage.** Fish carried around in your inventory can deteriorate as time passes. A productive night of fishing can lose much of its usefulness if the catch remains in your pockets instead of reaching suitable storage.

Before heading out specifically to supply your restaurant, check three things: whether a current menu item uses the fish, whether you have room to store it, and whether you will be able to return before the catch becomes unusable.

Catching large quantities simply because a fishing spot is productive can create more inventory than your restaurant needs.

Fishing is also not a permanent solution to an unprofitable venue. When Ramen Noir keeps losing money because of staffing, menu costs, or excessive restocking, those operating expenses still need attention. <h2 id="fishing-trip-planning" className="scroll-mt-24">
  What to Check Before a Longer Fishing Trip
</h2>

A short session at the Docks needs little preparation. A boat trip to a distant fishing location is different, especially when the return journey crosses several waterways.

It helps to decide which species or fishing area you are visiting before leaving. Inspect the Database, confirm that you have your rod, and check the Putter's fuel. If you are collecting fish for a restaurant, plan where the catch will be stored afterward.

Time matters as well. The city's **2:00 AM curfew** can interrupt a late-night trip when you are still far from somewhere safe.

As the clock approaches curfew, consider whether the next scanner signal is worth another detour. You may have enough time to finish a nearby catch, but continuing toward a distant district can leave you travelling under restrictions.

The <Link href="/nivalis-nights/curfew">curfew walkthrough</Link> explains what changes at night and how to handle being outside when CorpSec restrictions begin.

You can make offshore trips more productive by combining goals along the same water route. Catch a species you have not recorded, investigate a fishing area that matches your Database information, and collect useful salvage when it is nearby.

Once your fuel or storage starts becoming a concern, returning with what you already caught is often a better result than stretching the trip for one more uncertain signal.


<h2 id="achievement-fish" className="scroll-mt-24">
  How to Work Toward All 24 Fishing Achievements
</h2>

Nivalis Nights has **24 individual fish-species achievements**. Each one requires you to catch the named species, so catching another Pike does not replace a missing species elsewhere in the collection.

The most efficient way to make progress is through the Fish Database. Catch what is available in your current areas, use the information you unlock to identify new locations and conditions, and move into later tiers as your collection expands.

There is no need to finish every species before exploring other parts of the city. Fishing can progress alongside boat travel, restaurant supply runs, and quests that take you near new water.

When you want to see exactly which species you still need, use the <Link href="/nivalis-nights/achievements">120-achievement checklist</Link> to track your remaining fishing objectives.

The Fish Database tells you where to concentrate your next fishing trip. The achievement checklist tells you which catches are still unfinished.
