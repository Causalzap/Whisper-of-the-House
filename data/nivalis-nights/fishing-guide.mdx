Fishing starts with a free rod at the Docks, so there is no reason to spend
early restaurant cash on equipment just to make the first catch.

The system gets much deeper once the boat opens. Fish can depend on location,
time, and weather, the Database gradually reveals better information, the boat
scanner separates fishing activity from submerged objects, and Fishing levels
improve how efficiently you can search the water.


<h2
  id="get-fishing-rod"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Get the Free Fishing Rod From Boardwalk Addy
</h2>

Head to the **Docks** and find **Boardwalk Addy** near the waterfront.

Ask him about fishing and he offers his spare rod. The pole is old and rusty,
but it is enough to start the entire fishing progression.

Addy asks you to return after catching something. Do that before worrying about
better rods; the starter pole already lets you learn the casting loop and begin
filling the Fish Database.


<h2
  id="how-to-fish"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Equip the Rod, Cast, and Wait for a Bite
</h2>

Equip the fishing rod from your inventory, move to the shoreline, cast the
bait, and wait for the catch indicator before pulling it back.

Fishing works both **from shore and from a boat**, so the Putter is not required
for the first few catches.

Not every successful cast brings up a fish. Early fishing can also recover
things such as:

- antique lamps;
- old computer technology;
- lost jewelry;
- other submerged objects.

That does not mean the fishing spot is broken. Fishing and underwater recovery
share part of the same activity, and unusual objects can still be useful or
valuable elsewhere in Nivalis.

Keep unfamiliar finds until you know whether a buyer, quest, or later activity
needs them instead of immediately discarding everything that is not a fish.

Visible fish swimming through the water are also not a reliable substitute for
the fishing tools. Once you start looking for a particular species, follow the
Detector and Database rather than casting at every fish model you can see.


<h2
  id="get-the-boat"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Unlock the Putter for Offshore Fishing
</h2>

Shore fishing is enough to begin, but many useful fishing areas become easier
to reach once you have the **Putter**.

At the Docks, talk to **Salt Pete** and continue the conversation about the
boat. The next captain can claim the vessel through the maritime registration
process, after which Ava registers the claim and you can board the Putter.

The boat matters beyond fishing as well because it opens water routes and
locations that are not practical to reach from land. For fishing specifically,
its biggest advantage is access to scanner-marked activity away from the
waterfront.


<h2
  id="fish-detector-database"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Use the Fish Detector and Database for Specific Species
</h2>

Random casting is fine when almost every catch is new. It becomes inefficient
once you are looking for one missing species.

The fishing system gives you two important tools.

**Fish Detector** helps locate concentrations of fish.

**Fish Database** records information about discovered species, including
**where, when, and in what weather** they can be found.

  <Image

  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">
    The Fish Database becomes the main reference once a species has specific location, time, or weather conditions.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

The Database also has tier progression. Catching the fish available in one tier
reveals more useful information for later entries, so a higher-tier unknown is
not always something that needs to be solved immediately.

Finish the species your current Database can already identify, then use the new
information it reveals to move into the next set of catches.


<h2
  id="boat-scanner"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Read the Boat Scanner Before You Start Casting
</h2>

Once you are offshore, use the boat scanner instead of circling the water and
guessing.

Fishing signals can indicate **different fish types and fish density**, helping
you decide where to stop and cast.

**Yellow pulses are different.**

They indicate **submerged objects with unusual signatures**, not simply a
better school of fish.

That distinction saves time when an objective has a specific goal. Stay on the
fishing signals when you need a species. Follow yellow pulses when you are
looking for a submerged object or treasure target.


<h2
  id="first-fish"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Stickleback Is an Easy Early Species to Confirm
</h2>

**Stickleback** is one of the first species with useful location information in
the Database.

Its entry points toward:

- **Docks**
- **Mountain Town**
- **Sewers**

  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">

The same early fishing progression can also produce **Burbot** and **Pike**, so
you do not need access to every late-game district before the collection begins
moving.

One successful catch in one spot should not be treated as proof that every
early fish shares the same location. The Database exists precisely because
species can differ by area and other conditions.

Catch the fish once, unlock its information, then use the Database entry rather
than relying on a remembered fishing spot.


<h2
  id="fishing-levels"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Fishing Levels Improve How You Search the Water
</h2>

Fishing experience increases as you keep catching fish and other targets.

At **Fishing Level 3**, the **scanner radius increases**.

That makes the starter rod more useful than it first appears. Early casts are
not only adding fish to the Database; they are also improving the search tools
used for later species.

Better rods exist, but information is the more important early bottleneck. A
stronger rod does not solve a missing fish if you still do not know its
district, time, weather, or fishing area.


<h2
  id="fishing-for-money"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Fishing Can Provide Cash Outside Ramen Noir
</h2>

Fishing is useful when the restaurant is low on operating cash because catches
can produce fish and recoverable objects without requiring another ingredient
purchase first.

During one difficult business stretch, repeated casts produced lost jewelry,
old technology, and other finds alongside fish. That made fishing a practical
way to bring outside value back into a struggling run.

It is still a recovery option rather than a substitute for fixing a restaurant
that repeatedly loses money. If Ramen Noir keeps collapsing because of menu
costs, payroll, or automatic restocking, use the
<Link href="/nivalis-nights/business-guide">business guide</Link>
to fix the underlying problem.


<h2
  id="fishing-trip-planning"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Plan Longer Fishing Trips Before Leaving the Docks
</h2>

Once the Putter is available, it is easy to keep following another fishing
signal and lose track of how far you have traveled or how late it has become.

If the clock is approaching **2:00 AM**, decide where the night is ending before
starting another offshore run. The
<Link href="/nivalis-nights/curfew">curfew guide</Link>
covers shelters, CorpSec surveillance, and what to do if you are still outside
when the restriction begins.

For ordinary fishing progression, the better use of a long trip is to combine
several goals: reveal another Database entry, check a new fishing area, and
investigate scanner signals that are already on the route.


<h2
  id="achievement-fish"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Catch New Species as the Database Expands
</h2>

Fishing has **24 individual species achievements**, so unfamiliar catches are
more useful for completion than repeatedly catching something already recorded.

There is no need to memorize the entire species list while fishing. The
<Link href="/nivalis-nights/achievements">120-achievement tracker</Link>
shows which fish achievements are still unfinished.

For normal progression, the Database already gives you the cleaner loop:

**catch a new species → unlock its information → finish the current tier →
use the new clues to hunt the next one**

That keeps fishing useful for exploration, boat progression, and outside cash
without turning every trip onto the water into a full collectible sweep.
