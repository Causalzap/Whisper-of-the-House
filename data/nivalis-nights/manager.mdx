A Manager removes one of Ramen Noir's biggest interruptions: repeated grocery
runs.

Once the role is working, routine ingredient restocks and staff motivation no
longer require you to stay in Meridian Market. The tradeoff is that those
grocery purchases now happen automatically, so a restaurant with very little
working cash can spend itself into an unpaid-wage problem while you are away.

The useful breakpoint is therefore not simply "Manager unlocked." It is
"Manager unlocked, successfully restocking, and the restaurant can still pay
its staff afterward."

<h2
  id="unlock-manager"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  How to Reach Venue Level 3 and Unlock the Manager
</h2>

Ramen Noir levels up by building its **popularity through serving customers**.

The first major upgrade is Tier 2. After reaching it, the restaurant continues
giving you service objectives, including **Serve 20 Guests**. Keep Ramen Noir
stocked and operating while you work through that customer progression.

You do not need to buy a special Manager upgrade, visit another district, or
complete a separate Manager quest.

When the venue reaches **Level 3**, the upgrade appears automatically and Ava
explains that Managers are now available.

At Level 3, Ramen Noir also expands to:

- **20 tables**
- **10 staff**
- **20 menu items**
- **1.4 maximum price multiplier**

Those larger limits are useful, but you do not need to fill them before hiring
a Manager. The important requirement is reaching the third venue level.

If the upgrade is taking longer than expected, keep serving customers rather
than spending money just to fill every new table, staff slot, or menu slot.

For the broader restaurant progression, pricing, staff, debt, and profitability
decisions, use the

<h2

  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  How to Hire or Assign a Manager
</h2>

Managers use the same staff system as cooks, waiters, and cleaners. Open the
staff controls and inspect each worker's role skills before assigning the job.

One hiring screen showed **Dion with Manager skill 3**, making her a clear
candidate for the role.

  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">

An existing employee can also be reassigned when they have suitable management
skill, but their previous job still needs coverage. Moving a strong cook into
management while the kitchen is already overloaded can solve grocery shopping
and immediately create a meal-service bottleneck instead.

Shift length and hourly wage continue to affect employee happiness after the
assignment, so make sure the Manager is actually scheduled during the hours
you expect the restaurant to operate without you.


<h2
  id="manager-automation"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  What Does a Manager Actually Automate?
</h2>

The role handles a specific part of restaurant operation rather than every
decision associated with Ramen Noir.

| Manager handles | You still handle |
| --- | --- |
| Grocery shopping | Equipment purchases |
| Staff motivation | Furniture purchases |
| Routine ingredient restocking | Menu decisions |
| Day-to-day supply upkeep | Expansion and staffing decisions |

The biggest change is automatic grocery shopping. Before Level 3, emptying an
ingredient forces another trip to the vendors. Once the Manager is active,
those routine purchases can happen while you are somewhere else.

After Dion was hired, Ramen Noir produced an **Ingredients Restocked**
notification while she was working.

whether the restaurant's current menu, staffing, or expansion plan is
financially sensible.


<h2
  id="manager-cash-drain"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Why Can a Manager Leave Staff Unpaid?
</h2>

Automatic restocking still spends the restaurant's real cash.

During one later service cycle, the restaurant was operating with Manager
automation while the available balance was already thin. Ingredient purchases
continued, and staff were showing as unpaid shortly afterward.

The failure state is straightforward:

**low cash → automatic grocery purchase → payroll arrives → not enough money
for wages**

That is the main risk of hiring a Manager too early. The role fixes the
physical restocking work, but it does not reserve payroll money before buying
ingredients.

A large or ingredient-heavy menu can make the effect more noticeable because
more different supplies may need replenishing. The broader decision about menu
size and restaurant profitability belongs to the
<Link href="/nivalis-nights/business-guide">Ramen Noir business setup</Link>;
for Manager use, the key point is simply that every automatic restock needs
working cash behind it.


<h2
  id="when-to-leave"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  When Is Ramen Noir Ready to Be Left With a Manager?
</h2>

Venue Level 3 tells you when the role is available. It does not prove the
restaurant can already afford unattended automation.

Before leaving Meridian Market for a long trip, let Ramen Noir complete a
normal operating cycle with the Manager active. You want to see all of the
following happen without the balance collapsing:

- ingredients are automatically restocked;
- staff are paid;
- customers continue being served;
- usable cash remains after those expenses.

There is no single universal cash number because the cost depends on the active
menu, wages, staff count, and ingredient consumption. The restaurant's own
cycle is a better test than an arbitrary reserve.

A particularly useful checkpoint is the first successful **Ingredients
Restocked** notification. If the next payroll also clears and Ramen Noir still
has working cash, leaving for the Docks, greenhouse, or another district is
much safer.

If one grocery purchase immediately leaves the venue near zero, stay nearby and
rebuild the balance before relying on automation.


<h2
  id="manager-not-restocking"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  What to Check if the Manager Is Not Restocking
</h2>

When ingredients stay empty after hiring someone, check the staff setup before
assuming the system is broken.

First, confirm that the employee is actually assigned the **Manager** role.
Hiring a worker and assigning their job are separate steps.

Then check the worker's Manager skill and schedule. A skilled cook or waiter
does not automatically become effective management, and a Manager who is not
working during the current service hours cannot cover the period you expected.

Finally, check the restaurant balance. Automatic grocery shopping cannot occur
normally when there is no money available to buy the missing ingredients.

The successful state is easy to recognize: the Manager is assigned and working,
**Ingredients Restocked** appears, and service continues without another manual
market run.


<h2
  id="recover-manager-cash"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  How to Recover if Automatic Restocking Drains the Balance
</h2>

When Manager spending leaves staff unpaid, stop adding new costs until the
restaurant can complete another grocery-and-payroll cycle.

Reduce any unnecessary ingredient pressure, stay at Ramen Noir long enough to
help service recover, and wait for the balance to start rising again. The goal
is not to remove the Manager permanently; it is to restore enough working cash
for automatic purchases and payroll to coexist.

If the venue has fallen too low to restart normal service,
<Link href="/nivalis-nights/fishing-guide">fishing can provide outside cash and sellable finds</Link>
without requiring another restaurant ingredient purchase first.

Once Ramen Noir completes an automatic restock, pays the next round of wages,
and still has working cash left, leave the Manager running while you travel.

If the restock leaves staff unpaid or pushes the venue back near zero, stay at
Ramen Noir and rebuild the cash buffer before testing automation again.
