Farming begins with everything needed for a first harvest already close together:
Clen provides the Onion Seeds, Thaddius's old greenhouse provides the space, and
the existing Root Farming Module handles the opening crop.

That makes the early route much cheaper than it first appears. New modules,
extra greenhouses, and environmental controls all matter later, but none of
them are required before the first onions are growing.


<h2
  id="unlock-farming"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Talk to Clen at the Docks to Unlock Farming
</h2>

The farming sequence starts with **Clen at the Docks**. She knew Thaddeus and
directs you toward his old greenhouse after introducing the crop system.

For the first planting, Clen gives you **Onion Seeds**.

**seed + the correct farming module + time**

Onions are a practical starting choice because they can already feed back into
Ramen Noir instead of existing only to raise Farming progress.

Plant the onions, let the existing equipment work, and use the starter

spending on more capacity.


<h2
  id="harvest-and-replant"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Harvest, Replant, and Keep the Module Working
</h2>

Crops grow while you are doing other things around Nivalis, so farming works as
a return loop rather than an activity that needs constant attention.

Clen recommends checking the greenhouse regularly to harvest finished crops,
plant the next batch, and inspect the growing conditions. Once the onions are
ready, collect them and replant instead of leaving the Root Farming Module
empty.

The basic cycle is:

**plant → leave → return → harvest → replant**

Crops that match the restaurant menu can replace part of what you would
otherwise purchase from vendors. When ingredient spending starts affecting the
venue's daily result, the
<Link href="/nivalis-nights/business-guide">Ramen Noir business guide</Link>
covers the restaurant side of that decision.


<h2
  id="buy-seeds"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Buy New Seeds Only When You Can Use Them
</h2>

Clen supplies the opening seeds. More crop types are sold by the **Seeds
Peddler** around the Docks, with **Tomato Seeds** appearing as one early
option.

Seed purchases can become expensive surprisingly quickly. In one early run,
buying tomatoes left only about **10 Lims** available. That purchase makes far
less sense when the matching crop cannot yet be planted or has no immediate use.

Before adding another seed type, check two things: whether the greenhouse has
the correct module and whether the crop solves a current production need.
Restaurant ingredients are the easiest early purchases to justify; additional
varieties become more useful once the basic supply chain is already stable.

>
  Match Each Crop to the Correct Farming Module
</h2>

Different crop families use different farming modules. Clen also explains that
existing modules can be upgraded for **faster growth** and **better yields**,
while new farming equipment can be bought from hardware vendors.

Check the module requirement before buying several packets of a new seed. Seeds
that cannot be planted are only tying up cash in inventory.

The same rule helps with module purchases. Empty greenhouse space alone is not
a reason to install another module; add one when it unlocks a crop family you
actually intend to grow.

Clen adds an important limitation: **different crops have different needs, but
the settings apply to the whole greenhouse**.

Once several crop types share the space, environmental compatibility therefore
starts to matter alongside raw module capacity. Filling every available module
with unrelated crops can make the greenhouse harder to tune when their
preferred conditions differ.

The interface also shows later farming requirements beyond Level 4, so the
opening harvest does not require solving every advanced control immediately.

into a checklist.


<h2
  id="what-to-grow-first"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  What Should You Grow First?
</h2>

Start with crops that solve an existing supply need.

**Onions** are the obvious opening choice because Clen supplies the seeds, the
starter greenhouse has a compatible Root Farming Module, and Ramen Noir can
already use the harvest.

**Potatoes** and **tomatoes** become sensible additions once the matching seeds
and equipment are available.

The useful distinction is between production and variety. A restaurant that
keeps consuming onions gains more from another onion harvest than from an
unfamiliar crop sitting unused. Once the recurring supply is comfortable, new
crop types become easier to justify without weakening what the greenhouse is
already doing well.

The installed equipment can make the property useful later, but extra space has
little value while Thaddius's greenhouse still has idle capacity.

A second greenhouse becomes easier to justify when the starter modules are
regularly occupied, several required crop types are competing for the same
space, or additional production would solve a supply shortage that the first
greenhouse cannot keep up with.

Until one of those conditions exists, the starter space continues to advance
Farming without adding another property commitment.


<h2
  id="farming-priority"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  What to Prioritize After the First Harvest
</h2>

Keep the first greenhouse productive before making it larger.

Replant the onions, add seeds for crops you can actually use, and install new
modules when they unlock production you need. Farming Level 4 is the first
major mechanical breakpoint because humidity, temperature, and light begin to
change how mixed crops should be organized.

A second greenhouse comes after the starter space becomes a genuine production
limit. Until then, the strongest upgrades are the ones that improve something
already working: a useful new crop, a better module, a higher yield, or more
reliable supply.
