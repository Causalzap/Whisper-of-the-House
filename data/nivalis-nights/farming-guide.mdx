The first greenhouse in Nivalis Nights is already equipped for growing food. You do not need to buy a new farming module, rent Greenhouse 3B, or unlock advanced environmental controls before harvesting your first onions.

Clen provides the opening seeds, and the Root Farming Module inside Thaddius's old greenhouse can grow them immediately. After that first harvest, she introduces the next crop, while repeated planting gradually increases your Farming skill and individual crop levels.

What takes more thought is deciding how to use the space. Different crops need compatible equipment, humidity and temperature settings apply across an entire greenhouse, and adding another property only helps when you have enough production to justify it.


<h2 id="unlock-farming" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Where to Find Clen and Unlock Farming
</h2>

The farming storyline begins when Ramen Noir's ingredient costs become a concern. During the restaurant progression, you are directed to **Clen at the Docks** as someone who can help you grow vegetables instead of buying all of them.

Find her around the Docks greenhouse area and speak to her about growing food. Clen knew Thaddius and offers to help you get started at his old greenhouse.

For your first crop, she provides **Onion Seeds**.

She explains that growing a crop requires a seed, a compatible farming module, and enough time for the plant to mature.

Onions are a sensible starting point because they already have a use in Ramen Noir. More importantly, the required equipment is waiting inside the first greenhouse, so you can learn how planting works without spending money on an unfamiliar module.

Clen sends you uphill to Thaddius's old growing space. Follow that instruction before visiting equipment vendors or looking at additional greenhouse rentals.


<h2 id="first-greenhouse" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Find Thaddius's Greenhouse and Plant Your First Onions
</h2>

From the Docks, head up toward the nearby greenhouses and enter **Thaddius's old greenhouse**.

The existing equipment includes a **Root Farming Module**, which supports the Onion Seeds you received from Clen.

Interact with the module and use the seeds to begin growing the crop.

The distinction between a seed and its growing equipment matters. A seed packet does not create a farming slot by itself; the greenhouse must contain a module that supports that crop.

Once the onions are planted, you can leave the greenhouse. They continue developing while you work elsewhere in Nivalis.

There is little benefit to standing beside the module waiting for the harvest. Use the growing period to handle another task at the Docks or return to Ramen Noir.

When you come back, inspect the module rather than assuming the same amount of time is required for every crop. Later plants and upgraded equipment can have different growing characteristics.

The greenhouse system eventually supports much more than ordinary vegetables, including mushrooms, cheese production, and printed meat. Those possibilities do not change the first objective: successfully harvest the onions already growing in your starter module.


<h2 id="harvest-and-replant" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Harvest Your Onions, Return to Clen, and Grow Potatoes
</h2>

After the onions have finished growing, return to the Root Farming Module and collect the harvest.

**Do not overlook the conversation with Clen after your first successful harvest.**

She asks how the onions turned out and explains why it is worth checking the greenhouse regularly. This is also where you learn more about the environmental settings that affect growing conditions.

Once that conversation is complete, the next farming objective is to **plant and harvest potatoes**.

This is an important distinction if your journal still shows a farming task after you have already grown onions. Repeatedly harvesting more onions will not complete an objective that now specifically asks for potatoes.

You can obtain Potato Seeds from a seed vendor near the Docks.

Potatoes are another root crop, so the Root Farming Module can be used for them as well. If your existing module is still occupied, finish the current crop or use an additional compatible module if you already have one available.

There is no need to install a completely different crop system just to grow potatoes.

Clen encourages you to return to the greenhouse regularly to inspect, harvest, and plant new crops. Farming continues while you are away, so a visit between restaurant shifts or other errands is usually more practical than staying inside the greenhouse.

After harvesting potatoes, inspect the journal again before deciding what to grow next. Additional farming progression and Clen's conversations may introduce further objectives.

Your harvested vegetables also have practical uses outside the greenhouse. If you intend to supply Ramen Noir, check whether the current menu actually consumes the ingredient before committing most of your available space to producing it.


<h2 id="buy-seeds" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Where to Buy Onion, Potato, and Tomato Seeds
</h2>

Clen provides the first Onion Seeds, but later planting requires additional seed supplies.

The **Seeds Peddler near the Docks** sells seeds for other crops, including potatoes and tomatoes. Check this vendor when the next farming objective asks for something you do not already have.

Before buying several packets, inspect your greenhouse equipment and the crop you intend to plant. There are two different reasons a purchase might not help immediately.

The first is a missing module. If the seed requires equipment your greenhouse does not contain, you cannot plant it simply because the packet is in your inventory.

The second is limited working cash. Seeds may seem inexpensive individually, but a large purchase can still leave too little money for other needs. In one early farming session, buying Tomato Seeds reduced the available balance to roughly **10 Lims**.

That is a poor position to be in when you still have restaurant ingredients or wages to cover.

Buying a small quantity for the next objective is usually enough while you are learning how each crop works. Once you know which ingredients are being used regularly, larger purchases become easier to justify.

Potatoes are especially useful early because the next farming objective specifically requires growing and harvesting them. Tomatoes become a more attractive addition when you have both compatible growing capacity and a reason to use the resulting harvest.


<h2 id="farming-modules" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Which Farming Modules Do Different Crops Need?
</h2>

The greenhouse uses **farming modules** to produce different types of ingredients. A module must support the crop or product you want to grow.

Your first example is straightforward: Onion Seeds work with the Root Farming Module. Potatoes can also use root-growing equipment.

As farming expands, you can install modules for other kinds of production. The greenhouse interface includes options for vegetables, mushrooms, cheese, and printed meat.

You can purchase additional farming equipment from hardware vendors, and existing modules can be upgraded to improve growth speed or yield.

Those are two different uses of your money.

An additional module expands the crops you can grow or the amount of simultaneous production you can maintain. Upgrading an existing module improves equipment that is already producing something useful.

The second option is worth considering when the greenhouse has enough crop variety but cannot produce a particular ingredient quickly enough.

For example, replacing an idle section of the greenhouse with another unfamiliar module will not help much when your actual problem is that a frequently used root crop takes too long to grow.

On the other hand, improving the Root Farming Module does not unlock a different crop family if the required module type is missing.

Check what the next crop requires, inspect the equipment already installed, and choose between upgrading existing production and adding a new capability.

You can make that decision without purchasing every module as soon as it becomes available.


<h2 id="environment-controls" className="scroll-mt-24">
  When Do Humidity, Temperature, and Light Controls Unlock?
</h2>

The greenhouse displays **humidity, temperature, and light** settings early in the farming progression. However, some controls remain unavailable until your Farming skill increases.

The first important requirement shown in the interface is **Farming Level 4**.

Clen explains a detail that becomes increasingly important as you add crops:

**Different plants can prefer different growing conditions, but the environmental settings apply to the entire greenhouse.**

You cannot assume that every module has a completely independent climate.

That matters when you begin mixing crops with different requirements in one property. Improving the conditions for one plant may not produce equally good results for another.

Before Level 4, focus on planting, harvesting, and gaining Farming experience. Your first onion and potato crops do not require advanced environmental adjustments to begin growing.

Once the controls become available, check the needs of the crops currently installed before changing the greenhouse environment. A setting that helps one crop should not automatically be treated as optimal for everything sharing the same space.

Keeping plants with compatible growing needs together becomes more useful as production expands.

The interface displays further Farming-level requirements beyond the first environmental controls. You do not need to unlock all of them before building a productive starter greenhouse.


<h2 id="crop-levels" className="scroll-mt-24">
  How Crop Levels and Yield Bonuses Work
</h2>

Farming progression is not limited to the overall Farming skill.

Individual crops can improve as you continue producing them. This makes repeated harvests valuable even when you are growing an ingredient you have already discovered.

For example, onion production can reach **Crop Level 3**, where a **Yield Bonus** becomes available.

That changes the value of a well-used module. A crop that was initially worth growing for one restaurant recipe can become more productive as you continue harvesting it.

There is a tradeoff between growing familiar ingredients and experimenting with new ones.

Repeated onion harvests advance onion production and can improve its yield. Introducing a different crop lets you discover another production option and may satisfy a new farming objective, but it uses time and space that could otherwise support the onions.

Neither choice is automatically better.

When the restaurant needs a reliable ingredient supply, continuing to grow a useful crop makes sense. When existing ingredients are comfortably stocked, spare growing capacity is a good opportunity to experiment with another crop.

Individual crop bonuses can improve production without requiring you to rent a second greenhouse immediately.


<h2 id="what-to-grow-first" className="scroll-mt-24">
  What Should You Grow First: Onions, Potatoes, or Tomatoes?
</h2>

The first crop choices serve different purposes.

**Onions** are the easiest starting point. Clen supplies the seeds, the old greenhouse already has the correct module, and the harvest can be used in Ramen Noir. You also gain experience toward environmental controls and future module improvements.

**Potatoes** should come next when the journal asks you to plant and harvest them. They use root-growing equipment, so the transition does not require a completely different greenhouse setup.

**Tomatoes** are a reasonable additional crop once you have suitable equipment and a practical use for the harvest. Buying a large supply of Tomato Seeds before confirming those requirements is an unnecessary early expense.

I would prioritize completing the onion and potato objectives before committing most of the greenhouse to optional production.

After that, the useful crop depends on what you are trying to accomplish.

If Ramen Noir is consuming a particular vegetable every day, growing it yourself may reduce how often you need to purchase that ingredient. If the restaurant is already well supplied, producing a different crop can help advance farming progress and make better use of an unused module.

The restaurant's actual ingredient consumption matters more than which plant looks most valuable in isolation. You can use the <Link href="/nivalis-nights/business-guide">Ramen Noir business and menu walkthrough</Link> to compare the dishes customers are ordering and the recurring ingredients those dishes require.

A greenhouse full of produce that nobody needs is less useful than a smaller one regularly supplying ingredients that are being consumed.


<h2 id="greenhouse-3b" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Is Greenhouse 3B Worth Renting?
</h2>

Greenhouse 3B is one of the additional properties available around the Docks. You can inspect its rental listing while exploring the greenhouse area, even though Thaddius's original greenhouse is enough to begin farming.

The displayed Greenhouse 3B listing includes:

- **5,298.80 Lims** in current equipment value
- **10.00 Lims** in daily rent
- Multiple farming modules already installed

The equipment value is important because the property already contains production capacity. You should not treat that displayed value as the daily rent or assume it represents the exact amount you must pay upfront.

The property becomes attractive when the installed modules would save you from purchasing equivalent equipment elsewhere.

More space also allows you to keep separate crop groups when one greenhouse is becoming difficult to manage. That can help once you are growing several ingredients with different environmental needs.

There is no advantage to taking on another rental simply because it is available. If your existing Root Farming Module still spends much of its time empty, additional space will probably remain unused as well.

Before leasing Greenhouse 3B, examine the modules it contains and compare them with the crops you currently need. Equipment that supports useful production has value; equipment you cannot use yet is less compelling.

**Three additional greenhouses have also been added in the Industrial District.** That gives you more locations to investigate as farming expands, rather than making Greenhouse 3B the only possible next step.

The same decision applies to every additional greenhouse: look at the installed equipment, the crop types you want to produce, and the cost of keeping another property.

A second greenhouse makes the most sense when your first one is regularly producing at capacity or when separating crops would solve a genuine growing-condition problem.


<h2 id="farming-priority" className="scroll-mt-24">
  What to Do After Your First Successful Harvest
</h2>

The first onions establish that the greenhouse works. From there, the next farming milestones are about making that small setup more productive and expanding into crops you can use.

A practical order is:

1. **Return to Clen after harvesting onions.** Continue the farming conversation and take the potato-growing objective.
2. **Plant and harvest potatoes.** Use compatible root-growing equipment rather than purchasing an unrelated module.
3. **Keep useful crops growing.** Repeated harvests improve Farming experience and individual crop progression.
4. **Reach Farming Level 4.** Begin managing humidity, temperature, and light once those controls become available.
5. **Add equipment when production needs it.** Upgrade modules for stronger output or install new types to support different crops.

As you grow more ingredients, check whether the current greenhouse is struggling because it lacks space, lacks a particular module, or needs better environmental settings. Those problems have different solutions and do not all justify another rental.

The most useful greenhouse is not necessarily the largest one. It is the one producing ingredients you can harvest, use, and replace consistently.

Once the starter greenhouse is regularly occupied and the crop types you need can no longer fit comfortably together, Greenhouse 3B or another available property becomes a meaningful next investment.
