<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
The **Great Martyr's Blade** is obtained by clearing **Martyr's Prison** in
the western side of Mortal Shell 2.

From the northern part of the map, I head west through **Gloomshade Grove**.
If the route is still locked, I find the strange stone nearby, attack it to
draw out the **Blood-Cursed Lithopod**, and defeat that boss to open access
to this side of the Citadel.

From there, I head to **Martyr's Prison** rather than continuing deeper along
the Eredrim route. Clearing the Prison gives me the Great Martyr's Blade.

The sword is one of the slowest weapons in the game, so I do not judge it by
the base version alone. Its best setup uses **Frost**, which gives the weapon
the enemy control it needs to land its slow attacks safely.

My rule is:

**get the sword when the route is convenient → test the moveset → invest
heavily once Frost is available and I know I want to keep using it.** 
</GuideQuickAnswer>

<h2 id="great-martyrs-blade-location">
  Where is the Great Martyr's Blade?
</h2>

The **Great Martyr's Blade** comes from **Martyr's Prison**.

The easiest way I remember the route is:

**northern side of the map**

→ head west toward **Gloomshade Grove**

→ defeat the **Blood-Cursed Lithopod** if the Citadel-side access is still
locked

→ open that route

→ head toward **Martyr's Prison**

→ clear the Prison

→ collect the **Great Martyr's Blade**.

This is not a weapon sitting beside a road or hidden in a small chest.

I have to complete the Martyr's Prison route to get it.

<h2 id="blood-cursed-lithopod">

That draws the Blood-Cursed Lithopod out.

Citadel region.

<h2 id="eredrim-route-split">
  Do not follow the Eredrim route all the way
</h2>

This is the navigation mistake I find easiest to make.

The Great Martyr's Blade and Eredrim use some of the same world progression,
but they do **not** have the same final destination.

After opening access:

**Eredrim**

→ continue toward the Citadel interior and the Warden.

**Great Martyr's Blade**

→ redirect toward **Martyr's Prison**.

If I keep following the Eredrim path, I can spend a long time exploring the
Citadel while wondering why I have not found the sword.

Martyr's Prison is the destination that matters.

<h2 id="how-to-get-great-martyrs-blade">
  How to get the Great Martyr's Blade
</h2>

There is no extra quest item I need after reaching Martyr's Prison.

I clear the dungeon route and receive the **Great Martyr's Blade**.

At that point the acquisition part is finished.

I equip it immediately.

I do **not** immediately spend every upgrade material I have.

That is because the base Great Martyr's Blade can make a very bad first
impression.

It is extremely slow.

The first question I answer is not:

**How much damage can this eventually do?**

It is:

**Do I actually enjoy using this moveset?**

<h2 id="is-great-martyrs-blade-good">
  Is the Great Martyr's Blade good?
</h2>

**Yes, but I think the complete Frost setup is what turns it into a genuinely
excellent weapon.**

Without Frost, I consider it a powerful but demanding greatsword.

With Frost, I consider it one of the strongest heavy-control weapons I can
build around.

The reason is simple.

Its biggest weakness is speed.

If the enemy can move and attack normally, I have to wait for a very large
opening before committing.

Frost changes that relationship.

Instead of trying to make the sword fast, I make the **enemy slower and
easier to control**.

That gives the Blade time to do what it is already good at:

* land heavy individual attacks;
* create large stagger pressure;
* capitalize on long punish windows;
* convert one safe opening into a very large chunk of damage.

So my answer changes depending on where I am in progression:

| Situation                      | My verdict                     |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------ |
| Just found the sword           | **Good, but test it first**    |
| No Frost yet                   | **Strong but very slow**       |
| Frost available                | **Excellent**                  |
| Want a fast aggressive weapon  | **Use something else**         |
| Want heavy stagger and control | **Very strong choice**         |
| Hate long attack commitments   | **Probably not worth forcing** |

<h2 id="moveset">
  Great Martyr's Blade moveset
</h2>

The base moveset tells me immediately what kind of weapon this is.

The light chain is built around:

**thrust → large swing → thrust.**

The heavy chain uses slower committed swings.

The attacks hit hard, but I feel the startup and recovery much more than I do
with the Iconoclast, Axe & Dagger, or Axatana.

The running attacks are the part I like more than expected.

The **running light** gives me a large swinging opener.

The **running heavy** combines a spinning attack into a thrust.

Those attacks give me a better way to start an engagement than walking into
melee range and beginning a slow neutral combo.

My practical rhythm is:

**create space → approach with the correct opener → take one real punish
window → stop before greed creates a trade.**

I do not play the Great Martyr's Blade like Axe & Dagger.

Trying to force repeated neutral attacks is exactly what makes the weapon feel
worse than it is.

<h2 id="why-it-feels-slow">
  Why the Great Martyr's Blade feels so slow
</h2>

The problem is not simply animation speed.

It is the amount of time I am committed once I decide to attack.

With a fast weapon, I can often react after the first hit.

With the Great Martyr's Blade, I want to make the decision **before** I start
the swing.

That means I watch for:

* long enemy recovery animations;
* stagger windows;
* a boss finishing a committed attack;
* Frost creating enough control for me to swing safely.

If I begin attacking because I hope the enemy will stay still, the weapon
feels awful.

If I attack because I already know I have the window, it feels much better.

<h2 id="best-tarstones">
  Best Tarstones for the Great Martyr's Blade
</h2>

I do not build this weapon around making every normal attack slightly better.

I want the Tarstone to reinforce the large punish windows the weapon already
prefers.

One of the most useful heavy-finisher options gives the finisher an
additional **30% critical chance**.

That makes much more sense once I am already controlling enemies with Frost.

I am not trying to gamble on a critical hit during a bad opening.

I am creating a safer opening first and then increasing the payoff.

The light-finisher option is also usable, but the heavy critical setup fits
the way I naturally play the Blade better.

The complete build changes how the weapon functions in actual fights.

<h2 id="frost-location">
  Where to get Frost for the Great Martyr's Blade
</h2>

Frost is **not** obtained beside the sword.

That is the catch.

The Frost-related route is much later, at a gate in the **far southeast** of
the world.

I treat that as second-half progression.

So there are really two separate routes:

### Weapon route

**north/west → Gloomshade Grove → Blood-Cursed Lithopod → Martyr's Prison →
Great Martyr's Blade**

### Frost route

**later progression → far southeast gate → Frost setup**

I do not confuse the two.

The Great Martyr's Blade can be collected much earlier than the build piece
that makes me like it most.

<h2 id="captives-scabstone">
  Captive's Scabstone gives the Blade a huge punish attack
</h2>

The other piece I like on the weapon is **Captive's Scabstone**.

Its upgraded attack is built for exactly the kind of large opening I am
already trying to create.

The important values are:

* **300% Strike**;
* **250 Stagger Damage**;
* Resolve cost reduced to **75**.

Those numbers are excellent.

They do not change the rule of the weapon.

I still need enough time to land the attack.

That is another reason Frost fits the Blade so well.

Frost gives me control.

Captive's Scabstone gives me a way to turn that control into a very large
punish.

My ideal sequence is not:

**spam the special and hope.**

It is:

**build control → create the window → commit to the Scabstone attack.**

<h2 id="frost-and-scabstone">
  Why Frost and Captive's Scabstone work together
</h2>

The build makes more sense when I stop looking at each piece independently.

The Blade has a problem:

**slow commitment.**

Frost answers:

**control the target.**

Captive's Scabstone answers:

**make the safe opening hurt.**

The heavy-finisher critical option answers:

**increase the payoff of another large punish window.**

That gives the entire weapon one consistent direction.

I am not trying to turn it into a fast weapon.

I am turning each controlled opening into a much more valuable one.

That is when the Great Martyr's Blade starts to feel complete.

<h2 id="should-i-rush-frost">
  Should I rush Frost after getting the sword?
</h2>

Usually, **no**.

I only make the far-southeast trip early if I have already decided that the
Great Martyr's Blade is the weapon I want to keep.

The mistake I avoid is:

**find sword → hear Frost is strong → cross half the map → spend resources →
discover I hate slow greatswords.**

I reverse that.

My order is:

1. Get the Great Martyr's Blade.
2. Test the normal light and heavy attacks.
3. Test the running attacks.
4. Use it against normal enemies.
5. Try it in a real boss opening.
6. Decide whether the slow rhythm feels good.
7. Chase Frost only if the answer is yes.

That saves me both travel time and upgrade materials.

<h2 id="worth-getting-early">
  Is the Great Martyr's Blade worth getting early?
</h2>

I think it is worth **collecting early when I am already near the route**.

I do not think it is automatically worth restructuring my entire early game
around.

I go for it early when:

* I am already exploring the northern side;
* I have recently been around King's Crypt or Gloomshade Grove;
* the Blood-Cursed Lithopod route is already open or easy to handle;
* I specifically want a true greatsword;
* I am comfortable clearing Martyr's Prison.

If I recently picked up the{" "}

<Link href="/mortal-shell-2/kings-crypt-veterans-battle-axe">
  Veteran's Battle Axe
</Link>
{" "}and I am already in the north, continuing west makes the Great Martyr's
Blade route much more reasonable.

I wait when:

* I still have major progression blockers elsewhere;
* Martyr's Prison would be a large detour;
* I strongly prefer fast weapons;
* I am already short on upgrade materials;
* I only want the sword because I heard the Frost build is strong.

The distinction I keep making is:

**worth obtaining** does not automatically mean **worth maxing immediately**.

<h2 id="battle-axe-vs-great-martyrs-blade">
  Great Martyr's Blade vs Veteran's Battle Axe
</h2>

These are easy to compare because I can encounter both routes while working
through the northern part of the world.

I prefer the **Veteran's Battle Axe** earlier when I want:

* a heavy weapon that becomes useful immediately;
* a very strong running heavy;
* less extreme attack commitment;
* a weapon I can comfortably build around before late-game Infusions.

I prefer the **Great Martyr's Blade** when I want:

* a true slow greatsword;
* larger controlled punish windows;
* Frost;
* heavy stagger;
* a higher-investment specialist weapon.

If I have both and Frost is still unavailable, I am perfectly comfortable
staying with the Battle Axe.

I do not force the Great Martyr's Blade just because it was harder to obtain.

<h2 id="blade-vs-axatana">
  Great Martyr's Blade vs Axatana
</h2>

These weapons solve fights in almost opposite ways.

**Axatana** controls enemies through:

**speed + repeated hits + status buildup.**

The Great Martyr's Blade controls enemies through:

**Frost + large punish windows + heavy individual attacks.**

If I want constant aggression, Axatana is easier for me to recommend.

If I enjoy deliberately waiting for the correct opening and then deleting a
large part of an enemy's health or stagger bar, I prefer the Great Martyr's
Blade.

Neither choice is just about the damage number.

It is about which combat rhythm I want.

<h2 id="upgrade-priority">
  When I spend upgrade materials on the Great Martyr's Blade
</h2>

I upgrade the Blade seriously once three things are true:

**1. I like the moveset.**

If I dislike the light and heavy rhythm, no damage upgrade fixes that.

**2. I know I want Frost.**

That gives the weapon the build identity I care about.

**3. The Blade is replacing my current main weapon.**

If I am still swapping constantly between Iconoclast, Battle Axe, Axe &
Dagger, and the Blade, I keep rare resources in reserve.

Once those three answers line up, I am comfortable investing.

At that point, the weapon is no longer an experiment.

It is the build.

<h2 id="cant-reach-martyrs-prison">
  Why I cannot reach Martyr's Prison
</h2>

If the route to Martyr's Prison appears blocked, I check the shared Citadel
progression first.

My first question is:

**Have I defeated the Blood-Cursed Lithopod near Gloomshade Grove?**

If not, I return to the unusual stone and draw the boss out.

If I already killed it and opened the route, I check whether I accidentally
continued toward the Eredrim/Citadel path instead of redirecting toward
Martyr's Prison.

The two most common route problems are therefore:

**missing the Lithopod prerequisite**

or:

**following the wrong branch after the shared access opens.**

<h2 id="great-martyrs-blade-route">
  Great Martyr's Blade route recap
</h2>

The complete route I remember is:

**northern map**

→ head west

→ reach **Gloomshade Grove**

→ locate the strange stone

→ attack it to draw out the **Blood-Cursed Lithopod**

→ defeat the Lithopod if the route is not already open

→ use the newly available Citadel-side access

→ do **not** continue all the way along the Eredrim route

→ redirect toward **Martyr's Prison**

→ enter the Prison

→ clear the dungeon

→ collect **Great Martyr's Blade**

→ equip it and test the moveset

→ continue normal progression if I am still unsure about the weapon

→ reach the **far southeast** later for Frost

→ combine Frost with the Blade's large punish attacks

→ add **Captive's Scabstone** when I want the high-damage, high-stagger special

The short version is:

**Gloomshade Grove → Blood-Cursed Lithopod → Martyr's Prison → Great
Martyr's Blade → Frost later.**

And the decision rule I keep is:

**get the sword first; commit to the build only after I know I enjoy swinging
it.**
