<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
The **Great Martyr's Blade** comes from **Martyr's Prison** on the western side
of Mortal Shell 2.

From the northern part of the map, I head west through **Gloomshade Grove**. If
the route is still locked, I attack the strange stone nearby to draw out the
**Blood-Cursed Lithopod**, defeat it, and open access toward this side of the
Citadel.

After that shared route opens, I turn toward **Martyr's Prison** instead of
continuing deeper along the Eredrim path. Clearing the Prison gives me the
Great Martyr's Blade.

The base sword hits hard but is extremely slow. I consider it a strong
specialist weapon rather than an automatic upgrade until **Frost** becomes
available later. Frost does not make the Blade faster; it gives me enough enemy
control to land the attacks the weapon is already good at.

My progression rule is:

**get the Blade when the route is convenient → test the moveset → keep
progressing → commit heavily once Frost is available and I know I enjoy the
weapon.**
</GuideQuickAnswer>

<h2 id="great-martyrs-blade-location">
  Where is the Great Martyr's Blade?
</h2>

The **Great Martyr's Blade** is obtained by clearing **Martyr's Prison**.

The route I remember is:

**northern side of the map → Gloomshade Grove → Blood-Cursed Lithopod if
needed → Martyr's Prison → Great Martyr's Blade.**

This is not a weapon I pick up from a roadside chest. Reaching the correct part
of the map only gets me to the dungeon; I still have to finish the Prison route
before the weapon is mine.

<h2 id="blood-cursed-lithopod">
  Defeat the Blood-Cursed Lithopod if the route is locked
</h2>

The main prerequisite I check around **Gloomshade Grove** is the
**Blood-Cursed Lithopod**.

It is easy to miss because the boss is not simply standing in the middle of the
road.

I look for the unusual stone in the area and attack it.

That draws out the Blood-Cursed Lithopod.

Defeating it opens the access I need to move through this side of the Citadel
region.

<h2 id="eredrim-route-split">
  Where the Great Martyr's Blade route splits from Eredrim
</h2>

This is the navigation mistake I am most likely to make.

The Blade and Eredrim share part of the same world progression, but their final
destinations are different.

Once the access around Gloomshade Grove has opened:

**Eredrim → continue deeper toward the Citadel interior and the Warden.**

**Great Martyr's Blade → turn toward Martyr's Prison.**

If I automatically keep following the Eredrim route, I can spend a long time in
the Citadel wondering why the sword never appears.

For the weapon, **Martyr's Prison is the destination that matters**.

<h2 id="martyrs-prison">
  How to reach Martyr's Prison

Once the Blood-Cursed Lithopod prerequisite is handled, I follow the newly
accessible route toward **Martyr's Prison**.

At that point, I stop treating the sword like a quick pickup.

I am entering a dungeon for it.

The goal is to arrive at the weapon with enough resources left to handle the

<h2 id="how-to-get-great-martyrs-blade">
  How to get the Great Martyr's Blade
</h2>

There is no additional quest item after I reach Martyr's Prison.

I clear the dungeon route and receive the **Great Martyr's Blade**.

I equip it immediately.

I do **not** immediately spend every upgrade material I have.

The base weapon can make a rough first impression because the attack
commitments are so much longer than on weapons such as Iconoclast, Axe & Dagger,
or Axatana.

Before spending heavily, I want to know one thing:

**Do I actually enjoy this moveset?**

That matters more than how impressive the finished Frost build looks on paper.

<h2 id="is-great-martyrs-blade-good">
  Is the Great Martyr's Blade good?
</h2>

**Yes — especially with Frost.**

Without Frost, I consider it a powerful but demanding heavy weapon.

With Frost, it becomes one of my strongest choices for **heavy damage, stagger,
and enemy control**.

Its weakness is obvious: attack speed.

If an enemy can move and retaliate normally, I need a large opening before I
commit. Frost changes that relationship by making the target easier to control
instead of pretending the sword itself is fast.

My verdict depends on the build:

- **Just found it:** strong, but I test the moveset before investing.
- **No Frost yet:** powerful and very slow.
- **Frost available:** one of my favorite heavy-control setups.
- **I want constant aggression:** I would rather use a faster weapon.
- **I want stagger and large punish windows:** the Blade becomes much more
  attractive.
- **I hate long attack commitments:** I do not force myself to use it.

The Blade is good because its completed build works around its weakness rather
than ignoring it.

<h2 id="moveset">
  Great Martyr's Blade moveset
</h2>

The base moveset tells me immediately what kind of weapon I am holding.

The light chain follows:

**thrust → large swing → thrust.**

The heavy chain uses slower, more committed attacks.

Every hit has weight, but I feel the startup and recovery much more than I do
with faster weapons.

The running attacks help.

The **running light** gives me a broad swinging opener, while the
**running heavy** combines a spinning attack with a thrust.

Those are useful because I do not always have to walk directly into melee range
and begin a slow neutral chain.

My normal rhythm is:

**create space → approach with the right opener → take one real punish window →
leave before greed turns the hit into a trade.**

I do not try to play the Great Martyr's Blade like Axe & Dagger.

Repeatedly forcing neutral attacks is one of the quickest ways to make the
weapon feel worse than it really is.

<h2 id="why-it-feels-slow">
  Why the Great Martyr's Blade feels so slow
</h2>

The problem is not just animation speed.

It is **commitment**.

With a fast weapon, I can often make another decision after the first attack has
started.

With the Great Martyr's Blade, I want to make the decision before I swing.

I look for moments such as:

- a long enemy recovery;
- a stagger;
- a boss finishing a committed attack;
- Frost creating enough control for me to act safely.

If I attack because I **hope** the enemy will stay still, the Blade feels
terrible.

If I attack because I already know the opening is there, it feels completely
different.

I first create a safe opening, then make that opening more valuable.

<h2 id="frost-location">
  Where to get Frost for the Great Martyr's Blade
</h2>

Frost is **not** next to the sword.

The Great Martyr's Blade is acquired in the northwestern side of the world, but
the Frost-related route comes later at a gate in the **far southeast**.

I treat that as second-half progression.

So I separate the two milestones:

**Blade route**

→ Gloomshade Grove  
→ Blood-Cursed Lithopod  
→ Martyr's Prison  
→ Great Martyr's Blade

**Frost route**

→ continue normal progression  
→ reach the far southeast later  
→ add Frost once that area is available

This is why I can own the sword for quite a while before the version of the
build I actually want is ready.

<h2 id="captives-scabstone">
  Captive's Scabstone gives the Blade a huge punish attack
</h2>

The other important piece of my setup is **Captive's Scabstone**.

Its upgraded attack reaches:

- **300% Strike**
- **250 Stagger Damage**
- **75 Resolve cost**

Those numbers are excellent, but they do not remove the Blade's basic rule.

I still need enough time to land the attack.

That is exactly why it works so naturally alongside Frost.

Frost creates control.

Captive's Scabstone gives me something powerful to do with the opening that
control creates.

<h2 id="frost-and-scabstone">
  Why Frost and Captive's Scabstone work together
</h2>

I think of the setup as one connected combat loop rather than several unrelated
bonuses.

The Great Martyr's Blade has a problem:

**long attack commitment.**

Frost answers that by controlling the target.

Captive's Scabstone then converts the safer opening into a large
damage-and-stagger punish.

The heavy-finisher critical option can push the same idea farther by increasing
the reward when another large opening appears.

So I am not trying to turn the Great Martyr's Blade into a fast weapon.

I am trying to make every opening **safer and more valuable**.

That is the point where the weapon starts feeling complete.

<h2 id="should-i-rush-frost">
  Should I rush Frost after getting the Great Martyr's Blade?
</h2>

Usually, **no**.

The far-southeast trip makes sense early only when I have already decided that
the Great Martyr's Blade is the weapon I want to keep.

I avoid this sequence:

**find Blade → hear Frost is strong → cross half the world → spend upgrade
materials → discover I dislike slow greatswords.**

Instead, I get the weapon and use it first.

I test the normal light and heavy attacks, the running attacks, and the amount
of recovery I have to accept after each commitment. I take it into normal
fights and at least one situation where I have to work around a real enemy
opening.

If that rhythm feels good, Frost becomes a worthwhile destination.

If it does not, I keep progressing with something else.

<h2 id="worth-getting-early">
  Is the Great Martyr's Blade worth getting early?
</h2>

I think it is worth **collecting early when I am already near the route**.

I do not automatically restructure the whole early game around it.

The detour becomes attractive when I am already exploring the northern side,
have been around Gloomshade Grove or King's Crypt, and the Lithopod access is
either open or easy to handle.

It also makes more sense when I already know I want a true slow greatsword.

If I recently picked up the
<Link href="/mortal-shell-2/kings-crypt-veterans-battle-axe">
  {" "}Veteran's Battle Axe
</Link>
and I am still working through the north, continuing west toward the Blade is a
much smaller commitment than crossing the world specifically for it.

I wait when Martyr's Prison would be a major diversion, when I strongly prefer
fast weapons, or when upgrade materials are already tight.

The distinction that matters is:

**worth obtaining does not automatically mean worth maxing immediately.**

<h2 id="battle-axe-vs-great-martyrs-blade">
  Great Martyr's Blade vs Veteran's Battle Axe
</h2>

These two are easy to compare because both can become relevant while I am still
working through the northern part of the map.

I prefer the **Veteran's Battle Axe** when I want a heavy weapon that becomes
useful immediately.

Its running heavy gives it a strong opener, the attack commitments are less
extreme, and I can build around it before the later Infusion pieces of the
Great Martyr's Blade are available.

I prefer the **Great Martyr's Blade** when I specifically want the slower,
higher-commitment style:

**Frost → control → large punish → heavy stagger.**

If I own both and Frost is still unavailable, I am perfectly comfortable
staying with the Battle Axe.

The harder weapon to obtain is not automatically the weapon I should use.

<h2 id="blade-vs-axatana">
  Great Martyr's Blade vs Axatana
</h2>

Axatana and the Great Martyr's Blade solve fights in almost opposite ways.

**Axatana:**

**speed → repeated hits → rapid status buildup → sustained pressure.**

**Great Martyr's Blade:**

**Frost → controlled opening → heavy hit → stagger and burst damage.**

If I want constant aggression, Axatana is easier for me to recommend.

If I enjoy waiting for the correct opening and then taking a large chunk out of
an enemy's health or stagger bar, the Great Martyr's Blade fits better.

This is less about which weapon has the bigger theoretical number and more
about which combat rhythm I actually want to play.

<h2 id="upgrade-priority">
  When should I upgrade the Great Martyr's Blade?
</h2>

I start committing rare materials once the weapon has stopped being an
experiment.

For me, that happens when three things are true:

**I like the moveset.**

If the light attacks, heavy attacks, and recovery already feel miserable,
higher damage will not fix the problem.

**I want the Frost setup.**

That gives the Blade the control-focused identity that makes it stand out.

**The Blade is replacing my current main weapon.**

If I am still constantly switching between Iconoclast, Axe & Dagger, Battle
Axe, and the Great Martyr's Blade, I keep expensive upgrades in reserve.

Once all three answers line up, I am comfortable investing heavily.

<h2 id="cant-reach-martyrs-prison">
  Why can't I reach Martyr's Prison?
</h2>

If Martyr's Prison appears inaccessible, I check two things.

First:

**Have I defeated the Blood-Cursed Lithopod near Gloomshade Grove?**

If not, I return to the strange stone, attack it, and draw the boss out.

If that access is already open, I check my direction.

The Blade shares part of its approach with the Eredrim route, and it is easy to
continue toward the Citadel instead of turning toward **Martyr's Prison**.

So the two common route problems are:

**the Lithopod prerequisite is still incomplete**

or

**I followed the Eredrim branch after the shared route opened.**

<h2 id="great-martyrs-blade-route">
  Great Martyr's Blade route recap
</h2>

The complete route is:

**northern side of the map**

→ head west toward **Gloomshade Grove**

→ locate the strange stone

→ attack it to draw out the **Blood-Cursed Lithopod**

→ defeat the Lithopod if the route is still locked

→ use the newly opened Citadel-side access

→ split away from the Eredrim route

→ head toward **Martyr's Prison**

→ clear the dungeon

→ collect the **Great Martyr's Blade**

→ test the moveset before spending heavily

→ continue normal progression

→ reach the **far southeast** later

→ add **Frost**

→ combine Frost with **Captive's Scabstone** and the Blade's large punish
windows

The short version is:

**Gloomshade Grove → Blood-Cursed Lithopod → Martyr's Prison → Great Martyr's
Blade → Frost later.**

I am happy to get the sword early.

I only commit the build once I know I enjoy swinging it.
