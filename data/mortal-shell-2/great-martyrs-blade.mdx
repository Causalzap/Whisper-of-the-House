<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
To get the **Great Martyr's Blade** in Mortal Shell 2, I head west from the
northern side of the map toward **Gloomshade Grove**, open the nearby Citadel
access if I have not already done so, and continue into **Martyr's Prison**.

The normal prerequisite is the **Blood-Cursed Lithopod** near Gloomshade Grove.
Defeating it gives me the access item used for this side of the Citadel route.

Once that access is open, I leave the Eredrim route and head for
**Martyr's Prison** instead.

Clearing the prison gives me the **Great Martyr's Blade**.

I am happy to collect the sword early, but I do not automatically invest in it.
Its base moveset is extremely slow. **Frost** is the upgrade that changes how I
judge the weapon, and that comes much later in the far southeast.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

<h2 id="great-martyrs-blade-route">
  The Great Martyr's Blade route I use
</h2>

My short route is:

**northern route**

→ head west toward **Gloomshade Grove**

→ defeat the **Blood-Cursed Lithopod** if Citadel access is still closed

→ open the relevant Citadel access

→ leave the Eredrim route

→ enter **Martyr's Prison**

→ clear the dungeon

→ collect **Great Martyr's Blade**.

The important thing is that the Great Martyr's Blade and Eredrim share part of
the same world progression.

They do **not** share the same final destination.

For Eredrim, I continue into the Citadel toward the Warden.

For this weapon, I redirect toward **Martyr's Prison**.

<h2 id="why-i-wait-before-upgrading">
  I separate finding the sword from investing in it
</h2>

The Great Martyr's Blade can make a terrible first impression for a very simple
reason:

**it is extremely slow.**

Not just slightly slower than the Iconoclast.

Slow enough that attacking at the wrong time can let an enemy begin its own
animation after I have already committed and still punish me before I recover.

That makes me separate two decisions:

**Should I go get the weapon?**

and:

**Should I spend heavily on the weapon?**

I am willing to make the first decision fairly early.

I make the second one only after testing how the sword actually feels.

<h2 id="citadel-access">
  2. Open the Citadel access before chasing Martyr's Prison
</h2>

Near **Gloomshade Grove** is the shared prerequisite for this route.

If I have already opened this section while unlocking Eredrim, I do not need to
repeat anything.

I continue toward Martyr's Prison.

If the route is still closed, I deal with the **Blood-Cursed Lithopod** first.

The encounter is easy to miss because the creature does not simply wait in the
open.

I disturb the unusual stone in the area to draw it out.

After defeating the Lithopod, I gain the access needed for this side of the
Citadel route.

If I need the complete route from Gloomshade Grove through the Citadel access,
I follow the{" "}
<Link href="/mortal-shell-2/how-to-unlock-eredrim">
  Eredrim unlock route
</Link>
{" "}until that prerequisite is handled.

For the Great Martyr's Blade, my check is simple:

**Citadel access closed → deal with the Lithopod.**

**Citadel access already open → go toward Martyr's Prison.**

<h2 id="routes-split">
  The Eredrim and Great Martyr's Blade routes split here
</h2>

This is the part I find easiest to confuse when I am collecting several things
in the same region.

Opening the Citadel does not mean the sword is sitting somewhere beside Eredrim.

The routes now separate.

**Eredrim route:**
continue toward the Citadel interior and the Warden.

**Great Martyr's Blade route:**
redirect toward **Martyr's Prison**.

That distinction saves me from exploring the central Citadel while wondering
why the sword marker never seems to get closer.

<h2 id="martyrs-prison">
  3. Head to Martyr's Prison
</h2>

Once the shared access is open, **Martyr's Prison** becomes my actual
destination.

This is no longer a simple roadside weapon pickup.

I have to commit to the dungeon.

That changes how I think about the cost of getting the sword.

I do not judge the route by map distance alone.

I also ask whether I have enough healing and patience to finish another dungeon
before I turn this into my next weapon detour.

<h2 id="great-martyrs-blade-location">
  5. Collect the Great Martyr's Blade
</h2>

Clearing the Martyr's Prison route eventually gives me the
**Great Martyr's Blade**.

At this point I consider the acquisition route finished.

I do not immediately open my upgrade menu and spend everything.

First I equip it.

Then I find out whether I actually like using it.

<h2 id="test-before-spending">
  I test the moveset before committing upgrade materials
</h2>

The sword rewards deliberate openings.

That is the first thing I test.

Its normal attacks are slow and committed enough that I do not expect the
weapon to create its own safe window.

I want to create the window first.

Then swing.

The running attacks are easier for me to use because they let me begin the
exchange with momentum instead of already standing in the enemy's range.

That gives me my first practical rule:

**use a real opening → commit to the attack → leave before greed turns the next
swing into a trade.**

If that rhythm feels good, I keep testing the weapon.

If it feels miserable, I stop spending.

<h2 id="frost">
  Frost is the reason I reconsider the weapon later
</h2>

The mechanic that changes my opinion of the Great Martyr's Blade is **Frost**.

The Great Martyr's Blade is the **only melee weapon that can use Frost**.

Without Frost, I am always respecting the weapon's speed.

The enemy gets to move normally.

The enemy gets to attack normally.

And my sword still takes a long time to commit.

Frost changes that relationship.

Instead of trying to make the greatsword faster, I make the **enemy easier to
control**.

That is a much better solution to the weapon's weakness.

<h2 id="frost-location">
  Frost is a later upgrade, not part of the initial sword pickup
</h2>

The catch is that I do not get Frost beside the weapon.

Its route is much later.

The relevant gate is in the **far southeast of the world**, in an area I treat
as second-half progression.

That matters because it creates two completely different decisions:

**Should I get the Great Martyr's Blade?**

and:

**Should I cross the world early for Frost?**

I answer those separately.

The sword can be worth collecting while I am already working through the
northern and western routes.

Frost can still be too far outside my current progression to justify the trip.

<h2 id="should-i-rush-frost">
  I only rush Frost after deciding the sword is staying
</h2>

I do not cross into much later content just because Frost makes the Great
Martyr's Blade stronger on paper.

First I use the weapon.

I learn whether I actually enjoy its heavy rhythm.

I compare it with the weapons I already own.

Only after I decide:

**yes, this is the weapon I want to keep using**

does the long Frost detour start making sense.

If I am still switching between the Veteran's Battle Axe, Axe & Dagger,
Iconoclast, and Great Martyr's Blade, I stay on normal progression.

There is no reason to cross half the map for an upgrade that may sit unused.

<h2 id="worth-getting">
  Is the Great Martyr's Blade worth getting early?
</h2>

For me, it is worth **collecting early enough to test**.

It is not automatically worth **investing in early**.

I go get it when:

- I am already exploring around King's Crypt and Gloomshade Grove;
- the shared Citadel access is already open or easy for me to open;
- I specifically want to test a true slow greatsword;
- the Martyr's Prison detour fits naturally into my current route.

I wait on serious investment when:

- normal enemies keep interrupting my attacks;
- I fundamentally prefer faster weapons;
- I am expecting upgrade levels to fix a moveset I do not enjoy;
- Frost is still far outside my current progression;
- another weapon is already solving the same fights more cleanly.

And I make the later Frost trip when:

**I have already decided the Great Martyr's Blade is staying in my build.**

That order prevents the biggest resource mistake I can make with this weapon:

**investing in the idea of the sword before confirming I like the sword.**

<h2 id="great-martyrs-blade-route-summary">
  The route I remember
</h2>

My short version is:

**northern route**

→ head west toward **Gloomshade Grove**

→ if needed, draw out and defeat the **Blood-Cursed Lithopod**

→ open the shared Citadel access

→ leave the Eredrim route

→ head to **Martyr's Prison**

→ clear the dungeon

→ collect **Great Martyr's Blade**

→ test the moveset before spending

→ treat **Frost** as a later commitment.

If I cannot reach Martyr's Prison, I check the shared Citadel prerequisite.

If I already have the sword but hate how slow it feels, I do not immediately
throw more resources at it.

I decide whether I actually want the playstyle first.
