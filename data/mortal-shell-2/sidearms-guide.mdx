<h2 id="best-early-sidearm">
  Best early Sidearm: Naylshotte or Troubadour's Lute?
</h2>

For the first part of the game, my decision is simple.

### Naylshotte — best free starting Sidearm

I receive the **Naylshotte** automatically near the beginning.

That makes it the correct baseline.

I already have ranged utility, so I do not need to abandon progression just
to find another Sidearm.

### Troubadour's Lute — best early optional Sidearm

If I want to experiment with another ranged weapon, I prefer
**Troubadour's Lute**.

It is inside the tavern near the **One-Legged Wolf Beacon**.

The biggest advantage is not that I can prove it has the highest damage.

The advantage is that its acquisition cost is low.

I do not need to:

* clear a major dungeon;
* defeat a late boss;
* finish a long Shell route;
* cross the map only for one ranged weapon.

If I am already moving through the One-Legged Wolf area, I can collect it
without restructuring my entire route.

That makes it my favorite early Sidearm detour.

<h2 id="how-sidearms-work">
  How Sidearms and Resolve work
</h2>

Sidearms do not use a completely separate ammunition economy.

They spend **Resolve**.

I rebuild Resolve through melee attacks.

    attacks are part of the same combat loop rather than free damage from a
    separate ammo pool.
  </figcaption>

That creates the basic cycle:

**melee → build Resolve → fire Sidearm → return to melee.**

Once I understand that, Sidearm choice becomes much easier.

I stop asking only:

**Which weapon has the coolest shot?**

I start asking:

**How often can I afford to fire it without starving the rest of my build?**

<h2 id="best-sidearm-for-progression">
  The best Sidearm for progression is often the cheapest useful one
</h2>

I separate **weapon strength** from **acquisition cost**.

A later Sidearm can be interesting and still be a bad immediate target if it
requires me to abandon several useful progression objectives.

Early in the game, the Naylshotte already does an important job:

**it gives me a ranged attack.**

Troubadour's Lute gives me another option at low route cost.

That means I am not under pressure to chase Flooded Village, Sariel, Blackwell
Cavern, or Sentry's Grave only because another Sidearm exists there.

I let progression bring me closer first.

That keeps Sidearm collection efficient.

<h2 id="resolve-economy">
  How Resolve changes which Sidearm I use
</h2>

The Sidearm itself is only half of the decision.

The other half is:

**how much Resolve does the rest of my build need?**

If I generate Resolve quickly through melee and do not spend much elsewhere,
I can use ranged attacks aggressively.

If several parts of my setup are already consuming Resolve, every Sidearm shot
has a much larger opportunity cost.

That means I do not evaluate ranged damage in isolation.

I evaluate the entire loop.

One example is **Wounding Stone**.

It lets melee attacks apply **Perforation**, increasing Sidearm damage against
the affected target.

That creates a clean sequence:

**melee**

→ build Resolve

→ apply Perforation

→ use Sidearm for the stronger punish.

That is the kind of interaction that makes me invest in a ranged weapon.

<h2 id="ember-seed-stone">
  Ember Seed Stone gives me a Burn Sidearm setup
</h2>

Another ranged setup I consider is **Ember Seed Stone**.

It consumes **20 Resolve** to infuse the Sidearm with Burn.

Critical hits can then apply Burn.

Again, I do not look at this only as:

**more Sidearm damage.**

I ask whether my Resolve income can comfortably pay for:

**Sidearm shot + Infusion cost + everything else my build wants to use.**

If the answer is yes, a status-focused Sidearm becomes much more attractive.

If the answer is no, I would rather preserve Resolve for the part of my build
that is already working.

<h2 id="sidearm-and-melee-build">

Sidearms reward me for thinking about melee and ranged combat as one system.

My melee weapon determines:

* how safely I can build Resolve;
* how frequently I land attacks;
* how much time I spend close to the enemy;
* which status interactions I can set up.

The Sidearm then gives me a way to spend that resource.

<h2 id="slayer-seal-sidearms">

With it equipped, Sidearm attacks deal **Break Damage**.

That means ranged pressure can help expose a target for a Riposte instead of
only contributing normal damage.

The loop becomes more attractive:

**melee pressure → build Resolve → ranged Break pressure → expose target.**

But I do not choose Slayer Seal only because I want a stronger Sidearm.

<h2 id="when-to-upgrade-sidearm">
  When I upgrade a Sidearm
</h2>

I do not enhance every Sidearm immediately after finding it.

My order is:

**collect → test → enhance.**

Three things need to be true before I spend heavily.

### I actually use the Sidearm

If it remains holstered through most fights, it is not my first upgrade
priority.

### Its Resolve cost fits the build

If firing constantly prevents me from using abilities I value more, the
Sidearm is not really working with my setup.

### I am not about to replace it

If the next dungeon on my route gives me another Sidearm I already want to
test, I wait.

Once those checks are satisfied, I am comfortable spending Coin.

A Sidearm that regularly:

* finishes exposed enemies;
* gives me a safe ranged punish;
* applies useful status pressure;
* deals with awkward ranged targets;
* or contributes Break pressure;

is doing enough work to deserve investment.

<h2 id="sidearm-upgrade-rule">
  Why I do not upgrade all eight Sidearms
</h2>

Owning eight Sidearms does not mean I need eight upgraded Sidearms.

Every weapon I enhance competes for resources with equipment I may use much
more often.

So I concentrate upgrades.

I would rather have:

**one ranged weapon I use constantly**

than:

**several partially upgraded weapons I only fire when I remember they exist.**

This is especially important early, when Coin still has many other useful
destinations.

<h2 id="my-sidearm-rule">
  The Sidearm rule I use for the whole game
</h2>

I keep the entire system simple.

Early game:

**free Naylshotte → cheap Troubadour's Lute detour if I want another option.**

Mid and late game:

**progression route → collect the Sidearm that belongs to that route.**

For upgrades:

**collect → test → check Resolve demand → spend Coin only if the weapon stays
in my combat loop.**

That prevents two mistakes:

**crossing the map for a Sidearm I do not actually need**

and:

**spending heavily on a ranged weapon just before progression gives me another
one I prefer.**

For me, the best Sidearm is not automatically the rarest or latest pickup.

It is the one I can repeatedly fit between my melee attacks without breaking
the rest of the build.
