<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
There are **8 Sidearms** in Mortal Shell 2:

**Naylshotte, Troubadour's Lute, Forgotten Crossbow, Salvaged Trebuchaxe,
Triarch Repeater, Ballistazooka, Cursed Child, and Caged Hystrix.**

For an overall late-game choice, **Caged Hystrix** is the Sidearm I would build
around first when I want rapid sustained ranged pressure.

For progression, I make different choices:

- **Naylshotte** is the best starting option because I receive it automatically.
- **Troubadour's Lute** is my favorite early optional pickup because it is near
  the One-Legged Wolf Beacon and does not require clearing a full dungeon.
- **Forgotten Crossbow** is the first dungeon Sidearm I consider when Flooded
  Village already fits my route.
- **Salvaged Trebuchaxe** is the option I prefer when I want a heavier,
  committed ranged punish.

Sidearms spend **Resolve**, while melee attacks rebuild Resolve. That means the
best Sidearm is not only about damage; it also has to fit the resource loop I
am already using.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

<h2 id="best-sidearm">
  What is the best Sidearm in Mortal Shell 2?
</h2>

If I have to choose one overall Sidearm, I put **Caged Hystrix** first.

Its rapid-fire style gives me sustained ranged pressure instead of forcing
everything into one slow shot. That is especially useful when a boss gives me
a long enough opening to keep firing.

I still would not call it the correct first Sidearm to chase.

Caged Hystrix belongs to the **Sariel / Chamber of Becoming** progression
chain, while I receive Naylshotte for free and can pick up Troubadour's Lute
with a much smaller detour.

So I separate **power** from **acquisition cost**.

My practical choices are:

- **Best overall:** Caged Hystrix
- **Best starting Sidearm:** Naylshotte
- **Best cheap early alternative:** Troubadour's Lute
- **Best dungeon pickup to combine with progression:** whichever Sidearm sits
  in content I already want to clear

That last point matters more than it sounds.

After the early game, I rarely cross the map only because another ranged weapon
exists. I let normal progression bring me to the relevant dungeon, then make
sure I leave with its Sidearm.

<h2 id="how-sidearms-work">
  How Sidearms and Resolve work
</h2>

Sidearms are Mortal Shell 2's ranged weapons, but they do not use a completely
separate ammunition economy.

They spend **Resolve**.

I rebuild Resolve through melee attacks.

  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">
    Sidearm shots spend a resource I rebuild in melee, so ranged attacks and my
    main weapon are part of the same combat loop.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

The basic cycle is:

**melee → build Resolve → create an opening → fire Sidearm → return to melee.**

Once I understand that, I stop judging a Sidearm only by how impressive its
projectile looks.

The more useful question is:

**How often can I fire it without starving the rest of my build of Resolve?**

A Sidearm can be powerful and still be a poor fit if my Shell, abilities, or
other tools are competing for the same resource.

<h2 id="sidearm-build-synergy">
  How I build around a Sidearm
</h2>

I choose a Sidearm together with the rest of my combat loop.

My melee weapon determines how safely and quickly I rebuild Resolve.

The Sidearm determines how I spend part of that Resolve once an opening
appears.

**Wounding Stone** is one example of a useful interaction.

It lets melee attacks apply **Perforation**, increasing Sidearm damage against
the affected target.

That creates a clean sequence:

**melee → build Resolve → apply Perforation → use the Sidearm for the punish.**

Another option is **Ember Seed Stone**.

It consumes **20 Resolve** to infuse the Sidearm with Burn, and critical hits
can then apply Burn.

That can be useful, but the extra Resolve demand matters.

I do not judge it as free additional damage.

I ask whether my build can comfortably pay for the Sidearm shot, the Infusion,
and everything else I already use Resolve for.

The **Slayer Seal** also increases the value of Sidearms because ranged attacks
can contribute **Break Damage**.

That gives me another possible loop:

**melee pressure → rebuild Resolve → Sidearm Break pressure → expose the
target.**

<Link href="/mortal-shell-2/weapons-guide">

{" "}rather than ranking all eight melee weapons again here.

<h2 id="how-to-upgrade-sidearms">
  When I upgrade a Sidearm
</h2>

I do not enhance every Sidearm immediately after finding it.

My order is:

**collect → test → upgrade.**

Before spending heavily, I want three things to be true.

First, I actually use the Sidearm.

If it stays holstered through most fights, it is not an upgrade priority.

Second, its Resolve demand fits the build.

If firing it regularly prevents me from using abilities I value more, the
Sidearm is fighting against the rest of the setup.

Third, I am not about to replace it.

If the next dungeon on my current route contains another Sidearm I already want
to test, I keep my Coin until I know which one stays in the build.

Once a Sidearm regularly gives me a safe ranged punish, finishes awkward
targets, contributes useful status pressure, or helps create Break openings,
then I am comfortable investing in it.

Owning all eight does not mean I need all eight upgraded.

I would rather have **one ranged weapon I use constantly** than several
partially upgraded Sidearms I only fire occasionally.
