<h2 id="how-sidearms-work">
  Sidearms spend Resolve instead of normal ammunition
</h2>

The first thing that changed how I use Sidearms was realizing that they are not
a separate ammo system sitting beside melee combat.

They are part of the same combat economy.

Firing a Sidearm costs **Resolve**.

I rebuild Resolve by landing melee hits.

  />
  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">
    Sidearm shots spend a resource I earn back in melee, so ranged attacks are
    part of my combat loop rather than free damage from a separate ammo pool.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

That gives every shot an opportunity cost.

If my Shell, Tarstones, or other combat tools already use Resolve constantly, I
am more selective about firing.

If my melee setup generates Resolve comfortably, ranged pressure becomes much
easier to work into normal fights.

So I do not judge a Sidearm only by:

**How much do I like this shot?**

I also ask:

**How often can I afford to use it without starving the rest of my build?**

That question matters more to me than collecting all eight as quickly as
possible.

<h2 id="sidearm-upgrade-rule">
  I test a Sidearm before spending Coin on it
</h2>

Once Sidearm enhancement becomes available, upgrades start competing for
**Coin**.

I do not spend immediately.

Finding a new ranged weapon only proves that I own it.

It does not prove that I want to build around it.

My order is:

**collect → test → enhance.**

I want to know:

- whether I actually fire it in normal encounters;
- whether its Resolve demand fits my melee setup;
- whether another Sidearm on my current route is about to replace it.

Early in the game there are too many other useful things competing for my
resources for me to enhance every ranged weapon on sight.

One Sidearm I consistently use is worth more to me than three partially upgraded
ones sitting in the inventory.

<h2 id="salvaged-trebuchaxe">

That immediately makes it a different kind of Sidearm from Troubadour's Lute.

**leave the road for one minute and grab another weapon.**

If I already want to explore or clear the dungeon, the Trebuchaxe makes the
route more valuable.

If my only goal is trying another ranged weapon for a few fights, I choose a
cheaper acquisition first.

The important distinction is that the **dungeon is part of the cost**.

<h2 id="which-sidearm-first">
  Which Sidearm I would get first
</h2>

My early recommendation is based on **route cost**, not an unsupported damage
ranking.

The **Naylshotte** is automatic, so I start there.

After that, I like **Troubadour's Lute** because the tavern near One-Legged Wolf
is a relatively cheap optional pickup.

I go after the **Forgotten Crossbow** when Flooded Village naturally becomes
useful.

From there, I stop thinking in a fixed acquisition order.

I connect each Sidearm to the route that owns it:

**Ravaged Hideout → Salvaged Trebuchaxe**

**Sariel route → Caged Hystrix**

**Revered Beacon → Cursed Child**

**Blackwell Cavern → Triarch Repeater**

**Sentry's Grave → Ballistazooka**

That is more useful to me than pretending all eight should be collected in
numerical order.

The best next Sidearm is usually the one sitting behind content I already have
another reason to complete.

<h2 id="resolve-economy">
  I choose a Sidearm around my Resolve economy
</h2>

Sidearm damage is not free damage.

Every shot competes for **Resolve**.

That becomes more important once other parts of my build start interacting with
the same resource.

One example is **Wounding Stone**.

My melee attacks inflict **Perforation**, and affected targets take bonus
Sidearm damage.

That creates a clear loop:

**melee → build Resolve and apply Perforation → spend Resolve on the Sidearm
punish.**

Another example is **Ember Seed Stone**.

It consumes **20 Resolve** to infuse my Sidearm with Burn, and critical hits can
apply Burn.

Now the question is no longer:

**Which ranged weapon has the biggest-looking shot?**

It becomes:

**Does this Sidearm make sense with the Resolve loop I am already building?**

That is why I choose the ranged weapon and melee setup together.

<h2 id="slayer-seal-sidearms">
  Slayer Seal makes Sidearm pressure more valuable
</h2>

The **Slayer Seal** changes what Sidearms can contribute to a fight.

With it equipped, Sidearm attacks deal **Break Damage**.

That means a ranged attack is not only dealing damage from a safer distance.

It can also help expose a target for a Riposte.

That makes Sidearm pressure much more attractive in the Slayer Seal combat
loop.

But I do **not** equip Slayer Seal just because I want a stronger gun.

The Seal has a save-level achievement consequence, which is a much larger
decision than Sidearm optimization.

Here, the only Sidearm-specific conclusion I need is:

**Slayer Seal increases the value of ranged Break pressure.**

<h2 id="when-i-spend-coin">
  When I finally spend Coin on a Sidearm
</h2>

I enhance a Sidearm once three things are true.

**I actually use it.**

If it stays holstered through almost every normal encounter, it is not my first
upgrade priority.

**Its Resolve demand fits my build.**

I do not want a ranged weapon that constantly steals the resource I need for
the rest of my combat plan.

**I am unlikely to replace it immediately.**

If another Sidearm I already want is one dungeon away, I test that weapon before
committing heavily.

Once those checks are satisfied, I stop hoarding for the sake of hoarding.

A Sidearm that regularly:

- finishes exposed targets;
- applies useful status pressure;
- solves awkward ranged enemies;
- or gives me safe damage windows;

can absolutely deserve Coin.

I just want the investment to follow actual use.

<h2 id="my-sidearm-rule">
  The Sidearm rule I use for the whole game
</h2>

I do not chase all eight just because there are eight.

Early on, my route is:

**free Naylshotte → cheap optional Sidearm → dungeon pickup when that dungeon
already makes sense.**

Later, I connect the weapon to the content that owns it:

**Shell or boss route → take the Sidearm while I am there**

**major dungeon → do not leave without its Sidearm**

That keeps ranged weapons tied to progression instead of turning them into
another map-cleanup checklist.

And it prevents the resource mistake I care about most:

**spending Coin heavily on a Sidearm before I know whether the next meaningful
route is about to give me one I prefer.**
