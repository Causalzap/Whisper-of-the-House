<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
The **Axe & Dagger** is the reward for completing the **Shrine of Trials**
near Mushroom Village.

In the full release, I cannot simply collect the weapon near the Beacon as
older beta routes may show. I first need the **Chapel Key**, then I have to
complete the Shrine.

My route is:

1. Start at the **Mushroom Village Beacon**.
2. Go down the slope, pass through the gate on the left, and cross the bridge.
3. Walk through the **illusory stone arch** beside the bonfire.
4. Pull the rope on the hidden statue to get the **Chapel Key**.
5. Return to the Beacon, turn left, and enter the chapel with the key.
6. Face the king statue and **walk backward through the hidden wall**.
7. Push the first stone pillar onto its pressure plate.
8. Solve the second room by positioning **both pillars on the two pressure
   plates**.
9. Defeat the final armored enemy.
10. Collect the **Axe & Dagger** from the kneeling figure.

If I want a fast early weapon, the detour is worthwhile. I test the base
moveset first, then consider **Duality**, which turns the three-part light
sequence into six individual hits.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

<h2 id="axe-dagger-location">
  Axe & Dagger location and full-release route
</h2>

The **Axe & Dagger** is obtained inside the **Shrine of Trials**, close to
Mushroom Village.

One reason this weapon can be confusing is that its acquisition changed after
the beta.

If an older guide shows the Axe & Dagger sitting near the Mushroom Village
Beacon, that is the old route. In the full release, I need to find the
**Chapel Key**, open the Shrine of Trials, solve its puzzles, and clear the
final encounter before I can take the weapon.

The full route is:

**Mushroom Village Beacon → illusory arch → Chapel Key → Shrine of Trials →
hidden wall puzzle → first pillar puzzle → two-pillar puzzle → final enemy →
Axe & Dagger.**

<h2 id="chapel-key">
  Chapel Key location near Mushroom Village
</h2>

From the **Mushroom Village Beacon**, I head down the slope.

I go through the gate on the **left**, cross the bridge, and continue until I
reach a bonfire with bodies hanging above it.

There is a large stone arch beside this area.

At first glance, the arch looks blocked.

It is an **illusory gate**.

I walk straight through it.

<h2 id="shrine-of-trials">
  Where to use the Chapel Key
</h2>

Back at the Beacon, I turn **left** and run toward the chapel visible in the
background.

The Chapel Key opens the locked entrance.

I pass through the floor-level fog and enter the **Shrine of Trials**.

There is no second key inside.

From this point on, progression depends on combat and environmental puzzles.

I clear the first group of enemies before working on the puzzle so I am not
trying to read the room while something is attacking me.

<h2 id="shrine-first-puzzle">
  Shrine of Trials secret wall puzzle
</h2>

The first important clue is written beside the king statue:

**“Gaze upon your king. So true. Back and back you shall pass through.”**

    backward.

The solution is literal.

I stand facing the **king statue** and begin walking **backward** toward the
wall behind me.

I do not turn around.

As I continue backing up, I pass through the hidden wall.

If I face the wall and simply run at it, I am not following the clue.

There are enemies immediately beyond the illusion, so once I cross through I
turn around and deal with them before continuing.

I am also careful around the **iron maidens** in this part of the Shrine
because opening one can release another enemy.

<h2 id="shrine-second-puzzle">
  Shrine of Trials pillar and pressure plate puzzles
</h2>

The next two rooms use large stone pillars that move when I hit them.

The important rule for both puzzles is:

**my character is not supposed to stay on the pressure plate.**

I use the stone pillars as permanent weights so the doors remain open after I
move away.

### First pillar puzzle — one pillar, one pressure plate

The first version is simple.

There is one movable pillar and one pressure plate.

I stand on the side opposite the plate and hit the pillar toward it.

I may need to reposition and strike it again to line it up correctly.

</figure>

Once the pillar is resting on the button, the next door opens.

I leave the pillar there and continue.

### Second pillar puzzle — two pillars, two pressure plates

The next room is more complicated because I have **two pillars and two
buttons**.

I do not just start hitting both pillars toward whichever plate is closest.

The order matters.

First, I take the **right pillar** and hit it **straight down** from its
starting position.

Then I move to the other pillar.

I push that pillar toward the position where the right pillar originally
stood, then continue driving it forward.

The first pillar now acts as a **stopper**, preventing the moving pillar from
sliding too far.

That lets me leave one pillar correctly positioned on the first pressure
plate.

Once the first pillar is secured, I push the remaining pillar across to the
**second pressure plate**.

With both buttons held down, the gate opens.

The sequence I use is:

**right pillar straight down → move the other pillar into the right pillar's
old lane → push it until the first pillar stops it on the plate → move the
remaining pillar onto the second plate.**

If the door does not stay open, I check the pillar positions.

Trying to sprint through while temporarily standing on a button is not the
solution.

<h2 id="inside-shrine">
  Final Shrine of Trials enemy
</h2>

After the pillar puzzles, I continue through the Shrine and clear the remaining
enemies.

The final room contains a tougher **armored enemy**.

I heal before entering if I have taken damage during the dungeon, and I am
willing to spend Resolve here rather than saving everything after the route is
already almost complete.

There is no additional puzzle after this fight.

Once the enemy is dead, the Axe & Dagger reward is available.

<h2 id="axe-dagger-reward">

</h2>

After the final fight, I walk toward the **kneeling figure** at the end of the

I interact with it and receive the **Axe & Dagger**.

If I prefer constant close-range pressure, I can start testing it immediately.

<h2 id="duality">
  Duality is the upgrade that defines the Axe & Dagger
</h2>

The base light combo has **three parts**.

With **Duality**, each part attacks twice.

The sequence changes from:

**one → two → three**

to:

**one-two → one-two → one-two.**

That does not mean I assume practical damage literally doubles.

Enemies move, attacks can miss, and I still need to break off a combo when an
enemy is about to punish me.

What Duality reliably gives me is much higher **hit frequency**.

That makes Axe & Dagger especially attractive for aggressive builds and effects
that benefit from repeatedly connecting with the enemy.

<h2 id="worth-getting-early">
  Is the Axe & Dagger worth getting early?
</h2>

**Yes, if I already know I want a fast weapon.**

The route is close enough to Mushroom Village that I am comfortable doing it
early, but this is no longer the near-free pickup shown in older beta guides.

In the full release I need to:

**get the Chapel Key → clear the Shrine enemies → solve three puzzle sections
→ defeat the final enemy.**

That is a meaningful detour.

I do it early when:

- Iconoclast feels slower than I want;
- I prefer rapid close-range pressure;
- Duality fits the kind of build I want;
- I am comfortable clearing the Shrine before continuing normal progression.

I wait when my current weapon is already working well.

The reason is upgrade economy.

Unlocking another weapon does not mean I have to start investing in it
immediately.

I would rather fully support one weapon I enjoy than spread limited resources
across several early weapons before I know which one I want to keep.
