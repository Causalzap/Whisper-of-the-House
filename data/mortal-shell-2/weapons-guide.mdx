<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
Mortal Shell 2 has **eight main melee weapons**: the **Iconoclast, Axe & Dagger,
Veteran's Battle Axe, Great Martyr's Blade, Obsidian Hammer, Axatana, Black
Needle, and Clockwork Scythe**.

I do not replace the Iconoclast just because it is the starter weapon. I test
the **base moveset first**, then look at the Tarstones and Infusions that can
turn that moveset into a real build.

For an early alternative, I like the **Axe & Dagger** when I want speed and
repeated hits, or the **Veteran's Battle Axe** when I want a much stronger
running opener.

Later weapons are not automatic upgrades either. The Great Martyr's Blade only
becomes attractive to me once I am willing to build around Frost, while the
Obsidian Hammer, Axatana, Black Needle, and Clockwork Scythe each solve very
different combat problems.

My rule is simple:

**test the attacks → decide what problem the weapon solves → then spend heavily.**
</GuideQuickAnswer>

<h2 id="how-i-choose-a-weapon">
  How I decide whether a weapon deserves my upgrades
</h2>

The first thing I stopped doing was assuming:

**later weapon = better weapon.**

That is not how Mortal Shell 2 feels to me.

A weapon changes several things at once:

**attack speed**

**reach**

**recovery time**

**running attacks**

**Tarstones**

and:

**which Infusions I can actually equip.**

Some Infusions are restricted to specific weapons, so two weapons that look
similar on a basic stat comparison can develop into completely different
builds.

That is why my upgrade order is always:

**test the light chain → test the heavy chain → test the running attacks → check
Tarstone and Infusion options → then invest.**

If I already dislike the recovery time or attack rhythm, I stop.

I do not spend resources hoping a late upgrade will somehow make me enjoy a
moveset I already want to replace.

<h2 id="iconoclast">
  1. Iconoclast — the starter weapon I am happy to keep
</h2>

The **Iconoclast** is the weapon I start with, but I do not treat it like
temporary tutorial equipment.

Its base moveset is straightforward:

- a simple light chain;
- thrusting heavies that finish with a heavier overhead;
- a jumping running light;
- a wide running heavy.

That simplicity is useful early.

I can concentrate on learning enemy timing instead of learning an awkward
weapon at the same time.

The reason it stays relevant later is **Blood Curse**.

Once an enemy is Cursed, attacking me can backfire by damaging and staggering
the attacker.

The **Zealot Stone** is one Tarstone I notice immediately in normal combat.

Its light-combo finisher changes the third light into a two-hit sequence and
provides **150% Resolve** on that finisher.

That is useful because it improves something I am already doing instead of
asking me to build the entire weapon around one special attack.

For a weapon ability, I prefer **Strike Stone** once it becomes available.

I like the extra Break pressure and area coverage more than simply adding
another small damage increase to basic attacks.

### When I keep investing in the Iconoclast

I keep it when:

- I like a balanced moveset;
- Blood Curse fits the rest of my setup;
- I am already comfortable using its running heavy;
- replacing it would require a long detour without solving a real problem.

I stop investing when I realize the thing I dislike is the weapon itself.

If the speed or reach is wrong for me, another upgrade level is not the fix.

<h2 id="axe-and-dagger">
  2. Axe & Dagger — my early speed and multi-hit option
</h2>

The **Axe & Dagger** is the early weapon I consider when the Iconoclast starts
feeling too measured.

It is obtained through the **Shrine of Trials** route near Mushroom Village.

That route has its own key, hidden approach, and dungeon logic, so when the
weapon itself is my goal I use the{" "}

  Shrine of Trials and Axe & Dagger guide

What matters here is why I make the detour.

The biggest change is **Duality**.

The normal sequence is roughly:

**one → two → three**

With the Duality setup, those attacks become repeated paired strikes:

**one-two → one-two → one-two.**

That repeated hit count immediately changes how I think about status effects.

Fast repeated strikes give me more opportunities to apply pressure than a
weapon that spends the same amount of time winding up for one large hit.

For Infusions, I can push the weapon toward **Blood Curse** or poison.

I prefer Blood Curse when I already want to stay close and keep pressure on the
target.

The running light also gives me a useful multi-hit approach, while the running
heavy has a small leap that helps close the final bit of distance.

### When I go get the Axe & Dagger

I take the Shrine detour when:

- the Iconoclast feels too slow;
- I want repeated status application;
- I like staying close to the target;
- Duality is a mechanic I actually intend to invest in.

If my current weapon already feels good, I leave the Shrine for later.

The fact that I can unlock another weapon does not automatically make the
detour efficient.

I treat that as a real dungeon commitment rather than a quick weapon pickup.

**light → heavy → heavy**

the slower attacks.

The Battle Axe also has an important Infusion advantage:

**it can use permanent Burn.**

That gives it a clear build direction earlier than some weapons whose defining
setup appears much later.

### When I choose the Battle Axe

I like it when:

- I enjoy starting fights with running attacks;
- I want heavier individual hits without using the slowest greatsword;
- I want access to Burn;
- I can tolerate slower neutral attacks in exchange for a strong opener.

I leave King's Crypt for later when its acquisition cost is larger than the
problem the weapon would solve.

<h2 id="great-martyrs-blade">
  4. Great Martyr's Blade — I wait until Frost changes the weapon
</h2>

The **Great Martyr's Blade** was the easiest weapon for me to underestimate.

My first reaction was:

**this is too slow.**

That reaction is fair.

It is also incomplete.

The sword comes from the **Martyr's Prison** chain around Gloomshade Grove.

I leave the Lithopod, key, Prison route, and exact pickup sequence to the{" "}
<Link href="/mortal-shell-2/great-martyrs-blade">
  Great Martyr's Blade guide
</Link>

The reason this weapon matters here is **Frost**.

It is the only melee weapon in this set that gives me that build direction.

  </figcaption>

The sword does not suddenly become fast.

Instead, Frost changes the other half of the fight:

**the enemy gets less freedom to move.**

That makes the weapon's slow attacks much easier to justify.

One heavy-finisher option also adds **30% critical chance**, which becomes more
interesting once I am consistently creating larger punish windows.

The **Captive's Scabstone** pushes the same philosophy much farther.

Its upgraded attack can reach:

- **300% strike damage**;
- **250 Stagger Damage**;
- a Resolve cost reduced to **75**.

Those are large numbers.

I only care about them when my build can actually create the time needed to land
the attack.

### When I invest in the Great Martyr's Blade

I commit when:

- I specifically want Frost;
- I enjoy large stagger and punish windows;
- the supporting pieces that compensate for its speed are available.

I do not pour resources into it immediately after finding the sword if I am
still waiting for the setup that makes the speed tradeoff worthwhile.

This is one of the clearest examples of:

**find now, invest later.**

<h2 id="obsidian-hammer">
  5. Obsidian Hammer — the later weapon I use for stagger and control
</h2>

The **Obsidian Hammer** comes from the **Obsidianite Mines**, southwest of the
Outskirts of Mammon.

This is one of the later pickups where I expect the route itself to fight back.

The miners pressure me up close while other enemies can throw rocks from range,
so I go into the mines prepared for a dungeon rather than a quick collection
stop.

The base weapon behaves the way I expect a hammer to behave:

- vertical light attacks;
- wider sweeping heavies;
- a running jump attack;
- a large running slam.

The real reason I choose it is its Infusion support.

The Obsidian Hammer is the weapon I look at when I want **Trauma** or **Stasis**
on a heavy moveset.

With the Stasis setup I tested, each normal hit adds **5 Stasis stacks**.

That changes my objective against larger enemies.

I am no longer asking:

**Can I squeeze one more slow swing into this attack pattern?**

I am working toward the point where the target stops controlling the pace of
the fight.

If I do not want Stasis, **Trauma** gives the Hammer another natural direction
through stagger pressure.

The **Colossus Stone** reinforces that identity with large shock waves and area
stagger.

### When I choose the Obsidian Hammer

I go for it when:

- stagger is the combat problem I want to solve;
- I want Trauma;
- I want Stasis on a heavy weapon;
- I am comfortable trading speed for control.

By the time I reach Mammon, I do not replace a functioning weapon because I am
bored.

A late weapon needs to solve something.

<h2 id="axatana">
  6. Axatana — my fast late-game option
</h2>

The **Axatana** is one of the later weapons, but its actual pickup is less
committed than several earlier dungeon rewards.

Once I have opened the southern travel network, teleportation connections can
take me toward the small tower/island where it is found.

There is an Axatana-wielding enemy nearby, but I do **not** need to clear a full
dungeon boss before taking the weapon.

That makes it one of the late pickups I am more willing to detour for.

The light sequence is extremely fast.

The heavy attacks bring the axe side of the weapon forward for broader,
harder-hitting swings.

So I naturally divide the moveset into:

**fast blade attacks for single targets**

and:

**axe swings when I need wider pressure.**

Like the Axe & Dagger, Axatana can use **Duality**.

That already fits the dual-wield identity.

It can also use **Voltaic**, but the Infusion that changes difficult fights most
for me is **Stasis**.

The fast hit rate means I can build Stasis without waiting through a long
recovery between applications.

I can push buildup into the **99 range** while still playing an aggressive,
high-frequency moveset.

The **Hexopod Core** adds another rapid multi-hit option.

I am more cautious with it before its upgrade path gives me enough stagger
protection to commit safely.

### When I detour for Axatana

I chase it when:

- I want one of the fastest melee weapons;
- I like Duality;
- I want high-frequency Stasis pressure;
- I have already opened the southern travel network.

I do not cross half the world for it during early Fainweald progression.

The weapon is strong enough to justify a late detour.

It is not strong enough to make me break a smooth early route.

<h2 id="black-needle">
  7. Black Needle — the aggressive spear I build around Phantom
</h2>

The **Black Needle** comes late around **Cester's Gate**.

The gate boss is the important progression check.

By the time I naturally reach this weapon, I am not comparing it against an
unupgraded starter weapon.

I am comparing it against a build I already understand.

That raises the standard for switching.

The Black Needle is faster than I originally expect from a spear.

Its light chain mixes thrusts with a spinning attack, while the heavier sequence
adds sweeps before returning to thrusting pressure.

So it does not feel like:

**poke once → retreat → wait.**

The unique reason I use it is **Phantom**.

The Black Needle also has an ability that lets me throw the spear and have it
return.

That is useful when I want extra area coverage.

For direct damage, I prefer the harder-hitting **Conqueror's Reward**.

### When I use Black Needle

I choose it when:

- I like fast thrust weapons;
- Phantom is the mechanic I want to build around;
- I want melee reach without slow defensive pacing;
- I have naturally reached Cester's Gate.

I do not route toward it during the first few hours.

A late weapon has to beat an established build, not an empty equipment slot.

<h2 id="clockwork-scythe">
  8. Clockwork Scythe — the Resolve-powered multi-hit weapon
</h2>

The **Clockwork Scythe** is tied to the same late-game route as **Sariel** and
the **Chamber of Becoming**.

I do not duplicate that encounter here.

If the problem is reaching or defeating Sariel, I use the{" "}
<Link href="/mortal-shell-2/sariel-boss-guide">
  Sariel boss guide
</Link>

Once I have the weapon, though, the decision becomes completely different.

The base moveset uses wide reaping swings, which already helps when enemies are
crowding me.

Its real identity appears once I power the mechanism.

With the **Scholar's Wormstone**, I spend Resolve over time to activate the
Scythe's chainsaw mode.

Each chainsaw spin adds **35% of the weapon's base damage**.

The repeated spins turn normal swings into multi-hit attacks.

That becomes especially useful once the repeated hits are also feeding a status
setup.

For Infusions, the Scythe can move toward **Blood Curse** or **Voltaic**.

I usually prefer Blood Curse when the rest of the build is already centered on
sustained chainsaw pressure.

The **Thief Stone** also makes sense here because faster Warp-supported attacks
mean more opportunities to use the entire repeated-hit setup.

### When I switch to the Clockwork Scythe

I use it when:

- I am already far enough into the Sariel route to obtain it naturally;
- my Resolve generation can support chainsaw mode;
- I want wide attacks and repeated status hits;
- I enjoy maintaining pressure instead of waiting for isolated heavy punishes.

If my Resolve economy is poor, I do not judge the weapon by its best-case
chainsaw output.

A defining mechanic that I cannot keep active is a build problem, not a reason
to keep spending blindly.

<h2 id="best-early-weapon">
  Which weapon I actually recommend getting early
</h2>

I do not have one universal answer.

If the **Iconoclast already feels good**, I keep it.

That saves me a detour and lets me spend upgrades on something I already know I
can use.

If I want **speed and repeated hits**, I go for the **Axe & Dagger**.

If I want a heavier opener and am willing to clear King's Crypt, I choose the
**Veteran's Battle Axe**.

I do **not** recommend the Great Martyr's Blade as an automatic early upgrade
just because the individual hits are large.

I wait until Frost or the rest of its supporting setup gives me a reason to
accept the slow moveset.

<h2 id="later-weapons">
  I choose later weapons by the problem they solve
</h2>

Once I reach the southern half of the game, I stop thinking in terms of:

**Which weapon is later?**

I think in terms of:

**What does my current build lack?**

I take the **Obsidian Hammer** when I want stagger, Trauma, or heavy Stasis.

I take the **Axatana** when I want very fast Duality and Stasis buildup.

I take the **Black Needle** when I want Phantom and aggressive spear pressure.

I take the **Clockwork Scythe** when my Resolve economy can support repeated
chainsaw hits.

That makes late weapon acquisition much more deliberate.

I am no longer trying to collect an upgrade.

I am replacing a working build with something that needs to offer a clear
advantage.

<h2 id="upgrade-rule">
  The upgrade rule that saves me the most resources
</h2>

The weapon progression rule I keep using is:

**I upgrade the moveset I already want to play.**

Then I use Tarstones and Infusions to strengthen that moveset.

I do not reverse the process.

I do not buy upgrades first and hope they convince me to like the weapon later.

That is especially important with weapons such as the Great Martyr's Blade or
Clockwork Scythe.

Their strongest setups can look extremely attractive on paper.

But if I dislike the basic rhythm or cannot support the defining mechanic, those
numbers are not actually helping my run.

For me, the best Mortal Shell 2 weapon is not simply the one with the largest
single damage number.

It is the one whose **base attacks, unique mechanic, and resource economy all
solve the same problem.**
