<h2 id="best-weapon">
  What is the best weapon in Mortal Shell 2?
</h2>

For a complete late-game character, **Axatana is the weapon I recommend most
often**.

It does not have the biggest single hit or the most specialized build. What it
does have is a combination of speed, pressure, status support, and useful heavy
coverage without a major weakness I have to solve first.

Its light attacks are extremely fast. **Duality** pushes that hit frequency
even further, while the heavier axe attacks give me broader swings when I need
space instead of pure single-target pressure.

The Infusion that completes the package for me is **Stasis**.

There are still situations where I choose another weapon.

damage option, Clockwork Scythe becomes more attractive.

If stagger is the entire point of the build, Obsidian Hammer has a clearer
specialist role.

Axatana ranks first because it needs fewer conditions before it starts feeling
excellent.

<h2 id="best-early-weapon">
  Best early weapons in Mortal Shell 2
</h2>

Early in the game, I narrow the decision to three choices:

**Veteran's Battle Axe, Axe & Dagger, or keeping Iconoclast.**

### Veteran's Battle Axe — best early heavy weapon

I choose the **Veteran's Battle Axe** when I want heavier attacks and I am
willing to clear King's Crypt.

The running heavy is the main reason. It lets me begin a fight with a powerful
committed attack instead of standing next to an enemy and waiting for a slow
neutral swing to finish.

Its **Burn** option also gives it a strong early identity before several later
Infusion setups become available.

### Axe & Dagger — best early fast weapon

I choose **Axe & Dagger** when I want much faster pressure.

The important upgrade is **Duality**. It changes the rhythm of the normal
sequence into paired strikes:

**one-two → one-two → one-two.**

That matters because faster hit frequency improves both close-range pressure
and status buildup.

### Iconoclast — best choice if I do not need to switch

Sometimes the strongest early decision is to keep what I already have.

If the starter **Iconoclast** feels good and Blood Curse fits my build, I do
not spend an hour routing toward another weapon simply because a new pickup
exists.

Early upgrade materials are limited.

I switch when another weapon solves a real combat problem, not just because it
is available.

<h2 id="axatana">
  Axatana — best overall weapon
</h2>

Axatana is one of the fastest melee weapons in the game.

Its light attacks use the blade side for rapid pressure, while the heavy
attacks shift into broader axe swings.

That gives me a simple split:

**light attacks → speed and single-target pressure**

**heavy attacks → wider coverage and stronger commitments**

For Tarstones, **Duality** is the direction I value most. Turning fast attacks
into repeated paired strikes gives the weapon an even better foundation for
status buildup.

Axatana can use **Voltaic**, but I prefer **Stasis**.

Fast hits let me build Stasis while continuing to attack instead of waiting for
occasional heavy openings. Against bosses, that combination allows aggressive
pressure while the target becomes progressively easier to control.

The **Hexopod Core** adds another rapid multi-hit attack. I invest more heavily
in it once I can commit to the full animation without being interrupted
constantly.

### Axatana location

I find Axatana on a small southern tower or island reached through the
teleportation network.

An Axatana-wielding enemy is nearby, but the pickup itself is relatively
straightforward once the southern travel network is available. I do not have
to clear a full weapon dungeon before taking it.

That combination of acquisition, speed, Duality, Stasis, and heavy coverage is
why Axatana stays at the top of my list.

<h2 id="great-martyrs-blade">
  Great Martyr's Blade — best Frost weapon
</h2>

The **Great Martyr's Blade** is one of the strongest weapons I use once its
Frost setup is online.

The base problem is obvious:

**the sword is very slow.**

Its attacks hit hard and stagger well, but long commitments make mistakes
expensive.

The weapon comes from **Martyr's Prison**, while **Warden's Stone** provides
the Frost Infusion that changes how safely I can use it.

That changes the matchup from:

**slow weapon + enemy moving normally**

to:

**slow weapon + enemy I can freeze and punish.**

- **250 Stagger Damage**

attack.

<h2 id="clockwork-scythe">
  Clockwork Scythe — best high-investment weapon
</h2>

The **Clockwork Scythe** comes from the late-game **Chamber of Becoming**
route associated with **Sariel**.

Its normal reaping attacks already work well when several enemies are near me,
but the base moveset is not what makes the weapon S tier.

The defining mechanic is its powered **chainsaw mode**.

With **Scholar's Wormstone**, I spend Resolve over time to activate the
mechanism. Each chainsaw spin deals **35% of the weapon's base damage**, and
normal attacks become repeated-hit sequences while it is active.

<h2 id="iconoclast">
  Iconoclast — starter weapon worth keeping
</h2>

I do not treat **Iconoclast** as disposable tutorial gear.

Its straightforward light and heavy attacks make it easy to learn enemies
without also dealing with an unusual weapon.

The reason it remains relevant later is **Blood Curse**.

Once an enemy is Cursed, its attempt to attack can cause damage and stagger
instead. That gives Iconoclast much more identity than its starter status
suggests.

For Tarstones, I like **Zealot Stone**.

It changes the third light attack into a two-hit finisher and gives me
**150% Resolve** from that finisher.

For an active ability, **Strike Stone** is my longer-term choice for Break
pressure and area coverage.

If the weapon is already solving my early fights, keeping it also preserves
upgrade materials for later.

<h2 id="obsidian-hammer">
  Obsidian Hammer — best stagger specialist
</h2>

The **Obsidian Hammer** is found in the **Obsidianite Mines**, southwest of the
Outskirts of Mammon.

It plays exactly as heavily as it looks. The light attacks come down
vertically, heavier attacks give me broader sweeps, and the running options
include a jumping approach and large slam.

I use it for **control**, not attack frequency.

The Hammer can use **Stasis** or **Trauma**.

If I want a more stagger-focused alternative, Trauma fits the weapon naturally,

I choose Axatana when I want Stasis without giving up speed.

I choose the Hammer when controlling and staggering the enemy is already the
entire plan.

<h2 id="black-needle">
  Black Needle — best Phantom weapon
</h2>

The **Black Needle** is a later pickup around **Cester's Gate**.

Despite being a spear, it does not feel passive. Its light sequence combines
fast thrusts with a spin, the heavy chain mixes sweeps with stronger thrusts,
and the running attacks support an aggressive approach.

Its defining Infusion is **Phantom**.

<h2 id="best-status-weapons">
  Best weapons for Frost, Stasis, Blood Curse, Trauma, and Phantom
</h2>

Status access changes the ranking because some Infusions directly solve a
weapon's weakness or amplify what the moveset already does well.

### Frost

**Great Martyr's Blade + Warden's Stone**

This is my clear Frost pairing. Freezing the target gives an extremely slow
weapon the large punish windows it needs.

### Stasis

**Axatana** is my aggressive Stasis choice.

**Obsidian Hammer** is the slower alternative when I also want heavy stagger
and control.

### Blood Curse

My main choices are:

- **Iconoclast**
- **Axe & Dagger**
- **Clockwork Scythe**

Iconoclast gives me Blood Curse without replacing the starter.

Axe & Dagger combines it with much higher hit frequency.

Clockwork Scythe puts Curse on top of an already destructive late-game
multi-hit setup.

### Trauma

**Obsidian Hammer** is my natural Trauma choice because the effect reinforces
the weapon's stagger identity.

### Phantom

**Black Needle** owns this niche.

Phantom is the strongest reason I have to use Black Needle instead of treating
it as another late-game fast weapon.

<h2 id="upgrade-rule">
  Which weapon should I upgrade first?
</h2>

The mistake I avoid is upgrading first and deciding whether I like the weapon
afterward.

I reverse that.

Before committing expensive materials, I test:

1. the light chain;
2. the heavy chain;
3. the running light;
4. the running heavy;
5. recovery after the attacks I expect to use most;
6. the Tarstone I actually want;
7. the Infusion that gives the weapon its final identity.

Then I ask whether the new weapon solves something my current build cannot.

**Axatana** earns investment when I want speed, Duality, and Stasis.

**Great Martyr's Blade** earns it when I want Warden's Stone, Frost, and heavy
control.

**Clockwork Scythe** earns it when my Resolve economy can sustain chainsaw
mode.

**Obsidian Hammer** earns it when stagger, Trauma, or heavy Stasis is the
center of the build.

**Black Needle** earns it when Phantom fills a gap I cannot get elsewhere.

My upgrade rule is:

**test the moveset → identify the Tarstone → identify the Infusion → decide
whether the weapon improves the build → invest.**
