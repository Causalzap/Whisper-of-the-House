<h2 id="missable-achievements">
  The three achievement windows I protect on my first playthrough
</h2>

The first two are the dangerous ones because they happen in the **tutorial
section**.

New Game Plus does not send me back through that opening. It starts me at
Marrowkeep, so I cannot treat those tutorial interactions like normal
post-game cleanup.

The third is **Peter's Perfect Parry**.

I can attempt that condition again in a later cycle, but I would rather do it
during the first playthrough because The Nameless Captive becomes a harder
fight in NG+.

So my first-cycle rule is:

**opening interaction → Tar Golem challenge → normal progression → return for
Peter's Perfect Parry before ending the cycle.**

<h2 id="mid-summer">

</h2>

**Mid Summer?** has an 83.1% completion rate, which makes it look easy.

The requirement itself is easy.

The timing is what matters.

While I am moving through the opening toward my first Shell, I stop for
**Marigold** instead of running straight past the NPC interactions.

I accept the flower-crown / Festival interaction before continuing.

<h2 id="no-you-still-cant-win">
  No, You Still Can't Win — decide before leaving the Tar Golem
</h2>

The second tutorial achievement is much more demanding.

**No, You Still Can't Win** requires taking the **Tar Golem to minimum
Health**.

Only **12.9%** of players in the current snapshot have completed it.

The important decision happens before I leave the tutorial:

**am I only trying to progress, or am I protecting a 100% run?**

For normal progression, I can lose the Tar Golem fight and continue.

For the achievement route, I stay until I complete the challenge because I do
not plan on returning to this tutorial encounter in NG+.

<h2 id="peters-perfect-parry">
  Peter's Perfect Parry — I return later, but still in the first cycle
</h2>

**Peter's Perfect Parry** is where I give myself more flexibility.

The requirement is very specific:

**parry each one of The Nameless Captive's headspins.**

Simply defeating the boss for **Headcase** does not satisfy that condition.

That difference shows up clearly in the completion rates.

**Headcase** sits at **34.9%**.

**Peter's Perfect Parry** is only **4.1%**.

If I reach The Nameless Captive and my current Shell, Weapon, healing, or
general fight consistency feels poor, I do not force the parry challenge
immediately.

I leave.

I improve the build.

Then I come back **later in the same playthrough**.

That is the better compromise for me than deliberately leaving the achievement
for NG+, where I can technically try again but have to perform the condition
against a harder boss.

<h2 id="first-playthrough-route">
  How I route the rest of my first-playthrough achievements
</h2>

Once the three timing-sensitive jobs are under control, the achievement run
becomes much less rigid.

I stop thinking in achievement order and start thinking in **route overlap**.

If I enter a dungeon for a Weapon, I check whether it also contains a
Tarstone, Sidearm, map objective, boss, or Shell-related progression.

If I enter a new region for a boss, I finish the nearby exploration before
fast travelling somewhere else for one missing checkbox.

That naturally moves a large part of the list:

- boss achievements;
- individual Shell unlocks;
- individual Weapon unlocks;
- individual Sidearm unlocks;
- first Shell Memory progress;
- Tarforge progression;
- Beacon progress;
- Ova collection;
- Map Fragment collection.

This is also why I do not start a 53-step linear route.

Mortal Shell 2 opens enough routes that forcing every save into the exact same
order creates unnecessary backtracking.

<h2 id="seeking-the-past">
  Seeking the Past is the achievement I expect to finish last
</h2>

**Seeking the Past** only asks me to:

**watch all Shell Memories.**

The wording makes it sound like a normal Shell checklist.

It is not.

The bottleneck is the number of **Glimpses** needed to unlock the Memories.

I cannot treat Glimpses like an unlimited resource that I simply farm until
every Shell tree is finished.

That is why I expect this objective to continue into **New Game Plus and
beyond**.

The achievement rates show the difference between finding the Shells and truly
finishing them:

**Shell Seeker — 16.9%**

versus:

**Seeking the Past — 1.9%.**

If I have unlocked every Shell but Seeking the Past remains locked at the end
of the first cycle, I do not start randomly searching Fainweald for another
body.

I check whether I have actually opened and watched every available Shell
Memory and accept that the remaining Glimpse requirement belongs to later
cycles.

That keeps me from wasting time trying to solve a multi-cycle progression
requirement as if it were a missed first-playthrough collectible.

<h2 id="no-lifer">
  No Lifer is the final completion check
</h2>

**No Lifer** unlocks after the rest of the achievement list is complete.

Its current completion rate is only **1.2%**.

By the time I am close to it, I am not concerned about normal story
achievements.

My remaining checklist is much more likely to contain something like:

- a missed Tarstone;
- Peter's Perfect Parry;
- an unfinished Shell Memory;
- a secret interaction;
- one incomplete collection.

That is why I use the checklist from the start.

I can hide everything already finished, search the exact missing achievement,
and return only to the system that still needs work instead of rechecking the
entire world.
