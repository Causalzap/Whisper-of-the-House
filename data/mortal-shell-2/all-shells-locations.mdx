<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
There are **eight main Shells to unlock after the prologue** in Mortal Shell 2:
**Tiel, Proxima, Gragu, Eredrim, Smert, Genessa, Lazlo, and Sariel**.

I do not rush all eight.

In **Fainweald**, I usually test **Tiel and Proxima first** because both are
available close to the opening route without a major boss chain. After that, I
only chase Gragu, Eredrim, or Smert when I actually want what their kits do.

I leave **Genessa, Lazlo, and Sariel** until Mammon progression makes their
longer routes worthwhile.

My main rule is that **unlock order is not investment order**. I am happy to
collect a Shell early, but I keep my Glimpses concentrated until I know which
one I actually want to play.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

<h2 id="before-spending-glimpses">
  I unlock Shells faster than I invest in them
</h2>

The biggest resource mistake I stopped making was treating every new Shell like
a character I should immediately level.

Finding the body is often cheap.

**Committing Glimpses is the expensive decision.**

Glimpses open a Shell's memories and deeper progression, and I do not plan a
normal first run around fully developing every Shell I touch.

Shell Points are easier for me to experiment with because I can move them around
after opening the relevant progression.

That gives me a much safer testing order:

**unlock → use the base kit → test the playstyle → move Shell Points if useful →
commit more Glimpses only when I want to keep playing it.**

I would rather finish the game with one or two Shells I understand deeply than
eight partially developed builds.

That is also why an **all Shells route** is not the same thing as my recommended
progression route.

I can collect something early without deciding it deserves my next several
Glimpses.

<h2 id="harros">
  Harros is the prologue Shell, not one of my eight open-world choices
</h2>

**Harros** is the Shell I inhabit during the opening.

I do not include him in the eight-Shell investment decision because once the
prologue ends, my real roster begins with the Shells I can find throughout the
open world.

That makes **Widow's Overlook** the point where my first meaningful Shell choice
starts.

<h2 id="fainweald-shells">
  The Shells I look at first in Fainweald
</h2>

Fainweald contains most of the Shell decisions that shape my early and
mid-game.

The five I associate with this side of my progression are:

**Tiel → Proxima → Gragu → Eredrim → Smert**

I do not treat that as a mandatory unlock order.

Tiel and Proxima are cheap early tests.

Gragu and Eredrim have more meaningful route requirements.

Smert has a stranger unlock and a much more demanding playstyle.

  progression guide

<h2 id="tiel">
  1. Tiel — my safest first Shell
</h2>

If I want a Shell quickly without turning the opening hour into a boss run,
**Tiel** is one of my easiest choices.

From **Widow's Overlook**, I head southeast toward the Mushroom Village side of
Fainweald.

Before entering the village proper, I check the exposed graveyard beside the
route.

Tiel's body is there.

There is no mandatory boss attached to this pickup.

That low acquisition cost is one reason I recommend testing Tiel early even if I
am not sure he will become my main.

His kit revolves around **Lingering Shadow**.

The Perfect Dodge side of the kit also feeds into that shadow state, so his
movement rewards something I already need to learn during normal combat.

What I like most is that I do not need a specialized build before Tiel feels
useful.

Stealth helps me approach or bypass awkward enemies, while his mobility does not
force me into a separate high-risk resource loop.

There is also a very cheap first test:

**Tiel's first memory costs one Glimpse.**

That is low enough that I am comfortable seeing how his progression develops
without deciding the rest of my run around him.

### When I choose Tiel

I like Tiel when I want:

- a forgiving first Shell to explore with;
- mobility and stealth;
- a kit that rewards learning Perfect Dodge;
- something useful before I have a specialized weapon build.

I stop investing if I realize the shadow playstyle itself is not interesting to
me.

Being easy to obtain is not a reason to keep paying for him forever.

<h2 id="proxima">
  2. Proxima — the aggressive early alternative
</h2>

**Proxima** is the other Shell I like testing before committing my early
Glimpses.

From Widow's Overlook, I head north toward **Black Ridge Cliffs**, pass the
fighting enemies instead of clearing the entire route, then climb toward the
small cavern.

Her body is on the altar inside.

There is no mandatory boss here either.

Proxima immediately feels more aggressive to me.

Her **Biosampler** changes behavior depending on the target:

- against a weaker enemy, it can pull the target toward me;
- against a stronger target, it can pull me toward the target.

That makes it useful for isolating smaller enemies and closing distance on
larger ones.

Her **Grafted Armor** also gives me a concrete defensive safety net:

**15% chance to mitigate a hit**, with a successful trigger reducing that hit's
damage by **50%** and preventing its stagger.

I like having that passive.

I do not invest in Proxima because of the passive alone.

The reason I commit is when I enjoy using Biosampler and start building around
conditions such as **stasis**.

### When I choose Proxima

I prefer Proxima when I want:

- a more aggressive early Shell;
- a tool for moving enemies or closing distance;
- stasis or condition synergy;
- some passive protection without turning into a pure tank.

If the only part I like is the occasional Grafted Armor trigger, I keep my
Glimpses for somebody else.

<h2 id="gragu">
  3. Gragu — excellent while moving forward, less convenient for repeated bosses
</h2>

**Gragu** is the first Shell where I stop thinking in terms of a quick body
pickup.

I find him at the **One-Legged Wolf** tavern from the Mushroom Village side of
Fainweald.

The important part is that seeing him is not the unlock.

**He wants the Heart of Vatra.**

That turns Gragu into a deliberate route rather than something I casually grab
while passing through.

I do not repeat the Temple of Vatra steps here because the sequence contains an
important pre-Heart decision and deserves one Owner Page.

When Gragu is my actual target, I use the{" "}
<Link href="/mortal-shell-2/heart-of-vatra-gragu">
  Heart of Vatra and Gragu guide
</Link>

What matters for choosing him is his healing economy.

**Revered Heart replaces the normal healing rhythm with hearts earned from
enemies.**

That makes Gragu comfortable when I am moving continuously through a populated
region.

I kill enemies, maintain the resource, and keep advancing.

The same mechanic is less comfortable when I am dying repeatedly to one boss
and need to rebuild healing resources between serious attempts.

### When I choose Gragu

I like him when:

- I am clearing normal enemies continuously;
- I want healing generated by forward momentum;
- I enjoy his heavier, stagger-oriented style.

I like him less when:

- I am repeating one difficult boss;
- I do not want to rebuild healing resources between attempts.

That difference matters much more to me than putting him on a generic tier
list.

<h2 id="eredrim">
  4. Eredrim — the Fainweald detour I take for break pressure
</h2>

**Eredrim** costs more time to unlock than Tiel or Proxima.

That is the important fact I need from an all-Shells page.

His route is tied to **Gloomshade Grove**, Citadel access, and boss-gated
progression, so I do not chase him merely because his general location appears
reachable.

If I specifically want Eredrim, I follow the{" "}
<Link href="/mortal-shell-2/how-to-unlock-eredrim">
  Eredrim unlock guide
</Link>
{" "}for the Citadel Lever, entrances, encounters, and exact route.

The reason I accept that detour is his focus on **break and stagger pressure**.

His Shoulder Bash fits the way I play when I am tired of waiting for every enemy
to finish a full attack string before I can take control again.

I want Eredrim when breaking the enemy's rhythm matters more than adding another
small amount of raw damage.

### When I choose Eredrim

I chase him when:

- I want aggressive close-range pressure;
- break and stagger are solving real combat problems;
- I enjoy interrupting enemy tempo rather than playing around stealth or range.

If Tiel or Proxima is already carrying me comfortably through Fainweald, I do
not make the Citadel detour just to fill another Shell slot.

<h2 id="smert">
  5. Smert — the high-risk Shell with the strangest Fainweald unlock
</h2>

**Smert** is where I stop recommending Shells based on convenience.

I go after him because I specifically want the kit.

His unlock centers on **three blood pools**.

I enter a pool, allow the encounter to force me out of my Shell, recover, and
repeat the process.

There are **three pools**.

The useful part is that blood already contributed to a completed pool remains
there.

So even if the sequence costs me multiple deaths, I am not resetting all three
every time.

That makes the unlock awkward rather than brutally all-or-nothing.

Smert's kit is also much less forgiving than the early Shells.

His **Miracle** trades health for the ability to dramatically slow the fight
while he enters a combat stance.

His attacks can build **Chaos**, which can lead into conditions such as poison,
lightning, burn, or freeze.

Another important threshold is **Faith at 10% health or lower**.

Those are mechanics I build around deliberately.

I do not use Smert because I want a calm version of Tiel.

### When I choose Smert

I invest in Smert when:

- I enjoy high-risk builds;
- I am comfortable operating at low health;
- controlling the pace of an encounter is worth sacrificing safety;
- I want status and condition pressure.

If I am still struggling with basic dodges and guards, I leave him until later.

A powerful low-health mechanic is not helping me if I am reaching low health by
accident.

<h2 id="mammon-shells">
  The three Shells I leave for Mammon progression
</h2>

**Genessa, Lazlo, and Sariel** are where my Shell hunting changes.

I stop asking:

**Can I technically reach this Shell early?**

and start asking:

**Is this route worth interrupting my progression for?**

All three sit behind longer routes and more demanding progression than Tiel or
Proxima.

If my current Shell is already working, I let my normal route move into Mammon
before forcing these unlocks.

If one of their kits is the reason I want to make the trip, then the detour
becomes worthwhile.

<h2 id="genessa">
  6. Genessa — the ranged glass-cannon option I only chase deliberately
</h2>

**Genessa** is not a simple body pickup.

Her unlock takes me into later Mammon progression and **Revenant Graves**, then
eventually requires me to return **Sester's Censer** to her.

That one sentence is enough for me to know this is not an early casual detour.

If Genessa is the goal, I use the{" "}
<Link href="/mortal-shell-2/how-to-unlock-genessa">
  Genessa unlock guide
</Link>
{" "}for the full Revenant Graves route, Sester Secundus encounter, Censer, and
return trip.

What makes me interested in Genessa is **Faithful Doubles**.

She can create copies that attack with her, which gives me damage pressure from
more than one body instead of relying entirely on my own attack sequence.

That is much more attractive once I already own a weapon setup worth extending
through those doubles.

The tradeoff is that I treat her as a more demanding offensive Shell rather than
the Shell I choose because I want maximum forgiveness.

### When I choose Genessa

I want her when:

- I want damage coming from multiple bodies;
- I already have a weapon setup worth supporting;
- I am comfortable trading safety for higher offensive potential.

I do not cross Mammon just to add another Shell to the roster.

The route has to justify itself.

<h2 id="lazlo">
  7. Lazlo — the late Shell I choose for stability
</h2>

For **Lazlo**, the route takes me through the later Mammon side toward
**Deserted Slums**, **High Lord's Keep**, and eventually the **Royal Crypt**.

The part I care about operationally is simple:

**I activate the nearby beacon before committing to the Royal Crypt section.**

Lazlo is the Shell I look at when I want the build to become **more stable**
rather than more complicated.

His **Fortified Plate** starts with **10% damage reduction** while the armor is
active.

His active kit revolves around **Retribution**, fiery shock waves, and an
overheat system that can eventually feed into a larger explosion.

That combination gives me something I value late in the game:

a durable base that still leaves the weapon as the center of my build.

### When I choose Lazlo

I like Lazlo when:

- small mistakes are costing me too much health;
- I want passive mitigation;
- I want a stable Shell underneath a weapon-focused setup.

If I am looking for the most unusual mechanic in the roster, he is not my first
choice.

If I want consistency, he becomes much more attractive.

<h2 id="sariel">
  8. Sariel — the Shell I save until I want the entire encounter
</h2>

**Sariel** is the Shell I am least likely to add to an early collection route.

His unlock becomes a multi-stage encounter tied to the
**Chamber of Becoming**.

That alone tells me I should not treat him like Tiel with a longer walk.

If Sariel is the reason I am making the trip, I use the{" "}
<Link href="/mortal-shell-2/sariel-boss-guide">
  Sariel boss guide
</Link>
{" "}for the route, encounter stages, and mechanic that prevents the final fight
from ending normally.

The reason I eventually make the effort is his very unusual **Pain and Curse**
playstyle.

Incoming damage can become Pain, and melee pressure can help recover that gray
health.

That makes Sariel far more active than a normal tank.

I am not simply absorbing mistakes.

I am expected to respond aggressively enough to convert part of that damage
state back into survivability.

That is powerful.

It is also why I do not judge him from a thirty-second test and immediately
spend every remaining Glimpse.

### When I choose Sariel

I save resources for Sariel when:

- I enjoy managing Pain instead of a normal health loop;
- I want Curse and thorn-based pressure;
- I am already far enough into Mammon that his route fits naturally;
- his kit offers something my existing developed Shell does not.

By the time I reach him naturally, I usually already have a functioning main.

Sariel needs to justify replacing or supplementing that investment.

<h2 id="which-shell-first">
  Which Shell I unlock first
</h2>

For a normal first run, I keep this simpler than a full eight-Shell ranking.

**I test Tiel and Proxima first.**

They are close to Widow's Overlook, neither requires a major boss chain, and
they give me two very different early combat styles before I spend heavily.

After that, my route depends on the problem I want the next Shell to solve:

**Gragu** when I want an exploration-friendly healing loop.

**Eredrim** when break and stagger pressure matter more.

**Smert** when I am ready for high-risk health sacrifice and encounter control.

**Genessa** when I want aggressive multi-body damage.

**Lazlo** when I want late-game stability.

**Sariel** when I deliberately want to build around Pain and Curse.

I do not try to unlock those six in the order somebody else reached them.

The order that matters to me is:

**which Shell earns my next Glimpses.**

That is a much more expensive decision than which body I happen to find first.
