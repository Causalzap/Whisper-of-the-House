<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
My safest **Mortal Shell 2 progression route** is:

**Tar Golem → Marrow Keep → Widow's Overlook → early Fainweald → Mushroom
Village → Unblemished Ova → Gloom Siphon → Mether's Breath → reopen corruption
routes → Mammon → Walk the Unfound Path.**

I use **Marrow Keep as home**, but I leave it early instead of trying to learn
every system before exploring.

My first goal in Fainweald is not to clear the map. I get **one Shell I actually
like**, activate useful beacons, and only detour for another weapon if my current
moveset is causing a real problem. Once **Unblemished Ova** becomes important, I
let that progression take priority over random exploration.

After the Gloom Siphon gives me **Mether's Breath**, the corruption routes I
abandoned earlier become worth revisiting. That is also when I start moving more
seriously toward **Mammon**.

When the objective changes to **Walk the Unfound Path**, I treat it as the
signal that normal open-world progression is giving way to the endgame.

<h2 id="how-i-approach-progression">
  Mortal Shell 2 progression route at a glance
</h2>

The open world made me waste time at first because too many routes looked
equally important. They are not.

If I only want the progression order, this is the route I follow:

1. **Do not let the Tar Golem stop the run.**
2. **Reach Marrow Keep and leave once the lift opens.**
3. **Use Widow's Overlook to establish an early Shell and beacon.**
4. **Stay in Fainweald instead of rushing south.**
5. **Use Mushroom Village as an early route anchor.**
6. **Test another weapon only if the current moveset is actually holding me back.**
7. **Prioritize Unblemished Ova once the Gloom Siphon becomes the main progression
   driver.**
8. **Get Mether's Breath and revisit corruption-blocked routes.**
9. **Move into Mammon once my Shell, weapon, and route network feel stable.**
10. **Solve specific late-game blockers instead of collecting randomly.**
11. **Treat Walk the Unfound Path as the transition into the final stretch.**

The broader loop is:

**leave Marrow Keep → make one useful improvement → push something that changes
the world → return when I have something worth turning in or spending → move
farther out.**

I do not try to clear the map clockwise. Mortal Shell 2 regularly lets me reach
dungeons I do not need yet, long Shell routes, weapons behind puzzles or bosses,
and areas that are technically accessible before I have a good reason to stay
there.

If a branch is consuming healing without changing anything about my run, I
leave it and come back when the route has a purpose.

<h2 id="tar-golem">
  1. Do not let the Tar Golem hold up the opening
</h2>

The first place I can lose unnecessary time appears before the open world even
starts.

The **Tar Golem** looks like a mandatory tutorial boss, but I do not need to
beat it to continue.

On a first run, I use the encounter to learn spacing, recovery, and how quickly
Mortal Shell 2 punishes one extra attack. If I lose, I let the run continue
instead of turning the opening into a boss grind.

I only keep retrying when beating the Tar Golem is itself the goal. The
<Link href="/mortal-shell-2/tar-golem">
  {" "}Tar Golem guide
</Link>
covers the fight and retry behavior without making it part of the required
progression route.

<h2 id="marrow-keep">
  2. Leave Marrow Keep early, but keep using it as home
</h2>

Once I reach **Marrow Keep**, I start thinking of it as the center of the run.

I do not stay there trying to exhaust every system before leaving. A lot of what
the Keep offers makes more sense after I have actually found a Shell, picked up
some Coin, seen another weapon, recovered an Ova, and activated a few beacons
worth returning to.

So once the lift is available, I leave.

What matters is remembering to come back.

Marrow Keep is where exploration starts turning into progression. I use it to
turn in important items, reassess upgrades, and decide whether the next route
still deserves more time and resources.

<h2 id="widows-overlook">
  3. At Widow's Overlook, get one early Shell before chasing harder routes
</h2>

**Widow's Overlook** is the first place where the world really opens in several
directions. Enemies, routes, dungeon entrances, and Shells all compete for my
attention.

My first priority is not the hardest thing I can see. It is getting a Shell that
makes normal exploration comfortable.

**finding a Shell is not the same thing as deciding to invest in it.**

<h2 id="fainweald-first">
  4. Where to go first: stay in Fainweald instead of rushing Mammon
</h2>

The first broad region I treat as my working area is **Fainweald**.

Widow's Overlook and Mushroom Village begin connecting into a larger route
network here, and I can establish the systems that matter before taking on the
harder half of the world.

My early order is:

**Shell → beacon → weapon → Ova progression.**

I do not need every Shell or every weapon. I need enough stability that each new
branch feels like a decision rather than a gamble.

That is why I do not rush into Mammon simply because the larger world is already
visible.

If ordinary enemies in another direction suddenly take dramatically longer to
kill than everything I was fighting a few minutes earlier, I question the route
before I question the entire build.

Mortal Shell 2 lets me walk into trouble early. Being allowed through a path
does not automatically make it the best place to go next.

<h2 id="weapon-detour">
  6. Take an early weapon detour only if the current moveset is a problem
</h2>

The first weapon that gets me through the opening does not automatically deserve
all of my early upgrade materials.

If I already dislike its speed, reach, recovery, running attack, or basic combo
rhythm, I stop paying to make the wrong moveset stronger and test another weapon
first.

The **Axe & Dagger** is a useful early contrast because it feels very different
from the heavier choices, although getting it means committing to the
<Link href="/mortal-shell-2/shrine-of-trials-axe-dagger">
  {" "}Shrine of Trials route
</Link>
rather than grabbing it from the side of the road.

That detour makes sense when I genuinely want a faster moveset. If I am still
deciding what deserves my upgrade materials, my
<Link href="/mortal-shell-2/weapons-guide">
  {" "}weapons guide
</Link>
compares the melee options by progression timing, Infusions, Tarstones, and the
situations where I actually switch.

I treat Sidearms more casually during this part of the run. The **Naylshotte**
already gives me ranged utility, so I do not interrupt progression just to
complete the set. I only chase another one when I want a specific function, and
the
<Link href="/mortal-shell-2/sidearms-guide">
  {" "}Sidearms guide
</Link>
covers those pickups without pulling their routes into this page.

The important decision is not how many weapons I have found. It is whether a new
weapon changes something that is currently holding the run back.

<h2 id="ova-progression">
  7. Once Ova becomes the objective, let it drive progression
</h2>

The biggest early shift comes when the game starts asking me to recover
**Unblemished Ova**.

At Marrow Keep, returning Ova advances the story through the **Gloom Siphon**.
From that point, I stop treating every open route as equally valuable.

A branch leading toward an Ova, a story boss, a useful beacon, or something that
fixes a real weakness in my build gets priority. A branch that only consumes
healing can wait.

I also become more deliberate about returning to Marrow Keep. If I have
recovered something that can advance the Gloom Siphon, wandering through several
unrelated branches before checking it usually adds travel without moving the run
forward.

The
<Link href="/mortal-shell-2/ova-gloom-siphon">
  {" "}Ova and Gloom Siphon guide
</Link>
covers the exact Ova loop, corruption requirements, and rewards.

For this progression route, the important part is understanding what Ova
changes: it determines when old dead ends can become useful again.

<h2 id="corruption-and-methers-breath">
  8. Get Mether's Breath before forcing corruption-blocked routes
</h2>

When a route explicitly says it is **blocked by corruption**, I stop searching
the nearby walls.

I am not looking for a hidden lever, another doorway, a jump tucked around the
corner, or a weapon strong enough to break through.

The blocker is progression, so I leave.

After enough Ova progression at the Gloom Siphon, I gain
**Mether's Breath**. That is when the routes I abandoned because of corruption
become worth revisiting.

Mether's Breath also makes my beacon network more valuable because returning to
older areas no longer requires the same long walks once the relevant travel
points are active.

The distinction I use is simple:

**corruption before Mether's Breath → leave**

**corruption after Mether's Breath → return and see what the route now opens**

That is one of the biggest turning points in Fainweald progression.

<h2 id="when-i-return-to-marrow-keep">
  When should I return to Marrow Keep?
</h2>

I normally return when I have something that can change the next part of the
run.

That might be an **Ova** ready for the Gloom Siphon, a **Shell** I am seriously
considering, enough resources for a meaningful upgrade, or a route that has
started costing far more healing than it is giving back in progress.

The last situation matters. If ordinary enemies are taking too long, checkpoint
runs keep draining resources, and I am not learning anything useful by forcing
the area, I do not automatically start grinding.

Sometimes the better answer is simply to return home, spend what matters, and
take a different branch.

<h2 id="when-to-move-to-mammon">
  9. When should I go to Mammon?
</h2>

I do not define being finished with Fainweald as:

**every icon cleared.**

I move toward **Mammon** when going south is more useful than cleaning up early
branches.

Before I start treating Mammon as my main progression area, I want a Shell whose
movement and recovery I understand, a melee weapon I am willing to keep
upgrading, and enough confidence with dodging and Perfect Guard that ordinary
enemies are not consuming all my healing.

I also want useful Fainweald beacons active and enough Ova progression that
corruption is no longer deciding every route for me.

That does not require every Shell, every weapon, or every dungeon reward.

It only requires the run to be stable enough that I am moving forward rather
than using Mammon to test an unfinished build.

That is the difference between being **ready to progress** and being
**finished collecting**.

<h2 id="late-game">
  11. In Mammon, solve the blocker instead of collecting randomly
</h2>

By the time I am established in Mammon, my progression question changes.

Early on, I am usually asking:

**Where should I go next?**

Later, the more useful question is:

**What exactly is stopping me?**

If damage is the problem, I look at the weapon. If survivability or combat
rhythm is the problem, I look at the Shell. If a dungeon wants a key or a
specific interaction, I solve that requirement instead of wandering elsewhere.

Bosses need the same treatment. When a mechanic is what keeps ending the fight,
more random upgrades may not solve anything.

**Sariel** is a good example because the Chamber of Becoming sequence matters
more than reducing the encounter to “go there and keep attacking.” When Sariel
is the actual blocker, the
<Link href="/mortal-shell-2/sariel-boss-guide">
  {" "}Sariel boss guide
</Link>
covers that sequence directly.

That lets this page stay about progression decisions instead of becoming a copy
of every boss guide in the cluster.

<h2 id="difficulty">
  If the route seems right but combat feels too hard
</h2>

I do not immediately interpret a difficult encounter as proof that I need more
levels or more upgrades.

First I check whether I have simply walked into a later route before I needed
to. Then I look at whether I am investing in a weapon I already dislike or
trying to force a Shell I have barely tested.

I also make sure the blocker is actually combat. A corruption gate, puzzle,
unusual revive condition, key requirement, or boss objective will not disappear
because I added more damage.

If the route, build, and mechanic all make sense and I still want to change the
combat challenge itself, the
<Link href="/mortal-shell-2/difficulty-settings">
  {" "}difficulty settings guide
</Link>
covers the systems that can move difficulty in either direction.

I do not make the world harder while I am already struggling to understand
where I am going.

<h2 id="optional-detours">
  Take late detours for a reason, not to finish the map
</h2>

Late in the run, asking **what have I not collected yet?** can send me across
the world without improving the next objective.

A better question is:

**What would make the next part of the run easier or more interesting?**

If I already have a weapon I love, another long weapon dungeon can wait. If my
current Sidearm is doing its job, I do not cross Mammon just to complete the
set. A Shell route becomes attractive when I actually want the playstyle at the
end of it.

The same logic applies when I am stuck. If one boss is stopping progression, I
solve that boss before spreading resources across several unrelated systems.

By this point, the beacon network also makes intentional returns much cheaper.
I no longer feel pressure to finish every Fainweald branch before moving south,
because leaving something behind does not mean losing access to it.

</h2>

The recovered Ova are enough, and the remaining problem is the
**foul passenger**.

Then the objective changes to:

**Walk the Unfound Path.**

<h2 id="progression-rule">
  The progression rule I keep using
</h2>

From Fainweald through Mammon, the route works best when I prioritize whatever
actually changes my current state.

An Ova matters because it can advance the world. Mether's Breath matters because
it turns blocked routes into usable ones. A weapon detour matters when my
current moveset is holding me back, and a Shell route matters when I genuinely
want the playstyle it unlocks.

The same idea tells me when to leave. If ordinary enemies are making it obvious
that I arrived too early, or a branch is consuming resources without opening
anything important, I do not need to force it simply because the game allows me
to be there.

Mortal Shell 2 gives me enough freedom to spend a lot of time doing things that
are technically possible.

The useful distinction is knowing the difference between:

**I can go there** and **I should go there now.**
