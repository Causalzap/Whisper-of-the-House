<h2 id="where-to-go-next">
  How I decide where to go next in Mortal Shell 2
</h2>

I use **Marrow Keep as the anchor** for the wider world, but I do not treat
Mortal Shell 2 as a single chain of areas that must be cleared in one fixed
order.

When I reach a new branch, I make four checks.

**Is the road physically gated?**

Black corruption is the clearest example. If the game is visibly refusing
access, I stop looking for a hidden switch or alternate doorway and return to
the progression system that removes the gate.

**Is the route open but already draining my resources?**

If ordinary enemies are emptying my healing before I even reach the Shell,
weapon, dungeon, or boss I came for, I usually mark the route and leave. An
open path can still be a bad path for my current build.

**Does the reward change something I actually want to use?**

A new Shell or weapon can justify an early detour when my current moveset is
the problem. The same dungeon is much less urgent when I already like my setup.

**Am I approaching a point where unfinished work matters?**

Once the run starts moving toward **Walk the Unfound Path** and the endgame, I
stop treating every decision like another ordinary exploration branch. That is
when I check what I actually want finished before moving the save forward.

Those four questions are more useful to me than trying to clear the map from
north to south.

<h2 id="world-progression">
  Mortal Shell 2 progression without forcing one area order
</h2>

The broad route I keep in my head is:

**Fainweald opening → Marrow Keep → early Shell and weapon branches → Ova and
Gloom Siphon progression → previously blocked routes → Outskirts of Mammon and
southern objectives → later dungeons, Shells and bosses → Walk the Unfound
Path → endgame.**

That is only the shape of the run.

For the exact chronological route, including what I do after Marrow Keep,
which progression gates open next, and when the Unfound Path becomes relevant,
I use the <Link href="/mortal-shell-2/progression-guide">progression guide</Link>{"."}

The important difference is that I do **not** assume every branch I discover
belongs in the main route.

Some are progression.

Some are equipment detours.

Some are optional bosses or dungeons.

Some only become efficient after another system has opened.

Recognizing which type of route I am standing in front of usually saves more
time than memorizing one universal area order.

<h2 id="corruption-gates">
  Black corruption means I am missing progression, not a secret path
</h2>

Black corruption is the blocker I am least willing to brute-force.

When it seals a road, I leave.

The solution belongs to the <Link href="/mortal-shell-2/ova-gloom-siphon">Ova & Gloom Siphon guide</Link>, which covers Unblemished Ova, the Gloom Siphon, Mether's Breath, corruption cleansing, and the related Beacon travel progression.

Once that system advances, routes that previously looked like dead ends become
useful again.

That is why I do not spend ten minutes circling a corruption barrier looking
for a jump, lever, or back entrance that is not supposed to solve it yet.

<h2 id="detour-value">
  When an optional route is worth the detour
</h2>

The best detours give me **two reasons to be there**.

If a route gives me a Shell I actually want and also advances a dungeon,
Sidearm, Tarstone, boss, or achievement objective, I am much happier taking it
early.

A branch that gives me one item I probably will not use can wait.

### I choose a Shell by route cost as well as build

There are **eight permanent post-opening Shells**, but their acquisition costs
are not equal.

I use the <Link href="/mortal-shell-2/all-shells-locations">all Shell locations guide</Link> to compare the full roster before committing to a long unlock route.

**Gragu** is a good example of why route cost matters. Unlocking him is tied to
the Heart of Vatra chain, so when I decide I actually want that Shell I follow
the <Link href="/mortal-shell-2/heart-of-vatra-gragu">Heart of Vatra and Gragu route</Link> instead of treating Gragu like a body I can simply pick up beside a road.

**Eredrim** fits more naturally into the Citadel side of my progression.

**Genessa** asks for a much longer southern route through the Outskirts of
Mammon and Revenant Graves. When she is the Shell I actually want, I use the <Link href="/mortal-shell-2/how-to-unlock-genessa">Genessa unlock route</Link> rather than clearing southern content without a target.

**Sariel** is an even bigger commitment because the Shell is tied to the
Chamber of Becoming progression and its encounter. When that is the objective,
I let the <Link href="/mortal-shell-2/sariel-boss-guide">Sariel and Chamber of Becoming guide</Link> own the route.

I do not chase whichever locked Shell looks farthest away. I choose the one
whose playstyle and acquisition route make sense for what I am already doing.

### I change weapons when the moveset is the problem

If combat feels wrong because I dislike my current attacks, reach, speed, or
Resolve rhythm, a weapon detour can be worth more than another story objective.

I first compare the roster in the <Link href="/mortal-shell-2/weapons-guide">Mortal Shell 2 weapons and tier list</Link>{"."}

Then I decide whether the acquisition route is worth the interruption.

For a heavy early alternative, I can combine dungeon progress with the <Link href="/mortal-shell-2/kings-crypt-veterans-battle-axe">King's Crypt and Veteran's Battle Axe route</Link>{"."}

For a faster weapon, the <Link href="/mortal-shell-2/shrine-of-trials-axe-dagger">Shrine of Trials route</Link> takes me through the Chapel Key and puzzles to Axe & Dagger.

If I specifically want the Great Martyr's Blade and its later Frost setup, I
use the <Link href="/mortal-shell-2/great-martyrs-blade">Great Martyr's Blade guide</Link>{"."}

If my current weapon is already solving fights comfortably, none of those
dungeons becomes urgent simply because I discovered its entrance.

### I fold Sidearms into routes I already want to clear

Sidearms are easier for me to collect opportunistically.

**Naylshotte** arrives through the opening, while **Troubadour's Lute** is a
relatively cheap early alternative.

The later ranged weapons make more sense when their dungeon, boss, or Shell
route already gives me another reason to be there.

The <Link href="/mortal-shell-2/sidearms-guide">all Sidearm locations guide</Link> keeps the eight acquisition routes and the actual ranged-weapon comparison together, so I do not turn this Hub into a second equipment guide.

<h2 id="route-too-early">
  How I know a route is open too early for my build
</h2>

A locked road is easy to understand.

An **open but inefficient road** causes more wasted time.

My warning sign is not simply that enemies hit hard. It is that the approach is
consuming so much Health, healing, Resolve, or concentration that I am arriving
at the actual objective already compromised.

If that happens repeatedly, I stop assuming I need to farm the same corridor
until it works.

I ask whether one of these would change the run more efficiently:

- another Shell or weapon that better fits how I play;
- a few useful upgrades on the setup I already use;
- a different branch with progression rewards;
- returning after I have opened a more convenient Beacon or connection.

I am happy to leave a difficult branch unfinished when the game gives me other
productive routes.

Coming back stronger is not lost progress if the alternative is spending all
my resources just reaching the encounter I actually wanted to practice.

<h2 id="combat-difficulty">
  When the problem is combat itself rather than one route
</h2>

One expensive dungeon does not make me rebuild the whole save.

If **ordinary encounters across multiple areas** feel consistently wrong, I
treat that as a broader combat problem.

That is when I check the <Link href="/mortal-shell-2/difficulty-settings">difficulty settings guide</Link> instead of blaming one dungeon.

Night is a separate decision.

I prefer learning new routes under normal conditions, then using Night when I
understand the area well enough that the additional pressure and rewards are
actually useful.

The <Link href="/mortal-shell-2/night-mode">Night Mode guide</Link> covers Thestus, the unlock, encounter changes, and the reason I would choose Night rather than leaving it active automatically.

<h2 id="tar-golem">
  Tar Golem is the opening exception
</h2>

The opening **Tar Golem** breaks the normal boss logic because beating it is
possible, but victory is not required to continue the game.

That means I decide what I am trying to accomplish before spending repeated
attempts on it.

If I only want to reach the main game, I do not treat a loss like a blocked
save.

If I am protecting a 100% run, the fight matters much more because
**No, You Still Can't Win** is tied to this opening encounter.

The <Link href="/mortal-shell-2/tar-golem">Tar Golem guide</Link> owns the actual fight strategy, retry behavior, and what happens after a win or loss.

The <Link href="/mortal-shell-2/achievements">achievement and trophy roadmap</Link> owns the decision about when that challenge must be completed in a completion run.

<h2 id="boss-detours">
  I do not fight every boss the moment I find it
</h2>

A boss encounter is worth pushing when it advances something I already want.

If the reward, Shell, dungeon completion, or next route matters to my build, I
stay and learn the fight.

If I reached the boss through an expensive optional branch and the approach
already tells me I am underprepared, I am comfortable leaving.

For **Magdalena**, I use the <Link href="/mortal-shell-2/magdalena-boss-guide">Magdalena boss guide</Link> when the Lady of the Woods fight itself becomes the blocker.

For **Sariel**, I treat the boss and Chamber of Becoming as part of one larger
Shell objective rather than separating the fight from the route that led me
there.

This keeps optional bosses from dictating the order of the whole playthrough.

<h2 id="completion-route">
  When I start caring about achievements and full cleanup
</h2>

I collect convenient things from the beginning, but I do not play the entire
first half of the game as a 53-item trophy checklist.

The important completion jobs are the ones whose timing can actually hurt the
run.

The <Link href="/mortal-shell-2/achievements">Mortal Shell 2 trophy and achievement guide</Link> tracks all **53 achievements** and separates the short-lived conditions from Shell, Weapon, Sidearm, boss, Beacon, Ova, Tarstone, Map Fragment, and later Shell Memory cleanup.

That lets me keep normal exploration efficient.

If a collectible is ten seconds away, I take it.

If one unchecked achievement would send me across the world while the current
region still has several useful objectives, I finish the region first.

The checklist tells me what remains; it does not have to dictate every step I
take through the world.

<h2 id="endgame">
  What I check before the final stretch
</h2>

My priorities change once **Zmey**, **Walk the Unfound Path**, and the final
progression become relevant.

Before committing further, I ask whether I still care about:

- a Shell I genuinely wanted to play;
- a melee weapon or Sidearm I still wanted to test;
- an optional dungeon or boss that matters to the run;
- achievement or collection cleanup that is better handled in the current
  cycle.

I do not use that moment as an excuse to clear every icon for the sake of
clearing it.

I finish the things I would actually regret leaving behind.

For the route into this stage, I stay with the <Link href="/mortal-shell-2/progression-guide">progression guide</Link>{"."}

Once the question becomes **which ending, Baghead, Send Ova, final commitment,
or New Game Plus**, I switch to the <Link href="/mortal-shell-2/endings">endings guide</Link> so the detailed endgame sequence and spoilers stay on their Owner Page.

<h2 id="how-i-read-the-world">
  The rule I use when Mortal Shell 2 stops giving me a clear answer
</h2>

When I am unsure whether to continue or turn back, I reduce the decision to
three signals.

**A physical progression gate means I am missing progression.**

**An open route that consumes my resources before the objective means I should
consider returning stronger.**

**An optional branch is worth taking when its reward changes something I
actually want from the build or the run.**

That framework keeps working even after the exact early-game questions are
solved.

If I can go in several directions, I do not ask which path is technically
available.

I ask:

**which path gives me the most useful progress for what I am trying to do
right now?**
