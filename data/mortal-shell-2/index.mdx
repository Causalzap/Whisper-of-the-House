<h2 id="where-to-go-next">
  Where I go when I do not know what to do next
</h2>

When my problem is broad —

**I finished something, but I do not know what the game expects me to do next**

— I use the{" "}
<Link href="/mortal-shell-2/progression-guide">
  Mortal Shell 2 progression guide
</Link>

That is where I keep the chronological route from the early Fainweald opening
through Ova progression, Mammon, the Unfound Path, and endgame.

I do not try to clear every visible branch as soon as I find it.

My normal rule is:

**make useful progress → activate Beacons → improve the Shell and weapon I
actually use → return to blocked branches once I have the requirement.**

That works better than treating the open world like one giant checklist.

<h2 id="progression-blockers">
  Progression blockers I do not brute-force
</h2>

Some obstacles look like exploration puzzles but are really progression gates.

The important thing is recognizing which kind I am looking at.

### Black corruption

If a road is explicitly blocked by corruption, I stop searching the nearby
walls for a switch.

I use the{" "}
<Link href="/mortal-shell-2/ova-gloom-siphon">
  Ova & Gloom Siphon guide
</Link>

That route covers **Unblemished Ova**, the **Gloom Siphon**,
**Mether's Breath**, corruption cleansing, and the related Beacon travel
progression.

### The opening Tar Golem

If I am stuck at the first Tar Golem and wondering whether the game expects me
to win, I use the{" "}
<Link href="/mortal-shell-2/tar-golem">
  Tar Golem guide
</Link>

The important question there is not just how to fight it.

It is whether I need to beat it at all, what changes if I win or lose, and how
the retry works.

<h2 id="difficulty">
  Difficulty: I separate easier combat from Night Mode
</h2>

Mortal Shell 2 does not begin with a conventional Easy / Normal / Hard menu.

If I want to understand the actual difficulty options, I use the{" "}
<Link href="/mortal-shell-2/difficulty-settings">
  difficulty settings guide
</Link>

The **Slayer Seal** is the option I look at when I specifically want easier
combat.

The warning matters:

**equipping the Slayer Seal disables achievements on that save.**

I do not equip it casually if achievement eligibility matters to me.

Night is a different decision.

The{" "}
<Link href="/mortal-shell-2/night-mode">
  Night Mode guide
</Link>
{" "}covers how I unlock Night with **Thestus**, what changes in encounters, and
when I use it for challenge or rewards rather than ordinary progression.

My rule is:

**Standard when I am learning or progressing.**

**Night when my route and build are stable enough that the extra difficulty is
actually worth it.**

<h2 id="priority-order">
  The order I use when several problems are open at once
</h2>

When my map is full of possible objectives, I diagnose the problem in this
order:

**1. Do I simply not know where to go next?**

I check the progression guide.

**2. Is a corruption barrier physically stopping me?**

I check Ova, the Gloom Siphon, and Mether's Breath.

**3. Is a specific Shell unlock confusing me?**

I use that Shell's route.

**4. Am I looking for a weapon rather than actual story progress?**

I switch to the weapon or Sidearm guide instead of forcing the main route.

**5. Is one boss or dungeon mechanic consuming every attempt?**

I fix that mechanic before farming more upgrades.

**6. Is the game only feeling inefficient because I am playing in Night?**

I return to Standard progression and come back later.

**7. Am I already at the Unfound Path or ready to finish the story?**

I check the endings guide before making the final commitment.

That is the structure I use for the whole game:

**identify the blocker first, then solve only the blocker I actually have.**
