<h2 id="veterans-battle-axe-location">
  Veteran's Battle Axe location
</h2>

The **Veteran's Battle Axe is inside King's Crypt, on its lowest level**.

King's Crypt itself is directly north of the **Black Ridge Pass Beacon**.

That gives me two simple landmarks to remember:

**Black Ridge Pass Beacon → north to King's Crypt**

and once I am inside:

**keep working downward until I reach the lowest level.**

I use the Crypt itself as my destination rather than wandering toward every

<h2 id="kings-crypt-walkthrough">
  King's Crypt walkthrough
</h2>

My objective inside the Crypt is simple:

**keep descending through the main route until I reach the lowest level.**

I do not try to clear every optional room on the way down.

What changes is my movement.

Outside the Crypt, I am often comfortable sprinting past enemies that do not
matter.

Inside, I use a different rhythm:

**enter room → stop → check walls → check floor → identify enemies → move.**

The traps are more dangerous than many of the normal enemies.

That is why rushing King's Crypt often makes the dungeon feel harder than it
actually is.

<h2 id="wall-arrows">
  Wall-arrow traps — do not fight inside their firing lanes
</h2>

One of the first hazards I start watching for is the **wall-fired arrow trap**.

A body already covered in arrows is an obvious warning that I am entering one
of these lanes.

  <Image
    src="/images/mortal-shell-2/mortal-shell-2-kings-crypt-wall-arrow-traps.webp"

  />
  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">
    An enemy standing in an arrow lane does not make that position safe. I
    move the fight away from the trap before committing to a longer attack.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

If an enemy is waiting directly in front of the trap, I do not chase it into
the firing line.

I either:

- bait the enemy toward ground I already checked;
- wait for the trap to fire and move during the safe interval;
- use ranged pressure when that avoids entering the lane.

The rule I keep in mind is:

**enemy position is not necessarily safe position.**

The wall itself may be the bigger threat.

<h2 id="floor-spikes">
  Floor spikes — watch where the dodge ends
</h2>

The next major hazard is the **floor spike trap**.

These punish panic movement more than slow movement.

  <Image

  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">
    Avoiding an enemy attack is only half the dodge. I also need the dodge to
    finish on a safe section of floor.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

A dodge can avoid the enemy perfectly and still be a terrible dodge if it
lands me on spikes.

In narrow corridors, I would rather pull an enemy backward toward an area I
already checked than roll forward onto unknown flooring.

That becomes increasingly important as I descend.

The deeper I go, the less willing I am to assume that an apparently empty tile
is harmless.

<h2 id="optional-secret-wall">
  Optional hidden wall behind the exploding pots
</h2>

King's Crypt has side loot that is easy to miss if I only follow the main
Battle Axe route.

One optional opening I check is around the **cart and exploding pots**.

After clearing the pots, I check the wall behind that area. The hidden opening
can lead into additional loot rather than the mandatory route to the Axe.

I treat this as optional.

If my only objective is the Veteran's Battle Axe and I am already low on
healing, I continue downward instead of turning the run into a full secrets
clear.

That distinction saves time:

**main route gets me the weapon; side openings are extra.**

<h2 id="final-trap-section">
  Descend to the lowest level
</h2>

Once I am deep into King's Crypt, I resist the urge to move faster just because
the Battle Axe is close.

The final trap-heavy rooms punish exactly that kind of impatience.

My movement remains:

**watch → trigger → move → stop again.**

If a projectile lane and an enemy overlap, I solve the trap first.

If spikes limit my dodge direction, I move the fight back to ground I have
already checked.

Near the end of this section, I run past another cluster of traps and exploding
enemies, then slow down again at the next doorway. There is a **bear trap
directly in the entrance**, and it is easy to miss after focusing on the
enemies in the room.

I step around the bear trap before moving through the doorway.

From there, I **drop down into the final area**. That drop is my clearest sign
that I have reached the bottom section of King's Crypt and am close to the
Veteran's Battle Axe.

The weapon is not in an upper side chamber. I am trying to reach the Crypt's
lowest level.

<h2 id="final-guard">
  Defeat the guard near the Veteran's Battle Axe
</h2>

Near the end of the descent, the weapon is protected by a tougher enemy rather
than another major puzzle.

I clear the guard before trying to rush the pickup.

At this point I am willing to spend healing or Resolve I was saving earlier.

There is little value in dying with those resources unused when the weapon is
already within reach.

Once the enemy is down, I can safely approach the reward.

<h2 id="worth-doing-early">
  Is King's Crypt worth doing early?
</h2>

I make the early King's Crypt detour when I specifically want the
**Veteran's Battle Axe**.

It is worth doing when:

- I want a heavier weapon early;
- the running heavy fits how I like to start fights;
- I have enough healing to learn the trap layout;
- I am comfortable slowing down inside the dungeon.

I leave it for later when:

- my current weapon already feels good;
- I reach the Crypt nearly out of healing;
- I keep dying to traps without learning the rooms;
- I only want the Axe because I know it is technically available early.

**Available early does not mean mandatory early.**

If Iconoclast or Axe & Dagger already solves my current fights, I do not need
to force King's Crypt immediately.

If I specifically want a heavy weapon with a strong approach attack, the
detour makes much more sense.
