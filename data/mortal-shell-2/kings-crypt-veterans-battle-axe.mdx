**Black Ridge Pass**, curve around toward **King's Crypt**, and clear the main
route through the dungeon.

The weapon itself is not the hard part.

King's Crypt is packed with **wall-fired arrows, floor spikes, and trap-heavy

<h2 id="kings-crypt-route">
  The King's Crypt route I use
</h2>

My broad route is:

**Black Ridge Pass → northern route → King's Crypt → trap corridors →
Veteran's Battle Axe.**

I do not need to solve every side room in the dungeon to get the weapon.

The important change I make as soon as I enter is how I move.

Outside King's Crypt, I am comfortable running past enemies that do not block
my objective.

Inside King's Crypt, I stop assuming empty floor is safe.

<h2 id="why-kings-crypt-is-dangerous">
  Why King's Crypt kills me even when the enemies are manageable
</h2>

My first mistake was treating King's Crypt like another combat dungeon.

The enemies were not the main problem.

**The dungeon itself was.**

There are projectiles coming from the walls, spikes coming through the floor,
and narrow spaces where one normal dodge can put me directly into another
hazard.

That changes my basic rhythm.

Instead of:

**see enemy → rush enemy**

I use:

**enter → stop → check floor → check walls → move.**

That feels slower.

It is still much faster than dying to a trap I could have seen and repeating
the same corridor.

<h2 id="reach-kings-crypt">
  1. Head north through Black Ridge Pass
</h2>

I approach King's Crypt from the northern side of the map.

I head through **Black Ridge Pass**, keep working around the northern route, and
continue toward the Crypt.

King's Crypt gives me enough opportunities to make trap mistakes without
starting the run at low Health.

<h2 id="enter-kings-crypt">
  2. Slow down as soon as I enter King's Crypt
</h2>

Once the **King's Crypt** area appears, I immediately stop moving at my normal
exploration pace.

  <Image
    src="/images/mortal-shell-2/mortal-shell-2-kings-crypt-entrance.webp"
    alt="Entrance to King's Crypt in Mortal Shell 2"
    width={1600}
    height={900}
    className="rounded-xl"
  />
  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">
    The entrance is where I change my rule: traps first, enemies second.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

One of the early warnings is a body already covered in arrows.

I take that seriously.

I do not hug a wall because it looks safer than the middle of the corridor.

The wall may be the thing firing at me.

Before entering a new room, I look at:

- the floor directly ahead;
- both walls;
- where the enemy is standing;
- where my dodge would actually end.

That last one matters throughout the entire dungeon.

<h2 id="wall-arrows">
  3. Do not fight enemies inside the wall-arrow lanes
</h2>

The wall arrows are dangerous because they turn otherwise normal fights into
damage I never intended to take.

<figure className="my-8">
  <Image
    src="/images/mortal-shell-2/mortal-shell-2-kings-crypt-wall-arrow-traps.webp"
    alt="Wall-fired arrow traps inside King's Crypt in Mortal Shell 2"
    width={1600}
    height={900}
    className="rounded-xl"
  />
  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">
    An enemy standing in an arrow lane does not make that lane safe. I pull the
    fight away from the trap before committing to a longer combo.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

If an enemy is waiting directly in front of a projectile trap, I do not run
straight into its position just because that is where the target happens to be.

I either bait the enemy toward safer ground, wait for the trap to fire before
moving through, or use range when that saves me from entering the lane at all.

The important distinction is:

**enemy position is not necessarily safe position.**

I choose where the fight happens whenever the room lets me.

<h2 id="floor-spikes">
  4. Watch where the dodge ends around floor spikes
</h2>

The floor traps punish a different habit.

They punish panic movement.

  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">
    Avoiding the enemy attack is only half the dodge. I also need the dodge to
    end on a safe tile.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

Outside this dungeon, I can often react almost entirely to the enemy's attack.

Here I have to think about the space behind me as well.

A perfectly timed dodge is still bad if it deposits me onto spikes.

In a narrow corridor, I am more willing to pull an enemy backward into an area
I already checked rather than rolling forward into an unknown section of floor.

King's Crypt punishes movement without information.

<h2 id="side-rooms">
  I do not solve every suspicious side room during the weapon run
</h2>

King's Crypt has enough strange openings and partially visible rooms that it is
easy to turn one weapon route into a full dungeon investigation.

I do not do that on my first Battle Axe run.

If I can see something interesting behind a wall but the route is not obvious,
I remember the location and continue.

My rule is:

**if the room is not blocking the main route to the axe, it is not automatically
part of the axe route.**

That saves me from spending too long attacking suspicious walls or searching
for mechanisms that may only make sense once I reach another side of the
dungeon.

I can explore those areas later.

First I finish the objective I came here for.

<h2 id="final-trap-section">
  5. I stay cautious even when the Battle Axe is close
</h2>

The final stretch is where impatience becomes most expensive.

Once I know the weapon is close, it is very tempting to start moving faster.

I do the opposite.

I handle the last trap sequence the same way I handled the first:

**watch → trigger → move → stop again.**

I do not let seeing a reward ahead convince me that the dangerous part is over.

The dungeon does not care how close I am to finishing it.

<h2 id="check-dorsalite">
  Check the area around the weapon before leaving
</h2>

I do not teleport out the exact second I collect the axe.

There is useful loot immediately around this part of the dungeon, including
**Dorsalite**.

I make a quick check of the nearby rooms before leaving.

That is different from trying to 100% King's Crypt.

I am only checking the immediate reward area while I am already standing there.

If a secret requires another route or a puzzle I still do not understand, I
leave it.

The Battle Axe was my objective.

I do not turn every optional room into a condition for completing the run.

<h2 id="worth-doing-early">
  Is King's Crypt worth doing early?
</h2>

For me, King's Crypt is worth an early detour when I specifically want the
**Veteran's Battle Axe**.

I make the trip when:

- I want something heavier than my current weapon;
- I like opening fights with running attacks;
- the Battle Axe's running heavy solves a problem in my current moveset;
- I have enough healing to survive a few trap mistakes while learning the
  dungeon.

I leave it for later when:

- my current weapon already feels good;
- I am reaching King's Crypt almost out of healing;
- I am repeatedly dying to traps without making useful progress;
- I only want the axe because it is technically obtainable early.

That last distinction matters.

**Obtainable early does not mean mandatory early.**

If I already like the Iconoclast or the Axe & Dagger, I do not need another
weapon simply because King's Crypt is available.

If I want a heavier weapon with a running attack I can build my engagements
around, then the dungeon becomes worth the trouble.

<h2 id="kings-crypt-route-summary">
  The King's Crypt route I remember
</h2>

My short version is:

**Black Ridge Pass**

→ follow the northern route

→ enter **King's Crypt**

→ check walls for projectile traps

→ check floors before dodging forward

→ clear the final trap section

→ collect **Veteran's Battle Axe**

→ check the nearby reward area

→ leave when I am done.

If enemies keep killing me inside King's Crypt, I work on the fight.

If the environment keeps killing me, I slow down.

The Battle Axe is at the end of the route.

Getting there alive is the real puzzle.
