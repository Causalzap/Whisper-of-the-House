<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
**Mortal Shell 2 has two endings I can trigger in the same playthrough: the
main story ending and the secret Baghead ending.**

For the **Baghead secret ending**, I find Baghead on Desolate Peak, sit with
his followers, take the Special Moonshine, refuse to give it to him, put it
back, and return empty-handed. The answer to his request is literally
**nothing**. Completing the sequence triggers the joke ending and the
**Bag Holder** achievement, but I can Continue afterward and resume the same
playthrough.

For the **main ending**, I recover enough Ova to unlock the **Unfound Path**,
clear its final encounters, and defeat **Zmey, the Unbidden**. Zmey is not
the actual point of no return. I can still explore afterward.

The real point of no return is choosing **Send Ova** at the Gloom Siphon in
Marrow Keep. That triggers the final farewell and advances the save into
**New Game Plus**.

If I want to see both endings in one run, I do **Baghead first and the main
ending last**.

<h2 id="how-many-endings">
  How many endings are there in Mortal Shell 2?
</h2>

I count **two actual ending sequences** in Mortal Shell 2:

1. the **Baghead secret ending**;
2. the **main story ending** tied to Undermether, the Ova, and the Unfound Path.

They do not force me into two separate playthroughs.

That distinction matters because the two endings behave very differently.

The Baghead ending rolls its own credits, but I can load back in and continue
the same run.

The main ending is different.

Once I choose **Send Ova** at the end of the story, the game advances into
the **New Game Plus cycle**.

So my preferred order is simple:

**Baghead ending first → finish anything else I care about → main ending
last.**

<h2 id="baghead-secret-ending">
  How to get the Baghead secret ending
</h2>

The Baghead ending is the easier one to miss because his request sounds like
a normal fetch quest.

It is not.

The whole joke is that Baghead wants me to understand **nothing**.

I find Baghead on **Desolate Peak in western Fainweald**, near the
Illusionist's Cache area.

He is sitting on a throne with a group of followers around him.

The first time through, I expected him to want a rare item.

That expectation is exactly what makes the quest confusing.

<h3 id="baghead-steps">
  Baghead secret ending steps
</h3>

I use this sequence:

1. **Talk to Baghead repeatedly.**
   I keep going until he tells me to sit down and be quiet.

2. **Sit with his followers.**
   I use the nearby sitting interaction, wait briefly, then stand back up.

3. **Talk to Baghead again.**
   When he asks whether I think I am different, I answer **Yes**.

4. **Let him give me the request.**
   He sends me looking for something special.

5. **Pick up the Special Moonshine nearby.**
   It is close to the Illusionist's Cache entrance.

6. **Return to Baghead with the Moonshine.**
   When the conversation gives me the chance to treat it as the answer, I do
   **not** commit to giving it to him.

7. **Put the Special Moonshine back where I found it.**
   This is the step that makes the puzzle click.

8. **Return to Baghead empty-handed.**
   When he points out that I have brought him nothing, I confirm it.

9. **Confirm again when he warns that this is my final chance.**

10. **Talk to Baghead one more time.**
    The secret ending sequence begins.

The important answer is therefore:

**What should I give Baghead? Nothing.**

Not a better drink.

Not another rare item.

Not something hidden in a late-game dungeon.

I need to come back without the Special Moonshine and commit to having
brought him **nothing**.

<h2 id="what-happens-baghead-ending">
  What happens in the Baghead ending?
</h2>

Once Baghead accepts that I finally understand nothing, the game commits to
the joke.

The Harbinger effectively takes over the whole philosophy.

Instead of continuing the mission, I end up on the throne as the
**king of nothing**, surrounded by Baghead's followers while time passes.

It feels for a moment like I have actually thrown away the entire run.

I have not.

The credits roll, but this is not the same commitment as the main story
ending.

Afterward I can choose **Continue** and return to the playthrough.

That is why I am comfortable triggering Baghead as soon as I find him rather
than saving him until the end of the game.

<h2 id="bag-holder">
  The Baghead ending unlocks Bag Holder
</h2>

Completing the sequence also gives the hidden **Bag Holder**
achievement/trophy.

That is useful confirmation that I solved the quest correctly.

If Baghead keeps sending me away or the ending does not trigger, I check
three things first:

- I actually sat with his followers;
- I put the Special Moonshine back instead of keeping it;
- I returned to Baghead with nothing and confirmed my choice.

The Moonshine step is the easiest one to overthink.

The bottle looks like the obvious quest item because the game deliberately
puts it there for me to test.

The solution is understanding that **the item is not the answer**.

<h2 id="main-ending">
  How to get the main Mortal Shell 2 ending
</h2>

The main ending is tied to the Ova recovery story.

I eventually need enough recovered Ova for Undermether to tell me that they
can secure the future of the kin.

The threshold that opens the final route is **168 Ova**.

I do not need to treat every remaining collectible as mandatory before I can
progress.

Once I reach the required amount, the story changes from:

**find more Ova**

to:

**deal with the foul passenger and walk the Unfound Path.**

<h2 id="when-ending-starts">
  Walk the Unfound Path is the start of the final stretch
</h2>

The clearest signal that I have entered the final act is Undermether telling
me that the recovered Ova are enough, but that they still cannot be sent to
the heavens.

The remaining problem is:

**the foul passenger.**

That gives me the objective:

**Walk the Unfound Path.**

<h2 id="unfound-path">
  What I have to do in the Unfound Path
</h2>

The Unfound Path is not just one straight run to the final boss.

I have to clear its major branches and the encounters protecting the route
forward.

The important late-game fights include:

- **Malborn Offspring**;
- **The Monolith**;
- **Orrem**;
- and finally **Zmey, the Unbidden**.

The exact order in which I explore the branches matters less to me than
making sure the path to the center is fully opened.

This is also the point where I stop experimenting with completely new gear.

I would rather take:

- the Shell whose defensive rhythm I already understand;
- the weapon whose recovery windows I know;
- Tarstones and upgrades I have actually tested;
- enough healing and Resolve support to survive mistakes.

If I still have resources reserved for the weapon I already plan to use,
this is when I spend them.

I do not rebuild three different setups just because the word "final" has
appeared.

<h2 id="point-of-no-return">
  Send Ova is the real point of no return
</h2>

The action I treat as the actual point of no return is **Send Ova** at the
Gloom Siphon in Marrow Keep.

This is much later than I originally expected.

Neither entering the Unfound Path nor defeating Zmey is the final lock.

**Send Ova is.**

The game warns me that sending the Ova concludes this part of the story and
advances the **New Game Plus cycle**.

So my cleanup rule is:

**Do anything I want to preserve in the current cycle before selecting Send
Ova.**

That includes anything I want to finish for its own sake, not every icon on
the map just because it exists.

I do not need to panic when the Unfound Path opens.

I do need to stop and think before I send the Ova.

Then the ending changes focus from them to the Harbinger.

  Why the Harbinger stays behind
</h2>

The Harbinger does not leave with the others.

Undermether tells him:

**he was not made for the heavens.**

He must stay behind.

And, most importantly, he must:

**find his own meaning.**

This is the part of the ending that matters most to me.

The entire game trains me to think in assigned purposes.

The Harbinger is told where to go.

The Ova need to be recovered.

The foul passenger needs to be removed.

Every Shell carries the remains of another life, with its own history,
identity, and unfinished weight attached to it.

Then the final scene removes that structure.

The kin have somewhere to go.

The Harbinger does not.

For the first time, surviving is not the same thing as receiving another
objective.

<h2 id="ending-meaning">
  What the Mortal Shell 2 ending means
</h2>

I read the main ending as the Harbinger finally being separated from a life
defined by other people's purposes.

That idea fits the way the entire game works.

I spend the journey inhabiting Shells.

I uncover pieces of their memories.

I develop abilities built around identities that existed before me.

Even the main quest gives the Harbinger a purpose before he has the chance
to choose one for himself:

**recover the kin and help Undermether.**

By the time the foul passenger is gone, that purpose has been fulfilled.

The kin leave.

Undermether's task is finished.

The Harbinger remains.

So when he is told to find his own meaning, I do not read that as a hidden
hint toward one more quest marker.

I read it as the first time the game deliberately refuses to give him one.

<h2 id="ending-choice">
  Is there a good or bad ending choice?
</h2>

I do not get a final morality choice where I decide between a good ending
and a bad ending.

The main story outcome I reach follows the same final sequence:

**remove the foul passenger → send the kin onward → Harbinger stays behind.**

The alternate ending is Baghead.

It is not created by choosing the opposite answer during the final
Undermether scene.

So I would not replay Zmey repeatedly looking for a hidden dialogue branch
inside the main finale.

If I want the other ending sequence, I go back to **Baghead's nothing
quest** instead.

<h2 id="can-you-keep-playing">
  Can I keep playing after the ending?
</h2>

It depends on which ending I mean.

### After the Baghead secret ending

**Yes.**

The joke ending rolls credits, but I can choose Continue and return to the
same playthrough.

That is why I recommend doing Baghead first.

### After defeating Zmey

**Yes.**

Zmey is the final boss, but the current cycle remains open until I choose
Send Ova.

This is the best cleanup window if I have already beaten the final boss but
still want to finish something.

### After choosing Send Ova

**The game advances into New Game Plus.**

This is the one I treat as irreversible for the current cycle.

So if the question is:

**“Can I beat the final boss and still explore?”**

Yes.

If the question is:

**“Can I press Send Ova and stay in the same normal playthrough?”**

I do not plan around that.

I finish my current-cycle cleanup before sending them.

<h2 id="best-ending-order">
  Best order for getting both Mortal Shell 2 endings
</h2>

For one playthrough, I use this order:

1. Progress normally until I can reach **Desolate Peak**.
2. Complete Baghead's sitting interaction.
3. Pick up the **Special Moonshine**.
4. Refuse it as the answer and put it back.
5. Return with **nothing**.
6. Trigger the **Baghead secret ending**.
7. Choose Continue and resume the run.
8. Recover enough Ova to unlock the **Unfound Path**.
9. Clear the final route and defeat **Zmey, the Unbidden**.
10. Finish any current-cycle cleanup I still care about.
11. Return to the Gloom Siphon.
12. Choose **Send Ova**.
13. Watch the main ending and continue into **New Game Plus**.

That order gives me both endings without sacrificing the main playthrough to
Baghead's joke.

It also keeps the only truly irreversible action — **Send Ova** — until I am
actually ready to leave the current cycle behind.
