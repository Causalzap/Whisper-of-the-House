<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
**Yes, you can beat the Tar Golem in Mortal Shell 2, but you do not have to
beat it to continue the game.**

The Tar Golem is the opening boss in **Disciple's Grotto**. Losing advances
the prologue normally. Winning also advances the same opening sequence, but
defeating the boss before the encounter ends unlocks the hidden
**No, You Still Can't Win** achievement/trophy.

If I want the achievement, I play patiently: use **Perfect Guards** when I am
comfortable with the timing to build Break pressure, dodge the longer sweeping
sequences, take short punish windows, and stop attacking before greed turns a
good opening into a trade.

While I am still in the opening encounter, I can use the **cliff at the rear
of the arena** to discard a bad attempt. A retry starts from scratch with the
Tar Golem back at **full Health**.

Once the opening encounter has resolved and I continue the story, I do not
expect the Tar Golem to respawn later in that playthrough. If the achievement
matters to me, I get the kill before moving on.

<h2 id="can-you-beat-tar-golem">
  Can you beat the Tar Golem in Mortal Shell 2?
</h2>

**Yes. The Tar Golem is a real, winnable boss.**

I encounter it near the end of the opening prologue in **Disciple's Grotto**.
It has a normal Health bar, takes real damage, and can be reduced all the way
to zero.

**Can I kill the Tar Golem? Yes.**

<h2 id="do-you-have-to-beat-tar-golem">
  Do you have to beat the Tar Golem?
</h2>

**No. Losing to the Tar Golem does not block progression.**

If the boss defeats me, the prologue continues normally.

I do not need to:

- restart the save;
- search Disciple's Grotto for an upgrade I supposedly missed;
- lower the difficulty;
- find a hidden healing item;
- defeat the boss before the rest of Mortal Shell 2 becomes available.

This matters because the Tar Golem appears before I have access to the normal
range of Shells, weapons, upgrades, Tarstones, Infusions, Sidearms, and healing
options that shape combat later.

If my goal is simply to start the main game, losing here is a valid outcome.

If I want the hidden achievement or enjoy beating difficult opening bosses,
then the fight becomes an optional challenge worth retrying.

<h2 id="is-tar-golem-scripted-loss">
  Is the Tar Golem a scripted loss?
</h2>

**Not in the sense that the boss is impossible to defeat.**

A genuinely unwinnable scripted boss would prevent me from reaching zero
Health no matter how well I play.

Tar Golem does not.

I can empty its Health bar and win the fight.

What is scripted is the **direction of the prologue afterward**. Whether I win
or lose, the opening still moves toward the same broader progression instead
of splitting into two campaign routes.

The cleanest way I describe the fight is:

**winnable boss, expected loss, converging story outcome.**

That explains why the encounter can look like a forced defeat while still
rewarding players who actually manage to win.

<h2 id="what-happens-if-you-beat-tar-golem">
  What happens if you beat the Tar Golem?
</h2>

Defeating the Tar Golem unlocks the hidden:

**No, You Still Can't Win**

achievement/trophy.

That is the main reason I would deliberately stay in the prologue and keep
retrying the boss.

Victory does **not** open a secret campaign branch or let me bypass the
prologue's intended outcome. The opening sequence still continues after the
fight and my Shell is still lost as part of that progression.

So the reward for winning is primarily:

- proving I defeated the opening challenge;
- unlocking **No, You Still Can't Win**;
- seeing the victory version of the encounter before the prologue continues.

I do not grind the fight because I expect a unique main-story route, permanent
weapon reward, or different world state afterward.

<h2 id="how-to-beat-tar-golem">
  How to beat the Tar Golem
</h2>

When I decide I want the kill, the fight becomes much more manageable once I
stop treating every opening as permission for a full combo.

The Tar Golem is large, but its follow-up attacks arrive quickly enough to
punish greed.

My basic rhythm is:

**read the attack → defend or dodge the full sequence → punish briefly →
disengage → reset my spacing.**

### Use Perfect Guards when I know the timing

Perfect Guards are valuable because they let me build **Break pressure** while
defending.

I do not force them against attacks I still cannot read consistently. Missing a
risky guard is much more expensive here than it is later in the game when I
have a developed build and better recovery options.

Once I recognize a move reliably, Perfect Guard becomes one of the fastest
ways to turn defense into a larger opening.

### Dodge the full sweeping sequence before attacking

The mistake I made early was seeing the first swing miss and immediately
attacking.

Tar Golem can continue through wider sweeping sequences, so I wait until I am
sure the sequence has finished before committing.

One safe attack is better than landing two and eating the next swing.

### Use short punish windows

Until I have created a clear Break or stagger opportunity, I keep my punish
small.

My default is:

**one safe hit → leave**

or, when I clearly have more time:

**short combo → leave.**

I save the longer commitment for the openings I actually understand.

### Do not trade damage

The opening setup gives me very little room to recover from sloppy exchanges.

Later in Mortal Shell 2 I can sometimes accept a trade because healing,
upgrades, Shell abilities, and other systems give me ways to recover.

Here, every unnecessary hit makes the rest of the attempt harder.

I therefore prioritize:

**consistency over speed.**

The boss does not need to die quickly.

It needs to lose one full Health bar before I do.

I do not reset just because I took one hit.

I reset when the attempt has become a poor use of time, such as:

- several bad hits in the opening;
- Tar Golem still has almost all of its Health;
- I have stopped reading attacks and started panic dodging;
- I am repeatedly forcing unsafe punish windows.

If the boss is already low and I still have control of the fight, I keep going.

The cliff is simply a faster way to discard an attempt I no longer expect to
finish cleanly.

### Does Tar Golem Health reset when I retry?

**Yes.**

A retry is a completely new fight.

Damage does not carry between attempts.

<h2 id="does-tar-golem-respawn">
  Does the Tar Golem respawn later?
</h2>

I separate **retrying the opening fight** from **returning to the boss after the
prologue**.

While I am still in the Tar Golem encounter, I can discard a bad attempt and
try again.

Once the encounter has resolved and I continue beyond the prologue, I do not
expect Tar Golem to appear later as a normal respawning boss in that
playthrough.

That matters most for **No, You Still Can't Win**.

If I specifically want the achievement, I would secure the Tar Golem kill
before allowing the opening encounter to finish.

If I have already continued past it without the achievement, I treat a
**fresh save** as the reliable way to reach the fight again rather than
expecting a later rematch location to appear.

<h2 id="should-you-beat-tar-golem">
  Should you beat the Tar Golem on your first playthrough?
</h2>

I only insist on the win for one reason:

**I want the achievement or the challenge itself.**

If I want **No, You Still Can't Win**, this is the time to earn it.

If I mainly want to begin Mortal Shell 2, I do not turn the prologue into a
wall that the game itself does not require me to clear.

My decision is simple.

### I want normal progression

I fight the Tar Golem, learn the basic defensive rhythm, and continue if I
lose.

### I want the hidden achievement

I stay in the opening encounter, use Perfect Guards and short punish windows,
reset clearly bad attempts with the rear cliff, and keep going until I remove
one full Health bar.

That is the useful distinction behind the entire fight:

**Tar Golem is a boss I can beat.**

**It is not a boss I must beat before Mortal Shell 2 lets me continue.**
