<h2 id="do-you-have-to-beat-tar-golem">
  Do I have to beat the Tar Golem?
</h2>

**No.**

This is the most important thing I want to know before spending the opening of
the game restarting the same boss.

The Tar Golem looks like a normal progression gate.

It has a real boss health bar.

I can damage it.

And the game does not immediately put a message on the screen saying:

**you are allowed to lose here.**

That makes the first attempt feel much more important than it really is.

I enter the arena with **no healing available**, and the Tar Golem can attack
quickly enough that a few early mistakes make the fight look hopeless.

On a normal first playthrough, I do not interpret that as:

**I need to restart until I win.**

I treat the encounter as an introduction to what Mortal Shell 2 is about to ask
from me.

If I lose, progression continues.

<h2 id="is-it-scripted-loss">
  Is the Tar Golem a scripted loss?
</h2>

Not exactly.

Calling it a scripted loss makes it sound as though the game prevents me from
winning regardless of how well I play.

That is not what happens.

**The Tar Golem can be beaten.**

The better description for me is:

**winnable fight, expected loss.**

That distinction matters because the game is prepared for either outcome.

If I lose, the opening continues.

If I win, I have beaten a difficult opening challenge.

What I do **not** get is a completely different campaign route simply because I
managed to take the health bar to zero.

So I never feel obligated to prove the fight is winnable before allowing myself
to play the rest of the game.

<h2 id="what-happens-if-you-lose">
  What happens if I lose to the Tar Golem?
</h2>

The game continues normally.

That is the practical answer.

When the Tar Golem defeats me, the opening sequence moves forward instead of
sending me back to grind or forcing me to restart the save.

My Shell protects me, and the game continues introducing the systems that come
after the opening fight.

So after a normal loss, I do **not**:

- restart my character;
- reload because I think I failed a hidden objective;
- assume the main story is blocked;
- keep repeating the fight purely because I think victory is mandatory.

I let the sequence continue.

That is especially useful to know because the Tar Golem appears before I have
access to many of the tools that later make combat much more flexible.

The opening is deliberately asking a lot from a very limited setup.

<h2 id="what-happens-if-you-win">
  What happens if I beat the Tar Golem?
</h2>

Winning is possible.

What it does **not** do is meaningfully change the progression route that comes
next.

I do not treat beating the Tar Golem as an early shortcut into a different
version of the game.

The story still needs to move into the same broader opening progression.

That means I separate two reasons for fighting it.

If my goal is:

**start progressing through Mortal Shell 2**

then a loss is completely acceptable.

If my goal is:

**beat a difficult opening boss with the limited tools I have right now**

then the victory itself is the reason I keep trying.

I would not grind the encounter because I expect a completely different opening
story afterward.

And I would not claim that winning has no value at all — it is still a real
fight that I can beat.

It simply is not required for the progression outcome I care about here.

<h2 id="how-to-retry">
  How I retry the Tar Golem fight
</h2>

If I decide I want the win, I do not have to treat the first attempt as my only
chance.

There is a **cliff at the back of the arena**.

If the attempt is already going badly, I run to the rear and jump off.

I reset and start again.

If the opening is clean, I keep fighting.

<h2 id="boss-health-resets">
  Does Tar Golem health carry over between retries?
</h2>

No.

Resetting the fight gives me another chance, but it does **not** preserve the
damage from the previous attempt.

The Tar Golem comes back with a fresh health bar.

I reset when the attempt has become inefficient.

start fresh.

<h2 id="how-i-fight-tar-golem">
  How I approach the Tar Golem if I actually want to win
</h2>

The opening fight taught me one rule faster than anything else:

**do not turn one safe attack into three unsafe attacks.**

The Tar Golem is quicker than I initially expect from something that large.

I cannot assume every animation will give me a long, slow wind-up.

And when I find an opening, I do not automatically extend the punish just
because my first attack connected.

My safer rhythm is:

**wait for an attack → take the punish I understand → recover → watch again.**

The extra hit is rarely worth losing control of the attempt.

That is especially true here because I do not have a large healing buffer to
cover sloppy trades.

When I am deliberately practicing the fight, I use each attempt to answer one
specific question:

**Which attack did I safely punish?**

**Where did I get greedy?**

**Which dodge direction kept me in control?**

If I am learning something, the attempt has value even before I get the win.

<h2 id="when-i-reset">
  When I abandon an attempt and start fresh
</h2>

I do not use a fixed Health percentage.

I look at how the attempt feels.

I reset early when:

- I have already taken several bad hits;
- the Tar Golem still has most of its Health;
- I am panic dodging instead of reading attacks;
- I am no longer learning anything from the attempt.

I keep going when:

- I have already removed a meaningful part of the health bar;
- my spacing still feels controlled;
- I am recognizing the attack timing;
- another clean punish cycle could realistically finish the fight.

The cliff is what makes this useful.

I can discard a bad attempt without pretending every run deserves to reach its
natural conclusion.

<h2 id="should-you-beat-it">
  Is beating the Tar Golem worth it?
</h2>

For a normal first playthrough, I would **not make Tar Golem victory a
requirement for myself**.

The game already allows me to lose and continue.

If my goal is seeing what Mortal Shell 2 actually opens into, I would rather move
forward, reach the real progression systems, find a Shell, and begin exploring.

<h2 id="my-rule">
  The rule I use for the opening boss
</h2>

I reduce the whole Tar Golem decision to one question:

**Am I trying to start Mortal Shell 2, or am I trying to beat the Tar Golem?**

If I am trying to start the game:

**I fight it, learn what I can, and accept the loss if it happens.**

If I specifically want to beat it:

**I use the rear cliff to reset bad attempts and keep only the runs that still
have a realistic chance of clearing the full health bar.**

That keeps the fight in the right place for me.

It is a real boss I can beat.

It is not a boss I must beat before Mortal Shell 2 lets me continue.
