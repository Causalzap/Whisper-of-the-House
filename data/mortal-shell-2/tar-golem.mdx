<h2 id="can-you-beat-tar-golem">
  Can you beat the Tar Golem in Mortal Shell 2?
</h2>

**Yes.**

The Tar Golem has a real Health bar, takes real damage, and can be defeated.

The opening is not designed so that the boss becomes invincible at low Health
or kills me automatically before I can finish it.

That makes the best description of the encounter:

**winnable fight, expected loss.**

The distinction matters because the fight happens before I have access to most

I enter with an extremely limited setup.

I do not have a normal collection of healing resources, upgraded weapons,
Tarstones, Infusions, Sidearms, or a developed Shell build.

So if the Tar Golem destroys me on the first attempt, I do not interpret that
as evidence that I have missed an upgrade.

The game is prepared for me to lose.

<h2 id="do-you-have-to-beat-tar-golem">
  Do you have to beat the Tar Golem?
</h2>

**No.**

I can lose the opening Tar Golem fight and continue the game normally.

This is the single most useful thing to know before spending a long time
restarting the beginning of the game.

The encounter looks like a normal mandatory boss gate because:

* it has a boss Health bar;
* I can damage it normally;
* the game lets me keep fighting;
* nothing immediately tells me that losing is acceptable.

That presentation makes victory feel mandatory.

It is not.

If my only goal is to begin the actual Mortal Shell 2 progression loop, I
fight the Tar Golem, learn what I can from the encounter, and allow the
opening to continue if I lose.

I do not restart the save.

I do not assume I ruined the run.

I do not need to defeat it before I can begin exploring the rest of the game.

<h2 id="is-tar-golem-scripted-loss">
  Is the Tar Golem a scripted loss?
</h2>

**Not in the strict sense.**

A scripted-loss boss usually means the game intends to defeat me regardless
of how well I play.

That is not what happens here.

I can kill the Tar Golem.

So I do not call it an unwinnable fight.

What Mortal Shell 2 does instead is allow both outcomes:

**lose → opening continues**

**win → opening still continues**

That is why I think of it as an **expected loss rather than a forced loss**.

<h2 id="what-happens-if-you-beat-tar-golem">
  What happens if you beat the Tar Golem?
</h2>

Beating the Tar Golem proves that the opening boss is a real, winnable
challenge.

What it does **not** do is create a completely different progression route.

I still move into the broader opening of Mortal Shell 2 afterward.

So I separate two questions.

### Do I need to win for progression?

**No.**

### Can I win because I want the challenge?

**Yes.**

That distinction stops me from grinding the fight for the wrong reason.

If I am repeatedly fighting it because I enjoy the challenge, that makes
sense.

If I am repeatedly fighting it because I think the rest of the game is locked
behind the victory screen, I am solving a problem that does not exist.

<h2 id="should-you-beat-tar-golem">
  Should you beat the Tar Golem?
</h2>

For a normal first playthrough, **I would not make Tar Golem victory a
requirement before continuing**.

The rest of Mortal Shell 2 gives me:

* different Shells;
* weapon choices;
* upgrades;
* Tarstones;
* Sidearms;
* healing options;
* a large world to explore.

I would rather reach those systems than spend the entire beginning of the game
restarting one encounter simply because the boss Health bar makes it look
mandatory.

I keep trying when:

**I specifically want to beat the Tar Golem.**

I move on when:

**I specifically want to play Mortal Shell 2.**

There is no contradiction between those decisions.

<h2 id="how-to-retry-tar-golem">
  How to retry the Tar Golem fight
</h2>

If I decide I want to beat the boss, there is a useful way to discard a bad
attempt.

At the **back of the arena** is a cliff.

When an attempt is already falling apart, I can run to the rear and jump off.

<h2 id="does-tar-golem-health-reset">
  Does the Tar Golem's Health reset?
</h2>

**Yes.**

Every retry is a fresh fight.

Damage does not carry over.

If I jump from the arena and start again, the Tar Golem returns with a
**full Health bar**.

<h2 id="how-to-beat-tar-golem">
  How I beat the Tar Golem
</h2>

When I actually decide to go for the win, the biggest mistake I avoid is
**greed**.

The Tar Golem is much faster than its size initially suggests.

If I see one safe opening and mentally convert it into a three-hit combo, I
usually turn a good situation into a trade.

My safer rhythm is:

**watch attack → dodge → take the punish I understand → disengage → watch
again.**

I would rather land one attack I know is safe than a second attack that leaves
me committed when the Golem starts moving.

That matters even more in the opening because I do not have a normal healing
buffer.

Every unnecessary hit has a larger effect on the attempt.

<h2 id="tar-golem-first-attempt">
  What I learn on the first attempt
</h2>

I do not expect the first run to be the winning run.

Instead, I use it to answer a few practical questions.

### Which attacks give me a real punish window?

Not every missed Tar Golem attack means I have time for a full combo.

I first identify the recoveries that clearly give me time to attack.

### Where am I getting greedy?

If I consistently take damage after my second or third hit, the problem may
not be the dodge.

The problem may be that I stayed too long.

### Which dodge leaves me in a useful position?

A dodge that avoids damage but sends me far away can still cost an attack
window.

I pay attention to where I end up, not only whether I avoided the hit.

### Am I still reading the boss?

Once I start panic dodging, the attempt usually becomes much less useful.

At that point I either calm the fight down or reset.

<h2 id="tar-golem-no-healing">
  How I handle the fight without normal healing
</h2>

The lack of a normal healing buffer is one of the biggest reasons the opening
boss feels harsher than many fights that come later.

I cannot use the same strategy I might use once my build is established:

**trade damage → heal → continue pressure.**

Here, avoiding the unnecessary hit matters more.

That makes patience more valuable than raw aggression.

I do not need to win the fight quickly.

I need to keep the attempt stable long enough to remove the entire Health
bar.

So if choosing between:

**one safe hit**

and:

**two hits with a real chance of getting punished**

I take the first option.

<h2 id="when-to-reset-tar-golem">
  When I reset a Tar Golem attempt
</h2>

I do not use a fixed boss Health percentage.

I look at the state of the attempt.

I usually reset when:

* I took several bad hits immediately;
* the boss still has most of its Health;
* my dodging has become reactive and panicked;
* I am repeatedly forcing attacks that are not safe;
* the run has stopped teaching me anything useful.

I keep going when:

* I have already removed a large part of the Health bar;
* I am consistently recognizing its attacks;
* my spacing still feels controlled;
* the fight is one or two clean punish cycles from ending.

The rear cliff means I do not need to emotionally commit to every run just
because I started it.

A bad attempt can simply be a bad attempt.

<h2 id="tar-golem-keeps-killing-me">
  What if the Tar Golem keeps killing me?
</h2>

The first thing I check is **why I am still fighting it**.

If the answer is:

**because I think I have to win to progress**

then I stop.

I do not have to.

If the answer is:

**because I want to beat it**

then I simplify the fight.

I stop trying to discover a complicated strategy and focus on three things:

**survive the attack**

→ **recognize a safe punish**

→ **do not overstay**

If I am dying after successfully avoiding the initial attack, I look at how
many extra hits I am trying to squeeze in.

If I am dying before I ever get an opening, I spend another attempt watching
the boss instead of attacking immediately.

If an attempt begins terribly, I use the cliff and reset.

The fight becomes much more manageable once I stop treating every attempt as
the one that has to win.

<h2 id="tar-golem-win-vs-loss">
  Tar Golem win vs loss
</h2>

The decision is much simpler than the boss presentation makes it look.

| Result                         | What I do next                                     |
| ------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------- |
| I lose normally                | **Continue the story**                             |
| I beat the Tar Golem           | **Continue the story after winning the challenge** |
| Attempt starts badly           | **Use the rear cliff if I want another try**       |
| I restart the fight            | **Boss returns to full Health**                    |
| I only want normal progression | **I do not require a win**                         |
| I want the opening challenge   | **I keep retrying until I beat it**                |

The key point is that **winning and progressing are not the same requirement**.

That is what I wish I knew before treating the opening Health bar like a
mandatory wall.

<h2 id="tar-golem-faq">
  Tar Golem questions I would answer before retrying
</h2>

### Can the Tar Golem actually die?

Yes.

It is a real winnable boss encounter.

### Is the Tar Golem an unwinnable tutorial boss?

No.

The game allows victory.

### Am I supposed to lose?

The game clearly supports losing and continuing, so I treat defeat as an
expected outcome.

That is different from saying the fight cannot be won.

### Does losing ruin my playthrough?

No.

I continue into the game normally.

### Do I need to restart if I lose?

No.

### Does winning unlock a different main progression route?

I do not get a separate campaign route from beating the opening boss.

### Can I retry the fight?

Yes.

I can use the cliff at the back of the arena to discard an attempt and try
again.

### Does Tar Golem damage carry over?

No.

A retry starts with the boss back at full Health.

### Should I keep trying on my first playthrough?

Only if beating the opening challenge is something I actually want to do.

If I mainly want to begin exploring Mortal Shell 2, I am comfortable accepting
the loss and continuing.

<h2 id="tar-golem-decision">
  The Tar Golem rule I use
</h2>

I reduce the entire opening boss to one question:

**Am I trying to play Mortal Shell 2, or am I trying to beat the Tar Golem?**

If I am trying to play Mortal Shell 2:

**fight it → learn what I can → lose if I lose → continue.**

If I am trying to beat the Tar Golem:

**fight carefully → take short punish windows → reset clearly bad attempts →
defeat one full Health bar in a clean run.**

That keeps the encounter in the right place.

The Tar Golem is a **real boss I can beat**.

It is **not a boss I must beat before Mortal Shell 2 lets me continue**.
