<h2 id="opening-route">
  Challenge 5 is decided by when you move, not how fast you reach the gate
</h2>

The opening looks harmless enough that sprinting feels like the obvious choice.

That is where I would slow the run down.

As soon as Michael can move, let the first orderly coming from behind continue
past you. Do not try to beat him to the next section.

Walk down the route and stay near the edge until the lights ahead become your
reference point. Turn back there and **walk up the steps**.

The top of the steps is the first place where running becomes useful.

From there, move behind the ambulance.

Now count **two guards**.

The first passes fairly quickly.

The second takes longer because his movement is tied to the activity farther
down the route. Seeing the first flashlight disappear is not the signal to
leave.

Wait for the second guard as well.

Once he has passed, the route opens enough to move toward the next section
without trying to squeeze between two patrols.

That single wait saves more time than an aggressive start followed by another
restart.

<h2 id="restart-check">
  One stuck orderly is enough reason to restart
</h2>

There is a much better reset signal than “I think somebody might have seen me.”

Watch the orderly you reach after leaving the ambulance section.

On a normal attempt, he should continue through his patrol.

If he is standing in place when he should be moving, the route ahead no longer
matches the clean path.

This is the point where restarting makes sense.

You are still early enough in the chapter that little has been invested, while
continuing means every later timing window has to compensate for an NPC who is
already in the wrong place.

That is different from hearing an alarm later in the run.

A general alarm sound does **not** automatically mean Challenge 5 is dead. A
successful attempt can reach the later Smith's Grove section with the alarm
going and still finish the challenge.

The useful question is narrower:

**Did an orderly become alerted to Michael?**

If not, keep going.

<h2 id="power-box-patrol">
  Read the flashlight near the power box before taking the stairs
</h2>

The next section is easier to read if you stop looking at distance and start
looking at the guard's flashlight.

Near the power box, an orderly sweeps his light toward the stairs.

Wait until he turns his back.

Then start down the stairs **walking**.

Running immediately gives up the easiest visual cue in this section.

Let him continue until his patrol turns left toward the dead end or locked
fence. Move farther down the street, then pause again.

There is another decision point before the road fully opens.

Wait until the orderly shifts onto the **left side of the road**.

Now walk forward.

Once enough distance has opened behind you, change pace and sprint.

That pattern is the core of Challenge 5:

**walk while patrol direction matters, run after the patrol is behind you.**

Trying to sprint every safe-looking gap makes the route harder to read because
Michael reaches the next sightline before the NPC rotation has finished.

<h2 id="final-approach">
  Do not commit to the last approach until both sides look clear
</h2>

The downhill run eventually brings you to a section where the temptation is to
keep the sprint going.

Check the lights first.

If an orderly is still occupying the route, hold position. There are also
orderlies off to the right, so clearing the NPC directly ahead does not always
mean the whole crossing is ready.

This was the place where patience mattered most.

A guard moved into the route, so the correct move was simply to stay put.
Checking again a few seconds later gave a completely different opening.

When the near side clears and the other patrol is no longer covering the
crossing, go.

At the final motor interaction, do not panic if the prompt is not visible the
instant Michael reaches it.

Get into position and give the interaction a moment to appear.

A late prompt is still recoverable.

Running away from the motor because the icon did not appear immediately only
adds another exposed movement.

<h2 id="confirm-completion">
  Finish the chapter before judging the attempt
</h2>

Getting through the hardest patrol section does not give you an immediate
reason to relax.

Finish the remaining Smith's Grove escape.

The useful confirmation comes after the chapter resolves and the game actually
marks **Challenge 5 Complete**.

One detail is worth remembering: the successful attempt used for this route
finished with **zero stars**, yet Challenge 5 still completed.

So stars are not the test.

If the challenge card completes, the no-alert run worked.

If you still need Challenges 1–4 or 6, switch back to a combat-oriented attempt
rather than trying to preserve this route. The

fit much better into a separate clear where fighting and chasing orderlies are
