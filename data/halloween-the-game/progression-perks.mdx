<h2 id="progression-tracks">
  Profile, Killer, Civilian, and Weapon Progression
</h2>

The progression menu separates XP into four tracks:

- **Profile Level**
- **Killer Level**
- **Civilian Level**
- **Weapon Level**

They can move during the same match, but they do not represent the same thing.

**Profile Level** is the account-wide track. XP earned through Story Mode,
Civilian matches, or Michael play contributes to it.

**Killer Level** comes from playing Michael.

**Civilian Level** belongs to the individual Civilian you select. Progress on
one character does not automatically level the rest of the roster.

**Weapon Level** belongs to the specific equipable Michael weapon being used
against victims.

That means playing Michael can move Profile progress, Killer progress, and the
currently equipped weapon at the same time.

Playing a Civilian can advance Profile progress while also building that
character's own Civilian level.

The mistake is assuming those gains are interchangeable.

If the unlock is attached to a Civilian, more Profile XP does not replace the
Civilian-specific progress.

If the goal is tied to one Michael weapon, swapping to another weapon may keep
broader Profile and Killer progression moving while leaving the original weapon
track behind.

### What progression can unlock

Levels and challenge milestones feed several kinds of rewards, including:

- additional playable Civilians;
- Michael abilities;
- Civilian starting items;
- executions;
- higher challenge tiers;
- cosmetics and customization rewards.


Some progression rewards change only your appearance, while others unlock items or abilities that affect what you can bring into a match.

Not every playable Civilian is earned through leveling, however. Eugene Kelly and Michelle Blanton are included in the separately purchased Civilian Pack 1. Increasing your Profile Level or another Civilian's level does not unlock that pack.


That difference matters most on a younger account. A useful starting item or
Michael ability can change the next round immediately, while a cosmetic does
not change the options available once the match begins.

Michael and Civilians also build differently.

Michael starts with **Killer Sense** and **Shape Jump** as core traits and has
three selectable ability slots. Ability options shown include **Reality Tear**,
**Blackout**, and **Detection Pulse**.

Weapon choice adds another progression layer because individual equipable
weapons level separately.

Civilians do not use that same ability structure. Their setup combines
character attributes and traits with starting items and perk cards.


<h2 id="michael-abilities-1-2-0">
  How Did Patch 1.2.0 Change Michael's Abilities and Combat?
</h2>

Michael's selectable abilities change how he controls a match, and Patch 1.2.0 makes the difference between escape denial and aggressive repositioning more important.

<h3 id="blackout">
  Blackout — Closing Escapes and Locking Doors
</h3>

**Blackout now closes open escapes and closes and locks doors within its affected radius.**

That gives Michael another way to interfere with escape progress. When Civilians are gathering around a usable exit, Blackout can interrupt their plan without requiring Michael to begin a long chase away from the objective.

The useful decision is when to activate it. If an escape has become the center of the match, use Blackout while the Civilians are still trying to complete or reach their exit rather than waiting until everyone has already left the area.

Do not assume the effect behaves identically on every type of escape. The new Tower Farm Sewer Hatch has shown an unsuccessful Blackout interaction, so guarding that particular entrance remains important. See the <Link href="/halloween-the-game/tower-farm-map-guide">Tower Farm map guide</Link> for the hatch's conditions and route.

<h3 id="reality-tear">
  Reality Tear — Account for the Stagger After Shape Jump
</h3>

Using **Reality Tear to exit Shape Jump now staggers Michael**.

That makes an aggressive arrival less straightforward. Civilians can gain an opportunity to react while Michael recovers, so using Reality Tear directly beside an alert opponent carries a different risk than before.

Think about where Michael will emerge and what the target can do during that recovery. A position that blocks the Civilian's next route may be more useful than appearing as close as possible without considering the stagger.

The change does not establish an exact recovery duration, and it does not mean Reality Tear has become useless. Its value depends on positioning rather than an assumed immediate follow-up grab.

<h3 id="shadow-strike">
  Shadow Strike — Now Deals Damage
</h3>

**Shadow Strike now causes damage**, giving it a more direct combat effect.

This makes its offensive contribution worth reconsidering when comparing Michael's available abilities. However, the update does not establish an exact damage amount here, so do not plan a guaranteed kill or fixed number of hits around an unconfirmed value.

<h3 id="group-rage">
  Why Are Group Fights More Dangerous for Michael's Opponents?
</h3>

Michael also gains scaling combat bonuses when surrounded by **three or more nearby targets**.

The bonuses affect his damage, Bloodthirst gain, damage resistance, and status-effect resistance. A group that repeatedly surrounds Michael can therefore make him harder to interrupt while increasing the danger of staying close.

For Michael, that makes crowded encounters more favorable than before, although pursuing one distant Civilian can still leave escape objectives unguarded.

For Civilians, coordinated stuns and knockdowns still have a purpose, but an extended group melee is more dangerous. Create an opening, complete the immediate objective, and move away rather than assuming more attackers always improve the exchange.

Patch 1.2.0 also increases Bloodthirst gained from Stalking. Maintaining pressure on visible targets can therefore build Bloodthirst more quickly. For the updated Resident discovery rules and how target markers appear throughout a match, see the <Link href="/halloween-the-game/multiplayer-guide">multiplayer guide</Link>.

<h2 id="perk-points">
  Perk Points, Card Rolls, Upgrades, and Scrapping
</h2>

XP raises progression levels.

**Perk Points are a separate Civilian currency.**

Completing matches supplies Perk Points, and the perk menu lets you spend them
to roll random cards.

The roll screen shown here starts at **100 Perk Points**.

Adding more points increases the chance of receiving a higher-rarity result.

That creates an early-account tradeoff.

Paying more into each card roll improves the chance of a higher-rarity result, but it also means making fewer rolls with the same Perk Point balance.

When my collection is still thin, I value **usable options** more than chasing one expensive high-rarity result. Once a Civilian has a dependable loadout, spending more for stronger rarity odds becomes easier to justify.

<h3 id="perk-points-vs-match-xp">
  Why Do In-Match Perk Rolls Require More XP?
</h3>

Perk Points and in-match XP affect different parts of the perk system.

**Perk Points** are spent outside matches to acquire cards for your collection and temporarily upgrade owned cards. Increasing the amount spent on a card roll improves rarity odds but does not guarantee a particular card.

**In-match XP** controls when perk choices become available during gameplay. Patch 1.2.0 increased the XP required for these rolls, so do not expect them to appear at the same point in a match as before the update.

This XP change is separate from the Perk Point price displayed in the card acquisition menu.

### Judge perk cards by the job they change

Rarity tells you where a card sits inside the perk system.

Its effect tells you whether it actually belongs in the loadout.

For example, **Delicate** appears as an **Uncommon** card on the current roll
screen. At the displayed level, it reduces the transparency of the noise pings
you generate by **30%**.

That is a real stealth benefit.

It still does not make Delicate automatically better for every Civilian.

A character being used for repairs, resident interaction, movement, or
combat support can value completely different effects.

The useful comparison is therefore not just rarity versus rarity.

It is:

**Does this card improve the problem this Civilian is repeatedly being asked to
solve?**

### Equip, upgrade, or scrap owned cards

The perk inventory is not only a collection screen.

Owned cards can be **equipped**, **temporarily upgraded**, or **scrapped for Perk Points**.

Before playing, check that the intended Perk Deck is assigned to your active Civilian loadout. The update also addressed a problem where the active loadout could use the wrong deck.

A Perk Deck can contain no more than **two copies of the same perk**. If a loadout was assembled before that restriction took effect, check its contents rather than assuming every previously equipped duplicate is still active.

When building a deck, use the remaining slots to cover another useful situation instead of planning around a third copy of the same card.

For example, **Barricade** improves the durability of doors you lock, but its values changed in Patch 1.2.0. Check the current effect on your card rather than relying on the percentage shown in an older inventory screen.

The same applies to upgrade and scrap costs. Inspect the price displayed for the specific card before committing Perk Points, especially when a temporary upgrade would leave too little currency for future rolls.

That makes a weak or duplicate roll less final than it first appears.

A card that no longer belongs in any loadout can return some currency to the
account.

I would still avoid scrapping everything below the current favorite rarity.

A young collection needs variety. Two cards with different jobs can be more
useful than several versions of the same idea, even when one has a lower
rarity.

Keep cards while they solve distinct problems.

Recycle them once another option has clearly replaced what they do.

### Perk upgrades are temporary

Upgrading an owned perk does not permanently raise it forever.

The improvement lasts for a set number of matches.

That changes when the upgrade is worth buying.

A temporary upgrade is strongest when several upcoming rounds are already
planned around the same Civilian and the same card.

Spending heavily just before switching characters wastes much of the value.

Perk Points also compete with card rolls, better rarity odds, future upgrades,
and Prestige attempts.

I therefore buy temporary upgrades when I already know the next matches will
use that card, rather than simply because the current balance can afford the
button.

<h3 id="perk-changes-that-matter">
  Which Updated Perks Are Still Worth Using?
</h3>

**For movement and stamina:** Adrenaline now reaches 30% stamina recharge, while Unwinded reaches 25% reduced stamina use. Adrenaline helps when you have time to recover between sprints. Unwinded is more directly relevant when the repeated cost of movement itself is the problem. The better choice depends on whether you lose time recovering or run out of stamina before reaching cover.

**For staying ahead of Michael:** Aura of Awareness now reaches 65% increased personal Shape Jump prevention radius. That makes its protective role more attractive, particularly when a chase often begins with Michael approaching through Shape Jump. It does not replace the need to watch your surroundings.

**For Resident-focused play:** Panic Contagion now reaches 3% movement speed after helping a Resident escape, down from its previous 4% maximum. Voice of Reason has stronger published follower-fear multipliers. Prioritize the effect that solves your actual problem: protecting frightened Followers or improving movement after successful rescues.

**For fighting Michael:** Brutal Cleave, Relentless Steel, Deadshot, and Thick Skin all have adjusted values. A combat deck should not be judged by damage alone. Choose between heavier hits, weapon durability, ranged critical damage, and resistance according to how your group engages and disengages.

**For escaping a grab:** Slippery's published values now top out at 25%, while Dying Breath reaches 40% increased grab-escape chance at low health. They affect different parts of surviving a grab and should not be treated as interchangeable.

<h3 id="perk-upgrade-decisions">
  Should You Upgrade an Old Perk Deck After the Update?
</h3>

Do not scrap a card only because its maximum effect was reduced. A weaker percentage can still be useful when the card supports the objective you regularly perform.

Check three things before spending:

1. Whether the card's current effect still solves your most common problem.
2. Whether an owned alternative now offers a more useful effect for the same role.
3. Whether you will use the temporary upgrade during enough upcoming matches to justify its Perk Point cost.

If the deck was assembled before the update, confirm that it is still assigned to your active Civilian loadout before entering the next match.


<h2 id="challenge-rewards">
  Do Challenges Help You Level Up or Earn Perk Points?
</h2>

Challenges can award Perk Points, cosmetics, and other unlocks. These rewards can support your progression plan, but completing a challenge is not the same as increasing every XP track at once.

If you need more Perk Points for card rolls or temporary upgrades, check the rewards for the challenges you can realistically finish.

For the exact Story and Progressive requirements, Scythe unlocks, and current Patch 1.2.0 challenge targets, use the <Link href="/halloween-the-game/challenges">Challenges guide</Link>.

When you receive a card or currency reward, return to your active loadout and decide whether the new option helps the role you intend to play next.


<h2 id="civilian-stats">
  How Do Civilian Stats Affect Your Perk Deck?
</h2>

A perk works alongside the Civilian's existing attributes rather than replacing them.

The four attributes influence different parts of a match:

- **Athleticism:** Movement on foot and by bicycle.
- **Personality:** Resident interactions, authorities, Followers, Fear, and Stalk resistance.
- **Resourcefulness:** Repairs, environmental awareness, vehicles, and firearms.
- **Capability:** Melee weapons and throwable items.

Before adding a card, decide which part of that character's job needs help. An already mobile Civilian may benefit from further stamina support, but another character may get more value from survivability or objective-related effects.

The same card can therefore be useful on one character and a poor choice on another.

For the complete roster, individual traits, current attributes, and role comparisons, see the <Link href="/halloween-the-game/best-civilians">Best Civilians guide</Link>.

<h3 id="starting-items">
  How Do Starting-Item Unlocks Change Your Loadout?
</h3>

Civilian progression can unlock additional Starting Items. These provide a way to begin a match with equipment already selected rather than searching for every useful item after spawning.

The correct choice depends on your character and intended objective. Bringing a starting item does not guarantee that the same item will remain useful throughout the match.

For the uses and tradeoffs of individual items, see the <Link href="/halloween-the-game/items-guide">Items guide</Link>.

<h2 id="spending-plan">
  What to Spend and Level First
</h2>

The same Perk Point balance has a different job depending on how developed the
account is.

**Early progression:** build enough cards to create real loadout choices.

At this stage, I prefer multiple usable rolls over pouring too much currency
into rarity while the collection still has obvious holes.

**Established Civilian:** improve the cards that repeatedly help that
character.

Once a Civilian already has a clear role and a dependable set of cards,
targeted rolls and temporary upgrades make more sense.

**Near a progression cap:** stop treating every spare Perk Point as money that
has to be spent immediately.

If Prestige is the next goal, I keep a reserve instead of draining the balance on another roll or temporary upgrade. The <Link href="/halloween-the-game/prestige-guide">Prestige guide</Link> covers the current Prestige costs and when I would actually start an attempt.

The same principle applies to XP.

Profile progression happens across much of the game, so I do not spend much
time deliberately trying to force that track in isolation.

The more meaningful decision is where to concentrate the progression that is
specific to a character or weapon.

If Civilian multiplayer is the priority, staying with one character for
several matches builds that Civilian's progression while also teaching how
their stats, traits, starting items, and perk cards work together.

Constantly swapping Civilians spreads that character-specific progression
across the roster.

If Michael is the priority, Killer progression and the weapon being used most
often are the tracks I pay attention to.

Changing weapons can still move the broader Profile and Killer systems, but it
does not build the same weapon-specific track.

So I would not automatically level whichever number currently looks lowest.

I would put the next block of play into the progression lane attached to the
**Civilian, Michael loadout, weapon, or Prestige goal I actually expect to use
next**.
