<h2 id="arrest-sequence">
  How to Arrest Michael Myers in Halloween: The Game
</h2>

Arresting Michael is not the same as simply damaging or knocking him down.

He can be stunned, shot, and dropped to the ground before the match has reached
the state where an actual Arrest interaction is possible.

For a clean detainment attempt, I use this seven-step sequence:

1. **Get police into the match.**
2. **Get one human player into spectator mode.**
3. **Use the CB radio to call Loomis, or use Reinforcements to return as police.**
4. **Keep Michael close to police while the arrest state builds.**
5. **Wait until the arrest state is ready before committing to the knockdown.**
6. **Knock Michael down and trigger Arrest.**
7. **Finish the short input interaction before Michael gets back up.**

The important part is the order.

If Michael keeps standing back up after your team shoots, stuns, or knocks him
down, more damage is not necessarily the answer.

I first check which part of the arrest sequence has not been established yet.

<h2 id="police-setup">
  Step 1 — Get Police Into the Match
</h2>

The arrest route begins with the same tools Civilians already use during a
normal multiplayer round.

Use a safe phone to call the police.

Residents can also help build police presence when their interaction gives you
the **Call For Help** option.

Those calls matter because the later arrest phase needs Michael close to
authority pressure. Fighting him alone in an empty part of the map can consume
weapons without moving the team any closer to detaining him.

If phones, residents, escape tools, and normal Civilian objectives are still
unfamiliar, the
<Link href="/halloween-the-game/multiplayer-guide">
  {" "}multiplayer guide
</Link>
is a better place to learn those systems before building an entire match around
an arrest attempt.

I still collect useful weapons during the setup.

What I do not do is spend every stun or shot on Michael as soon as I see him.

Those control tools become much more valuable once police are actually in
position to benefit from the interruption.

<h2 id="spectator-mode">
  Step 2 — Get One Player Into Spectator Mode
</h2>

The unusual part of the arrest route is that one player needs access to the
spectator side of the match.

A human player can reach spectator mode by **escaping or dying**.

From there, that player can interact with the **CB radio**.

This gives an early Civilian escape a second purpose.

Normally, an escaped player is finished with the active escape.

During a detainment attempt, reaching spectator mode can open the next stage
for everybody still inside the match.

If a safe exit is already available, I would rather let one player take it than
deliberately sacrifice a healthy Civilian just to create a spectator.

The requirement is spectator access.

It does not need to come from an unnecessary death.

<h2 id="call-loomis">
  Step 3 — Use the CB Radio to Call Loomis
</h2>

Once the spectator can reach the radio, complete the Loomis call.

Loomis can then enter the active side of the match and become part of the
detainment attempt.

  </figcaption>

Once the call is complete, I stop hanging around the radio.

The useful part of the map is now wherever Michael can be kept close to the
police.

A teammate chasing him through empty yards may be winning the chase itself,
but they can also be pulling the target away from the people needed for the
arrest.

That is why I treat Loomis as a transition point:

**before Loomis, assemble the arrest pieces; after Loomis, start bringing those
pieces together around Michael.**

<h2 id="reinforcements">
  Reinforcements Can Return a Spectator as Police

While completing spectator-side minigames, **Reinforcements Available** can
appear.

That gives the team another source of authority pressure and another player who
can help hold Michael in the correct area.

I would still use the CB radio when the Loomis interaction is already
available.

Reinforcements are useful when they appear.

Waiting around for them while a known Loomis call is ready only delays the
arrest route you already have.

<h2 id="police-pressure">
  Step 4 — Keep Michael Near Police Until the Arrest State Is Ready
</h2>

Once Loomis or a returned police player is active, positioning matters more
than finding another random weapon.

Michael needs to stay where police can keep pressure on him.

The detainment state becomes visible during this phase, including the
handcuff-style indicator around Michael.

  <Image

  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">
    The important resource here is police pressure. Stunning Michael beside the

    against it.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

This is where I start valuing stuns differently.

A firearm can interrupt Michael.

A throwable can interrupt him.

A flashlight can help hold pressure on him.

But the important part is what happens **after** the interruption.

If Michael is stunned beside several officers, those seconds support the
detainment setup.

If he gets stunned alone at the far end of the street, the team may have dealt
damage without improving the position that actually matters.

That also changes how I spend ammunition.

Shots used early just to annoy Michael are shots I may not have when the team
finally gets him beside the police.

I try to preserve enough control tools for this stage.

### What POLICE WAGON CALLED means

During a live detainment attempt, the HUD can show:

**POLICE WAGON CALLED**

I treat that as confirmation that the match has moved deeper into the arrest
sequence.

If that message is active and Michael is already showing the handcuff-style
indicator, I do not break away to search houses again.

The team has spent the round assembling this state.

Keep Michael close to the authorities and protect it.

<h2 id="knockdown">
  Step 5 — Fill the Arrest State Before Knocking Michael Down
</h2>

Michael hitting the ground is not enough by itself.

That is one of the easiest mistakes to make because an unsuccessful attempt can
look almost identical to the final successful knockdown.

You can drop Michael too early and simply watch him get back up.

The useful knockdown comes **after the arrest state has been built**.

Once Michael goes down in the correct state, somebody should already be close
enough to interact.

Do not spend most of the down time running over from another yard.

Do not stop beside him to heal unless you will otherwise be unable to survive
the interaction.

And do not assume an NPC officer will automatically finish the job.

Look for the **Arrest** prompt.

<h2 id="final-arrest">
  Step 6 — Trigger Arrest Before Michael Gets Back Up
</h2>

As soon as the **Arrest** prompt appears, commit to it.

The final step is a short input minigame.

Michael's knockdown window is limited, so the interaction needs to finish
before he recovers.

There is another reason not to crowd him carelessly:

**Michael can still be dangerous while he is down.**

A low-health player can lose the final interaction even after the team has
already completed the difficult setup.

The cleanest finish I have found is to let one player commit to the Arrest
prompt while the others protect the space around them.

Several players standing directly on Michael do not make the minigame finish
faster.

What matters is giving the player on the prompt enough time to complete it.

<h2 id="detained">
  Step 7 — MICHAEL DETAINED! Confirms the Arrest
</h2>

A completed Arrest interaction immediately resolves the detainment.

The result screen confirms the win with:

This is why the arrest route changes the value of several resources across the
whole round.

An escaped player can create spectator access.

The CB radio can bring Loomis in.

Reinforcements can return a spectator as police.

Weapons and flashlights can control Michael while the arrest state builds.

Police calls create the authority presence needed for the later fight.

None of those pieces is especially useful by itself.

The arrest works when they converge in the same place at roughly the same
time.

<h2 id="escape-instead">
  When I Stop Trying to Arrest Michael and Take the Escape Instead
</h2>

Detaining Michael is a strong win condition, but it is also a route with
several prerequisites.

I am much more willing to commit to it when the match already has most of these
pieces:

- police active in the area;
- a spectator who can use the CB radio;
- Loomis active or Reinforcements available;
- at least one useful stun option;
- enough surviving players to keep Michael near the police;
- a realistic chance to reach the Arrest prompt after the final knockdown.

If several of those pieces are still missing late in the match, I compare the
arrest route with whatever escape the team has already built.

If the Storm Cellar is open while nobody has reached the CB radio, Michael
controls the police area, and the team's weapons are disappearing, the escape
is not the lesser win condition.

It is the completed one.

Repeated knockdowns away from police are another warning sign.

They do not mean the team is almost finished if the arrest state is not
building.

When the arrest pieces are converging, I commit to detainment.

When they are not, I use the exit the team has already earned.
