<h2 id="arrest-sequence">
  The arrest only works after the match reaches the right state
</h2>

Michael can be stunned, shot, and knocked down long before he is actually ready
to be arrested.

That is why failed attempts can look almost identical to successful ones until
the last few seconds.

The full sequence is:

1. **Get police involved**
2. **Move a human player into spectator mode**
3. **Use the CB radio to call Loomis, or bring a spectator back as police**
4. **Keep Michael under police pressure until the arrest meter is ready**
5. **Knock him down**
6. **Use the Arrest interaction**
7. **Finish the short minigame before Michael recovers**

The order matters more than raw damage.

If Michael keeps standing back up after your team unloads weapons into him,
check which stage of that sequence has not happened yet.

<h2 id="police-setup">
  Build police presence before trying to hold Michael in one place
</h2>

The arrest attempt starts with the same tools Civilians already use during a
normal match.

Use a safe phone to call the police.

Talk to residents and use their **Call For Help** option when the interaction
gives you that choice.

Those calls start putting authority figures into play, which becomes important
later when Michael needs to be pressured rather than simply chased.

If phones, residents, escape items, and normal Civilian objectives are still
unfamiliar, start with the
<Link href="/halloween-the-game/multiplayer-guide">
  {" "}multiplayer guide
</Link>
before building an entire round around detainment.

Weapons are worth collecting during this setup, but there is no need to turn
every early Michael encounter into a fight.

Keep something available for the stage where a stun can actually hold him near
the police.

<h2 id="spectator-mode">
  One player has to leave the active Civilian side
</h2>

The unusual requirement is **spectator access**.

At least one human player needs to escape or die and enter spectator mode.

That player can then interact with the **CB radio**.

This makes an early Civilian escape useful in a way that is easy to miss.

Normally, leaving means that player is finished with the active escape.

During an arrest attempt, entering spectator mode can unlock the next stage of
the plan.

If a safe exit already exists, letting one player take it is much cleaner than
deliberately feeding a healthy Civilian to Michael just to create a spectator.

The goal is spectator access, not an unnecessary death.

<h2 id="call-loomis">
  Use the CB radio to bring Loomis into the active match
</h2>

Once the spectator reaches the radio, complete the Loomis call.

Afterward, Loomis can enter the active side of the match and take part in the
detainment attempt.

At this point, stop spending time around the radio.

The next useful position is wherever police pressure and Michael can be brought
together.

A teammate sprinting after Michael through empty yards may be winning a chase,
but they are moving the target away from the people needed for the arrest.

<h2 id="reinforcements">
  Reinforcements can return a spectator as a police officer

While completing the spectator-side minigames, **Reinforcements Available** can
appear.

Reinforcements are useful when they appear, but waiting around for them while a
known Loomis call is ready only delays the part of the match that matters.

<h2 id="police-pressure">
  Stop chasing Michael away from the officers
</h2>

Once Loomis or a returned police player is active, positioning becomes more
important than finding another weapon.

Michael needs to stay where the police can keep pressure on him.

The arrest state is visible during this phase, including the handcuff-style
indicator around Michael.

<figure className="my-8">
  <Image
    src="/images/halloween-the-game/halloween-the-game-arrest-michael-police-pressure.webp"
    alt="Michael Myers under police pressure with a detainment indicator visible"
    width={1600}
    height={900}
    className="rounded-xl"
  />
  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">
    Police pressure is the condition to protect. Pulling Michael away from the
    officers works against the arrest even if your team is still hitting him.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

This is where stuns become valuable.

A firearm can interrupt him.

A throwable can interrupt him.

A flashlight can help keep pressure on him.

What matters is what happens **after** the interruption.

If Michael is stunned beside several officers, those seconds help the arrest
state keep building.

If he is stunned alone at the far end of the street, the team may have dealt
damage without advancing the detainment.

The same rule changes how ammunition should be spent.

Shots used earlier just to annoy Michael may be missing when the team finally
has him in the correct place.

Save enough control tools to keep him contained during this phase.

<h2 id="police-wagon">
  POLICE WAGON CALLED means the arrest attempt has moved forward
</h2>

During a live detainment attempt, the HUD can show:

**POLICE WAGON CALLED**

That is another useful sign that the match has progressed beyond ordinary
police harassment and into the arrest sequence.

If that message is up and Michael has the handcuff indicator around him, stay
with the pressure already established.

This is not the moment to break away and resume searching houses.

The team has spent the whole match assembling this state.

Keep Michael near the authorities and protect it.

<h2 id="knockdown">
  Fill the arrest state before using the knockdown window
</h2>

Michael hitting the ground is not enough by itself.

Teams can knock him down before the arrest sequence is ready and watch him get
back up again.

The useful knockdown comes **after** the arrest meter has been built.

Once Michael goes down in the correct state, somebody should already be close
enough to act.

Do not burn most of the down time running in from another yard.

Do not stop beside him to heal unless you cannot survive the interaction
otherwise.

Do not assume an NPC officer will automatically finish the arrest.

Look for the **Arrest** prompt.

<h2 id="final-arrest">
  One player needs to finish the Arrest interaction before Michael gets up
</h2>

Trigger **Arrest** as soon as the prompt becomes available.

The last step is a short input minigame.

Michael's knockdown does not last forever, so the interaction has to be finished
inside that window.

There is another reason not to crowd him carelessly:

**Michael can still be dangerous while he is down.**

A player rushing in at low health can lose the final interaction even after the
team completed every difficult part of the setup.

The cleanest finish has one player committed to the Arrest prompt while the
others protect the space around them.

More people standing directly on Michael does not make the minigame complete
faster.

<h2 id="detained">
  MICHAEL DETAINED! ends the match
</h2>

A successful Arrest interaction does not lead into another escape phase.

The result screen confirms the outcome as:

**MICHAEL DETAINED!**

This is why arresting Michael changes the value of resources across the whole
round.

An escaped player can create spectator access.

The CB radio can bring in Loomis.

Reinforcements can put a spectator back into the match as police.

Weapons and flashlights can keep Michael controlled long enough for the arrest
state to build.

Police calls create the authority presence that the final fight needs.

Those pieces only become powerful when they arrive in the same place at roughly
the same time.

<h2 id="escape-instead">
  Take the escape when too many arrest pieces are still missing
</h2>

The arrest route becomes fragile when the team is still waiting for several
prerequisites late in the match.

A strong attempt usually already has most of these pieces:

- police active in the area;
- a spectator who can use the CB radio;
- Loomis active or Reinforcements available;
- at least one useful stun option;
- enough surviving players to keep Michael near police;
- a realistic chance to finish the Arrest interaction after the knockdown.

Compare that with the alternative.

If the Storm Cellar is already open while nobody has reached the CB radio,
Michael controls the police area, and the team's weapons are disappearing, the
escape is no longer the lesser win condition.

It is the completed one.

Likewise, repeated knockdowns away from officers are not evidence that the team
is “almost there.” If the arrest state is not building, the important part of
the sequence is still missing.

Commit to detainment when the pieces are converging.

When they are not, use the exit you already earned.
