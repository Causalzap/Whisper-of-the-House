<h2 id="main-street-route">
  Protect the challenge states before you start clearing the block
</h2>

Main Street gives Michael enough freedom that a kill can be successful for the
story and still ruin the challenge attached to that target.

The six objectives are:

1. **Execute Tanya using the cash register**
2. **Execute Rachel using an environmental kill**
3. **Grab and execute Slab while he's peeing**
4. **Execute Eric using the shelf kill in the hardware store stockroom**
5. **Kill all three of Rachel's friends**
6. **Complete the chapter without alerting anyone**

The safest order is:

**hardware store → Tanya → Rachel's gathering → Slab's split → Rachel and the
remaining friends → required body cleanup → Judith's grave → car**

Eric and Slab are the least recoverable mistakes because their required states
occur at specific points in the chapter.

Rachel's friends create the opposite problem: their challenge can remain alive,
but losing track of them can turn a short sequence into a long search.

For a normal story clear without challenge cleanup, use the

  {" "}complete story walkthrough
</Link>

<h2 id="hardware-store">
  Get the mask and preserve Eric for the stockroom shelf
</h2>

The hardware store is both the opening progression point and Eric's challenge
location.

Michael gets his mask here, and the chapter starts making heavier use of
**Shape Jump** and **Killer Sense**.

One early blocker is easy to misread:

**a lit doorway can block Shape Jump.**

If the ability will not trigger at a door that otherwise looks usable, inspect
the light covering the route before circling the building for another entrance.

Eric's requirement is much narrower:

**execute Eric using the shelf kill in the hardware store stockroom.**

Killer Sense can expose environmental execution points with skull icons, but
that does not make every skull interaction interchangeable.

The challenge specifically names the **stockroom shelf**.

Keep Eric alive until that interaction is available rather than using the first
environmental execution you find.

If Eric dies before the shelf setup and you are only a few minutes into the
chapter, restarting is reasonable.

If Tanya, Rachel, and the later objectives are already complete, finish the run.
Eric's required state is gone, and searching the store again will not bring it
back.

<h2 id="tanya-cash-register">
  Control Tanya's shop before moving her onto the register
</h2>

Tanya's challenge is less about speed than keeping the room predictable.

Before entering, check the phone line.

Cutting it removes an obvious way for the situation to spread beyond the shop
while you are arranging the execution.

Then locate Tanya and keep her close enough to the **cash register** for the
contextual kill to become available.

Power is optional here.

Turning the lights off may help Michael move, but it can also change Tanya's
path and make the register setup worse.

If the room is already controllable, there is no need to disturb a useful
routine.

Once the register interaction is lined up, grab Tanya and complete it.

Remember where her body ends up, but do not assume hiding Tanya later is always
a required progression step. The late HUD distinguishes her cleanup from
Rachel's.

<h2 id="no-alert">
  Dead-End Job survives on information control
</h2>

**Dead-End Job** asks for a chapter completion without alerting anyone.

That makes phones and resident movement more important than creating darkness
everywhere.

Before entering an occupied building, check three things:

- whether the phone is still usable;
- where the resident is moving;
- whether the lighting blocks or helps the approach you want.

A live phone can turn one bad sighting into a larger problem.

A light can sometimes block Shape Jump.

A power cut can also move an NPC away from the position you were preparing.

Use each interaction for a reason rather than shutting down every system you
see.

Once somebody is clearly alerted, treat Dead-End Job as failed for that run.

The other five challenges can still be worth finishing, so there is little
benefit in restarting late just to preserve a stealth condition that has
already been lost.

<h2 id="slab">
  Slab has to reach the animation before the grab
</h2>

Slab separates from Rachel's group because he needs to relieve himself.

That departure tells you the sequence has started.

It does **not** tell you the challenge window is active.

The requirement is:

**Grab and execute Slab while he's peeing.**

Follow him without closing the distance too early.

Wait until he reaches the spot and visibly begins peeing.

Then make the grab.

This is why killing him beside the correct spot can still fail.

The location can be right.

The target can be right.

The timing can still be wrong.

If he has already stopped or reacted to Michael, continue with Rachel and the
other friends instead of turning Slab into an ordinary kill and assuming the
challenge will recover.

<h2 id="rachel-group">
  Read the group before taking Rachel's environmental kill
</h2>

Rachel's gathering contains several objectives at once.

Slab needs to leave for his timed challenge.

Rachel needs an environmental execution.

All three of her friends have to be accounted for.

The useful move is to let the group reveal its routes before collapsing the
scene.

Handle Slab first.

Then watch the other friends long enough to see where they head.

Only after that do I spend Rachel's challenge state.

Her requirement is:

**execute Rachel using an environmental kill.**

Unlike Tanya or Eric, Rachel is not tied to one uniquely named prop.

The fence interaction shown here works, with **Head Impale** available as the
contextual execution.

Killing Rachel too quickly creates a navigation problem rather than a combat
problem.

While Michael is occupied with her, the remaining friends can already be moving
away.

Know their direction before committing to the execution.

<h2 id="rachels-friends">
  Broken Record is easier if you follow the split immediately
</h2>

**Broken Record** requires all three of Rachel's friends.

Slab is easy to remember because his separate animation gives him a distinct
route.

The other two are easier to lose.

In the route shown here, they move toward a nearby house after the gathering
breaks apart.

    The useful clue is their direction of travel. Follow it while it is fresh
    instead of turning the block into a door-to-door search.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

One of the remaining targets can be reached from outside before Michael fully
commits to the house.

Then work inward toward the last friend.

Do not expand the hunt simply because Killer Sense shows more residents nearby.

Unrelated people do not advance Broken Record.

They only add movement, possible alerts, and bodies that compete for your
attention.

Once all three friends are accounted for, the group objective is finished.

<h2 id="body-cleanup">
  Rachel's late hiding objective is required; Tanya's can be optional
</h2>

After the target sequence, the chapter can appear to stall.

Read the current objective before searching for another person.

The captured HUD state shows:

**HIDE RACHEL'S BODY**

and separately:

**OPTIONAL: HIDE TANYA'S BODY**

If the cemetery route has not advanced, solve the required Rachel objective
first.

Do not waste time trying to make every corpse satisfy the same condition.

Use a hiding interaction the game recognizes rather than assuming any grave or
dark corner will count.

When the required cleanup clears, the chapter moves toward Judith Myers.

Interact with the grave and take the tombstone.

Then return it to the car.

That completes the Chapter 2 route.

This is also the right point to stop trying to repair failed challenge states.

If one of Rachel's friends is still alive and you remember where they went, a
short return trip may still work.

Eric's missed shelf cannot.

Slab's missed animation cannot.

A lost Dead-End Job run cannot.

Those need another chapter attempt.

<h2 id="replay-priorities">
  Reset early failures; finish late ones
</h2>

Main Street has a useful dividing line between mistakes that justify an
immediate reset and mistakes that are better cleaned up later.

**Reset early when:**

- Eric has already died and the stockroom shelf challenge was the main purpose
  of the run;
- the no-alert challenge fails almost immediately and Dead-End Job is the only
  reward you came back for.

**Finish the chapter when:**

- Slab's timing was missed but Rachel and her friends are still available;
- one challenge failed after Tanya and Eric were already completed;
- only a recoverable friend or body objective remains;
- you have already reached the cemetery.

A six-challenge run does not need to become a wasted run because one condition
failed.

Bank the challenges whose states are still intact.

Then build the next replay around the one failure that actually requires an
earlier setup.
