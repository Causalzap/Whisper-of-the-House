<h2 id="main-street-route">
  Six Chapter 2 challenges, but three states decide whether the run is still alive
</h2>

Main Street gives Michael enough freedom to kill the right person in the wrong
way.

The six challenges are:

1. **Execute Tanya using the cash register**
2. **Execute Rachel using an environmental kill**
3. **Grab and execute Slab while he's peeing**
4. **Execute Eric using the shelf kill in the hardware store stockroom**
5. **Kill all three of Rachel's friends**
6. **Complete the chapter without alerting anyone**

I would move through the chapter in this order:

**hardware store → Eric → Tanya → Rachel's group → Slab → Rachel and her
remaining friends → required body cleanup → Judith's grave → car**

Three conditions deserve extra protection.

**Eric** becomes unrecoverable once he dies away from the stockroom shelf.

**Slab** only gives you a short window after he reaches the peeing animation.

**Dead-End Job**, the no-alert challenge, is gone as soon as somebody is
properly alerted.

Rachel and her friends are different. They can become annoying to locate, but
losing sight of them for a moment does not destroy their challenge state in the
same way.

If you only want to clear Chapter 2 and move on with the campaign, the
<Link href="/halloween-the-game/walkthrough">
  {" "}story walkthrough
</Link>
takes the shorter route. The sequence below assumes the six Main Street
challenges are the reason you replayed the chapter.

<h2 id="hardware-store">
  The hardware store gives you the mask and the first irreversible challenge
</h2>

Michael gets his mask here.

This is also where Eric can ruin a challenge run before Main Street has really
opened up.

Two mechanics become much more useful from this point onward:

- **Killer Sense** helps locate people and exposes environmental execution
  points.
- **Shape Jump** gives Michael faster routes through buildings and around the
  block.

One early frustration comes from lighting.

A doorway can look perfectly usable while Shape Jump refuses to trigger. If
the route is lit, check the light before assuming you need to circle the whole
building for another entrance.

Eric is the more important decision.

His challenge specifically requires the **shelf kill in the hardware store
stockroom**.

A skull icon somewhere else does not substitute for that shelf.

Keep him alive until you have him in the stockroom and the correct contextual
execution is available.

If Eric dies somewhere else during the opening minutes and his challenge is
the reason for this run, restarting now is cheap.

Ten or fifteen minutes later, after several other challenge states have
already been secured, the calculation changes. At that point I would finish
what is still recoverable and come back for Eric on the next replay.

<h2 id="tanya-cash-register">
  Tanya is easier when the shop stays predictable
</h2>

Tanya needs the **cash register execution**.

The target is obvious. Getting her into a useful position is where the run can
become messy.

Before committing to the shop, check the phone line.

Cutting the phone removes one way for a bad sighting to spread beyond the room,
which matters even more if Dead-End Job is still alive.

Then watch Tanya's movement around the register.

Power is a judgment call here.

Darkness can make Michael easier to move, but changing the power can also
change Tanya's routine.

If she is already walking into a clean register setup, I leave the room alone.

If lighting is blocking the approach I actually need, then interfering with
the power has a purpose.

That distinction matters throughout Main Street. Switches, phones, and lights
are tools, not a checklist.

Once Tanya reaches the register window, grab her and take the contextual kill.

Remember where the body ends up.

You may see a cleanup objective later, but Tanya and Rachel are not treated
the same way by the late-chapter HUD.

<h2 id="dead-end-job">
  Dead-End Job is mostly lost before a fight even starts
</h2>

The sixth challenge requires you to finish Main Street **without alerting
anyone**.

The useful information is not how many lights Michael can turn off.

It is whether the next person has a clean path to notice him or call for help.

Before moving into an occupied space, I check:

- the phone;
- the resident's current direction;
- nearby sightlines;
- whether light is blocking the Shape Jump route I want.

A power cut is worth using when it solves one of those problems.

Turning everything off automatically can make an NPC move away from a setup
that was already working.

The same applies to speed. Entering the room faster does not help if Tanya,
Eric, or another resident is still facing the space Michael needs.

Once somebody is clearly alerted, Dead-End Job is finished for that attempt.

How much has already been completed decides whether I restart.

If the alert happens near the beginning and the no-alert challenge is the main
reason I loaded Chapter 2, I start again.

If Eric and Tanya are already finished and the run has moved deep into the
chapter, throwing away every successful setup to protect one failed condition
costs more than it saves.

Keep the remaining challenges alive and bank those first.

<h2 id="rachel-group">
  Rachel's group is a tracking problem before it becomes a killing problem
</h2>

Rachel's gathering puts three challenge conditions in the same scene:

- Slab needs his timed peeing execution;
- Rachel needs an environmental execution;
- all three of Rachel's friends need to die.

Moving too quickly through the gathering makes the later search harder.

Let the group start separating.

Watch where people go.

Slab gives you the easiest landmark because his route eventually leads into a
specific animation. The other friends are easier to lose once Michael commits
to another kill.

I use the first few seconds of the split to learn directions rather than
immediately grabbing Rachel.

That small delay saves a much longer door-to-door search later.

<h2 id="slab">
  Slab leaving the group is not the challenge window
</h2>

Slab eventually separates because he needs to relieve himself.

Following him is correct.

Grabbing him as soon as he reaches the area is not.

The requirement says:

**Grab and execute Slab while he's peeing.**

Those last three words are the important part.

Stay far enough back that he can finish moving into position.

When the animation visibly starts, close the gap and grab him.

This is one of the clearest examples of why Main Street challenges are about
**state**, not just target and location.

You can have:

- the correct NPC;
- the correct area;
- the correct execution;

and still miss the challenge because the interaction happened before the
required animation became active.

If Slab sees Michael, reacts, or has already left that state, the window is
gone.

There is nothing productive to gain from repeatedly grabbing him beside the
same spot afterward.

Continue with Rachel's group and decide at the end whether the rest of the run
was valuable enough to keep.

<h2 id="rachel-environmental-kill">
  Spend Rachel only after you know where her friends went
</h2>

Rachel's requirement is broader than Eric's or Tanya's:

**execute Rachel using an environmental kill.**

She is not tied to one specifically named prop.

The fence interaction shown here works and offers **Head Impale**.

The kill itself is not the difficult part.

The expensive mistake is spending several seconds on Rachel while her friends
are already leaving the gathering.

Before taking the environmental execution, look away from Rachel long enough
to establish where the others are heading.

Then use the environmental kill.

Now the scene has two fewer uncertainties:

Rachel's challenge is finished, and the remaining search has a direction.

<h2 id="rachels-friends">
  Follow the split while it is still visible
</h2>

**Broken Record** requires all three of Rachel's friends.

Slab is difficult to forget because his challenge gives him a distinct route.

The remaining two can disappear into the neighborhood much more quietly.

In this run, their movement leads toward a nearby house.

One target can be reachable before Michael fully commits to the house.

Take that opportunity if it is clean, then continue toward the last friend.

Killer Sense may show unrelated residents nearby.

Those people are noise for Broken Record.

Chasing every highlighted person increases travel, creates more chances for an
alert, and makes it harder to remember which member of Rachel's group is
actually still missing.

Once all three friends are accounted for, stop expanding the search.

<h2 id="body-cleanup">
  When Main Street seems stuck, read the body objective literally
</h2>

After the target sequence, the chapter can feel finished before the HUD agrees.

This is one of the places where wandering around looking for another victim
makes the problem worse.

The late objective can show:

**HIDE RACHEL'S BODY**

while Tanya appears separately as:

**OPTIONAL: HIDE TANYA'S BODY**

That wording determines the next move.

If the cemetery objective has not appeared yet, deal with Rachel first.

Use a hiding interaction the game recognizes.

A random dark corner or convenient-looking grave does not automatically count
as a valid body-hiding state.

Tanya can wait when her objective is explicitly marked optional.

Once Rachel's required cleanup clears, Main Street starts pushing toward
Judith Myers.

<h2 id="judith-gravestone">
  Judith's grave is the last outbound trip; the car is the finish
</h2>

Go to **Judith Myers' gravestone** when the chapter sends you to the cemetery.

Interact with the grave.

Take the tombstone.

Return to the car.

That closes Chapter 2.

The cemetery also gives me a practical cutoff for failed challenge states.

A surviving member of Rachel's group may still be recoverable if I know
exactly where they went and the return trip is short.

Eric's missing stockroom shelf is not recoverable.

Slab cannot be put back into the peeing animation on demand.

An alert cannot be undone for Dead-End Job.

Once the tombstone route is active, those failures belong to another replay.

A late restart throws away every challenge that already survived the chapter
just to repair one condition whose setup happened much earlier.

Finish the car objective, keep the successful states, and make the next Main
Street run about the one challenge that actually needs another opening setup.
