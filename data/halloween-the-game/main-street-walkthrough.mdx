<h2 id="main-street-route">
  Chapter 2: Main Street Walkthrough Route and Challenge Timing
</h2>

Main Street gives Michael much more freedom than the Prologue, but the chapter
still has a clear progression spine once you know which events actually move it
forward.

For a normal Chapter 2 clear, I follow this route:

**hardware store → Eric and Tanya area → Rachel's group → Slab → Rachel and her
friends → required body cleanup → Judith Myers' grave → car**

If I am also clearing Chapter 2 challenges, I use the same story route but protect the character states that disappear once a target dies, moves on, or becomes alerted. The <Link href="/halloween-the-game/challenges">Challenges guide</Link> has the current six Chapter 2 requirements; here, the important part is keeping those requirements possible while the story advances.

Three conditions are especially easy to lose.

**Eric** becomes unrecoverable once he dies somewhere other than the stockroom
shelf.

**Slab** only gives you his challenge window once the peeing animation has
actually started.

**Dead-End Job**, the no-alert challenge, is gone once somebody becomes properly
alerted.

Rachel and her friends are more forgiving. Losing sight of them makes the route
slower, but it does not destroy their challenge state in the same way.

If you only want the broader Michael's Story route across every chapter, use the <Link href="/halloween-the-game/walkthrough">complete Story Mode walkthrough</Link>. The route below stays longer in Main Street because it preserves the Chapter 2 challenge opportunities that can disappear as the story advances.

<h2 id="hardware-store">
  Start Chapter 2 at the Hardware Store and Get Michael's Mask
</h2>

The hardware store is the first important Chapter 2 progression point.

Michael gets his mask here, and the building also contains Eric's
challenge-specific execution.

    Finish the mask sequence, then keep Eric available for the stockroom shelf
    instead of using him on the first execution Michael can reach.
  </figcaption>

Two mechanics become much more useful once Michael has the mask:

- **Killer Sense** helps locate people and exposes environmental execution points.
- **Shape Jump** gives Michael faster routes through buildings and around the block.

Lighting can interfere with Shape Jump.

A doorway may look usable while the ability refuses to trigger because the route
is lit. When that happens, check the nearby light before assuming you need to
circle the building for another entrance.

Eric is the more important decision inside the store.

His challenge specifically requires the **shelf kill in the hardware store
stockroom**.

A skull icon somewhere else does not substitute for that shelf.

Keep Eric alive until he is inside the stockroom and the correct contextual
execution is available.

If he dies somewhere else during the opening minutes and his challenge is the
reason for this replay, restarting here is cheap.

Ten or fifteen minutes later, after several other conditions are already secured,
the decision changes. At that point I would finish the challenges that are still
recoverable and return for Eric on another Chapter 2 replay.

<h2 id="tanya-cash-register">
  Tanya — Use the Cash Register Execution Before Leaving the Shop
</h2>

Tanya needs the **cash register execution**.

The target is obvious.

Getting her into a useful position beside the register is where the setup can
become messy.

Before committing to the room, check the phone line.

Cutting the phone removes one way for a bad sighting to spread beyond the shop,
which matters even more if Dead-End Job is still active.

Then watch Tanya's movement around the register.

Power is a judgment call here.

Darkness can make Michael easier to move, but changing the power can also change
Tanya's routine.

If she is already walking into a clean register setup, I leave the room alone.

If lighting is blocking the approach I actually need, then interfering with the
power has a purpose.

That distinction matters throughout Main Street.

Switches, phones, and lights are tools for solving the route in front of you,
not a checklist that needs to be cleared every run.

Once Tanya reaches the register window, grab her and take the contextual kill.

Remember where her body lands.

A body-cleanup objective can appear later, but Tanya and Rachel are not treated
the same way by the late-chapter HUD.

<h2 id="dead-end-job">
  Dead-End Job — Complete Chapter 2 Without Alerting Anyone
</h2>

Dead-End Job requires you to finish Main Street **without alerting anyone**.

The challenge is usually lost before a real fight starts.

The useful question is not how many lights Michael can turn off.

It is whether the next person has a clean path to notice him or call for help.

Before moving into an occupied space, I check:

- the phone;
- the resident's current direction;
- nearby sightlines;
- whether light is blocking the Shape Jump route I want.

A power cut is worth using when it solves one of those problems.

Turning everything off automatically can make an NPC move away from a setup
that was already working.

The same applies to speed.

Entering a room faster does not help if Tanya, Eric, or another resident is
still facing the space Michael needs to cross.

Once somebody is clearly alerted, Dead-End Job is over for that attempt.

How far the rest of the run has progressed decides whether I restart.

If the alert happens near the beginning and the no-alert challenge is the main
reason I loaded Chapter 2, I restart.

If Eric and Tanya are already complete and the run has moved deep into Main
Street, I usually keep going.

Throwing away several successful challenge states to repair one failed
condition costs more than it saves.

Bank the other challenges first and make the next replay specifically about
Dead-End Job.

<h2 id="rachel-group">
  Rachel's Group — Watch the Split Before You Start Killing
</h2>

Rachel's gathering is where several Chapter 2 objectives overlap.

Three challenge conditions are tied to this group:

- Slab needs his timed peeing execution;
- Rachel needs an environmental execution;
- all three of Rachel's friends need to die.

Moving too quickly through the gathering makes the later part of Main Street
harder.

Let the group begin separating.

Watch where everybody goes.

Slab is the easiest landmark because his route eventually leads into a specific
animation.

The other friends are easier to lose once Michael commits to another kill.

I use the first few seconds of the split to learn directions rather than
immediately grabbing Rachel.

That small delay saves a much longer door-to-door search later.

<h2 id="slab">
  Slab — Wait Until He Is Actually Peeing Before You Grab Him
</h2>

Slab eventually separates from Rachel's group because he needs to relieve
himself.

Following him is correct.

Grabbing him as soon as he reaches the area is not.

The requirement is:

**Grab and execute Slab while he's peeing.**

Those last three words are what the challenge is actually checking.

  />
  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">
    Let Slab reach the actual peeing animation. Reaching the correct location is
    not enough by itself.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

Stay far enough back that he can finish moving into position.

When the animation visibly starts, close the gap and grab him.

This is one of the clearest examples of why Main Street challenges depend on
**state**, not just target and location.

You can have:

- the correct NPC;
- the correct area;
- the correct execution;

and still miss the challenge because the interaction happened before the
required animation became active.

If Slab sees Michael, reacts, or has already left that state, the opportunity is
gone.

There is little value in repeatedly grabbing him beside the same spot afterward.

Continue with Rachel's group and decide later whether enough of the run succeeded
to justify finishing instead of restarting immediately.

<h2 id="rachel-environmental-kill">
  Rachel — Check Her Friends' Direction Before Using the Environmental Kill
</h2>

Rachel's requirement is broader than Eric's or Tanya's:

**execute Rachel using an environmental kill.**

She is not tied to one specifically named prop.

The fence interaction shown here works and offers **Head Impale**.

The kill itself is not the difficult part.

The expensive mistake is spending several seconds on Rachel while her friends
are already leaving the gathering.

Before taking the environmental execution, look away from Rachel long enough to
establish where the others are heading.

Then use the environmental kill.

Now the scene has two fewer uncertainties:

Rachel's challenge is finished, and the remaining search has a direction.

<h2 id="rachels-friends">
  Follow Rachel's Three Friends Before the Split Goes Cold
</h2>

**Broken Record** requires all three of Rachel's friends.

Slab is difficult to forget because his own challenge gives him a distinct
route.

The remaining two can disappear into the neighborhood much more quietly.

In this run, their movement leads toward a nearby house.

One target can be reachable before Michael fully commits to the house.

Take that opportunity if it is clean, then continue toward the last friend.

Killer Sense may show unrelated residents nearby.

Those people are noise for Broken Record.

Chasing every highlighted person increases travel, creates more opportunities
for an alert, and makes it harder to remember which member of Rachel's group is
actually still missing.

Once all three friends are accounted for, stop expanding the search.

<h2 id="body-cleanup">
  Hide Rachel's Body When the Chapter Stops Advancing
</h2>

After the target sequence, Main Street can feel finished before the HUD agrees.

This is one of the places where wandering around looking for another victim
makes the problem worse.

The late objective can show:

**HIDE RACHEL'S BODY**

while Tanya appears separately as:

**OPTIONAL: HIDE TANYA'S BODY**

That wording determines the next move.

If the cemetery objective has not appeared yet, deal with Rachel first.

Use a hiding interaction the game recognizes.

A random dark corner or convenient-looking grave does not automatically count
as a valid body-hiding state.

Tanya can wait when the HUD explicitly marks her cleanup as optional.

Once Rachel's required cleanup clears, Chapter 2 pushes Michael toward Judith
Myers.

Interact with the grave.

Take the tombstone.

Return to the car.

That closes Chapter 2.

The cemetery also gives me a practical cutoff for failed challenge states.

A surviving member of Rachel's group may still be recoverable if I know exactly
where they went and the return trip is short.

Eric's missing stockroom shelf is not recoverable.

Slab cannot be put back into the peeing animation on demand.

An alert cannot be undone for Dead-End Job.

Once the tombstone route is active, those failures belong to another replay.

A late restart throws away every challenge that already survived the chapter
just to repair one condition whose setup happened much earlier.

Finish the car objective, keep the successful challenge clears, and make the
next Main Street run about the one condition that actually needs another opening
setup.
