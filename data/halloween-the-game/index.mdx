<h2 id="where-to-start">
  Which Halloween: The Game Guide Do You Need?
</h2>

*Halloween: The Game* gets much easier to navigate once the immediate problem is
clear.

A first Story run usually needs a destination. A challenge replay may need one
specific target or scene preserved. Civilian multiplayer is more fluid: the
important decision can change from character selection to inventory, escape
progress, Michael pressure, or whether the team should stop searching and commit
to what it already has.

This is the split I use:

- **Do not know where to go in Story Mode:** use the <Link href="/halloween-the-game/walkthrough">complete Story Mode walkthrough</Link>.
- **Working on Story, Progressive, or Passive Challenges:** use the <Link href="/halloween-the-game/challenges">Challenges guide</Link>.
- **Choosing a Civilian:** use the <Link href="/halloween-the-game/best-civilians">Best Civilians guide</Link>.
- **Unsure what an item does or whether it deserves an inventory slot:** use the <Link href="/halloween-the-game/items-guide">All Items & Uses guide</Link>.
- **Choosing which multiplayer escape to finish:** use the <Link href="/halloween-the-game/escape-helper">Escape & Spawn Helper</Link>.
- **Learning the wider Civilian match flow:** use the <Link href="/halloween-the-game/multiplayer-guide">Civilian Multiplayer Guide</Link>.
- **Trying to detain Michael instead of escaping:** use <Link href="/halloween-the-game/how-to-arrest-michael-myers">How to Arrest Michael Myers</Link>.
- **Spending XP, Perk Points, or building perk cards:** use the <Link href="/halloween-the-game/progression-perks">Progression & Perks Guide</Link>.
- **A level track has reached its cap:** use the <Link href="/halloween-the-game/prestige-guide">Prestige guide</Link>.
- **Cleaning up trophies or achievements:** use the <Link href="/halloween-the-game/achievements">all 50 achievements guide</Link>.
- **Returning for another finale:** use the <Link href="/halloween-the-game/endings">endings guide</Link>.

<h2 id="story">
  Story Mode — Follow the Current Objective Before Cleaning Up Everything Else
</h2>

The campaign runs from the Prologue through five chapters. On a first clear, I
usually follow the required objective once the HUD has clearly moved Michael to
the next part of the chapter instead of turning every optional interaction into
a detour.

The <Link href="/halloween-the-game/walkthrough">complete Story Mode walkthrough</Link> follows that progression from Smith's Grove through Chapter 5 and is the main route when the question is simply **where do I go next?**

Two chapters have enough timing-sensitive movement to justify their own detailed
routes. Use the <Link href="/halloween-the-game/main-street-walkthrough">Chapter 2 Main Street walkthrough</Link> for the hardware store, Rachel's group, body cleanup, cemetery, and return to the car, and the <Link href="/halloween-the-game/the-babysitters-walkthrough">Chapter 4 Babysitters walkthrough</Link> for the Wallace House sequence through Laurie and Loomis.

<h2 id="challenges">
  Challenges — Check the Requirement Before You Spend the Opportunity
</h2>

There are **36 Story Challenges** across the Prologue and Chapters 1–5, alongside
Progressive and Passive Challenges in multiplayer. Patch 1.0.3 also changed a
number of challenge requirements and tracking behaviors.

Use the <Link href="/halloween-the-game/challenges">Halloween: The Game Challenges guide</Link> for the current Story requirements, Progressive and Passive Challenge rules, Patch 1.0.3 changes, and the most common reasons a counter does not move.

A few Story conditions need much more than the requirement text alone. The <Link href="/halloween-the-game/prologue-challenges">Prologue Challenges guide</Link> shows how I split the six Smith's Grove objectives across clean and combat runs, while the <Link href="/halloween-the-game/prologue-challenge-5">Challenge 5 no-alert route</Link> handles the patrol timing for that single stealth condition.

For Chapter 2 and Chapter 4, the detailed walkthroughs are where the timing belongs: <Link href="/halloween-the-game/main-street-walkthrough">Main Street</Link> and <Link href="/halloween-the-game/the-babysitters-walkthrough">The Babysitters</Link> show which character states and story scenes need to remain available as the chapter advances.

<h2 id="multiplayer">
  Civilian Multiplayer — Follow the Match State, Not a Fixed Route
</h2>

Civilian matches do not have Story Mode's fixed sequence. Early information can
point toward several exits at once, then one Repair Kit, key, phone, Resident,
police interaction, or teammate action suddenly makes one route much more real
than the others.

The <Link href="/halloween-the-game/multiplayer-guide">Civilian Multiplayer Guide</Link> covers that wider match flow: opening priorities, Residents, calls, escape discovery, coordination, and when to stop exploring.

Once the problem becomes narrower — **which available escape should the team actually finish now?** — switch to the <Link href="/halloween-the-game/escape-helper">Escape & Spawn Helper</Link>.

<h2 id="best-civilians">
  Best Civilians — Pick for the Job the Match Needs
</h2>

Laurie Strode is the safest general-purpose choice, but repairs, Resident support,
anti-Michael pressure, and mobility can all make a specialist more useful in the
right lobby.

The <Link href="/halloween-the-game/best-civilians">Best Civilians guide</Link> compares all 12 current characters, their stats and traits, and the situations where I would move away from the general ranking.

<h2 id="items">
  Items — Keep What Solves the Current Problem
</h2>

Three small-item slots make inventory a tradeoff rather than a collection
checklist. An item that was valuable during the opening can become disposable
once its escape interaction is finished, while a defensive or healing item can
become much more important after Michael starts controlling the route.

Use the <Link href="/halloween-the-game/items-guide">All Items & Uses guide</Link> for what each pickup does, when I keep it, and when its job is finished.

<h2 id="escape-search">
  Escape Decisions — Stop Searching Once the Team Has Enough to Commit
</h2>

A multiplayer round can stall because the team keeps looking for a theoretically
better exit after one route is already close to completion.

I re-evaluate after a useful escape is discovered, a teammate confirms a critical
item, a known barrier is cleared, or a new route such as police becomes available.
At that point, the next useful action may be finishing what the team already
earned rather than opening another search loop.

The <Link href="/halloween-the-game/escape-helper">Escape & Spawn Helper</Link> is built for that decision.

<h2 id="arrest-michael">
  Arrest Michael — Only Commit Once the Detainment Setup Exists
</h2>

Damaging or knocking Michael down is not the same as being close to an arrest.
Detainment becomes a real alternative to escaping only after the required police,
Loomis or Reinforcements, control tools, and final interaction begin lining up.

Use <Link href="/halloween-the-game/how-to-arrest-michael-myers">How to Arrest Michael Myers</Link> for the full sequence once the team has actually committed to that route.

<h2 id="progression">
  Progression — Separate XP From the Resources You Choose to Spend
</h2>

Profile, Killer, Civilian, and Weapon progression do not all control the same
unlocks, and Perk Points belong to a separate card economy.

The <Link href="/halloween-the-game/progression-perks">Progression & Perks Guide</Link> covers those tracks, card rolls, upgrades, scrapping, Civilian attributes, starting-item unlocks, and how I decide where the next block of progression should go.

Challenge requirements themselves now live in the <Link href="/halloween-the-game/challenges">Challenges guide</Link>; this progression route is for what happens to XP, Perk Points, cards, and builds around those rewards.

<h2 id="prestige">
  Prestige — Check the Active Requirement Before Spending More Resources
</h2>

Prestige begins after the relevant progression track reaches its cap. From there,
the problem changes from ordinary leveling to eligibility, cost, an active timed
challenge, and the reward attached to that attempt.

The <Link href="/halloween-the-game/prestige-guide">Prestige guide</Link> covers those requirements, rewards, Perk Point costs, Private Match progress, and when I would start or delay an attempt.

For Passive Challenge behavior after Prestiging or general challenge tracking,
use the <Link href="/halloween-the-game/challenges">Challenges guide</Link> instead.

<h2 id="endings">
  Endings — Return to Chapter 5 Instead of Rebuilding the Whole Campaign
</h2>

After the first Story clear, alternate ending cleanup begins in **Into the
Shadows** rather than back at Smith's Grove.

The <Link href="/halloween-the-game/endings">endings guide</Link> covers the railroad, cornfield, and sewer routes and the points where those finale paths become committed.

<h2 id="achievements">
  Achievements — Work by Mode Instead of Treating All 50 as One List
</h2>

Story, Civilian, Michael, Loomis, hidden, and progression requirements become much
easier to clean up once I know which mode can actually advance the one I am
working on.

The <Link href="/halloween-the-game/achievements">all 50 achievements guide</Link> contains the full checklist, hidden requirements, Story cleanup, role-specific objectives, and late progression work.

<h2 id="collectibles">
  Collectibles — Pick Them Up When They Already Fit the Story Route
</h2>

On a first clear, I take recordings that sit naturally beside the required path
and leave long detours for later cleanup.

The <Link href="/halloween-the-game/achievements#collector">Collector section of the achievement guide</Link> has the recording locations when completion becomes the actual goal.

<h2 id="multiplayer-maps">
  Multiplayer Maps — Use Map Knowledge to Shorten the Next Search
</h2>

The four current multiplayer maps are **East Haddonfield, Haddonfield Heights,
Orange Grove Estates, and Haddonfield Town Center**.

Knowing every possible object is less important than knowing which part of the map
can answer the current problem. A nearly finished Sedan makes Gas candidates
valuable; an undiscovered Sedan makes car candidates more important; another
route may need an entirely different search.

The <Link href="/halloween-the-game/escape-helper">Escape & Spawn Helper</Link> lets me remove checked candidate areas and keep the search focused on what the match still needs.
