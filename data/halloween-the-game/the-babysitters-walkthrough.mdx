<h2 id="babysitters-sequence">
  The six Chapter 4 challenges are tied to one Wallace House sequence
</h2>

The Babysitters looks open because Michael can move around several nearby
houses.

The challenge run is much narrower.

Four of the six objectives depend directly on Annie, Bob, or Lynda reaching a
specific scene:

1. **Strangle Annie in the car**
2. **Pin Bob to the wall in the kitchen**
3. **Kill Lynda with the telephone while wearing the Sheet**
4. **Perform a Bloodthirsty execution**
5. **Fully stalk Bob and Lynda while they are making out in the living room**
6. **Complete the chapter while matching the film's plot**

That makes the Wallace House event chain more important than clearing the
neighborhood.

A reliable order is:

**Annie's setup → Bob and Lynda stalking → Bloodthirsty on a spare resident →
Bob's kitchen wall → Sheet → Lynda's telephone → upstairs staging → Laurie**

Only Bloodthirsty gives you much freedom over the victim.

The other challenges become impossible once the wrong person dies or the
required scene is interrupted.

For a normal Chapter 4 story clear without challenge cleanup, the

</Link>
can stay much closer to the active HUD objective.

<h2 id="annie">
  Annie's car challenge starts long before she reaches the car
</h2>

Annie is the first place where being faster can make the run worse.

Her objective may already tell Michael to deal with her, but the challenge
requires something much more specific:

**Annie has to die in the car sequence.**

She needs time to move through her routine first.

The part I watch is her transition away from the house and back toward the car.
If Michael forces contact before that sequence develops, Annie can die in the
correct area without ever creating the challenge interaction.

There is also another moving part near the opening: **Lester**.

In repeated Chapter 4 attempts, Lester being discovered can interfere with the
clean Annie setup. One route that avoids leaving his body beside Annie's path
is to deal with him immediately near the front yard and move the body away from
the Wallace property.

The timing is tight enough that this should happen at the start, not halfway
through Annie's routine.

By the time that opening cleanup is finished, Annie may already be moving
toward the laundry-room part of her sequence.

That is the point to stop wandering.

Stay around the Wallace House and let her continue.

There are two bad timings here.

Hide far too early and Michael can spend a long stretch sitting in the car
while Annie is still working through another state.

Wait until she has already moved past the usable interaction and the clean
window is gone.

The useful sequence is:

**Annie leaves the immediate area → her routine advances → she returns for the
car → Michael hides inside → Annie approaches → trigger the kill**

Once the car kill is finished, follow any body-placement objective the chapter
gives you rather than improvising a hiding place.

Her body matters again later when the house is staged for Laurie.

<h2 id="bob-lynda-stalk">
  Bob and Lynda are worth more alive together than as two early kills
</h2>

Bob and Lynda create the next temporary state.

Challenge 5 requires Michael to:

**fully stalk Bob and Lynda while they are making out in the living room.**

Both parts matter.

You need Bob.

You need Lynda.

You need them together during that scene.

This is one of the cheapest challenge completions in the chapter if you wait.

It becomes expensive only after somebody gets impatient.

Once Michael interrupts the scene, their later objectives begin separating.
Moving bodies around afterward will not put them back into the original
stalking state.

Let the stalking meter finish before advancing either character.

<h2 id="bloodthirsty">
  Use a spare resident for Bloodthirsty
</h2>

Bloodthirsty is the one challenge where the victim does not need to be one of
the chapter's main three.

That makes target choice simple.

Annie already belongs to the car.

Bob belongs to the kitchen wall.

Lynda belongs to the Sheet and telephone.

Spending any of them on Bloodthirsty exchanges a flexible requirement for a
scripted one.

Use another resident instead.

Once a valid Bloodthirsty execution is available, take it on somebody whose
death does not remove a later movie scene, then return to the Wallace House.

There is no reward for extending this into a neighborhood sweep.

One Bloodthirsty execution completes the requirement.

<h2 id="bob-wall">
  Bob is not finished until the kitchen wall prompt is ready
</h2>

After the shared stalking scene, Bob becomes the next resource you have to
protect.

His challenge does not say:

**kill Bob in the kitchen.**

It requires:

**pin Bob to the wall in the kitchen.**

That difference is where an otherwise successful kill fails.

Bring Bob into the kitchen sequence and look for the contextual wall
interaction.

A regular grab inside the room is not enough.

Another environmental kill a few feet away is not enough.

The wall prompt is the condition.

If it has not appeared, Bob is still more useful alive.

Adjust the position and let the interaction become available before committing
to the execution.

Once Bob dies through the wrong interaction, that challenge cannot be rebuilt
during the same run.

At that point I keep going.

Lynda, Bloodthirsty, the film sequence, and the rest of the chapter can still
produce useful progress.

<h2 id="sheet">
  Bob's death changes Michael's next objective from killing to disguise
</h2>

After Bob's scene, take the **Sheet**.

Put it on.

Then keep it on.

Lynda's challenge is a three-part check:

**Lynda + telephone execution + Sheet equipped**

Reaching Lynda early does not improve that setup.

If she is accessible downstairs while the telephone sequence has not developed,
leave the kill alone.

Let her scene progress.

The disguise only becomes valuable when it survives all the way to the
telephone interaction.

<h2 id="lynda-telephone">
  Lynda's challenge is ready when all three pieces meet upstairs
</h2>

Once Lynda reaches the correct part of the sequence, the challenge becomes very
clear.

Michael is still wearing the Sheet.

Lynda is available.

The telephone interaction is ready.

Use the telephone execution without removing the disguise.

This is not a section where improvising helps.

If the telephone prompt exists, use it.

If it does not, wait for the scene rather than spending Lynda on another
environmental interaction.

Once the execution completes, the chapter has finished the three most rigid
victim-specific setups:

- Annie — car;
- Bob — kitchen wall;
- Lynda — Sheet and telephone.

From here, the emphasis shifts to preparing Laurie’s arrival.

<h2 id="stage-house">
  The bodies are still part of the sequence after the kills are finished
</h2>

Chapter 4 does not discard Annie, Bob, and Lynda once they are dead.

The house now needs to become the scene Laurie discovers.

Follow the body-placement objectives and move the required victims upstairs.

Judith Myers' tombstone also becomes part of the arrangement.

This is another point where leaving the area costs time.

The major Annie, Bob, and Lynda challenge states have already been decided.

A random resident outside cannot restore one that failed.

Finish the required staging instead.

The film-plot challenge also depends on keeping this sequence moving in the
expected order, so rearranging your priorities this late creates more risk than
value.

<h2 id="film-plot">
  The film-plot challenge is protected by keeping the scenes in order
</h2>

Challenge 6 is broader than a specific kill:

**complete the chapter while matching the film's plot.**

I treat it as a sequence check rather than another task to perform separately.

The important chain is:

**Annie's car → Bob and Lynda together → Bob's death → Sheet → Lynda's
telephone scene → upstairs staging → Laurie**

The more closely those events stay in order, the less mystery there is about
what the chapter expects next.

This is also why random early kills are so costly here.

Annie dying before the car removes her scene.

Breaking Bob and Lynda apart before the stalking condition finishes removes
their shared window.

Lynda dying before the Sheet and telephone setup skips another major beat.

Once the chapter has moved beyond one of those states, running back through the
house cannot rebuild it.

<h2 id="laurie">
  Laurie is the moment when waiting stops being useful
</h2>

Most of The Babysitters rewards patience.

Laurie reverses that rule.

When the HUD changes to:

**KILL LAURIE BEFORE SHE CALLS FOR HELP**

the setup phase is over.

Stay with her.

The useful targets are now in front of Michael, not behind him.

A missed Bob wall interaction cannot be repaired.

Annie cannot be put back into the car routine.

Lynda cannot return to the telephone sequence.

Leaving Laurie to inspect one of those rooms only creates distance from the
objective that can still advance.

Keep pressure on her through the house until the sequence reaches Loomis and
Chapter 4 closes.

<h2 id="replay-decisions">
  A failed challenge does not always justify throwing away the whole chapter
</h2>

The cost of a restart depends on when the failure happened.

Annie dies outside the car during the opening setup?

Restarting is cheap if her challenge or the film-plot run is your main goal.

Bob misses the kitchen wall after Annie and the stalking challenge are already
complete?

There is more value in finishing Lynda and banking the successful conditions.

The same calculation applies late in the house.

Once Lynda's telephone execution and the upstairs staging are finished, there
is little reason to abandon the attempt because one earlier scene failed.

Chapter 4 has six separate challenge results.

One mistake does not erase the other five automatically.

I use the next replay for the earliest broken state:

- **Annie failed:** preserve her routine and get into the car at the right
  return window.
- **Bob/Lynda stalking failed:** leave them together longer.
- **Bob wall failed:** keep Bob alive until the kitchen interaction appears.
- **Lynda failed:** keep the Sheet equipped and wait for the telephone.
- **Film plot failed:** reduce interference and follow the Wallace House scenes
  in order.

The Babysitters gets faster when the replay becomes narrower.

You already know where everybody goes.

The only question left is which scene needs another clean attempt.
