<h2 id="babysitters-sequence">
  Chapter 4 works like a sequence of scenes, not a neighborhood sweep
</h2>

The Babysitters gives Michael several houses to move through, but the Wallace
House is where the important sequence develops.

Go there first.

  <Image
    src="/images/halloween-the-game/halloween-the-game-the-babysitters-wallace-house.webp"

  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">
    Wallace House anchors the chapter. Annie, Bob, Lynda, and Laurie all feed
    into the same event chain.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

The six challenges are:

1. **Strangle Annie in the car**
2. **Pin Bob to the wall in the kitchen**
3. **Kill Lynda with the telephone while wearing the Sheet**
4. **Perform a Bloodthirsty execution**
5. **Fully stalk Bob and Lynda while they are making out in the living room**
6. **Complete the chapter while matching the film's plot**

The challenge order is not simply a kill list.

Annie needs time to reach the car.

Bob and Lynda need time together before their scene is interrupted.

The sheet has to exist before Lynda's kill can be completed correctly.

The upstairs house setup comes after those kills rather than before them.

Random residents are therefore a distraction unless they solve the flexible
Bloodthirsty challenge.

<h2 id="annie">
  Annie creates her own car-kill window if you leave her routine alone
</h2>

The objective can tell you to kill Annie before the challenge is actually ready.

Do not grab her at the first safe opportunity.

Her challenge requires the **car**.

Follow the scene long enough for Annie to deal with the car situation, leave,
and later return toward it.

The useful moment comes when she is about to get inside.

Enter the car hiding interaction shortly before she reaches it.

  </figcaption>

Hiding too early only leaves Michael waiting through an uncertain part of her
routine.

Acting after she has already moved past the interaction misses the cleaner
window.

The sequence to watch for is:

**Annie leaves → Annie returns for the car → she approaches the door → Michael
is hidden inside → trigger the car kill**

After Annie dies, move her body upstairs when the chapter asks for the house to
be prepared.

That body placement becomes relevant later when Laurie arrives.

<h2 id="bob-lynda-stalk">
  Finish Bob and Lynda's stalking challenge before either one becomes a kill target
</h2>

Bob and Lynda are valuable while they are still together.

One challenge requires Michael to **fully stalk both of them while they are
making out in the living room**.

That scene is temporary.

Once Michael interrupts it, killing them and moving their bodies cannot recreate
the untouched stalking opportunity.

Stay outside their immediate space and let the stalking progress finish first.

There is no upside to attacking a few seconds early.

The victims are already where the challenge wants them.

Let the meter finish.

Then move into the next part of the Wallace House sequence.

<h2 id="bloodthirsty">
  Spend Bloodthirsty on somebody who does not have another scripted job
</h2>

Bloodthirsty is the flexible challenge in this chapter.

It asks for a Bloodthirsty execution on a resident without tying the kill to
Annie, Bob, or Lynda.

That makes a side resident useful.

Annie is reserved for the car.

Bob is reserved for the kitchen wall.

Lynda is reserved for the sheet and telephone.

Using one of those three merely because Bloodthirsty is ready risks trading a
specific challenge for a generic one.

A nearby resident without another scripted role is the cheaper target.

Once the Bloodthirsty execution is complete, return to the Wallace House event
chain rather than continuing through the neighborhood looking for more optional
kills.

<h2 id="bob-wall">
  Bob needs the kitchen wall interaction, not simply a kill inside the kitchen
</h2>

After the stalking challenge is finished, Bob becomes the next delicate part of
the sequence.

His challenge specifically requires Michael to:

**pin Bob to the wall in the kitchen.**

Bring the sequence into the kitchen and look for the contextual wall execution
before committing to the kill.

A normal grab in the kitchen is not the same condition.

A different execution in the kitchen is not automatically the wall challenge.

If the contextual wall option is not available yet, keep Bob alive while
adjusting the setup rather than spending him on another execution.

The important distinction is the **interaction**, not merely the room.

If Bob dies without the wall kill, continue with Lynda and the remaining
Chapter 4 objectives. His earlier setup cannot be repaired after the target is
gone.

Lynda's challenge combines three conditions:

**Lynda + telephone + Sheet equipped**

All three need to meet in the same sequence.

With the disguise active, allow Lynda to continue reacting to Michael and move
the scene toward the upstairs telephone setup.

Attacking her downstairs because she is already reachable throws away the
reason you put the sheet on.

<h2 id="lynda-telephone">
  Keep the Sheet equipped until the telephone kill is finished
</h2>

Once Lynda reaches the correct upstairs sequence, the remaining pieces are easy
to read.

Michael has the sheet.

Lynda is in position.

The telephone is available.

Use the telephone execution without changing out of the disguise.

This is one of the least ambiguous challenge moments once it is ready.

There is no need to substitute another environmental kill or chase Lynda out of
the room.

Finish the telephone interaction and let the house move into its final setup.

<h2 id="stage-house">
  After the three main victims are down, prepare what Laurie is about to find
</h2>

Chapter 4 does not immediately discard Annie, Bob, and Lynda after their kills.

Their placement becomes part of the next scene.

Move the required bodies upstairs and follow the house-preparation objectives.
Judith Myers' tombstone is also incorporated into the staged interior.

At this point the neighborhood no longer matters much.

The major challenge opportunities involving Annie, Bob, and Lynda have already
either succeeded or failed.

Stay around the Wallace House and finish the staging objectives.

Leaving to hunt another resident creates distance from the next scripted event
without reopening any of those earlier windows.

<h2 id="laurie">
  Laurie ends the waiting game
</h2>

Everything changes when Laurie enters the sequence.

The earlier parts of Chapter 4 reward patience because Michael needs Annie, Bob,
and Lynda to reach specific moments.

Laurie does not give you that luxury.

The HUD changes to:

**KILL LAURIE BEFORE SHE CALLS FOR HELP**

Stay on her route through the house.

Do not leave the chase to inspect Bob's kitchen again.

Do not go back for a missed side resident.

Do not try to repair an Annie or Lynda challenge that has already failed.

Those moments are over.

Keep pressure on Laurie until the sequence reaches the confrontation with
Loomis and Chapter 4 closes.

<h2 id="film-plot">
  The film-plot challenge survives only if the major scenes stay in order
</h2>

The sixth challenge is broader than a single prop or execution:

**complete the chapter while matching the film's plot.**

The easiest way to protect it is to think in scenes:

**Wallace House → Annie's car → Bob and Lynda together → Bob → Sheet → Lynda
and telephone → staged upstairs room → Laurie**

Do not rush ahead of that chain.

Annie dying before the car breaks an early scene.

Interrupting Bob and Lynda before the stalking window removes another.

Killing Lynda without the sheet-and-phone combination breaks her scripted part.

Once the house has reached the Laurie section, earlier failures are no longer
recoverable inside that attempt.

That does not make the rest of the chapter worthless.

If Annie's car kill, the Bob/Lynda stalking challenge, Bloodthirsty, or Lynda's
telephone kill are already complete, finish the sequence and keep those results.

Replay Chapter 4 only for the scene that actually failed.

<h2 id="replay">
  On a replay, wait for the missing scene instead of changing the whole route
</h2>

The Babysitters becomes much faster after the first completion because its
difficult moments are mostly timing windows.

Missed Annie?

Get back to the Wallace House and wait for her car routine.

Lost the Bob and Lynda stalking challenge?

Leave them together longer before entering.

Missed Bob's wall interaction?

Protect Bob until the correct kitchen execution is available.

Killed Lynda without the full disguise setup?

Do not touch her until Michael has the sheet and the telephone scene is ready.

A Chapter 4 replay does not need more exploration.

It needs less interference.

The characters already know where they are supposed to go. Give them enough
time to get there.
