<h2 id="best-civilians">
  Which Civilian Is Best in Halloween: The Game?
</h2>

For general multiplayer, I rank the current roster like this:

- **S Tier:** Laurie Strode, Alexis Purcell, Marcus Navarro
- **A Tier:** Rachel Calahan, Tanya Harrison, Jennifer Aarons
- **B Tier:** Eric, Richard, Thomas, Annie, Linda, Bob

That order is useful before a match, but it is not the same thing as saying
Laurie should always replace Rachel or Marcus should always replace Jennifer.
Civilian matches ask for very different jobs. One team may spend most of the
round repairing and committing to an escape, while another spends its time
moving Residents, building police pressure, or repeatedly putting Michael on
the ground.

The question I use is therefore not just **"Who has the strongest stats?"**

It is **"Which character makes the job I expect to do easier?"**

Laurie is still my default because her **8 Capability, 6 Athleticism, 9
Personality, and 7 Resourcefulness** leave no severe weakness. Marcus goes in
the opposite direction and reaches **10 Resourcefulness**, while Alexis trades
direct Capability for **10 Personality** and unusually strong follower traits.

Rachel, Tanya, and Jennifer are where the ranking starts becoming dependent on
the lobby. A team that already wants to fight Michael can make Rachel look
better than an S-tier character. A scattered team that keeps getting caught
while rotating around the map can make Jennifer the more useful choice.

<h2 id="laurie">
  Laurie Strode Is the Best Pick When You Are Not Sure What the Match Will Need
</h2>

Laurie's **8 / 6 / 9 / 7** spread gives me the most comfortable starting point
because none of the four main stats forces the rest of the match into one
narrow role. She can contribute to objectives, talk to people effectively,
move well enough to recover from ordinary mistakes, and still bring useful
tools when Michael appears.

**Spatial Awareness** highlights escapes within **25 meters while calm**. I
value that most early in a round, before the team has committed to the Sedan,
Storm Cellar, police, or another way out. It helps turn nearby escape
information into a decision without sending me across the whole map looking
for something that may not be better.

**Knockout** keeps Michael down **10% longer** when Laurie helps with the
knockdown.

group needs. If three teammates are clearly building Resident progress, Alexis
can create more value. If a partially repaired escape is already controlling
the round, Marcus becomes more attractive. Until one of those conditions
exists, Laurie gives me the fewest ways to regret the pick.

<h2 id="marcus">
  Marcus Navarro Is the Pick When the Team Keeps Losing to Unfinished Objectives
</h2>

Marcus has **7 Capability, 5 Athleticism, 4 Personality, and 10
Resourcefulness**. The 10 Resourcefulness is not just a large number on the
character screen; his traits keep pushing him toward the same job.

**Repair Expert** gives a **20% chance not to consume a Repair Pack**.
**Trespasser** lets him pick a locked door with a **5-second hold
interaction**, and **Lone Wolf** gives him additional value if the match
collapses until he is the last Civilian alive.

The important timing with Marcus is **when the team stops exploring and starts
finishing**.

During the opening minutes, a Repair Kit sitting in my inventory does not
automatically make one escape the right route. I still want enough information
to know what the team has actually found.

His value jumps once several pieces line up. A Sedan that already has real
progress, a known Storm Cellar whose remaining barrier has been identified, a
damaged phone the team needs, or another objective that is one repair away
from becoming useful gives Marcus a concrete job.

At that point I stop treating every unexplored part of the map as equally
important. Spending another several minutes searching for a theoretically
better route can cost more than simply finishing the one the team has already
earned.

<h2 id="alexis-tanya">
  Alexis Purcell and Tanya Harrison Win Resident-Heavy Matches in Different Ways

Resourcefulness**. Looking only at Capability makes her seem much worse than
Laurie or Marcus, but that misses what the character is built to do.

**Popular** makes her Followers take **90% less damage**, gain **20% less
fear**, and fight more aggressively while protecting her. **Savior Stimulant**
adds **2% movement speed** after helping a Resident escape, while **Smooth
Talker** expands the perfect area of the dispatch-call skill check by **50%**.

<h2 id="rachel">
  Rachel Calahan Becomes S-Tier When the Team Is Already Fighting Michael Well
</h2>

Rachel's base line is **7 Capability, 5 Athleticism, 2 Personality, and 8
Resourcefulness**. She does not want the same match as Alexis. Her strengths
become more visible once Michael is already being contested.

**Full Moon** makes damage and knockdowns against Michael deal **40% more
damage to his Bloodthirst**. **Aura of Awareness** increases Rachel's personal
Shape Jump prevention radius by **15%**, while **Sixth Sense** gives her an
additional warning when Michael is close during Shape Jump.

The **40% Bloodthirst effect is only valuable when the team is actually
creating those hits and knockdowns**. That sounds obvious, but it is the main
reason Rachel can feel either excellent or ordinary from one lobby to the next.

Four Civilians with weapons, space, and enough coordination to punish Michael
can extract repeated value from Full Moon. A Rachel who is alone, poorly
equipped, or surrounded by teammates who immediately scatter does not get the
same return.

Her 5 Athleticism matters here as well. Once a fight turns into a chase, she
does not have Jennifer's movement margin to repair every mistake.

For that reason I move Rachel toward S tier after matches where my group is
already controlling engagements successfully. I move her back toward A when
the team is surviving by rotating away from Michael rather than knocking him
down.

<h2 id="jennifer">

Resourcefulness**. Nine Athleticism is the standout stat, but the more useful
part is how cleanly it fits her traits.

**Ghost** increases stalk degradation by **5%**. **Dreamer** makes Residents
she successfully interacts with harder for Michael to stalk, while **Survival
Rage** gives her **10% more melee damage while terrified**.

<h2 id="b-tier">
  The B-Tier Civilians Become Good Picks When Their Condition Is Already Present
</h2>

The six B-tier Civilians are not six bad characters. What they share is a
narrower window where their advantages clearly beat the safer choices above
them.

### Eric — equipment and escape information

Eric has **6 Capability, 3 Athleticism, 6 Personality, and 8
Resourcefulness**.

**Hunter Sense** can show faint noise pings and highlight escapes within
**25 meters** while he is not terrified. **Fast Fingers** increases ranged
weapon and flashlight reload speed by **10%**, and **Brimstone Fuse** makes
his firecrackers disorient Michael **50% longer**.

That is a useful equipment kit, especially when the group is already using
flashlights, ranged weapons, and firecrackers deliberately.

Three Athleticism is the cost. A bad doorway, a late rotation, or an isolated
encounter with Michael is much harder to recover from than it is with
Jennifer.

### Richard — high payoff after a very specific setup

Richard has **6 Capability, 7 Athleticism, 9 Personality, and 5
Resourcefulness**.

His most dramatic condition comes from **Affluent Partier**, which can add
**40% melee damage while he is both intoxicated and high**.

Forty percent is large enough to matter. The reason he remains B tier is the
setup cost: I need both status conditions active before that part of the kit is
doing anything.

When the consumables are already available and the group wants to fight, his
ranking rises. Building the whole round around finding those conditions makes
him harder to recommend over Rachel, who contributes to anti-Michael pressure
more directly.

### Thomas — strong people skills, weak objective specialization

Thomas brings **7 Capability, 8 Athleticism, 10 Personality, and only 2
Resourcefulness**.

**Inspiring** gives townsfolk he tells to hide **20% more maximum health**.
**Barricade** makes doors he locks **10% tougher for Michael to destroy**.

That combination gives Thomas a reasonable Resident-protection identity, plus
enough Athleticism to move well.

Two Resourcefulness is the number that keeps me from using him as an
objective-first pick. When repairs and escape interactions become the main
bottleneck, Marcus solves that problem more directly.

### Annie — useful when escape discovery is already the goal

Annie has **5 Capability, 4 Athleticism, 6 Personality, and 8
Resourcefulness**, with **Exit Strategy** among her traits.

I like her more when my immediate purpose is escape-oriented utility and the
match has not yet settled into a single route. Four Athleticism is the part
that makes me more cautious once Michael starts controlling the same area.

### Linda — stronger when teammates use the opening she creates

Linda has **4 Capability, 8 Athleticism, 7 Personality, and 5
Resourcefulness**.

**Blinding Light** keeps Michael blinded **50% longer**. That can create an
excellent window, but the value depends heavily on what happens during those
extra seconds.

A coordinated team can heal, reposition, attack, or finish another action.
Linda creating the same blind while everybody is scattered produces much less.

### Bob — strong body, less distinctive match role

Bob has **9 Capability, 8 Athleticism, 4 Personality, and 3
Resourcefulness**.

Those first two numbers are excellent. **Lethal Pitch** makes thrown items and
weapons deal **10% more damage**, while **Runner's High** reduces the movement
penalty from exhaustion by **5%**.

Bob is therefore not weak in a direct sense. The reason I keep him in B tier
is that the stronger picks answer a multiplayer problem more clearly: Marcus
finishes objectives, Alexis handles Followers, Rachel changes Michael
pressure, and Jennifer controls movement.

Bob becomes easier to justify once I specifically want his physical profile
and thrown-weapon pressure rather than a broader team role.

<h2 id="ranking-changes">
  What Makes Me Change My Civilian Pick After a Few Matches?
</h2>

The most useful tier-list information usually appears after a loss. The reason
the last few matches failed tells me more than the letter beside a character's
portrait.

When the team repeatedly **finds escape pieces but cannot finish the work**, I
move toward **Marcus**. Another strong fighter does not solve an unfinished
repair.

When Residents keep dying before they create value, **Alexis** becomes more
interesting. If the Resident plan is working but phone information keeps
slowing us down, I would rather have **Tanya**.

When the group is already landing stuns and knockdowns cleanly, **Rachel**
moves upward because Full Moon now has repeated opportunities to matter.

When Michael keeps catching people between objectives instead of at the
objective itself, **Jennifer's 9 Athleticism and Ghost** become much more
valuable.

When I cannot predict which of those problems the next lobby will create, I go
back to **Laurie**.

That is why I do not treat the tier list as permanent character strength. The
best pick is often the character that fixes the failure pattern the team has
already shown.

<h2 id="michael-pressure">
  Michael's Ability Choice Can Also Move a Civilian Up or Down
</h2>

Some Michael abilities punish particular Civilian plans much harder than
others.

**Reality Tear** can turn a Shape Jump approach into a stun and immediate grab
threat. That increases the value of Rachel's Shape Jump-related tools and makes
Jennifer's ability to create distance more attractive. Laurie remains useful
because her kit does not require Michael to behave one specific way.

**Evil Presence** makes repeated close-range engagements more expensive. A team
that keeps trying to bully Michael through every encounter can discover that a
second or third fight is much worse than the first one.

The practical response is not always to find more damage. Sometimes the right
answer is letting one Civilian keep Michael occupied while Marcus finishes an
escape, or allowing Jennifer to rotate away rather than turning another
meeting into a fight.

This is also why I do not rank Civilians from combat output alone. Civilian
multiplayer is still won by completing something useful before Michael removes
the team's options.

<h2 id="perk-direction">
  Perks Should Strengthen the Job That Made the Civilian Worth Picking
</h2>

The perk deck can either reinforce a character or blur the reason I selected
them.

Marcus already has 10 Resourcefulness and Repair Expert, so repair and
objective-oriented effects stack naturally with the work I expect him to do.
Jennifer's 9 Athleticism and Ghost make stamina and movement support easier to
convert into real distance. Rachel benefits more from effects that keep her
alive during coordinated pressure than from pretending she is an
objective-first character.

The same rule applies to Alexis and Tanya. A perk that improves Resident or
team utility is easier to justify when the character's base kit is already
creating value from those interactions.

I therefore judge perk cards by the repeated job they change. A higher-rarity
card that solves the wrong problem can still be a worse choice than a simpler
effect that makes the Civilian's main job more reliable.

<h2 id="who-to-pick">
  Which Civilian Should You Pick Next?
</h2>

Pick **Laurie Strode** when you want the safest answer and do not yet know what
the lobby will demand.

Pick **Marcus Navarro** after matches where escape progress keeps stalling on
repairs, locked access, or unfinished objective work.

Pick **Alexis Purcell** when Followers are central to the plan, or **Tanya
Harrison** when you want that Resident strength with better phone and local
information utility.

Pick **Rachel Calahan** when your team already knows how to pressure and knock
down Michael together.

Pick **Jennifer Aarons** when the costly part of the match is moving between
objectives, shedding stalk, and avoiding another extended chase.

The B-tier characters become the right choice once their condition is already
part of the plan: Eric for equipment, Richard for his status-based melee
setup, Thomas for Resident protection, Annie for escape-oriented utility,
Linda for coordinated disruption, and Bob for direct physical pressure.

That is the point where the letter tier matters less than the job the next
match actually needs.
