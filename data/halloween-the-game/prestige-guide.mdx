<h2 id="how-to-prestige">
  How to Prestige in Halloween: The Game
</h2>

Prestige starts with the progression track I am actually trying to advance.

The current level caps are:

- **Profile:** Level 100
- **Michael Myers:** Level 50
- **Civilian:** Level 25
- **Weapon:** Level 10

Those tracks are separate. Reaching Profile 100 does not make a Level 18
Civilian or Level 7 weapon ready to Prestige.

If I am still working toward one of those caps, I keep that grind separate
from the Prestige attempt itself. The
<Link href="/halloween-the-game/progression-perks">
  {" "}progression and perks guide
</Link>
covers Profile, Michael, Civilian, Weapon XP, and the Perk Point economy before
Prestige becomes the next decision.

Once the correct track is capped, I open its Prestige section and check the
challenge before committing.

That last step matters because Prestige is not an automatic reset or reward.
Starting it activates an objective that has to be finished within its time
limit.

Only **one Prestige Challenge can be active at a time**.

<h2 id="starting-a-prestige-challenge">
  Check the Cost and Objective Before You Start
</h2>

A Prestige button is not something I press immediately after reaching the cap.

On Jennifer's Prestige 1 screen, starting the challenge costs **3,000 Perk
Points**.

The same screen also warns that failing to complete the challenge in time
means spending **more Perk Points** to try again.

I would not turn that **3,000** into a universal Prestige price yet. It is the
cost shown for this Jennifer Prestige attempt, and other tracks or later ranks
may not use the same number.

It still changes how I manage Perk Points.

Burning the balance on card rolls immediately before Prestige can leave me
unable to recover cleanly from a failed attempt. When I know a Prestige cap is
close, keeping a reserve starts to matter more than squeezing one more perk
roll out of the account.

I also check that the objective text has actually appeared.

If the screen is blank, gives an error, or does not tell me what action will
advance the challenge, I do not start guessing. Prestige has already had
problems with missing challenge criteria, so I would rather restart and check
again than activate a timer without knowing the requirement.

<h2 id="confirmed-prestige-challenges">
  Prestige Challenges and Rewards I Could Confirm
</h2>

I would not call the current information an exhaustive list of every Prestige
Challenge yet.

Different Prestige tracks can ask for completely different behavior, and some
objectives are still appearing inconsistently.

The useful approach right now is to separate challenges I can tie to a
specific track from objectives that have only appeared without enough context.

<h3 id="profile-prestige">
  Profile Prestige: 30 Bloodthirsty Pitchfork Executions
</h3>

The Profile Prestige challenge I worked with required:

**Perform 30 Bloodthirsty Pitchfork Executions.**

The reward shown for that challenge was **Clown Myers**.

The hard part is the word **Bloodthirsty**.

This is not 30 normal Pitchfork kills. I need **Bloodthirst Tier 3** before
going for the qualifying execution.

That turns every attempt into a short setup:

1. Build Bloodthirst to Tier 3.
2. Find or keep a Pitchfork.
3. Put the target somewhere the execution has enough room to play.
4. Complete the Bloodthirsty Pitchfork execution.
5. Check that the Prestige counter actually moved.

The space requirement caused more failed setups than I expected.

Tight interiors, furniture, bodies, and awkward doorways can leave me with the
right weapon and the right Bloodthirst level but no clean execution position.
Once the grind is underway, open ground is more valuable than taking the first
target I happen to catch.

Michael can also lose the Pitchfork after being knocked down.

When that happens, recovering the weapon is more important than immediately
taking another normal kill. A regular execution may help the match, but it
does not advance an objective that specifically wants the Bloodthirsty
Pitchfork version.

<h3 id="profile-prestige-private-match">
  Private Matches Make the Profile Challenge Much Easier
</h3>

For this particular objective, a Private Match removes a lot of variables.

NPC executions can advance the challenge, so I do not need to rely on human
Civilians giving me 30 clean setups.

My route is:

1. Start a Private Match as Michael.
2. Stalk until Bloodthirst reaches Tier 3.
3. Get a Pitchfork.
4. Favor NPCs already standing in open areas.
5. Perform the Bloodthirsty Pitchfork execution.
6. Check progress before committing to another long batch.

That sixth step is important.

I had a round where I expected completed executions to increase the counter,
returned to the menu, and found that the progress had not registered.

Because of that, I test the counter early. If one or two clean executions move
it, I continue. If they do not, I stop before turning a tracking problem into
an hour of wasted grinding.

Even with controlled matches, the Profile challenge is not short. One attempt
was approaching **three hours** and was still one execution away from 30.

A 24-hour limit sounds generous until every qualifying kill also depends on
Tier 3 Bloodthirst, the correct weapon, enough execution space, and a counter
that is actually recording the result.

It should influence where I go when there is room to do so, not make me ignore
an escape that is already working.

<h2 id="prestige-not-working">
  Why Your Prestige Challenge May Not Be Working
</h2>

Prestige has several different failure states, and they do not all need the
same response.

### The challenge is active, but the counter does not move

This is where I test one or two clean qualifying actions first.

For the Profile challenge, that means a genuine Tier 3 Bloodthirsty Pitchfork
execution rather than a normal Pitchfork kill.

If the requirement is clearly satisfied and the number still does not change,
I stop grinding and recheck the challenge state.

Doing another 20 identical attempts will not fix a counter that is not
registering.

### The game will not give you a Prestige Challenge

Another failure state is much more obvious:

**“Cannot receive a new Prestige Challenge at this time.”**

If I see this, I do not spend time trying to satisfy an objective the game has
not actually issued.

I back out, restart the game, and check the Prestige section again.

The same principle applies when the Prestige page loads but the objective text
is missing. The first problem is the challenge state itself, not my route.

### The objective finishes, but Prestige does not resolve

Jennifer produced the worst version of the problem.

The required townsfolk interactions took roughly **six and a half hours** to
finish, but completing the displayed objective did not lead to the expected
Prestige result.

At that point I stop treating the issue as player error.

If the counter never moves, I verify the requirement.

If the counter reaches its target and Prestige still fails, repeating the same
multi-hour grind is not the next useful move.

<h2 id="before-starting-prestige">
  When I Would Start a Prestige Challenge
</h2>

I use four checks before activating one.

**The correct track has reached its cap.**  
Profile, Michael, Civilian, and Weapon progression are separate. I make sure
the cap belongs to the thing I actually intend to Prestige.

**The objective is visible.**  
A blank challenge or an error screen is a reason to restart first.

**I have time to work on it.**  
The challenges I have tested use a 24-hour timer, while the actual grind can
still take several hours.

**I can afford the attempt without emptying my Perk Point balance.**  
Jennifer Prestige 1 showed a 3,000-point start cost, and another attempt can
cost more after failure.

If one of those conditions is missing, waiting is usually cheaper than
starting a timed challenge badly prepared.

<h2 id="other-prestige-objectives">
  Other Prestige Objectives I Am Still Verifying
</h2>

Another objective reported during these Prestige attempts asks for:

**25 one-hit kills with the Pickaxe.**

A one-hit Pickaxe kill appears to depend on **Bloodthirst Tier 3**, so the
short objective text hides another setup requirement.

I am not assigning that objective to a fixed Michael or Weapon Prestige track
yet.

There is enough instability around current Prestige criteria that I would
rather keep unconfirmed objectives separate than turn them into a misleading
“all challenges” list.

That is also why I am keeping this page as the Prestige Owner Page instead of
splitting individual challenge objectives into separate guides.

As more tracks and later Prestige ranks become consistent, they can be added
here with their exact requirement and reward.

<h2 id="private-match-prestige">
  When Private Matches Are Actually Worth Using
</h2>

Private Matches are strongest when the Prestige objective asks Michael to
repeat an action against targets he can control.

The Profile Pitchfork challenge fits that pattern well.

They can also help while finishing Michael, Profile, or Weapon progression
before Prestige. A controlled Michael round can take around **five to six
minutes** and may produce roughly **one to two levels**, depending on where the
track starts and how efficiently the round is played.

That method does not automatically transfer to every Prestige Challenge.

Jennifer needs townsfolk interactions.

A Michael execution challenge needs Bloodthirst, a weapon, and targets.

A Weapon Prestige objective may ask for another specific condition entirely.

The fastest route therefore comes from matching the farming method to the
actual objective rather than treating Private Match as a universal shortcut.

<h2 id="after-prestige">
  What Happens After a Successful Prestige?
</h2>

This is the part I would avoid making assumptions about.

The Profile challenge clearly listed **Clown Myers** as its reward, and
Prestige progression can also be tied to customization rewards.

What I have not had is a clean enough successful Prestige sequence on the
current build to verify every post-Prestige reset rule with confidence.

Until that is clearer, I would not dismantle a working loadout, scrap perk
cards, or spend currency because I expect Prestige to wipe a particular level
or inventory category.

Complete the challenge first.

Then make the next progression decision from the state the game actually
leaves behind.
