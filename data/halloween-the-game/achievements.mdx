<h2 id="achievement-roadmap">
  The route I use to unlock all 50 achievements
</h2>

The achievement list looks much worse if I treat every requirement as a separate
job.

A cleaner run has three phases.

I start with Story Mode because the prologue and five chapters already cover the
chapter-completion achievements, give me a place to work on secondary
challenges, and put all 14 recordings and dozens of pumpkins directly in my
path.

Multiplayer comes second. At that point I stop thinking in terms of one
achievement per match. A Civilian round can advance resident rescues, police
calls, bolt-cutter progress, thrown-item hits, match completion, wins, and
character XP at the same time. Michael works the same way with stalking,
executions, Resident kills, lights, hiding spots, and weapon counters.

Only the objectives that need a very specific state are worth forcing. **Put him
away**, **I found the Car!**, and **Risky Escape** are the clearest examples.
If the right role, escape route, items, or teammates are not there, I keep
progressing something else instead of wasting the whole match trying to create
the condition from nothing.

<h2 id="story-mode-achievements">
  Clear Story Mode before turning the achievement list into a grind
</h2>

The basic Story achievements follow the campaign naturally:

- **Escape** — finish Smith's Grove
- **The Jump Suit** — finish Road to Haddonfield
- **The Mask** — finish Main Street
- **He Came Home** — finish Returning Home
- **Babysitters** — finish Babysitters
- **Into the Shadows** — finish Into the Shadows
- **The End** — finish Story Mode

I would finish that route before replaying individual chapters for score or
challenge cleanup. The full chapter order and progression blockers belong in the
<Link href="/halloween-the-game/walkthrough">
  {" "}Story Mode walkthrough
</Link>
, so I use this page only to decide what matters for the achievement run.

Two Story achievements need more deliberate play.

**Thorough** asks for every secondary challenge in a single chapter. I use a
chapter I already know rather than picking the longest map just because I am
currently there. A failed attempt still teaches the patrols and context-kill
positions, but repeating a twenty-minute setup for one missed challenge is much
slower than cleaning an early chapter whose route is already familiar.

If the prologue is where the remaining challenge is blocking you, the exact
conditions and route are covered in the
<Link href="/halloween-the-game/prologue-challenges">
  {" "}Prologue Challenges guide
</Link>
.

**The Perfect Killer** is the stricter version of the same idea. I aim for the
cleanest chapter I can reliably finish at a five-star level instead of trying to
perfect whichever chapter happens to be next in the menu.

has not always persisted correctly after returning to the menu. I have seen a
run award its achievement while the chapter screen later behaved as if some
challenges were unfinished.

That changes my save habit. Once a difficult Story achievement pops, I return to
the menu and confirm what actually persisted before starting another long
cleanup attempt.

<h2 id="collector">
  Collector: all 14 recording locations in one route
</h2>

**Collector** is only at **0.9%** on the current achievement list, but the route
itself is manageable if I collect recordings while advancing the story instead
of replaying all six sections afterward.

There are **14 recordings** in total.

### Prologue

1. **Loomis Log #2** — immediately after starting, turn around and go to the
   opposite corner of the fenced area.
2. **Loomis Log #4** — when the route asks you to lower the gate, go left
   instead. The recording is on the trash can at the small bus shelter.

### Road to Haddonfield

3. **Loomis Log #6** — inside the diner/bar, go to the back dressing-room area
   and check the dresser.
4. **Loomis Log #8** — during the mechanic area, enter the garage and take the
   middle route into the office. It is on the desk.

### Main Street

5. **Loomis Log #9** — enter the music store near Tanya and check the boxes
   against the blue wall in the back.
6. **Loomis Log #3** — go to the house directly north of the cemetery. Enter
   through the front door and check the fireplace on the right.

### Returning Home

7. **Loomis Log #5** — after dealing with the intruder, enter the house to the
   southeast with the yellow van outside. Check the bedroom desk across from
   the dining room.
8. **Loomis Log #1** — enter the house to the left of Marcus's location and
   follow the hallway to the cabinet at the back.

### Babysitters

9. **Loomis Log #10** — enter the house opposite the Wallace house, turn left
   past the dining table, and check the cabinet between the couches.
10. **Loomis Log #7** — near the southern end of the area, go behind the house
    used during the Annie objective and enter the small shed. The log is on the
    washing machine.
11. **Sheriff Brackett's Recording** — from that rear-building area, move to
    the neighboring house and enter through the front door. Check the dining
    table.

### Into the Shadows

12. **Laurie's Recording** — before leaving the house where the chapter starts,
    stay on the ground floor, reach the dining room, and check the small table
    beside the lamp.
13. **Loomis Log #11** — leave through the front door, cross diagonally to the
    large white house, go upstairs, turn left, and continue to the room at the
    end of the hall.
14. **Nurse Chambers's Recording** — go to the top-right part of the map and
    enter the house to the right of the sports field. In the living room, check
    the large china cabinet opposite the fireplace.

<h2 id="civilian-achievements">
  Bundle Civilian achievements around residents, escapes, and useful items
</h2>

Civilian achievements become much faster once I stop treating escape as the only
measure of a good round.

**Convincing**, **Eye Witness**, **Hunker Down**, and **Heroic** all reward the
work I am already doing while searching houses and dealing with Residents.
When I find a Resident early, I make a decision immediately: hide them if the
area is unsafe, or start moving them toward an escape if our team has already
opened one.

That is better than leaving every Resident standing where I found them and
trying to manufacture fifty hiding commands or one hundred escapes later.

The broader multiplayer flow stays in the
<Link href="/halloween-the-game/multiplayer-guide">
  {" "}Halloween multiplayer guide
</Link>
.

For this achievement run, there are a few specific conditions I track.

### Unchained

**Unchained** requires **15 chains or padlocks removed with Bolt Cutters**.

Bolt Cutters are valuable enough that I do not carry them around with no plan
once someone has already found a locked escape. If a teammate knows where the
padlock is, bringing the cutters there advances the route and the achievement at
the same time.

### As a matter of fact, it was.

This one is character-specific.

Play **Laurie Strode**, keep a Pocket Knife available, and let the knife break
Michael's grab.

I would not burn the Pocket Knife on something optional if this achievement is
still unfinished. The hard part is not understanding the condition; it is
having Laurie, the knife, and an actual grab happen in the same round.

### I found the Car!

The car achievement is one of the best examples of why I wait for the match to
give me a workable setup.

A Sedan escape uses three important pieces:

- **Sedan Key**
- **Repair Kit**
- **Gas**

Those items do not have to sit in one player's inventory. In multiplayer I have
seen one teammate hold the key while another carried the Repair Kit and Gas.
Once the team has confirmed all three pieces, searching random houses for more
escape loot becomes wasted time.

For **I found the Car!**, the extra condition is the important one: leave with
the car **full of Residents**.

That means I prepare the car first, identify where the remaining Residents are,
then commit to the drive. Leaving early with empty capacity throws away the
hardest part of the setup.

### No Friend Left Behind

This is one I leave open until Michael creates the opportunity.

The condition is rescuing a special target or Civilian from Michael's grasp.
Running around hoping somebody gets grabbed usually costs more than it gains.
When Michael is already pressuring another player or Resident, I stay close
enough to intervene instead of crossing the map for this achievement alone.

<h2 id="michael-achievements">
  Michael's list is fastest when several counters share the same match
</h2>

Most Michael achievements are cumulative, so spreading them across different
matches is fine.

The easy foundation is **A Personal Touch** for 5 executions, then
**Slaughterhouse** at 100 Resident kills and **Purely and simply Evil** at 250.
Those numbers climb naturally while I work on more specific actions.

The same is true of **Improvisational Evil**. Context executions are worth
varying while I still need ten unique ones. Repeating a familiar execution may
be convenient, but it stops helping that achievement once I have already
registered it.

### Combine the stalking achievements

Three achievements overlap especially well:

- **Right of Passage**
- **Natural Observer**
- **Stalker**

When a target gives me a safe stalking opportunity, I would rather finish the
stalk than rush the first possible hit.

**Right of Passage** wants a Resident at **stalk level 3**, followed by a Kitchen
Knife execution.

**Natural Observer** specifically wants a full stalk while Michael is standing
inside a stealth bush.

**Stalker** eventually wants **100 fully stalked victims**.

A bush setup can therefore contribute to the long counter while also clearing
Natural Observer, and the right Resident can then become the Right of Passage
target.

### Wrong Number needs timing, not grinding

For **Wrong Number**, destroying random Phone Boxes is not enough.

A Civilian or Resident has to be **actively placing a Police call** when Michael
disables the box.

I listen for the call and move on the phone once the interaction is underway.
Breaking every phone the moment I see it can actually remove the opportunity I
need.

### Ready or Not and Close Quarters

Both involve hiding spots, but their conditions are different.

**Ready or Not** wants Michael to pull **10 victims out of hiding spots**.

**Close Quarters** wants an actual **execution inside a hiding spot**.

When a match has players repeatedly hiding, that is the time to work on both.
There is little reason to spend an otherwise clean kill opportunity dragging a
victim across the map looking for the right furniture.

### Speed Kills

The wording is literal: stand in front of a moving escape car and make it stall.

I look for a Sedan that is already operational rather than camping a broken car
for ten minutes. Once players start driving, Michael's body becomes the blocker.

### Heavy Handed and Pincushion

These are weapon counters:

- **Heavy Handed** — 75 victims with two-handed melee weapons
- **Pincushion** — 15 kills with thrown weapons

I progress them when the correct weapon is already available. A match with poor
loot is still useful for stalking, Resident kills, executions, lights, or
hiding-spot progress.

That keeps one rare weapon from dictating the entire round.

<h2 id="loomis-achievements">
  Save Put him away for a match where the whole team can collapse on Michael
</h2>

Loomis has three achievements that naturally form a small progression.

**Pardon Me I’m Uh...Loomis** only needs me to return to the match as Dr.
Samuel Loomis.

**I shot Him 6 times!** then asks Loomis to use the revolver to put Michael into
a downed state.

The real difficulty is **Put him away**, currently only **2.5%**.

Michael can be knocked down repeatedly and still stand back up before the team
finishes the detain interaction. I treat this as a coordinated endgame rather
than a solo duel: keep damage and ammunition ready, bring surviving players
toward Michael, force the down, and start the detain immediately while the
window is still open.

<h2 id="hidden-achievements">
  All three hidden achievements and when to attempt them
</h2>

There are three hidden achievements in the current list.

### Off Balance

As Michael, throw a body into another person hard enough to make them stumble.

The setup is much easier when two targets are already close together. I wait for
that spacing instead of dragging a body across an empty street hoping another
victim walks into range.

### Risky Escape

This is the hidden Civilian achievement:

**get drunk and escape the map while driving the car.**

The order matters for how much risk I take, even if the achievement description
does not force a specific repair order.

I prepare the Sedan first. That means the **Sedan Key, Repair Kit, and Gas** are
accounted for and the car route is actually viable.

Only then do I spend time creating the drunk state.

Getting drunk while the team is still missing the Gas or Sedan Key just makes
the dangerous part of the round longer. Once the car is ready, drink, get into
the driver's seat, and leave through the car escape.

### Pumpkin Smasher

**Destroy 50 Pumpkins throughout Haddonfield.**

This one is cumulative, so I start it during Story Mode instead of saving fifty
pumpkins for a dedicated cleanup session.

Whenever a chapter route already passes pumpkins, I break them before moving
on. The counter eventually popped at 50 even though the game did not make the
running total especially clear.

<h2 id="progression-cleanup">
  Keep long progression achievements moving while the other 40-plus unlock
</h2>

The final group is not mechanically difficult. It is expensive in time if I
spread progress too widely.

**Haddonfield Local** needs **50 completed multiplayer matches** and **Off to a
Great Start** needs **10 victories**. Both should be mostly finished by the time
the role-specific achievements are under control.

**Twice the Hero** is more deliberate because it requires **two different
Civilian characters at max level**.

Once I know which two Civilians I enjoy playing, I concentrate XP on them.
Spreading the same hours across every character makes the final two max-level
targets much slower.

**Determination** requires a successful Prestige. Reaching the level cap is
not enough; the active Prestige Challenge still has to be completed and
registered successfully. If that is the achievement blocking the run, the
<Link href="/halloween-the-game/prestige-guide">
  {" "}Prestige guide
</Link>
covers the level requirements, timed challenge rules, current objectives, and
the errors that can stop a Prestige attempt from completing.

**No Page Unturned** requires **100% completion of any passive challenge book**.
I pick the passive book already closest to completion and finish that one.
Trying to advance every book equally leaves a lot of partial progress and no
achievement.

Character leveling, perk unlocks, and the decisions around where to spend
levels before Prestige are covered in the
<Link href="/halloween-the-game/progression-perks">
  {" "}Progression and Perks guide
</Link>
.

The achievement route only needs one principle from that system: once I am near
the end, progression should become concentrated rather than broad.

Before calling the run finished, I check:

- all **14 recordings** collected;
- one Story chapter with every required challenge complete;
- **The Perfect Killer** cleared;
- all **3 hidden achievements**;
- **Put him away** completed as Loomis;
- the full-car Resident escape;
- two max-level Civilians;
- Prestige;
- one passive challenge book at 100%;
- any remaining Michael cumulative counters.

That order keeps the low-percentage conditions visible without turning every
ordinary match into a single-purpose achievement attempt.
