<h2 id="challenge-types">
  Which Halloween: The Game Challenge Type Are You Actually Working On?
</h2>

A challenge that refuses to move is not automatically broken. The first thing I
check is whether the action I am doing belongs to the challenge type currently
on screen.

**Story Challenges** belong to Michael's single-player campaign. There are six in
the Prologue and six in each of Chapters 1 through 5, for **36 Story Challenges**
in total. Their requirements are usually narrow: perform a specific execution,
keep a named target alive long enough to stalk them, avoid detection, use a
particular weapon, or complete an action before the chapter ends.

**Progressive Challenges** are longer multiplayer chains tied to Michael,
Civilians, weapons, or specific collections. Progress does not come from one
universal pool. A valid action may add nothing when I am using the wrong
character, the wrong weapon, or a different stage of the same collection.

**Passive Challenges** build in the background through normal play. They became
especially confusing around Prestige because the menu could make a challenge
look locked even when the underlying progress had not actually stopped.

XP tracks, Perk Points, perk rolls, card upgrades, and loadout decisions are
separate from figuring out why a challenge does or does not count. For those
systems, use the <Link href="/halloween-the-game/progression-perks">progression and perks guide</Link>.


<h2 id="unlock-scythe">
  How Do You Unlock the Scythe in Halloween: The Game?
</h2>

The Scythe has two separate uses: Michael can unlock it as a Starting Weapon, while a Scythe can also be found and picked up during a match on Tower Farm. Finding the weapon on the map does not mean its starting-weapon unlock has been completed.

To unlock the Scythe without buying a Michael skin:

1. Open **Progressive Challenges** and navigate to **Collection 3/3**, where the final reward card shows the Scythe.
2. If the reward is locked, return to the earlier collections and find the next available challenge.
3. Complete the active requirement, claim its reward when available, and continue through the following stages until the Scythe unlocks.

Cards marked **Locked — Complete Previous Challenge** cannot be completed out of order. Check the requirement on each newly available card before starting another match.

The paid alternative is the **Halloween 5: The Revenge of Michael Myers (1989)** skin, which unlocks the Scythe immediately as a Starting Weapon. The separate Halloween (2018) skin does not provide that same stated unlock.

Buying the skin is unnecessary if you can complete the free challenge route and are willing to work through its requirements. It makes sense only when you specifically want that Michael appearance or immediate access to the weapon.

The Scythe can also be discovered during matches on <Link href="/halloween-the-game/tower-farm-map-guide">Tower Farm</Link>. That gives you a way to encounter the weapon without treating a map pickup as permanent starting-weapon ownership.

<h3 id="scythe-progressive-challenges-locked">
  Why Are My Scythe Progressive Challenges Locked?
</h3>

Seeing the Scythe reward marked **Locked** in Collection 3/3 is normal while earlier challenges remain incomplete. The message **Complete Previous Challenge** means you need to advance through the preceding cards, not that the Scythe reward itself is broken.

Move back through the collections and look for an available challenge with a visible objective and progress counter. Complete that requirement before trying the next locked card.

If every preceding card remains inaccessible, or an available challenge does not record a correctly performed action, check the selected collection and requirement before repeating the match. A locked final reward alone is not evidence of a tracking failure.

The Halloween 5 (1989) Michael skin offers immediate access to the Scythe Starting Weapon without completing the free challenge sequence.

<h2 id="progressive-goals-1-2-0">
  Which Progressive Challenge Requirements Changed in Patch 1.2.0?
</h2>

Several Progressive Challenges became shorter in the October 27 update. Before grinding a familiar objective, check the current requirement rather than using a target from an older challenge card.

Some of the most useful current requirements are:

- **1978 Michael — Lethal Efficiency: The Sequel:** Execute **40** victims stalked to Tier 2, reduced from 50.
- **Alexis — Craftswoman:** Successfully blind, disorient, or stun Michael **20** times, reduced from 30.
- **Laurie — Final Girl:** Survive the night alive **10** times, reduced from 15.
- **Laurie — Watching the Kids:** Escort **30** Residents to an escape, reduced from 45.
- **Bob — Prank Caller:** Place **10** unconvincing calls to dispatch.
- **Jennifer — Wallflower:** Restore your own health with a health spray **10** times.
- **Clown Michael — Kitchen Knife Prelude:** Execute all four Civilians within a single match **5** times.
- **Clown Michael — Bedroom Window Gaze:** Fully stalk **50** Residents inside a structure while you remain outside.
- **Clown Michael — Distant Terror:** Kill **35** terrified Civilians with a thrown weapon.
- **Clown Michael — Slow Panic:** Cause **25** terrified victims to bleed to death from wounds you inflicted.

Do not mix up similar-sounding objectives. Distant Terror requires a thrown-weapon kill, whereas Slow Panic requires the victim to die from bleeding. Completing the wrong type of kill will not advance the other challenge.

Likewise, Clown Michael's objectives belong to that Michael collection. Their reduced requirements do not establish the steps for unlocking the Scythe.

<h3 id="reduced-progress-not-completing">
  What If Your Old Progress Already Exceeds the New Goal?
</h3>

A saved counter can exceed a newly reduced requirement without immediately awarding completion.

For example, if your account shows 45 qualifying Tier 2 Stalk executions for Lethal Efficiency: The Sequel, the current goal of 40 has already been exceeded.

Perform one more qualifying Tier 2 Stalk execution, then reopen the challenge card and check whether completion has registered. Earlier challenge adjustments used this additional-progress trigger to re-evaluate saved counters.

If another valid action still does not advance or complete the objective, distinguish that tracking failure from using the wrong character, wrong weapon, or wrong challenge stage.

<h2 id="smash-50-pumpkins">
  How Do You Complete the 50 Pumpkins Challenge?
</h2>

**Smash 50 pumpkins with weapons before November 3, 2026, at 10 AM PT** to unlock the **Jack Ghoul Goggles** Civilian cosmetic.

The important requirement is the action that counts: pumpkins must be smashed with weapons. Merely finding the decorations, running into them, or assuming a vehicle collision counts is not a reliable way to complete the objective.

Tower Farm has a recognizable pumpkin patch in its harvest festival area. Use the festival stalls, picnic area, and hayride as landmarks when looking for a group of pumpkins instead of searching every building individually.

For the approach to the harvest festival and its surrounding roads, use the <Link href="/halloween-the-game/tower-farm-map-guide">Tower Farm map guide</Link>.

After destroying several pumpkins, check the weekly challenge counter. Confirm that the weapon hits are being recorded before committing several matches to finishing the remaining count.

The reward is time-limited. Finish the challenge before the deadline and check that the Jack Ghoul Goggles reward has been unlocked.

<h2 id="all-story-challenges">
  All 36 Story Challenges in Halloween: The Game
</h2>

Every Story stage has six optional challenges. Finishing the chapter does not
automatically finish those six conditions, and several challenges can disappear
from the run once a target dies, a scene advances, or an escape is triggered.

I therefore decide which requirements can safely share one attempt before moving
too far into the chapter. Named environmental executions usually combine well
with general kill objectives, while stealth and no-detection requirements are
better separated from aggressive cleanup runs.

### Prologue — Escape From Smith's Grove

1. **Terrify an Orderly and cause him to flee.**
2. **Grab and execute 3 Orderlies with bare hands.**
3. **Execute an Orderly with the Telephone Pole.**
4. **Throw an Orderly to his death.**
5. **Complete the chapter without alerting any Orderlies.**
6. **Kill or drive away every required Orderly before escaping.**

Challenge 5 deserves a clean stealth attempt. Mixing it with the execution and
terror objectives adds unnecessary chances to trigger an alert before the chapter
is finished.

Challenge 6 has the opposite rhythm. Before leaving, I sweep back through the
earlier area and make sure no required Orderly has been skipped. The <Link href="/halloween-the-game/prologue-challenges">Prologue Challenges route</Link> covers the easy-to-miss Orderly, the clean stealth attempt, and the separate combat cleanup route.

### Chapter 1 — The Road to Haddonfield

1. **Kill everyone inside the Rabbit in Red.**
2. **Use Shove to stumble a Biker off balance.**
3. **Kill any target with the Broken Pool Cue.**
4. **Execute a Biker with a Context Execution.**
5. **Kill any target with a thrown Meat Cleaver.**
6. **Kill 3 Residents during the chapter.**

Most wasted Chapter 1 attempts come from leaving the Rabbit in Red before the
bar-specific work is finished. I handle the people, improvised weapons, and
execution opportunities inside the building before moving outward for the broader
Resident objective.

For Challenge 1, I check the rooms and side areas before leaving rather than
chasing a scripted character who has already exited the scene.

The Broken Pool Cue and Meat Cleaver also need different handling. The pool cue
challenge wants the target killed with that weapon, while the Meat Cleaver
condition specifically requires a **throw**. Using the cleaver as an ordinary
melee weapon can consume the opportunity without satisfying the challenge.

### Chapter 2 — Main Street

1. **Kill Tanya using the cash register Context Execution.**
2. **Kill Rachel with an environmental execution.**
3. **Grab and execute Slab while he is relieving himself.**
4. **Execute Eric using the hardware store shelf interaction.**
5. **Kill Rachel's 3 friends.**
6. **Complete the chapter without alerting anyone.**

Main Street contains several challenge windows that close once the story moves
forward. Slab is the clearest example: reaching him later is not the same as
catching him during the required scene.

Challenge 6 is cleaner as its own run. Once I am trying to manipulate several
named execution scenes in the same attempt, the no-alert condition becomes much
harder to protect.

For the order of Tanya, Rachel, Slab, Eric, the cemetery handoff, and the points
where moving too early can ruin an attempt, use the <Link href="/halloween-the-game/main-street-walkthrough">Main Street walkthrough</Link> to follow the sequence without losing a challenge opportunity.

1. **Use the Campfire Kill to execute Jennifer.**
2. **Use the Weight Bench Kill to execute Thomas.**

The first three challenges are the ones I protect most carefully. Jennifer,

execution becomes available. Killing the correct person with the wrong method

Before grabbing one of those targets, I build the required Stalk and confirm that
the correct execution prompt is actually available. When the prompt is missing,
I stop there instead of killing the target another way and discovering the
mistake at the results screen.

Challenge 5 is easier to plan from the start. Once I find a Wood-Axe, I keep it
until three valid kills are finished and avoid spending those targets if one of
them is still needed for a named execution.

Challenge 6 then works naturally as cleanup. After the named targets and Wood-Axe
kills are complete, I sweep the remaining neighborhood before committing to the

<h2 id="chapter-4">
  Chapter 4 — The Babysitters
</h2>

1. **Strangle Annie in the car.**
2. **Pin Bob to the kitchen wall.**
3. **Kill Lynda with the telephone cord while wearing the Sheet.**
4. **Perform a Bloodthirsty Execution.**
5. **Fully Stalk Bob and Lynda while they are making out.**
6. **Complete the chapter while matching the film sequence.**

This chapter punishes correct actions performed too early. Bob and Lynda need to
survive long enough to reach the scene required by Challenge 5, while the final
challenge depends on allowing the wider Wallace House sequence to develop in the
right order.

That means an easy kill is not always useful. Removing Bob or Lynda as soon as
they become reachable can erase the later challenge window entirely.

three Resident kills and police-car destruction are already complete.

the chapter. The target needs to be police **inside the Wallace's house**.

For Challenge 5, I treat a Loomis detection as a failed attempt. Breaking line of

<h2 id="progressive-challenges">
  Why Is My Progressive Challenge Counter Not Increasing?
</h2>

Check the requirement on the active card, then reproduce the exact action once.

A counter that stays unchanged can mean the wrong character, weapon, collection stage, or action was used. A separate problem occurs when the counter is already above a reduced goal but the completion reward has not triggered.

For weapon challenges, an execution or attack performed with the wrong weapon does not satisfy a weapon-specific requirement. For communication objectives, the required event may be restoring a phone, CB radio, or phone box after Michael has destroyed it. Repairing unrelated equipment does not replace that interaction.

If the counter already exceeds a newly reduced requirement, try one additional qualifying action and check again.

A collection that cannot be opened at all is different from a visible counter that fails to increase. The Scythe's locked collection should not be treated as proof that an unrelated challenge needs more repetitions.


<h2 id="passive-after-prestige">
  Why Do Passive Challenges Look Locked After Prestige?
</h2>

A locked-looking Passive Challenge does not necessarily mean its saved progress has disappeared. Complete one action that satisfies the challenge's condition, then check whether its counter increases.

If progress still appears blocked after Prestiging, check the <Link href="/halloween-the-game/prestige-guide">Prestige guide</Link> for the changes to Passive Challenge tracking and the next checks to make.

<h2 id="private-match">
  Should You Use Private Match to Grind Challenges?
</h2>

Private Match is strongest when I need to repeat an interaction that I can
actually control. Weapon objectives, environmental actions, and some Michael
requirements become much easier to test when random teammates are not changing
the setup.

It does not remove the challenge condition itself. An objective that needs
Michael to destroy a particular object still needs Michael to perform that
destruction. A rescue condition still needs the rescue event. Standing near the
right object or reproducing only half of the sequence is not enough.


An earlier update addressed an issue that prevented Private Match hosts from unlocking certain trophies or challenges. Private Matches are useful for controlled attempts, but check the counter after one qualifying action before repeating the setup.


For repetitive objectives, test one qualifying action and confirm the counter increases before repeating the setup.

The 50 Pumpkins challenge can progress in Private Matches. On Tower Farm, head to the pumpkin patch near the harvest festival and destroy pumpkins with a weapon.

Check the challenge counter after destroying the first few pumpkins. Completing the required 50 pumpkins in a Private Match has successfully unlocked the Jack Ghoul Goggles, but do not assume every match configuration will track correctly. If the counter does not increase, stop and check the active challenge before continuing.

<h2 id="why-it-did-not-count">
  Why Didn't My Challenge Count?
</h2>

Before replaying an entire chapter or committing another hour to multiplayer, I
check the failure point in a fixed order.

**Wrong mode or wrong selection:** Story Challenges belong to Story Mode.
Progressive Challenges can depend on a particular Civilian, Michael setup,
weapon, or collection stage.

**The named action did not finish the target:** standing beside the correct object
does not turn an ordinary kill into a Context Execution. A thrown-weapon
requirement is not satisfied by using that same weapon for a melee hit.

**The opportunity already passed:** Slab can leave the required scene, a Chapter 3
target can die before the environmental execution is ready, and Chapter 5 can end
before the police car is destroyed.

**The Stalk setup is incomplete:** when an environmental execution prompt does not
appear, I check the target's Stalk state before assuming the prop itself is wrong.

**A reduced challenge goal has not triggered completion:** If your saved progress already exceeds the current requirement after a patch, perform one additional qualifying action and check the reward again.

A Story chapter that reaches the ending but fails to save properly is a different
problem from failing an individual challenge condition. Repeating the same kill
with a different weapon will not repair a chapter-state or results-screen issue.
