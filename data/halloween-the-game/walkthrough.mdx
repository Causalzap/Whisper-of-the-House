<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
**Follow the required HUD objective and move on as soon as the chapter gives you
a new destination or escape route.**

Story mode has six stages: the Smith's Grove Prologue followed by five
chapters. For a first clear, finish the required target chain in each stage and
leave optional challenges, extra kills, collectibles, and alternate exits for
chapter replay.

<h2 id="story-order">
  Six stages, with the HUD deciding when each one is finished
</h2>

The campaign runs in this order:

1. **Prologue — Escape From Smith's Grove**
2. **Chapter 1 — The Road To Haddonfield**
3. **Chapter 2 — Main Street**
4. **Chapter 3 — Returning Home**
5. **Chapter 4 — The Babysitters**
6. **Chapter 5 — Into The Shadows**

The easiest way to make story mode longer than it needs to be is to treat
every nearby person, skull icon, collectible, or challenge condition as part of
the main route.

They are not.

I use the required HUD objective as the handoff between one piece of a chapter
and the next. If the objective has changed, I change direction with it.

That matters almost immediately at Smith's Grove.

<h2 id="prologue">
  Prologue — once the gate objective appears, start leaving Smith's Grove
</h2>

The Prologue introduces stalking, grabbing, executions, and the way orderlies
react when Michael gets too close or leaves a body where it can be discovered.

For the first few minutes, following the obvious route works.

The point where I would stop treating the grounds as a combat area is when the
objective becomes:

**Find a way to lower the gates.**

    The gate objective is the cue to work toward the exit controls instead of

  </figcaption>

This is an easy place to lose several minutes because there can still be
orderlies behind you and activity elsewhere on the grounds.

The remaining head count is not the story objective.

Move through the accessible route until you can interfere with the facility
power and open the way out. Once the escape path is active, use it.

A chapter replay is a much better place to worry about the optional objectives.
The
<Link href="/halloween-the-game/prologue-challenges">
  {" "}six Prologue challenges
</Link>
reward a different style of run and include conditions that actively conflict
with a normal first clear.

For the story, the gate is what matters.

Leaving Smith's Grove moves Michael onto the road toward Haddonfield.

<h2 id="road-to-haddonfield">
  Chapter 1 — keep the Rabbit in Red sequence from turning into a map-wide chase
</h2>

The Road To Haddonfield opens around the **Rabbit in Red**.

Compared with the Prologue, this area gives NPCs much more room to scatter.
Someone can break away from the main group, another person can move around the
building, and Michael can easily spend time following a target who no longer
matters to the current story objective.

  </figcaption>

The useful check is simple:

**Did the HUD make that fleeing person your next required target?**

If yes, follow.

If the objective already points somewhere else, chasing someone through the
parking area is usually time spent on an optional kill.

The same applies inside the Rabbit in Red. There are enough people and
environmental opportunities here to make the building feel like it needs a
full sweep.

It does not.

Once the required sequence advances away from the bar, leave with it.

I would only search the rooms again on a replay where the goal is a missed
challenge or collectible. On a first completion, the next story marker is
worth more than another lap around the building.

Finish the required Road To Haddonfield sequence and the chapter carries
Michael closer to town.

<h2 id="main-street">
  Chapter 2 — get the mask, follow the named targets, then commit to the cemetery
</h2>

Main Street is where story mode starts putting several useful systems in front
of you at the same time.

The opening commercial area leads Michael to his mask.

<figure className="my-8">
  <Image
    src="/images/halloween-the-game/halloween-the-game-main-street-mask.webp"
    alt="Michael Myers obtaining his mask during the Main Street chapter"
    width={1600}
    height={900}
    className="rounded-xl"
  />
  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">
    Getting the mask is part of the opening Main Street sequence before the
    chapter spreads its targets across several locations.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

After that, **Killer Sense** and **Shape Jump** create a lot of tempting
detours.

Killer Sense can expose people and environmental execution points. A skull is
useful information, but it is not automatically the next story destination.

Shape Jump makes moving between spaces much faster, although lighting can
interfere with some places that initially look usable.

For a normal story clear, I give named special targets priority over nearby
execution markers.

That keeps Main Street moving through its intended sequence instead of turning
every building into a search for the next skull.

The chapter eventually shifts toward the cemetery and **Judith Myers'
gravestone**.

That change is the important one.

Once the grave objective becomes active:

1. go to the cemetery;
2. finish the required grave interaction;
3. take the required item;
4. return it to the car.

At that point, Main Street is effectively done.

There is little value in reopening shops because a challenge kill was left
behind after the story has already moved to the cemetery.

Players replaying the chapter for its specific objectives have a different
route. The
<Link href="/halloween-the-game/main-street-walkthrough">
  {" "}Main Street walkthrough
</Link>
owns those challenge setups and the places where interrupting an NPC too early
can break the attempt.

For the main story, cemetery first once the HUD sends you there.

<h2 id="returning-home">
  Chapter 3 — the Myers house comes before the wider neighborhood
</h2>

Returning Home opens with a much simpler priority than the number of visible
houses suggests.

Go to the **Myers house**.

The house triggers the childhood sequence, and the chapter temporarily stops
behaving like the more open sections around it.

  <Image
    src="/images/halloween-the-game/halloween-the-game-returning-home-childhood-flashback.webp"
    alt="Young Michael Myers entering the Myers house during the Returning Home flashback"
    width={1600}
    height={900}
    className="rounded-xl"
  />
  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">
    The Myers house starts the required childhood sequence before the chapter
    opens back into the surrounding neighborhood.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

The route inside the flashback is direct:

**go around to the back of the house → enter → move upstairs → put on the
mask → continue to Judith.**

There is no advantage to treating this part like the open neighborhood that
comes later.

Finish the house sequence and let the game return Michael to the present.

Only then does the chapter widen into a larger hunt.

That second half is where I would watch the HUD closely. Houses, residents,
weapons, and environmental interactions can make it feel as though the chapter
expects a clean sweep of the neighborhood.

It does not.

A required special target gets priority.

A nearby resident with no connection to the current objective can stay alive
for now.

Eventually the objective changes again and tells Michael to escape.

That is the point to leave.

If the escape instruction is already on screen, crossing back through two
houses because one resident was missed is optional cleanup, not story
progression.

<h2 id="babysitters">
  Chapter 4 — let the Wallace House sequence reach its next state before interfering
</h2>

The Babysitters changes the kind of mistake that wastes time.

Earlier chapters can punish wandering too far.

Chapter 4 punishes arriving at the right place **too early** and changing a
character state before the sequence is ready.

The **Wallace House** is the anchor.

  <Image
    src="/images/halloween-the-game/halloween-the-game-the-babysitters-wallace-house.webp"
    alt="Michael Myers approaching the Wallace House in The Babysitters"
    width={1600}
    height={900}
    className="rounded-xl"
  />
  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">
    The Wallace House carries the main Chapter 4 event chain. The nearby
    neighborhood gives Michael room to move, but the story keeps coming back to
    the characters tied to this house.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

Annie moves through her routine first.

Bob and Lynda need time to reach their own sequence.

Laurie belongs later.

That order matters more than how quickly Michael can physically reach any one
of them.

I found this chapter much easier once I stopped asking, “Who can I kill right
now?” and started checking, “Who did the objective just move to?”

If the required character is still moving into position, let that state
change happen.

Random aggression around the house can create a confusing result: Michael is
in the correct neighborhood, but the interaction you expected never appears
because the event chain was disturbed earlier.

Stay close enough to follow the required Wallace House sequence as the target
changes.

When Laurie becomes the active objective and the chapter turns into a chase,
follow her.

Going back to inspect an earlier house at that point gains nothing for the
story clear.

Continue through the Laurie sequence until Loomis arrives and the chapter
resolves.

The optional Chapter 4 challenge route demands much tighter timing around the
same characters. Those exact setups belong in
<Link href="/halloween-the-game/the-babysitters-walkthrough">
  {" "}The Babysitters walkthrough
</Link>
not in the main-story path.

<h2 id="into-the-shadows">
  Chapter 5 — rebuild Michael before trying to force a path through the police
</h2>

Into The Shadows begins with Michael in a much weaker state than the previous
chapter ended.

He has taken serious damage, and **Shape Jump is temporarily unavailable**.

The immediate job is to rebuild him to **Tier 3**.

At the same time, police introduce a different pressure: **detainment**.

A dense group of officers is a bad place to recover.

Use space.

Move away from the strongest police concentration and look for safer stalking
angles or isolated opportunities while rebuilding Michael.

Once Tier 3 returns, Shape Jump becomes available again and the map becomes
much easier to cross.

**Blackout** also becomes valuable when several officers are covering an area
with flashlights. Creating a dark window can be more useful than trying to
fight through every authority figure in front of you.

The chapter still contains optional objectives, but they stop being a sensible
priority once the game opens a real escape.

<h2 id="final-exit">
  When an exit becomes usable, take the ending you already have
</h2>

The final streets can support more than one way out of Haddonfield.

You do not need to discover every route on the same run.

Once the objective gives you a usable escape, the job has changed.

Michael is no longer searching for the next target.

He is leaving town.

Every extra confrontation after that point carries a cost. More officers can
raise detainment pressure, knock Michael down, or force another route through
an area you had already survived.

A nearby optional target is not automatically worth that risk.

Neither is an unopened branch that might lead to a different ending.

Take the available exit and finish the campaign.

If the goal later becomes seeing the other escape routes, the
<Link href="/halloween-the-game/endings">
  {" "}Halloween: The Game endings guide
</Link>
covers those branches without forcing the main walkthrough to duplicate their
commit points.

<h2 id="chapter-replays">
  After the first clear, replay the chapter that owns the thing you missed
</h2>

Story mode gives you chapter replay, which changes how much cleanup is worth
doing during the first run.

If a challenge is still locked in Smith's Grove, replay the Prologue.

If Main Street is incomplete, start Chapter 2 instead of running the whole
campaign again.

If the Wallace House sequence was broken while chasing optional conditions,
restart The Babysitters.

A different final escape belongs to Into The Shadows.

The same logic works for collectibles.

A pickup visible in a side room is useful, but it does not justify turning
every first visit into a full search while the required objective is already
moving somewhere else.

The first clear is fastest when each chapter gets one job:

**follow the active story target until the game tells you to leave.**

Once that route is familiar, the optional objectives become much cheaper to
clean up.
