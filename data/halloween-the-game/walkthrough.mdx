<h2 id="story-order">
  Halloween: The Game Story Mode Chapter Order
</h2>

Michael's Story has **six playable stages** if the Prologue is included:

1. **Prologue — Escape From Smith's Grove**
2. **Chapter 1 — The Road to Haddonfield**
3. **Chapter 2 — Main Street**
4. **Chapter 3 — Returning Home**
5. **Chapter 4 — The Babysitters**
6. **Chapter 5 — Into The Shadows**

For a first Story Mode clear, this is the progression spine I follow:

1. **Smith's Grove:** lower the gates, deal with the facility power, and leave
   once the escape route opens.
2. **The Road To Haddonfield:** follow the Rabbit in Red target chain and move
   on when the required objective leaves the area.
3. **Main Street:** get Michael's mask, follow the named targets, then finish
   the Judith Myers cemetery sequence.
4. **Returning Home:** complete the Myers house flashback first, then follow
   the required present-day targets until the escape objective appears.
5. **The Babysitters:** let the Wallace House character sequence develop in
   the required order, then follow Laurie until Loomis resolves the chapter.
6. **Into The Shadows:** rebuild Michael, recover his stronger movement
   options, deal with the increased police pressure, and take an available
   escape route.

The mistake that made my first runs longer was assuming everything nearby had
to be completed before the story would advance.

It does not.

A skull icon, an optional kill, a collectible, or a character standing close
to Michael can all be useful without being the next Story Mode objective. I
treat the HUD change as the handoff: once the game names a new target,
destination, or exit, that becomes the route.

<h2 id="prologue">
  Prologue — Escape From Smith's Grove
</h2>

**Main story route:** work through the opening Smith's Grove sequence → reach
the gate objective → deal with the facility power → open the way out → finish
the escape.

The Prologue introduces stalking, grabbing, executions, and the way orderlies
react when Michael gets too close or leaves a body where it can be discovered.

For the first part, following the obvious route works well enough.

The point where I stop treating Smith's Grove as a combat area is when the
objective becomes:

**Find a way to lower the gates.**

There can still be orderlies behind you and activity elsewhere on the grounds,
which makes it easy to assume the chapter expects a clean sweep.

The remaining head count is not the story objective.

Work toward the power and gate sequence instead. Once the way out is active,
take it rather than making another lap through Smith's Grove for optional
kills.

That separation matters even more on replay because several Prologue
challenges actively ask you to play the chapter differently from a normal
first clear.

  {" "}Prologue Challenge 5 walkthrough
</Link>
covers the ambulance wait, patrol cues, restart condition, and final approach
separately.

For Story Mode, the gate and escape route are what matter.

Leaving Smith's Grove moves Michael onto the road toward Haddonfield.

<h2 id="road-to-haddonfield">
  Chapter 1 — The Road To Haddonfield
</h2>

**Main story route:** reach the Rabbit in Red → follow the required biker and
target sequence → stay with the active objective as NPCs scatter → leave the
area once the story marker advances.

The **Rabbit in Red** is the main anchor for Chapter 1.

Compared with the Prologue, this area gives NPCs much more room to spread out.
Someone can break away from the main group, another person can move around the
building, and Michael can easily spend time following a target who no longer
matters to the current story step.

The check I use here is:

**Did the HUD make that fleeing person my next required target?**

If yes, follow.

If the objective already points somewhere else, chasing that NPC through the
parking area usually turns into optional cleanup.

The same applies inside the Rabbit in Red. There are enough people,
environmental interactions, and possible kills to make the building look as
though it needs a full sweep before Michael can continue.

It does not.

Follow the required target chain through the area, but stop treating the bar
as unfinished once the objective advances away from it.

This is one of the chapters where staying faithful to the active target saves
more time than trying to predict which nearby NPC the story might use next.

Once the required Road To Haddonfield sequence resolves, move on instead of
searching the rooms again for a missed challenge or collectible.

Those are much cheaper to clean up on chapter replay.

<h2 id="main-street">
  Chapter 2 — Main Street: Get the Mask, Then Finish at the Cemetery
</h2>

**Main story route:** get Michael's mask → follow the named story targets →
move to Judith Myers' gravestone when the cemetery objective appears → finish
the grave interaction → take the required item back to the car.

Main Street puts several useful systems in front of Michael at once, which
makes optional detours look more important than they are.

The opening commercial area leads to Michael's mask.

After that, Killer Sense and Shape Jump make it easy to turn every visible
skull or nearby opportunity into a detour.

For a first clear, I give **named story targets** priority.

The decisive handoff comes when the chapter shifts toward the cemetery and
**Judith Myers' gravestone**. Once that objective appears, I stop reopening
shops or chasing optional execution opportunities and finish the cemetery
sequence.

That closes the Story Mode route through Main Street much more cleanly.

<h2 id="returning-home">
  Chapter 3 — Returning Home: Go to the Myers House First
</h2>

**Main story route:** Myers house → back entrance → upstairs → put on the mask
→ continue to Judith → return to the present → follow the required special
targets → leave when the escape objective appears.

Returning Home initially looks more open than its first required route
actually is.

The surrounding neighborhood can wait.

Go to the **Myers house** first.

The route inside the flashback is direct:

**go around to the back of the house → enter → move upstairs → put on the mask
→ continue to Judith.**

There is little reason to treat this section like the open neighborhood that
comes afterward.

Finish the house sequence and let the game return Michael to the present.

Only then does Returning Home widen into a larger hunt.

That second half is where the visible houses, residents, weapons, and
environmental interactions can make the chapter look as though it expects a
full neighborhood sweep.

I still prioritize required special targets.

A resident with no connection to the active objective does not need to become
a detour just because Michael can reach them.

Eventually the objective changes again and tells Michael to escape.

That is the signal to leave.

If the escape instruction is already active, crossing back through several
houses because somebody survived is optional cleanup rather than Story Mode
progression.

<h2 id="babysitters">
  Chapter 4 — The Babysitters: Do Not Interrupt the Wallace House Sequence Early
</h2>

**Main story route:** let Annie reach her required state → allow Bob and
Lynda's sequence to develop → follow Laurie when she becomes the active target
→ continue until Loomis resolves the chapter.

The Babysitters creates a different kind of progression trap.

Earlier stages mostly punish wandering too far from the current objective.

Chapter 4 can punish reaching the right character **too early**.

The **Wallace House** is the main anchor.

Annie belongs earlier in the sequence.

Bob and Lynda need time to reach their own state.

Laurie comes later.

I found the chapter much more reliable once I stopped asking who Michael could
reach immediately and started watching which character the objective had
actually moved to.

Stay close enough to follow the Wallace House sequence as it changes, but do
not interrupt a character simply because the game lets Michael reach them.

When Laurie becomes the active target, follow that chain until Loomis arrives
and the chapter resolves.

The exact character timing becomes much more important when replaying the
chapter for its optional conditions. Those routes belong in the

<h2 id="into-the-shadows">
  Chapter 5 — Into The Shadows: Rebuild Michael Before Forcing the Final Route
</h2>

**Main story route:** rebuild Michael to Tier 3 → recover Shape Jump → avoid
getting pinned down by concentrated police → use Blackout when flashlights
close off a route → follow the final objectives → take an available escape.

Into The Shadows starts with Michael in a much worse state than the previous
chapter ended.

He has taken serious damage, and **Shape Jump is temporarily unavailable**.

That changes the opening completely.

My first priority here is not clearing the police.

It is getting Michael functional again.

A dense group of officers is a bad place to recover, so I move away from the
strongest concentration and look for safer stalking angles or isolated
opportunities while rebuilding toward **Tier 3**.

Once Tier 3 returns, Shape Jump becomes available again and moving across the
map gets much easier.

Police also introduce **detainment**, which makes long fights in exposed
streets much less attractive than they were earlier in Story Mode.

When several flashlights are covering the route I want, **Blackout** is usually
more useful than trying to force my way directly through the group. Create the
dark window, reposition, and keep following the active objective.

The important transition comes when the chapter stops asking Michael to hunt
and gives him a usable way out of Haddonfield.

At that point, I leave.

There is no requirement to discover every possible escape on the same run.
Continuing to fight officers or search for another route only adds more chances
to be detained after the chapter is already ready to end.

If you specifically want to see the other ways Michael can leave Haddonfield,
use the
<Link href="/halloween-the-game/endings">
  {" "}Halloween: The Game endings guide
</Link>
for the different escape branches and their commit points.

<h2 id="chapter-replays">
  Use Chapter Replay for Challenges, Collectibles, and Alternate Exits
</h2>

Story Mode does not require you to turn the first clear into a completionist
run.

If a Prologue challenge is unfinished, replay Smith's Grove.

If a Chapter 2 objective is missing, return directly to Main Street.

If the Wallace House sequence needs a challenge-specific setup, replay The
Babysitters instead of running the campaign again.

If the goal is another final escape, return to Into The Shadows.

The same logic applies to collectibles. A pickup in a side room can be worth
coming back for without being worth a long detour while the active story
objective is already sending Michael somewhere else.

That is why I give each first visit one job:

**finish the required story chain and leave when the chapter tells you to
leave.**

Once the route is familiar, chapter replay is the better place to clean up
everything Story Mode did not require.
