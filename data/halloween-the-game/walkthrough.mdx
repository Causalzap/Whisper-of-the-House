<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
**Follow the required HUD objective through the Prologue and five chapters.**

Escape Smith's Grove, finish the Rabbit in Red sequence on the Road to
Haddonfield, get Michael's mask and complete the Main Street objectives, return
to the Myers house, follow the Wallace House sequence in The Babysitters, then
survive the police search in Into the Shadows until an exit becomes available.

For a first completion, leave optional chapter challenges for replays.

<h2 id="story-order">
  Story order from Smith's Grove to Into the Shadows
</h2>

The campaign runs in this order:

1. **Prologue — Escape From Smith's Grove**
2. **Chapter 1 — The Road To Haddonfield**
3. **Chapter 2 — Main Street**
4. **Chapter 3 — Returning Home**
5. **Chapter 4 — The Babysitters**
6. **Chapter 5 — Into The Shadows**

The important distinction is between the **required objective** and the
chapter's challenge list.

They often occupy the same space, but they do not ask you to play the same way.

The Prologue makes that clear immediately. One optional challenge wants an
orderly terrified badly enough to flee, while another rewards finishing without
alerting orderlies. Those conditions are useful on separate challenge attempts;
they are poor directions for navigating the story itself.

For the route below, the HUD objective is the anchor. When a chapter opens up,
I check that before choosing which person, house, or interaction to chase.

<h2 id="prologue">
  Prologue — get the Smith's Grove gates open
</h2>

Michael begins at **Smith's Grove Sanitarium** under heavy sedation.

The opening introduces the basic actions you need for the rest of story mode:
stalking, grabbing, executions, and the way nearby staff react once somebody
spots Michael or discovers what he has done.

At first, keep advancing through the grounds and deal with anyone directly
blocking the route.

The first real navigation stop arrives when the objective changes to:

**Find a way to lower the gates.**

This is where I lost time on my first pass.

There can still be staff and police moving around the grounds, so it is easy to
assume the chapter wants the area cleared before the gate will open.

It does not.

Move through the remaining accessible section until you can interfere with the
facility power and open the gate route. Once the path out is active, take it.

There is no reason to sweep the Sanitarium again because one orderly is still
somewhere behind you unless you are specifically chasing the challenge for
clearing or driving away everyone.

The escape sequence then carries Michael out of Smith's Grove and into the road
sequence leading toward Haddonfield.

<h2 id="road-to-haddonfield">
  Chapter 1 — finish the Rabbit in Red sequence and keep moving
</h2>

The Road To Haddonfield opens around the **Rabbit in Red**.

Unlike Smith's Grove, this area gives several NPCs room to scatter. People can
fight, run, separate from the group, or move toward vehicles, which makes the
chapter feel less linear than it actually is.

The chapter challenge list encourages very specific kills, including particular
weapons and contextual executions.

Ignore that list while deciding where to go next.

If one biker bolts away from the main scene, check whether the HUD has made that
person your required target before starting a long chase. The same area contains
enough optional victims to pull Michael well away from the actual sequence.

When the objective advances away from the Rabbit in Red, follow it immediately.

I stop searching the building and parking area at that point. Chapter 1 has
already given the story what it needs, and remaining challenge targets can be
cleaned up from a chapter replay.

The chapter closes by continuing Michael's journey toward Haddonfield.

<h2 id="main-street">
  Chapter 2 — get the mask, follow the special targets, then finish at the cemetery
</h2>

Main Street is the first chapter where several mechanics, buildings, and special
targets are active at the same time.

The opening commercial area gives Michael access to the mask and expands the
toolkit you will use through the rest of the campaign.

<figure className="my-8">
  <Image
    src="/images/halloween-the-game/halloween-the-game-main-street-mask.webp"
    alt="Michael Myers obtaining his mask during the Main Street chapter"

  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">

    Street expands into several target locations.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

Two mechanics start doing much more work here:

**Killer Sense** helps locate people and highlights environmental execution
opportunities.

**Shape Jump** helps Michael move through otherwise awkward routes.

If Shape Jump refuses to work at a door that appears usable, lighting can be the
reason. The
<Link href="/halloween-the-game/main-street-walkthrough">
  {" "}Main Street walkthrough
</Link>
covers that interaction along with the chapter's six specific challenges.

For the story route, the bigger trap is Killer Sense itself.

A skull icon means an environmental kill is available nearby. It does **not**
mean the story requires that execution.

Follow the current special target instead of trying every highlighted prop.

Main Street moves through its named targets before eventually sending Michael
toward the cemetery and **Judith Myers' gravestone**.

Once the grave sequence is active, finish it and return the required item to the
car.

That is the chapter endpoint.

Do not reopen stores looking for missed challenge kills after the cemetery
objective has already advanced.

<h2 id="returning-home">
  Chapter 3 — go to the Myers house before hunting the neighborhood
</h2>

Returning Home starts with one destination that matters more than anything else
visible around you:

**the Myers house.**

Go there first.

The house triggers a childhood flashback, and for a while the game stops
behaving like the open Haddonfield chapters around it.

  <Image
    src="/images/halloween-the-game/halloween-the-game-returning-home-childhood-flashback.webp"
    alt="Young Michael Myers entering the Myers house during the Returning Home flashback"
    width={1600}
    height={900}
    className="rounded-xl"
  />
  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">
    The Myers-house flashback is a required story sequence. Follow it before
    worrying about the residents scattered around the neighborhood.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

During the flashback, take the route the objectives give you:

**go to the back of the house → enter → move upstairs → put on the mask →
continue to Judith.**

Trying to explore the neighborhood logic from outside the flashback does not
help here. Finish the house sequence and let the chapter return Michael to the
present.

Afterward, the neighborhood opens into a broader hunt.

This is the point where Returning Home can become much longer than necessary.

Its optional challenges include specific environmental kills, multiple weapon
conditions, and clearing additional residents spread among houses and outdoor
areas.

If the HUD gives you a special target, pursue that person.

If somebody nearby is only useful for a challenge you are not currently
attempting, leave them alone and preserve the route.

The chapter eventually tells Michael to escape.

Take that cue literally. Once the escape objective is up, backtracking through
houses for one more optional resident only extends the run.

<h2 id="babysitters">
  Chapter 4 — stay with the Wallace House event chain
</h2>

The Babysitters is less about covering ground and more about allowing its
characters to reach the right states.

Your first meaningful destination is the **Wallace House**.

<figure className="my-8">
  <Image
    src="/images/halloween-the-game/halloween-the-game-the-babysitters-wallace-house.webp"
    alt="Michael Myers approaching the Wallace House in The Babysitters"
    width={1600}
    height={900}
    className="rounded-xl"
  />
  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">
    Wallace House anchors Chapter 4. Nearby houses are explorable, but the story
    advances through the Annie, Bob, Lynda, and Laurie sequence centered here.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

This chapter punishes impatience more than the earlier neighborhood sections.

Annie has to move through her routine before the car sequence becomes useful.

Bob and Lynda need time to reach their scene.

The sheet and telephone interactions come later.

Laurie's arrival belongs near the end of that chain.

Killing or interrupting people simply because Michael can reach them may leave
you wondering why the expected interaction has not appeared yet.

For a normal completion, stay close enough to the Wallace House sequence to
follow the next required target as it changes.

If you are trying to complete the movie-specific challenge conditions — Annie's
car kill, Bob's kitchen wall condition, stalking Bob and Lynda, the sheet, or
Lynda's telephone kill — use the
<Link href="/halloween-the-game/the-babysitters-walkthrough">
  {" "}The Babysitters walkthrough
</Link>
instead. Those depend on the exact event order rather than the broad story path.

Near the end, Laurie enters the sequence and the objective changes into a chase.

Once the game tells you to stop Laurie before she calls for help, stay on her.

Going back to earlier houses at this point gains nothing for the current story
run.

Continue until Loomis arrives and Chapter 4 resolves.

<h2 id="into-the-shadows">
  Chapter 5 — rebuild Michael before crossing police-heavy areas
</h2>

Into The Shadows begins with Michael in a worse position than he was at the end
of The Babysitters.

He has taken serious damage.

**Shape Jump is temporarily unavailable**, and the game requires Michael to
reach **Tier 3** before that mobility returns.

At the same time, police now create a new failure condition: detainment.

The opening therefore rewards space more than aggression.

A cluster of officers is a poor place to rebuild Michael.

Move away from the strongest police concentration, find safer stalking angles,
and take isolated opportunities until Tier 3 restores the mobility you lost at
the start.

This is also where Blackout becomes useful. If multiple officers are relying on
flashlights, disabling their lights can create enough room to move through a
dangerous section rather than fighting the entire group.

Chapter 5 contains several optional challenges involving officers, residents,
Loomis, Blackout, and a police car.

Those are separate from the job that finishes the campaign.

Once an exit objective appears, your priorities have changed.

<h2 id="final-exit">
  When an exit opens, stop treating Chapter 5 as a hunt
</h2>

The finale can send Michael out of Haddonfield through more than one route.

The story walkthrough does not require you to search for all of them during the
same run.

The moment a usable exit becomes active, the chapter has effectively moved from
**surviving the search** to **escaping the town**.

Follow that exit.

This matters because the final streets can still contain tempting optional
objectives. A police car may remain intact. An officer you need for a challenge
may still be nearby. You may be one action short of another reward.

Those targets become expensive once a valid ending route is already available.

Every extra encounter gives police another chance to build detainment pressure
or knock Michael down before the story is finished.

Take the exit you have.

If you want another final route later, replay **Into The Shadows** directly.

</Link>
covers the railroad, Cornfield, and Sewer routes separately, including where
each branch commits.

<h2 id="chapter-replays">
  Use chapter replays for the objectives you actually missed
</h2>

After the first completion, the campaign becomes much easier to clean up because
you no longer need to treat it as one continuous run.

Missed something on Main Street?

Restart Main Street.

Broke the event order in The Babysitters?

Replay Chapter 4.

Want a different final escape?

Go back to Into The Shadows.

Returning to the Prologue only makes sense when the unfinished objective is
actually there.

The same principle applies to collectibles. One visible pickup is not enough
reason to turn every first visit into a full neighborhood search. Once the
required routes are familiar, checking side rooms and optional areas costs far
less time.

The story already gives you chapter selection.

Use it.

<h2 id="route-check">
  If you feel lost, check what changed on the HUD
</h2>

Most story-mode stalls happen after the game has already advanced the objective
but the player is still acting on the previous one.

At Smith's Grove, that means continuing to hunt after the gate objective has
appeared.

On Main Street, it means chasing environmental kills after the story has moved
toward the cemetery.

In Returning Home, it means clearing houses after the escape instruction is
already active.

In The Babysitters, it means leaving the Wallace House event chain while a
scripted character is moving into the next state.

And in Into The Shadows, it means continuing to fight police after an exit has
opened.

When the route suddenly feels vague, I check the required objective before
crossing another street or entering another house.

If the HUD changed, the chapter probably changed with it.
