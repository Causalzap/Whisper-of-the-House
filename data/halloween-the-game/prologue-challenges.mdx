**There are six Prologue challenges in Halloween: The Game, and the easiest
way to clear all six is to split them across two Smith's Grove runs.**

Use one clean attempt for Challenge 5. On the combat run, let one orderly flee,

orderly to his death, then **kill or drive away all 12 orderlies** before you
escape.

Finish Smith's Grove after each successful attempt or the completed challenge

<h2 id="best-run-order">
  Best Route to Complete All 6 Prologue Challenges
</h2>

The awkward part about the Smith's Grove challenge list is that it rewards two
opposite ways of playing.

Five challenges tolerate — and often require — direct contact with the
orderlies.

Challenge 5 wants the opposite: finish Smith's Grove without alerting any of
them.

Trying to combine both styles into one attempt creates more resets than it
saves.

I clear the six challenges like this:

- **Clean run:** Challenge 5.
- **Combat run:** Challenges 1, 2, 3, 4, and 6.

On the combat run, I handle the short conditions while steadily working toward
Challenge 6 instead of treating each challenge as a separate playthrough.

A good order is:

1. **Challenge 1:** make one orderly flee.
2. **Challenge 2:** execute three orderlies with bare hands.
3. **Challenge 3:** save an orderly for the telephone-pole execution.
4. **Challenge 4:** use the nearby route to throw another orderly to his death.
5. **Challenge 6:** account for all 12 orderlies before leaving Smith's Grove.

Challenge 1 fits especially well into Challenge 6.

An orderly who successfully flees has still been removed from the active
Smith's Grove group, so the 12-orderly run does not require every target to die
by Michael's hand.

If you only want to finish the Prologue and continue Michael's Story, use the <Link href="/halloween-the-game/walkthrough">complete Story Mode walkthrough</Link>. The challenge route deliberately spends more time around orderlies that the normal Smith's Grove escape does not require.

<h2 id="challenges-1-to-4">
  Challenges 1–4 — Clear the Short Objectives During the Combat Run
</h2>

Challenges 1–4 become much simpler once you stop treating them as four separate
runs.

The main mistake is escaping as soon as the normal gate route becomes
available.

For a Story Mode clear, leaving at that point makes sense.

For the challenge run, stay inside Smith's Grove until the required
interactions are finished and enough orderlies have been accounted for toward
Challenge 6.

<h3 id="challenge-1">
  Challenge 1 — Terrify an Orderly and Make Him Flee
</h3>

You do not need a special location for Challenge 1.

Grab an orderly and let the encounter break in his favor instead of finishing
him.

Once he gets loose, give him room to run.

Do not immediately chase him down.

The result you care about is the game registering that the orderly has escaped.
That can take a little longer than expected because he needs to get far enough
away before the flee actually counts.

This is also why I prefer doing Challenge 1 during the Challenge 6 attempt.

The fleeing orderly can still count toward clearing Smith's Grove, so the
challenge does not necessarily cost you progress on the 12-orderly sweep.

<h3 id="challenge-2">
  Challenge 2 — Execute Three Orderlies With Bare Hands
</h3>

This is the easiest Prologue challenge to overcomplicate.

Grab an orderly and use the normal bare-hand execution instead of carrying him
toward an environmental interaction.

Repeat that until the third bare-hand execution registers.

I do these early.

Later sections of Smith's Grove contain the useful environmental setups for
Challenges 3 and 4.

If too many nearby orderlies have already been spent on ordinary executions,
you can reach the correct environmental location with nobody left to use on
it.

Three is the number that matters.

Once the third bare-hand execution counts, preserve the next convenient
orderlies for the two environmental challenges.

<h3 id="challenge-3">
  Challenge 3 — Execute an Orderly With the Telephone Pole
</h3>

The telephone pole is near the **power box used late in the Smith's Grove
escape route**.

When you reach the power-box area, look to the right before continuing directly
through the normal escape objective.

The important resource here is the orderly, not the pole.

Finding the interaction after clearing every nearby NPC does not help.

Keep one orderly alive as you approach the power box, grab him, move him into
the telephone-pole interaction zone, and use the environmental execution.

Once the kill registers, I move directly toward Challenge 4.

Its useful setup is close enough that there is little reason to return toward
the opening area first.

<h3 id="challenge-4">
  Challenge 4 — Throw an Orderly to His Death
</h3>

From the telephone-pole area, continue onto the nearby path and take the right.

There is an orderly farther along who can be seen shining a flashlight toward a
Smith's Grove patient.

Approach from behind, grab him, and carry him a short distance down the road
until the environmental throw interaction appears.

Challenge 4 is much faster when it immediately follows Challenge 3.

If you leave the power-box side of Smith's Grove and start exploring another
branch, you are adding distance without improving the setup.

Telephone pole first, nearby throw second, then return to the wider orderly
sweep.

<h2 id="challenge-5">
  Challenge 5 — Escape Smith's Grove Without Alerting Any Orderlies
</h2>

Challenge 5 deserves its own run.

The habits that make Challenges 1–4 and 6 efficient are exactly what ruin this
one.

The opening patrols need to move before Michael crosses their space, and there
are stretches where walking is safer than sprinting.

There is also a useful reset signal.

If a key orderly is stuck instead of continuing the patrol the route depends
on, I restart rather than spend several more minutes trying to repair the run.

For the exact stealth path, use the <Link href="/halloween-the-game/prologue-challenge-5">Prologue Challenge 5 no-alert route</Link>. It covers the patrol timing, ambulance wait, safe movement windows, flashlight cues, when sprinting becomes safe, and the point where I restart.

For the all-six plan here, the important decision is simple:

<h2 id="challenge-6">
  Challenge 6 — Kill or Drive Away Every Orderly Before You Escape
</h2>

Challenge 6 asks you to **kill or drive away every orderly before escaping
Smith's Grove**.

There are **12 orderlies to account for**, but they do not all need to be
executed by Michael.

An orderly who is driven away and successfully escapes can still count.

That is why Challenge 1 fits naturally into this same run.

I use Smith's Grove as a loop instead of chasing every NPC the moment I see
him.

Start around the opening stairs.

The first two orderlies can be handled once their movement separates them.

After that, move back down through the route instead of waiting around the
ambulance area for more NPCs to arrive.

Take the next orderly on the lower section, then continue along the path the
mission naturally uses.

When a moving group appears, I avoid turning the attempt into a blind chase.

Let the route narrow their movement first.

The sweep continues through the lower ground and drainage section before
climbing toward the later part of Smith's Grove.

This is where keeping a running count becomes more reliable than memory.

If one orderly flees, include him in that count instead of assuming Challenge 6
has failed.

Past the power-box side of the route, work uphill and remove the remaining
accessible orderlies.

The last awkward target is easy to see but initially annoying to reach.

One orderly can remain near the area where the mission began, with a gate
between him and Michael.

Michael cannot simply use that gate as a shortcut.

Continue along the accessible route, take the left path downhill, loop back
toward the opening section of Smith's Grove, and approach him from the other
side.

Once all **12 orderlies** have been accounted for and Challenge 6 registers,
stop sweeping the grounds.

At that point, the useful move is to finish Smith's Grove.

Searching for another NPC after the challenge has already completed only adds
risk to a run you have effectively finished.

<h2 id="finish-the-chapter">
  Finish Smith's Grove So Your Challenge Progress Saves
</h2>

A challenge checking off during the run is not the final step.

**You still need to complete the Prologue for that progress to save.**

That changes how I handle mistakes late in an attempt.

If Challenge 3 and Challenge 4 have already completed, I do not restart simply
because the rest of the run became messy.

Finish the chapter and bank those objectives.

The same applies to Challenge 6.

Once the 12-orderly requirement has registered, leave Smith's Grove and let the
Prologue resolve.

Restart when the challenge you are currently attempting has become impossible.

Do not restart after a challenge has already succeeded if finishing the chapter
will preserve the progress you just earned.
