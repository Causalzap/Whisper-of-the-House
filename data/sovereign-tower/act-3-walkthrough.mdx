<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
My Act 3 route is built around one rule:

**finish permanent kingdom progress before Arthur turns the Act into final
preparation.**

I split the Act into four stages:

1. **Finish permanent routes first.** Resolve counties, valuable Knight routes,
   and anything that changes the kingdom before filling the board with ordinary
   work.
2. **Keep the roster flexible.** Level **15** is the cap in my run, so I stop
   sending capped specialists away just for XP. I also delay routes like
   Victoria when an emergency needs those Knights more.
3. **Treat the 13-cycle warning as the closing clock.** Once it appears, I stop
   opening long optional chains and finish the county state and Golden Key route
   I actually want.
4. **Build the Arthur party from the real assignment screen.** My final
   confrontation showed **3–6 Damage Risk**, with **Max Strength**, **Max Wit**,
   high Agility, and useful Magic coverage.

A few late-game numbers change my decisions:

- the Moonvale dragon quest showed **5–7 Damage Risk**;
- Victoria's Sword gives **+5 STR, +4 AGI, +2 CHA, +1 MAG, -3 LCK**;
- the full-county Golden Key search showed **1–5 Damage Risk**;
- the Arthur confrontation showed **3–6 Damage Risk**.

The mistake I avoid is continuing to chase XP, Gold, and ordinary side quests
after permanent progression and final-party availability have become more
valuable.

<h2 id="act-3-route">
  The Act 3 route I use from King Slayer to Arthur
</h2>

Act 3 feels different from Act 2 because I am no longer trying to recover from a
single central crisis like the Tower murder.

Instead, several long-term decisions finally start paying off at the same time.

I may still have:

- counties that can become allies;
- Knight personal routes that are one or two steps from completion;
- powerful late recruits;
- political problems inside Britannia;
- Arthur and Angalore pressing from outside;
- the Golden Key sitting somewhere between legend and actual objective.

So I divide Act 3 into four phases.

### Phase 1: finish permanent expansion

I complete county routes that still change the political map.

This is where I value an alliance, recruit, unique mount, or major relic much
more than another ordinary Gold contract.

### Phase 2: stabilize late characters and threats

Victoria, Epicr, late Knight routes, and internal emergencies can all consume
good Knights.

I do not start every one just because the audience appears.

I check how close each route is to a permanent result.

### Phase 3: obey the real ending clock

Once Arthur and the Golden Key conflict turns into a visible countdown, I stop
treating Act 3 as open-ended.

The **13-cycle** window is preparation time, not permission to start thirteen
new stories.

### Phase 4: build the final state

I bring back the Knights I actually need, repair against the displayed maximum
Damage Risk, finish the Golden Key state I want, and save before the Arthur
branch.

If you are still at the King Slayer and have not crossed the Act 2 checkpoint,
finish the
<Link href="/sovereign-tower/king-slayer">
  {" "}King Slayer route
</Link>
first.

<h2 id="act-3-start">
  Enter Act 3 assuming Act 2 is already permanent
</h2>

I do not begin Act 3 by planning another large rewind.

The post-King-Slayer Demon warning was my opportunity to decide whether I was
willing to carry that Act 2 state forward.

Once I cross that line, I treat my roster as real.

That means I immediately check:

- who is still on the Round Table;
- who is temporarily absent;
- which county routes are unfinished;
- which Knights still have personal progression I actually care about;
- which specialists I would hate to lose to a long optional assignment.

The biggest difference from early game is that I no longer need every Knight to
be constantly productive.

A free Knight is useful.

A max-level Knight sitting at the Tower is often more useful than that same
Knight spending three cycles earning XP that can no longer improve them.

The game explicitly frames **diplomacy and war as distinct approaches**.

That is useful information even if the route later becomes more complicated.

My mistake on a first pass was mentally converting:

**Arthur appeared**

into:

**the final battle is about to happen.**

That made me too conservative too early.

Instead, I treat his first appearance as a change in priority.

I still finish high-value permanent routes.

I stop beginning low-value multi-cycle routes.

And I keep more of my strongest roster within one or two cycles of the Tower.

<h2 id="act-3-priorities">
  I prioritize permanent results, not the number of quests completed
</h2>

By Act 3, the board can look absurdly busy.

That does not mean all of it matters.

The things I still value highly are:

- a county becoming an ally;
- a unique Knight joining;
- a character route reaching a permanent transformation;
- a relic that solves a late stat problem;
- a mount that shortens several future assignments;
- a political result needed for the final kingdom state.

The things I start valuing less are:

- pure XP for a Knight who is already strong;
- small Gold rewards when I already have a healthy reserve;
- faction movement that does not unlock anything I am pursuing;
- repeatable work with no deadline and no permanent reward.

One good Act 3 example is the **Basalt Islands**.

Completing that political route in one of my runs gave me the alliance and a
Griffin.

That is exactly the kind of compound payoff I still make time for.

A random hunt that gives another modest pile of Gold is not in the same class.

<h2 id="level-15">
  Stop farming XP once a Knight reaches level 15
</h2>

This became one of my simplest late-game filters.

In my run, **level 15 was the Knight level cap**.

    matching the next important assignment.
  </figcaption>

This changes how I evaluate otherwise harmless quests.

Early in the game, I may send a strong Knight on an easy job because:

- they gain XP;
- they improve;
- I get Gold;
- the assignment is low risk.

At level 15, one of those benefits disappears.

So if Rufus, Gideon, or another core Knight is already capped, I ask:

**Does this quest do anything permanent besides occupying them?**

If the answer is no, I am much happier leaving them home.

This is especially important once Arthur is active.

The late game punishes me more for having the wrong Knight three cycles away
than it rewards me for clearing one extra icon.

<h2 id="late-damage-risk">
  Late Act 3 finally makes me spend Gold on repairs
</h2>

I stay conservative with repairs through most of Sovereign Tower.

Act 3 is where that policy becomes conditional.

Some late assignments stop being small chip-damage problems.

One dragon route in my run showed:

**5–7 Damage Risk.**

    This is where my repair rule changes. A Knight who was safe carrying two or
    three missing armor earlier can become a real death risk when the displayed
    ceiling reaches seven.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

At that point, I do not use my earlier-game rule of:

> close enough, save the Gold.

I use:

**Can this Knight survive the worst displayed roll?**

If not, I either:

- repair;
- replace the Knight;
- use a safer branch;
- delay the quest until the right Knight returns.

I still do **not** repair the whole roster.

That wastes Gold and repair opportunities.

I repair the Knight exposed to the actual risk.

That distinction becomes even more important near the final confrontation.

<h2 id="victoria">
  Resolve Victoria only when I can afford the roster and damage commitment
</h2>

Victoria is one of the best examples of an Act 3 route that can look urgent
without always being the most urgent thing on the board.

In my run, I repeatedly delayed her because:

- an emergency appeared;
- another route had a deadline;
- I did not like exposing a weak Knight to the possible damage;
- the Knights who covered the required stats were already away.

That was the right instinct.

When I finally committed to the ambush, I prepared specifically for that
assignment instead of forcing it into an already crowded cycle.

exactly the stat I was struggling to cover.

So my Victoria rule is:

**do not rush the route while the board is unstable, but do not forget the
late-game value of its reward.**

I am not treating this walkthrough as a complete Victoria recruit-versus-fight
guide.

The important Act 3 lesson is timing.

<h2 id="epicr">
  Keep Epicr's route alive if I want access to his late-game branch
</h2>

Epicr became much more important than I expected.

At first, his route looks like another political storyline involving Brimwood,
Gavault, a murder accusation, and a trial.

Later, it connects directly to the larger question of what Britannia should
become.

In one of my later runs, I made room for him on the active roster instead of
treating him as optional flavor.

That decision matters most if I am pursuing the special endgame route.

I therefore do not dismiss or ignore him just because his raw combat profile is
less impressive than one of my dedicated fighters.

Late-game value is not only:

**who has the biggest Strength number?**

It is also:

**who unlocks a branch I cannot replace with equipment?**

For this walkthrough, I only need the scheduling rule:

**if Epicr is part of the ending I want, I preserve his route before the final

<h2 id="thirteen-cycles">
  The 13-cycle warning is when I stop starting long routes
</h2>

The most useful Act 3 number in my run was:

**13 cycles.**

When I refused to commit the Golden Key to the Emperor, the game did not force
an immediate fight.

It gave me a preparation window.

I treat that as Act 3's equivalent of the King Slayer warning.

Thirteen cycles sounds generous.

It is not generous if I fill them with:

- a four-cycle side route;
- another three-cycle county problem;
- two injured Knights returning late;
- an emergency;
- a personal quest I forgot I had started.

So after that warning, I sort everything into three groups.

### Finish now

Anything already one step from:

- a county alliance;
- a unique recruit;
- a permanent character outcome;
- the Golden Key state I need.

### Finish only if cheap

A one-cycle assignment with useful loot or a quick faction correction.

### Ignore

A brand-new optional chain whose reward does not affect the kingdom, roster, or
ending I am pursuing.

This is also when I stop buying speculative equipment.

If I cannot point to the quest where a new sword, mount, or potion matters, I
keep the Gold.

<h2 id="finish-counties">
  Finish the counties before waiting for the Golden Key phase to solve itself
</h2>

One of my real late-game stalls was reaching a point where the board felt like
busy work and wondering:

> What am I waiting for now?

In my second run, the answer was **county completion**.

I had reached the point where ordinary quests were still appearing, but the main
story no longer seemed to move. Grinding more XP or clearing another random
contract did not fix that.

What finally changed the state was bringing the remaining counties under the
same banner.

Once Britannia was fully united, the game recognized that political state and
moved the Golden Key progression forward.

That gave me a much better late-game rule:

**if Act 3 feels stalled, I check unfinished county routes before I start
grinding filler work.**

I do not use this walkthrough to repeat the Golden Key acquisition sequence.

The exact all-counties trigger and the alternate Key search belong in the
<Link href="/sovereign-tower/golden-key">
  {" "}Golden Key guide
</Link>

This taught me not to solve a progression stall by:

- grinding XP;
- training already-capped Knights;
- clearing random hunts;
- waiting for an audience without changing the kingdom state.

I check the map.

If one county route is still unresolved, that is where I look first.

Once Britannia reached the required state in my run, the game moved directly
toward the Golden Key search.

The exact ways to obtain the Key belong in the
<Link href="/sovereign-tower/golden-key">
  {" "}Golden Key guide
</Link>

For Act 3 routing, the important point is:

**county completion can be the progression gate, not another cycle of generic
work.**

<h2 id="golden-key-phase">
  Once the Golden Key phase opens, protect it like a final story quest
</h2>

When the Key becomes actionable, I stop treating it like an optional relic.

The full-county search route in my run could show **up to 5 Damage Risk**.

That is enough for me to check armor deliberately.

I do not repair every participant to perfection.

I do make sure the Knight I actually need cannot disappear on the displayed
maximum.

The Key also changes how I value unrelated quests.

If I have:

- the ending-related Key quest ready;
- only a limited number of strong Knights;
- one or two late emergencies;

then I do not send the Key party away to earn a better side reward first.

This is the point where “finish the main route” becomes the efficient play.

<h2 id="final-preparation">
  Prepare the final roster from the assignment screen, not from a favorite-party list
</h2>

When the Arthur confrontation is finally close, I stop trying to guess the
perfect party from character reputation.

I open the actual assignment and build around what it asks for.

In my tested route, the final setup made me care heavily about:

- **Strength**;
- **Agility**;
- enough **Magic** that the team did not have a major dead zone.

I also looked at the damage ceiling before deciding who needed a final repair.

The important part is that I do not automatically buy six new pieces of gear.

I first move around what I already own.

By Act 3 I have accumulated:

- late relics;
- powerful weapons;
- mounts;
- stat food;
- consumables;
- equipment that was previously sitting on the wrong Knight.

That inventory is usually worth checking before the Forge.

This is also where a reward such as **Victoria's Sword +5 Strength** can suddenly
become more useful than it looked when I first obtained it.

That is enough that I want the final group safely above the danger line.

The same screen also exposed the stat problem clearly.

I had spent much of the game with flexible specialists.

Arthur made me notice that my roster was not naturally a high-Strength team.

So I:

1. chose the strongest suitable Knights first;
2. moved my best Strength equipment onto borderline contributors;
3. covered Agility instead of assuming raw Strength alone was enough;
4. kept a usable Magic contributor;
5. repaired the actual final group;
6. ignored equipment on Knights who were not going.

I do not use the rule:

> everyone must be fully repaired.

I use:

**everyone entering the final confrontation must survive the displayed maximum.**

That is much cheaper.

<h2 id="ending-branch">
  Save before Arthur because the final branch changes what the ending actually shows
</h2>

I make a separate save before committing the final Arthur branch.

Not because I expect the battle itself to be impossible.

Because the result changes what I see afterward.

The special Golden Key route and the standard route do not simply show the same
epilogue with different dialogue.

They emphasize different things.

So this is one of the few places where I strongly prefer preserving a branch
point rather than replaying a large chunk of Act 3.

The exact requirements, Tower decision, special ending, standard ending, and
character epilogues belong in the
<Link href="/sovereign-tower/endings">

For the Act 3 walkthrough, I stop at this rule:

**finish the kingdom state, finish the Key state, get the roster home, then save
before committing Arthur.**

<h2 id="act-3-mistakes">
  Act 3 mistakes that cost me the most time
</h2>

### Treating every Knight as if they still need XP

Level 15 is the cap in my run.

Once a Knight is capped, XP alone is not a reason to send them away.

### Sending max-level specialists on low-value multi-cycle work

Late Act 3 is about availability.

I would rather have my best Strength or Magic option idle at the Tower than
three cycles away earning a reward I do not need.

### Repairing the whole roster

I repair against the next assignment's actual Damage Risk.

Five, six, or seven possible damage changes the decision.

A harmless side quest does not.

### Refusing to repair because repairs are expensive

The opposite mistake is just as bad.

If the displayed maximum can remove a Knight I need, I pay.

Late-game Gold is less valuable than keeping the correct Knight alive.

### Fighting Victoria the moment the route appears

I delayed Victoria when an emergency or more important deadline was active.

The route can wait until the roster can support it.

### Forgetting Victoria's reward when building the final party

Victoria's Sword gives **+5 Strength** in my run.

I check Storage before buying another late Strength item.

### Assuming Arthur's first audience means the battle is next cycle

It does not.

I use the audience as a priority warning, not as permission to abandon every
unfinished permanent route.

### Treating 13 cycles as unlimited time

Thirteen cycles disappears very quickly when several assignments last two,
three, or four cycles.

I count Knight return times, not just the number printed on the countdown.

### Grinding because the Golden Key route appears stalled

If the late story stops moving, I check county completion.

In one run, unifying the counties was what pushed the Key phase forward.

### Rewriting the entire Golden Key solution inside Act 3

For my routing, I only need to know when the Key becomes priority.

The acquisition choices themselves belong on the dedicated Golden Key route.

### Going into Arthur with a favorite party instead of a requirement-based party

The final assignment showed me exactly what I was weak in.

I move equipment and choose Knights after reading that screen.

### Continuing optional work after the final confrontation is ready

At that point, I am done expanding the board.

A small Gold reward is not worth having the one Knight I need still traveling
when the final route is ready.
