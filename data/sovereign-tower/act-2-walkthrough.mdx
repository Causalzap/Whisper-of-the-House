when I know what I am trying to change**.

The sequence I use is:

**enter Act 2 → accept that Act 1 is locked → use the 8-slot Round Table as a

  showed **2–5 Damage Risk**.

It is knowing **which Knights will still be available two or three cycles from

<h2 id="act-2-route">
  The Act 2 route I use from the opening cycle to King Slayer
</h2>

Act 2 is where I stopped trying to clear every quest on the board.

Too many systems begin overlapping.

A county can suffer a serious setback.

A Knight can become unavailable because of a personal route.

The Tower murder can force me to reconsider several earlier cycles.

A county chain can occupy two Knights for multiple cycles.

And eventually the King Slayer gives the Act a real closing window.

I therefore play Act 2 in five phases.

### Phase 1: stabilize the new Act

I accept that Act 1 is behind me, check the eight-slot Round Table, and keep Gold
flexible instead of restoring every Knight to perfect condition.

### Phase 2: learn before rewinding

If Clovermont, the murder, or another major event goes badly, I do not
automatically restart.

I keep playing until I know what earlier decision I would actually change.

### Phase 3: rebuild the timeline I want to keep

Once I know the intervention, I rewind only as far as necessary.

Then I stabilize the murder outcome and rebuild the roster around whoever is
still available.

### Phase 4: spend cycles on permanent rewards

This is where I prioritize:

- county alliances;
- Knight recruitment;
- character routes with permanent mechanical changes;
- important relic or progression chains.

### Phase 5: stop expanding

When the King Slayer announces **five circles**, I stop treating Act 2 like an
open checklist.

From that point on, every new assignment has to justify the Knights, Gold, and
cycles it consumes.

<h2 id="act-2-start">
  Act 2 locks the previous Act behind you
</h2>

The first thing I noticed after entering Act 2 was the Demon Power timeline.

The earlier Act was no longer freely available.

In my run, the rewind wheel did not let me return to **Cycle 8 or lower**.

That means I do not enter Act 2 thinking:

> I can always go back later and clean up the Groveshire/Gavault decision.

I cannot rely on that.

This becomes even more obvious later when new information about old county
events appears only after Act 2 has already started.

Knowing more does not automatically reopen the old timeline.

The full rule belongs in the
<Link href="/sovereign-tower/demon-power">
  {" "}Demon Power guide
</Link>

For this walkthrough, I use a simpler principle:

**once Act 2 begins, I build forward from the Act 1 state I already accepted.**

If I had an Act 1 outcome that was structurally unacceptable, the correct place
to change it was the Demon checkpoint before leaving that Act.

<h2 id="eight-knight-slots">
  The eight-slot Round Table is a buffer, not eight permanent quest slots
</h2>

Act 1 gave me room for **6 Knights**.

Act 2 expands the Round Table to **8**.

My first instinct was to see that as permission to run more assignments at
once.

I now think that is the wrong way to use the upgrade.

The two extra slots are most valuable when I leave some flexibility.

They let me absorb situations like:

- Gwendan being removed from the active roster;
- a two-Knight county expedition;
- one Knight being locked into a personal route;
- somebody taking enough damage that I do not want to send them immediately;
- a late emergency appearing unexpectedly;
- a recruit temporarily leaving during a transformation or treatment.

If every available Knight is away, having eight slots instead of six has not
made my run safer.

The resource I am managing is not simply:

**number of Knights owned.**

It is:

**number of useful Knights available next cycle.**

<h2 id="early-act-2-spending">
  Keep early Act 2 Gold flexible until you know which timeline you will keep
</h2>

Several Knights may enter Act 2 carrying damage.

I do not respond by repairing the entire roster.

I look at the assignments I am actually considering.

If a Knight can safely absorb the worst displayed Damage Risk, I usually leave
the armor alone.

That matters because Act 2 can ask for Gold in more meaningful places:

- rebuilding a damaged location;
- solving a major county problem;
- buying equipment for a real stat gap;
- preparing long expeditions;
- repairing a Knight before a 4- or 5-damage assignment;
- preparing for the King Slayer endgame.

I am especially conservative before the murder timeline is stable.

If I already suspect that I may rewind several cycles, spending heavily on a
temporary branch gives me very little value.

I spend enough to survive and learn.

I spend more aggressively once I know which timeline I want to keep.

If I know the intervention, I rewind.

If I am guessing, I keep collecting information.

<h2 id="tower-murder">

A Knight dies inside the Tower.

The useful information comes after the murder.

The investigation can expose contradictions and give me the knowledge needed
to make an earlier intervention meaningful.

In my route, letting **Gwendan** participate in the investigation eventually
produced the crucial result that he had behaved suspiciously and presented
inconsistent evidence.

<h2 id="gwendan-gap">
  Fix the murder first, then plan around the missing Charisma
</h2>

The successful intervention I tested removed Gwendan from the murder setup
before the event.

That solved the structural problem.

It also created a roster problem.

Gwendan had been one of my stronger Charisma options.

Once he was out of the active Round Table, diplomacy and social investigations
looked noticeably worse.

I did not immediately spend large amounts of Gold trying to turn another Knight
into Gwendan.

Instead I changed how I routed quests.

If an investigation offered:

- a Charisma approach;
- or a Wit approach;

I used the stat my surviving roster already covered.

That is one reason the **Tower Library** later made sense for my South Bay Lamp
investigation.

This is the Act 2 pattern I keep returning to:

**change the route before buying your way through a stat problem.**

<h2 id="major-routes">
  Once the murder timeline is stable, I prioritize compound rewards
</h2>

After the murder is in a state I am willing to keep, I become much more
comfortable spending time and Gold.

I prioritize routes that solve several problems at once.

A good Act 2 route can give me:

- an alliance;
- a recruit;
- a character transformation;
- a useful stat specialist;
- a relic;
- a permanent political result.

That is usually worth more than another repeatable contract with a modest Gold
reward.

South Bay and the later Wolf/Rufus route are good examples.

The important point for this page is **when I fit them into the Act**.

The exact solutions stay on their dedicated pages.

<h2 id="south-bay-scheduling">
  Start South Bay early enough to absorb both the island trip and a bad branch
</h2>

The South Bay Lamp route is easy to underestimate because its opening
investigation looks small.

It is not.

The first investigation has a **3-cycle deadline**.

The Tower Library route I used can show **0 Damage Risk**, so that stage does
not need an unnecessary armor repair.

But the later **Djinn Island, Southbay** expedition is a real roster commitment.

My version showed:

- **3 duration**
- **2 Knight slots**
- **1–2 Damage Risk**
- **75 Gold**

A mount reduced the duration by one in my run.

That is why I start South Bay while I still have scheduling room.

I do not want to begin a two-Knight, multi-cycle expedition just as King Slayer
pressure is about to start.

The complete route is in the
<Link href="/sovereign-tower/south-bay-lamp">
  {" "}South Bay Lamp guide
</Link>

For Act 2 planning, there are two other things I now account for.

First:

**the Lamp being missing from Djinn Island is progression.**

I do not repeat the island and waste more cycles.

Second:

**the clean Lamp result is not the only way South Bay can continue.**

If Yannis dies, Ligia can inherit the county and a later
**South Bay political instabilities** assignment can appear.

That route adds another assignment to the board and can show meaningful Damage
Risk.

So a bad South Bay family outcome is not necessarily a reason to blow up the
entire Act 2 timeline.

It may be recoverable politically.

I decide whether saving Yannis matters enough to me personally before I commit
to a large rewind.

<h2 id="wolf-to-rufus">
  Do not trigger the Wolf cure without accounting for his temporary absence
</h2>

The Groveshire Wolf route can also change the Act 2 roster temporarily.

Later, Belelladana can reveal that the Wolf is actually a human trapped under a
curse.

On the cure route I tested, choosing treatment caused the Wolf to **leave the
Round Table temporarily**.

That is the scheduling detail that matters here.

If I currently have:

- two Knights on Djinn Island;
- Gwendan unavailable;
- another Knight on a personal route;
- an emergency already open;

then temporarily losing the Wolf can turn an eight-slot roster into a very
small working team.

I therefore do not trigger the cure purely because the dialogue is available.

I check the board first.

When the treatment finishes, the Wolf returns as **Sir Rufus**.

The transformation, changed stats, Wolf Habits, and the actual cure decision
belong in the
<Link href="/sovereign-tower/groveshire-wolf">
  {" "}Groveshire Wolf and Rufus guide
</Link>

For this walkthrough, my rule is:

**start the cure when I can survive a short-term roster loss.**

<h2 id="quest-capacity">
  Count future availability before starting another optional quest
</h2>

By the middle of Act 2, looking only at the current Round Table is not enough.

Before I start a new quest, I mentally project two or three cycles forward.

I ask:

- How many Knights does this assignment consume?
- How long are they gone?
- Is a mount shortening the whole party or only part of the problem?
- Is somebody likely to leave for a personal route?
- Which specialist remains at the Tower?
- Is South Bay still active?
- Am I about to cure the Wolf?
- Has the King Slayer countdown started?
- Do I need Gold for an upcoming high-risk assignment?

This is why I am comfortable leaving ordinary work unfinished.

The board does not need to be empty.

My roster needs to remain functional.

A no-deadline contract can wait.

A major county chain, murder intervention, or Act-ending threat usually cannot.

<h2 id="late-emergencies">
  The last five circles can still contain expensive emergencies
</h2>

King Slayer pressure does not freeze the rest of the world.

Late assignments can still appear.

An **Army of the Dead** emergency in my run showed:

- **2–3 Damage Risk**
- **80 Gold**

That is manageable on its own.

The danger is what it overlaps with.

If my likely final Knights are already:

- damaged;
- away;
- carrying my best supernatural equipment;
- needed for another urgent route;

then one more emergency can break the schedule.

This is why I leave some roster capacity unused near the end.

I also change how I think about repairs.

Earlier in Act 2, I may refuse to repair for:

**0 Damage Risk**

or a safe:

**1–2 Damage Risk**

assignment.

But my tested King Slayer purification later showed:

**2–5 Damage Risk.**

That is the type of assignment I save repair Gold for.

<h2 id="final-resolution">
  Do not assume the final King Slayer route needs the entire roster
</h2>

Another reason I avoid locking every Knight into one imagined final build is
that the King Slayer does not necessarily resolve through one fixed
six-Knight assignment.

When my countdown ended, I received multiple final resolution choices.

The route I selected was **King Slayer purification**.

That option used:

**4 Knights**

while another visible route required:

**6 Knights**.

This matters for Act 2 planning.

Before seeing the final choice, I had been thinking about “the final party” as
if every available Knight would automatically be consumed.

That was too rigid.

The better approach is:

**preserve a strong roster, then build around the final resolution you actually
choose.**

I do not duplicate the purification setup here.

The dedicated
<Link href="/sovereign-tower/king-slayer">
  {" "}King Slayer guide
</Link>
covers the four-Knight route, Damage Risk, equipment, Glass Blade, and Spectral
Eye.

For this walkthrough, the important scheduling point is:

**do not waste earlier cycles optimizing six specific Knights for a resolution
you have not seen yet.**

<h2 id="final-cycles">
  In the final cycles, permanent outcomes beat board cleanup
</h2>

When only a few circles remain, I become aggressive about saying no.

I finish something if it is one step away from:

- an alliance;
- a recruit;
- a character route I intentionally chose;
- a useful long-term relic;
- a structural fix I do not want to carry into Act 3.

I leave behind work that is mainly:

- ordinary Gold;
- small faction gains;
- repeatable experience;
- a result I can live without.

I also stop chasing perfection.

A normal Success is enough if it solves the problem.

One Knight taking non-fatal damage is acceptable.

An optional contract expiring is acceptable.

What is not acceptable is arriving at the final King Slayer resolution with the
right Knights still several cycles away.

That is the actual failure state I plan around.

I do **not** rewind because:

- one ordinary quest failed;
- a faction number could be prettier;
- I missed a small Gold reward;
- somebody took a little unnecessary damage;
- I did not clear every icon on the map.

Those are normal imperfections.

<h2 id="act-2-mistakes">
  Act 2 mistakes that cost me the most cycles
</h2>

### Treating Act 1 as if it can always be fixed later

It cannot.

Once Act 2 starts, the old stretch can already be locked.

### Filling the new eight-slot Round Table immediately

The two extra slots are most valuable as flexibility.

I do not turn them into two more permanent assignments.

### Repairing everyone at the start of Act 2

I repair against actual Damage Risk.

I keep Gold for the problems that can really hurt later.

### Rewinding Clovermont without knowing the intervention

Going backward does not create new information by itself.

### Rewinding the Tower murder as soon as I see the body

I investigate first.

The failed timeline can contain the knowledge needed to fix the earlier one.

### Spending heavily before the murder timeline is stable

If I already expect to rewind, I keep the temporary branch cheap.

### Treating Gwendan's absence as a permanent roster collapse

It creates a Charisma gap, but the Act can continue and Gideon later gave my
roster another strong social option.

### Forcing every social investigation through Charisma

If Wit gives me a better route with the Knights I actually have, I use Wit.

### Starting South Bay too late

The opening investigation has a deadline, and Djinn Island can occupy two
Knights for multiple cycles.

I want room for the whole chain, not just its first assignment.

### Reloading because the Lamp is missing from Djinn Island

The theft is progression.

Repeating the island wastes time.

### Assuming a bad Yannis outcome permanently destroys South Bay

The county can continue through Ligia's political-instability route.

Whether I rewind depends on how much I care about the family outcome, not on an
assumption that the alliance is automatically gone.

### Starting the Wolf cure while the roster is already thin

The Wolf temporarily leaves during the treatment route I tested.

I account for that absence before triggering it.

### Starting another long optional chain after the five-circle warning

Once the King Slayer countdown begins, I want Knights returning, not leaving.

### Repairing low-risk side quests and then lacking Gold for the endgame

A zero-risk investigation does not need armor.

A final assignment that can reach **5 damage** might.

### Assuming King Slayer requires every Knight before seeing the final choices

My tested purification used four Knights, while another visible route used six.

I wait for the resolution screen before finalizing the party.

### Clicking through the post-King-Slayer sequence

The Demon gives one last **now-or-never** Act 2 rewind opportunity.

I decide whether I want this timeline before crossing into Act 3.
