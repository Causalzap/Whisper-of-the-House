<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
I use **Demon Power after I know what I am going back to change**, not simply
because something bad happened.

A rewind can return me to an earlier point in the **current Act** while keeping
important knowledge I have already discovered, including:

- Knight tags;
- Knight preferences;
- quest requirements;
- Sovereign archetype progress.

That information can change what I know when an earlier conversation or quest
happens again.

My rule is:

**see the consequence → learn why it happened → identify the intervention →
rewind to the latest useful point before that intervention.**

If the same disaster still happens after a rewind, I do not repeat the exact
same loop. I check whether I am missing information, chose the wrong point, or
have not yet reached the conversation where the retained knowledge matters.

Also stop at every end-of-Act Demon warning. Once you accept the next Act, the
previous Act's timeline is no longer available through that rewind.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

<h2 id="how-demon-power-works">
  Demon Power is a knowledge rewind, not a normal reload
</h2>

The first time I used Demon Power, I treated it like a more elaborate save
reload.

That is not the part that makes it powerful.

The useful part is that the Sovereign can return to an earlier point while
still knowing things learned in the later timeline.

The game explicitly tells me that I can keep information such as:

- Knight tags;
- Knight preferences;
- quest requirements;
- Sovereign archetype progress.

That means a failed timeline can still produce permanent value.

I can learn that:

- a Knight has an attribute I did not know before;
- a quest really needs a different type of Knight;
- an apparently innocent event leads to a later disaster;
- a conversation contains information that changes an earlier decision.

Then I rewind with that knowledge.

So I do not judge every bad timeline as wasted time.

Sometimes the first version exists to teach me how to build the second one.

<h2 id="what-you-keep">
  What actually stays after a rewind
</h2>

The information I rely on most is **Knight knowledge**.

If I discovered a hidden Knight tag in the later timeline, I can still use that
knowledge after going back.

The same applies to preferences and known quest requirements.

This matters because Knight information is part of assignment planning.

A quest I originally approached with the wrong Knight may be much easier the
second time simply because I now understand:

- which attributes matter;
- which work the Knight likes or dislikes;
- which requirement was hidden the first time;
- which character is actually relevant to the outcome.

Sovereign archetype progress also persists.

That matters because archetype progression can affect available dialogue.

So even if the calendar says I have returned to an earlier Cycle, I am not
necessarily playing an identical version of that Cycle.

The Sovereign knows more.

<h2 id="what-rewinds">
  What I expect to replay after going back
</h2>

I do **not** treat everything completed after the selected rewind point as
permanently banked.

If I jump backward several cycles, events after that point can have to happen
again.

In Act 2, a deep rewind meant replaying things that had already occupied my
Knights in the discarded timeline.

That can include:

- audiences;
- Knight assignments;
- county quests;
- spending decisions;
- personal conversations;
- damaged or destroyed locations;
- other consequences that occurred after the selected point.

This is the real cost of choosing an unnecessarily early rewind.

It is not just more dialogue.

It is more roster scheduling, Gold decisions, and opportunities for the new
timeline to diverge.

That is why I do not select the beginning of the Act unless the decision I need
is actually that early.

<h2 id="choose-rewind-cycle">
  Choose the latest rewind point that is still early enough
</h2>

The Demon interface lets me inspect earlier parts of the Act rather than forcing
one all-or-nothing reset.

My decision is:

**How late can I rewind and still intercept the problem?**

Not:

**How far back can Demon Power take me?**

If the important conversation happens in an afternoon, going all the way back
to the beginning of the Act usually gives me no additional benefit.

It only creates more work.

Before confirming the rewind, I identify:

1. the bad outcome;
2. the information I learned from it;
3. the earlier event that can use that information;
4. the latest timeline point before that event.

That fourth point is normally where I go.

</h2>

Some important events make this easier because Demon Power gives contextual
timeline labels.

The Tower murder is a good example.

After I had progressed far enough into that problem, the timeline showed
**Before murder** markers.

<h2 id="after-rewind">
  Slow down when you reach the event you rewound for
</h2>

After rewinding, I do not automatically skip every conversation because I
remember seeing it before.

That can make me skip the entire reason I went back.

Retained knowledge can create a response that did not exist—or did not make
sense—the first time.

I watch for:

- **Omniscient**-style choices;
- dialogue that assumes knowledge from the discarded timeline;
- a new accusation;
- a new warning;
- a response tied to information I learned later.

The new option may not appear immediately after the rewind.

The timeline can replay normally for a while before I return to the exact event
where the retained knowledge becomes useful.

So if the first audience looks unchanged, I do not immediately assume the
rewind failed.

I continue until I reach the decision I actually targeted.

<h2 id="audiences-can-change">
  Do not expect the replayed audience queue to be identical
</h2>

Another important detail is that a rewind does not guarantee the exact same
audience order.

I have replayed earlier cycles and seen the audience sequence change.

That means I do not plan a rewind around:

> Person A must appear in the morning and Person B must appear in the afternoon.

I plan around the objective instead.

For example:

> I need this Knight available before the intervention.

or:

> I need this county route advanced before the deadline.

That survives timeline variation much better.

A different audience is therefore not enough evidence that I selected the wrong
Cycle.

The replayed timeline is allowed to diverge.

<h2 id="why-rewind-fails">
  Why Demon Power can still lead to the same bad outcome
</h2>

This is the most important troubleshooting point.

I have rewound, replayed the relevant part of the Act, expected the outcome to
change—and reached essentially the same disaster again.

The Demon explicitly warns that two things can cause this:

**you do not have the proper information**

or:

**you used the power at the wrong moment.**

When the same result happens twice, I stop repeating the identical loop.

I check for missing information instead.

The things I look for are:

- an investigation I ended too early;
- another Knight who can investigate the event;
- a personal conversation I have not exhausted;
- a follow-up audience after what looked like the conclusion;
- evidence explaining **why** something happened rather than only **who** did
  it.

That last distinction mattered a lot in the murder route.

Knowing which Knight was involved was not automatically the same as
understanding what earlier event needed to change.

The rewind became useful only when the information became actionable.

<h2 id="when-to-rewind">
  I save Demon Power for structural consequences
</h2>

Not every bad Quest result deserves a new timeline.

I normally keep moving after:

- an ordinary failed contract;
- a small Gold loss;
- some avoidable armor damage;
- a faction result I can recover naturally;
- a success that was merely less efficient than I wanted.

Those problems can usually be repaired in the current timeline.

I consider Demon Power when the result changes something I actually care about
carrying forward:

- a Knight dies;
- a county ends in the wrong state;
- a recruit is lost;
- a major personal route closes;
- later knowledge gives me a clear earlier intervention;
- an Act-defining choice goes in a direction I deliberately want to change.

That distinction keeps Demon Power from turning every imperfect Cycle into a
replay.

<h2 id="act-checkpoints">
  The end-of-Act Demon warning is the real point of no return
</h2>

Demon Power has one hard practical boundary:

**the current Act.**

At the end of Act 1, after the Dragon Knight sequence, the Demon warns me that
continuing will make that first stretch of the timeline unavailable.

I take that warning more seriously now because later knowledge can explain an
older decision **without reopening the Act where that decision happened**.

Gideon's later investigation gave me the clearest example.

During Act 2, information about his past pointed back toward the old
**Groveshire/Gavault** conflict. By the time I learned enough to reconsider what
that earlier county choice meant, Act 1 was already outside my current Demon
Power window. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

That changed how I think about retained knowledge.

Demon Power can let me carry useful information backward **inside an Act**.

It does not mean every revelation I discover later gives me permission to jump
back across an Act boundary.

So before I accept an end-of-Act warning, I ask:

- Is there a Knight outcome I genuinely want to change?
- Is there a county result I already know how to improve?
- Did I miss a recruitment or major personal route I care about?
- Do I already know the earlier intervention I would make?
- Am I comfortable carrying this political state into the next Act?

If I cannot name a concrete intervention, I usually continue.

A vague hope that:

> maybe the next version will be cleaner

is not enough reason for me to replay a large part of the Act.

A known intervention is.

The same rule applies at the end of Act 2.

After the King Slayer sequence, the Demon explicitly warns that the power is
still available **for this last stretch**, but not forever. Once I accept that
checkpoint, Act 3 begins. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

That is why my checkpoint rule is:

**use Demon Power before crossing the Act boundary, not after later story
information makes me wish I had.**

<h2 id="rewind-achievement">
  It's Rewind Time! requires 10 Demon Power uses
</h2>

The Sovereign menu tracks how many times I have used Demon Power.

That gives me an easy way to check progress toward **It's Rewind Time!**.

The achievement requires:

**Use the power of the demon 10 times before reaching the end.**

I do not force ten meaningless rewinds at the beginning of the game.

Natural route testing already gives me legitimate opportunities to use the
system.

If I am deliberately achievement hunting, I check the counter later and make
sure I reach **10** before the end rather than discovering after the final
sequence that I stopped at eight or nine.

The important part is that I do this while there are still useful branches to
test.

Ten empty rewinds give me the achievement counter.

Ten informed rewinds can give me the counter **and** better route knowledge.

<h2 id="demon-power-stuck">
  What I check when Demon Power seems not to work
</h2>

### I rewound but there is no new dialogue

I keep playing until I reach the exact event I targeted.

If that event also contains no useful new option, I consider whether I am still
missing information.

### I got the same bad result again

I stop repeating the same loop.

The next step is usually more investigation or a different rewind point.

### The audiences are different after the rewind

That can happen.

I track objectives, available Knights, and deadlines rather than expecting an
identical visitor order.

### The point I want is no longer in Demon Power

If I already crossed the Act checkpoint, I treat the earlier Act as committed.

### I am not sure whether a rewind is worth it

I ask one question:

**Can I name the decision I am going back to change?**

If I cannot, I usually keep playing until I can.
