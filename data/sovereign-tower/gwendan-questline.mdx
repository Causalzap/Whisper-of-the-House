<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
The Gwendan route has two separate problems that are easy to mix together:

**his debt** and **the Tower murder**.

In my route, paying Gwendan's **350 Gold debt** did **not** stop the murder.
The useful murder route was:

**let the murder happen → investigate it → use Gwendan as an investigator →
notice his inconsistent evidence → confront him until he explains the
whispers and occult influence → rewind to a point marked Before murder →
dismiss Gwendan from the Round Table before the event → verify that the murder
does not happen.**

Dismissal is temporary rather than permanent. In my tested timeline, several
cycles after removing Gwendan before the murder, the intended victim was still
alive and Gwendan was still shown as wanting to rejoin.

The dismissal UI also says a Knight can later be called back through the
**Map Room**.

I would not hard-code one victim name, because the murder target can change
between timelines.

If you are trying to complete Gwendan's personal story as well, keep following
his later conversations after the murder problem is stabilized. The
**True Noble Soul** achievement specifically asks you to make Gwendan overcome
his personal issues.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

<h2 id="gwendan-route">
  The Gwendan route I use to solve the Tower murder
</h2>

Gwendan's Act 2 story looks like one long character quest, but I found it easier
to separate it into three stages.

**Stage 1 — Personal problem**

I learn about his debt and decide whether to help him.

**Stage 2 — Murder investigation**

A Knight dies inside the Tower, Gwendan becomes an actionable suspect, and I
keep investigating until I understand what happened.

**Stage 3 — Timeline intervention**

I use what I learned to return to the period before the murder and remove
Gwendan from the setup.

The important sequence in my successful route was:

1. deal with Gwendan's debt conversation;
2. let the Tower murder occur in the information-gathering timeline;
3. investigate rather than immediately trying to erase the event;
4. assign Gwendan to the murder investigation;
5. read the result showing suspicious behavior and inconsistent evidence;
6. confront him;
7. keep pressing until he explains the strange whispers;
8. recognize that this is not a normal motive-driven murder;
9. use Demon Power to return to a **Before murder** point;
10. dismiss Gwendan from the Round Table while still before the event;
11. continue through the murder window;
12. confirm that the death does not occur in the new timeline.

That last verification matters.

I do not assume the route worked just because Gwendan is gone.

I play through the point where the murder previously happened and confirm the
victim is still alive.


<h2 id="gwendan-debt">
  Paying Gwendan's 350 Gold debt does not solve the murder
</h2>

Gwendan's debt appears before the murder route becomes fully understandable.

In my game, helping him cost **350 Gold**.

I chose to pay it.

That was reasonable as a character decision, and Gwendan later even discussed
repaying the money after finding financial help elsewhere.

But this is the key distinction:

**paying the debt did not prevent the murder.**

I replayed the early Act 2 sequence after learning about the murder and still
paid Gwendan's debt.

The dangerous event remained part of the timeline.

So I would not treat:

> Pay Gwendan's debt

as:

> Save the murder victim.

They are separate parts of his storyline.

The debt helps with Gwendan's personal route.

The murder requires a timeline intervention.

This is especially important if you are low on Gold.

Do not spend **350 Gold** expecting it to function as the murder solution.

Pay it because you want to continue that version of Gwendan's personal story.

<h2 id="tower-murder">
  Let the Tower murder give you the information you need
</h2>

In one of my timelines, the victim was **Goberto**.

He was found dead inside the private quarters.

The investigation makes it clear that the problem comes from inside the Tower:
one of the Knights had access to the private area, but the motive and method are
not initially clear.

I do not immediately erase this timeline.

The death is terrible for the roster, but the failed timeline is also where I
learn who needs to be investigated.

There is an important warning here:

**I would not hard-code Goberto as the universal murder victim.**

Different timeline states can change who dies.

The useful part of this guide is therefore not:

> save Goberto on Cycle X

It is:

> identify the murder → investigate Gwendan → learn the real intervention →
> remove him before the murder window.

That sequence survives timeline variation much better.

<h2 id="investigate-gwendan">
  I let Gwendan investigate the murder
</h2>

The investigation gives me a choice in who handles it.

A safe instinct would be to choose the Knight I trust most.

Instead, I tested Gwendan.

That produced exactly the kind of information I needed.

His investigation came back with the warning that he had:

**behaved suspiciously and presented inconsistent evidence.**

That result is much stronger than simply thinking:

> Gwendan seems arrogant, so maybe he did it.

Now the game itself is telling me something about his behavior during the
investigation does not line up.

This is the point where I stop treating the entire Round Table as equally
suspicious.

I focus on Gwendan.

<h2 id="confront-gwendan">
  Confront Gwendan and keep pressing past the contradictions
</h2>

When Gwendan is summoned, his first reaction does not give me a clean
confession.

He pushes back against the investigation.

The contradictions I was able to challenge included things such as:

- testimony that did not match;
- supposed magical traces that other evidence did not support;
- the possibility that evidence had been destroyed;
- his presence around the private quarters.

The conversation can look like it is going nowhere.

I do not stop at the first denial.

I keep asking for an explanation.

Eventually Gwendan opens up about what had been happening to him before the
murder.

He had been hearing a **voice at night**.

The whispers followed him even into bed.

During one of those sleepless nights, he heard the voice particularly clearly
near the victim's room.

He describes the voice as threatening and mocking him until he lost control.

Afterward, even Gwendan recognizes that what happened did not feel rational.

He describes it as something that, in hindsight, seemed:

**magical**

and:

**occult.**

That changes how I interpret the entire murder.

This is not simply:

> Gwendan secretly hated another Knight.

It is a manipulated Gwendan becoming the mechanism for the murder.

That is why fixing his debt alone cannot solve it.


<h2 id="murder-intervention">
  The murder solution I confirmed is removing Gwendan before it happens
</h2>

Once I know enough, I stop trying to repair the already-dead timeline.

I go back.

The general mechanics of choosing the rewind point belong in the
<Link href="/sovereign-tower/demon-power">
  {" "}Demon Power guide
</Link>

For Gwendan's route, I only need one practical rule:

**return to a point the game identifies as being before the murder, while there
is still enough time to remove Gwendan from the setup.**

I do not hard-code one Cycle number as universally correct.

I have seen the relevant part of the timeline represented with
**Before murder** markers around this section of Act 2, and the useful point can
depend on where the current replay is.

What matters is whether Gwendan can still be removed before the event resolves.

<h2 id="dismiss-gwendan">
  Dismiss Gwendan from Tower View before the murder
</h2>

This is the intervention that worked in my tested timeline.

I open **Tower View** and dismiss Gwendan from the Round Table.

There are two interface details worth knowing.

First:

**you can only dismiss Knights while you are on Tower View.**

Second, the game explicitly says the dismissed Knight can later be called back
through the **Map Room**.

That made this decision much easier for me.

I am not permanently sacrificing a useful Knight just to test the murder
solution.

I am temporarily taking him out of the environment where the murder occurs.

<h2 id="verify-murder">
  Verify the timeline instead of assuming the dismissal worked
</h2>

After dismissing Gwendan, I do not immediately declare the murder solved.

I continue far enough past the point where the death originally occurred and
check two things:

- the expected murder audience does not appear;
- the intended victim is still alive.

That worked in my tested timeline.

The original bad timeline had **Gberto dead**. After rewinding to a
**Before murder** point and dismissing Gwendan from the Round Table, I passed
the same event window without the murder triggering.

More importantly, this was not only a one-cycle observation.

Several cycles later, my continued Act 2 timeline still had:

**Gwendan off the Round Table → Gberto alive.**

That gives me much more confidence that dismissing Gwendan before the event is
a real intervention rather than merely delaying the murder by one audience.

I still keep the wording narrow.

I do **not** claim that Gberto can never die later from another event, or that
every possible Tower murder victim is always Gberto.

The victim can differ between timelines.

What I can confirm is:

**in the timeline I tested, dismissing Gwendan before the murder prevented that
murder event from occurring, and the intended victim remained alive well after
the original murder window.**

<h2 id="recall-gwendan">
  Dismissing Gwendan does not permanently remove him
</h2>

The dismissal warning is important because this route sounds harsher than it
actually is.

When I dismiss Gwendan from **Tower View**, the UI says that he leaves the
Round Table but can later be called back through the **Map Room**.

My later Act 2 timeline gave me another confirmation that this is not a
permanent deletion.

Even after several cycles with Gwendan absent and the murder successfully
avoided, the game still showed that **Gwendan wanted to rejoin**.

So I treat dismissal as:

**a temporary roster and timeline intervention**

rather than:

**choosing to erase Gwendan from the rest of the playthrough.**

That distinction matters because dismissing him has an immediate cost.

I lose one active Knight and one of my stronger Charisma options while he is
gone.

I therefore plan the next few cycles around the smaller roster rather than
assuming I must immediately undo the murder solution just to get him back.

I also do not recall him automatically the moment the option appears.

If my reason for dismissing him was to keep him out of the murder setup, I make
sure I am safely past that event before experimenting with his return.

<h2 id="gwendan-debt-afterward">
  Paying the debt can still matter for Gwendan's personal route
</h2>

The fact that the debt does not prevent the murder does **not** make the debt
conversation useless.

It belongs to a different objective.

After I paid Gwendan's debt, I later reached a conversation where he said he had
found unexpected financial help and intended to repay me.

That tells me the game continues tracking this personal problem after the
initial **350 Gold** choice.

So my approach is:

**murder problem**

→ solve with information + rewind + temporary dismissal

**Gwendan personal problem**

→ continue his personal conversations separately

I do not try to solve both with the same choice.

<h2 id="true-noble-soul">
  True Noble Soul is tied to resolving Gwendan's personal issues
</h2>

The achievement description for **True Noble Soul** is very specific:

**Make Gwendan overcome his personal issues.**

That makes this achievement a natural continuation of his route rather than a
reward for simply identifying him during the murder investigation.

I therefore keep following Gwendan's later personal events after the immediate
murder problem is under control.

I have reached a later personal-resolution scene consistent with Gwendan
finally becoming more settled.

What I have **not** cleanly captured is the achievement pop on the exact line
that resolves it.

So I would not tell you:

> choose this one sentence and True Noble Soul instantly unlocks.

The reliable part I can support right now is:

- the achievement belongs to **Gwendan's personal issues**;
- paying attention to his debt/personal conversations is therefore still
  relevant;
- solving the murder alone is not something I would label the confirmed
  achievement trigger.

If I get a clean final trigger later, this is the section I would tighten rather
than creating another separate Gwendan achievement page.

<h2 id="gwendan-stuck">
  What I check when the Gwendan route seems stuck
</h2>

### I paid Gwendan's 350 Gold debt, but the murder still happened

That happened in my route too.

The debt choice is not the murder intervention.

Keep investigating the death.

### I know a murder happened, but I do not have enough evidence against Gwendan

I continue the investigation.

Assigning Gwendan himself to investigate produced the inconsistent-evidence
result in my timeline.

### Gwendan keeps denying everything

Keep pressing the contradictions until the conversation reaches the strange
nighttime whispers and occult influence.

### I rewound, but the murder still happens

Do not treat the rewind itself as the solution.

Make sure you are still **before the murder** and actually change Gwendan's
presence in the setup.

### I cannot find the dismiss option

Go back to **Tower View**.

That is where I was able to dismiss him.

### I dismissed Gwendan and am worried I lost him permanently

The dismissal is not permanent.

The UI says a dismissed Knight can later be recalled through the **Map Room**,
and several cycles later my Act 2 timeline still showed that Gwendan wanted to
rejoin.

### I dismissed him but do not know whether it worked

Continue past the point where the murder originally happened.

In my successful timeline, the murder did not trigger, and several cycles later
the intended victim was still alive while Gwendan remained off the Round Table.

That is the confirmation I use rather than checking only the next audience.

### My murder victim is not Goberto

Do not abandon the route just because the victim differs.

I treat the victim as timeline-dependent and focus on the Gwendan investigation
and pre-murder intervention.

### I solved the murder but True Noble Soul did not unlock

Keep treating **True Noble Soul** as a Gwendan personal-story objective.

Its achievement description refers to making him overcome his personal issues,
not simply stopping the Tower murder.
