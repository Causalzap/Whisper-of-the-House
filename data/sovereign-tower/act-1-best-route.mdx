<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
For my first Act 1 clear, I prefer **Groveshire** over Gavault.

The route is easier to keep under control because the county problem has a
clear progression path and can give me the **allied county** I need before the
Dragon Knight confrontation.

Once the Dragon Knight challenge appears, I stop trying to complete everything
on the board. On my Groveshire run, the preparation targets were:

- **Merchant satisfaction: 10**
- **Noble satisfaction: 8**
- **Gold: 400**
- **Allied counties: 1**

Those are preparation bonuses, not four mandatory conditions for reaching the
fight.

My priority is:

**finish the county alliance → protect the 400 Gold target → take the faction
threshold that is easiest to reach → bring the core Knights home for the final
fight.**

If one satisfaction target is still short, I do not ruin a healthy Act 1 route
trying to force it.

<h2 id="act-1-route">
  The Act 1 route I use for a cleaner first clear
</h2>

My biggest Act 1 mistake was treating every audience request and open quest as
if it deserved immediate attention.

It does not.

The early game keeps adding:

- side contracts;
- county problems;
- faction requests;
- Knight conversations;
- investigations;
- equipment;
- short-deadline emergencies.

If I try to clear all of them, I eventually reach the Dragon Knight window with
the wrong Knights away from the Tower and too much Gold tied up in things that
do not help the confrontation.

My first-clear route is:

1. build a usable Round Table instead of trying to optimize every Knight;
2. keep enough Gold flexible for the route that is actually developing;
3. choose **Groveshire** at the first county split;
4. keep its main county chain moving until the alliance is secured;
5. prioritize short deadlines over ordinary rewards;
6. when the Dragon Knight preparation screen appears, stop spending casually;
7. finish the easiest useful preparation targets rather than forcing all four;
8. bring the Knights I actually want for the confrontation back to the Tower;
9. beat the Dragon Knight;
10. decide whether this is the Act 1 timeline I want before crossing the final
    Demon checkpoint.

That route leaves room for an imperfect cycle or failed optional quest.

Act 1 does not need to look perfect before I move on.

<h2 id="early-gold">
  Keep early Gold flexible until the Dragon Knight targets appear
</h2>

I do not try to save every coin in Act 1.

I also do not use the Forge as soon as an item looks slightly better than what I
already own.

The point where my spending changed was the Dragon Knight preparation screen.

In the run I kept, the UI showed:

- **Cycle 4**
- **Targeted Cycle 8**
- **167 Gold**
- **400 Gold target**
- **0/1 allied counties**

That immediately told me what the next four cycles needed to accomplish.

I was still **233 Gold short** of the preparation target, and I had not secured
the county alliance yet.

From that point on, a purchase had to answer a specific question:

**Does this item help me finish a route that matters before Cycle 8?**

If not, I leave it.

A weapon that fixes an actual stat gap on an urgent quest can still be worth
buying.

A general upgrade for a Knight who already has a workable assignment can wait.

The important Act 1 distinction is not “never spend.”

It is:

**do not spend the Gold you are still trying to turn into a Dragon Knight
preparation bonus.**

<h2 id="groveshire-or-gavault">
  Groveshire or Gavault: which route should you choose?
</h2>

For a first clean run, I choose **Groveshire**.

Gavault can still reach an alliance, so this is not a claim that the other route
is a dead end.

The difference is how much uncertainty I have to manage before the Dragon Knight
arrives.

Groveshire gives me a much easier objective to schedule:

**keep investigating the beast problem until the county is willing to ally with
me.**

Gavault becomes a political rebellion with more opportunities for a solution to
create another problem.

In the alternate route I tested, an attempted negotiation pushed the rebellion
back but also resulted in the **young princess dying**. The county could still
be rallied later through a more radical resolution, including the
**Brutal Repression** outcome, but that is not the route I use when my only
goal is reaching Act 2 with a stable board.

That is the entire reason for my recommendation.

I am not choosing Groveshire because every reward is mathematically better.

I am choosing it because I can predict the remaining Act 1 workload more
reliably.

There is one later consequence worth knowing without changing my first-clear
recommendation.

**Minor Act 2 spoiler:** Gideon's personal investigation eventually connects
his past to the old conflict between Groveshire and Gavault. He learns that he
was taken as a child during a Gavault attack on a Groveshire convoy, and later
information suggests his origins may be tied to both counties.

I do not take that as proof that Gavault is secretly the better Act 1 route.

What it tells me is that the Groveshire/Gavault decision has consequences beyond
the immediate county alliance. The game can bring that choice back into later
Knight and political storylines.

For a first clear, I still choose Groveshire because its Act 1 workload is
easier to control.

<h2 id="quest-priority">
  Prioritize deadlines before rewards
</h2>

By the middle of Act 1, I usually have more attractive quests than available
Knights.

I do not sort them by Gold.

I sort them by what disappears first.

My practical order is:

1. one-cycle emergencies;
2. the county route needed for the alliance;
3. Dragon Knight preparation opportunities;
4. Knight-specific progression that is about to disappear;
5. normal contracts with no immediate deadline.

The pirate attack on **Grest** is a good example.

When that appears as a short-deadline threat, I handle it before sending the
same Knights on a normal contract that can still be completed later.

I also stop automatically stacking my two best Knights on one quest.

If one Knight already makes an assignment viable, I would rather keep the
second one free for the next deadline than turn a likely success into an
overwhelming success.

Act 1 rewards having another body available more often than it rewards squeezing
a little more score out of an already-safe contract.

<h2 id="dragon-knight-objectives">
  Read the Dragon Knight preparation screen before using the last cycles
</h2>

The Dragon Knight challenge is the point where I stop playing Act 1 as an
open-ended management game.

On my Groveshire route, the preparation screen asked for:

- **Merchant satisfaction: 10**
- **Noble satisfaction: 8**
- **Gold: 400**
- **at least one allied county**

The most important thing I learned is that these are **bonuses for the
confrontation**, not four gates that all have to be completed.

That changes the final few cycles completely.

If Noble satisfaction is one easy audience decision away, I finish it.

If Merchant satisfaction is still far behind and every available way to raise
it conflicts with stronger progression, I stop chasing it.

In one run I reached **9 Noble satisfaction** while Merchant satisfaction was
still the awkward target.

Trying to bend every remaining choice toward Merchants at that point made less
sense than finishing the county alliance and protecting the Gold I already had.

For my first clear, I value the four targets like this:

**1. Allied county**

If Groveshire is already close, finish it. This is permanent route progression
and a Dragon Knight preparation bonus at the same time.

**2. 400 Gold**

Once I am within reach, I stop buying upgrades that do not solve an immediate
Act 1 problem.

**3. The satisfaction target already closest to completion**

If I can finish one cleanly, I take it.

**4. The remaining satisfaction target**

Useful if the opportunity appears naturally. I do not wreck the route to force
it.

<h2 id="last-cycles">
  Bring the core Knights home before the confrontation
</h2>

The easiest way to waste a good Act 1 route is to use the final cycle as if the
Dragon Knight were still several days away.

I stop starting long optional work.

Before committing the final assignments, I check:

- Which Knights do I actually want in the Dragon Knight party?
- Are any of them still away for more than one cycle?
- Does a current emergency matter more than another ordinary contract?
- Is important equipment sitting on somebody who will not fight?
- Is anyone in the final party vulnerable enough that the confrontation could
  remove them?

This is where I am willing to spend Gold again.

Not to make the inventory look clean.

To fix a concrete final-party weakness.

The general equipment and armor rules belong in the
<Link href="/sovereign-tower">
  {" "}Sovereign Tower guide hub
</Link>
For this fight, I only care about whether the actual party I am sending can
survive and cover the displayed requirements.

I also make a save before locking the confrontation.

If the final assignment is bad, I want to change the **party setup**, not replay
half the Act.

<h2 id="act-1-checkpoint">
  Decide whether to keep this timeline before Act 2
</h2>

After the Dragon Knight sequence, the Demon gives one final opportunity to
reconsider Act 1.

I take this checkpoint more seriously now because some information that changes
how I understand an Act 1 decision does not appear until later.

The clearest example in my route came from **Gideon**.

During Act 2, his personal investigation revealed that he had been taken as a
child during a **Gavault attack on a Groveshire convoy**. Further information
connected his past to the long-running tension between the two counties.

That immediately made the old Groveshire/Gavault choice feel relevant again.

The problem was that I learned it too late.

By then, the county choice belonged to Act 1, while my current Demon Power
window could no longer reach that far back.

That is why I do not treat the post-Dragon-Knight warning as a routine
confirmation screen.

At this checkpoint, I ask:

- Did a Knight die that I genuinely want to save?
- Did the county route end in a result I specifically want to change?
- Did I learn something **during Act 1** that gives me a clear earlier
  intervention?
- Am I comfortable carrying my Groveshire or Gavault decision into later
  character and political routes?
- Do I deliberately want to test the other county route?

If the answer is no, I commit.

A missing faction bonus, a little lost Gold, or a failed normal contract is not
enough reason for me to rebuild Act 1.

The important distinction is:

**later knowledge can make an earlier decision more meaningful without giving
me access to that earlier timeline again.**

Once I accept the timeline, Act 2 begins and I do not assume I will be able to

<h2 id="act-1-mistakes">
  Act 1 mistakes that actually changed my route
</h2>

### Leaving the Groveshire alliance until the final cycles

The county alliance is both real progression and one of the Dragon Knight
preparation targets.

If Groveshire is already moving, I finish it before low-priority contracts.

### Expecting one Gavault negotiation to finish the rebellion

My alternate route kept escalating after what looked like a possible resolution.

I leave more cycle room for Gavault if I deliberately choose that branch.

### Treating all four Dragon Knight targets as mandatory

They improve the confrontation.

They do not all need to be green before the fight can happen.

### Sending too many core Knights away near Target Cycle 8

A good optional quest is still badly timed if the Knight I need for the Dragon
Knight will not return.

### Spending toward a hypothetical upgrade while still short of 400 Gold

Once the preparation target appears, I only buy something if it fixes a current
Act 1 problem.

### Rewinding because the preparation screen was imperfect

I rewind for a route result I know how to change.

I do not rewind merely because one satisfaction number could have been higher.
