<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
When the **King Slayer** appears in Act 2 and warns that it will return in
**five circles**, I stop treating the Act as open-ended.

My preparation route is:

**stop starting long optional quests → finish only near-complete permanent
rewards → follow useful King Slayer preparation leads → keep the core roster
available → clear overlapping undead emergencies → wait for the final King
Slayer resolution choice → choose the route that fits my roster → repair for
the displayed Damage Risk → redistribute equipment → resolve the encounter →
take the Spectral Eye → stop at the Demon checkpoint before Act 3.**

The most important new detail is that the final King Slayer screen can offer
**different resolution routes**.

In my run, I selected **King Slayer purification**.

That route:

- required **4 Knights**
- showed **2–5 Damage Risk**
- rewarded a relic
- had lower risk than another visible route that required **all 6 Knights**

I did not fully test the 6-Knight alternative, so I do not call purification
the universally best route.

My successful purification party used **Rufus, Gideon, Aaron, and Ursula**,
but I would not copy that lineup blindly. The available stats and discovered
attributes can differ.

If I already have the **Glass Blade**, I keep it available. Its item description
gives **+1 Agility, +2 Magic, and a passive**, and says it is often used to seal
demons and spirits. It produced a useful interaction during a King Slayer
resolution I tested.

After the successful encounter, I received the **Spectral Eye**, which gives
**+3 MAG**.

Do not rush into Act 3 afterward. The Demon explicitly gives one final
**now-or-never** chance to revisit the end of Act 2.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

<h2 id="king-slayer-route">
  The King Slayer route I use from five circles to Act 3
</h2>

The King Slayer changes how I play the rest of Act 2.

Before the warning, I am comfortable sending useful Knights away for several
cycles when a county route, recruit, or relic is worth it.

After the warning, I get much stricter.

My sequence is:

1. stop opening long optional routes from zero;
2. finish permanent rewards that are already close to completion;
3. follow King Slayer-specific preparation leads that are still available;
4. bring my core Knights back toward the Tower;
5. leave enough capacity for late emergency quests;
6. preserve specialized equipment instead of wasting it on ordinary contracts;
7. wait for the actual final King Slayer resolution screen;
8. compare the available routes before choosing;
9. build the party for the route I actually selected;
10. repair around its displayed maximum Damage Risk;
11. redistribute weapons, mounts, and food around that party;
12. resolve the King Slayer encounter;
13. collect the Spectral Eye;
14. stop at the Demon warning before committing to Act 3.

circles belongs in the

<h2 id="five-circles">
  Five circles means I stop starting long optional work
</h2>

The warning is real scheduling pressure, not flavor dialogue.

When the King Slayer appears, I still finish something if it is already one
step from a permanent result such as:

- an allied county;
- a useful recruit;
- an important relic;
- a personal-route payoff I know I want.

What I stop doing is starting another three-cycle chain just because its reward
looks interesting.

My decision becomes:

**Will this improve the roster, equipment, support, or permanent state I can
actually use before the King Slayer returns?**

If the answer is no, I leave it.

That is especially important because the final stretch can still generate
other problems.

Five circles does not mean the board becomes quiet.

<h2 id="king-slayer-support">
  I follow the sorceress lead instead of rushing straight to the final fight
</h2>

After the King Slayer warning, the game explicitly suggests finding additional
help.

One clue points toward the realm's powerful sorceress, who is away to the east.

I followed that lead.

That eventually brought **Belelladana** into my Tower.

Her immediate King Slayer value in my route was practical rather than a hidden
boss mechanic:

**she gave me access to potions that could temporarily shore up Knight stats.**

I do not call Belelladana mandatory.

I have not verified that failing to summon her blocks every King Slayer
resolution.

What I can confirm is that the game deliberately points toward outside help,
and in my run that help expanded the temporary stat options I had available
before the final encounter.

That is enough for me to prioritize the lead while the countdown is still
comfortable.

<h2 id="late-emergencies">

The countdown overlaps with ordinary Act 2 pressure.

In one of my runs, **Army of the Dead** appeared while I was already thinking
about the final King Slayer setup.

That assignment showed:

- **2–3 Damage Risk**
- **80 Gold**

The 2–3 risk itself is not terrifying.

The real cost is sending:

- my best Magic Knight;
- someone carrying specialized equipment;

So I stop filling every available Round Table slot.

A slightly weaker result on an ordinary emergency is acceptable if it protects
the four or six Knights I may soon need for the Act-ending problem.

<h2 id="king-slayer-final-choice">
  The final King Slayer screen gives more than one resolution
</h2>

This is the biggest difference from how I originally understood the encounter.

When the countdown finally ended, the game did **not** simply throw one fixed
boss assignment onto the Round Table.

I received a final decision.

One visible option allowed me to:

**purify the King Slayer.**

Another visible route required a larger party.

On my screen:

- the **purification route needed 4 Knights**;

I do **not** conclude from one run that:

> purification is always the best King Slayer choice.

I did not finish and compare every alternative resolution.

The useful rule is instead:

**wait until you can see the final routes, then build for the route you actually
choose.**

That is much more efficient than repairing and equipping six Knights before I
even know whether my selected solution needs six.

The route I tested was **King Slayer purification**.

It used:

**4 Knight slots**

and displayed:

**Damage Risk: 2–5**

That changes two things immediately.

First, I do not need to commit the entire roster.

I can leave other Knights free rather than forcing all six into the final
assignment.

Second, **5 damage** becomes my armor threshold.

<h2 id="king-slayer-repair">
  Repair for the possible 5 damage, not because every armor bar looks imperfect
</h2>

The final assignment is where I become willing to spend heavily on armor.

I inspect the four Knights I am actually sending.

For each one, I check:

- current survivable armor;
- the possible **5-damage** result;
- whether losing that Knight matters beyond this assignment;
- the stats required by the purification;
- any attributes that change how they behave on this type of quest.

If a five-damage result can take down a Knight I need, I repair.

If the Knight is already comfortably outside that danger range, I do not repair
them purely to make the bar look full.

That distinction matters.

I may have spent much of Act 2 refusing unnecessary repairs on 0- or 1-damage
assignments.

This is exactly the kind of quest I was saving that Gold for.

**2–5 Damage Risk is high enough that I do not gamble a required final-party

<h2 id="king-slayer-stats">
  I solve the purification's weak stats instead of copying one universal build
</h2>

I do not recommend one fixed King Slayer stat template.

In my purification setup, **Magic was important enough that I actively shored it
up**, but Strength and Wit also mattered when I was deciding which four Knights
fit the assignment best.

The better method is:

**choose the party → identify the weak contributions → move equipment to those
weak points.**

That also prevents me from over-investing in one spectacular Knight.

If one character already covers a requirement comfortably, another +2 in that
same stat may add less value than moving the item to the borderline member.

One subtle example from my run was **Aaron**.

His later character state gave him a significant bonus on combat quests and a
penalty on other work.

But the final route I chose was a **purification**, not a normal combat quest.

So I did not assume:

> Aaron has a combat bonus, therefore he automatically dominates King Slayer.

I still looked at the actual assignment contribution.

That is exactly why I trust the quest screen more than character labels.

<h2 id="final-party">
  Make the final party first, then redistribute equipment
</h2>

By late Act 2, I have started associating certain weapons and mounts with
particular Knights.

I ignore that for the King Slayer.

I do not ask:

> Who normally carries this item?

I ask:

> Who needs this item for my chosen King Slayer resolution?

My final preparation order is:

1. select the resolution;
2. choose the Knights that fit it;
3. check the maximum Damage Risk;
4. repair only the actual final-party danger cases;
5. remove useful equipment from Knights who are not going;
6. cover the weak stats in the selected party;
7. assign mounts where they still matter;
8. keep the Glass Blade on a relevant final-party Knight if I have it;
9. use consumables only where they improve a borderline contribution;
10. check the assignment one last time;
11. save;
12. commit.

My successful purification party did not require a perfect Act 2.

I had already made compromises elsewhere.

The important thing was that the **final four** worked.

Before continuing, I check only major outcomes:

- Is the Tower murder timeline acceptable?
- Is there a Knight loss or dismissal I genuinely want to change?
- Did I abandon a county route that matters to the next Act?
- Is there a personal-route result I already know how to improve?
- Am I happy with the Knights and alliances I am carrying forward?

If yes, I commit.

If there is a structural problem and I already know the earlier intervention
that fixes it, this is where I consider

I do not rewind Act 2 because:

- one normal quest only got Success instead of Great Success;
- I missed a small amount of Gold;
- a faction bar could be slightly higher;
- someone took avoidable but non-fatal damage.

Those are normal imperfections.

Once I continue past the warning, Act 2 ends.
