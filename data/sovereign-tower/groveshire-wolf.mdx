<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
To get the **Wolf** in Sovereign Tower, I choose **Groveshire**, keep the beast
investigation moving until it reaches the **Black Forest**, learn that the
supposed monster is actually a wolf, and use **Angelica** for the humane
resolution.

The route I confirmed is:

**choose Groveshire → follow the beast investigation → Black Forest → reveal the
beast is a wolf → send Angelica → secure the Groveshire alliance → let the Wolf
follow her back to the Tower → accept him as a Round Table Knight.**

That recruitment gives me the **Good Boy** achievement.

The route does not necessarily end there.

Later, once **Belelladana** examines the Wolf, she reveals that he is actually a
**human trapped under a powerful curse**. I chose to let her remove the curse.
The Wolf temporarily left the Round Table and later returned as **Sir Rufus**,
the former Beast of Groveshire.

The two forms play differently:

- **Wolf form:** strong Wit and Agility, unusual party-affinity behavior, and
  his own built-in mount with **-2 cycle duration**
- **Rufus:** no longer has the Wolf's built-in mount, but later reveals
  **Wolf Habits**, which reduces his quest duration by **1 cycle** and can be
  combined with a normal mount

If you only want **Good Boy**, you do not need to wait for the curse storyline.
The achievement comes from recruiting the Wolf.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

<h2 id="groveshire-wolf-route">
  The Groveshire Wolf route from county problem to recruitment
</h2>

This page starts after I have decided to support **Groveshire**.

If the real question is still whether Groveshire or Gavault is the better Act 1
choice, I use the
<Link href="/sovereign-tower/act-1-best-route">
  {" "}Act 1 best route guide
</Link>
instead.

For the Wolf route itself, my sequence is:

1. choose Groveshire at the county split;
2. keep following the beast investigation;
3. reach the Black Forest lead;
4. learn what the supposed beast really is;
5. keep Angelica available for the final resolution;
6. use her for the humane outcome;
7. secure the Groveshire alliance;
8. continue past the alliance scene;
9. accept the Wolf when he reaches the Tower;
10. keep progressing his later conversations if I want to investigate the curse.

There are therefore two separate milestones.

The first is:

**Groveshire becomes an allied county.**

The second is:

**the Wolf joins the Round Table.**

I do not stop after the first one.

<h2 id="send-angelica">
  Angelica gave me the tame-and-recruit outcome
</h2>

I kept Angelica available as the Groveshire investigation approached its final
step.

Sending her produced the result I wanted:

- the Wolf survived;
- the Groveshire beast problem was resolved;
- Groveshire became an ally;
- the Wolf followed Angelica back to the Tower.

I deliberately do not claim that every other Knight automatically kills the
Wolf or fails the route.

I have not tested every possible final assignment.

What I can confirm is narrower:

**Angelica gave me the clean alliance + Wolf recruitment outcome.**

If that is the route I want, I do not send her away on a long unrelated quest
while Groveshire is waiting for its final resolution.

<h2 id="recruit-wolf">
  Accept the Wolf when he asks to join the Round Table
</h2>

After the county problem is resolved, the Wolf follows Angelica back to the
Tower.

He is then offered as an actual Knight.

I accept him.

The game treats the scene playfully, but the recruit is mechanically real.

The Wolf receives:

- his own stats;
- his own preferences;
- his own attributes;
- normal assignment participation;
- unusual mobility and affinity mechanics.

This is also where I got the route's most obvious achievement payoff.

<h2 id="good-boy">
  Good Boy unlocks from recruiting the Wolf
</h2>

The **Good Boy** achievement asks me to:

**Get a truly good boy at your side.**

In my route, it unlocked through the Wolf recruitment outcome.

The path I used was:

**Groveshire → beast investigation → Black Forest → Wolf reveal → Angelica →
Groveshire alliance → Wolf reaches Tower → accept him.**

I did not need to:

- level the Wolf;
- complete a certain number of assignments with him;
- discover his later human identity;
- cure the curse.

So if **Good Boy** is the only objective, the route is effectively complete as
soon as the Wolf successfully joins.

<h2 id="wolf-stats">
  The Wolf is strongest as a fast Wit and Agility specialist
</h2>

The two stats that immediately stood out to me were:

- **Wit**
- **Agility**

His known preferences also include work such as:

- hunting;
- assassination.

I therefore start by looking at investigations, hunts, scouting-style work, and
other assignments where those strengths matter.

I do not turn him into a generic fighter just because he is an unusual recruit.

His most useful advantage is not one giant combat stat.

It is the combination of:

**good specialist stats + unusual movement + unique party behavior.**

<h2 id="wolf-mount">
  Wolf form has a built-in -2 cycle-duration mount
</h2>

This is one of the most useful parts of the original Wolf form.

The Wolf does not need a normal horse.

His profile effectively treats his own movement as a mount and shows:

**Cycle duration reduction: -2**

That makes him especially valuable on travel-heavy assignments.

A quest that would normally remove another Knight for several cycles can become
much easier to fit around a deadline.

It also means I can leave my normal horses available for the rest of the Round
Table.

While he remains a wolf, that is one of the biggest reasons I use him.

<h2 id="wolf-affinity">
  Wolf affinity depends on the Knights assigned with him
</h2>

The Wolf also behaves differently from a normal Knight when it comes to
affinity.

I cannot adjust his affinity in the usual way.

Instead, his score receives a modification based on the **average affinity of
the other party members assigned with him**.

That means team composition matters even after the stat requirements are
already satisfied.

If two parties both solve the assignment, I prefer putting the Wolf with
Knights whose affinity is already healthy.

I treat this as a Wolf-form mechanic.

Once his later curse route changes him into Rufus, I re-check his profile
instead of assuming every Wolf-specific rule survives unchanged.

<h2 id="wolf-curse">
  Belelladana eventually reveals that the Wolf was human

The biggest surprise in the route happens later.

Once **Belelladana** is present in my Tower, she eventually examines the Wolf.

She does not treat him as an unusually intelligent animal.

She identifies him as:

**a human trapped under a powerful curse.**

<h2 id="break-curse">
  I chose to break the Wolf's curse
</h2>

I tested the cure route.

I told Belelladana to treat him.

The Wolf approved the decision and temporarily left the Round Table while she
worked on the curse.

That temporary departure matters.

If my roster is already stretched thin, I do not trigger the cure casually in
the middle of a deadline-heavy cycle.

The Wolf is unavailable while this transition is happening.

I have **not** played the keep-him-as-a-Wolf branch far enough to compare its
later rewards.

So I do not claim that curing him is universally better.

The confirmed tradeoff is:

**keep current Wolf form**

or:

**temporarily lose him and continue into Rufus's human route.**

For my run, I chose the second.

He introduces himself as **Rufus** and explicitly identifies himself as the
former Beast of Groveshire.

His profile also reveals that he came from an old Groveshire noble family.

Mechanically, this is not just a portrait swap.

His Knight setup changes.

The first things I noticed were:

- his stats were different from Wolf form;
- Strength was much more prominent;
- his old built-in four-legged movement was gone;
- the game let me distribute stat points again.

So I treat Rufus almost like rebuilding an existing recruit.

I do not blindly copy the equipment plan I used for the Wolf.

I inspect the new profile and decide what role I want the human version to fill.

The game also allowed me to refuse Rufus when he returned.

I accepted him.

<h2 id="rufus-wolf-habits">
  Rufus loses the Wolf mount but can unlock Wolf Habits
</h2>

The human transformation initially looks like a mobility downgrade.

Rufus now needs a normal mount.

He no longer has the Wolf form's built-in:

**-2 cycle duration**

But later I investigated his remaining **Wolf Habits**.

That revealed a new personal effect:

**quests take 1 less cycle to complete.**

That creates a different kind of scheduling tool.

Wolf form gives me a powerful built-in movement bonus without consuming a
horse.

Rufus gives me:

**personal -1 cycle duration + access to a normal mount.**

That means I can stack his personal speed with an actual mount when the
assignment allows it.

There is one limitation I keep in mind.

On a multi-Knight quest, Rufus finishing quickly does not automatically make
the slower members disappear from the assignment equally fast.

So I get the most value when the entire assigned party can keep pace.

I do not waste his speed by pairing him with a team that remains unavailable
for much longer anyway.

<h2 id="wolf-route-stuck">
  What I check when the Groveshire Wolf or Rufus route seems stuck
</h2>

### I chose Groveshire but have not seen the Wolf

Keep progressing the beast investigation.

The recruit does not appear directly from the first county audience.

### The investigation still calls it a beast

Continue until the route reaches the Black Forest and reveals what the creature
actually is.

### Who should I send for the humane outcome?

Angelica is the route I personally confirmed.

I have not verified that every other Knight fails.

### Groveshire is already allied but the Wolf did not join

Keep progressing.

The county alliance happens before the later Tower recruitment scene.

### How do I get Good Boy?

Finish the Wolf recruitment.

In my route, **Good Boy** unlocked when the Wolf joined my side.

### Do I need to cure the Wolf for Good Boy?

No.

I already had the achievement before the later curse storyline.

### Why is Belelladana talking about the Wolf?

She can identify that the Wolf was originally human and is trapped under a
powerful curse.

### Should I break the Wolf's curse?

I tested the cure route.

It temporarily removes the Wolf from my roster and later brings him back as
Sir Rufus.

I have not completed the alternate keep-Wolf branch far enough to call one
choice universally better.

### My Wolf disappeared after I chose the cure

That happened in my route.

He temporarily leaves while the curse is being removed.

Continue playing.

### Who is Rufus?

Rufus is the human identity of the Beast of Groveshire.

He returns after Belelladana removes the curse.

### Why are Rufus's stats different?

The transformation changes his Knight setup.

In my run, his stats changed and I was given stat points to distribute again.

I rebuild him instead of assuming the old Wolf build still applies.

### Why does Rufus need a horse now?

He no longer has the Wolf form's built-in mount.

Human Rufus uses normal mounts.

### Did Rufus lose all of the Wolf's speed?

No.

Investigating **Wolf Habits** revealed that his quests take **1 less cycle**.

That personal reduction can be combined with a normal mount.
