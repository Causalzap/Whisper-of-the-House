Transport Fever 3 has eight campaign missions, and the first six keep changing
what counts as a successful network. They move from **New Orleans in 1906** to
the Alps in **1920**, **Wadi Rum in 1956**, **Bethel in 1969**, Norway in
**1971**, and finally the Philippines in **1999**.

A solution that was efficient in the previous mission can become a distraction
in the next one. Saving Mardi Gras is mostly sequential. Alpine Crossing keeps
the calendar running while rail capacity is rebuilt. Desert Adventure can
destroy trucks on a perfectly connected road. Biggest Festival Ever stacks
temporary deliveries on top of passenger growth. Oil for the People introduces
a live competitor, and Island Expansion replaces one fixed build order with a
series of timed contracts spread across several islands.

Read the objective counter before committing money. Build enough to advance that
counter, then decide whether the infrastructure still has a useful job after the
objective changes.


<h2
  id="mission-1-saving-mardi-gras"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Mission 1: Saving Mardi Gras
</h2>

**Saving Mardi Gras** takes place in **New Orleans in 1906** after a storm has
damaged roads, disrupted lumber production, and threatened the festival.

This mission introduces the basic road, depot, cargo, and passenger tools
without requiring a large permanent network. Most objectives arrive in a fixed
sequence, so building far ahead rarely helps.

The useful order is:

**alligators → washed-out road → 6 wood → Lakeshore bus extension → parade**


<h3
  id="saving-mardi-gras-alligators"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Find All 5 Alligators at the Logging Camp
</h3>

The logging camp cannot resume normal operation until the alligators around it
have been found.

There are **5 alligators** to click. Search the immediate logging-camp area
carefully rather than moving on to road construction too quickly. They appear
around the street near the gate, the trees, a shack, the logs, and the camp
area itself.

becomes useful for the next cargo task.


<h3
  id="saving-mardi-gras-road"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Restore the Washed-Out Road
</h3>

The next blocker is the damaged road connection.

Remove the fallen trees and rebuild the missing road section where the storm
washed it away. There is no need to redesign the surrounding streets; the
mission only needs the damaged connection restored.

Once the road is open, the hotel construction objective begins.


<h3
  id="saving-mardi-gras-wood"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Deliver 6 Wood to the Hotel Construction Site
</h3>

Build the Road Depot when prompted and buy a wagon that can carry wood.

The cargo line runs from the **logging camp to the French Quarter hotel
construction site**. The requirement is **6 wood**.

The depot is only where the wagon is purchased. The line itself starts at the
logging camp.

That distinction matters because industries can provide their own usable
loading access. The broader loading and terminal rules are covered in the
<Link href="/transport-fever-3/cargo-industry-guide">cargo and industry guide</Link>.


<h3
  id="saving-mardi-gras-lakeshore"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Extend the Existing Bus Line to Lakeshore
</h3>

When the musicians around Lakeshore need transport, use the passenger network
that already exists.

Add the Lakeshore stop to the current New Orleans bus line. The existing coaches
can handle the extension, so there is no need to create another passenger fleet
for this objective unless the current route has genuinely become overloaded.

The objective completes through passenger movement, not by simply placing the
stop.


<h3
  id="saving-mardi-gras-parade"

>
  Run the Parade From City Park Along Canal Street
</h3>

The final transport task is the Mardi Gras parade.

Create the requested line from **City Park along Canal Street**, then assign the
parade vehicles waiting in the depot beside the French Quarter Hotel.

into a large free-play network. Completing the requested tasks cleanly is enough.


<h2
  id="mission-2-alpine-crossing"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Mission 2: Alpine Crossing
</h2>

**Alpine Crossing** moves to Italy in **1920** and changes the pressure
completely.

The railway already has a job. Your challenge is to expand the Simplon route
while continuing to move passengers through it.

The central requirement is:

**Transport 300 passengers through the tunnel.**

For completion, finish before **1924**. For the three-star time target, reach
the requirement before the end of **1922**.

There is also a separate skier objective involving **Brig and Zermatt**.


<h3
  id="alpine-crossing-plan"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Pause When a New Rail Objective Appears
</h3>

The clock keeps moving while you experiment with the railway.

Pause before major track work, inspect exactly which section the mission is
asking you to change, and make the construction while time is stopped whenever
possible.

The 300-passenger counter cares about tunnel crossings. Spending months on
extra track that does not increase those crossings makes the three-star target
harder for no benefit.


<h3
  id="alpine-crossing-construction"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Keep the Tunnel Project Supplied
</h3>

The tunnel expansion creates a temporary logistics network alongside the
passenger railway.

Construction materials and vehicles need to keep the project moving while
passenger operations continue. If progress on the tunnel slows, inspect the
construction deliveries before assuming the passenger trains are the problem.

This temporary network only needs to exist long enough to complete the tunnel
work. Once the new track is open, the construction vehicles no longer need to
remain in service.


<h3
  id="alpine-crossing-second-train"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Add the Second Passenger Train When Capacity Falls Behind
</h3>

The first passenger train eventually stops being enough for the mission's
throughput.

When the objective calls for another train, add it rather than waiting for the
existing service to clear the growing passenger count by itself.

The second train is also the point where train interaction matters. One train
can use a simple line with very little control; two trains need the requested
siding and signal work before the route can move them efficiently.


<h3
  id="alpine-crossing-signals"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Place 3 Signals in Each Direction South of Domodossola
</h3>

The siding south of Domodossola has a specific campaign requirement:

**3 signals in one direction + 3 signals in the opposite direction.**

<figure className="my-8">
  <Image
    src="/images/transport-fever-3/transport-fever-3-alpine-crossing-signals.webp"

  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">
    Complete the requested three signals in each direction so the siding can handle the additional train traffic.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

Match the direction requested by the objective. This is not the place to add
extra signals simply because there is empty track.

If a train later reports no path or the signal direction is unclear, the
<Link href="/transport-fever-3/rail-signals-guide">rail and signals guide</Link>
covers the underlying track and signal behavior.


<h3
  id="alpine-crossing-electrification"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Electrify the Required Route Before Replacing the Train
</h3>

The next railway change introduces the electric Crocodile.

Electrify the track the replacement service will actually use, including its
station approaches and necessary connecting sections, before replacing the
existing passenger train.

There is no advantage in electrifying unrelated branches while the timed
passenger target is still running.


<h3
  id="alpine-crossing-zermatt"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Transport Skiers From Brig to Zermatt
</h3>

The optional skier objective requires a real passenger connection to Zermatt.

Build the branch, place the Zermatt station, create the line from Brig, and
assign a passenger train.

<figure className="my-8">
  <Image
    src="/images/transport-fever-3/transport-fever-3-alpine-crossing-zermatt.webp"

    height={900}

  />

  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">
    Track reaching Zermatt is not enough; the objective needs passengers to make the trip.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

A simple single-track branch is enough when it only needs to carry the objective
service. Do not duplicate the main tunnel railway just for this side route.


<h3
  id="alpine-crossing-finish"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Keep the Final Passenger Trips Focused on the Tunnel
</h3>

Once tunnel construction is complete, remove temporary construction vehicles
that no longer serve a purpose and concentrate on the passenger counter.

The objective is **300 passengers through the tunnel**, not maximum railway
coverage across the whole map.

If trains are cycling slowly, check what they are waiting for before buying
several more. A second or third train only increases throughput when the line
has enough room for it to move.

For three stars, the difficult part is usually the calendar. Time lost while
building and correcting the railway is much harder to recover than the final
few passenger trips.


<h2
  id="mission-3-desert-adventure"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Mission 3: Desert Adventure
</h2>

**Desert Adventure** takes place in **Wadi Rum, Jordan in 1956**.

The expedition has three conditions worth remembering from the moment the map
opens:

- complete the excavations;
- lose **fewer than 3 trucks** before the excavation finishes;
- lose **no trucks** for the stronger star condition;
- recover the remains for the special reward.

The road itself is not the danger. The gaps between maintenance areas are.


<h3
  id="desert-adventure-first-camp"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Deliver 5 Tools to Establish the First Maintenance Camp
</h3>

The expedition cannot safely push deep into the desert from Aqaba immediately.

Start by delivering **5 tools** to the marked location outside Aqaba. That
establishes the first useful maintenance point on the route.

  <Image

  </figcaption>

A single vehicle is enough for this opening delivery. Sending the entire convoy
ahead before maintenance coverage exists only increases the number of trucks at
risk.


<h3
  id="desert-adventure-condition"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Use Maintenance Camps as Stepping Stones
</h3>

Trucks begin losing condition when they remain outside maintenance coverage.

The safe route is therefore not one long uninterrupted drive from Aqaba to the
final excavation. Advance from one protected area to the next.

A short uncovered gap is survivable when the next camp is close. A long
unexplored gap is different: by the time the condition warning becomes serious,
turning around may leave the truck even farther from safety.

<figure className="my-8">

  </figcaption>
</figure>

When another camp becomes available, work it into the expedition route before
sending trucks farther into the desert.

If a vehicle is already losing condition, head for the nearest reachable
maintenance area rather than automatically sending it all the way back to
Aqaba.


<h3
  id="desert-adventure-remains"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Recover the Remains Before Leaving the Area
</h3>

The special reward comes from recovering the remains discovered during the
expedition.

Handle this side objective while the convoy and maintenance route are already
operating nearby. Waiting until the final excavation is complete gives you no
advantage and can force an unnecessary return trip.

  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Use the Terrain Tool to Find the 3 Artifacts

The final excavation is not another transport delivery.

Once the highlighted dig area appears, use the terrain tool directly inside it
to uncover the artifacts. The objective tracks **3 finds**.

<figure className="my-8">

A clean run can finish with all three stars and the special reward. The safest
path is to establish each maintenance stage first, protect truck condition,
recover the remains while nearby, and only then finish the excavation.


<h2
  id="mission-4-biggest-festival-ever"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Mission 4: Biggest Festival Ever
</h2>

**Biggest Festival Ever** takes place in **Bethel, United States in 1969**.

The attendance targets are:

- **500 attendees** for completion;
- **750 attendees** for the next star target;
- **1,000 attendees** for three stars.

The special objective is to find the missing instruments.

Unlike the earlier missions, this one keeps several active systems running at
once. The campground needs construction material, food, medicine, waste
removal, passenger access, storm supplies, and enough road capacity for all of
those services to coexist.


<h3
  id="festival-stage"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Deliver 200 Wood to Build the Stage
</h3>

The festival cannot proceed until a proper stage is built.

The stage construction site needs **200 wood** from the nearby lumber producer.
Set up the delivery first and let those vehicles finish their job before
treating the campground like a fully developed transport network.

Once the stage delivery is complete, the temporary wood line no longer needs to
keep all of its original vehicles. Reassigning vehicles to the next active
deliveries keeps the campground from filling with obsolete traffic.


<h3
  id="festival-food-and-medicine"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Keep the Campground Supplied Without Letting One Terminal Stop Everything
</h3>

Food and pharmaceutical deliveries become important as the campground grows.

The mission can look healthy while one supply is moving and another is stuck.
If trucks begin piling up, check whether several lines are all waiting for the
same loading or unloading position before adding more vehicles.

Use alternative platforms when they are already available and the queue is
clearly forming at the terminal.

If the problem is no longer specific to this mission, the
<Link href="/transport-fever-3/cargo-industry-guide">cargo and industry guide</Link>
covers terminal use, unloading, storage, and line-rate problems in detail.


<h3
  id="festival-sewage"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Keep a Continuous Sewage Service Running
</h3>

The toilets are not finished when they are placed.

The mission eventually requires a **continuous transport service between the
festival toilets and the sewage plant south of the campground**. The objective
tracks **30 wastewater**.

Make sure the waste can actually be picked up from the toilet area and delivered
to the plant. Once the service is active, leave it running while the campground
continues to grow.


<h3
  id="festival-traffic"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Open Another Crossing When Every Festival Line Uses the Same Road
</h3>

By the middle of the mission, several deliveries are competing for a small road
network.

A second bridge can help when it gives major festival routes a genuinely
different path across the area. The value comes from separating traffic that
would otherwise use the same crossing, not from the bridge itself.

Watch which lines are creating the queue before spending on another crossing.

For general bypasses, junctions, lane rules, and freight routing, use the

</h3>

The largest avoidable delay in Mission 4 is waiting too long to bring passengers
into the campground.

Supplies can keep the site developing, but public transport noticeably speeds
the attendance climb. Starting it only when the festival is already close to
the final target leaves you waiting for population growth at the end.

A successful run can move through the final few attendees very quickly once

<h3

>
  Deliver 240 Blankets During the Storm
</h3>

The storm introduces a temporary blanket requirement.

The mission asks for **240 blankets**. Move them from the textile producer toward
the festival, using the available rail and road connections as needed.

This is not permanent freight demand.

Once the storm task is complete, remove or reassign vehicles that were serving
only the blanket line. Leaving one-off mission vehicles circulating adds traffic
to roads that are already carrying food, medicine, sewage, and passengers.

<h2
  id="mission-5-oil-for-the-people"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Mission 5: Oil for the People
</h2>

**Oil for the People** takes place in **Norway in 1971** and introduces a
competitor who is working toward the same oil license.

The main race is simple to describe and much harder to execute: deliver
**1,000 oil to the inland refinery before Astrid**.

The tighter star targets depend on Astrid's progress:

- finish before Astrid reaches **750 oil** for two stars;
- finish before Astrid reaches **500 oil** for three stars.

The separate special objective is to rescue the lighthouse keeper.

This mission punishes slow setup more than imperfect-looking infrastructure.
Pause when a new construction task appears, spend only on the part that moves
oil toward the refinery, and avoid waiting for a beautiful final network before
the first revenue-producing delivery exists.


<h3
  id="oil-people-prospecting"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Prospect Enough Oil Before You Apply for the License
</h3>

The survey ship starts by testing the marked buoys.

Anchor at a buoy and let the drilling finish before moving to the next one.
Some sites are poor, while others reveal usable reservoirs. Finding more sites
gives you more extraction potential later, but every extra reservoir also means
another platform to build and another place that needs transport.

The yellow buoys are the straightforward searches. Red buoys sit in Astrid's
territory and can also hide strong reservoirs, but reaching them means working
around her patrol boats.

<figure className="my-8">
  <Image

  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">
    Additional successful test drills increase the number of reservoirs available once the race begins.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

Do not spend forever searching simply because more buoys remain. Once you have
enough viable reservoirs to support the race, apply for the extraction license
and move into construction. Astrid is already building while you are still
surveying.


<h3
  id="oil-people-steel"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Deliver 125 Steel to Get the Oil Platforms Built
</h3>

The license application does not immediately give you working platforms.

The dry dock needs **125 steel** before the offshore platforms can be deployed.
The awkward terrain makes the land leg the first real delay: an existing
passenger train shares part of the corridor, steep grades slow heavy cargo, and
the final transfer still has to reach the dry dock.

  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">

  </figcaption>
</figure>

You only need enough rail and road capacity to keep steel moving. If the cargo
train is spending most of its trip waiting behind the passenger service, add
passing opportunities where they actually shorten the cycle instead of
double-tracking the entire map immediately.

For signal direction, crossovers, and trains that cannot find a usable path,
use the
<Link href="/transport-fever-3/rail-signals-guide">rail and signals guide</Link>.


<h3
  id="oil-people-supply-chain"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Get the First Oil Moving Before You Build the Perfect Supply Chain
</h3>

Once the platforms are ready, the race changes again. The license goes to the
first side that delivers **1,000 oil to the inland refinery**.

A limited working chain is better than a large unfinished one. Get oil from at
least one productive platform onto a tanker, bring it to shore, and connect that
transfer to the inland refinery. The first delivery starts generating money that
can fund the next ship, train, storage expansion, or platform connection.

Do not duplicate every vehicle at once. Watch where the oil is waiting.

If offshore storage is filling, the tanker leg is behind. If oil is piling up
onshore, the inland leg is behind. If the train is empty while ships arrive
full, check the transfer and storage connection before buying another train.

The general loading, warehouse, and transfer rules are covered in the
<Link href="/transport-fever-3/cargo-industry-guide">cargo and industry guide</Link>.


<h3
  id="oil-people-lighthouse"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Rescue the Lighthouse Keeper While the Oil Ships Are Already Moving
</h3>

A distress call arrives after the platform stage. The lighthouse keeper has been
stranded for **3 days** and needs a boat ride back to the mainland.

Do this while the maritime network is already active rather than leaving the
special objective until the final oil delivery.

The rescue has a practical payoff as well. The follow-up introduces a ship
improvement, and the fleet later runs **30% faster**. In a mission decided by
how quickly oil can move from several offshore platforms to one refinery, that
speed increase is worth taking before the final race becomes tight.


<h3
  id="oil-people-workers"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Bring Workers to the Platforms When Oil Production Falls Behind Transport
</h3>

Later in the race, workers from the nearby settlement can be transported to the
oil platforms.

That is useful only when production is the part holding you back. If tankers are
already leaving oil behind at the platforms, worker transport can raise the
amount available for the shipping leg. If ships and trains are the bottleneck,
more production simply creates a larger offshore queue.

The mission also offers a machine upgrade path for the drilling operation. It
can help when the race is still close, but it is not worth building a long new
production chain if the existing platforms already hold enough oil to reach
1,000 and the real delay is transport.


<h3
  id="oil-people-final-race"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Watch Astrid's Counter During the Final 1,000-Oil Push
</h3>

The last part of the mission is not the time for broad expansion.

Keep the productive platforms emptied, keep the shore transfer clear, and make
the inland leg cycle as quickly as the route allows. In the run used here, the
steep railway became the late bottleneck, so stronger locomotives, more useful
wagon capacity, and better train spacing mattered more than adding another
offshore platform.

<h2
  id="mission-6-island-expansion"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Mission 6: Island Expansion
</h2>

**Island Expansion** takes place in the **Philippines in 1999** and is much less
linear than the previous missions.

The star thresholds are based on completed subsidiary contracts:

- **5 contracts** for one star;
- **8 contracts** for two stars;
- **12 contracts** for three stars.

The **Special Award** requires you to construct the
**Trans-Filippine Railroad**.

The dangerous part is not choosing the wrong contract. It is accepting a timed
contract before the route is ready.

Many offers have a deadline and carry a penalty if they fail. Build most of the
required infrastructure first, confirm the vehicle or cargo producer is available,
then accept the contract and start its timer.


<h3
  id="island-expansion-opening"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Use the First Contracts to Create Revenue Around Manila
</h3>

The mission opens with **30 million** in cash, expensive infrastructure, and no
loan available to rescue an overbuilt start.

That makes the first contracts important for more than the star counter.

A practical opening is to establish passenger service around Manila and include
the factories that request workers. The route is already useful to the city,
and a worker contract can then pay for infrastructure you would have wanted
anyway.

Fuel is another strong early target. Oil can arrive by ship, be processed, and
then move toward Manila through the rail and local delivery network. When Manila
finally receives enough fuel for a steady energy supply, that infrastructure
continues earning after the contract itself is finished.

Prefer contracts that strengthen the network you are already building. A remote
delivery with a short deadline can wait until the required ship, station, road,
or factory connection is already in place.


<h3
  id="island-expansion-contract-timers"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Prepare the Route Before You Accept a Timed Contract
</h3>

Island Expansion repeatedly offers jobs that look profitable but become
expensive mistakes when accepted too early.

Before clicking accept, check four things: where the cargo or passengers start,
where they must finish, whether the correct vehicle can make the trip, and how
much of the route still has to be built.

Do the expensive construction while the contract timer is stopped. Once the
line is functional, accept the job and let the vehicles start moving
immediately.

This is especially important on island deliveries. A contract can involve a
ship, a harbor, a warehouse or transfer, and a final road or rail leg. Missing
one of those pieces after the countdown begins can consume most of the available
time before the first unit even moves.


<h3
  id="island-expansion-school"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Deliver 50 Logs to Rebuild the School
</h3>

A later social-services phase asks you to rebuild a school.

The construction site needs **50 logs**. The nearby wood producer makes this a
short temporary job rather than a reason to create another permanent
cross-country freight network.

Once the school is standing again, more public-service requests appear. These
include visitor transport, medical supplies, food deliveries, and passenger
connections between settlements.

Do not rebuild each one from scratch. Existing rail stations, bus loops, and
harbors can often serve the new task with a short feeder or an additional stop.


<h3
  id="trans-philippine-railroad"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Build the Trans-Filippine Railroad in Stages
</h3>

The railroad objective requires one continuous line from **Tuguegarao** in the
north to **Pagadian City**, passing through **Manila, Tagkawayan, Butuan, and
Davao**.

You do not need to build the entire route in one spending burst. Extend it in
stages as contract payouts arrive, but keep the final requirement in mind: all
six named cities must belong to the same continuous rail connection.

Trying to fund and finish the entire railway as soon as the objective appears is
unnecessary. Contracts are still paying for the country around it.

Extend the line in stages. Connect a useful city, put that section to work where
possible, then continue south when the next contract or payout gives you enough
cash for the following piece.

When a required station cannot sit directly on the marked destination, use local

If trains later begin blocking one another on the longer route, solve that as a
rail-capacity problem instead of rebuilding the campaign objective itself.


<h3
  id="island-expansion-social-services"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Reuse the Growing Network for the Social-Service Contracts
</h3>

After the school is rebuilt, the mission opens several requests at once.

Some settlements need visitors, others need pharmaceuticals or food, and others
need a reliable passenger connection. At this point, the easiest contracts are
usually the ones that can attach to infrastructure already earning money.

A railway that passes three towns can support local bus feeders. A harbor used
for industry can also become the transfer point for an island supply. A
passenger route that already reaches Manila may only need one extension to
satisfy another contract.

The goal is not to make every city perfect before moving on. Finish enough
useful contracts to keep the star counter rising while the railroad continues
to expand in the background.


<h3
  id="island-expansion-tourism"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Tourism Adds Passenger Contracts After the Railroad Is Nearly Finished
</h3>

Later, the focus shifts toward tourism.

New requests send visitors to nature and resort destinations and ask for
passenger movement between different parts of the country. By this stage the
largest mistake is building isolated routes for every request.

Use the long-distance passenger network first. Extend it where the destination
already lies near the corridor, then add a short local connection where the
railway or harbor does not directly cover the attraction.

The Trans-Filippine Railroad can finish before the final contract count does.
Once the continuous route is complete, stop extending it merely to make the map
look more complete and concentrate on the remaining timed requests.


<h3
  id="island-expansion-yacht"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Remove the Diesel From the Stranded Yacht Before the Reef Is Damaged
</h3>

A late emergency interrupts the tourism phase when a billionaire's yacht runs
aground on a coral reef.

The mission gives you **4 months** to move the diesel from the yacht to safe
storage on land.

The important requirement is the storage type. Build a **liquid-capable
warehouse** near a cargo harbor, create the ship connection to the yacht, and
make sure the vessel actually loads from the yacht before sending it back toward
the warehouse.

Do not wait for the ship to discover a broken destination after it arrives. The
harbor and liquid storage need to be connected before the countdown becomes
tight.

Once the diesel is removed, the tourism phase continues with additional visitor
and supply contracts.


<h3
  id="island-expansion-finish"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Finish 12 Contracts After the Railroad Is Complete
</h3>

The final stretch is mostly about finishing the contract count without
overbuilding.

By now the country can have passenger trains, buses, cargo ships, factories,
harbors, road deliveries, and several temporary lines operating at the same
time. Keep the routes that still serve an active task or produce useful income,
and remove one-off vehicles once their job has ended.

For three stars, push the completed-contract counter to **12** while keeping the
Trans-Filippine Railroad intact. The final tourism report arrives after the last
required work is finished, closing the mission with the economy, social
services, and tourism all operating across the expanded network.
