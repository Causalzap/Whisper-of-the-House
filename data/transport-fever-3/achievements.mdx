  <GuideQuickAnswer title="How Do You Get All 41 Transport Fever 3 Achievements?">

  Start a Free Game for the easy one-off achievements, finish all eight Campaign

  long-term totals, all-climate requirements, and specific setup achievements.

</div>


Transport Fever 3 has **41 achievements**, but they are not all asking for the
same kind of playthrough.

Some unlock during the first few routes of a normal Free Game. Four are tied
directly to Campaign completion and its rewards. The rarest group asks for
things such as **1,000,000 passengers**, **1,000,000 cargo items**, every
climate, every vehicle, every cargo type, a **50,000-population map**, or a
warehouse holding more than **10,000 cargo items**.

Trying to force all of those at once creates unnecessary work. Keep one healthy
long-running Free Game for cumulative progress, use separate climate saves where
the requirement explicitly spans climates, and leave destructive or artificial
conditions such as **0% town reputation** until they cannot interfere with a
network you still care about.

<h2
  id="campaign-achievements"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Finish the 4 Campaign Achievements Together
</h2>

The Campaign has four achievement checks:

- **The Journey Begins** — complete the first Campaign mission;
- **Story Master** — complete all Campaign missions;
- **Phalerist** — earn every Campaign medal;
- **Story Legend** — collect every star in every Campaign mission.

There is little reason to make four separate Campaign passes. Complete the
missions first, then return only to the missions where a medal or star condition
is still missing.

The eight missions already become much more demanding after the opening pair, so
the useful cleanup target is the objective you failed rather than a full restart
of the Campaign. The
<Link href="/transport-fever-3/campaign-walkthrough">Campaign walkthrough</Link>
keeps the mission requirements, star conditions, special objectives, and the
individual blockers in one route.

For **Story Master**, finishing Mission 8 is enough regardless of how many stars
are missing. **Phalerist** and **Story Legend** are the cleanup achievements:
mission completion alone does not satisfy them.


<h2
  id="quick-free-game-achievements"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Clear the Easy Free Game Achievements Before Chasing Large Totals
</h2>

Several achievements need only one action and can be removed from the checklist
during an ordinary early save:

- **All Aboard** — get the first passenger onto a passenger vehicle;
- **Package Arrived** — make the first successful cargo delivery;
- **Promotion** — gain the first company rank;
- **Timekeeper** — change the daytime;
- **Weatherman** — change the weather;
- **Landscaper** — plant one tree;
- **Perfectionist** — demolish and rebuild a station twice in a row.

Do these while the network is still small enough that experimenting costs very
little.

**Perfectionist** is the only one here that asks you to destroy something
deliberately. Use a cheap station or a location you are already rebuilding
instead of tearing apart a busy hub merely for the achievement.

**Offshore** is similarly simple once oil infrastructure exists, but it requires
a helicopter to land on an oil platform. There is no benefit in building an
offshore network early solely for that condition; clear it when oil platforms
are already part of the save.


<h2
  id="town-achievements"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Town Achievements Range From First Growth to 50,000 Population
</h2>

The first town achievements arrive naturally:

**Urban Development** needs one town to reach its next level, while
**Increased Demand** requires a town to begin requesting an additional cargo
type. Once a freight network is established, **Caretaker** unlocks when the
town's supply rating is satisfied.

The later population achievements are much larger:

- **Megatropolis** — one town reaches at least **3,000 population**;
- **Populated Country** — total population reaches **50,000 on one map**;
- **Global Population** — increase population by **100,000 across Free Games**.

Do not confuse the last two. Populated Country is a single-map target, whereas
Global Population is cumulative across Free Games.

For a town that has stopped growing, fix the actual supply and reputation limits
before adding more passenger vehicles. The
<Link href="/transport-fever-3/city-growth-guide">city growth guide</Link>
covers those growth conditions without repeating them here.

**It Wasn't Me** goes in the opposite direction: one town must reach **0%
reputation**. That is a poor objective to mix into a city you are still trying
to grow. Save it for a town or save state where damaging reputation will not
undo hours of useful development.


<h2
  id="subsidy-achievements"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Do Not Break the Subsidy Streak by Accepting a Bad Contract
</h2>

**On Behalf Of** needs only one successful subsidy.

**Subsidy Streak** needs **10 successful subsidies in a row**.

The second requirement changes how I treat offers. A subsidy that needs a route
I can build cheaply is useful; one that would force a long rail line, expensive
fleet, or awkward cargo chain is not automatically worth accepting just because
it appeared.

Build the necessary infrastructure first when the contract allows it, then
accept the subsidy once the route can actually meet the timer.

For the streak, the expensive mistake is not declining an unattractive offer.
It is accepting a contract you cannot finish and breaking the run of successful
subsidies.


<h2
  id="industry-achievements"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Build Industry Achievements Around Throughput, Not Just Production
</h2>

Three of the rare achievements need deliberately large industry setups.

**Boost the Pack** requires active boosters on **10 industries at the same
time**. Ten industries must therefore be boosted concurrently; activating one,
removing its supply, and moving to the next does not create the required state.

Before expanding booster routes, make sure those industries can actually use
the extra production. A raw producer already sitting on a full output stock does
not become a better network because its production rate rises again. The
<Link href="/transport-fever-3/production-chains">production chains guide</Link>
shows how required inputs, workers, cargo boosters, and output stocks interact.

**Car Imperium** is stricter: one industry must produce **500 cars within 12
months**. The limiting factor can sit several stages upstream, because car
production depends on multiple processed inputs. Get all required inputs flowing
before trying to push the final factory to the 12-month target.

**Warehousing Titan** asks for more than **10,000 cargo items in a single
warehouse**. Do not spread the stock across several warehouses. The condition
specifically requires one warehouse to hold the total, so the useful setup is a
large inbound flow with outbound capacity temporarily low enough for stock to
accumulate there.


<h2
  id="money-achievements"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Bank's Best Friend and Evil Money Reward Opposite Financial Behavior
</h2>

**Bank's Best Friend** unlocks after paying more than **10 million in bank
interest**.

That makes it a poor reason to keep an efficient company in debt. Let it happen
during a leveraged expansion or use a save where carrying debt is intentional;
there is no advantage in slowing a profitable network purely to increase
interest payments early.

**Evil Money** is the opposite long-term target: earn a cumulative
**$100 billion** across Free Games.

That total is better served by mature, profitable networks than by a special
achievement setup. The
<Link href="/transport-fever-3/economy-money-guide">economy and money guide</Link>
is the place to troubleshoot a company that is technically moving passengers
and cargo but is still failing to generate enough profit.


<h2
  id="long-term-achievements"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Leave the Million-Unit and Century Achievements Running in the Background
</h2>

Several achievements are designed to accumulate while a successful company keeps
operating:

- **Bicentennial Tycoon** — keep one Free Game running from **1900 to 2000**;
- **Around the World** — build **40,075 km** of streets and tracks combined;
- **Cargo Pioneer** — transport **1,000,000 cargo items** combined;
- **Passenger Pioneer** — transport **1,000,000 passengers** combined;
- **Global Population** — add **100,000 population** across Free Games.

None of these needs an early grind.

For Bicentennial Tycoon, the important part is preserving a 1900 start long
enough to reach 2000. For the cumulative distance, cargo, passenger, and
population achievements, normal expansion continues to add progress even while
you work on other goals.

A developed network therefore has more achievement value than a disposable save
that exists only to run empty vehicles back and forth.


<h2
  id="all-climates-achievements"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Globetrotter and Tycoon of Tycoons Need Every Climate
</h2>

**Globetrotter** only asks you to play a Free Game in every climate zone.

**Tycoon of Tycoons** goes much further: reach the **Tycoon company title in
every climate zone**.

Those should be treated as two different milestones. Opening the remaining
climates can finish Globetrotter long before each climate has a company mature
enough for Tycoon of Tycoons.

Keep the climate saves you intend to develop instead of deleting them after
Globetrotter unlocks. They can later carry the company-title requirement and
also help with cargo or vehicle achievements that are easier in different
economies.


<h2
  id="all-of-type-achievements"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Track Vehicles, Cargo, Ships, and Animals Across Multiple Saves
</h2>

Four achievements punish guessing because each asks for an entire category:

**Connoisseur** requires every vehicle to be used at least once.

**Master Salesman** requires every cargo type available in any climate zone to
be delivered.

**Heritage Fleet** requires all available ship types from **1900 through 2050**
to be operating.

**Animal Lover** requires every animal to be spotted at least once.

Do not assume one large map can automatically finish all four. Climate-specific
industries change which cargo chains are available, and vehicle availability
changes over time.

For Master Salesman, a delivery only needs to count toward the cargo
requirement; there is no reason to turn every cargo type into a huge permanent
network after it has served that purpose.

Heritage Fleet is different because the requirement explicitly says the ship
types must be **in operation**. Keep that condition in mind before selling an
older vessel simply because a newer ship becomes available.


<h2
  id="company-achievements"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Carreer Ladder Is a Company Progression Goal
</h2>

**Carreer Ladder** requires the highest company title.

Unlike Promotion, there is no single early action that clears it. The company
has to keep developing until the top title is reached, so this belongs in the
same mature Free Game used for the larger money, passenger, cargo, and
population totals.

That overlap is useful. A save moving enough people and freight to chase the
million-unit achievements is also a much better place to raise company
progression than a small achievement-only map.


<h2
  id="specific-action-achievements"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Save the Artificial One-Off Setups for Cleanup
</h2>

A few rare achievements are easiest once money is no longer tight.

**Centralized** needs all **10 landmarks** inside one municipal area. Leave
enough usable space before placing them; scattering landmarks around several
municipal areas does not satisfy the requirement.

**Summit Stopover** needs a train to stop at a station situated above
**500 meters**. The station elevation is the important condition, not simply
running track through high terrain.

**Oldschool** needs a **steam locomotive in the 2000s**. Keep or buy the
appropriate locomotive when the calendar reaches that era rather than retiring
every steam vehicle automatically.

**Offshore** needs the first helicopter landing on an oil platform.

These are better cleanup targets than opening moves. Building awkward mountain
rail or landmark layouts while the company is still short on money gives the
achievement far more control over the network than it deserves.


<h2
  id="ufo-achievement"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Encounter of the 3rd Kind Is the Hidden UFO Achievement
</h2>

**Encounter of the 3rd Kind** is hidden and its requirement is to **catch a UFO
for the first time in Free Game**.

The achievement requirement itself does not provide a fixed map position,
climate, year, or guaranteed spawn route. Do not spend money rebuilding an
otherwise healthy network around an assumed UFO location simply because the
achievement is still locked.

Keep this entry visible in the tracker while working through the known
requirements. Once the UFO event appears in a Free Game, catching it is the
action the achievement is checking.


<h2
  id="cleanup-order"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  A Practical Order for the Final Achievement Cleanup
</h2>

After the normal Campaign and a developed Free Game, the shortest cleanup path
is usually:

1. finish all eight Campaign missions;
2. clean up missing Campaign medals and stars;
3. clear cheap one-off Free Game actions;
4. complete the 10-subsidy streak;
5. finish the 3,000-town and 50,000-map population targets;
6. set up the 10-industry booster, 500-cars, and 10,000-warehouse conditions;
7. keep the mature saves running for passenger, cargo, money, population, and
   distance totals;
8. finish the remaining climate, vehicle, ship, cargo, animal, landmark,
   mountain-station, steam-locomotive, and UFO requirements.

There is no benefit in rebuilding a successful company because a cumulative
achievement is still incomplete. The tracker tells you whether the last goal is
a counter that needs more time or a specific condition that needs a different
setup.
