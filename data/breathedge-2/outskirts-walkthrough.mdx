<h2 id="power-the-train">
  My Outskirts route starts with the crashed train, not the nearest marker
</h2>

The first few minutes make the Outskirts look like a place where I should fly
toward whatever icon happens to be closest.

That approach gave me more backtracking than progress.

The crashed train is where I recover, craft, store materials and repeatedly
return between jobs, so I get its basic systems running before taking the
scattered objectives seriously.

My first useful tool needs **2 Metal**. Once I can smash containers and panels,
basic scavenging becomes much less random and I can start working through the
power problem.

Babe's route sends me away from the train along the station cabling.

I follow it until the orange wires lead into the **External Module**. The empty
module needs a battery, so I install one there and immediately head back toward
the train rather than continuing deeper into the wreckage.

Back at the car, I raise the pantograph when prompted.

Power does more than switch on one machine. Ice around the station begins
thawing, previously dead equipment becomes usable and several early routes make
more sense.

The Outskirts gets much easier once I have a functioning place to return to.

<h2 id="stabilize-food-water">
  I make food and water predictable before spending time on optional repairs
</h2>

The damaged food equipment is worth dealing with early because it turns
otherwise poor organic finds into a dependable recovery option.

Water is even easier to plan around once the kettle works.

Using Ice there gave me **3 bottles of water** from a processing cycle in my
early setup. That was enough to stop thirst from forcing constant short trips
back to the train.

I leave part of the Ice stack untouched.

Drinking water is only one use for it, and converting everything immediately
can create a different survival problem later when oxygen infrastructure starts
mattering.

This is also where I become selective with repairs.

A broken object sitting five meters away is not automatically worth fixing. If
food and water are still unreliable, stabilizing those systems has more value
than spending components on something that does not help the next trip.

<h2 id="restore-communications">
  The interphone and antenna jobs become my main route through the Outskirts
</h2>

Once the train is usable, the journal starts filling faster than I want to clear
it.

I use the communication chain to separate story movement from background work.

That means returning to the **interphone**, repairing the associated wiring and
following the beacon and antenna objectives that grow out of those calls.

Some of those jobs look unrelated when they first appear. In practice, they
keep feeding back into the same problem: getting a working signal out of the
station.

Electrical Tape is one component I avoid wasting during this stretch.

Metal and Electronics show up frequently enough in broken machinery that I can
usually recover from spending them. Tape is more annoying when a communication
repair suddenly asks for it and I have already used the last pieces somewhere
optional.

Eventually the repaired equipment begins returning signals.

<h2 id="ticket-booth">
  At the ticket booth, stopping is safer than trying to beat the turret
</h2>

The ticket-booth area looks like a small combat encounter when the security
turret starts reacting to me.

It is much easier if I obey the mechanic.

When the turret orders me to **Freeze**, I stop moving.

Trying to sprint through its line of fire burns health and turns a simple
objective area into a fight I do not need. Pulling apart some nearby machinery
does not necessarily make the turret permanently harmless either, so I spend my
effort on the actual interaction rather than clearing the whole structure.

Once the booth becomes usable, an **old ticket** can be exchanged for a
**token**.

Tokens give me another option at the vending machines, but I treat that as
backup food. Once the train is already producing reliable supplies, repeatedly
coming back here just to feed myself costs more travel time than it saves.

  The Public Oxygen Station makes the outer half of the region much more practical

The next change I feel immediately is the **Public Oxygen Station**.

After the station thaws enough for me to repair it, the Outskirts stops being
one long trip from the train and back.

<h2 id="attendant-side-route">
  The Attendant fits into the route until the missing arm starts wasting time
</h2>

The broken Attendant sits close enough to my normal path that I usually collect
her larger pieces while doing other work nearby.

The final arm is where the task changes character.

It is tiny compared with the surrounding machinery and easy to overlook even
when I am searching the correct general area.

At that point, continuing farther into random wreckage is not useful.

If the communication chain is ready to move, I let it move. The Attendant does
not need to be finished before every other Outskirts objective.

For the body, head and difficult arm route, I use the
<Link href="/breathedge-2/attendant-parts">
  {" "}Attendant Parts guide
</Link>

That keeps the side quest convenient without allowing one hidden object to
control the pace of the entire region.

<h2 id="cold-protection-100">
  Cold Protection 100 is the gate I solve before committing to the colder route
</h2>

The Outskirts eventually stops being limited mainly by how far I can fly.

Temperature becomes the next problem.

The journal makes the requirement explicit:

**Raise Cold Protection to 100.**

I can enter some of the colder wreckage below that value, but there is little
point in reaching an objective if the environment removes most of the time I
need to work there.

When 100 appears in the journal, I return to the train and use the Space Suit
wardrobe.

I leave the individual Cardboard, Foam, suit-stat tradeoffs and frostbite
recovery to the
<Link href="/breathedge-2/cold-protection">
  {" "}Cold Protection guide
</Link>

For the Outskirts progression route, crossing **100** is enough information:
once that requirement clears, I go back to the colder branch and continue the
communication work.

<h2 id="finish-antenna-debris">
  Collect Antenna Debris 0/2 is one of the last Outskirts communication jobs
</h2>

The late communication sequence can feel repetitive because I have already
worked on several pieces of antenna hardware by this point.

Then the journal adds:

**Collect the antenna debris 0/2.**

This is still part of the same progression chain.

The two required sections sit among familiar wreckage rather than announcing
themselves as another huge standalone antenna.

I pay attention to the exposed wiring while working around the structure.
Touching the wrong part can shock me, and losing health here adds nothing to

After the second section is complete, **New Coordinates Detected** appears.

That message changes what I consider unfinished business.

Optional Outskirts markers can remain on the map. The main route is already
leaving the region.
