<h2 id="raise-cold-protection">
  Raise Cold Protection to 100 at the Space Suit wardrobe
</h2>

**100 Cold Protection is the number that matters for this objective.**

When the journal first sends me toward the colder wreckage, I can still fly
into the area with a weaker suit. That does not clear the requirement, and the
time I gain by hopping between warmer spots is too short to make the deeper
route comfortable.

The actual fix is back at the train.

I open the Space Suit wardrobe and fill the available protection slots with
improvised insulation.

The suit works by body part rather than through one permanent cold upgrade.
Different pieces can go on the torso, arms and legs, and every swap changes the
total protection shown on the suit screen.

That makes the number at the top more useful than judging the outfit by how
many insulation pieces I have equipped.

<h2 id="reach-100-cold">
  My first successful setup reached 106 Cold Protection
</h2>

I first reached **90 Cold Protection** and thought I was nearly done.

The objective remained active.

Filling the remaining slots moved the total through:

**90 → 94 → 98 → 102 → 106**

The important step in that sequence is crossing 100. Everything after that is
a buffer, not another progression requirement.

One 106-point setup used:

- **Knitted Teddy Bear chest:** +34 Cold Protection
- **Foam left arm:** +22
- **Foam right arm:** +22
- **Cardboard left leg:** +14
- **Cardboard right leg:** +14

Together, those pieces give **106 Cold Protection**.

I do not keep replacing gear simply because another piece adds a few more Cold
points.

At this stage, **102 or 106 solves the same story problem**. The better choice
is whichever combination gets me over the line without creating an ugly
penalty somewhere else.

<h2 id="cardboard-and-foam">
  Cardboard and Foam are enough to build around early
</h2>

I do not need rare equipment to reach 100 Cold Protection.

Cardboard appears early enough that I can use it to fill weaker suit slots while
I am still working around the station.

Foam gives me a stronger insulation option. I recognize it as the yellow
material attached to station structures and cut it once I have the appropriate
tool.

<h2 id="space-suit-tradeoffs">
  More Cold Protection can change hunger, armor and other resistances
</h2>

The wardrobe becomes more interesting once I stop looking at the Cold number by
itself.

The pieces in my 106 setup have very different side effects.

The **Knitted Teddy Bear** chest provides **+34 Cold Protection** and **+18
Radiation Protection**, but it comes with an armor penalty.

Foam gives strong Cold Protection on the arms, while also increasing **Satiety
Drain**.

Cardboard is weaker against cold than the Foam pieces I used, but it can also
add Electrical Protection and Armor.

This is why maximum Cold Protection is not automatically my best early setup.

Once I am already above 100, another upgrade needs to improve the trip as a
whole. A small Cold increase is harder to justify if it noticeably increases
food consumption or strips away protection I need for another hazard.

During this part of Chapter 1, my target is therefore:

**enough Cold Protection to open the route, with tolerable penalties elsewhere.**

<h2 id="why-you-still-freeze">
  100 Cold Protection does not make the suit immune to freezing
</h2>

Clearing the objective does not switch cold damage off.

I learned that after pushing deep enough into the colder route that the safer
direction was more than **400 meters** away.

Damage had compromised part of my protective setup. The warning escalated to
**Risk of Frostbite**, hypothermia followed, and my vision began closing in.

A direct flight back was no longer the safest answer.

I used the heated route instead:

**reach the nearest working heater → recover → find the next warm section →
continue toward safety.**

This is where the Service Warmers in the
<Link href="/breathedge-2/depot-walkthrough">
  {" "}Depot walkthrough
</Link>
become more than quest machinery. Once repaired, they also give me places to
stabilize when something goes wrong with the suit.

The other lesson is equipment condition.

A build that showed 106 when I left home is not still a 106-point solution if
one of the pieces providing that protection has been damaged or lost.

<h2 id="when-to-turn-back">
  Frostbite warnings and broken insulation matter more than one more marker
</h2>

I no longer set my retreat point by how close the next question mark looks.

For cold routes, three things make me reconsider the trip quickly:

- an important protection piece has been damaged;
- frostbite or hypothermia warnings are already escalating;
- I no longer know where the next working heater or warmer area is.

Once those start stacking together, the next piece of loot is rarely worth the
risk.

Oxygen can end the same trip for a different reason, but I keep that system
separate. The Public Oxygen Station, Oxyworms and portable oxygen route are
covered in the
<Link href="/breathedge-2/oxygen-guide">
  {" "}Oxygen guide
</Link>

For this page, the cold decision is simpler: **if my protection is deteriorating
faster than I can reach another warm point, I head back through the safe route
I already know.**

<h2 id="after-100-cold">
  Once the objective clears, I stop farming insulation and move on
</h2>

Cardboard and Foam become much easier to notice after I know what they look
like, which makes it tempting to keep collecting every piece.

I do not need an inventory full of either material for this stage.

A small reserve can help if I later replace damaged protection, but the 100-point
objective is already solved. Materials for the next repair or tool are more
useful than another pile of insulation with no immediate job.

With the suit above 100 and the cold route now manageable, I return to
progression.

The purpose of the build is not to create a final armor set.

It is to turn a part of the map that kept forcing me home into a route I can
actually finish.
