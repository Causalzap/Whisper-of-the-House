<h2 id="where-to-start">
  Start with whatever is actually blocking the run
</h2>

Breathedge 2 can fill the journal long before I understand which objective is
moving the story and which one can safely wait.

I use this page differently depending on the problem in front of me.

If I have lost the main story thread entirely, I go to the
<Link href="/breathedge-2/walkthrough">
  {" "}Chapter 1 walkthrough
</Link>

If I am still working around the crashed train, ticket booth and early station
structures, the
<Link href="/breathedge-2/outskirts-walkthrough">
  {" "}Outskirts walkthrough
</Link>
is more useful because it stays inside that region.

A tiny missing object calls for a different answer. The
<Link href="/breathedge-2/attendant-parts">
  {" "}Attendant Parts guide
</Link>
covers the body, head and hidden arm, while the
<Link href="/breathedge-2/antenna-locations">
  {" "}Antenna Locations guide

handles Three Antennas and **Collect Antenna Debris 0/2**.

And if I already know where I need to go but cannot stay outside long enough to
do it, I look at the survival system causing the failure rather than the quest

<h2 id="chapter-1-route">
  The broad route runs from the Outskirts into Depot and then toward the jammer
</h2>

The story becomes much easier to read when I reduce it to regions and major
changes in purpose.

I begin around the damaged train in the **Station Outskirts**.

That part of the game is about getting a usable base online, restoring the
station's communication route and surviving long enough to reach the objectives
that sit farther from home.

  </figcaption>

Eventually the communication work produces a destination beyond those familiar
structures.

The next major stage is **Depot**, where the map, environmental pressure and
repair loop all change.

After Depot, the story returns to the communications problem and eventually
turns it into the search for the signal jammer.

For exact objective order between those points, I stay with the full
<Link href="/breathedge-2/walkthrough">
  {" "}walkthrough
</Link>
rather than trying to compress every step onto this page.

<h2 id="outskirts">
  The Outskirts is where most early search problems begin
</h2>

The Station Outskirts mixes story objectives with things that are useful,
optional or simply difficult to see.

Two searches caused much more trouble for me than their quest descriptions
suggested.

The first is **Assemble the Attendant**.

The body and head are manageable, but the missing arm is tiny enough to blend
into the station wreckage. The
<Link href="/breathedge-2/attendant-parts">
  {" "}Attendant Parts guide
</Link>
uses the nearby bridge, blue machinery and orange-ball landmarks to narrow that
search down.

The second is the antenna chain.

Several objectives reuse antenna terminology even though the hardware changes
as the quest progresses. Later, **Collect Antenna Debris 0/2** asks for two
small parts around one antenna structure.

The
<Link href="/breathedge-2/antenna-locations">
  {" "}Antenna Locations guide
</Link>
is where I go once the communication route itself is clear but the object on
screen is not.

If neither of those is the problem and I am simply unsure what to do next in the
region, I stay with the
<Link href="/breathedge-2/outskirts-walkthrough">
  {" "}Outskirts walkthrough
</Link>

<h2 id="survival">
  A reachable objective can still be outside my practical range
</h2>

Longer trips introduce two separate limits.

**Oxygen** determines how much time I have away from a refill.

The
<Link href="/breathedge-2/oxygen-guide">
  {" "}Oxygen guide
</Link>
covers the Public Oxygen Station, Oxyworms, Purified Oxygen and the refill
points that extend a route beyond the starting train.

**Cold Protection** decides whether some colder areas are practical at all.

When the journal asks for **100 Cold Protection**, the
<Link href="/breathedge-2/cold-protection">
  {" "}Cold Protection guide
</Link>
covers the suit setup, insulation choices and what happens if that protection is
damaged farther from safety.

I treat those as different failures.

More air does not fix a suit that cannot survive the temperature, and better
insulation does not give me more breathing time.

<h2 id="depot">
  Depot is the first regional change I treat as a fresh progression problem

When **DEPOT** appears on the map, I stop interpreting every active objective as

<h2 id="resources-crafting">
  When a repair is missing one material, I search for the object that produces it
</h2>

Scavenging becomes much less random once I recognize what resources are
attached to.

Electrical Tape can still be wrapped around a pipe.

Electronics can come out of an appliance.

A Light Bulb may require me to unscrew the correct lamp fitting.

Rubber can look like part of an industrial panel.

Titanium does not resemble a normal ore deposit at all.

The
<Link href="/breathedge-2/resources-crafting">
  {" "}Resources and Crafting guide
</Link>
collects those visual clues and the material decisions that matter as Chapter 1
moves farther from the train.

That is also where I look when deciding whether something such as the Backpack
is worth spending a scarcer material on.

For general progression, I rarely need everything I pass.

I need the materials that finish the repair or craft currently blocking me.

Anything still left on the map is now something I finish because I want the
reward, research, materials or exploration — not because Chapter 1 secretly
continues behind it.
