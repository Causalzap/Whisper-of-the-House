<h2 id="chapter-1-route">
  The Chapter 1 route I follow from the Outskirts to the jammer
</h2>

Chapter 1 looks busier than it actually is because the journal starts collecting
side quests, repairs, survival jobs and story objectives almost immediately.

I make the route easier to read by reducing it to three stages:

**make the Outskirts usable → open the route through the Depot → track down the
signal jammer.**

Side quests can remain unfinished while the story moves forward. I do not wait
for every marker around the starting station to disappear before following a
new main objective.

That distinction matters early. The Attendant, ticket booth, antenna jobs,
collectibles and broken machinery can all be active while the communication
chain is already pointing toward the next stage.

<h2 id="restore-train">
  Get the crashed train working before taking longer Outskirts trips
</h2>

Chapter 1 leaves me around the Station Outskirts with the damaged train car as
the closest thing to a base.

The first useful job is restoring its power.

I follow the wiring outside to the **External Module**, install the battery,
then return to continue the train sequence. Once power comes back, frozen
systems begin thawing and the starting area becomes much easier to use.

<h2 id="restore-communications">
  Follow the communication objectives when the Outskirts journal gets crowded
</h2>

After the train is usable, communications become the main route I keep returning
to.

The interphone work eventually grows into the **Three Antennas** chain, which
moves through several different pieces of communication equipment: the damaged
station hardware, a beacon and the old government probe.

structures.

<h2 id="optional-attendant">
  I finish the Attendant when it fits naturally into the Outskirts route
</h2>

The broken Attendant sits close enough to the early route that I usually make
progress on her while I am already scavenging around the station.

The body and head are relatively easy to recognize. The missing arm is the
piece that can turn a quick side job into a much longer search because it is
tiny compared with the surrounding wreckage.

I do not hold the communication chain hostage to that one part.

If the arm is still missing while another main objective is ready, I continue
the story and return when the detour makes sense. The exact three-part route is
in the
<Link href="/breathedge-2/attendant-parts">
  {" "}Attendant Parts guide
</Link>

<h2 id="prepare-for-cold">
  Raise Cold Protection to 100 before pushing through the colder wreckage
</h2>

Temperature becomes the first hard environmental gate.

I can physically enter colder areas before the suit is ready, but reaching them
does not help if the cold forces me back before I can finish the objective.

When the journal asks for **Raise Cold Protection to 100**, I return to the
train and handle the suit there.

The exact Cardboard, Foam and equipment tradeoffs are covered in the
<Link href="/breathedge-2/cold-protection">
  {" "}Cold Protection guide
</Link>

For progression, the important threshold is simply **100 or higher**. Once the
suit clears it, I resume the route instead of continuing to farm insulation for
a perfect early-game setup.

The major environmental objective becomes **Raise Ambient Temperature to
100%**.

That happens through a series of **Service Warmers** spread across the Depot.
Each repair raises the usable temperature of the area, and some also melt ice
that was blocking the next route.

Rather than diving toward the deepest marker immediately, I work through the
reachable warmers and use the newly opened space to move farther into the
region.

The individual heater route and repair requirements are in the

Chapter 1 starts moving forward again once the warmer chain opens the path back
toward the main antenna objective.

<h2 id="find-main-antenna">
  Follow the cryogel route when Find a Way to the Antenna returns
</h2>

The journal eventually brings back **Find a way to the antenna**.

This is a later story objective, not leftover work from the earlier Outskirts
antenna repairs.

The route now pushes deeper through the cold structures. Robots move through the
cryogel ahead, giving me a much better direction than trying to interpret every
old antenna marker around the station.

At this point I leave optional Depot research alone unless it is directly on my
path. Small Engines, Oxyworm Nests and the other side objectives can wait; the
antenna route is what carries the story into its final section.

The communications problem soon changes again.

This time, someone is actively jamming the signal.

<h2 id="triangulate-jammer">
  Find three strong jamming readings to locate the interference source
</h2>

The jammer cannot be located from the first strong reading.

I follow the interference until the game identifies one strong point, then keep
moving with the directional signal.

The second reading narrows the search but still does not produce the target.

The **third strong point** is the important one. Once all three readings are in,
the system can plot the reverse jamming vectors and identify the structure that
contains the source.

<h2 id="enter-jammer-facility">
  Restore the jammer facility entrance and move inside
</h2>

The jammer structure does not open immediately.

I restore the external power connection first and work through the entrance
system. Once communications are available again, the access information comes
through the story rather than requiring a blind search around the outside for a
hidden handwritten code.

Gravity returns inside the facility, which makes this stretch feel very
different from the open-space searches outside.

The **Camerabot** is part of the progression. Once it accepts the repair-crew
explanation, I can continue through the facility instead of treating it as a
combat obstacle.

The rooms also contain useful supplies, so I take what is already on the route
without turning the facility into a full cleanup run.

<h2 id="open-airlock">
  Sprayron and the profile update open the route to the shuttle
</h2>

Farther inside, the progression splits between the **Sprayron** and the blocked
airlock.

One Sprayron interaction can initially ask for a higher level than I have. That
does not mean the entire route is locked behind an immediate upgrade.

Another part of the sequence involves recovering the missing head, updating the
profile and using that identity to get the airlock doors open.

Once the profile is accepted, the shuttle route becomes available.

At that point I leave unrelated lockers and unfinished room cleanup alone and
continue with the main objective.

<h2 id="shuttle-alkaline">
  Fill the shuttle with alkaline solution before the jammer route can continue
</h2>

The shuttle does not take me straight to the jammer.

The trip is interrupted by the larger parasite-like creatures, and the journal
now shows the chain clearly:

**Find the signal jammer → Fill the shuttle with alkaline solution → Retrieve
the jammer.**

I use the Sprayron on the creatures around the marked burrows and collect the
alkaline material from that route.

Once I have enough for the shuttle, there is little reason to keep exploring
unrelated caves. I return, open the tank cap and fill the system so the story
can move on.

<h2 id="cooling-system">
  Start the cooling system, then reactivate the airlock manually
</h2>

Back inside, the next objective revolves around the **cooling system**.

Opening the control panel starts the pump-out sequence, but it also locks the
airlock and adds a **manual airlock reactivation** step.

I finish that before following the route farther down.

This is one of the few places where fatigue can become more than background
noise. If I am already close to exhausted, I use the available rest opportunity
before the next sequence rather than entering a hostile section with reduced
stamina.

Once the cooling system and airlock are sorted, the shuttle route takes me into
the final jammer area.

<h2 id="retrieve-jammer">
  Clear the parasite sequence and retrieve the jammer
</h2>

The last facility section is visually chaotic enough that it is easy to lose
the objective behind the creatures and environmental effects.

I keep clearing the parasite sequence until the affected host finally exposes
the object I came for.

That is the **signal jammer**.

The final mechanical interaction uses the **circular sensor**.

I wait until the reading stabilizes and turns green, then hit it with the
crowbar. Swinging while the sensor is still moving only adds noise to an
otherwise simple timing step.

Completing that activation finally opens the transmission the crew has been
trying to reach.
