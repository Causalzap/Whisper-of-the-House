<h2 id="where-ending-starts">
  The ending starts once the jammer is finally in my hands
</h2>

I consider the final story sequence underway once **Retrieve the Jammer** is
finished.

Everything before that — triangulating the interference, getting inside the
facility, opening the shuttle route and dealing with the alkaline-solution
objective — is still part of reaching the ending rather than understanding it.

Earlier coordinates usually meant immediate progression: get a location, stock
up, then fly toward it.

This one behaves differently.

There is no new destination marker waiting behind another repair, no terminal
where I need to enter **779014-EE5**, and no second region that opens if I go
back through the Depot looking for something I missed.

The coordinates are a promise of where the story is going next.

They are not a playable objective yet.

Finding Mary is only half of what the final conversation reveals.

The danger is the transmission itself.

If it was intercepted, whoever is listening may know the group's quadrant and
possibly their exact coordinates. The conversation shifts almost immediately
from finding Mary to getting out before that information can be used against
them.

That makes the cliffhanger more interesting than a simple locked destination.

Chapter 1 leaves me with two unresolved problems at once:

**Mary is somewhere ahead at 779014-EE5, and the people trying to reach her may
now have been located themselves.**

There is no playable escape sequence after that reveal yet.

<h2 id="is-there-chapter-2">
  Can I start Chapter 2 after the transmission?
</h2>

No.

This is not one of those endings where the next quest exists but is hidden
behind an optional conversation or another unfinished side objective.

The dialogue openly jokes about the next chapter not being available, and the
game then puts the boundary directly on screen:

**Thanks for playing Early Access!**

Returning to the Depot to repair one more machine will not unlock Chapter 2.

There is little value in filling every container with speculative materials for

<h2 id="how-long-chapter-1">
  How long Chapter 1 took me depended heavily on how much I explored
</h2>

A broad Chapter 1 run can fall around the **8–10 hour** range when I mix the
main story with normal scavenging, side objectives and wrong turns.

That number is better used as context than as a completion target.

Zero-gravity navigation can add a lot of time. So can searching for one tiny
quest object, returning for a missing repair material or investigating every
question mark before moving on.

A cleaner story-focused route can finish sooner.

A completion-minded run can easily take longer.

So if I am already eight or ten hours in but still have an active main quest
for the antenna, Depot or jammer, I have not accidentally passed the ending.
The main-story objective itself is the better indicator of progress.

<h2 id="is-this-full-ending">
  This is a Chapter 1 cliffhanger, not Breathedge 2's final ending
</h2>

Nothing about the final sequence feels like the larger story has resolved.

Mary is still somewhere beyond the current playable route.

The betrayal has only just been exposed.

The crew still needs to escape a position that may no longer be safe.

And **779014-EE5** has not yet become a destination I can visit.

That is why I treat the Early Access ending as a cliffhanger rather than a
finale.

For the current version, **Thanks for playing Early Access!** is the point where
I consider Chapter 1 finished. After that, I stay for side content because I
want to, not because I am still searching for a hidden path into the next
chapter.
