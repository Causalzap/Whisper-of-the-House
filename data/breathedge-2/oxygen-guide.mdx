<h2 id="repair-oxygen-station">
  Fix the Public Oxygen Station before the Outskirts starts pulling me farther away
</h2>

The first oxygen upgrade that noticeably changes how I explore is not a larger
tank.

It is the **Public Oxygen Station**.

Once the early Outskirts systems begin thawing, I can repair it with:

- **3 Metal**
- **2 Electronics**
- **1 Wire**
- **3 Electrical Tape**

I gather the full set before making the repair trip. Electronics and Electrical
Tape are easy to spend on nearby machinery, and using either stack elsewhere
can turn one simple repair into another supply run.

The station becomes most valuable when several objectives are spread around the
same part of the map.

I can refill, continue to the next wreck, then decide whether there is enough
air left for another detour. That is much more efficient than treating the
train as the only safe endpoint.

<h2 id="keep-ice-for-oxygen">
  I stopped turning every piece of Ice into water
</h2>

Ice creates an easy early-game resource conflict.

At first I treated it mainly as drinking water because dehydration is visible
and immediate.

Then I reached an oxygen terminal after converting my remaining Ice and found
myself standing next to a refill point I could not use.

After that, I started reserving part of the stack for oxygen.

I do not need a huge pile. I just avoid processing the last useful pieces into
water before a longer trip.

That becomes more important as routes leave the comfortable early loop. A
working station only extends my range if I still have what it needs when I get
there.

<h2 id="unlock-purified-oxygen">
  The first Oxyworms give me a portable answer to bad route planning
</h2>

Fixed refill points solve predictable travel.

They are less helpful when the real problem begins **after** I reach the marker.

That is where Oxyworms matter.

Collecting them unlocks **Purified Oxygen**, which gives me a way to carry extra
breathing time instead of relying entirely on the train or a station.

I still prefer a station for ordinary refills.

Purified Oxygen is most useful when I expect uncertainty: circling a structure
for an entrance, looking for a small quest object, entering an unfamiliar
interior or realizing I approached a wreck from the wrong side.

Those are the situations where the marker distance badly understates the real
oxygen cost.

<h2 id="purified-oxygen-recipe">
  Purified Oxygen restores 120 Oxygen
</h2>

The recipe uses:

**2 Oxyworms + 1 Metal + 1 Ice**

and the finished item restores **120 Oxygen**.

  </figcaption>

My early suit carries **600 Oxygen**, so a 120-point refill is large enough to
matter without replacing normal station use.

I usually keep it in reserve while I still know where the next refill is.

Using one five seconds away from the Public Oxygen Station saves almost nothing.
Using it underneath a wreck after a long search can be the difference between
finishing the objective and abandoning the entire trip.

The recipe also consumes Metal and Ice, both of which have competing uses.
That keeps me from crafting a pile of portable oxygen just because I have spare
Oxyworms.

For familiar short routes, fixed refills are cheaper.

For a new route with no obvious backup, one portable refill is much easier to
justify.

<h2 id="oxyworm-nests">
  Oxyworm Nests can turn distant routes into two shorter legs
</h2>

Farther from the starting train, I eventually find **Oxyworm Nests** that can
restore oxygen directly.

One of these refilled my suit without forcing a full return to the Outskirts
station.

That makes raw distance less useful than it looks.

A farther objective with a nest between me and the destination can be safer
than a shorter route with no oxygen source at all.

where long travel, environmental hazards and repair stops begin competing for
the same trip.

<h2 id="when-to-turn-back">
  A 250-meter trip taught me to budget oxygen for the search, not just the flight
</h2>

One early marker sat roughly **250 meters** away.

The outbound flight looked manageable.

By the time I arrived, found the structure and started looking for the useful
way inside, the oxygen calculation had already changed. The remaining bar could
get me home, but it could not safely pay for an unknown amount of exploration
and still cover the return.

So I left.

That failure gave me a much better way to read distance markers:

**reaching the objective is only the outbound cost.**

A 250-meter flight can be cheap when the interaction is sitting in the open.

The same distance becomes expensive when I need to:

- circle a wreck looking for an entrance;
- search several rooms;
- hunt a small quest object;
- stop for combat or repairs;
- backtrack because I entered from the wrong direction.

This is why I no longer use a fixed percentage such as “turn around at half
oxygen.”

The useful question is whether the remaining supply can cover **what I still
need to do plus the route home**.

If the answer is uncertain before I have even started the objective, I refill
first.

<h2 id="cold-and-oxygen">
  Cold Protection can open a route without making the oxygen budget any better
</h2>

Later exploration adds another environmental limit.

Once my suit can survive the colder region, I can stay in places that previously
forced an immediate retreat. That extra freedom also makes it easier to spend
too much time there.

Cold and oxygen solve different problems.

The
<Link href="/breathedge-2/cold-protection">
  {" "}Cold Protection guide
</Link>
covers the suit threshold and frostbite risk. Here I only care about how much
breathing time remains after the environment is survivable.

A warm route can still be too long.

<h2 id="oxygen-vs-gas">
  Gas is not extra Oxygen
</h2>

The HUD separates **Oxygen** and **Gas**, and upgrades can change one without
changing the other.

My early suit showed **600/600 Oxygen** with a separate **50 Gas** capacity.

Later, an equipment change increased Gas from **50 to 200** while Oxygen
remained at **600**.

That is enough to rule out a common misunderstanding: increasing Gas capacity
does not secretly enlarge the breathing tank.

For my route planning:

- **Oxygen Station, Oxyworms, Oxyworm Nests and Purified Oxygen** affect how
  long I can keep breathing away from a refill;
- **Gas** belongs to equipment that consumes its own supply.

I keep those budgets separate when deciding whether an upgrade actually helps
the trip I am about to make.

<h2 id="oxygen-check-before-leaving">
  Before an unfamiliar route, I decide where the next refill will come from
</h2>

By the time Chapter 1 starts sending me well beyond the train, the most useful
preparation is no longer a long checklist.

I want an answer to one question:

**where do I breathe again if the objective takes longer than expected?**

Sometimes the answer is the Public Oxygen Station.

Sometimes it is an Oxyworm Nest.

Sometimes I carry Purified Oxygen because there is no dependable refill ahead.

If I cannot name any of those and the destination is unfamiliar, I treat the
full **600 Oxygen** as a round-trip budget rather than permission to keep flying
until the bar is nearly empty.

That approach leaves room for the part of Breathedge 2 that consumes the most
unexpected oxygen: not travelling to the marker, but figuring out what to do
after I arrive.
