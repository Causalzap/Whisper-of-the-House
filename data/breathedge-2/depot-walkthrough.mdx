<h2 id="reach-depot">
  Follow New Coordinates until the map changes to Depot
</h2>

The Depot route begins after the previous antenna work produces **New
Coordinates**.

From the position where I first checked the marker, the destination was roughly
**230 meters** away. That number is only useful as a scale reference because it
changes with my position; the important confirmation is the **DEPOT** area name
appearing on the map.

Depot introduces its central problem almost immediately:

**Raise Ambient Temperature to 100%.**

There is no single temperature control that finishes the objective. The
percentage rises as individual **Service Warmers** come back online.

That turns the region into a moving repair route rather than a search for one
final console.

My route moved through:

<h2 id="service-warmer-materials">
  Light Bulbs and Wires caused most of my Service Warmer backtracking
</h2>

The Depot contains plenty of visible machinery, but the components I actually
ran short of were often much smaller.

A Service Warmer I encountered needed:

**4 Metal + 1 Light Bulb + 4 Wires + 2 Electronics**

**Light Bulbs** were the least intuitive component for me.

Ordinary lights and televisions looked like obvious sources, but they were not
the objects I needed. The useful lamps have a specific interaction: I approach
the assembly, **unscrew the clamp**, then take the Light Bulb.

When I need more, I strip electrical panels and consoles that are already along

<h2 id="choose-next-warmer">
  The nearest Service Warmer usually gives me the best next foothold
</h2>

The three-dimensional layout makes a fixed left-to-right Depot route difficult
to describe usefully.

Marker distance works better.

At one stage I could see one warmer around **139 meters** away while another was
more than **400 meters** out. Later the choice looked more like **75 meters**
versus **200+ meters**.

In both cases I took the closer repair first.

The benefit is not merely shorter travel. A repaired warmer creates another
heated position, increases the ambient-temperature objective and may open a
passage that changes how I reach the more distant marker.

When I lose orientation, my recovery method is simple:

1. pin the Service Warmer objective;
2. compare the visible fire-marker distances;
3. repair the nearest practical one;
4. inspect anything its heat just uncovered;
5. recheck the remaining markers from the new position.

This works better for me than memorizing directions such as “left of the
asteroid.” After rotating through tunnels and wreckage, those directions stop
meaning much.

<h2 id="cold-route">
  Working heaters become part of the route when the Depot gets dangerous
</h2>

The farther I move into the region, the more important the repaired warmers
become outside the quest itself.

They give me known heated positions between colder sections.

If my suit is already struggling, I move through those known warm areas rather
than gambling on a long direct crossing to the next objective.

The full **100 Cold Protection** setup, damaged insulation and frostbite

Inside the Depot, I mainly care about whether the next warmer can be reached,
repaired and used as another safe point without sacrificing the whole trip.

<h2 id="foreman">
  The Foreman needs to be discharged before the calibration test works
</h2>

The Foreman encounter looks broken if I reach the robot and expect an ordinary
repair prompt.

The missing action is **discharge the Foreman**.

This is one of the places where the chicken becomes mechanically useful rather
than simply following me around.

After the discharge, the Foreman can proceed into the calibration sequence.

There is a second, much smaller trap during the test.

After choosing an answer, I still need to press **Confirm**.

When the questions appeared to stop advancing, that button was the problem in
my run, not a failed attempt limit or a broken interaction.

Once the calibration is complete, I return to the heating route. The Foreman is
worth handling while I am nearby, but finishing him does not replace the
**Raise Ambient Temperature** objective.

<h2 id="small-engines">
  Small Engine research is worth taking when it costs me almost no detour
</h2>

Small Engines begin appearing during the Depot progression and can be studied
for later movement technology.

I examine them when the required parts are already in my inventory.

If an engine wants a component I have just exhausted on Service Warmers, I leave
it for another pass rather than interrupting the heating chain.

Completing the research eventually makes the **Booster** available.

That is useful in a region where distances keep increasing, but unlocking the
blueprint and having a functioning movement setup are different things. I can
finish the Depot without waiting for the Booster to solve the route for me.

<h2 id="finish-depot">
  The Depot heating route is finished only after the last Service Warmer
</h2>

Reaching **78/100%** still left another heater ahead.

I keep following the remaining fire markers until the Service Warmer objective
is finally exhausted and the ambient-temperature route reaches its end.
