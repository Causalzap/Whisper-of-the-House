<h2 id="three-antennas">
  Three Antennas is a sequence of different communication repairs
</h2>

The quest name is more confusing than the route itself.

**Three Antennas** does not send me around the Outskirts looking for three
matching satellite dishes. The journal gradually moves between several related
objectives, including the damaged antenna, homemade beacon, old government
probe and the later **Collect Antenna Debris** job.

The order becomes much easier to follow when I think of it as restoring pieces
of one broken communication network.

I finish one device, listen for the next update, then reopen the journal before
flying somewhere else.

<h2 id="repair-first-antenna">
  Repair the damaged antenna and collect its sector-frequency data
</h2>

The first antenna is the most conventional part of the chain.

The journal says it was damaged by debris and that the pieces should still be
nearby. I stay around the station structure rather than following distant
question marks, because “nearby” in zero gravity can still mean above, behind
or underneath the object I am facing.

Once I have the required repair materials, I return to the damaged antenna and
finish it there.

The useful result is the **sector frequency data**.

Babe can build a sector-frequency map from it, which tells me there is nothing
else to gain by repeatedly inspecting the same dish.

From there I move to the next communication objective rather than searching the
surrounding wreckage for “antenna number two.”

<h2 id="homemade-beacon">
  The homemade beacon needs a Tuning Unit, power and another repair
</h2>

The homemade beacon is less straightforward because simply locating it does not
finish anything.

Inspecting the structure adds more specific jobs. In my route, one of the
pieces involved was a **Tuning Unit**, and the beacon also needed a battery
before its systems could move forward.

  </figcaption>

Powering it still did not finish the beacon for me.

The remaining repair left me short **2 Electrical Tape**.

That is the part worth planning around, because Electrical Tape is used by
several other Outskirts repairs. If this beacon is the job I want to finish, I
save those last two pieces rather than spending them on unrelated machinery.

Some taped pipes and wreckage can be cut directly for Electrical Tape. The
broader material sources are in the
<Link href="/breathedge-2/resources-crafting">
  {" "}Resources and Crafting guide
</Link>

Once the beacon is powered and repaired, I return to the journal for the next
piece of the communications chain.

<h2 id="government-probe">
  Start the government probe's onboard clock, then repair its electronics
</h2>

The old government probe is the point where the quest name becomes especially
misleading.

It is a large spacecraft-like object rather than another dish antenna.

The useful component is its **onboard clock**.

I move toward the cockpit side and use the crank-style starter.

Once the mechanism begins working, I leave the crank alone and follow the new
objective rather than repeatedly trying to start it.

The next problem is **Repair the Electronics**.

The interaction point is small enough to fly past, so if that objective remains
active I slow down around the probe itself and look for the repair prompt before
assuming I am missing another remote component.

After the electronics are restored, the probe begins receiving signals.

<h2 id="collect-antenna-debris">
  For Collect Antenna Debris 0/2, stay above the Attendant bridge
</h2>

**Collect Antenna Debris 0/2** was the part of this chain that took me the
longest to read correctly.

The search area is much smaller than the wider Outskirts makes it feel.

I return to the antenna structure around the same general bridge area where the
hard-to-see Attendant arm can be found. If that landmark is unfamiliar, the
<Link href="/breathedge-2/attendant-parts">
  {" "}Attendant Parts guide
</Link>
shows the bridge and nearby blue machinery.

From the bridge, I move **up toward the antenna structure**.

I do not fly outward toward another distant wreck.

<h2 id="cant-find-debris">
  Still stuck at 0/2? Search closer, not farther
</h2>

The amount of debris around this structure makes a wide search actively worse.

If the counter has not moved, I reset around the bridge and antenna instead of
continuing farther into the station.

My search becomes:

1. return to the antenna structure above the Attendant bridge;
2. move close enough for object labels to appear;
3. circle the outer frame and underside slowly;
4. look specifically for **Antenna part**;
5. after placing the first piece, stay around the same structure for the
   second.

I also avoid the exposed wiring while circling the antenna.

Touching it can shock me, which is particularly annoying when I finally have
the correct search area and only need another minute to inspect it.

If I have to leave and come back, I return to the same bridge landmark. Changing
the entire search area because the first attempt failed only recreates the
original problem.
