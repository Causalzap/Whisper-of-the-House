<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
Pick up the free **Toothbrush** at the station, clean a nearby hex, and deposit your Dust for money. Buy a wider brush, increase its container capacity when return trips become frequent, and use the Moon Map to choose your next cleaning area. Save for a Garage or automation once your manual setup is earning consistently.

Moon Sweepers starts with one Toothbrush and a cleaning assignment that is much larger than your equipment can handle. The first few upgrades make an enormous difference: a wider brush clears more ground per stroke, a larger container reduces the number of capsules you need to handle, and completing individual hexes gives you extra money beyond the Dust you sell.

Later, vehicles, production machines, and automated cleaners take over much of the physical work. The useful progression is not simply buying every item as soon as it becomes affordable. Some purchases improve how much ground you clear, others improve the value of what you collect, and others only become worthwhile after you can unload or transport the Dust they produce.

## What Should You Do First in Moon Sweepers?

The starting station contains a free Toothbrush on the supply wall. Take it before leaving. You do not need to buy a tool to begin cleaning, and the early economy is small enough that even a $5 purchase represents several trips.

Start close to the station rather than walking into distant terrain. A short route back to the Depot matters while your brush has very little Dust capacity.

1. Take the Toothbrush and clean the dirty ground outside the station.
2. Watch the Dust container fill as you brush.
3. Use **Q** to pack collected Dust into a capsule when needed.
4. Bring the Dust back to the station and use the **Depot deposit interaction**.
5. Use the money to improve your cleaning tool, then return to the unfinished area.

The first load is tiny, so frequent deposits are normal. You can pack capsules while working and bring them back together rather than stopping after every small amount of Dust.

The important distinction is that collecting Dust into your brush does not automatically sell it. The material has to reach the Depot's receiving system.

### Finish the first hex instead of leaving several almost clean

When a hex is nearly complete, it can be tempting to move immediately to fresh ground because the remaining patches take longer to spot.

Finishing it is valuable for a different reason: **a completed hex awarded $10 in an early cleaning run**, separate from the money earned by depositing Dust.

That payout is meaningful when the next tool costs $10 or $25. Several mostly cleaned hexes may look like more progress, but a fully completed nearby hex gives you a concrete reward you can immediately reinvest.

Work around the edges first, then sweep through the interior. Near the end, inspect the corners and thin strips of remaining Dust instead of repeatedly brushing the clean center.

You can keep two nearby hexes in progress if their borders overlap your cleaning path. Just return to finish them before traveling far away.

## Which Brush Should You Buy First?

The starting tool shop offers a straightforward progression:

A larger brush clears a wider strip, but that does not automatically solve the other early limitation: **how little Dust you can hold before packing another capsule**.

The best purchase depends on which task is taking most of your time.

### Nail Brush, Handbrush, or Household Broom?

The Nail Brush is cheap enough to provide a quick improvement over the Toothbrush. The Handbrush costs $10 and increases the working width again.

However, these inexpensive tools are transitional purchases. Once the Household Broom is affordable at **$25**, you gain a much more practical tool for working across an entire hex.

There is no reason to spend a long time maximizing the Toothbrush when your goal is to clear large areas. Buying a wider tool changes how quickly each pass removes Dust.

For an ordinary opening, a reasonable route is to make enough money with the Toothbrush to buy an affordable brush upgrade, then work toward the Household Broom. A player already close to $25 can consider skipping another small purchase rather than delaying the larger tool.

### Upgrade capacity when capsules become the bottleneck

The Household Broom provides a useful example of this tradeoff.

Its initial container holds approximately **3 kg**. A **$30 container upgrade** increases that capacity to **6 kg**.

![Household Broom container upgrade showing the $30 cost, 6 kg capacity, and ownership requirement.](/images/moon-sweepers/moon-sweepers-household-broom-capacity-upgrade.webp)

That is twice as much Dust before the tool fills.

The choice between that upgrade and saving for a Wide Broom depends on how you are working:

- **Too many capsules to pack and carry:** The 6 kg capacity upgrade reduces interruptions.
- **Container rarely reaches full capacity:** A wider brush makes more sense because you are still limited by the area cleaned per pass.
- **Working far from the station:** Extra carrying capacity becomes more useful because returning to the Depot takes longer.
- **Already carrying a large supply of packed capsules:** Improve the delivery routine before assuming another brush upgrade will solve the problem.

A wider brush and a larger container solve different problems. The first increases cleaning coverage; the second reduces the frequency of handling collected Dust.

### When to buy the Wide and Extra Wide Brooms

The Wide Broom costs **$75**, and the Extra Wide Broom costs **$150**.

The larger tool is a noticeable step up in cleaning width. One shop comparison showed the move from approximately **65.9 cm** to **98.8 cm** when upgrading to the Extra Wide Broom.

That additional width is particularly useful on broad, open sections of terrain where long passes can stay on dirty ground.

Small, nearly finished hexes are different. Precise cleanup around their edges can still require extra attention even with the largest handheld brush.

A good stopping point for expensive hand-tool upgrades is when you begin saving for vehicles. Continuing to invest in your broom may be worthwhile, but compare the next upgrade cost against the amount needed to start clearing much larger strips with a vehicle.

## Which Upgrade Matrix Purchases Are Worth the Money?

The Upgrade Matrix contains improvements that affect different parts of your work. A broader cleaning head, larger Dust container, faster movement, and higher sale payout should not be valued as though they all do the same thing.

The first question is where your time is going.

### Cleaning and container upgrades

A **Wide Head** upgrade increases cleaning width. One early version offered an **8% width increase for $40**.

That percentage is useful when you expect to keep using handheld tools for a substantial amount of time. It is less attractive if you are about to replace manual sweeping with a large vehicle.

A **Dust Compactor** upgrade can increase the amount held by all tool containers. An early version showed **15% additional container capacity for $150**, with a container-upgrade prerequisite.

At that price, it is a poor substitute for the inexpensive first capacity upgrade on a small broom. Its value improves when you are regularly carrying larger loads across multiple upgraded tools.

**Auto Packer** addresses a different interruption. Its description indicates that when your container becomes full during cleaning, the tool can automatically pack a capsule at your feet.

This can reduce repeated manual packing, but it does not replace the need to deposit or transport those capsules.

### Market Contracts versus another brush

**Market Contracts** improves the payout from lunar Dust sent through the launch system.

One early upgrade cost **$120** and displayed a **6% launch payout increase**.

That is attractive because it improves the return on Dust you are already selling. It does not directly widen your brush or increase the quantity of material collected.

Choose based on the immediate limitation:

- When each cleaning pass is still painfully narrow, the next brush may offer a faster improvement.
- When you already produce plenty of Dust and sell it regularly, a better payout applies to a larger existing flow of material.
- When Dust is accumulating without reaching the Depot, fix the delivery problem before buying an earnings multiplier.

Later Market Contracts levels become more expensive. Check the current price and displayed benefit rather than assuming every level offers the same percentage.

### Supplier Network and Region Bounty

**Supplier Network** reduces equipment or upgrade costs. One option displayed a **5% discount**.

A discount is more valuable before several expensive purchases than after you have already bought most of your equipment. On a single inexpensive brush, the saving is relatively small.

**Region Bounty** increases the reward for completing cleaned regions. One upgrade offered an additional **$15 per completed region**, making it more valuable when you regularly finish nearby areas.

This makes sense when you are actively finishing regions rather than leaving many areas partly cleaned. Its value depends on how frequently you complete those regions, not just how much Dust you collect.

The general buying order is simple: improve the activity you are performing repeatedly, then purchase upgrades whose benefits apply to that larger amount of work.

## How to Use the Moon Map to Find Dirty Areas and Stations

The Moon Map is accessible from a station's main console. It lets you distinguish untouched ground from areas you have started cleaning and sections you have fully completed.

The map's colors have specific meanings:

- **Orange:** Ground that has not yet been cleaned.
- **Blue:** Ground where cleaning has started but remains incomplete.
- **Green:** Completed ground.

![Moon Map showing untouched, partly cleaned, and completed sectors, together with the station and exploration counters.](/images/moon-sweepers/moon-sweepers-moon-cleaning-progress.webp)

The colors are especially helpful once the surrounding landscape becomes difficult to recognize. A surface that appears mostly gray from your position may still contain enough Dust to prevent local completion.

### Find unfinished work before choosing another destination

Return to the map after several completed hexes and look at the shape of your cleaned area.

Blue sections near a green cluster are often worth checking before beginning a completely separate patch. You have already done part of the work there, and their remaining Dust may be relatively close to the station.

For a new cleaning route, look for a broad orange area that is easy to approach with your current equipment. The ideal destination changes when you move from a handheld brush to a vehicle with a much wider cleaning strip.

The **Field Manual**, available with **H** on keyboard, also describes the current-sector scan and how to interpret the area's cleaning state.

### Waypoints and stations

The Moon Map has a waypoint system for marking destinations. Use it to keep a clear target when traveling beyond the immediate station area.

One Moon Map displayed **12 stations**, a separate flag counter, and a surveyed-sites counter. Those are exploration indicators, not evidence that every distant location is a usable replacement for your current Depot.

A station shown on the map should be distinguished from a new collector or selling point you can personally construct. Before planning a distant operation, establish where its Dust can actually be delivered.

This becomes more important after buying a vehicle. Driving to an untouched area is easy compared with moving a very large collected load back into your selling system.

## What Do Flags and Lunar Survey Sites Give You?

Exploring the Moon can produce rewards without requiring you to clean every hex along the way.

Flags are one example. A player encountering a flag was able to **untangle it** and receive **$100**.

That is a substantial early reward compared with selling individual small capsules of Dust.

### How to collect a flag reward

When you find a flag, stop close enough to reach its interaction prompt.

Use the flag's **untangle interaction** and wait for the action to finish. Simply sweeping around its base or driving past it is not the same thing as completing that interaction.

Several flags can be found during vehicle travel. The Moon Map records progress separately from ordinary cleaning, so checking its flag count helps you see whether your exploration has advanced.

A $100 reward can be worth a short detour while you are nearby. Traveling a very long distance only for a single flag becomes less compelling once your production line is already generating money efficiently.

### What about Survey Sites?

Lunar sites have their own exploration progress. An earlier exploration run also awarded **$250 for a completed survey**.

That reward makes surveying potentially worthwhile while exploring with a vehicle, particularly before you have a mature factory.

Treat surveying as a distinct interaction from cleaning Dust or untangling flags. When you encounter a site, inspect the available interaction and confirm that the survey completes before leaving.

The map helps with overall exploration progress, but you still need to recognize the individual location and its interaction when you reach it.

### Should you explore or keep cleaning?

During the Toothbrush stage, staying close to the Depot is usually more useful. A long journey costs too much travel time and leaves you with limited ability to clean along the way.

Exploration becomes more practical once you own a vehicle and can cover distance while collecting Dust.

At that point, a good trip can combine three activities: clean fresh ground on the route, collect nearby flag or survey rewards, and return with a load worth depositing.

There is no need to turn every flag or site into a separate journey before progressing your cleaning equipment.

## Which Moon Sweepers Vehicle Should You Buy First?

Vehicles are the major alternative to upgrading a handheld brush indefinitely.

The **Garage costs $200**. It stores your vehicles and brings them into its central bay when selected.

The Garage is a separate expense. Starting with a Push Sweeper therefore requires **$500 in total** if you do not already own the Garage.

Buying the vehicle does not automatically complete its unloading or selling route.

### Push Sweeper or another broom upgrade?

A Push Sweeper becomes attractive once you have outgrown the area cleared by handheld tools.

At $300, however, it is a significant commitment compared with a $30 container upgrade or a $75 Wide Broom. It makes sense when you can afford the Garage and expect to spend substantial time clearing new ground.

A player who still has unfinished nearby hexes, a small brush, and very little savings can continue earning through manual cleaning first.

For someone already collecting and selling Dust consistently, the Garage begins to open up a more useful route toward larger vehicles.

### When the Ride-on Sweeper becomes useful

The **Ride-on Sweeper costs $1,000** and provides a drivable step beyond the Push Sweeper.

Driving changes the way you clear terrain. Rather than working with individual brush strokes, you begin planning longer passes and turning around at the end of each strip.

This helps on broad areas but can be less convenient for precise cleanup of the last dusty corner inside a hex.

If you are primarily working near the station and still have many partly cleaned sections, finish the most convenient ones before spending money solely to get more vehicle speed.

### Is the $2,500 Lunar Sweeper worth saving for?

The Lunar Sweeper has **three brushes**, a **2.8-meter cleaning strip**, and a **1,200 kg hopper**.

![Lunar Sweeper purchase panel showing its $2,500 price, three brushes, 2.8-meter cleaning width, and 1,200 kg hopper.](/images/moon-sweepers/moon-sweepers-lunar-sweeper-stats.webp)

Its wider coverage and large storage capacity make it a substantial step beyond handheld cleaning. The enclosed cab also provides a different driving experience from the smaller machines.

One run found it easier to control than the earlier vehicles, although that is a handling observation rather than a guaranteed performance advantage for every player.

At $2,500, it makes sense when vehicle cleaning is already your main activity and you have a reliable way to empty large loads.

Buying a larger hopper without considering unloading just moves the bottleneck from collecting Dust to processing or delivering it.

### When to upgrade to the Mega Vacuum Rover

The **Mega Vacuum Rover costs $6,000** and has a large front intake.

Its displayed specifications include a **4-meter intake** and a **3,000 kg tank** before additional capacity upgrades.

That lets it collect much larger amounts of Dust per trip, but a huge hopper is not always the most important upgrade.

The vehicle's **Suction Control** creates a useful tradeoff: increasing suction widens the cleaning strip but reduces travel speed.

Choose a high-suction setting for dense, uncleared ground where you want to maximize the area cleaned per pass. More travel speed can be useful when crossing already clean terrain to reach another destination.

An upgraded hopper reached **5,700 kg** in one late-game configuration. That is a result of additional vehicle investment, not the Mega Vacuum Rover's starting capacity.

### Return to the Garage before buying more vehicles

The Garage handles one selected vehicle in its bay at a time. Move the current vehicle out or recall it as needed before trying to deploy another.

A collector pad can unload vehicle Dust into storage, but the load still needs a working Conveyor output. Connect the receiver and test the [vehicle unloading route](/moon-sweepers/automation#vehicle-unloading) before returning with a full hopper.

The decision to buy another vehicle should depend on the amount of new ground you can realistically clean and how quickly your current setup can empty its collected Dust.

## How Do Money, Vehicles, and Upgrades Work in Multiplayer?

Moon Sweepers supports several players cleaning in the same world, but shared work does not mean every purchase is automatically shared.

The Garage's vehicle description specifies that **each player buys their own vehicles**.

Tool upgrades also need attention. In one multiplayer session, individual brush capacity upgrades remained personal to the player rather than automatically applying to everyone.

That changes how a group should spend money.

A player focused on large-area cleaning benefits from their own equipment upgrades. Someone handling construction and production needs access to the relevant Building Tool and infrastructure. Having one teammate purchase an expensive personal upgrade does not guarantee that every teammate receives the same benefit.

### Divide the work by actual equipment

There are several useful ways to organize a cleaning session:

- **Nearby manual cleaning:** Players finish adjacent hexes and bring Dust back to the same station area.
- **Vehicle exploration:** A player with a suitable vehicle clears distant terrain and handles nearby flags or survey sites.
- **Construction and logistics:** One player checks that collected Dust is entering the transport and selling system instead of accumulating outside the Depot.

The advantage comes from reducing wasted work. Several players can be busy collecting Dust while the entire operation earns little money because none of them has checked the receiving route.

Before spending group time on a major expansion, establish which player can use the equipment and what each person needs to buy personally.

For achievement tracking, multiplayer participation and the associated unlock are covered in the [Moon Sweepers achievements checklist](/moon-sweepers/achievements#coop-vehicle).

## What Should You Do When the Moon Gets Dark?

Moon Sweepers has a day-night cycle, and the nighttime darkness can make manual cleanup noticeably harder.

The Field Manual describes the helmet lamp activating in darkness, with upgrades that can improve its brightness.

A dark starting area is not necessarily a broken save. In one early run, the player began cleaning in poor visibility and daylight arrived naturally later.

### Cleaning while visibility is low

When the ground is difficult to see, work close to an area whose borders you already recognize.

Short passes near the station or between completed hexes are easier to control than driving into unfamiliar terrain without a clear destination.

The Moon Map remains useful for finding untouched and partly cleaned regions even when surface details are hard to recognize in person.

Upgraded lighting and better movement equipment become more attractive if you regularly spend long periods working in darkness or traveling over uneven terrain.

### Why a Powered Factory May Stop at Night

Manual cleaning can continue in darkness, but solar-powered machinery may stop when the grid runs low on stored energy. Check the battery and connected power demand before replacing equipment that worked during daylight.

The [Moon Sweepers power setup](/moon-sweepers/automation#power) explains how to read the grid status and maintain production through the night.

## When Should You Stop Buying Brooms and Start Automating?

Manual cleaning and vehicles remain useful, but they require someone to operate them. Automation changes that relationship by letting machines collect, transport, and process Dust.

The first affordable fixed cleaner is the **Robot Vacuum Mk I**, priced at **$250**. Its dock covers **19 world hexes**, and the collected Dust can be packed into capsules for a Conveyor.

That sounds attractive while you are still using a broom, but its coverage is limited. It eventually finishes the dirty ground around its dock.

The important decision is whether you need to clear new terrain yourself or want a fixed area cleaned while you do something else.

### Three reasons to invest in automation

**You are spending too much time transporting capsules.** A Robot Vacuum and Conveyor can reduce manual handling for a fixed cleaning area.

**Your vehicle collects more Dust than you can comfortably process.** A proper unloading route and processing system can turn large vehicle loads into regular sales.

**You want to work toward long-term Moon completion.** Larger automated cleaners, including JONAS, can operate beyond one small dock's fixed coverage, although the actual rate of whole-Moon progress still needs to be measured.

The full production system has different decisions from the early-game purchase route: Conveyor direction, collector outputs, Moon Sand production, Stone Press material inputs, electricity, and machine bottlenecks.

Follow the [Moon Sweepers automation guide](/moon-sweepers/automation) when you are ready to build that system.

There is no requirement to complete all manual tool upgrades before construction. Equally, owning a Building Tool does not mean you should spend your remaining money on machinery whose output you cannot yet sell.

## How Far Are You from Cleaning the Entire Moon?

Local progress and worldwide progress operate at dramatically different scales.

You may clean a nearby section completely, earn its completion reward, and still see almost no visible movement in the overall Moon percentage.

A Moon Map from an active run displayed **0.09% completion** despite substantial cleaning around the starting area. The same map showed more than **163,000 total sectors**.

This is why the first few hours can feel productive without bringing you anywhere near a full Moon cleanup.

As your operation expands, check progress through three different results:

1. **Completed ground:** Are you actually increasing the number of fully cleaned sectors rather than leaving every region partly finished?
2. **Money earned:** Is your collection and selling operation producing enough to afford the next meaningful improvement?
3. **Time spent handling Dust:** Are you still manually packing, carrying, and depositing most of the material, or has equipment taken over some of that work?

The balance between these changes over time. Early in the game, earning enough for a wider broom is an immediate win. Later, you may have plenty of money while still facing an enormous number of untouched sectors.

The game has **33 achievements**, including milestones for Dust collected, money earned and spent, successful deposits, special interactions, and **1%, 10%, 50%, and 100% Moon completion**.

The [Moon Sweepers achievements checklist](/moon-sweepers/achievements) tracks those requirements individually. Its Moon-completion section also explains how to estimate remaining cleaning time using progress measured from your own game.

For now, the most useful improvement is not necessarily the most expensive machine available. It is the purchase that removes your current limitation while still helping you clear more of the Moon.
