A Robot Vacuum can be cleaning perfectly while its dust capsules sit motionless at the end of a Conveyor. A Pressure Chamber can be fully connected but produce nothing because its input faces the wrong direction. Even a working Moonstone factory can grind to a halt when one machine cannot process materials as quickly as the others deliver them.

The first goal is to get dust moving from a collection machine to the Depot without carrying each capsule yourself. Once that works, you can add processing machines, branch the belts, and expand the system without rebuilding everything each time.

## How to Set Up Your First Automatic Dust Conveyor

A **Robot Vacuum Mk I costs $250** and cleans the **19 world hexes around its dock**, covering two rings. It returns collected dust to its dock, which packages the dust into capsules for a Conveyor.

The important limitation is its fixed cleaning area. It will not keep traveling across the entire Moon after finishing those hexes. Place it somewhere with plenty of untouched ground rather than directly beside the regions you have already cleared.

### Buy the building equipment without running out of money

The Building Tool costs **$50**. Once equipped, it opens the structures, logistics, production, and power options needed to establish automation.

For the first setup, budget for:

- **Building Tool — $50**
- **Robot Vacuum Mk I — $250**
- **Conveyor — $0.50 per meter**

A 30-meter Conveyor section costs $15, bringing this example setup to approximately **$315**. Actual Conveyor costs depend on the distance to your Depot.

Keep extra money available for power equipment when you begin adding processing machines. A Solar Post costs $75.

### Place the Robot Vacuum and connect its dock

Find a patch of Moon where the Robot Vacuum's cleaning radius will cover mostly dirty hexes.

1. Equip the Building Tool and select **Robot Vacuum Mk I**.
2. Choose an uncleared area with enough room for the dock and outgoing Conveyor.
3. Rotate the dock so its capsule output faces an accessible route toward the Depot.
4. Place the dock and start a Conveyor from its output.
5. Extend the Conveyor toward the Depot, checking the belt direction at each connection.

![Robot Vacuum Mk I dock with a conveyor belt being connected to its capsule output.](/images/moon-sweepers/moon-sweepers-robot-vacuum-dock.webp)

*Start the Conveyor at the vacuum dock's output and follow the arrows away from the machine. Extend the belt toward the Depot to transport collected dust capsules.*

The Conveyor tool can place up to **30 meters in one section**. Longer routes are built by extending the belt in additional sections.

Check the direction of travel each time you extend it. A continuous-looking belt is not useful if one section sends capsules the wrong way.

### Make sure the capsules actually reach the Depot

When the first capsules arrive at an unfinished belt, they can accumulate at its end without dropping off or selling.

Extend the Conveyor into the Depot's receiving area. Once properly connected, capsules can be delivered and sold automatically.

![Dust capsules traveling along a conveyor belt inside a lunar station.](/images/moon-sweepers/moon-sweepers-conveyor-depot-connection.webp)

Do not judge the installation immediately after placing the robot. Give it time to collect dust, return to its dock, and release capsules.

The complete working sequence is:

**Dirty Hexes → Robot Vacuum → Output Dock → Conveyor → Depot**

A successful setup should show all three results: the vacuum removes dirt, capsules travel along the belt, and money increases when they reach the Depot.

If the robot is cleaning but the balance never changes, follow the capsules instead of purchasing another vacuum.

## Robot Vacuum Mk I or Mk II: How Much Can Each Clean?

The two robot models differ primarily in how much ground they can cover from a single dock.

Mk II covers **18 more hexes** than Mk I. That is almost twice the area for twice the purchase price, before considering the Conveyor route, available terrain, and any supporting infrastructure.

The map is useful for checking which areas your robots are assigned to clean. A vacuum working on a remote hill may be difficult to see from the Depot, even when it is functioning normally.

### Where should the next vacuum go?

When the first robot is nearing the end of its assigned area, inspect the surrounding Moon before buying another.

A new dock is most useful where its coverage extends into fresh regions. Placing several vacuums next to one another can leave them working on overlapping terrain, reducing the additional area gained from each purchase.

There is another practical consideration: the new dock needs a route for its capsules. A good cleaning location is not necessarily a good logistics location.

For a remote vacuum, check both the dirty hexes around the planned dock and the cost of connecting its output to an existing Conveyor. Sometimes a slightly closer site gives you a simpler system with less rebuilding.

Once a vacuum has finished its reachable dirty ground, move your attention to a fresh area rather than waiting for it to clean outside its assigned range.

## How to Unload Sweepers into a Silo and Conveyor

Vehicles are a different kind of dust collection. Instead of a stationary robot gathering material around a dock, you drive or operate the vehicle through dirty regions and return with a much larger load.

The transport problem changes accordingly: you need a place where that load can enter your automated system.

The **Garage** provides access to vehicles, while the associated collection and silo equipment handles unloading. Check the collector pad and its Conveyor connection before making a long trip.

### Connect the unloading route first

Prepare the receiving side before filling the vehicle.

1. Set up the Garage and the vehicle's unloading area.
2. Identify the **collector pad** used to receive the vehicle's dust.
3. Connect the silo's capsule output to a Conveyor.
4. Run that Conveyor toward the Depot or a processing machine.
5. Return with a dust load and use the unloading interaction.
6. Confirm that the silo begins releasing capsules into the connected system.

The silo provides temporary storage. It does not eliminate the need for a working outgoing route.

A vehicle can unload successfully while the money remains unchanged because its contents are still waiting inside the logistics network.

### What does the Packager do?

The **Packager costs $100** and offers another way to handle dust at a collection point. It becomes useful when repeated trips around the base and manual capsule handling are slowing down the operation.

![A Packager being used beside the dust collection and transport system.](/images/moon-sweepers/moon-sweepers-packager.webp)

When deciding whether to build one, look at the actual problem:

- Repeatedly walking around to handle capsules is a good reason to improve the collection point.
- A fully loaded vehicle with nowhere to unload needs a receiving setup.
- Capsules already waiting at a processing-machine input need a production fix, not another collection device.
- Capsules waiting at the end of a Conveyor need a working destination.

For a Push Sweeper, a Garage and an unloading route can make a much greater difference than another small improvement to your handheld brush.

<section id="pressure-chamber">

## Pressure Chamber: How to Turn Moon Dust into Moon Sand

Selling raw Dust gets the first Conveyor running, but a **Pressure Chamber** adds a processing step that increases its sale value.

The machine costs **$500** and turns Moon Dust into **Moon Sand**, which sells for **three times the value of raw Dust**.

Its listed processing capacity is **up to 5 kg every 12 seconds**. That works out to a theoretical maximum of **25 kg per minute** while continuously supplied and operating normally.

This is a machine capacity, not a guaranteed whole-factory production rate. Material delivery, power, and the outgoing Conveyor can all reduce actual throughput.

### Connect the inlet before unloading dust

The Pressure Chamber has a directional input and output. Its orientation is particularly easy to misread when placing it near a Conveyor that already exists.

![The Pressure Chamber's inlet orientation and the connected dust Conveyor.](/images/moon-sweepers/moon-sweepers-pressure-chamber-inlet.webp)

The input is on the **side without the handle**. Point that side toward the incoming Dust Conveyor.

Build the simplest possible line first:

**Dust Conveyor → Pressure Chamber → Moon Sand Conveyor → Depot**

Watch the first dust capsule enter the machine. Then wait for Moon Sand to appear on the output.

A machine that sits beside a belt without accepting material is not correctly connected merely because the two objects look close together.

### Why does the Pressure Chamber have dust but produce nothing?

The Pressure Chamber requires **10 kW** to operate.

![Pressure Chamber information showing its processing capacity and power requirement.](/images/moon-sweepers/moon-sweepers-pressure-chamber-power.webp)

A basic Solar Post provides **3 kW**. Two posts therefore provide only 6 kW of nominal generation, which is below the Pressure Chamber's stated requirement.

For comparison, the **Solar Twin (M)** provides **7.5 kW** of nominal generation.

A single Pressure Chamber therefore needs more than three basic Solar Posts to meet its 10 kW demand through solar generation alone. Two Solar Twin (M) units can nominally provide 15 kW in sunlight, but other connected machines add to the total demand.

Before spending more money, inspect three different things:

- **Input:** Is Dust actually entering the Pressure Chamber?
- **Power:** Does the connected network have enough generation and stored energy?
- **Output:** Is there a clear route for the Moon Sand to leave?

A reversed machine, an insufficient power supply, and a blocked output can all look like the same problem from a distance.

<section id="power">

## How to Keep Automation Running When Power Drops

The factory can appear healthy during daylight and then begin producing intermittently as stored energy runs out.

Expanding a factory also increases the load on the power network. A supply that was sufficient for one Pressure Chamber may no longer support a second processing machine.

The known requirements establish the scale:

- **Solar Post:** 3 kW nominal generation.
- **Solar Twin (M):** 7.5 kW nominal generation.
- **Pressure Chamber:** 10 kW required.
- **Stone Press:** 18 kW required.

A Stone Press and Pressure Chamber together require **28 kW** of operating power before accounting for other connected equipment.

Do not confuse the number of solar structures with the available power. Compare their stated generation against the machines that are connected to them, then inspect the network's stored charge.

Solar generation also depends on daylight. A system with ample nominal generation may still stop at night if the stored energy cannot sustain the machines.

### Check the battery before replacing anything

When a factory stops without any obvious Conveyor damage, look at the power display before demolishing a machine.

![Solar Twin power status showing zero solar generation at night, battery discharge, and a fully loaded power grid.](/images/moon-sweepers/moon-sweepers-solar-power-status.webp)

*At night, solar output can fall to zero while the battery supplies the connected machines. Check stored energy and grid demand before expanding the factory.*

Check whether the network has sufficient charge, whether the required devices are connected, and whether the system resumes when sunlight returns.

Additional solar generation helps restore energy while sunlight is available. Stored power determines whether that production continues through periods without enough sunlight.

A particularly expensive mistake is buying a Stone Press with just enough money for the machine and no budget left for the power infrastructure needed to run it.

<section id="stone-press">

## Stone Press: How to Make Moonstone from Dust and Sand

The **Stone Press costs $1,000** and processes **1 kg of Moon Dust and 1 kg of Moon Sand into 2 kg of Moonstone every 6 seconds**.

![Stone Press production requirements showing its recipe, six-second processing cycle, power demand, and purchase price.](/images/moon-sweepers/moon-sweepers-stone-press-stats.webp)

Moonstone sells for **seven times the value of raw Moon Dust**. The machine requires **18 kW** and can theoretically produce up to **20 kg of Moonstone per minute** when continuously supplied and powered.

Moon Sand must be produced by a Pressure Chamber, while part of the incoming Dust must remain unprocessed to supply the Stone Press's other input.

### Split the Dust before the Pressure Chamber

A working Moonstone line needs two separate material feeds. Sending all incoming Dust through the Pressure Chamber leaves the Stone Press without the raw Dust required for its recipe.

1. Split the incoming Dust Conveyor into two branches.
2. Connect one branch to the Stone Press's Dust input.
3. Feed the other branch into a Pressure Chamber to make Moon Sand.
4. Connect the Pressure Chamber's Sand output to the Stone Press's remaining input.
5. Connect the Stone Press's output to the Conveyor leading toward the Depot.

![Stone Press placement preview showing the conveyor connection direction and input/output orientation.](/images/moon-sweepers/moon-sweepers-stone-press-layout.webp)

*Check the input and output orientation before placing the Stone Press. Reversing the machine can leave both material lines disconnected.*

### Is Moonstone always the better way to make money?

The higher selling value is attractive, but it does not automatically make every factory faster.

The Stone Press consumes two types of material and requires more power than the Pressure Chamber. Adding it to a small factory can create a new bottleneck even while the individual output capsules are worth more.

Consider the state of your current operation before investing:

- A small stream of Dust that barely keeps one Pressure Chamber working does not yet demonstrate a need for a Stone Press.
- A growing stockpile with sufficient power may justify adding processing capacity.
- A Stone Press that repeatedly waits for one ingredient needs its input branches examined before you buy a second press.
- A factory whose output is blocked will not improve merely because the processed material is more valuable.

The seven-times sale multiplier is a material value, not a measured hourly profit multiplier. The only useful comparison is how much money the entire working system produces over the same period.

<section id="splitters-mergers">

## How to Use Splitters and Mergers Without Rebuilding the Factory

The first serious expansion usually happens when a processing machine cannot handle everything arriving from a vehicle or collection dock.

Capsules begin accumulating, and income arrives more slowly than new Dust is delivered.

**Splitters** divide one Conveyor route into multiple branches. **Mergers** combine branches back into a shared route.

These connections can snap onto an existing Conveyor, so expanding a production line does not necessarily require demolishing the entire original route.

### Add a second Pressure Chamber

A growing queue of Dust capsules at the Pressure Chamber's input can indicate that the machine cannot keep up with incoming material.

Build another Pressure Chamber nearby, then place a Splitter on the existing incoming Conveyor.

![A Splitter installed on the existing Conveyor, with a new belt connection being drawn.](/images/moon-sweepers/moon-sweepers-conveyor-splitter.webp)

Connect one branch to each Pressure Chamber's input. The Splitter divides the incoming route, but both branches still need their own belt connections.

After the chambers, use a Merger to bring their outputs back onto the Conveyor leading to the Depot.

![A Merger being positioned on the Conveyor, with its input and output direction shown.](/images/moon-sweepers/moon-sweepers-conveyor-merger.webp)

Check the Merger's input and output orientation before completing the connection. Two branches can appear close together without actually feeding into the same outgoing belt.

### When the Splitter will not connect

A Splitter may refuse to snap into the desired spot, especially when the Conveyor is close to another machine or surrounded by existing branches.

Move farther along the incoming belt and try a section with more open space.

A branch can also leave the Splitter in the wrong direction after rotation. Check its output before completing the next long Conveyor segment.

Building several disconnected pieces and trying to force them together afterward can make the problem worse. Establish the branch direction first, then complete the run toward the machine.

### Route Conveyors over one another

Conveyors can be elevated and brought back down. This is useful when two material branches need to cross without joining.

Raise the new route, carry it above the existing belt, and lower it near the correct machine input.

An elevated crossing does not automatically connect to the belt beneath it. The two routes can remain separate until you deliberately add a Merger.

Keep the final approach into each machine simple. Complicated bends and height changes immediately beside an input make it much harder to recognize a reversed or disconnected segment.

### Rebuilding has a cost

Removing a structure can return less than its original purchase price.

One $200 structure returned $150 when removed, leaving a $50 loss. Check the removal preview before dismantling expensive machinery, rather than assuming every structure follows the same refund rule.

Before placing a processing machine, identify its inputs, output, available space, and power connections.

<section id="production-bottlenecks">

## Why Is My Automated Factory Making Money So Slowly?

Once the machinery becomes more complicated, the amount of Dust collected is no longer a reliable measure of how much money the factory can produce.

A Sweeper can bring back hundreds of kilograms while the processing machines remain busy with an earlier load.

The growing pile does not mean collection has failed. It means material is entering the system faster than at least one part of the line can clear it.

### Find the slowest stage before expanding

Follow one shipment through the entire route.

**Collection:** Does the robot or vehicle release its Dust into the transport system?

**Transport:** Are capsules moving continuously, or is a Conveyor segment obstructed?

**Processing:** Are the Pressure Chambers and Stone Presses accepting their required materials?

**Power:** Do the machines keep running, or do they stop as stored charge falls?

**Delivery:** Does processed output reach the Depot and produce a payout?

The location of the accumulating capsules is often more useful than the amount of money in your account.

A backlog before the Pressure Chamber suggests that its available processing capacity cannot keep up with the incoming stream, provided the machine is powered and connected correctly.

A backlog between the Pressure Chamber and Stone Press suggests that the later production stage needs attention.

Dust and Sand sitting at a Stone Press's inputs require a closer check of its operating power, available ingredients, and output route.

### When is another Stone Press worth buying?

A larger setup can run multiple Stone Presses in parallel.

Split the required Dust and Sand streams across the machines, then merge the Moonstone outputs into a shared delivery line.

This expansion helps when the original Stone Press is the limiting step and both additional machines receive enough materials and power.

Adding another Press while the Sand supply is already inadequate merely creates two machines competing for the same ingredient.

Likewise, doubling processing equipment without increasing power capacity can make the whole installation less reliable.

### Check throughput using actual inventory

A useful test needs only a short period of ordinary factory operation.

1. Stop adding new vehicles or machines.
2. Observe where capsules are collecting.
3. Allow the current installation to run under adequate power.
4. Check whether each queue grows, shrinks, or remains stable.
5. Expand the stage whose queue persists despite normal operation.

The Pressure Chamber can process up to **25 kg of Dust per minute**, while the Stone Press consumes **10 kg of Dust and 10 kg of Moon Sand per minute** to produce up to **20 kg of Moonstone**. These are theoretical capacities under continuous operation, not guaranteed factory output.

Compare the material queues at each machine before expanding. More Dust collection will not increase Moonstone production when the Sand supply, power network, or outgoing Conveyor is already limiting the line.

You want the expensive machinery receiving material regularly without leaving its output trapped downstream.

## How to Set Up JONAS and the Capsule Catcher

The fixed Robot Vacuums eventually finish their assigned areas. Their cleaning radius makes them useful for controlled local automation, but the Moon contains far more dirty hexes than a single dock can reach.

**JONAS** is the major change to that system.

It costs **$20,000** and uses six arms to clean the nearest remaining dirty hexes, one hex at a time. Unlike a stationary Robot Vacuum, its cleaning task is not limited to the original dock's 19- or 37-hex area.

JONAS is expensive enough that the rest of your logistics network should already be capable of processing and delivering large quantities of Dust before you purchase it.

### Buy the Capsule Catcher as well

JONAS fires collected Dust capsules toward the nearest **Capsule Catcher**, which costs **$1,500**.

Without a Capsule Catcher, JONAS brings a full container back home and waits. That interrupts the unattended collection cycle.

A practical initial purchase budget is therefore:

- **JONAS — $20,000**
- **Capsule Catcher — $1,500**
- **Total — $21,500**, before additional Conveyor construction or processing expansion.

Place the Capsule Catcher where its output can connect cleanly to your existing logistics network. Inspect the output direction before committing to a long Conveyor route.

Then purchase and deploy JONAS, allow it to reach a dirty hex, and observe the full collection cycle.

**JONAS → Capsule Catcher → Conveyor → Processing → Depot**

The Capsule Catcher does not replace the Pressure Chamber, Stone Press, or Depot. It provides the connection between JONAS's remote collection and your existing production line.

### How fast can JONAS clean the Moon?

The important advantage is that JONAS can seek remaining dirty hexes without requiring you to reposition a stationary dock for every new cleaning area.

Its real cleaning speed, however, needs to be measured during sustained operation. A short observation of a few cleaned hexes does not establish a reliable number of hexes per hour.

Multiple JONAS units also should not automatically be treated as perfectly linear scaling. Actual results may depend on travel, target selection, capsule delivery, and the performance of the rest of the factory.

Before spending another $20,000, determine whether the first machine is continuously cleaning and successfully delivering its Dust.

A second automated collector adds little value to your income if the production line is already unable to handle the first.

For the long-term **1%, 10%, 50%, and 100% Moon** targets, keep track of your actual cleaned-hex progress. You can record completion milestones in the [Moon Sweepers achievements tracker](/moon-sweepers/achievements) while expanding the automated system.

## Automation Not Working? Check These Failures

Follow the material until you find the first point where movement or production stops. The location of that failure usually tells you what to inspect next.

- **Robot Vacuum cleans, but no money arrives:** Follow a capsule from the dock. If it stops at an unfinished belt, extend or reconnect the Conveyor. Confirm that delivery to the Depot produces a payout.
- **Pressure Chamber refuses Dust:** Check that the incoming belt reaches the inlet on the side without the handle. If Dust enters but no Sand appears, inspect power and the output route.
- **Stone Press stays idle:** Look for Dust and Moon Sand arriving at their separate inputs. When both are present, check operating power and whether Moonstone can leave the machine.
- **Production stops after sunset:** Inspect battery charge and total grid demand. A machine that works during daylight may stop when stored energy runs low.
- **Capsules keep piling up:** Find the first queue that does not shrink. Clear a blocked route or increase the processing capacity at that stage.
- **JONAS returns and waits:** Check the Capsule Catcher and its outgoing Conveyor. Capturing Dust is not enough if the capsules cannot move onward.
- **Robot Vacuum stops cleaning:** Check the remaining dirty ground within its assigned coverage. Once the local area is finished, use another location rather than extending the same dock's Conveyor.

Correct the first failing connection or machine, then let one complete batch reach the Depot before expanding the factory again.

<section id="expansion-decisions">

## What Should You Upgrade Next?

A working factory creates more choices than the early Toothbrush stage, but each purchase should solve a specific limitation.

The next purchase depends on what is currently holding up your progress.

- **Manual capsule handling is taking too much time:** Improve the collection and unloading route.
- **A Robot Vacuum has finished its nearby hexes:** Place a new dock where it covers fresh ground, or work toward JONAS.
- **Raw Dust is already reaching the Depot reliably:** Consider a Pressure Chamber to increase the value of processed material.
- **Dust is piling up before the Pressure Chamber:** Check power and input orientation, then consider another chamber if capacity is the actual limit.
- **Moon Sand is available and power is sufficient:** A Stone Press becomes a practical option for higher-value processing.
- **Moonstone production cannot keep up with both ingredient streams:** Expand the bottleneck, not the entire factory at once.
- **The factory depends on daylight to function:** Improve the power network before adding more machinery.

One efficient change is usually more valuable than several purchases made without checking what is already working.

When the collection, processing, and delivery stages all operate continuously, the factory begins doing the work that previously required repeated manual trips back to the Depot. That is the point where further expansion becomes a deliberate capacity decision rather than another attempt to repair a disconnected belt.

</section>
