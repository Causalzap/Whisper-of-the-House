**Moon Sweepers has 33 Steam achievements**, but reaching 100% completion involves several different kinds of progress. Cleaning the Moon advances the worldwide percentage milestones. Collecting Dust, making Depot deposits, launching containers, and earning or spending money have their own requirements.

Some achievements unlock during ordinary play. Others need a particular action, such as cleaning while driving, starting a multiplayer game, or sending Dust around a closed Conveyor loop. The longest goals reach **10,000 Depot deposits**, **$1 million earned and spent**, and **100% of the Moon cleaned**.

The useful way to approach them is to finish the special actions as their equipment becomes available, then let your normal cleaning and production work contribute toward the larger totals.

## First Achievements: Brush, Deposit, Launch, and Building

Five achievements can be handled before investing in a complicated factory. They also establish an important distinction: **collecting Dust, depositing Dust, and launching a container are different actions**.

### One Small Sweep and Dust Delivery

**One Small Sweep** unlocks after cleaning your first patch of lunar dust. Take the starting Toothbrush from the supply wall, leave the station, and brush a dirty surface until the game registers the first cleaned patch.

For **Dust Delivery**, return to the Depot with collected Dust and complete the deposit interaction. Filling your brush's storage or packing a capsule outside the station is not the same as depositing it at the Depot.

The first container can be small. You do not need a better brush, a completed region, or an automated Conveyor for either of these achievements.

### Return to Sender — Launch a Full Container

The requirement is to **launch a full dust container into space**, not merely collect enough Dust to create one capsule.

Carry Dust to the station and deposit it into the Depot's receiving system. Use the launch control once the container is full and ready to send. Check that the launch actually completes.

![Manual Dust deposit at the lunar station showing 0.5 kg added and the Deposited confirmation.](/images/moon-sweepers/moon-sweepers-depot-manual-deposit.webp)

If the achievement does not appear after depositing Dust, check whether you have completed the launch itself. A successful deposit can increase stored Dust without satisfying an achievement that explicitly requires a launch.

This distinction also matters for **Frequent Flyer Dust**, which tracks contributions to multiple successful launches rather than ordinary deposits.

### Get A Better Brush — Upgrade the Tool

**Get A Better Brush** requires upgrading a brush for the first time.

Open the **Upgrade Matrix** and purchase an available brush upgrade. Simply collecting money or switching to a different brush is not the same action as applying an upgrade.

If you're deciding where to spend your first earnings, compare the [brush prices and container upgrades](/moon-sweepers#brush-upgrades) before purchasing.

Early upgrades can improve carrying capacity and cleaning performance. For this achievement, one actual upgrade is enough; there is no need to maximize an entire brush's upgrade tree.

### Moon Improvement Project — Place a Structure

**Moon Improvement Project** unlocks when you place your first structure.

The **Building Tool costs $50** and gives access to construction. Equip it, choose an affordable structure, and complete the placement.

Buying the Building Tool alone is not the achievement condition. The structure needs to be placed in the world.

A first Robot Vacuum or another useful piece of construction can contribute to this milestone while also developing your cleaning operation. The full machine setup is covered in the [Moon Sweepers automation guide](/moon-sweepers/automation).

## Let the Belt Do It and the Hidden Conveyor Loop Achievement

There are two Conveyor-related achievements, but their requirements are different.

**Let the Belt Do It** needs Dust to move on a Conveyor for the first time. **It'll Get There Eventually** requires Dust to travel around a **closed Conveyor loop**.

A working delivery belt can satisfy the first requirement without satisfying the hidden one.

### Let the Belt Do It — Get a Dust Capsule Moving

Start with a short Conveyor connected to a source of Dust capsules, such as a Robot Vacuum's output dock.

1. Place the collection equipment and identify its capsule output.
2. Connect a Conveyor in the direction of travel.
3. Let the equipment collect Dust and release a capsule.
4. Watch the capsule move along the belt.

The requirement is actual Dust movement, not simply placing a Conveyor on the ground.

A Robot Vacuum may take time to collect its first load. If nothing moves immediately, inspect the output dock, belt direction, and whether capsules have begun appearing.

You can extend the belt toward the Depot afterward. For a complete automatic collection and selling setup, follow the [Robot Vacuum and Conveyor instructions](/moon-sweepers/automation#first-automation).

### It'll Get There Eventually — Closed Conveyor Loop

This hidden achievement requires sending Dust **around a closed Conveyor loop**.

A curved belt or a Conveyor that turns back toward the Depot is not necessarily a closed loop. The requirement describes a continuous circuit that allows Dust to travel around it.

For an attempt, use open ground near an available Dust capsule:

1. Build a short Conveyor circuit with enough space to turn the route back toward its starting point.
2. Check whether the game's connection indicators allow the final section to join the beginning of the circuit.
3. Introduce a Dust capsule into the connected route.
4. Watch whether it continues circulating instead of stopping at an open end or leaving for the Depot.

Do not mistake a loop-shaped construction for a working closed circuit. The important test is whether the capsule can keep traveling around the connected path.

Keep the test circuit small. Make sure the belt forms a continuous connection rather than merely ending beside its starting point. After a capsule travels around the loop, check whether the achievement unlocks before changing the layout.

## OH You Have Friends? and Finally, Wheels

These two achievements require actions that ordinary handheld cleaning will never complete.

### OH You Have Friends? — Start a Multiplayer Game

**OH You Have Friends?** requires starting a game with at least one friend or other player.

Create or open a multiplayer-accessible session and have another player join. The achievement has been triggered during an active session when a second player arrived.

Merely opening a lobby that allows friends to join is not enough to demonstrate that the multiplayer requirement has been fulfilled. Make sure someone actually enters the game.

For a player who usually plays solo, this is worth handling early because the requirement depends on another participant.

Multiplayer also has a practical complication: not every tool upgrade automatically transfers between players. A second player may need to purchase their own brush upgrades, even while taking part in the same cleaning operation.

### Finally, Wheels — Clean While Driving

**Finally, Wheels** requires cleaning Dust **while driving a vehicle**.

Purchasing a vehicle, entering the Garage, or moving around without cleaning does not complete the stated objective.

Drive a cleaning vehicle over uncleared ground and activate its cleaning function. If you haven't bought one yet, compare the [Ride-on Sweeper and Lunar Sweeper](/moon-sweepers#vehicles). Simply crossing already clean terrain will not satisfy the requirement.

Do not assume that operating a walk-behind Push Sweeper automatically fulfills an achievement specifically worded around driving.

Once the action registers, you can continue using vehicles to gather larger Dust loads. Vehicle choice and progression fit into your wider cleaning strategy; connecting vehicle output to a Conveyor belongs to the [automation setup](/moon-sweepers/automation#vehicle-unloading).

## Dust Bunny to Moon Dust Removal Specialist: 10–10,000 kg

The four Dust collection achievements form one cumulative progression.

- **Dust Bunny:** Collect **10 kg**.
- **Pocket Full of Moon:** Collect **100 kg**.
- **A Literal Ton of Dust:** Collect **1,000 kg**.
- **Moon Dust Removal Specialist:** Collect **10,000 kg**.

The early thresholds can be reached with handheld tools. The 10 kg milestone appears during the opening cleanup, and the 1,000 kg milestone is reachable through continued tool upgrades and manual collection.

The main improvement is reducing how frequently you have to stop cleaning.

A larger brush capacity allows more Dust to be gathered before packing or depositing it. Wider cleaning equipment makes individual passes more productive. Vehicles become increasingly useful once the repeated walk back to the station consumes more time than clearing the next dirty region.

### Does Automated Dust Count Toward These Achievements?

A machine collecting and selling Dust is not automatically proof that all of it contributes to your personal **Collect X kg** total.

These achievements use the word **collect**, while other achievements explicitly track **deposits** or **earnings**. Those counters should not be treated as interchangeable.

For the long 10,000 kg goal, watch your in-game achievement progress after manual collection and after operating automated equipment. Use whichever collection method actually advances the required counter.

If a factory earns money without moving the personal collection milestone, the problem is not necessarily low production. The achievement may be measuring a different action from the one your factory performs.

The most reliable initial path is to build the 10 kg, 100 kg, and 1,000 kg milestones through ordinary active cleaning, then measure how your later equipment contributes before relying on it for the remaining total.

## Depot Resident and Do You Live Here? — Deposit Counts Explained

There are three Depot deposit milestones and one separate launch milestone:

- **Regular Customer:** Deposit Dust at the Depot **100 times**.
- **Depot Resident:** Make **1,000** deposits.
- **Do You Live Here?:** Make **10,000** deposits.
- **Frequent Flyer Dust:** Contribute Dust to **10 successful launches**.

These requirements are about events rather than simply collecting a certain total weight.

### Regular Customer — Reach 100 Deposits

Regular Customer can unlock during repeated manual deliveries to the station, before reaching large-scale automation. In one early cleanup session, the **100-deposit achievement** appeared while the player was repeatedly bringing capsules back to the Depot.

If you are working toward this milestone, keep making actual Depot deposits and check the counter or achievement unlock instead of estimating progress from the total kilograms collected.

A large Dust load does not necessarily mean the game has registered a large number of separate deposit events.

### Frequent Flyer Dust — Ten Successful Launches

The achievement description is **contribute dust to 10 successful launches**.

Keep the Depot supplied and allow containers to complete their launches. Do not confuse ten deposits with ten successful launches.

You also do not need to assume you personally must operate the launch control ten times: the requirement is worded around **contributing Dust** to successful launches.

Check the launch outcome rather than counting every capsule as a separate launch.

### Can Conveyors Complete 1,000 or 10,000 Deposits?

Automated Conveyors can deliver Dust without the same manual handling required early in the game, but that does not establish exactly how the achievement counter counts each automated delivery.

For **Depot Resident** and **Do You Live Here?**, this distinction can decide whether an unattended factory is helpful or whether repeated player-controlled deposits remain necessary.

Test it before committing to the 10,000-deposit milestone:

1. Note your current progress toward the next deposit achievement, if the game displays it.
2. Complete a number of manual deposits.
3. Compare the result with a similar period of automated delivery.
4. Continue with the method that actually advances the counter.

Do not assume that processing more kilograms through one shipment produces more deposit events.

A 10,000-deposit requirement is large enough that finding the correct counted interaction matters more than increasing Dust production alone.

## Making a Dent, Patchwork Moon, and Hex Appeal

These achievements require the cleaning of regions or sections, rather than simply collecting Dust.

Their thresholds are:

- **That's Going to Take a While:** Clean your first **region**.
- **Making a Dent:** Help complete **10 cleaning regions**.
- **A Good Start:** Help clean **100 sections**.
- **Patchwork Moon:** Help clean **1,000 sections**.
- **Hex Appeal:** Help clean **10,000 sections**.

The game uses **regions** and **sections** in different achievement descriptions. Keep the two requirements separate instead of treating ten regions as equivalent to ten sections.

### Check When a Section Is Fully Cleaned

A section may still contain narrow strips of Dust even when most of its surface looks clean. Use the local completion percentage to find the remaining ground, then continue until the display reaches **100%**.

![Sector 58066 showing 100% and the SECTION COMPLETE notification.](/images/moon-sweepers/moon-sweepers-section-100-percent.webp)

The SECTION COMPLETE message confirms that the local cleaning target is finished. Completing an early hex can also award a $10 bonus, but that reward is separate from the achievement's required region or section count.

### Why 100 Sections Is Not 100% of the Moon

The **100-section**, **1,000-section**, and **10,000-section** achievements count completed sections.

They are not the same as the Moon-wide 1%, 10%, 50%, and 100% milestones.

This difference becomes obvious when checking the Moon Map. A substantial number of cleaned hexes can still represent a tiny fraction of the full lunar surface.

For the section achievements, use your map and the game's relevant cleaning counters to identify the remaining work. Completing scattered nearly-finished areas may help with local milestones, while longer-term percentage progress eventually demands cleaning a much larger number of hexes.

Robot Vacuums eventually finish the dirty ground within their assigned coverage. Compare [Robot Vacuum Mk I and Mk II coverage](/moon-sweepers/automation#robot-vacuum-coverage) before placing another dock near an already cleaned area.

## Earn $1 Million and Spend $1 Million: All Money Achievements

There are two separate economy tracks. One measures how much money you have earned over time; the other measures how much you have spent.

### Money earned

- **Dust Pays the Bills:** Earn **$1,000** in total.
- **Filthy Rich:** Earn **$10,000** in total.
- **Lunar Payday:** Earn **$100,000** in total.
- **The Moon Is a Gold Mine:** Earn **$1,000,000** in total.

These achievements specify cumulative **earnings**, not the amount currently sitting in your account.

Money earned from Dust sales advances the earnings milestones, even when you spend that money on new equipment afterward. The amount currently in your account is not your lifetime earnings total.

Processed Dust becomes more valuable, but the earnings counter only benefits when that material reaches the selling system. If valuable capsules accumulate before reaching the Depot, fix the delivery route using the [Moon Sweepers automation instructions](/moon-sweepers/automation#production-bottlenecks).

### Money spent

- **Reinvesting in Dust:** Spend **$10,000** on tools, vehicles, or structures.
- **A Very Expensive Hobby:** Spend **$100,000**.
- **Small loan of $1 million:** Spend **$1,000,000**.

The $10,000 spending milestone can be reached while buying upgrades and expanding the factory. It has been triggered during a session in which the player was purchasing additional economy upgrades.

The larger spending achievements require continued investment. Simply earning $100,000 or keeping $1 million in the bank is not sufficient for a requirement that explicitly says **spend**.

### Spend Without Stalling Your Cleaning Progress

The spending milestones count purchases, while the earnings milestones track money received. You can advance both through normal equipment upgrades and factory expansion.

Keep enough money for the connections and supporting equipment needed to operate your next purchase. An expensive machine left idle without power or an outgoing Conveyor may increase your spending total without helping you earn enough for the next upgrade.

Work toward the $100,000 and $1,000,000 spending milestones through useful purchases rather than repeatedly replacing equipment that already works.

## You Missed a Spot to Time To Go Sleep... — How Much of the Moon Must You Clean?

These four achievements are tied to the overall percentage of lunar surface cleaned.

- **You Missed a Spot:** Clean **1%** of the Moon.
- **Visible From Space:** Clean **10%**.
- **Half Way There:** Clean **50%**.
- **Time To Go Sleep...:** Clean **100%**.

The challenge is the scale of the map, not an additional button or purchase requirement listed in the achievement descriptions.

### Check Moon-Wide Progress, Not the Current Region

A local region reaching 100% does not mean the full Moon is even close to complete.

![The Moon Map displaying the total number of cleaned and uncleared lunar sections.](/images/moon-sweepers/moon-sweepers-moon-cleaning-progress.webp)

One Moon Map showed **0.09% of the Moon cleaned**, with the following totals:

- **163,830 sectors** across the Moon
- **145 cleaned**
- **182 partly cleaned**
- **163,503 not cleaned**

The upper progress display also showed **127 of 163,830 sectors**. Because that count differs from the map's cleaned-category total, use the displayed overall percentage to track progress rather than calculating an exact completion rate from either count alone.

Even with local sections already reaching 100%, this Moon Map still showed less than 0.1% of the entire Moon cleaned.

### Measure Your Real Cleaning Speed

A useful estimate needs two readings of actual Moon-wide progress.

Record:

- The number of sections cleaned at the beginning of a session.
- The number cleaned after a measured period of active play.
- The time elapsed between those two readings.

Calculate:

**Cleaning rate = New sections cleaned ÷ Hours elapsed**

Then:

**Estimated hours remaining = Sections not yet cleaned ÷ Cleaning rate**

For example, an increase of **400 cleaned sections over 2 hours** represents an observed rate of **200 sections per hour**.

That rate is not a permanent property of your save. Installing more cleaners, changing their positions, running out of power, or waiting for materials to unload can alter it substantially.

Measure again after major factory improvements rather than projecting the entire 100% journey from the opening Toothbrush phase.

### Measure Your Cleaning Rate After Adding Automation

Take another Moon Map reading after installing a new cleaner and letting it operate for a full session. Compare the increase in completed sections with the time elapsed.

A larger number of machines does not guarantee a proportional improvement. A vacuum may have exhausted the dirty ground within its reach, while JONAS may be waiting for capsule collection or operating with an interrupted delivery route.

If the number of completed sections barely changes, inspect the cleaner and its collection system before buying another. The [Robot Vacuum and JONAS instructions](/moon-sweepers/automation#jonas) explain their operating limits.

Use your newer measured cleaning rate when estimating the remaining time to 100%.

## How to Finish the Remaining Moon Sweepers Achievements

As the factory grows, the 33 achievements stop advancing at the same pace.

Someone can accumulate substantial earnings without completing many new sections. Another player might clean hundreds of hexes while making very few individual Depot deposits. Multiplayer can add more workers, but it does not automatically establish how every personal achievement counter is credited.

Before committing to another long session, look at which of these four kinds of unfinished progress remains:

**Special interactions:** Multiplayer, driving while cleaning, the first Conveyor movement, and the closed Conveyor loop require specific actions. Complete them deliberately rather than hoping the normal production line eventually triggers them.

**Collection and delivery counters:** Dust collected, individual Depot deposits, and successful launches are different measurements. Check the required activity before repeating thousands of actions.

**Economy totals:** Earnings and spending both increase naturally as your operation grows, but reaching one total does not automatically mean you reached the other.

**Moon-wide cleanup:** Monitor the Moon Map's overall percentage and compare cleaning rates between sessions. A completed local region is only a small part of the worldwide objective.

The most useful next step is whichever action changes your missing achievement counter. Keep the checklist matched to your actual unlocks and let the cleaning equipment handle the work it can perform reliably.
