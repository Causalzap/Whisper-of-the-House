<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
In **Little Great Britain**, start by placing **two Cattle Ranches** on fertile land and connecting them to roads. Build a **Tannery** close to the ranches, then add a **Transport Office** so your Leather can reach the Docks for export.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

The **Tropico 7 Demo** begins in **Little Great Britain**, the first Campaign mission. You're the Governor of a British colony, with a limited mandate, a small settlement, and an eventual goal of gaining independence from the Crown.

Your first assignment is simple enough: raise cattle. What follows is more demanding. Leather must reach the port, the Crown expects increasing trade income, the Revolutionaries need support, and your mandate keeps counting down while everything is being built.

This walkthrough covers the opening production chain, the faction assignments, the steps toward independence, and the economic problems that can slow down your progress. It also explains when expanding your island helps and when another construction project is more likely to waste your remaining time.

## Little Great Britain: What to Build First

The island starts with a functioning settlement, including housing, plantations, service buildings, a Dock, and construction facilities. You don't need to rebuild the town before following the first order.

Lord Roger Windham wants **two Ranches producing Cattle**. Penultimo introduces the fertility map, where greener ground indicates better conditions for agricultural production.

Open **Production → Ranch**, select Cattle, and place two ranches on suitable land.

![The opening Little Great Britain objective requires two ranches producing cattle.](/images/tropico-7/little-great-britain-cattle-ranches.webp)

The placement matters for more than fertility. Your next assignment uses the Hides produced by these animals, so leave space for an industrial building nearby.

Connect both ranches to the road network before allowing construction to proceed. The road builder supports curved roads and multiple connection points, but a road running close to a building does not always count as a valid connection.

The opening task makes that distinction clear: a Ranch without a proper road connection cannot function as intended.

You can place the Ranch first and then draw a road toward its connection point. This is often easier than building a long road across the island before deciding where the production buildings should go.

### What about Goats, Sheep, and other animals?

The Ranch menu offers several animal types, including Goats, Sheep, Pigs, Llamas, Crocodiles, and Cattle. They produce different resources, but the Crown specifically requests Cattle.

Cattle supply **Hides and Meat**. Hides feed your first industrial production chain, while Meat can contribute to the island's food supply.

Later, other animals become useful for faction tasks. For now, complete the Cattle requirement rather than spending the opening budget on several different types of Ranch.

## Turn Hides Into Leather and Complete the First Export

Once the two Cattle Ranches are operating, the Crown requests Leather.

The required production chain is:

**Cattle Ranch → Hides → Tannery → Leather → Docks**

Open Production and place a **Tannery** close to the Ranches. Positioning matters because compatible neighboring buildings can supply goods directly to one another.

![The Tannery placement preview shows how nearby producers can supply raw materials directly.](/images/tropico-7/little-great-britain-ranch-tannery.webp)

The Tannery's placement information explains that highlighted nearby producers can supply raw materials directly. Keep the Tannery close to your Cattle Ranches so Hides can enter production without relying on regular freight transport for that connection.

This is a useful improvement over spreading every production building around the island and relying entirely on road freight.

The Tannery requires workers and creates considerable pollution. A location near the Ranches but away from the center of your residential area is a reasonable compromise.

Allow the Tannery to finish construction, then inspect its storage.

Hides should enter as an input while Leather accumulates as the output. At this point, the production chain may be functioning perfectly even though the Crown's objective has not advanced.

That's because **the Crown requires Leather to be exported, not merely produced**.

The opening shipment requires at least **1,500 Leather** to reach the Docks. The goods must then be collected by a departing freighter.

### Why is my Leather not reaching the Docks?

The next assignment introduces the Transport Office, and its pickup rule explains one of the most confusing early delays.

**Transporters normally wait until 1,500 goods are available for pickup.**

The transportation instructions describe this as a measure to reduce unnecessary traffic.

![The transportation instructions explain the 1,500-goods pickup threshold.](/images/tropico-7/little-great-britain-transport-threshold.webp)

This means a Tannery can have several hundred Leather sitting in storage without a transporter immediately collecting it.

When your goods aren't moving, check the entire route:

1. Confirm that the Ranches and Tannery are producing their expected goods.
2. Check that the required buildings are connected to roads.
3. Build the additional Transport Office requested by the mission.
4. Inspect the available workers and whether goods have accumulated for pickup.
5. Watch the Docks for the delivered Leather and the next departing freighter.

Transporters prioritize deliveries to stores, then production buildings, and finally the Docks. Nearby buildings with direct supply connections can avoid some intermediate freight movement.

The initial Leather shipment advances only after the goods are actually exported.

### Should you build another Transport Office?

The island begins with some freight capacity, and the tutorial asks you to build an additional Transport Office.

As more production buildings start operating, extra transport capacity becomes valuable. The Transport Office also offers a **Second Shift** upgrade, providing additional workers for moving goods.

Don't mistake the pickup threshold for a shortage of vehicles, though. An extra office does not remove the need for goods to accumulate.

Before expanding transport, look at where the bottleneck occurs. Raw materials piling up at the Ranches, Leather piling up at the Tannery, and exported goods waiting at the Docks are three different situations.

## How to Make Money in the Tropico 7 Demo

The Crown eventually demands **$25,000 in monthly trade profit**, so completing the first Leather shipment is only the beginning. Your existing production buildings need to keep generating exports rather than delivering a single large shipment.

This is a trade-performance objective, not a requirement to hold $25,000 in the treasury. The most useful investment depends on which part of your economy is slowing down.

Expanding every industry at once is expensive. Each additional Ranch, Tannery, or Transport Office needs workers, and an unfinished or understaffed building contributes little to the next shipment.

Before spending money, inspect the production buildings already operating. Their input and output storage will show whether you need more raw materials, processing capacity, or freight transport.

### When is another Tannery worth building?

Inspect the existing production buildings before spending money.

- **Hides accumulating at the Ranches:** additional processing capacity may help.
- **Tannery waiting for Hides:** investigate production, staffing, or supply connections first.
- **Leather accumulating at the Tannery:** look at transport capacity and the export threshold.
- **Goods reaching the Docks:** allow time for the next freighter rather than immediately expanding production.

Adding a second Tannery becomes more attractive when the Ranches consistently produce more Hides than one building can process.

Later in the mission, Crocodile Ranches are another possible source of Leather-related raw materials. They aren't necessary for the opening cattle objective, and their different suitability requirements should be considered before replacing existing production.

There is no fixed Ranch-to-Tannery ratio that works regardless of workers, upgrades, or production settings. Use the storage figures to identify which part of your chain needs more capacity.

### Keep enough workers for the buildings you already own

A growing industrial district can run short of employees even when construction is proceeding normally.

The Almanac separates unemployed citizens, homeless citizens, and other population information. Check it before building several more Ranches or production facilities.

A finished building with vacant jobs won't operate at its intended capacity.

Housing also needs to be in a useful location. A home may remain vacant because the rent is too high, it is too far from available jobs, or the surrounding neighborhood doesn't meet residents' needs.

This becomes particularly noticeable when agricultural and industrial areas expand away from the original settlement.

You can build housing closer to the new jobs or improve transportation connections between the neighborhoods and workplaces. The Bus Terminal allows you to establish a route with up to **five stops**, giving workers another way to travel around the island.

Avoid creating jobs faster than the population can fill them. Unstaffed buildings still tie up construction money without delivering the production you expected.

## How to Extend Your Mandate Before Time Runs Out

The Governor's mandate is one of the biggest dangers in Little Great Britain.

You can have profitable industries and a growing population but still run out of time. Completing Crown assignments extends your mandate, so you need to keep accepting jobs that your island can realistically finish.

Open the domestic policy interface at the Palace to inspect available faction and Crown assignments.

The choices change as you progress. Some require producing goods, others require exports, constructing buildings, issuing Edicts, or paying money.

### Monarchist and Revolutionary assignments

One Monarchist option requires **exporting 2,500 Cocoa**.

To complete it, you need a Cocoa Plantation on suitable land, enough workers for production, and time for the goods to reach the Docks and leave on a freighter.

The Revolutionaries can offer an assignment to **produce 400 Milk**. A Goat Ranch is one possible way to fulfill it.

These tasks shouldn't be confused:

- **Produce 400 Milk** requires the relevant production to take place.
- **Export 2,500 Cocoa** requires the goods to leave the island.
- Construction and Edict assignments have their own completion conditions.

Export tasks can take substantially longer than their displayed quantities suggest. New fields need time to produce, transporters may wait for full loads, and the next freighter may not arrive before the deadline.

The Milk and Cocoa assignments are available choices, not universal mandatory steps that every player must complete in the same order.

### When should you pay for mandate time?

A later Crown option allows a payment of **$21,000** in exchange for **seven additional months**.

It's expensive, but it avoids waiting for an entirely new production chain.

When the treasury is healthy and your remaining mandate is dangerously short, paying can be safer than accepting an export order that requires new construction.

That doesn't mean you should pay for every extension. A task involving an Edict you can already issue may be much faster and cheaper.

For example, later assignments can ask you to activate an available policy such as **Employee of the Month** or **Tropican Dream**. Check whether the Edict is accessible and affordable before choosing another long export.

Employee of the Month increases production-building and Mine efficiency by **20%**, but decreases job quality by **15%**. Completing a quick task this way still carries an ongoing economic trade-off.

### Can you cancel a task?

Abandoning an active assignment can cost **three months of mandate time**.

This is particularly dangerous when the Governor has only a few months remaining.

The Banana export assignment provides a useful warning. A Plantation can be producing Bananas, with goods waiting in storage, while the export target remains unfinished. Time may expire before the shipment reaches a freighter.

Even speeding up construction doesn't eliminate the harvest and shipping delay.

The safer approach is to inspect the remaining mandate before selecting the assignment. If you don't already have the crop producing, a shorter objective may be a much better choice.

## How to Start the Independence Objectives

Once the initial Leather shipment is accepted, **Victoria Guerra Pérez** introduces the Revolutionary movement and the plan to free Tropico from British control.

The next objective asks for **two Fisherman's Wharves**.

Open the production menu and place them in suitable coastal locations. They introduce additional fishing production and advance the early Revolutionary sequence.

The Crown subsequently demands more trade income, while the Revolutionaries introduce changes to Ranch wages and work modes.

This part of the mission builds directly on the economy you've already established.

### Increase Ranch wages and use More Animals

The Revolutionary assignments introduce higher Ranch salaries and the **More Animals** Work Mode.

Select a Cattle Ranch, inspect its budget or salary controls, and increase the wages when the assignment requires high salaries.

Then open Work Mode and select **More Animals**.

This increases the number of animals a Ranch can support, improving production potential while reducing job quality.

A later objective requires multiple Ranches using More Animals, with the task counter reaching **five Ranches**.

Check every existing Ranch before constructing new ones. The Goat Ranch built for the earlier Milk assignment can also be adjusted, so it may already contribute toward the required total.

Building the full requirement from scratch would be unnecessarily expensive if you have suitable Ranches operating elsewhere on the island.

The **Animal Vaccinations** upgrade provides an efficiency increase of **10%** with additional upkeep. It's useful when you can afford the operating costs and want more production without continually adding buildings.

More Animals and higher wages can work together, but they don't remove the need to watch employment, job quality, and production efficiency.

### Convert citizens into Revolutionaries with a Newspaper

After the Ranch work-mode objectives, the Revolutionaries request more supporters.

Build a **Newspaper** from the Media and Education category, preferably close to a populated residential neighborhood.

Select the Newspaper and change its Work Mode to **The King Is Dead**.

![The King Is Dead work mode allows the Newspaper to influence nearby citizens toward the Revolutionary faction.](/images/tropico-7/little-great-britain-revolutionary-newspaper.webp)

This Work Mode encourages nearby citizens to join the Revolutionaries. Its description gives an average influence rate of **50% of affected citizens per year**.

The mission requires **10 citizens** to be converted.

Placing the Newspaper near homes gives it a better opportunity to influence residents than locating it on an isolated industrial road.

The conversion takes time. A constructed Newspaper with the correct Work Mode won't necessarily complete the objective immediately.

Keep monitoring the mandate while citizens gradually change their faction support.

## Gold, Bananas, and Food Satisfaction: Later Colonial Tasks

The independence-related assignments continue to overlap as the island develops.

Among the later requirements are **1,500 Gold exported to the Crown**, a **2,500 Banana export** choice, and **52% Food Satisfaction**.

![The later Little Great Britain objectives include Gold exports, a Banana shipment, and Food Satisfaction.](/images/tropico-7/little-great-britain-gold-food-objectives.webp)

These objectives don't all belong to one fixed construction sequence. Some come from different faction assignments, and the Banana export can be a choice rather than a compulsory step.

### Export 1,500 Gold to the Crown

Open **Production → Mine** and locate a Gold deposit.

Place the Mine where it can access the appropriate resource, connect it to the road network, and provide enough workers.

The Crown's Gold requirement involves an export, so check **Goods and Trade** for the corresponding trade arrangement.

Mining takes time, and the transport threshold can make the output appear stationary while Gold accumulates.

Building another Mine may increase production, but only if the island has the workers and suitable deposits to operate both facilities.

Look at the Mine's actual output before investing further. A second Mine won't fix an unstaffed building or disconnected road.

### How to increase Food Satisfaction to 52%

Food Satisfaction becomes another obstacle later in Little Great Britain.

The island can have multiple food-producing buildings while its satisfaction remains around **42–44%**. Adding more plantations does not automatically make the number jump to 52%.

Separate the supply problem from the access problem.

First, make sure your existing Ranches, fishing facilities, and Plantations have workers and are producing. Then inspect whether stores are serving the neighborhoods where residents live.

Small Stores provide access to food and consumer goods. A growing agricultural settlement may need additional stores when housing spreads far from the original town.

More food variety and better local availability are sensible improvements, but they take time to affect satisfaction. The existing production and service problems should be corrected before adding several redundant food buildings.

A later faction request can raise the displayed Food Satisfaction requirement to **54%**. Check the current objective rather than assuming the target remains 52% throughout the mission.

## Logging Camps, Sawmills, and the Planks Export

The colonial production objectives continue after Gold and the earlier independence preparations.

The Crown introduces a timber industry assignment that requires **two Logging Camps and a Sawmill**.

Logging Camps produce Logs, which the Sawmill processes into Planks.

Place the camps near suitable forested land and connect them to your road network. The Sawmill should be positioned to receive those Logs without creating an unnecessarily long supply route.

The later export objective requires **4,500 Planks**.

![The later colonial assignment requires an export of 4,500 Planks.](/images/tropico-7/little-great-britain-planks-objective.webp)

This is a significantly larger amount than the first Leather requirement. It becomes important to watch production storage and transport capacity as multiple industries compete for the same workers.

The Sawmill also has a high fire risk, so consider nearby firefighting coverage when expanding this industrial area.

The Planks assignment is part of the continuing colonial objectives. It is not itself a confirmation that the complete independence sequence has ended.

## Terraforming, Plantation Fields, and Political Decisions

Little Great Britain also introduces several new building and political systems. Some are useful immediately; others are better left alone until your economy is stronger.

### Terraforming can consume your early treasury

Terraforming allows you to reshape terrain and even create additional land around the island.

It's tempting to start connecting islands or flattening mountains as soon as the tool becomes available, but major changes can be extremely expensive.

One large mountain-flattening preview displays a cost of approximately **$441,000**. A separate land-expansion preview is also in the hundreds of thousands.

Those are costs for particular terrain changes, not fixed prices for every Terraforming operation.

Early in Little Great Britain, the money is generally more useful for production, freight, housing, and mandate requirements. A large coastal expansion doesn't help much when your Leather is still waiting to be exported.

Small terrain adjustments are possible, but inspect the projected cost before confirming them.

### You can reshape Plantation fields

Plantations allow you to edit their cultivation areas, including while the building is under construction.

This helps when a default field layout doesn't fit your roads or fertile ground.

Select the Plantation, open the field-editing controls, and remove or add cultivation areas as needed.

A larger field can improve production potential, but it also affects labor requirements and working conditions. Expanding a field without enough workers is unlikely to deliver the output you expect.

For export deadlines, suitable fertility, available employees, and a functioning freight route matter more than filling every available tile.

### Why are some Edicts unavailable?

The **Political Matrix** shows the political positions of El Presidente, citizens, and factions.

Available Edicts depend on your political position and Constitution. Some laws are unavailable because their political alignment does not match your current position.

Changing the Constitution can alter which policies you can use. Certain faction rewards may also grant access to an otherwise unavailable Edict.

This matters when choosing a fast political assignment. Before accepting a request to issue a particular law, make sure the required Edict can actually be activated.

The Demo takes place in the Colonial Era, so later-era systems such as regular elections aren't all available yet.

## What Should You Do Next in Little Great Britain?

As the colonial objectives begin to overlap, the remaining mandate becomes just as important as your treasury.

Open the Campaign and Tasks panel before accepting another assignment. A production requirement may be completed with buildings you already own, while an export requires enough goods to reach the Docks and leave on a freighter. Faction recruitment takes time even after the necessary building is operating.

Prioritize objectives that use your existing industries, especially when the mandate is short. Starting a new Plantation for a large export can consume months that would be better spent completing an available Edict or construction task.

After an objective finishes, check which Crown or Revolutionary requirement has become active. The first Leather shipment and Newspaper recruitment are only stages of the independence campaign, not the end of British rule.

</section>
