A Year 33 victory on Peasant or Squire does not mean the same setup will survive King difficulty. Compare how each army survives its first battles, which effect lets it keep growing, and what still works when a Rainbow reward never appears.

## King of Greed Build: How Do You Scale Mercenaries With Gold?

**Core:** Palace, Vault, Mercenary, Dispenser. **Useful additions:** Steel Coat, Midas Touch, Demon Altar, Cemetery, and Golden Hour when available.

The central interaction is simple: **Mercenary maintains one additional troop for every nine gold you hold**, while **Vault provides gold each year and levels itself up**. Dispenser gains attack speed as you *earn* gold, so the same economy helps both your frontline and a damage tower. Your Palace provides another attack from behind the troops.

### Start With Palace, Mercenary, and Vault

1. Place **Palace** where it will not be the first plot enemies reach. Keep useful adjacent spaces available.
2. Put **Mercenary** toward the front so the early battles have a real troop defending the Palace.
3. Take an early **Vault** and let its yearly income build. Leveling Vault improves the amount it pays and, indirectly, the number of Mercenaries supported by your treasury.
4. If you receive **Steel Coat**, use it to help the Mercenary plot survive its opening engagements.
5. Add **Dispenser** when offered. The more gold the run generates, the more attack speed it accumulates.

The key distinction is **gold earned versus gold currently held**. Dispenser benefits from earning money; Mercenary troop count responds to the treasury you keep. You can have a fast Dispenser and a smaller Mercenary army if you spend most of your savings.

### Add Blood Cards Without Sacrificing the Economy

A useful secondary route is **Demon Altar** supported by deaths in the frontline, with **Cemetery** adding replacement units when adjacent troops die. These cards are not required for Mercenary to work. They give you another source of pressure while you save gold, particularly if the enemy begins killing some of your hired troops.

In the late game, look for effects that turn gold generation into more immediate damage. **Midas Touch** can provide gold on kills; the 1.0 **Golden Hour** Decree makes the Palace fire additional beams when gold is earned during battle. Together, they can turn a successful fight into a much faster one. Do not plan your entire opening around receiving that Decree: the build already needs to survive before it appears.

### Why Does the Mercenary Build Suddenly Get Weaker?

Check your remaining gold. Spending heavily on rerolls or cards can reduce the Mercenary troop count even when the Vault is working correctly. If the front line is already close to failing, buying a marginal upgrade may be worse than keeping enough money to maintain it.

Also check whether all of your damage depends on one frontline plot. A Dispenser, protected Palace, and a secondary source of units give you ways to keep fighting when Mercenaries take heavy losses. This economy-based combination can defeat the Year 33 final assault on a lower difficulty, but that result does not guarantee the same layout at the highest difficulty.

## King of Nothing Build: Paladin, Farm, and Mob for a First Win

**Core:** Castle, Paladin, Farm, Blacksmith. **Helpful rival cards:** Mob, Raptor, Beacon, Steel Coat.

This is the simplest place to learn why **one developed troop plot** can be better than three separate weak armies. Paladin is durable, Farm can add units to an adjacent troop each year, and Blacksmith adds combat strength. The early goal is to put those benefits onto the same part of your kingdom.

### Set Up One Strong Paladin Plot

1. Place **Castle** without blocking the spaces needed around your main Paladin plot. Keep room for Farm and Blacksmith to support the same troops.
2. Place **Paladin** in a location where a **Farm** can be adjacent to it. When you receive more Paladin cards, favor leveling the existing plot instead of spreading them across your board without a reason.
3. Put **Farm** beside Paladin. An early extra unit is a much bigger relative increase when the troop count is still small.
4. Add **Blacksmith** where it buffs your main troops. Do not fill the neighboring spaces with unrelated structures before deciding which troop needs those bonuses.
5. If the King of Nomads offers **Mob**, take advantage of its interaction with units gained by other troops. Position it to benefit from your growing army. A **Raptor** can provide another useful interaction with Mob when offered.

An early **Beacon** can help your troops and base act faster, but it should not force you to remove the Farm or Blacksmith that is already making your Paladins effective.

### When Should You Stop Adding Troops and Start Upgrading?

If the Paladin plot survives comfortably, focus on making the existing army stronger. Keep your useful enhancement cards for the units that actually win fights. A late side project can consume cards and space without catching up to the troops you spent twenty years growing.

If you are losing early, check **where the Farm is applying its effect**, whether the Blacksmith reaches the troop you intended, and whether your Paladins were split across too many plots. ## Joker Leech Build: Treant, Wall Maker, and Defenders

**Core plan:** Treant base, a dependable troop such as Defenders, Wall Maker, and **Leech** when available. **Strong additions:** Mantra, Patronage, Rewind, and helpful leveling effects.

The major 1.0 interaction is **Leech**. It takes **9% of the damage and HP of adjacent plots each year**, improving itself while drawing strength from its neighbors. In a Treant kingdom, you can combine a healing and damaging base with durable units and a large defensive wall. The result is a deck with several ways to stay alive while individual plots become exceptionally strong.

### Build Around a Wall Without Forgetting Your Troops

Begin with **Treant**, but keep room for a combat troop and its support. Deploy **Defenders** before investing too heavily in **Wall Maker**: a wall can delay the enemy, yet something still has to defeat it. Leave a useful neighboring position for Leech rather than filling every available plot with unrelated buildings.

Placing **Leech** is a choice about which plot will pay for its growth. A durable wall may be able to lose some Damage and HP each year, while draining the only troop that can kill enemies is much riskier. If **Mantra** or **Patronage** appears, direct the extra levels toward the troop or plot that is still deciding your battles.

A full cross-king setup can also exploit **Rewind** to repeat useful cards. The practical limit is your ability to draw and play the right effects: do not assume every Rainbow card will appear during a normal run.

### What Can Go Wrong With Leech?

A fast-growing Leech can hide a shrinking frontline. Check the Damage and HP on the troop plots beside it each year. If those troops begin losing fights, reinforce them instead of assuming the larger Leech can carry the battle; on your next attempt, reserve a less critical neighboring plot before placing Leech.

There is a second mistake: building an impressive wall with too little troop damage. Make sure your actual army can still beat the final enemy even if the Treant and Wall Maker survive easily. ## Joker Lab Rat Poison Build: How Do You Grow Rats and Spread Poison?

**Core plan:** Treant base, Lab Rat, Cauldron, Procreate, Clone, Wild Card, Overhaul, and Rewind. **Optional Rainbow addition:** Gigantify. The goal is not simply to own many Rats: it is to keep improving a main Lab Rat plot while using poison and the Treant's broad damage field to hit enemies.

### Combine Leveling With Cauldron

1. Put **Lab Rat** close enough to plots you intend to level. Lab Rat can benefit as nearby plots gain levels, making placement important from the opening years.
2. Use **Cauldron** to bring poison into the fight. Position it so its effect reaches the Treant or another relevant attacker rather than an isolated plot with no useful targets.
3. Use **Procreate** to add Rats and **Clone** or **Wild Card** when you need more levels or copies of your key cards.
4. Treat **Overhaul** as a way to convert dispensable plots into further growth. Do not destroy a vital support plot merely because another level is available.
5. Save **Rewind** for turns when replaying the cards you have just used will meaningfully increase your Rat count, poison output, or scaling.

**Gigantify** can create much larger Rats, but making a troop enormous is not the same as protecting it. Use it on a troop that already contributes rather than relying on size alone to fix an otherwise weak opening.

### Which Perks Help the Poison Plan?

Look for bonuses that improve **poison ticks, Lab Rat starting levels, Cauldron's poison stacks, available leveling, and access to Procreate**. A loss-triggered source of Procreate can help recover after an early failure, but it is not a reason to deliberately sacrifice a life. Extra rerolls also help when your chosen cards are not appearing, provided the spending does not interrupt your setup.

This kind of build can complete Year 33, but it has a real weak point: **Bombers or other heavy area damage can remove a group of Rats before they finish the opponent**. Keep the Treant's damage growing as a second route to victory. If most of the final battle's damage comes from Treant, the Rats were support for that particular run, not proof that Rat scaling alone is unbeatable.

## King of Spells Rainbow Build: Wizard, Library, and Replicator

**Starting cards:** Citadel, Wizard or Warlock, and Library. **Reward-dependent additions:** Replicator, Procreate, and useful Rainbow cards. **Optional Perk:** Lunatic when you want to encounter the Jack of Rainbows more consistently.

King of Spells can grow a small army through enchantments and repeated Tome use. Wizard is particularly interesting when you can build its enchantment bonuses early, while Library can help provide repeated value to a nearby unit. A Replicator reward gives you a second way to scale through troop numbers instead of relying on one unusually strong Wizard.

### Get the Library Working Before Adding More Units

1. Start with **Citadel** and a troop that can fight the first battle. If you choose Wizard, begin developing its enchantments; if you start Warlock, remember that its strengths depend on different supporting effects.
2. Place **Library** next to a troop that will remain important. Moving the troop or filling the wrong adjacent slots later can waste the early investment.
3. If **Replicator** appears, place it where it has room to grow and where future buffs can reach it. **Procreate** can help the growing troop plot gain numbers earlier.
4. Keep upgrading the actual damage dealers. Do not assume more troops compensate for a lack of damage, especially against enemies that can hit many units.
5. Take advantage of Rainbow rewards that fit the board you have, rather than trying to force every new effect into an unrelated setup.

If you use **Lunatic**, read its requirement carefully: it starts a war with the Jack of Rainbows **when it is your only Perk**. Adding an otherwise useful Perk can prevent that particular opening from working as expected.

### Why Can a Huge Rainbow Army Still Lose?

A large troop count is not sufficient if the cards stop those troops from attacking. One unsuccessful approach converted the entire army into **Goats**. The result looked impressive in numbers, but the troops could not provide the damage needed to finish the battles. Build a damage source first and treat transformation effects as a risk until you understand exactly what survives the change.

The successful variation combined **Wizards with useful buffs, Replicators supplying extra bodies, and Dwarves opening space**. It relied on several complementary pieces rather than one guaranteed combo and was played at a reduced difficulty. It is a good route for experimentation, not a promise of a highest-difficulty win.

## Which New 1.0 Rainbow Cards Are Worth Building Around?

Nine Rainbow cards arrived in version 1.0. The right choice depends on your board and hand: **Leech** needs neighbors worth draining, **Mantra** needs further leveling, and **Accumulator** rewards you for keeping it in hand rather than immediately playing it.

Two mistakes are easy to make with these cards. **Reflector** is about Tome effects applied to a neighboring plot; do not treat every Enchantment as a Tome and expect it to copy the effect. **Accumulator** gets stronger the longer it remains in your hand, so spending it at the first opportunity may waste the very advantage you selected it for.

A Rainbow card can be a major improvement, but it should solve a real problem in your kingdom. If your frontline is already dying, a card that only provides future scaling may be less useful than immediate protection or damage.

## Which Perks and Decrees Matter Most for These Builds?

A Perk that strengthens your opening can matter before the first battle, while a Decree tied to a specific card becomes useful only when that card is on the board. With **King of Nothing**, extra starting Farms or Paladin units reinforce the first troop plot. With **King of Greed**, **Golden Hour** becomes attractive once Palace and in-battle gold generation are working; it cannot rescue an opening that lacks a frontline.

**Lunatic** is different: with **King of Spells**, it starts the war with the Jack of Rainbows only when it is your sole Perk. For a Joker poison deck, choose bonuses that affect the Cauldron and Lab Rat combination you actually use; for a Leech deck, prioritize levels on the troop still doing the fighting. To change Perk positions or enabled Decree pools, use the <Link href="/9-kings/joker-mode">Joker Mode settings</Link>.

## What Should You Do if the Build's Key Card Never Appears?

Not every run offers the card you planned around. Decide whether the missing card is **the engine itself** or only an improvement to an engine already on your board:

A Greed run can still function without **Dispenser**. Keep Palace protected and let Vault's income support the Mercenaries already on the board; spending too much gold rerolling for another tower can cost you the very troops protecting it. Without **Leech**, a Treant deck must rely on Treant's damage and its Defenders while Wall Maker buys time. Preserve a useful neighboring space for Leech instead of abandoning a defense that is still working.

**Wizard + Library** also gives King of Spells something to develop when **Replicator** never appears. Continue strengthening the unit that deals damage rather than filling the board with more bodies that cannot end a fight. If you need a specific troop-count or damage milestone, check the <Link href="/9-kings/achievements">achievement requirement</Link> before spending your remaining cards.

Save expensive rerolls for a genuine turning point. If you cannot afford another turn of searching, invest in what already survives the next battle.

If you are also working toward a specific unlock, check the <Link href="/9-kings/achievements">99-achievement checklist</Link> to see whether your current build can meet its requirement.
