<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
To win your first run in **9 Kings 1.0**, start with **King of Nothing** on a comfortable difficulty, place your **Castle** away from the first line of contact, and keep a fighting troop between the enemy and your base. Each year, improve the plots that already win battles instead of scattering upgrades across the kingdom. Save space for useful support buildings, choose rewards you can actually use, and prepare for the **Year 33 final assault**, when you must play or discard every remaining card. Winning opens **Quest Mode**; continuing the run takes you into **Endless**.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

Your first game can go wrong long before the enemy army looks enormous. You might put your Castle where the enemy reaches it immediately, place a useful building where it cannot help anyone, or spend all your gold searching for a card that will not save the next fight. The way through is to build one workable defense first, learn what the reward screens are offering, and expand only when the existing kingdom can survive another year.

## What Should You Do First in 9 Kings?

Select **Singleplayer** and begin with **King of Nothing**, the king available on a new save. Peasant is a sensible starting difficulty if you want to learn the interface without testing the limits of every card immediately. Your first goal is to stop enemies from reaching the Castle, not to assemble a spectacular late-game combination.

A standard run begins with a base card and three other cards. The opening kingdom is a small grid of plots. Place your base first, then build enough of a defense to survive the opening battle.

### Where Should You Place the Castle?

**Keep it behind a fighting plot unless you have a specific reason to expose it.** If an enemy reaches your base, you lose a life. Putting the Castle in the center may leave more adjacent spaces available for buffs, but that advantage does not help if an early attacker reaches it before your troops can intervene.

You do not have to place it in the furthest corner every time. Look at the enemy's approach, leave room for a frontline troop, and keep at least some neighboring plots free for support. A base with strong future bonuses is not a substitute for a defense that works in Year 1.

### Which Cards Should You Play Before the First Battle?

Read the cards by what they do rather than by their color alone:

- **Base:** Your kingdom's critical plot. For King of Nothing, that is the Castle.
- **Troops:** Fight incoming enemies. A Paladin is a durable starting example; a Soldier or Archer plays a different combat role.
- **Buildings and towers:** Supply troops, improve nearby plots, or attack enemies. Their positions matter as much as their effects.
- **Tomes and enchantments:** Modify an existing plot or unit. Some do not belong on an empty plot.

Put a combat unit down before investing everything in support. **Wildcard**, for example, can upgrade a card you have already placed; using an upgrade well is different from having an extra troop on the field. If a card's text mentions an adjacent plot, inspect the board before committing to a position you cannot easily undo.

For exact Paladin, Farm, and Blacksmith combinations once you know the basics, see the <Link href="/9-kings/best-builds">9 Kings Best Builds guide</Link>.

## How Do Years, Battles, and Card Rewards Work?

A normal run lasts **33 in-game years**. Each year moves through your card decisions and a battle, with additional events appearing at certain points. In a standard run, combat starts after you have played or discarded enough cards to leave **two cards in hand**; you cannot keep every card indefinitely just to avoid the next fight.

Your troops fight automatically, but you can also aim the base attack yourself or use **Auto**. If enemies are slipping past your main group, manual targeting can help remove a dangerous unit before it touches the Castle. Use the speed controls when you need to see why a position is failing.

After a battle, you select a card reward. **Winning gives you a choice from the defeated king's cards; losing gives you a card from your own king.** This makes the identity of the next opponent relevant to your future card pool, not just to the next combat encounter.

### Which Battle Reward Should You Pick?

Start with the problem the current kingdom has. If the enemy is reaching the Castle, a troop or immediate defense matters more than an upgrade that needs several years to pay off. If you already survive comfortably but fights are getting longer, strengthen an existing damage source before adding an unrelated unit.

Ask three questions before accepting the reward: **Can I use this card soon? Which plot will it affect? What will I give up to make room for it?** A powerful building that needs an adjacent troop is not useful when the only space left is too far away. Similarly, a second copy of an upgrade can be more valuable than a new card type when it improves the unit carrying the run.

If the offers are **Scout Tower**, **Steel Coat**, and **Paladin**, the right choice depends on what happened in the last battle. Pick Paladin if you need another troop to hold the route to your Castle. Choose Steel Coat when an existing frontline needs help surviving, or Scout Tower when your troops hold their ground but could use another source of damage. Before committing, make sure the card has a useful place on your board.

### What Are Royal Decrees, Prophecies, and Blessings?

Some years interrupt the usual battle-and-reward rhythm. A **Royal Council** offers a Decree with an ongoing effect. A **Prophecy** tells you a Blessing is coming, so the choice you make now can matter several years later. Other events bring a Merchant, diplomacy decisions, or more land.

The mistake is to accept every impressive-looking bonus without checking whether your current board can use it. A tower bonus does little for an army with no useful towers; a delayed Blessing may not solve an immediate defense problem. Read the condition first and decide whether it supports the kingdom you already have.

## Should You Spend Gold, Reroll, or Unlock More Plots?

Gold gives you options, but it does not repair a weak kingdom by itself. Merchant cards can improve the deck, enemy rerolls can change which rival you face next, and plot expansions create space for units and support buildings. They solve different problems.

**Spend on the obstacle in front of you.** If the board has nowhere useful to place a reward, opening space may be better than buying yet another card. If you are losing because the frontline is too weak, a usable troop upgrade is more urgent than paying for a different enemy. If the kingdom is already stable, you can afford to save gold for a stronger choice later.

### When Should You Unlock More Land?

Expand when the new plot has a job. A plot next to a useful troop may let a support building affect it; an isolated plot might not help the next battle at all. Some expansions also uncover chests that offer an additional card from your king's deck. Check what the newly available spaces connect to before choosing where to grow.

### Should You Throw Cards Into the Pit?

Use the **Pit** when a card cannot help your current board or when a specific effect rewards discarding it. You are giving up that card, so do not discard the last reliable answer to an approaching enemy just to clear your hand. At the same time, keeping a card you never intend to play is not automatically better; ordinary turns eventually move toward the next battle, and Year 33 has an even stricter hand requirement.

## Why Do You Keep Losing Lives in the Early Years?

**The base was reached.** In a standard run, losing a life means an enemy got through to your Castle or other base objective. A weak damage number is only one possible reason; the frontline's position, enemy approach, and missing support can matter just as much.

If an early battle goes badly, identify which of these happened before restarting:

1. **Enemies passed around your troops.** Move the focus of your next placement toward the lane they actually used. Do not assume a troop will protect the entire board from any position.
2. **A support card buffed the wrong plot—or no useful plot.** Check its affected spaces. A building cannot rescue troops outside its range.
3. **Your cards are spread across too many units.** One surviving troop that grows steadily often does more work than several underleveled plots.
4. **The enemy changed but your plan did not.** Look at the next attacking king and check whether you need more frontline, more damage, or a different target for your base attack.
5. **You spent the resources needed for the next fight.** If you rerolled repeatedly but never strengthened the field, the new choices may have cost more than they gained.

Most importantly, **a lost battle does not always end the run**. A standard run begins with three lives. Keep playing when you have a realistic way to fix the failure, but treat every life lost as evidence that something on the board needs to change.

## How Do You Prepare for the Year 33 Final Battle?

**Year 33 is the finish line for a standard victory, not just another normal wave.** The enemy king's final army is led by a champion, and you must **play or discard every card left in your hand** before the battle begins. You cannot carry an unused collection of upgrades through the final assault.

Before entering it, make four final decisions:

1. **Use any upgrades you have been holding.** Check whether they improve the frontline, your most reliable damage source, or the base itself.
2. **Resolve your remaining cards.** If a card does not fit, discard it instead of expecting to carry it into the final fight.
3. **Inspect the lane enemies can use to reach your base.** Do not leave the path open just because the previous battle ended quickly.
4. **Keep damage as well as defense.** A kingdom that survives but cannot finish the champion still has a problem.

The final battle is unforgiving: **losing a life there ends the run even if you entered the battle with lives remaining**. Treat it as the moment to commit your remaining resources, not as another opportunity to test an unfinished army. If you want to see complete Year 33 card combinations instead of general preparation, compare the <Link href="/9-kings/best-builds">builds that have reached the final assault</Link>.

### Can You Keep Playing After Year 33?

Yes. **Winning Year 33 counts as a completed run**, and you can choose to continue into **Endless**. Additional years let your board grow further and expose it to tougher challenges. You do not need to keep playing Endless to claim an ordinary first victory.

## How Do You Unlock All Nine Kings?

Only **King of Nothing** is available at the start. The other regular kings unlock from milestones reached as you play. **Progress toward these requirements carries across runs**, so a failed attempt can still bring another playable king closer to unlocking.

  rows={nineKingsUnlockRows}

Your next king does not have to be the one with the most impressive-looking deck. If you enjoy building strong troop plots, choose a ruler whose cards support that plan. If you prefer experimenting with spells, gold, or unusual buildings, use the unlock milestones to decide which playstyle to try next.

A king's **level** is also different from a plot's level. Playing runs earns experience toward king levels and their available Perks; upgrading a troop or building during a run improves that particular plot. If you are chasing a king unlock, track the requirement shown for that king rather than assuming every plot upgrade advances it.

## Which 9 Kings 1.0 Game Mode Should You Play Next?

After your first win, you have more options than simply repeating the same opening. Version 1.0 includes four main modes, each suited to a different goal.

### How Do You Unlock Quest Mode?

**Win one Singleplayer run on any difficulty.** Quest Mode then opens its map of **nine lands**, with **81 base challenges** to work through. Completing challenges reveals more of the map and its available tasks. You do not need to finish all 81 quests just to begin using the mode.

The **Daily Mayhem** challenge is a further goal: its island unlocks after you complete all **81 base quests** on any difficulty. It generates a different custom challenge each day. Start with the regular Quests before treating Daily Mayhem as a new objective.

For custom deck selection, Perks, and Conditions, follow the <Link href="/9-kings/joker-mode">9 Kings Joker Mode guide</Link>.

## What Changed in the 9 Kings 1.0 Release?

The November 12, 2026 release added **Joker Mode**, nine new **Rainbow cards**, new **Decrees** and **Perks**, additional **Blessings**, and a **records system** for comparing your end-of-run performance. It also introduced 45 Steam achievements and expanded the game's visual, audio, and performance options.

The new cards and Decrees are most useful when they reinforce an army that can already survive its battles.
