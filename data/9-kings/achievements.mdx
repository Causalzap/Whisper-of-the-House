A normal victory covers only a small part of the achievement list in *9 Kings*. Some goals depend on a precise card interaction, such as using **Balance** on a **Paladin**. Others depend on keeping effects on the same plot, reaching a large troop count, winning under a restriction, or completing challenges across many runs.

Several goals can advance during the same run. You might work toward wins with different kings while leveling those rulers, but a requirement that specifies one turn must be completed before that turn ends. Check your unfinished entries before using a rare card so an easy action is not missed.

## Which 45 Achievements Were Added in 9 Kings 1.0?

Some 1.0 achievements can be attempted as soon as the needed action is available. **Ragequitter** requires forfeiting a battle, **Smells Like Meta** requires using a Tome on a Golem, and **Balance My A\*\*** requires Balance on a Paladin. Check the named card and target before acting; a similar effect on a different unit does not meet the same requirement.

Other conditions depend on how the run unfolds. **Now What** needs a stalemate, **Cursed** needs a Blessing to be missed completely, and **The Devs Rigged My RNG** needs three Library failures in succession. Treat these as opportunities when the situation arises rather than assuming that merely owning the relevant card will trigger the achievement.

## How Do You Get the Win and Difficulty Achievements?

**His Winningness** requires one game win. **True Royalty** specifies a win on **King difficulty**, while **Self-Sufficient** adds another restriction: finish a King-difficulty run **without Perks**. These are not interchangeable; if you are attempting Self-Sufficient, check your Perks before beginning rather than discovering halfway through that one is equipped.

The other victory conditions change what counts as success:

- **Tower Defense:** Win a run without using troops. Do not confuse a small troop army with a troop-free run.
- **Piece of Cake:** Win the final battle in **less than five seconds**. It is the final battle that is timed, not the entire run.
- **So You Think You Can Speedrun:** Beat the game in **under nine minutes**. This is a separate overall time target.
- **By a Whisker:** Win a battle after an enemy has already entered your plots. Winning before that happens does not meet the stated condition.
- **Shrekmate:** Win a battle with an army consisting only of Ogres.

**Die Hard** instead requires surviving to **Year 99**. Reaching Year 33 and winning its final assault is not enough; continue the run and survive the additional years. If you need the standard opening and Year 33 sequence, use the <Link href="/9-kings">9 Kings beginner guide</Link>.

**Smurf** asks you to play on Peasant difficulty with a king at level 9 or above. **This Game Sucks**, **Ragequitter**, and **Now What** concern losing lives, forfeiting a battle, and reaching a stalemate respectively; they are not ordinary win conditions. Follow their precise checklist descriptions instead of assuming one kind of defeat counts for all three.

## Master of Nine, King of Kings, and The Conqueror: What's Different?

These milestones look similar because they require progress beyond one ordinary run, but they measure different achievements:

- **Master of Nine (1.0):** Win a run with **each of the nine kings**.
- **King of Kings:** Beat **King difficulty with all kings**.
- **Your Grindness:** Get **all nine kings to level 9**.
- **Crownmaxxing:** Get **one king to level 9**.

For **Master of Nine**, track wins by king rather than total runs. Repeating a victory with one ruler does not count as a win with another; for **King of Kings**, those victories must also be on **King difficulty**. If one ruler keeps losing, compare its troop and support choices in the <Link href="/9-kings/best-builds">9 Kings build examples</Link>.

Quest Mode has its own progression milestones:

- **Adventurer:** Complete one Quest Mode challenge.
- **Bounty Hunter:** Complete nine Quest Mode challenges.
- **Voyager:** Discover all nine regions on the Quest Mode map.
- **The Conqueror (1.0):** Beat **all 81 quests**, on **any difficulty**.
- **Time Bender:** Travel to a negative year.

The important distinction is **discovering a region versus clearing every quest**. Voyager does not require the same total as The Conqueror. If your target is the latter, the completion count is the deciding factor, not merely opening the full map. For how to reach the modes after your first victory, see the <Link href="/9-kings">beginner and progression overview</Link>.

## Which Card, Gold, and Hand Achievements Need Special Planning?

Several card goals can be completed while building an ordinary kingdom, provided you do not accidentally spend or discard the resource you are trying to count.

**Decked up** needs **nine cards in hand**, whereas **Collector (1.0)** needs the **maximum possible hand size**. The wording does not say that nine cards must satisfy both achievements. Treat them as separate targets, especially if your run has effects that change how many cards you can hold.

Other useful distinctions are:

- **Flush... Kinda:** Hold five cards from the **same king**, not merely five cards.
- **King of Recycling:** Throw nine cards into the Pit **during one run**.
- **Meh, Whatever:** Throw **all your cards** into the Pit **in Year 33**. The year matters.
- **Just One More:** Reroll nine times on **one screen**.
- **One Dollar Short:** Arrive at a Merchant encounter with **29 gold**.
- **Cheapskate:** Save **999+ gold** in a run.
- **Sir Spends-a-Lot:** Buy **nine cards in one run**.
- **Spoiled:** Pay **999+ gold for one reroll**. This measures the price of a single reroll, not total gold spent on rerolls.

The new version also includes **Rake It In** (receive **999 gold in one turn**), **Desperate Measures** (play **nine Tomes in one turn**), **Gambling Problem** (activate nine Gachas in a run), and **One-trick Pony** (play **Rewind twice in the same turn**). Do not spread their explicitly single-turn or single-run actions across several runs and expect them to add together.

**All-in** uses the game's *Randomfy* wording for changing your base card. If the action has a different label on a particular screen, check that it is the actual base-changing effect before attempting the achievement.

## How Do You Complete Troop Count and Mount Achievements?

The older list already asks for unusual troop numbers, while 1.0 adds much larger totals and more specialized units. Check whether the goal refers to **one plot**, **one unit**, or the **whole army**:

- **Like Sardines:** 99 units in one plot.
- **Flash Mob:** 999 Mob troops on **one plot**.
- **Suicide Squad:** 99 Bombers in a single plot.
- **United We Lag (1.0):** An army of **9,999+ units**.
- **The Great Unifier (1.0):** **Unify 999+ troops**, which is not the same wording as simply fielding an army of that size.
- **Goat Farmer (1.0):** 99 Goats in one plot.
- **Alibaba:** 40 Thieves in a single plot.

**Ratticus the First** asks for a **level 99 Lab Rat**. **Big Guy (1.0)** instead asks you to apply **Gigantify three times to one troop within a single run**. Spreading Gigantify across three different troops does not match that requirement.

Mounting also has distinct targets: **Crossboar** requires a Ballista mounted on a Boar, and **Mountception** requires a Boar riding a Raptor. **The Fellowship (1.0)** needs a Wizard, Elf, Dwarf, and Soldier present together in your kingdom. Focus on the named units, not merely their total count.

If you are using a custom Joker deck to bring otherwise separate cards together, the <Link href="/9-kings/joker-mode">Joker Mode setup guide</Link> explains how to select the deck; the achievement-specific requirement remains here.

</section>

## How Do You Complete Plot, Building, and Adjacency Achievements?

These goals reward deliberate placement. Reserving the right spaces before your kingdom is full can be more useful than adding another powerful troop.

**Full-service (1.0)** requires a **Blacksmith, Library, Beacon, and Forest** to buff **the same troop plot**. Placing all four somewhere in the kingdom is not enough. Pick the troop first and check that each support effect actually applies to that plot.

**Stardew Kingdom** requires **four Farms around one troop**, while **Suburban King** needs **nine copies of the exact same card on nine different plots**. These ask for specific layouts, not just a large quantity of buildings.

Several other goals refer to distinct limits:

- **King of the Hill:** Get your **base to level 9**.
- **Welcome to my Crib (1.0):** Get a base plot **three levels above its cap**.
- **King of Nothing Left to Do:** Max-level **all plots** in one run.
- **Kings Without Borders:** Unlock **every plot** in a run.
- **Great Leap (1.0):** Level up plots **nine times in one turn**.
- **Barren Land (1.0):** Raze **three plots** in your kingdom.
- **Infestation (1.0):** Reach **nine or more Slime plots**.

**Sacrilege (1.0)** calls for a Shrine effect to fail specifically because **all your plots are maxed out**. Simply owning a Shrine is not the requirement. **It's Super Effective** specifies that **one X-Ray** must level up **five plots**; combining the effects of several X-Rays is not the described target.

The remaining building-specific achievements include **Wonderwall** (nine Wallmakers), **Double Double Double Dragon** (nine Dragon's Dens), **The Spire Slays** (nine Spires), **Wall Street** (Vaults on every unlocked plot except the base), and **It's a Trap** (99 traps on the field).

**Chain Chain Chain Reaction (1.0)** specifically requires one Concabulator to trigger a second and then a third. It is a chain of effects, not simply owning three Concabulators. **Taylorism** requires one building to apply its effect nine times **in the same turn**. Both deserve a setup that can demonstrate the exact chain or count before assuming the unlock should appear.

</section>

## Which Combat and Status Achievements Need an Exact Trigger?

A high final damage number is not enough for every combat achievement. Read whether the requirement is for a **plot**, a **single hit**, a particular **card**, or a status applied to **one enemy**.

- **Beefed Up:** Have one plot deal **999,999+ damage**.
- **Overkill (1.0):** Have a unit deal **99,999+ damage in a single hit**.
- **Death Cheater (1.0):** Get an **Obelisk** to **9,999+ damage**.
- **Brrrrrrrt (1.0):** Have a tower reach **maximum attack speed**.
- **Everyone's a Critic (1.0):** Get a plot to **100% critical chance**.
- **Roadrunner (1.0):** Get a troop to **9.0 movement speed**.

The status achievements are equally specific. **Esoteric** needs nine enchantments on one troop, while **Agnostic** needs **nine different** enchantments on one unit. **Cold, Cold Heart** calls for nine Frost stacks on a unit. **Plan A, B, and C (1.0)** requires the **same enemy unit** to be poisoned, frozen, and stunned simultaneously; applying those conditions to different targets does not match the description.

**Don't You Know That You're Toxic (1.0)** requires **999 poison stacks on one enemy**. **The Last of Us** asks for **Mycelium's Sporeling to kill a boss**. A boss dying while Sporelings are present does not by itself establish that the specified unit landed the finishing blow.

Some of the new triggers are direct card actions: **Balance My A\*\*** requires using Balance on a Paladin; **Smells Like Meta** requires a Tome on a Golem; and **Bad Move** requires Echoform on your lowest-damage troop. By contrast, **The Devs Rigged My RNG** depends on a Library effect failing **three times in a row**. That is a streak condition, not a guaranteed result of selecting a Library three times.

If your army cannot reach a damage or troop threshold, compare the <Link href="/9-kings/best-builds">King and Joker combinations</Link> before another attempt. Then check whether the achievement needs one hit, one plot, or one enemy to reach the stated value.

</section>

## How Do Diplomacy, Time, and Chaos Achievements Work?

**Toxic Relationship** requires you to **declare war and make peace with the same king during one run**. **Nobel Prize** requires peace with all enemy kings simultaneously, while **The Enemy Of My Enemy Is My Enemy** asks you to be at war with all kings at once. The last two need opposite diplomatic states; do not attempt to maintain both at the same moment.

**Timerelapse** asks you to travel backward in time **three times in one run**. **Time Bender**, on the other hand, requires reaching a **negative year**. One is a number of time-travel actions; the other is a destination.

Version 1.0 also adds **Chaotic Good**, for defeating the **Jack of Chaos**, and **You're So Crazy**, for using **nine Chaos cards**. The requirements name different actions. Defeating the Jack of Chaos does not automatically prove that nine Chaos cards were used.

**Cursed** requires a Blessing to be missed completely, not merely for its reward to be undesirable. Check whether the Prophecy's condition actually failed instead of judging the Blessing by how useful it would have been.
