<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
Unlock **all nine kings** to unlock **the Joker** in *9 Kings*. Select the Joker to build a **custom nine-card deck** using cards from different kings, choose your Perks, and open **Conditions** to set the difficulty, starting resources, enemies, Decrees, and other rules. **Check the enemy kings before pressing Play**: a deck can be ready while the opponent selection is not.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

The Joker gives you control over the part of *9 Kings* that usually depends on whichever king you pick. Instead of starting from one ruler's fixed selection, you choose the cards you want to play together, then decide what kind of game they will face. It is a good way to try cross-king combinations, but an impressive-looking deck can still fail if you forget a combat unit, choose incompatible Perks, or start against the wrong opponents.

<h2 id="unlock-joker">
  How Do You Unlock Joker Mode in 9 Kings?
</h2>

**Unlock all nine playable kings.** Once you have done that, the Joker becomes available from the king selection screen. It is not unlocked simply by winning one run or by completing the Quest Mode map.

<h3 id="joker-locked">
  Why Is the Joker Still Locked?
</h3>

If even one regular king is still locked, the Joker remains unavailable. Open king selection to identify the missing ruler, then check the <Link href="/9-kings">nine-king unlock requirements</Link> for the milestone you still need. Raising an already unlocked king's level does not replace unlocking another one.

<h2 id="build-custom-deck">
  How Do You Make a Custom Joker Deck?
</h2>

The Joker lets you **hand-pick a nine-card deck** from cards belonging to different kings, including Rainbow cards. Start by choosing the **base**, then fill the deck with cards that can work together. Unlike a normal run, you can decide on a cross-king combination before the first battle rather than hoping to find every required card later.

Set up the Joker's starting deck before entering a run:

1. **Select your base.** Choose one of the available base cards at the bottom of the screen.
2. **Fill the nine card slots.** Pick cards from the available selection, including cards belonging to different kings.
3. **Review your selection.** Check that the cards you intended to use appear in the nine slots. Change any selection you do not want before starting.
4. **Open Perks.** Configure the bonuses you want to use with the selected deck.
5. **Check Conditions.** Confirm the difficulty, enemy kings, and other settings before pressing Play.

<h3 id="rainbow-cards">
  Can You Put Rainbow Cards in a Joker Deck?
</h3>

**Yes.** The Joker can include Rainbow cards in its custom nine-card deck, alongside cards from the regular kings. Version 1.0 expanded the available Rainbow card selection.

For specific Rainbow card effects, combinations, and ways to scale your deck, see the <Link href="/9-kings/best-builds">9 Kings Best Builds guide</Link>.

<h2 id="joker-perks">
  How Do You Choose Perks in Joker Mode?
</h2>

Open **Perks** after selecting the cards. The Joker can combine options from different king-related pools, so you are not limited to making the deck behave like one ordinary ruler.

Perk placement matters because some bonuses depend on their position in the layout. For example, **Authoritarian** makes the top-leftmost regular Perk **x3**. It does not automatically triple every Perk you have equipped.

Check which regular Perk receives the multiplier before starting your run. If you change the layout, inspect the active x2 and x3 indicators again to make sure the intended Perks are being affected.

<h2 id="joker-conditions">
  What Can You Change in Joker Mode Conditions?
</h2>

**Conditions** is where you decide what the Joker deck will face. You can adjust the difficulty, starting gold, lives, and rerolls, choose opposing kings, and alter the rules and Decree availability. Version 1.0 also allows game events to be disabled.

<h3 id="starting-enemies">
  How Do You Choose Enemy Kings and Starting Wars?
</h3>

Look at the opponent selection **before** pressing Play. Choosing who you fight is more than a difficulty preference: defeating rival kings can give you access to their cards during a run. Choosing a particular opponent can therefore be part of getting the cards you need.

If you want to stress-test a deck, start with several enemies or choose a more demanding configuration. If you are learning how two cards interact, a simpler matchup makes the result easier to understand.

**Do not assume the game will automatically fill an empty opponent selection with random enemies.** One Joker run began with only a self-rebellion matchup because the intended rival kings had not been selected. The player returned to Conditions and changed the starting wars before trying again.

<h3 id="choose-decrees-events">
  Should You Enable Every Decree and Event?
</h3>

Not necessarily. More available Decrees give you a wider range of possibilities, but they can also make a particular result less predictable. If you want to test one deck's interaction with a certain king's Decrees, review which pools are enabled rather than leaving the choice to chance.

The same principle applies to other events. Turning an event off changes the opportunities and interruptions that appear during the run. Keep the events your deck relies on, and make deliberate changes when you are testing how the deck performs without them.

<h2 id="first-joker-run">
  How Do You Test a New Joker Deck?
</h2>

Begin with a matchup where you can observe your opening cards without losing immediately. During the first few years, watch whether your troop stops the first attack, whether the chosen Perks affect the intended cards, and whether the available rewards can develop the deck. A hand full of late-game support effects will not become a working army just because the starting Conditions are generous.

If an interaction fails, change the part of the setup responsible for it rather than changing the deck, Perks, and opponents at the same time. A missing combat unit needs a different starting card; a bonus applied to the wrong Perk needs a layout change; an absent rival's cards call for a check of the starting wars. For combinations that survive the opening but need more damage later, compare the <Link href="/9-kings/best-builds">Joker build examples</Link>.

If the setup survives the opening but stalls later, it is likely a deck-development problem rather than an unlock problem. The <Link href="/9-kings/best-builds">best builds guide</Link> covers complete card combinations, scaling choices, and the weaknesses exposed during actual runs.

<h2 id="joker-problems">
  Why Isn't Your Joker Mode Setup Working?
</h2>

<h3 id="only-rebellion">You only fight your own rebellion</h3>

Return to **Conditions** and check the enemy king selections. Choose the rivals you intend to face instead of relying on an empty selection to turn into a random matchup.

<h3 id="no-defense">Your starting hand has no reliable defense</h3>

Return to the deck and replace a purely supportive card with a troop or another early damage source. A future scaling combo cannot help if your base falls before you assemble it.

<h3 id="perks-not-working">Your Perks appear weaker than expected</h3>

Check the descriptions and their positions. A modifier that affects certain slots or certain kinds of Perks may not improve every effect in the layout.

<h3 id="card-copies-missing">You cannot find the extra cards your setup needs</h3>

Check which kings you are fighting and whether your deck has another way to obtain or upgrade the required cards. Selecting one Rainbow card for the starting deck does not make future copies automatic.

<h3 id="incorrect-conditions">Your difficulty or starting resources look wrong</h3>

Reopen **Conditions** before starting another run. Verify the selected difficulty, gold, lives, rerolls, and event settings instead of assuming they match your previous run.

If the correct rivals, starting cards, and Perks are selected but the same battle keeps ending in defeat, the remaining problem is how those cards fight together. Try changing the weakest combat plot before changing the entire Joker setup.
