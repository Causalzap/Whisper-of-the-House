## How Do You Build a Team for Open Forest Hard Without a Legendary Carry?

In one Open Forest Hard clear, Ent Lord and a three-star Arch-Shaman formed part of the late-game team. The Wave 30 result named Ent Lord as Best Unit. That does not identify the highest damage dealer, so check the damage report before deciding whether your next absorbed unit belongs on the frontline or with the spellcaster.

The team did not begin with a perfect composition. It developed through several uncomfortable rounds in which the remaining lives dropped and the player had to decide whether another spin was worth more than improving an existing unit.

### Develop Arch-Shaman Without Neglecting the Frontline

The Mage line becomes particularly valuable when its damage continues increasing through the middle waves. The late-game team included a three-star Arch-Shaman, but the result does not establish it as the highest damage dealer. Invest further in Arch-Shaman when its damage or survival needs justify the resources. Otherwise, strengthen the frontline that keeps it attacking.

A useful order of decisions is:

1. Keep an early melee unit in front so the Mage can attack without taking the first hits.
2. Promote a Mage toward **Shaman** when its damage options fit your current team.
3. Feed units into the Shaman when doing so produces a meaningful level or skill improvement.
4. Keep a healing unit available instead of converting every Mage-family unit into damage.
5. Check the damage summary after fights. If Shaman is carrying, stop splitting your best absorption material among weaker attackers.

A promoted melee unit is only valuable while it still contributes to the team. Before absorbing one, check whether it is taking the first hits, protecting Arch-Shaman, or providing another ability that your remaining units cannot replace.

### Position Ent Lord to Protect the Backline

Ent Lord provides a strong frontline presence while Arch-Shaman attacks from behind it. Keep the defender where it can intercept approaching enemies without exposing the spellcaster to the first contact.

Don't push every strong unit into the same part of the battlefield. If the enemy reaches your backline, move the melee defenders to intercept it. If the defenders survive comfortably but the fight lasts too long, invest in the damage dealer instead of adding another defensive unit.

The successful Hard clear also used healing and a Guardian Golem effect. These helped the team endure longer engagements, but they should not be mistaken for mandatory starting units or guaranteed shop rewards.

### What Should You Change Before the Final Boss?

During the last waves, keeping the damage dealers protected became increasingly important. The frontline had to absorb pressure while Arch-Shaman and the remaining support units continued contributing.

The player moved the main damage source farther back before the final battle. The enemy dealt area damage, but the team's healing was sufficient to keep the formation together through the win.

Use that situation as your final check:

- Is your Shaman still producing the damage needed to end the fight?
- Can a defender take the first contact without exposing the Shaman?
- Does your healing cover the damage actually reaching the team?
- Are you keeping a weak extra unit when absorbing it would improve your main carry?

If enemies reach Arch-Shaman before Ent Lord can intercept them, move the defender toward the incoming attack path. Losing the spellcaster early can remove sustained damage even when the rest of the formation survives.

## How Do You Build Around Dread Cavalry, Nightmare, and Apocalypse?

**Dread Cavalry → Nightmare → Apocalypse** is a powerful melee progression when you obtain the Legendary cavalry unit. A Deep Cave Hard clear developed this unit to level 99, and the final battle damage summary showed **11,599 damage from Apocalypse**.

That result demonstrates the unit's late-game potential, but the early stages were much less comfortable.

### Do Not Treat Dread Cavalry as an Instant Win

In this run, Dread Cavalry carried the **Revival I Imprint**, which revived it once at 40% HP and granted 20% more Attack for eight seconds. Even with that recovery, the cavalry could still charge ahead of its support and fall again before making a meaningful contribution.

That is the first problem to solve. A fast unit that enters combat ahead of the rest of the army can lose much of its value before it is properly developed.

When Dread Cavalry appears:

1. Keep your existing defenders and support units instead of immediately absorbing the whole army.
2. Move the Legendary back if it is running ahead and dying before your other units engage.
3. Start feeding it suitable summons to reach the first important promotion.
4. Reassess its placement after each promotion, because its movement and combat behavior may change.

The unit becomes much more useful once its promotion skills can contribute to the fight.

These are different roles, not two versions of the same damage upgrade.

A high damage total depended on that continued investment. It was not produced by an untrained Dread Cavalry.

### Why Frost Empress and Ant Lord Help

**Frost Empress** supplied freezing and control during the run. This gave the aggressive melee carry more opportunities to attack without taking uninterrupted pressure.

**Ant Lord** contributed a durable frontline presence. That matters when Apocalypse is positioned to deal damage rather than absorb every enemy attack itself.

Before the Deep Cave final boss, **Cave Basilisk**, the player adjusted the formation again to avoid exposing a key unit unnecessarily. Apocalypse then delivered the majority of the final damage.

## How Do You Make a Fire Build Work in Wasteland Hard?

A Wasteland Hard victory came from a combination of **Flare's fire damage**, a developed **Ant Lord**, and supporting units that kept the formation functional.

This run is useful because it did not depend on achieving a high faction-synergy tier. The team entered the late waves without an active faction combination, yet its individual units were strong enough to win.

### Combine Burning Attacks With Skills That Exploit Burn

**Flare's Combustion Blast** hits up to three enemies for 100% Attack damage and applies Burn. If an enemy is already burning, the skill also triggers an explosion dealing an additional 80% Attack damage.

Together, these effects give Flare a way to apply Burn before Combustion Blast lands. Keep Flare attacking consistently so burning targets are available when its six-second skill cooldown finishes.

For a similar team, check the following:

- Can your damage dealer apply Burn before its strongest fire skill hits?
- Does the next skill actually benefit from the Burn status?
- Are you improving the damage dealer's attack opportunities, or spending resources on an unrelated unit?
- Can your frontline survive while the burn-based damage takes effect?

A fire ability does not become stronger merely because several other units happen to have fire-themed names. The interaction between the actual skill effects is what matters.

### Use Ant Lord to Protect the Damage Dealers

Ant Lord was another major part of the Wasteland setup. Its durability and melee presence gave the rest of the team room to operate.

Several Duelist-family units were retained early in the run, allowing the main Duelist line to keep developing. Later, the player considered whether Ant Lord should take a more aggressive position and whether another unit needed protection instead.

If the Ant Lord is taking almost no damage while a different frontline unit repeatedly falls, the formation may be wasting its defensive strength. Move it to a position where it can intercept attacks without dragging enemies into the backline.

### What Happened Against Wasteland Piper?

The final boss was **Wasteland Piper**.

The fire-focused damage dealer performed strongly during the last encounter and the boss went down. The player identified Flare as the standout unit of that run.

This does not prove that Wasteland Piper has a universal elemental weakness. The result shows that the developed burn setup was effective against that boss in this particular fight.

If Combustion Blast keeps hitting targets that are not already burning, the additional explosion damage will not occur. Keep Flare attacking long enough to apply Blazing Blade's Burn, and position Ant Lord where it can prevent enemies from interrupting the damage sequence.

The Lost Ruins Hard clear was an unusually fortunate run. A **Legendary Skull Fighter appeared around wave 3**, and several Rare units were added later.

The early Skull Fighter was eventually developed into **Gaze**, which appeared as Best Unit on the Wave 30 victory screen. The rest of the team supplied melee protection, ranged attacks, control, and support during the Lost Ruins Hard clear.

Skull Fighter became one of the team's main investments as it developed into Gaze. Later summons helped raise its level and strengthen its combat contribution, while other promoted units continued covering frontline and support duties.

However, the rest of the army still had important jobs. Several Rare units helped provide frontline coverage, while ranged and support units added damage, healing and control.

Do not automatically sacrifice every other promoted unit to a Legendary. If an ally is the only source of healing, crowd control, or protection, absorbing it can make the main carry less effective.

### When Is Forest Synergy Worth Completing?

Late in the Lost Ruins Hard run, the Forest Synergy counter showed **3/4 types**. The army already had the two-type Regeneration bonus, but it was one distinct Forest type short of the next tier.

At the next Class Promotion, **Crystal Mage** offered a Forest option. Choosing it could fill that missing type and raise the counter to **4/4**, provided the army did not already have the same eligible type.

At four types, Forest Regeneration restores **16% of each affected Forest unit's maximum HP**, up from 10% at two types. It triggers the first time that Forest unit falls to 50% HP or below during a battle, once per unit per battle. The six-type tier restores 22%.

Check the faction icon and counter before choosing. Another copy of a type you already have will not fill the missing slot.

## Which Units Should You Absorb, Promote, or Keep?

The most important choice often happens after a spin gives you several units. Some are useful as immediate defenders, some have a better Imprint than your current unit, and others should be absorbed to finish a promotion.

Use the current battlefield problem to choose a recipient.

### When Is a Strong Imprint Worth Keeping?

**Imprints should support the unit's job.**

A repeated-hit effect is most useful when the unit can attack often enough to benefit from it. A defensive or survival effect matters more on a character expected to receive hits. A skill that damages additional targets is less helpful if the unit struggles to reach or attack the enemy in the first place.

In Lost Ruins, Skull Fighter's **Earthquake I Imprint** gave its attacks a chance to damage and stun enemies across the battlefield. In Deep Cave, Dread Cavalry's **Revival I Imprint** provided one recovery opportunity after taking lethal damage.

These effects serve different purposes: Earthquake can interrupt enemies, while Revival helps a vulnerable unit survive one defeat. Neither replaces the need for appropriate positioning or support.

Avoid choosing Imprints solely by their displayed rarity or by the size of a percentage without reading what triggers them.

## Which Faction Synergies Are Worth Building Around?

A faction bonus becomes useful when the actual promoted units in your team meet its requirement. Keeping several copies of the same unit is not necessarily equivalent to having several eligible faction types.

The successful teams illustrate three different approaches:

Faction bonuses can decide close fights, but they do not replace damage, healing or positioning.

Before promoting a unit just for its faction, look at the next battle. If the promotion would remove your only useful defender or damage source, the bonus may not compensate for what you lose.

### What If the Faction Synergy Counter Does Not Increase?

Check the promoted class and the eligible unit types currently present. A newly summoned unit does not necessarily count toward the faction you are targeting.

If several units seem to share a faction but the counter remains unchanged, inspect their class names and the synergy panel rather than assuming duplicate bodies count as additional types.
