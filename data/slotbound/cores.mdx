## What Is the Difference Between Cores, Items, and Imprints?

**Cores** modify the rules or rewards of your run. They can affect spin costs, how symbols move, when extra symbols appear, or how certain results pay out.

**Items** are purchased through the shop and provide effects such as healing, damage, protection, movement bonuses, or other battlefield advantages.

**Imprints** and **Awakening** affect individual units rather than changing the slot machine or granting a purchased Item effect. They matter when developing a unit, but they serve a different purpose from Core selection.

Choose Cores when the reels, summon opportunities, or spin costs are holding you back. Look at Items when your current army needs help surviving or finishing battles. For unit-specific Imprints, promotions, and combat roles, see the <Link href="/slotbound/builds">Slotbound builds and evolutions guide</Link>.

### Which Core Should You Take When Gold Is Running Low?

**Gold Tooth** is valuable when you can already complete a winning spin early in each wave. Its first-win trigger makes the following spin free, providing another opportunity without the usual gold cost.

If you are regularly absorbing units, **Nutrient Leaf** offers a different way to reduce spending. After the first absorption of a wave, your next spin costs 50% less.

The difference is when you earn the discount:

- Gold Tooth needs a winning spin.
- Nutrient Leaf needs an absorption.
- Neither makes every spin free or permanently reduces all future prices.

If you keep winning early, Gold Tooth can provide consistent value. If you are feeding many spare units into a carry, Nutrient Leaf can support that process.

Be careful when both discounts could affect the same upcoming spin. Check the displayed price rather than assuming their benefits combine in a particular order.

### Is Echo Mirror Worth Picking?

Echo Mirror pays off when a losing spin contains at least four copies of one symbol. The result still does not summon a unit, but the extra Essence can help fund a shop purchase.

Choose it when unsuccessful spins frequently meet that condition and you can use the additional Essence. If you need another summon immediately, Copycat's ability to repair a qualifying reel addresses a different problem.

## Should You Choose Double Ledger, Copycat, or Another Reel Core?

These Cores act at different stages of a spin. The important question is whether your current board needs help **after a win** or **after a failure**.

### Double Ledger: Make the First Win Do More

Double Ledger removes symbols from winning lines and lets new symbols fall into the empty positions. Its displayed trigger applies to the first win of each wave.

This is useful when your board already produces winning lines. The new symbols may create another promising arrangement.

However, a fresh symbol entering the board is not automatically another winning line. You still need the new arrangement to satisfy the relevant matching condition.

**Choose Double Ledger when your first wins are consistent enough to trigger its effect.** If your problem is getting any win at all, it does not solve that initial failure.

### Copycat: Repair a Specific Near-Miss

Copycat works after a failed spin, but only when the top and bottom symbols of a reel match.

It changes that reel's middle symbol to match the other two, once per wave.

That makes it useful for a particular arrangement: two matching symbols separated by an unhelpful middle symbol.

It does not allow you to choose any new symbol or freely rearrange the complete board.

Check the reel before committing to another spin. If its top and bottom symbols do not match, Copycat has no qualifying pattern to repair.

### Oracle's Mirror: See More Before Using Blast

Oracle's Mirror displays the first incoming symbol on two random reels while you prepare a Blast.

That gives additional information before committing to a board change.

It is most useful if Blast is already part of the way you manage the reels. If you rarely use Blast, a Core that changes spin costs or produces more summons may help more often.

Previewing two reels also does not mean that every incoming symbol is known. Check which reels are actually covered by the preview.

## When Are Jester Cores Worth Taking?

The number of spins available changes which Jester Core is useful. **Black Note** supplies a Jester Hat on the first spin of each wave, while **Torn Note** requires reaching every sixth spin. If you cannot afford that many spins, Torn Note will not activate before you stop.

**Joker Seed** and **Wildcard** provide additional symbol opportunities without waiting for those scheduled spin positions. Their full effects are listed in the table above.

When your goal is to summon Jester, compare these triggers with the Gold and Nudge available in your current wave. For the actual three-hat match and Jester promotions, see the <Link href="/slotbound/jester">Jester summon and class instructions</Link>.

## Which Items Should You Buy in Slotbound?

Items are most valuable when they fix a weakness you have already observed.

Buying another damage bonus is not always the best choice. If a strong attacker dies just before activating its next skill, healing or temporary protection might contribute more to the following battle.

These Items provide healing, basic-attack damage, mobility, or protection against terrain penalties.

### First Aid or More Damage?

At **Lv.1**, First Aid instantly restores 20% maximum HP to the first ally that falls to 30% HP or lower, once per battle. It is useful when a valuable unit repeatedly survives the opening attack but falls during the remaining fight.

**Sturdy Fist** provides a different benefit: at Lv.1, all allies gain **6% basic attack damage**. Choose it when your units survive long enough to attack repeatedly but need more damage to finish enemies.

The distinction is survival versus sustained basic-attack damage. First Aid cannot repeatedly heal the same battle, while Sturdy Fist does not prevent a fragile unit from being eliminated before it can contribute.

### When Is Wasteland Camel Worth Buying?

**Wasteland Camel** creates a mount that takes hits before its rider.

Its displayed effects grant the rider:

- **60% additional movement speed**.
- **25% additional Attack**.
- Protection through the Camel taking hits first. When the Camel falls, the rider dismounts and continues fighting.

The movement bonus is especially useful when a unit needs to reach enemies quickly. But speed is not automatically beneficial for every character.

A fragile attacker that runs into enemies ahead of its defenders may still be in danger once the Camel falls.

Choose the rider deliberately. The Camel is an additional layer of protection, not unlimited healing or permanent immunity.

### Does Hardening Powder Remove the Lost Ruins Marsh Penalty?

**Hardening Powder** is useful when Marsh terrain interferes with an important unit's movement or attack speed.

When an ally enters Marsh, **Hardening Powder** grants four seconds of Hardening. During that time, Marsh no longer reduces the ally's movement or attack speed. The effect can trigger once per Marsh per battle.

### How Do You Avoid Failing a Fate Stone Quest?

Check the quest's progress, remaining waves, and required result before committing to it. A winning streak, a family requirement, and a remaining-HP condition are separate checks.

For **Origin**, make sure the required units are present during the consecutive victories. For **Milestone** and **Blessing**, winning alone is insufficient if your team finishes below the required HP threshold.

A quest can also expire before its requirements are completed. Watch the deadline shown on the Fate Stone rather than waiting until the last wave to check your progress.

If a promotion-related quest fails to update immediately despite meeting its condition, make sure the game is updated. The November 13, 2026 hotfix addressed a problem with Fate Stone promotion goals not registering immediately.
