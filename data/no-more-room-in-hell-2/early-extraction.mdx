I use **Early Extraction** when preserving the Responder matters more than
finishing the current mission.

It is not available the moment I spawn. After the run has been active for a

looking for the active exit.

On one run, the exit was a **small hatch clearly labeled Early Extraction**.
Other routes can require me to reach or prepare the extraction point first, so
I follow the current marker instead of memorizing one permanent location.

The tradeoff is important:

**Early Extraction**

→ keeps the Responder alive

→ gives **no character progression**

→ pays only a **small portion of Credits**

**Final Extraction**

→ gives character XP

→ converts the team's collected Supplies into the full Credit payout

I take the early exit when infection is getting out of control, the team is
collapsing, my resources are nearly gone, or I am protecting a developed
Responder that is no longer worth gambling on one more objective.

<h2 id="how-early-extraction-works">How Early Extraction works</h2>

Early Extraction is the game's emergency exit.

I do not need to complete the full Objective scenario before using it, but it
also is not something I can immediately trigger the second the run begins.

The CRC makes the option available after the mission has been active for a
short period.

That announcement is the point where I start treating early extraction as a
real option.

I do **not** use an estimated timer from memory.

I wait until the game confirms that an early exit is available.

That matters because in one run we loaded in specifically intending to leave as
soon as possible. We spent the opening minutes asking whether the extraction
had appeared yet, then only started tracking it once the option actually became
available.

My sequence is:

**wait for Early Extraction availability**

→ **check the current extraction direction**

→ **reach the exit**

→ **finish any local access interaction**

→ **commit only when I am sure I want to abandon the full mission**

The last step is important.

Early Extraction is not just another checkpoint.

I am choosing survival over normal mission progression.

<h2 id="find-early-extraction">
  Look for the active extraction point, not one memorized location
</h2>

The clearest early extraction I encountered was a small hatch.

When I walked up to it, the interaction itself identified it as **Early
Extraction**.

That is much more reliable than trying to identify every bunker or hatch by
appearance.

There can also be more than one possible extraction direction around a run, so
I keep checking my current navigation information rather than running toward
the exit I remember from the previous match.

In another attempt, we were actively searching for early extraction and knew
there were several possible exits ahead.

That changed how I moved through the map.

I stopped:

- checking every weapon crate;
- clearing buildings that were not on the route;
- doubling back for attachments;
- fighting zombies that were not blocking progress.

Once I have decided to leave, the mission is no longer a loot run.

The safest route to extraction becomes the objective.

<h2 id="early-extraction-access">
  Some early exits still need a local access step
</h2>

Finding the extraction area does not always mean I can instantly disappear.

During a Nightmare Broadway run, I found wiring associated with an early
extraction route.

I do not turn that into a universal rule that every Early Extraction requires
wiring.

The useful rule is broader:

> **If I reach the extraction area and it is not immediately usable, I check
> the nearby active interaction before leaving the area.**

That is especially important when enemies are close.

It is easy to assume the exit is broken, run away, and then discover that I
only needed to finish one nearby access step.

If I am playing with teammates, one person handles the interaction while the
others create space.

I do not make four people stand on the same switch.

<h2 id="when-to-early-extract">
  I decide based on the Responder, not on how much of the map I already finished
</h2>

The hardest part of Early Extraction is not finding the hatch.

It is accepting that leaving can be the correct decision.

I used to think:

> We already completed this much. We might as well finish.

That is exactly the kind of thinking that gets a valuable Responder killed.

What I have already completed is a sunk cost.

The decision I make now is based on what remains.

I look at:

- infection;
- current health;
- remaining healing;
- ammunition;
- how many teammates are still functional;
- how dangerous the next objective is;
- how valuable the Responder is to me.

If the next phase is a short objective and the team is healthy, I keep going.

If the next phase is a long holdout and I am already almost empty, I start
looking at the early exit much more seriously.

<h2 id="infection-early-extraction">
  Infection is one of the strongest reasons I leave early
</h2>

Infection is where Early Extraction changes from an optional safety valve into
a way to save an entire character.

If I still have enough Phalanx Pills and Experimental Gene Therapy is already
solving the problem, I am comfortable continuing.

If the visual symptoms are getting worse, Gene Therapy is nowhere to be found,
and my remaining pills are disappearing, I change priorities.

I do not spend the next ten minutes searching another optional building while
the Responder gets closer to turning.

<h2 id="low-resources">
  Low ammo alone does not make me extract, but a collapsing loadout can
</h2>

I do not early extract every time my primary weapon runs low.

That would be too conservative.

The real warning is when several resources fail at once.

For example:

- primary ammo almost empty;
- no useful secondary ammo;
- little or no healing;
- weak melee option;
- long objective still ahead;
- teammates also running dry.

That is when the run can change from recoverable to dangerous very quickly.

I have reached late sections with only a handful of shotgun shells and still
finished because the rest of the squad was stable.

I have also been in runs where everybody was calling for ammunition and
healing at the same time.

Those are different situations.

Before taking the early exit I ask:

> **Can the squad realistically recover from the next fight?**

If the answer is yes, I continue.

If the answer is basically:

> only if the next room gives us exactly the loot we need

I stop relying on luck.

<h2 id="multiplayer-early-extraction">
  In multiplayer, I make sure everyone knows we are actually leaving
</h2>

I do not casually trigger an early extraction interaction while the rest of the
team is still treating the match like normal.

One multiplayer run made that lesson very obvious.

We reached the early extraction area, somebody committed to it, and the match
went from normal movement to:

> **we abandoned**

almost immediately.

The players who were not expecting it were confused because everyone simply
disappeared from the active run.

So my multiplayer check is:

**Is everyone leaving?**

**Is anyone still carrying a mission item we care about?**

**Is somebody separated and still trying to reach us?**

**Does anyone think we are only regrouping here?**

Then I commit.

The point of Early Extraction is preserving the squad's Responders.

Creating confusion at the exit defeats the purpose.

<h2 id="early-extraction-rewards">
  Early Extraction saves the Responder, not the full progression payout
</h2>

This is the tradeoff I care about most.

Early Extraction preserves the Responder, but I give up the progression value
of completing the mission normally.

Under the current 1.0 rules, an early exit gives:

**no character progression**

and only:

**a small portion of Credits**

That is very different from Final Extraction.

At final exfil, the Responder receives character experience and the team's
collected Supplies are converted into the full Credit payout.

So I do not think about Early Extraction as:

> free progress without finishing.

It is closer to:

> **paying an opportunity cost to keep the character alive.**

That is why I usually stay when the run is healthy.

The final payout is better.

But when I am protecting a high-level Responder, the reward calculation
changes.

Giving up character XP from **one run** can be cheaper than losing the character
that survived many previous runs.

<h2 id="survival-early-extraction">
  Leaving a Survival run early follows the same survival-first logic
</h2>

I have also used an early exit during Survival instead of staying for the full
run.

The immediate reason was not a complicated strategy.

The group simply decided it was time to leave and took the available extraction.

The result reinforced the same progression rule: we did not receive normal
character progression for abandoning the run early.

That makes the option useful, but not something I use to farm character levels.

If my goal is progression, I finish.

If my goal has changed to preserving the Responder, I leave.

<h2 id="early-vs-final">
  The decision I make before giving up Final Extraction
</h2>

I use one final test.

### I stay for Final Extraction when

The squad still has a functional loadout, infection is controlled, and the
remaining objective looks survivable.

The extra risk has a real payoff because Final Extraction gives the character
progression and full Supplies-based Credit result I am playing for.

### I take Early Extraction when

The Responder itself has become the thing I am trying to protect.

That usually means:

- uncontrolled infection;
- almost no remaining infection treatment;
- critical resource shortage;
- teammates dead or unable to help;
- a high-level Responder facing a bad risk/reward situation;
- a run that is clearly falling apart before a long final phase.

I do not need the situation to be literally unwinnable.

I only need the expected value of continuing to become worse than the value of
keeping the character.

<h2 id="early-extraction-mistakes">
  The Early Extraction mistakes I avoid now
</h2>

### Looking for it immediately after spawning

Early Extraction becomes available after the run has been active for a while.

I wait for the CRC availability call instead of wasting the opening searching
for an inactive exit.

### Memorizing one hatch location

One run gave me a clearly labeled hatch.

That does not mean every map or scenario puts Early Extraction in the same
place.

I follow the current marker.

### Assuming the exit is broken when it needs another interaction

I check the immediate area for wiring or another active setup step before
leaving.

### Treating Early Extraction like normal completion

It is not.

I preserve the Responder, but I give up character progression and most of the
Credit payout.

### Staying because I already invested time in the run

The last twenty minutes do not make the next ten minutes safer.

I judge the remaining risk, not the time already spent.

### Leaving too early with a healthy squad

Early Extraction is not automatically the smart option.

If the team is stable, Final Extraction is much more valuable for progression.

### Triggering it before the squad agrees

In multiplayer, I call the exit decision clearly before committing.

A survival mechanic is much less useful if half the team does not know the run
is ending.
