<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
*No More Room in Hell 2* has **35 base-game achievements**, but I do not treat
them as 35 separate grinds.

My efficient route is:

**finish the Tutorial**

→ **clear all six Objective maps and three Survival maps on Normal or higher**

→ **keep one dependable Responder alive for 10 successful matches and push that
character toward levels 50 and 100**

→ **develop two more Responders to level 50**

→ **complete four Assignments while normal runs are already progressing**

→ **let firearm, melee, explosive, loot, padlock, healing, revive, sharing,
Credit, and Account XP totals build in the background**

→ **do the restrictive one-match challenges once I already know the maps**

→ **combine Pacifist and Pulling a Trigger is Like Ordering Takeout in one
no-weapon Objective run**

→ **leave the 10 Nightmare completions and long Account/Credit totals for later**

The achievements I deliberately plan are **Pacifist**, **Pulling a Trigger is
Like Ordering Takeout**, **Nice Shootin', Tex**, **Cowboy Action Shooter**,
**General Contractor**, **All-Star Cast**, and **Waking Nightmare**.

Those are the ones where one reflex input, one lost Responder, or one missing
weapon can waste an otherwise useful run.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

<h2 id="best-achievement-route">
  The achievement route I use to avoid unnecessary replays
</h2>

The biggest waste of time is treating the achievement list as a queue. A large
part of the list advances naturally while I am learning maps, keeping
Responders alive, and completing normal multiplayer runs.

My route is:

1. **Finish the Tutorial.**
2. Start clearing maps on **Normal** once I understand the basic controls.
3. Use one dependable Responder through as many early successful runs as I safely can.
4. Complete Assignments whenever they fit a run I was already planning.
5. Let cumulative kills, loot, padlocks, healing, revives, sharing, Credits, and Account levels build without dedicated farming.
6. Keep the first Responder moving toward 10 successful matches and levels 50 and 100.
7. Develop the second and third Responders for All-Star Cast without unnecessarily risking the finished characters.
8. Do Nice Shootin', Tex, Cowboy Action Shooter, and General Contractor when the right equipment appears in a stable run.
9. Do **Pacifist + Pulling a Trigger is Like Ordering Takeout together** by following Pacifist's stricter rules.
10. Start counting Nightmare clears only after ordinary map navigation is no longer causing failures.
11. Leave Account level 100 and the lifetime Credit totals for long cleanup.

A normal Objective run can advance a map clear, Veteran, Responder levels,
Account levels, Credits, Range Day, Rip & Tear, Loot Goblin, padlocks, co-op
support achievements, and an Assignment without changing the main reason I
entered the match.

That overlap is where most of the time saving comes from.

<h2 id="map-achievements">
  Do the nine map achievements on Normal or higher
</h2>

There are **nine map-completion achievements**: six Objective maps and three
Survival maps.

Every one specifies **Normal or higher**.

I still use Beginner if I genuinely need to learn something, but I do not clear
every map there and assume the completion achievements are being solved at the
same time. Once the route feels reliable enough to survive, I move to Normal.

The Objective maps are:

- Power Plant;
- Pottsville;
- Lewiston;
- Broadway;
- Beaulieu Hospital;
- Raven Rock.

I keep their detailed progression in their individual walkthroughs because the
achievement condition itself is simple:

> **Finish the map on Normal or higher.**

For Flooded, Night of the Living Dead, and Lighthouse, I use the same rule. If
the map completion is still missing, surviving the full Survival run matters
more than forcing another restrictive challenge into the same match.

<h2 id="closing-tickets">
  Closing Tickets: complete four Assignments during useful runs
</h2>

**Closing Tickets** requires **four completed Assignments**.

I do not create four disposable matches purely for that achievement. I check
the current Assignments before deploying and look for goals that overlap with
something I already want from the run.

Assignments I have encountered include things such as:

- blunt-weapon kills;
- pistol kills;
- shotgun kills;
- barricading a door or window;
- placing deployable equipment;
- extracting multiple times.

If I need blunt kills and still need a Normal map clear, I use a blunt weapon
when the fight is safe. If the Assignment needs extractions, I become less
willing to throw away a stable finale for optional loot.

Barricade progress is also better when the barricade is solving a real defense
problem instead of being placed on a random doorway only for the counter.

Four useful Assignments are better than four runs built around checkboxes.

<h2 id="cumulative-achievements">
  I let the cumulative achievements build instead of farming them early
</h2>

Several achievements are long counters that ordinary progression already feeds:

- 500 firearm kills;
- 500 melee kills;
- 100 explosive or fire kills;
- 1,337 total zombie kills;
- 1,000 item pickups;
- 25 padlocks.

I do not deliberately grind these during my first map clears. There are already
enough zombies, containers, locked routes, and equipment pickups in normal play
to move them naturally.

I only correct obvious imbalance.

If nearly everything I have killed has been with firearms, I use melee more
often when a fight is controlled. If several zombies are already standing near
an explosive barrel, that is useful explosive progress without wasting another
magazine.

If a padlock is directly on the route, I deal with it rather than ignoring it
because I am rushing toward the next objective.

The goal is background progress, not turning a productive mission into a stat
farm.

That gives me a reason not to rotate characters after every extraction.

<h2 id="all-star-cast">
  All-Star Cast means three level-50 Responders alive at the same time
</h2>

**All-Star Cast** is easy to misread.

It does not mean:

> reach level 50 three times.

It means:

> **simultaneously have three Responders at level 50 or above.**

That makes preserving finished characters part of the achievement.

I have played with a roster where two Responders were already in their 50s
while the third was still behind. At that point I stop casually risking the two
finished characters and concentrate on developing the third.

My progression order is:

**Responder 1 → level 50**

→ protect it

**Responder 2 → level 50**

→ protect both

**Responder 3 → level 50**

→ All-Star Cast

If I lose one of the finished characters while leveling the third, I have
created another progression grind for myself.

<h2 id="coop-achievements">
  Sharing, revives, and healing are easiest when I use them as real support
</h2>

Three achievements reward ordinary multiplayer support:

**Sharing is Caring**

→ share items 50 times

**Never Gonna Let You Down**

→ help fallen allies up 10 times

**Heroes Never Die**

→ heal other players 20 times

I do not manufacture fake situations for these.

If a teammate needs ammunition that I am not using, I share it. If somebody is
injured and I have spare medical supplies, I heal them. If another Responder
goes down and the revive is safe, I take the opportunity.

The only judgment that matters is whether the action helps the run.

I do not enter a zombie pile to force revive progress when the likely result is
two dead Responders instead of one.

<h2 id="nice-shootin-tex">
  Nice Shootin', Tex: count six Model 13 headshots before touching anything else
</h2>

The requirement is very specific:

**6 zombie headshot kills**

with the **Model 13**

without:

**reloading**

or:

**switching weapons**.

I do not leave this one to muscle memory.

Once the attempt starts, I count:

**1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 5 → 6**

I avoid the automatic habit of reloading after two or three shots, and I do not
switch to melee just because one zombie gets close.

The cleanest attempt is a controlled area with normal zombies and enough space
to line up deliberate headshots.

If the room turns into a panic fight, I abandon the achievement attempt before
I sacrifice a developed Responder for it.

Another Model 13 will appear.

A dead character is a more expensive reset.

<h2 id="no-gun-vs-pacifist">
  Pacifist + Pulling a Trigger: I target both in the same Objective run
</h2>

These achievements are related, but their written conditions are not identical.

**Pulling a Trigger is Like Ordering Takeout** asks me to:

> **Get extracted without firing a gun.**

On its own, that does not forbid melee or kills.

**Pacifist** is stricter:

> **Complete any Objective map without using a weapon or getting a kill.**

Because Pacifist already forbids the weapon use that would fail the no-gun
achievement, I target both at once by following the **stricter Pacifist rule
for the entire run**:

> **No shooting. No melee swings. No explosives. No weapon use. No kills.**

That removes the ambiguity.

### I keep a weapon out of my hands when I can

The easiest failure is not a planned fight. It is a reflex.

A zombie appears close, I panic, click, and the entire attempt is over.

I therefore avoid walking around with a firearm or melee weapon ready to use
when the objective does not require it. A healing or support item is much safer
to keep available because my normal attack reflex is less likely to ruin the
run.

### I still help with objectives

Pacifist does not mean I stop participating.

I move with the team, use non-combat objective interactions, carry what the
mission safely allows me to carry, and stay close enough that I do not create a
second rescue problem for everyone else.

What I do **not** try to do is solve a zombie blockage myself.

This run is much easier with teammates who know what I am attempting. They can
clear the route while I stay with the objective group and avoid becoming
isolated.

### Positioning becomes the real combat system

Without weapons, a bad room entry becomes much more dangerous.

Before moving through a crowded interior or narrow corridor, I look at where I
can leave if several zombies turn toward me. I would rather wait a few seconds
for armed teammates to push through than sprint first into a room with no clean
exit.

Runners are especially dangerous because I cannot simply turn around and kill
one that stays attached to me.

When a runner locks onto me, I move toward teammates who can deal with it
instead of dragging it deeper into another objective area.

### Teammates can create the opening

Radios, pipe bombs, and other distractions can create a few seconds of space.

On a Pacifist attempt, I let teammates handle those tools rather than testing
whether my own use of a damaging or weapon-like item might invalidate the
stricter achievement.

Once the zombies turn away from the route, I use the opening and move.

### Climbable objects are breathing room, not a permanent safe spot

Cars, vans, tables, and other climbable scenery can sometimes buy enough time
to read the room or let teammates catch up.

I use those spots as temporary spacing.

I do **not** build the attempt around one billboard, vehicle, or ledge being
permanently unreachable. A location that behaved safely in another build is
not something I want an entire achievement attempt to depend on.

### Pacifist also gives me room to play support

Because I am not building a normal combat inventory, I can carry healing and
other useful supplies for the squad.

That naturally creates opportunities to advance **Heroes Never Die** and
**Sharing is Caring** while the other players are keeping the route open for
me.

I do not force either counter, but the roles fit together well:

**teammates handle combat**

→ **I stay involved with objectives**

→ **I heal or share supplies when useful**

→ **we keep moving**

### Extraction is the finish line

The achievement is not solved because I survived most of the map without
attacking.

I still need to complete the Objective mission and extract.

My complete attempt is therefore:

**do not use a weapon**

→ **do not get a kill**

→ **stay with the objective team**

→ **use positioning instead of fighting**

→ **let teammates clear dangerous blockers**

→ **support the squad when I safely can**

→ **extract alive**

If I only need **Pulling a Trigger is Like Ordering Takeout**, its wording is
looser and I do not technically need to follow Pacifist's full restriction.

But if both are still missing, I see no reason to schedule two separate runs.

I use the Pacifist rules and target both together.

<h2 id="cowboy-action-shooter">
  Cowboy Action Shooter needs 28 weapon-specific kills in one match
</h2>

The requirement is:

- **10 Model 13 kills**
- **10 1873 Lever Action kills**
- **8 DB12 kills**

in the **same match**.

That is **28 specified kills**.

Getting 28 zombie kills is not the difficult part. The real problem is finding
all three required weapons and keeping an accurate count before extraction.

I do not start mentally committing to the achievement until the run has a
realistic weapon path.

Once the set comes together, I finish one quota before moving to the next:

**Model 13 — 10**

→ complete

**1873 Lever Action — 10**

→ complete

**DB12 — 8**

→ complete

I do not rely on:

> I probably got about ten.

If I genuinely lose count, I add another confirmed kill before switching.

Random weapon availability already creates enough uncertainty. My count does
not need to create more.

<h2 id="general-contractor">
  General Contractor is six melee kills, not six weapons carried at once
</h2>

General Contractor needs at least one kill with each of these during one match:

- Mallet;
- Small Wrench;
- Large Wrench;
- Hammer;
- Crowbar;
- Sledge.

The inventory trap is assuming I need to keep every completed weapon.

I do not.

My loop is:

**find required tool**

→ **get one confirmed kill**

→ **mark that tool complete**

→ **drop it if inventory space becomes important**

→ **look for the next one**

The **Small Wrench** and **Large Wrench** are separate requirements.

The **Hammer** and **Sledge** are also separate.

I do not substitute something that merely looks similar.

If I am already at five of six late in a stable run, I become much more willing
to search nearby side rooms for the final tool.

If I am at zero of six and the mission is already falling apart, I do not turn
the entire map into a desperate melee scavenger hunt.

<h2 id="waking-nightmare">
  I start Waking Nightmare only after I already know how to survive the maps
</h2>

**Waking Nightmare** requires **10 successful Nightmare matches**.

Failed attempts do not solve that requirement.

When I checked Nightmare, the game recommended a **level 40+ Responder**.

The listed pressure included:

- reduced UI;
- zombies dealing more damage;
- more Prime zombies;
- more armor and shields;
- found firearms carrying less ammunition;
- less ammunition to find.

That makes Nightmare the wrong place for me to learn basic route geometry.

If I am still asking:

> Which staircase leads to the next objective?

I stay on Normal or Hard.

When I already understand the major progression gates, long holdouts, resource
drains, disengagement routes, and final extraction, Nightmare becomes an
achievement run rather than a map-learning run.

My progression is:

**learn the map**

→ **build a reliable Responder**

→ **finish harder runs consistently**

→ **start counting the 10 Nightmare completions**

I do not enter Nightmare simply because the achievement exists.

<h2 id="account-and-credit-achievements">
  Account levels and lifetime Credits are cleanup goals, not early farms
</h2>

The long account requirements are:

**Getting The Hang of This**

→ Account level 50

**Apocalypse Hobbyist**

→ Account level 100

**Easy Come ...**

→ earn 100,000 lifetime Credits

**... Easy Go**

→ spend 100,000 lifetime Credits

I treat all four as background progression.

The Credit achievements are lifetime totals, so I do not need to hold 100,000
Credits at once. A useful loadout purchase does not sabotage Easy Come, and the
money I legitimately spend is already moving Easy Go.

The same principle applies to Account XP.

Map clears, Assignments, Survival runs, combat, and achievement work are already
building the account.

I do not create a separate Account XP farm unless those level milestones are
literally among the last unfinished entries.

<h2 id="final-cleanup">
  What I check when only a few achievements remain
</h2>

Near the end, I stop replaying random maps and diagnose the missing condition.

### Missing a map achievement

I check whether the clear was actually on **Normal or higher**.

A Beginner completion does not satisfy the nine map achievements.

### Missing Closing Tickets

I check whether I have actually **completed four Assignments**, not simply made
progress on four.

### Missing Veteran

The 10 successful matches need to be survived by the **same Responder**.

### Missing All-Star Cast

I look at the current roster.

The requirement is three level-50-plus Responders **alive at the same time**,
not three historical characters that once reached level 50.

### Missing Nice Shootin', Tex

I check my own reload and weapon-switch habits before assuming the headshots
failed to register.

The six headshot kills need to happen without reloading or switching away from
the Model 13.

### Missing Pulling a Trigger is Like Ordering Takeout

If I am doing it by itself, the important condition is **extract without firing
a gun**.

If Pacifist is also missing, I stop planning two runs and use the stricter
Pacifist route for both.

### Missing Pacifist

I check for the two real failure points:

**Did I use a weapon?**

**Did I get a kill?**

I do not apply the looser no-gun rules here.

### Missing Cowboy Action Shooter

I recheck all three quotas:

**10 Model 13**

**10 1873 Lever Action**

**8 DB12**

It is easy to finish an otherwise perfect match with one weapon sitting at
nine.

### Missing General Contractor

I check the exact six tool names.

Small Wrench and Large Wrench are separate, just as Hammer and Sledge are
separate.

### Missing Waking Nightmare

Only **successful Nightmare completions** advance the requirement.

Attempts are not progress.

### Missing We Got This, Man!

I do not farm it directly.

It is the final completion achievement for clearing the rest of the base-game
challenge list.

At that point I filter the checklist above to incomplete entries and solve the
specific remaining condition instead of starting another random match.
