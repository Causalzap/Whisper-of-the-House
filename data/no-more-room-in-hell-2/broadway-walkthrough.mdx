<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
The most important thing I learned on **Broadway** is that I cannot memorize one
fixed list of locations and expect the next run to follow it.

One full route I cleared went:

**restore communications**

→ **retrieve the escape plans**

→ **follow the sewer route**

→ **search stores for 3 hard drives**

→ **upload all 3 CRC files**

→ **secure the Grocery Store**

→ **complete the Courthouse C4 branch**

→ **stoke the bonfire**

→ **signal the extraction helicopter**

→ **reach the rooftop Fast Rope**

But Broadway can change its middle route.

I have also had a run use the **subway instead of the Courthouse C4 branch**,
and extraction does not always finish in the same place.

The rule that saves me the most time is:

**finish what the HUD currently asks for before trying to activate the next
landmark.**

If a Grocery Store wire, window, door, or Courthouse interaction is inactive, I
check the unfinished objective first instead of immediately treating the
interaction as broken.

<h2 id="broadway-route">The Broadway route I cleared</h2>

Broadway is not one permanent sequence, but having one complete route makes the
map much easier to understand.

My confirmed run moved through these major phases:

1. Restore communications.
2. Retrieve the latest escape plans.
3. Enter the sewer route.
4. Reach the CRC Safe Zone.
5. Search stores for **3 hard drives**.
6. Upload all **3 CRC files**.
7. Leave the Safe Zone and follow the next route.
8. Secure the Grocery Store.
9. Complete its door and window objectives.
10. Follow the Courthouse branch.
11. Place the required C4.
12. Locate wood and stoke the bonfire.
13. Signal the extraction helicopter.
14. Reach the final extraction.

The important part is not memorizing those fourteen steps as universal.

I use them to recognize what phase of Broadway I am in.

Across different runs I have encountered Broadway objectives around:

- the Fire Station;
- Grocery Store;
- Courthouse;
- Movie Theater;
- Gymnasium;
- Train Yard;
- subway and sewer routes.

A run does not necessarily use all of them.

If the HUD sends me underground, I go underground.

If it sends me to the Courthouse, I stop searching for the subway route I used
last time.

<h2 id="escape-plans">
  Finish the escape plans before predicting the next landmark
</h2>

After communications came back online, I was told to retrieve the latest
**escape plans** from the army recon team.

That objective is an important Broadway transition.

I do not run ahead to the Grocery Store, Courthouse, Train Yard, or extraction
while it is still active.

The escape plans tell me what the mission has generated next.

In this run, finishing that phase sent me toward Broadway through the sewer
network and a CRC Safe Zone.

That is when I commit to the underground route.

<h2 id="sewer-route">
  Follow the active sewer route instead of every open tunnel
</h2>

The sewer section produced one of my biggest Broadway detours.

I saw an explorable lower route, kept moving deeper into it, and eventually
realized the active objective was not continuing in that direction.

We had to go back the way we came.

**Is the active objective still getting closer?**

and:

**Did this door or tunnel actually advance the mission state?**

If the answer to both starts becoming no, I turn back.

Broadway has enough side space that exploring the wrong sewer branch can waste
several minutes without giving me any mission progress.

This is a phase where I follow the objective wording literally.

I search the nearby stores rather than wandering into the next major landmark.

Once somebody finds a drive, I keep track of the shared objective count instead

The target is **three total**.

When the third hard drive is secured, the mission is ready to move into the
upload step.

<h2 id="upload-crc-files">
  Upload the 3 CRC files before trying to leave the Safe Zone
</h2>

Finding the hard drives is not the end of this section.

The next state I saw at the Diner was:

**Upload CRC Files (3/3)**

That is my confirmation that the collection phase succeeded.

I finish the upload before trying to force the next Broadway route.

This matters because Broadway repeatedly locks later interactions behind the
current local state.

A door that looks usable or a destination that looks familiar does not mean the
mission is ready for it.

Once the CRC file upload finishes and the objective changes, I leave the Safe
Zone and follow the newly generated route.

<h2 id="grocery-store">
  The Grocery Store branch has ordered security, door, and window steps
</h2>

My next branch eventually sent me toward the **Grocery Store**.

This is one of the places where Broadway can feel broken when it is actually
waiting for an earlier objective.

I reached interactions that looked usable:

- security controls;
- wiring;
- doors;
- windows.

Some would not activate when I first approached them.

The important discovery was that the local objectives were ordered.

After the earlier security step completed, the later interactions became
available.

During this stage my HUD showed:

**Seal 1st Floor Doors (1/4)**

**Seal 1st Floor Windows (2/4)**

That gives me a much better troubleshooting check than testing random
interactions.

If I am at:

`1/4 doors`

I know three door interactions still need to be completed.

If I am at:

`2/4 windows`

I know two window interactions still remain.

If the next door or window refuses to work, I check whether another local
security or wiring objective is still active.

I do not immediately leave the building or reload the run.

<h2 id="grocery-store-priority">

The Grocery Store can contain useful supplies, but the mission state is more
important than clearing every shelf.

Once I have enough resources to keep moving, I prioritize the active:

**security**

→ **doors**

→ **windows**

Broadway still has major objectives ahead.

If the squad is struggling with ammunition, stamina, or healing, I fix the

The branch is tracking **four C4 placements**.

One C4 search slowed us down because the team had spread across different
floors.

A useful callout from that run was:

**down the main stairs**

→ **straight ahead**

→ **right at the end of the hallway**

→ **right again**

That led us toward one of the explosives we needed.

I treat that as a search clue, not a permanent spawn guarantee.

Broadway can rearrange enough objective details that I do not want to promise
the same C4 will always be in exactly that room.

The hard number is more useful:

> **I need four successful placements before this branch is finished.**

Once the C4 objective completes, I leave the Courthouse.

<h2 id="subway-variant">
  If the HUD sends me to the subway, I skip the Courthouse route
</h2>

The Courthouse is **not** a universal Broadway requirement.

Another Broadway run did not use the same C4 sequence.

Instead, the mission sent us through the **subway tunnels**.

That path eventually brought us back onto Broadway closer to the later part of
the run.

This is the route variation that changed how I use the whole walkthrough.

I no longer think:

> Broadway means Grocery Store, then Courthouse.

I think:

> **Broadway has generated this branch for this run. Finish it and wait for the
> next objective.**

If the current objective points underground, I do not cross the map looking for
C4.

If it says Courthouse, I do not search for the subway entrance from my previous
clear.

The HUD wins the argument.

<h2 id="timed-wood-objective">
  Locate the wood before committing to the bonfire timer
</h2>

Late in my Courthouse route, Broadway moved into a timed objective involving
wood and the bonfire.

This was the phase where preparation saved the run.

Before fully committing to the timer, we looked through the nearby buildings
and noticed where several wood pieces were sitting.

Once the objective started, that meant we were moving known pieces instead of
searching every room under pressure.

We finished with only about **6 seconds** remaining.

That is close enough that I now change what I do before the timer.

I identify:

- nearby rooms where I already saw wood;
- the shortest path back to the bonfire;
- which responders are already close to a piece;
- whether somebody can keep the route open while the wood is moving.

I do not spend the timed phase searching a room that I could have checked
before starting it.

The general carrier-and-cover combat setup is useful here, but the
Broadway-specific advantage is knowing the wood locations **before the clock is
already draining**.

<h2 id="signal-extraction">
  Stoke the bonfire, then signal the extraction helicopter
</h2>

Once enough wood reached the fire, the HUD changed again.

I saw:

**Stoke the Bonfire**

complete,

followed by:

**Signal Extraction Helicopter**

That is my signal to stop searching for more wood.

The bonfire phase is over.

I activate the next extraction step and move with the new marker.

In this run the extraction HUD then showed an arrival countdown of about:

**1m27s**

That is the point where I stop thinking about the Broadway objective chain and
start thinking only about reaching the generated exit.

The remaining Broadway problem is getting to the correct exit before the run

<h2 id="broadway-stuck">
  If I am stuck on Broadway, I check the current mission state first
</h2>

There are a few Broadway failure states I can diagnose quickly now.

### I am deep in the sewers and the route no longer makes sense

I turn back toward the last confirmed objective path.

An open tunnel is not enough evidence that I am progressing.

### The Grocery Store wire, door, or window will not activate

I check the HUD for an earlier security or local objective.

The interactions in my run became available in sequence.

### My Grocery Store HUD still says 1/4 doors or 2/4 windows

I stay in the Grocery Store.

Those counters tell me the local objective is not finished yet.

### I reached the Courthouse but cannot finish the C4 objective

I check the objective counter.

My branch required **4 placements**.

I do not leave after finding or placing only one charge.

### I remember the Courthouse but the HUD points underground

I follow the subway branch.

Courthouse C4 is not required in every Broadway run.

### The bonfire timer keeps beating me

I scout the nearby wood before committing to the timed phase.

The difference mattered enough that our successful run still finished with
only about **6 seconds** left.

### I reached the extraction phase but the rooftop I remember is not active

I stop using the previous run as the map.

Broadway can produce different extraction routes, so I follow the current
marker instead.
