**Acid rain** is the first farm problem in Doloc Town that can punish a good
planting decision after the seeds are already in the ground. The important part is
not reacting to every green cloud the same way. I separate crops into two groups:
**End Crops that can survive the extreme weather**, and vulnerable crops that need
temporary protection or an indoor plan.

My rule is simple:

**check the forecast before I commit expensive seeds → leave End Crops outside →
cover vulnerable crops with Plastic Mulch when bad weather is close → use the
two-day protection window instead of treating mulch as permanent → move valuable
long-term crops indoors when severe weather becomes frequent.**

    <li>Build a Weather Station once I begin planting enough vulnerable crops that a bad forecast can erase a large investment.</li>
    <li>Use indoor growing as the long-term answer for valuable crops during the harsher weather periods.</li>

<h2 id="acid-rain-starts">
  Protect the crop first when the rain turns dangerous
</h2>

<AdUnit placement="article_after_first_h2" />

The first time acid rain catches me with an outdoor field, I do not finish my normal
watering, crafting, or town route first. I deal with the exposed crops immediately.

Acid rain is not just cosmetic weather. Unprotected vulnerable crops can stop
growing and eventually die, and the player can also take damage while standing out
in it.

I use this priority order when the weather has already turned:

1. protect any valuable non-End Crops that are still exposed;
2. avoid starting new outdoor planting;
3. check whether my electrical farm equipment is still operating;
4. finish only the outdoor jobs that cannot wait;
5. then move back under cover or continue with indoor tasks.

I do **not** throw protection on every plant automatically. Doloc Town gives some
crops natural resistance, and wasting mulch on those removes the reserve I need for
the crops that can actually die.

<h2 id="end-crops">
  Know which crops can stay outside during acid rain
</h2>

The game calls the naturally resistant group **End Crops**. Two early examples are
**Endam** and **Succulent**.

Those are the crops I am comfortable leaving outside when acid rain arrives. The
point of the End Crop tag is that these plants have adapted to the postwar weather,
so I do not spend emergency protection on them just because the forecast looks bad.

That changes how I spend money on seeds.

If I am early in the game, have limited cash, and know severe weather is close, I
prefer a crop I can actually keep alive over a more interesting seed that will need
extra protection immediately.

I also avoid assuming that every crop sold during the current period is safe in
acid rain. **Season compatibility and extreme-weather resistance are separate
checks.** A crop can fit the season and still need protection from bad weather.

The exact profit ranking belongs in the crop-profit page. Here I only use the
weather rule:

> **If the crop is not an End Crop, I plan its protection before I scale the
> planting.**

<h2 id="plastic-mulch">
  Use Plastic Mulch as a two-day emergency buffer
</h2>

For exposed vulnerable crops, **Plastic Mulch** is my first emergency solution.

One application protects the crop for **two days**. That makes it useful for getting
a valuable crop through a short bad-weather window, but it is not something I treat
as permanent infrastructure.

At the **Manual Workbench**, I can make the protection in two useful ways:

- **1 Waste Plastic Bottle → 2 Plastic Mulch**;
- **1 Plastic → 10 Plastic Mulch**.

The first route is excellent early because plastic bottles are common scavenging
material. I do not sell every bottle for a tiny amount of cash when I still have
unprotected crops outside.

The Plastic route is better once I can process materials in larger quantities. One
Plastic giving me ten units is the point where keeping an emergency weather reserve
becomes much easier.

### When I use mulch and when I do not

I use it immediately when:

- a vulnerable crop is already planted outside;
- acid rain is on the near forecast;
- the crop is close enough to harvest that two protected days can save the cycle;
- or the seed was expensive enough that losing it costs more than the protection.

I am more willing to **wait or move the crop indoors** when:

- I have not planted the seed yet;
- the crop still needs a long growing window;
- I am entering a harsh weather period with repeated bad days;
- or covering a very large field would consume my entire reserve.

That is the biggest difference between emergency protection and a real farm plan.
Mulch rescues a planting decision. It should not force me to maintain a bad layout
forever.

<h2 id="weather-station">
  Build a Weather Station before a bad forecast becomes expensive
</h2>

I do not consider the Weather Station urgent when my farm is still a handful of
cheap End Crops. It becomes valuable when I start putting real money, rare seeds,
or long grow times into vulnerable outdoor plants.

The current Weather Station recipe is:

- **50 Wood**;
- **3 Copper Ingots**;
- crafted at the **Equipment Workbench**.

That cost is meaningful early, so I build it when the forecast can actually change
my planting decision rather than because the tech tree says I can.

Weather pressure also changes across the four weather periods.

During **Light Rain** and **Early Dry**, the bad-weather rate is relatively mild:
the guidance I use is roughly **one bad-weather day every six days**.

During **Heavy Rain** and **Harsh Dry**, the risk rises sharply; close to **half of
the days** can be bad weather. That is when checking the forecast stops being a
nice optimization and becomes part of my planting routine.

My planting check is therefore:

**forecast → crop resistance → remaining grow time → protection available → plant.**

I do not reverse that order by buying seeds first and only checking the weather
after every basin is full.

<h2 id="move-indoors">
  Move valuable vulnerable crops indoors when temporary protection stops making sense
</h2>

Plastic Mulch is deliberately temporary. Once I am repeatedly covering the same
kind of crop, I treat that as a signal that the farm layout should change.

The first long-term advice I follow is to move vulnerable crops **indoors**, with
the tent providing the early protected option before larger indoor farming spaces
become practical.

I make that switch sooner for:

- expensive seed batches;
- long-growing crops;
- crops I need for a quest or processing chain;
- and periods where several severe-weather days can land close together.

I make the switch later for cheap short-cycle crops that can finish comfortably
inside a safe forecast window.

This page does not turn into a full indoor-farm or automation guide. The important
decision here is simply **when temporary outdoor protection has stopped being the
efficient answer**.

<h2 id="electric-equipment">
  Do not assume powered farm equipment will ignore extreme weather
</h2>

Extreme weather can also stop **electrical equipment** that is left exposed.

That matters once I have started depending on powered equipment to keep a larger
farm running. A field that looks automated on a clear day can still become a
problem if I leave vulnerable crops outside and assume the electrical setup will
carry them through every storm.

I use two checks before leaving the farm for a long exploration day:

1. are the valuable crops protected from the forecast itself?
2. if they depend on electrical equipment, is that equipment still going to be
   reliable in the coming weather?

I do not expand the generator, battery, sprinkler, or drone systems here. Those
belong to the farming-automation Owner Page. The weather lesson is only that
**automation does not remove the need to plan around extreme conditions**.

<h2 id="building-damage">
  Treat building damage as a separate problem from saving the crop
</h2>

Acid rain can damage buildings as well as crops.

When both happen at once, I save the vulnerable plants first. A damaged structure
can be repaired; a dead crop may erase the seed cost and several days of growth.

I also do not turn this page into a building-repair guide. If the tent or another
structure loses durability, I handle that after the crop emergency instead of
mixing building maintenance into the acid-rain planting decision.

<h2 id="decision-rules">
  The planting decisions I use before the next weather change
</h2>

### I plant outside now when

- the crop is an End Crop;
- or the forecast is safe for the required growth window and I have protection in
  reserve.

### I keep the seed for later when

- I am entering Heavy Rain or Harsh Dry;
- the crop is vulnerable;
- I do not have enough protection for the field;
- and there is no reason the seed must be planted today.

A seed in storage is not a failed crop. I would rather lose one day of theoretical
growth than lose the entire batch to weather I already knew was coming.

### I use Plastic Mulch instead of moving the crop when

- the crop is already established;
- the danger window is short;
- and the two-day protection is enough to carry it through or reach harvest.

### I move production indoors when

- I keep protecting the same vulnerable crop repeatedly;
- I want to scale that crop;
- or severe-weather frequency makes outdoor planting too dependent on consumables.

### I turn back from exploration when

- a dangerous forecast is imminent;
- high-value crops are still exposed;
- and I have not applied the protection yet.

That is one of the few times I let farm maintenance interrupt a main-story trip.
There is no advantage in reaching a new area a little earlier if the cost is losing
an entire valuable crop cycle at home.

<h2 id="next">
  What I do after the farm is weather-safe
</h2>

Once the exposed crops are protected and the forecast is under control, I go back
to progression instead of spending the whole day reorganizing the farm.
