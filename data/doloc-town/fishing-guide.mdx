Fishing in Doloc Town stopped feeling random to me once I separated three different
problems that look similar at first:

**am I using the right rod → am I handling the fishing meter correctly → am I
actually fishing under the condition the target needs?**

The Old Fishing Rod is enough to learn the system, but it is not the rod I want to
force every difficult catch with. Some targets are tied to weather, water type, or a
short event window, and repeated Sardines or junk do not automatically mean I am in
the wrong place.

My practical route is:

**meet Light Man at the Fishing Fort → take the free Old Fishing Rod → learn cast
distance and the hold/release meter → ignore yellow bonus windows until I can land
the fish reliably → upgrade the rod only when the current target is fighting the
weak setup → use weather and location clues for special fish → treat Salmon Fest as
a scheduled fishing window → send breeding formulas, achievement cleanup, and fish
profit decisions to their own Owner Pages.**

<h2 id="get-fishing-rod">
  Get the Old Fishing Rod from Light Man before I spend Gold on fishing
</h2>

<AdUnit placement="article_after_first_h2" />

I start fishing at the **Fishing Fort near the dock**.

Light Man is working on a new rod made from tough Rockwood, but he gives me his
**Old Fishing Rod** as the beginner handoff. That is important because the item data
puts the Old Fishing Rod at **rod level 1**.

There is also a weaker **Simple Fishing Rod** at **level 0** with a listed buy price
of **350G**.

So I do not pay 350G for the level-0 rod when the story can hand me a level-1 rod for
free.

If ordinary catches are already turning into long meter fights and the next target
is clearly harder, I start looking at the Bamboo tier.

<h2 id="fishing-controls">
  Cast for the water I want, then control the meter with hold and release
</h2>

The fishing tutorial gives me the whole catch loop on one screen.

First, I **charge the cast to control distance**. I do not automatically max the
cast; I only throw as far as the water I actually want to reach.

When the fish bites, I **lift the rod**.

After that, I follow the color on the Fishing Meter and use **hold / release**
instead of rapid clicking. I make small corrections rather than holding until the
meter swings too far in the opposite direction.

### The yellow sections are extra rewards, not the main catch

The narrow yellow sections are for **extra rewards**.

I stopped chasing them on every catch once I realized they can make an already
awkward fight harder with the Old Fishing Rod. If reaching the yellow section makes
me lose control of the fish, I ignore it and finish the catch normally.

Once my hold-and-release timing is reliable, I take the bonus windows when they
appear naturally. I do not turn a successful catch into a failed one just to force
an extra reward.

<h2 id="rod-upgrades">
  Upgrade the rod when the target justifies it, not every time a new tier appears
</h2>

The current item data gives me five rod tiers.

| Rod | Level | Listed buy price | How I use it |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| **Simple Fishing Rod** | 0 | 350G | I skip it if I can take Light Man's free Old Fishing Rod. |
| **Old Fishing Rod** | 1 | 100G | My free learning rod and early common-fish tool. |
| **Bamboo Fishing Rod** | 2 | 1,000G | My first serious upgrade when larger fish or repeated failures become the blocker. |
| **Fiberglass Fishing Rod** | 3 | 3,000G | I buy it for sustained mid/late fishing, not for one easy quest catch. |
| **Carbon Fiber Fishing Rod** | 4 | 6,000G | I save it for large or difficult targets when fishing is now an actual priority. |

The Bamboo rod's description specifically says it can handle **larger fish** than
ordinary wooden rods. Fiberglass is positioned as an advanced angler tool, while
Carbon Fiber is explicitly aimed at big fish.

That is enough for me to use rod tier as a progression decision without inventing
exact catch thresholds that my current material does not verify.

### When I buy Bamboo

I buy the 1,000G Bamboo Fishing Rod when two things are true:

1. I have a specific fish, quest, festival, or fishing objective I am trying to
   complete;
2. the Old Fishing Rod is repeatedly turning that objective into slow or failed
   catches.

I do **not** buy it just because the fishing shop opened.

Early Gold still competes with bag space and required tool progression. If I am
saving for a story-critical purchase, I keep fishing as a side activity instead.

The wider spend-versus-save decision belongs to the <Link href="/doloc-town/money-guide" className="font-semibold text-sky-700 underline decoration-sky-300 underline-offset-4 hover:text-sky-900">Doloc Town Money Guide</Link>.

### When I wait on Fiberglass and Carbon Fiber

A 3,000G or 6,000G fishing rod is no longer a casual experiment.

I buy those tiers when I am deliberately targeting harder fish, working through
Kon-Tiki fish requests, or spending enough time fishing that the better rod will
keep paying me back in easier catches.

If I only need one Sardine, a little Kelp, or a basic quest fish, I keep the money.

<h2 id="fishing-rng">
  Repeated Sardines, Kelp, or junk do not automatically mean I chose the wrong spot
</h2>

One of the first real fishing frustrations I hit is trying to pull a specific item
from water while the results keep changing.

During an early Kelp request, the same fishing stretch produced repeated
**Sardines**, a **Plastic Bottle**, and other results while I was still waiting for
the Kelp I actually wanted.

That is the point where I stop reading every failed pull as location information.

My response depends on why I am fishing.

### If I need a quest item today

I give the spot several attempts.

If the fishing shop is offering the exact common resource I need and buying it is
cheap enough for my current budget, I may simply purchase it rather than burn the
entire in-game day on RNG.

### If I am fishing for money

I stop once the session becomes slower than my other income routes.

Fishing can produce saleable items, but it is not automatically the best early Gold
method when the Old Fishing Rod turns every harder catch into a long fight. Selling
and processing value belongs to the <Link href="/doloc-town/money-guide" className="font-semibold text-sky-700 underline decoration-sky-300 underline-offset-4 hover:text-sky-900">Money Guide</Link>.

### If I am fishing for breeding parents

I keep the target adults instead of automatically selling them.

<h2 id="condition-fish">
  Check weather and water type before I grind a condition-based fish
</h2>

Some fish are not just "keep casting until RNG works."

The strongest clues in my current data tie specific targets to weather, season, or
water type.

I use these as fishing-day triggers:

- **Grass Carp** — a town request explicitly says it appears on **rainy days**.
- **Golden Fish** — a request points me toward a **pond deep in the woodland**.
- **Fluorescent Eel** — a request points to the **sea during intensely sunny/hot weather**.
- **Sturgeon** — the Kon-Tiki research note says it appears in **mild weather** and
  in **high-quality freshwater**.
- **Dumbo Octopus** — the same research route points to **seawater during the Heavy
  Rain period**.
- **Mahi-mahi** — the same route points to **seawater during the Harsh Dry period**.
- **Eel** — an Early Dry board request is a good reminder for me to target Eel in
  that period, but I do not claim from that clue alone that Early Dry is its only
  possible condition.

This changes how I troubleshoot.

If I am trying for Grass Carp on a clear day, I do not solve that by buying a
6,000G rod and casting another fifty times.

I wait for rain.

If I am trying for a special seawater target under the wrong climate, I change the
day or the target before I change equipment.

### I use the calendar as part of the fishing kit

The same calendar logic that matters for crops also matters here.

When a rare fish has a weather or seasonal clue, I decide **before leaving the farm**
whether today is a fishing day.

That prevents the worst kind of wasted trip: reaching the correct water with the
correct rod on the wrong climate day.

<h2 id="salmon-fest">
  Salmon Fest is a three-day fishing window, not a normal random fishing session
</h2>

Salmon is the clearest scheduled target.

Light Man explains that from the **25th through the 27th**, Salmon return for the
annual Salmon Fest.

During that window, they appear widely **except in shallow pools**.

I prepare before the 25th:

- clear enough bag space for repeated catches;
- bring the strongest rod I can justify;
- avoid spending the whole day on unrelated errands;
- and decide in advance whether the Salmon are for a festival objective, a quest,
  breeding parents, or sale.

If breeding is the goal, I do not sell the whole event catch and then try to rebuild

<h2 id="special-fish-priority">
  I target rare fish only when the condition and rod are both ready
</h2>

The late fishing mistake I avoid is chasing prestige fish in the wrong order.

Kon-Tiki requests eventually ask for unusual fish such as Sturgeon, Dumbo Octopus,
and Mahi-mahi. Those targets are useful because the request text itself tells me
that **weather and water quality/type matter**.

I therefore use a two-check rule.

### Check 1: is the condition correct?

If the target wants Heavy Rain seawater, I wait for that combination.

If it wants mild-weather freshwater, I choose a freshwater route on a mild day.

### Check 2: is my rod the current bottleneck?

If the condition is right but the actual meter fight is consistently beyond the
Old or Bamboo rod, then I upgrade.

I do not reverse that order.

Buying the most expensive rod cannot make the wrong weather correct.

That single rule saves more time than blindly fishing every available water source
with the strongest rod I can afford.

<h2 id="keep-or-sell">
  Decide why I caught the fish before I sell it
</h2>

I use a simple priority whenever I land something I do not usually catch:

1. **active quest requirement**;
2. **breeding parent I still need**;
3. **festival or faction request**;
4. **research or collection use**;
5. **processing or sale**.

That order keeps me from selling one rare parent and then discovering that the next
system wants exactly that fish.

It also keeps this page from becoming another economy database.

The exact direct-sale and processing decision belongs to the Money Guide. The exact
parent ratios belong to Fish Breeding. This page stops at the point where I have
successfully caught the fish.

<h2 id="fishing-troubleshooting">
  What I check when fishing feels broken
</h2>

### The cast never reaches the water I want

I charge the cast longer.

Cast distance is a separate step before the bite, so I fix the distance first
instead of blaming the fishing meter.

### The fish bites but I lose it immediately

I make sure I am actually lifting the rod when the bite happens, then switch to
controlled hold/release inputs.

I do not mash while also trying to hit every yellow bonus section.

### The yellow sections make the minigame much harder

I ignore them.

They are extra-reward opportunities, not a requirement for landing the fish.

### Even Sardines feel harder than they should

I stop assuming every failure is player timing.

The Old Fishing Rod is only level 1. If normal catches are already unpleasant and I
am about to target bigger fish, that is a reasonable point to consider the
**1,000G Bamboo Fishing Rod** rather than brute-force the weak setup.

### I keep getting Sardines or junk instead of the quest item

I give the spot more than one attempt because the fishing result can vary.

If the exact common resource is available from the fishing shop and I only need it
to unblock a quest, I compare the purchase against wasting the rest of the day.

### Grass Carp will not appear

I check the weather. The current quest data points to **rainy days**.

### Salmon will not appear during Salmon Fest

I check two things:

- is the date between the **25th and 27th**?
- am I avoiding **shallow pools**?

I change those before I start buying new gear.

### I have the right fish but cannot make the variant

That is no longer a catching problem.

I move to the <Link href="/doloc-town/fish-breeding" className="font-semibold text-sky-700 underline decoration-sky-300 underline-offset-4 hover:text-sky-900">Fish Breeding Guide</Link> for tank composition, roe, fry, and special variants.

<h2 id="when-to-stop">
  Stop fishing when the current job is finished
</h2>

I do not make every fishing trip a completion session.

If I came for one quest fish and caught it, I leave.

If I came for Salmon Fest and have enough for the objective I planned, I decide
whether another hour of fishing is actually worth more than the rest of the day's
work.

If I am only trying to push a long achievement counter, I wait until the rod and
normal progression have made fishing easier.

The achievement snapshot puts **Fishing 101** at **20 fish** and **Fish Lord** at
**200 fish**, but those numbers belong to the completion route rather than the
basic fishing system.

For me, the useful fishing loop is:

> **correct water and weather → rod strong enough for the target → clean
> hold/release control → keep the fish for its highest-priority use → stop when the
> objective is done.**
