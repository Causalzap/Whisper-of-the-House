Doloc Town's livestock system is much easier once I unlock it in the order the
tutorial expects. I do **not** buy animals first and then figure out where to put
them. I prepare the shed, feeding setup, and a reliable source of feed before I
start expanding the herd.

My early route is:

**meet Kenanimo → talk to Gerald → bring 100 Wood, 100 Fiber, and 5 Copper Ingots
for the first Small Livestock Shed → place the shed → unlock Animal Basics → build
a Feeding Trough → move the first Stilttail Pheasant into the shed → keep it fed and
petted → save the first Egg for Kenanimo → unlock the Breeding Room → only then
decide whether more animals are worth the Gold and shed space.**

<h2 id="unlock-livestock">
  Talk to Gerald after Kenanimo starts the livestock tutorial
</h2>

<AdUnit placement="article_after_first_h2" />

Kenanimo is the point where I stop treating animals as something I should already be
able to buy. His tutorial sends me to the local builder first because the farm needs
a proper livestock building before the animal loop makes sense.

Gerald gives me the first Small Livestock Shed deal, but there is an easy wording
trap here: **the first shed being "on the house" does not mean I can build it with
zero materials.**

I still need to bring:

- **100 Wood**
- **100 Fiber**
- **5 Copper Ingots**

That is a big early pile of Wood and Fiber, so I do not empty those materials into
optional decorations just before starting the livestock quest.

If I am short on Fiber late in the day, I also do not panic and abandon the rest of
my farm plan. Animals are useful, but this is not the moment where I sell important
progression materials or delay a required tool just to finish the shed one day
earlier.

Once I have the materials, I return to Gerald. He uploads the Small Livestock Shed
design to the construction system, and I can finally place it on the farm.

<h2 id="shed-placement">
  Leave a real footprint for the shed instead of fighting the Plant Basins
</h2>

The first placement problem is simple but annoying: the shed cannot overlap the
Plant Basins I already placed.

I ran into this after getting the shed ready and trying to fit it into an area that
was already doing useful farm work. My fix is not to dismantle a productive crop
layout just because the new building has appeared.

I move the shed toward a clear edge of the usable farm and leave enough room around
it for the livestock equipment I know is coming next.

This matters beyond the initial placement. The livestock system soon adds a trough,
optional manure collection, and eventually breeding equipment. Cramming the shed
into the smallest possible gap saves space for a moment and creates a worse layout
as soon as I start caring for more than one animal.

<h2 id="feeding-trough">
  Unlock Animal Basics and put the Feeding Trough inside the shed
</h2>

After the shed is standing, I return to Kenanimo instead of buying another animal.

The next progression step is **Animal Basics** and the **Feeding Trough**.

The trough recipe is cheap compared with the shed:

- **15 Wood**
- **10 Stone**

I place it **inside the livestock shed** and fill it before I treat the animal system
as ready.

Kenanimo also gives me Alfalfa Seeds and explains the important fallback: Alfalfa is
excellent animal feed, but Fiber and other crops can work when I do not have enough
grass ready.

That is useful because Alfalfa is not an all-season emergency answer. A normal
Alfalfa Seed costs **50G**, takes about **4 days** to mature, grows during rainy
periods, and can be harvested repeatedly.

So I use this rule:

- if the current period supports Alfalfa, I start a small repeat-harvest feed patch;
- if I just unlocked livestock and have no mature Alfalfa yet, I use Fiber or spare
  crops instead of leaving the trough empty;
- if the weather is wrong for Alfalfa, I do not force the whole farm plan around one
  feed crop.

<h2 id="first-pheasant">
  Use the first Stilttail Pheasant to prove the whole care loop
</h2>

The tutorial gives me my first **Stilttail Pheasant**. I put the animal sack into the
livestock shed rather than carrying it around while I reorganize the rest of the
farm.

At that point the care loop becomes visible:

- release the animal into the shed;
- keep food available;
- pet it to improve mood;
- collect its products;
- watch the building&apos;s capacity because different animals use different amounts of
  space.

I do not repeatedly chase the same animal trying to pet it over and over. In my
early setup, petting is a once-per-day interaction, so once that is done I move on
to crops, machines, or exploration.

The more important check is the trough. I have returned to the shed and realized the
animals had already eaten through the grass while I was focused elsewhere. That is
why I refill feed **before** leaving for a long exploration route rather than after I
come home.

<h2 id="first-egg">
  Keep the first Egg instead of cashing it out
</h2>

The first animal product is also a progression checkpoint.

Kenanimo specifically asks me to bring back **1 Egg** after the first Stilttail
Pheasant grows and begins producing.

A normal Egg sells for **75G** in the current item data. Early on, 75G is noticeable
enough that selling it automatically is tempting.

I do not sell the first one.

That Egg is worth more as a tutorial handoff because returning it opens the next
livestock step. I put the first Egg in my inventory, take it back to Kenanimo, and
only start treating later Eggs as sale, cooking, crafting, or faction resources.

This is one of those small decisions where "sell everything after harvest" is the
wrong habit. The immediate 75G is not worth creating a progression question for
myself five minutes later.

<h2 id="buy-more-animals">
  Buy more Stilttail Pheasants before a Horn Alpaca when cash is tight
</h2>

Once the first animal is working, I check the livestock shop again.

The early prices make the decision very different:

- another **Stilttail Pheasant** costs **800G**;
- a **Horn Alpaca** costs **3,000G**.

I have hit this exact choice with only about **2,000G** available. At that point the
Alpaca is not merely "expensive"; it is impossible without waiting, while two more
Pheasants cost **1,600G** total and immediately strengthen the livestock loop I
already know how to feed.

That is the type of expansion I prefer early.

I buy another animal when:

- the trough is staying supplied;
- the shed still has comfortable capacity;
- I want another producer or a breeding pair;
- and the purchase will not delay a story-critical tool, seed setup, or industrial
  material.

I wait when:

- I am already forgetting to refill feed;
- the shed is close to its capacity;
- I have not finished the first Egg handoff;
- or spending 3,000G would wipe out the money I need elsewhere.

Different species occupy different amounts of shed space, so I do not use the number
of animals alone as my capacity check. I look at the shed before buying the next
one.

<h2 id="breeding-room">
  Return the first Egg before spending Copper on the Breeding Room
</h2>

Returning the Egg is the point where Kenanimo teaches animal breeding.

The rule is straightforward: put a suitable pair into the **Breeding Room**, and if
the pair is willing, they can produce offspring. The babies can then be kept or
sold.

The Breeding Room itself is not a tiny starter craft. Its current recipe is:

- **50 Wood**
- **30 Fiber**
- **10 Copper Ingots**
- **5 Burlap**

That **10-Copper-Ingot** cost is why I separate *unlocking* animal breeding from
*investing* in animal breeding.

If I have one Pheasant, low cash, and Copper still competing with tools and machines,
I leave the recipe unlocked and keep progressing.

If I already have a stable group of Pheasants, plenty of feed, and I want to grow the
herd without repeatedly paying shop prices, the Breeding Room starts to make sense.

I also keep the movement tools unlocked by this step in mind. Kenanimo teaches the
sack method for moving animals after the breeding handoff, so I do not assume an
animal must remain permanently attached to the first layout I built.

<h2 id="animal-toilet">
  Build the Animal Toilet when I actually want manure
</h2>

The **Animal Toilet** is useful, but it is not part of my minimum first-animal route.

Its current recipe is:

- **30 Wood**
- **10 Soil**

Its job is to collect manure.

I build it when I have enough animals that manure is becoming a useful farm input.
I do not delay the Feeding Trough, first Egg, or a required Copper purchase just
because the livestock menu contains another craftable object.

That gives me a simple priority:

**shelter → food → first animal → first product → breeding decision → optional
manure infrastructure.**

The order matters more than trying to build every livestock device at once.

<h2 id="daily-route">
  Keep animal care inside one short morning route
</h2>

Once the livestock system is stable, I do not let it take over the entire day.

My morning loop is:

1. enter the shed;
2. check whether the Feeding Trough still has food;
3. refill it if necessary;
4. pet the animals once;
5. collect ready products;
6. check shed capacity before adding a new animal;
7. leave and continue with crops, machines, quests, or exploration.

This is also how I decide whether the herd is becoming too large.

If I keep returning from exploration to an empty feeder, I do **not** buy another
animal. I fix the feed supply first.

If I have a pile of Alfalfa, plenty of shed space, and products are being collected
without disrupting the rest of the farm, expansion is much easier to justify.

The livestock system should add a productive loop to the farm, not create another
daily emergency.

<h2 id="stuck">
  The livestock checks I use when the tutorial seems stuck
</h2>

### Gerald is not giving me the Small Livestock Shed

I make sure I have actually started Kenanimo&apos;s livestock tutorial first. The intended
handoff is Kenanimo → Gerald, not "find a shed blueprint somewhere on the farm."

When I reach Gerald, I also remember that the first shed still needs **100 Wood, 100
Fiber, and 5 Copper Ingots**.

### The shed will not place

I check for existing Plant Basins and other occupied farm space.

I do not keep rotating the building over the same blocked tiles. I move toward a
clearer section of the farm and leave room for the trough and later livestock
equipment.

### I built the shed but the animal tutorial is not moving

I go back to Kenanimo.

The next steps are **Animal Basics → Feeding Trough → first Stilttail Pheasant**. A
shed by itself is not the end of the tutorial.

### My Feeding Trough recipe is missing

I check that I have unlocked **Animal Basics** after returning to Kenanimo.

The normal trough uses **15 Wood + 10 Stone**.

### I have no Alfalfa ready

I do not stop the livestock system. Fiber and other crops can be used as temporary
feed while I establish an Alfalfa supply.

If I want to grow Alfalfa itself, I remember that the seed is a rainy-period crop
with an approximately four-day first growth time.

### I got an Egg but nothing new unlocked

I stop treating the Egg as ordinary farm income and take one back to Kenanimo. That
handoff is what leads into the Breeding Room tutorial.

### I cannot afford the Horn Alpaca

I leave it.

The early Horn Alpaca costs **3,000G**. I would rather keep a working Pheasant setup
than empty the farm account just to add a more expensive species before the rest of
the livestock system is ready.

<h2 id="when-to-expand">
  My stopping rule before I buy the next animal
</h2>

I expand livestock only when all three parts of the current loop are working:

**space + feed + useful output.**

If one is missing, I fix that bottleneck first.

A bigger herd does not solve an empty Feeding Trough. A Breeding Room does not solve
a one-animal setup. A 3,000G animal does not solve a farm that still needs its next
progression upgrade.

Once the shed stays fed without constant babysitting, products are being collected
during a short daily route, and the next purchase no longer competes with something
more important, that is when I add another animal.
