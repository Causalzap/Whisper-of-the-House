There is no single **best crop in Doloc Town**. The answer changes depending on
whether I need cash now, have the right planter, can process the harvest, or have
started breeding multi-gene seeds.

That distinction matters more here than it does in a simple farm sim. A crop with a
high sell price can still be a bad early purchase if it takes too long to mature,
needs a Shrub Plant Basin I have not unlocked, or only becomes excellent after I
build the processing chain behind it.

For normal play, I use this progression:

**Endam for cheap early cash → Coffee or Tea Tree once shrub planting is practical
→ Grape when I can process crops into alcohol → wet-season fungi for short-cycle
processing → Tomato for easy late dry-season direct sales → Agave when I can afford
a long crop and actually use its processing value.**

<h2 id="starter-crops">
  Start with crops I can afford to replant
</h2>


My first farm is too small and my wallet is too thin to optimize around theoretical
endgame profit. I need crops that turn back into money quickly enough that I can
still buy seeds, tool upgrades, and progression materials.

That is why I keep coming back to **Endam** at the start.

The basic numbers are simple:

- seed cost: **20G**;
- first harvest: about **2 days**;
- direct sale value: **40G** for the normal one-crop harvest;
- normal direct profit: about **10G per growing day** after the seed cost.

Those numbers are not spectacular later. Early on, that is not the point. Endam is
cheap enough that I can fill several basins without emptying my wallet, and its
short cycle gives me another buying decision quickly.

I do **not** sink all of my opening money into the 100G mixed seed pack just because

They are a bad foundation when I need a predictable crop cycle.

The same rule is why I do not rush a **Wax Apple** just because the seed is more
expensive. It has long repeat-harvest value, but the initial investment, planter
requirements, and climate window are more important than the sticker price on one
fruit.

<h2 id="unlock-seeds">
  Unlock better seeds before judging the whole crop system
</h2>

The early shop is not the complete seed pool.

Alchemy's Plant Analyzer opens the route to new seeds. I feed harvests into the
research loop, turn them into Plant Research Reports, and use those reports to
finish seed research. Newly researched seeds then become available through
Villain's shop.

That means I do not judge Doloc Town's farm economy from the first shelf of seeds.

<h2 id="climate-periods">
  Match the crop to Light Rain, Heavy Rain, Early Dry, and Harsh Dry
</h2>

Before I spend heavily on seeds, I check **which climate period I am actually in**.

Doloc Town rotates through:

**Light Rain → Heavy Rain → Early Dry → Harsh Dry.**

A crop being profitable does not matter if I plant it into a period it cannot
survive.

I hit this exact decision with Potato and Chinese Cabbage: Potato belongs to the
dry side of the calendar, while Chinese Cabbage is a rainy-period crop. Buying the
right seed at the wrong time is still a bad purchase.

<h2 id="light-rain">
  Light Rain: use cheap crops early, then move into repeat-harvest shrubs
</h2>

In Light Rain, my best crop depends heavily on how developed the farm is.

### Early farm: keep using Endam until the next purchase is safe

I do not abandon a working two-day crop just because Coffee has a better long-term
return. If buying the shrub seed and building the correct planter would leave me
unable to fund the rest of the farm, I wait.

### Established farm: Coffee is my strongest direct-sale shrub

A normal Coffee seed costs **450G**. It takes about **12 days** for the first
harvest and then regrows on roughly a **3-day** cycle. The current no-gene model
works out to about **51.2G/day in direct-sale profit** over its repeat-harvest life.

Tea Tree is close behind. Its seed costs **400G**, the first harvest is about
**10 days**, and its no-gene direct model is about **47G/day**.

I choose Coffee when I want the stronger raw-sale return. I choose Tea Tree when
it fits the processing and layout I am already building.

The important catch is that **both are shrub crops**.

<h2 id="shrub-basin">
  Do not buy Coffee or Tea Tree before I can actually plant them
</h2>

This is one of the easiest ways to waste money in the midgame.

A Tea Tree seed can look perfect for the current climate, but a normal crop basin
will reject it. I need **Shrub Planting** and the appropriate Shrub Plant Basin.

5. then buy the expensive seed stack.

I do not reverse that order and buy five 400G–450G shrubs before I know where I am
putting them.

<h2 id="grape-processing">
  Grape becomes the standout mild-period crop when I can process it
</h2>

If I am selling everything raw, **Coffee beats Grape** on the normalized no-gene
direct-sale model: about 51.2G/day versus about 40G/day.

The answer flips once I can consistently process the harvest.

A normal Grape seed costs **200G**, takes about **10 days** to reach the first
harvest, regrows in about **5 days**, and can produce repeatedly. In the current
no-gene model:

- direct-sale profit is about **40G/day**;
- processing the harvest through the wine chain raises the modeled return to about
  **173.3G/day**.

That is a large enough gap that I do not casually sell a mature Grape harvest raw
after investing in the processing setup.

processed model.

<h2 id="heavy-rain">
  Heavy Rain: short-cycle fungi are stronger than they first look
</h2>

Heavy Rain gives fungi a very different economic profile.

**Chanterelle** takes about **1 day** to mature. With no genes, the normal direct
model is only about **30G/day** after seed cost, but its processing model reaches
about **210G/day**.

**Mushroom** takes about **2 days**. Its no-gene direct model is about **50G/day**,
while the processing model is about **140G/day**.

So my choice is:

- **Mushroom** if I want a stronger simple direct sale with very little waiting;
- **Chanterelle** if I already have the ingredients and cooking/processing path
  needed to capture its much larger processed value.

I do not call Chanterelle "the best Heavy Rain crop" unless I can actually complete
that processing chain. A theoretical recipe that is blocked by another ingredient
is not income.

Coffee, Tea Tree, and Strawberry remain useful during this period, especially if
they are already established. I do not tear out productive repeat-harvest shrubs
just to chase a one-day crop ranking.

<h2 id="early-dry">
  Early Dry: Grape returns, while Coffee and Tea Tree still have time
</h2>

Early Dry is another strong **Grape** period, so a mature processing setup keeps
paying off.

Coffee and Tea Tree can also continue here; neither belongs in the driest period,
so this is the last climate stage where I am comfortable expanding them outside
without relying on special protection or indoor production.

This is where I start looking ahead instead of only looking at today's profit.

If Harsh Dry is close, I do not spend all of my money on a new long-lived crop that
cannot survive the next stage outdoors. I either:

- plant something that finishes or remains valid through the change;
- reserve the seed for later;
- or move the valuable production indoors.

<h2 id="harsh-dry">
  Harsh Dry: Tomato for flexibility, Agave for a long processing commitment
</h2>

Harsh Dry is where I separate **easy cash** from **maximum processing value**.

### Tomato is the safer normal choice

A normal Tomato seed costs **120G**. It reaches the first harvest in about **7
days**, regrows in about **4 days**, and the no-gene model produces about
**34.7G/day direct** or **94.7G/day processed**.

That makes Tomato easy to justify when I still need cash flow and do not want to
lock a planter into a very long cycle.

### Agave is the slow high-value project

Agave costs **400G** per seed and takes about **20 days** to mature without gene
modification.

Raw, it is not dramatically better than the simpler dry crops: the normal direct
model is about **30G/day**.

The value is in the alcohol chain. Turning Agave into its processed product raises
the modeled return to about **105G/day** before gene optimization.

That is why I only plant a big Agave block when:

- I can afford the 400G seeds without stalling progression;
- I am comfortable waiting through the long first growth;
- the required planter space is available;
- and I have a real plan to process the harvest.

If I need money soon, I plant Tomato. If I have the infrastructure and can wait, I
start Agave.

<h2 id="genes">
  Genes can completely change the ranking, so I do not mix endgame numbers into beginner advice
</h2>

The gene system is where a static "best crop" list becomes misleading.

With optimized three-gene setups, the modeled processed returns can jump to roughly:

- **Grape: 380.6G/day**;
- **Agave: 458.8G/day**;
- **Chanterelle: 567.6G/day** in its valid wet period.

Those are not beginner numbers. They assume I have progressed far enough to work
with gene activation and that I can support the resulting harvest and processing
volume.

I therefore use gene-heavy rankings for **late farm optimization**, not for deciding
what to buy with my first few hundred Gold.

The exhaustive combinations also do not belong in this article. That is exactly
the kind of problem I would rather solve with a filterable crop calculator than
repeat as dozens of static rows.

<h2 id="when-not-to-buy">
  I skip a profitable seed when the farm is not ready for it
</h2>

A crop can rank well and still be the wrong purchase.

### I do not buy the seed yet if the planter is missing

Coffee and Tea Tree taught me this immediately. If the crop requires a shrub basin,
I unlock and place the shrub setup before buying a stack.

### I do not buy a long crop right before an incompatible climate period

The seed can wait in storage. The crop cannot undo several wasted growing days.

### I do not expand a processing crop past my machine capacity

Grape is excellent when the wine chain keeps moving. A mountain of unprocessed
fruit is not the same thing as 173.3G/day.

### I do not spend my last Gold chasing a higher theoretical return

If buying the "best" seed leaves me unable to upgrade the Pickaxe, buy required
materials, or keep the main story moving, I use the cheaper crop for another cycle.

That is why my real crop progression is not a spreadsheet ranking from day one. It
is a series of upgrades:

**reliable crop → researched seed → correct planter → weather control → processing
capacity → gene optimization.**
