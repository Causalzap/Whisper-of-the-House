Fish breeding in Doloc Town became much easier for me once I stopped treating the
Fish Tank like a normal storage box.

The important rule is that **the fish inside the tank are a recipe**. Their species
and count determine which special roe can roll when the tank finishes a production
cycle. The result is still probabilistic, so having the right parents does not mean
the first output will be the variant I want.

For normal progression, I use this route:

**build one Fish Tank → build the Fish Incubator → learn the Blackback Sardine
tutorial with 1 Catfish + 5 Sardines → breed the six-slot variants first → keep
seasonal parents instead of selling them → move to the Large Fish Tank only when I
need 8- or 12-fish combinations.**

<h2 id="unlock-fish-tank">
  Build the Fish Tank and Fish Incubator before I chase variants
</h2>

<AdUnit placement="article_after_first_h2" />

The first mistake I made was thinking that catching the right fish was the whole
job. It is only the parent-stock step.

I need the **Fish Tank** unlocked in the Life branch before those fish become a
breeding system at all.

In the current crafting data I use for this guide, the basic Fish Tank costs:

- **2 Glass**
- **2 Rubber**
- **5 Copper Ingots**

I do not build several of them immediately. One tank is enough to prove that I
understand the loop, and Copper still competes with tools, power, and other farm
equipment.

The second machine matters even more than it first appears: the **Fish Incubator**.

Its current recipe is:

- **1 Glass**
- **5 Electrical Wires**
- **5 Iron Ingots**

The tank produces **Fish Roe**. The incubator turns that roe into a named fry, and
the fry can then go back into a tank and grow into the actual fish.

That is why I build the incubator before I run several tanks in parallel. Different
species can give me items that all display as Fish Roe, and those roe do not give me
a useful species name while they are still eggs. If I let several mystery stacks
pile up, I make my own inventory problem.

<h2 id="how-breeding-works">
  The correct fish combination gives me a chance, not a guaranteed variant
</h2>

A breeding tank works in cycles. When production completes, the game checks the fish
inside the tank and can roll a special breeding result if the composition matches a
valid combination.

The practical consequence is important:

> **If I put 1 Catfish and 5 Sardines into a tank and the first output is not
> Blackback Sardine roe, I do not rebuild the tank. I keep the same six parents,
> keep the tank fed, collect the output, and let it roll again.**

The same logic applies to Nightbone Fish, Silverlight Eel, Humpback Salmon, and the
other variants below.

This is also why I do not keep changing the ratio after every ordinary result.
Correct composition and guaranteed output are not the same thing.

### Keep the output clear

The basic tank only gives me a small output buffer. I collect completed items when I
pass the tank instead of leaving them there for days.

A full output can waste the next completed production cycle. That is a much worse
loss than taking ten seconds to empty the tank during my normal farm route.

### Fry are growing, not breeding

Once roe becomes a fry, I can put the fry back into a tank and wait for it to mature.

While a fry is growing, I do **not** count that slot as part of my breeding engine.
Fry do not consume feed while growing, but they also do not contribute normal tank
production progress.

If I need six adult Eels for a Silverlight Eel roll, replacing one of those adults
with an Eel Fry means I no longer have the six-parent setup I intended.

<h2 id="blackback-sardine">
  Start with Blackback Sardine because the game teaches the exact ratio
</h2>

My first real breeding test is **Blackback Sardine**.

The setup is:

**1 Catfish + 5 Sardines → chance of Blackback Sardine Roe**

The current breeding reference puts the special-roe chance at about **66.67%** for
that correct setup.

This is a good tutorial combination because the basic tank has exactly enough room
for it. I do not need a larger aquarium, a rare chain parent, or an expensive
late-game fish.

There is also a good inventory lesson here: I stop automatically selling every
Sardine once fish breeding is close. Early on I can catch several Sardines while
trying to fish something else, and five of those "unwanted" fish are exactly what
this recipe needs.

My order is simple:

1. catch or keep **5 Sardines**;
2. keep **1 Catfish**;
3. load exactly those six adults into the Fish Tank;
4. add feed;
5. collect the tank output whenever it finishes;
6. put Fish Roe into the Fish Incubator;
7. if the fry is Blackback Sardine, return it to a tank and let it mature.

I keep the original parents until I have actually confirmed the target fry. Roe by
itself is not enough evidence for me to dismantle the setup.

<h2 id="small-tank-variants">
  Breed the six-slot variants before I pay for a Large Fish Tank
</h2>

Most of the useful achievement progress can be done without the large aquarium.

I use one compact table here because the **ratio is the answer**; repeating all of
these combinations in separate paragraphs would make the page harder to scan.

| Variant I am breeding | Parent setup | Tank I use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Blackback Sardine** | 1 Catfish + 5 Sardines | Basic Fish Tank |
| **Nightbone Fish** | 3 Mimicry Skeleton Fish | Basic Fish Tank |
| **Crimson Cod** | 3 Cod | Basic Fish Tank |
| **Azure Pike** | 6 Pike | Basic Fish Tank |
| **Silverlight Eel** | 6 Eels | Basic Fish Tank |
| **Humpback Salmon** | 6 Salmon | Basic Fish Tank |
| **Terror Fish** | 3 Hoplias + 3 Pike | Basic Fish Tank |
| **Leaf Slug** | 3 Aquatic Chameleon Bugs + 3 Grass Carp | Basic Fish Tank |
| **Mist Eel** | 4 Loach + 4 Ricefield Eels | Large Fish Tank |
| **Monarch Tuna** | 3 Shiny Golden Fish + 9 Tuna | Large Fish Tank |
| **Abyss Lantern Seabass** | 4 Seabass + 8 Fluorescent Eels | Large Fish Tank |

That list gives me **eight breeding-only targets before the large-tank step**. For
the **Abundant Collection** achievement, which asks me to breed **10 distinct fish
variants**, I therefore do not need to begin with the most expensive 12-fish
recipes. I clear the eight small-tank variants first, then choose at least two of
the three large-tank variants.

The current achievement snapshot shows Abundant Collection at **6.9%**, which fits
how late this system becomes in a normal save: it is not just "catch ten different
fish."

<h2 id="which-small-variants-first">
  I order the small-tank variants by the parents already in storage
</h2>

I do not follow the table from top to bottom blindly.

After Blackback Sardine, I check my boxes and ask which complete parent group I
already own.

### Nightbone Fish: 3 Mimicry Skeleton Fish

This is one of my favorite early cleanup targets because it only needs **3 parent
fish**.

The current special-roe chance for the correct setup is about **66.67%**. Its roe
takes about **12 hours** to hatch, and the fry takes about **1 day** to mature.

If I already have three Mimicry Skeleton Fish, I do this before spending time
catching six of something else.

### Crimson Cod: 3 Cod

Crimson Cod is another low-slot recipe.

I hold **3 Cod** instead of selling all of them during rainy-period fishing. I do not
fill the remaining tank slots with unrelated fish "for efficiency" while I am
trying to prove the recipe; I keep the breeding tank clean so I always know what
composition is producing the output.

### Azure Pike: 6 Pike

This one fills the basic tank completely.

Once I have six Pike, I leave all six in place until I confirm the variant. Pulling
one parent out to use in another recipe breaks the full setup.

### Silverlight Eel: 6 Eels

The current special-roe chance is about **50%** with the six-Eel setup.

That means an ordinary output on the first cycle is not surprising. I keep the tank
running instead of assuming the combination failed.

### Terror Fish: 3 Hoplias + 3 Pike

I do this after Azure Pike if possible because both routes want Pike.

The mistake is using my only three Pike in Terror Fish first and then realizing I
need six adults for Azure Pike. When I am short on parents, I finish the six-Pike
route first, then reuse three of those parents with three Hoplias.

### Leaf Slug: 3 Aquatic Chameleon Bugs + 3 Grass Carp

This is another full six-slot recipe.

I do not throw extra Aquatic Chameleon Bugs into the tank after seeing other odd
outputs from them. For the variant attempt, I keep the **3 + 3** ratio intact.

<h2 id="salmon">
  Save six Salmon during Salmon Fest before the window closes
</h2>

**Humpback Salmon** is the small-tank variant I plan around the calendar instead of
around my storage boxes.

The parent setup is:

**6 Salmon → Humpback Salmon**

The current special-roe chance is about **50%**.

Salmon are the problem, not the tank. Light Man's Salmon Fest explanation gives me a
short annual window: **the 25th through the 27th**, with Salmon appearing widely
outside the shallow pools during the event.

far longer than the Gold from selling one extra Salmon is worth to me.

I also do not dismantle the six-Salmon tank after the first ordinary roe. The
special roll is not guaranteed.

<h2 id="large-fish-tank">
  Upgrade to the Large Fish Tank only when a recipe physically needs more than six fish
</h2>

The **Large Fish Tank** is not my first fishery purchase.

In the current crafting data, it costs:

- **8 Glass**
- **4 Plastic**
- **20 Rockwood**
- **2 Metal Frameworks**

That is a meaningful jump from the basic tank.

I build it when I am ready for combinations that physically cannot fit in six
slots, not because "large" sounds automatically better.

The three advanced targets I care about for variant completion are:

### Mist Eel: 4 Loach + 4 Ricefield Eels

This is the cheapest advanced composition in terms of slot count: **8 adults**.

If my only goal is to move Abundant Collection from eight variants to nine, this is
usually the first large-tank recipe I attempt.

### Abyss Lantern Seabass: 4 Seabass + 8 Fluorescent Eels

This uses all **12 fish slots**.

The real bottleneck is the **8 Fluorescent Eels**. I do not build the large aquarium
first and then discover I only own two of the rare parent. I count the parents
before spending the Glass, Plastic, Rockwood, and Metal Framework.

### Monarch Tuna: 3 Shiny Golden Fish + 9 Tuna

This is the recipe I leave until last unless I already have the parents.

It also consumes all **12 slots**, and the Shiny Golden Fish parent can itself take
extra work. A breeding route for Shiny Golden Fish uses **6 Carp + 6 Koi** and has a
much lower special-result chance than my early variants.

If I already caught Shiny Golden Fish elsewhere, Monarch Tuna becomes much more
reasonable. If not, I usually finish Mist Eel and Abyss Lantern Seabass first and
only then decide whether I still need Monarch Tuna.

<h2 id="koi-and-shiny-golden-fish">
  Use Koi as a breeding chain parent instead of treating every result as a final target
</h2>

Koi is useful even before I care about the rarest fish.

A simple setup of **3 Carp** can produce Koi, with a current special-roe chance of
about **66.67%**.

I keep the first Koi rather than immediately selling it because Koi feeds later
breeding chains.

The expensive jump is **Shiny Golden Fish**:

**6 Carp + 6 Koi → Shiny Golden Fish**

The special-result chance is only about **10%** in the current breeding reference.

That is exactly the kind of recipe I refuse to brute-force early. It needs a Large
Fish Tank, twelve parents, and potentially several production cycles. Unless I need
Shiny Golden Fish itself or want Monarch Tuna, the cheaper breeding-only variants
give me better progress per unit of setup time.

<h2 id="feed-and-speed">
  Add feed production and Oxygenation Pumps only after the breeding ratios are correct
</h2>

A slow correct tank is more useful than a fast wrong tank.

I solve the parent ratio first. Only then do I spend materials to speed or simplify
the system.

### Fish feed

The current Feed Machine recipe is:

- **5 Electrical Wires**
- **10 Copper Ingots**
- **2 Rubber**

Two useful fish-feed recipes are:

- **Grain Fish Feed:** 3 Wheat
- **Protein Fish Feed:** 3 Shrimp + 1 Kelp

I build the Feed Machine when several tanks are making manual feeding annoying. For
one test tank, ten more Copper Ingots may still be more valuable in another
progression system.

### Oxygenation Pump

The Oxygenation Pump costs:

- **3 Copper Ingots**
- **1 Iron Ingot**
- **1 Battery**

Its purpose is speed. It does not repair a bad breeding ratio.

I add one after I have a tank that I know I will keep cycling. I do **not** spend a
Battery on an accelerator while I am still swapping random fish in and out trying
to discover what the parent recipe should be.

<h2 id="ecological-fish-tank">
  Use the Ecological Fish Tank for convenience, not for 12-fish recipes
</h2>

The Ecological Fish Tank removes the feeding chore by using power.

Its current crafting recipe is:

- **4 Glass**
- **2 Sand**
- **5 Electrical Wires**
- **5 Steel Ingots**

I like it for a six-fish breeding or production setup that I intend to leave running
for a long time.

I do **not** treat it as a replacement for the Large Fish Tank. The convenience tank
still handles a six-fish composition, so it cannot hold an 8-fish Mist Eel recipe or
the 12-fish Monarch Tuna and Abyss Lantern Seabass recipes.

Steel is also expensive enough that I do not build the Ecological Fish Tank just to
avoid feeding one basic tank. I move to it when feeding several cycles has become a
real maintenance problem.

<h2 id="roe-troubleshooting">
  When the right fish are in the tank but I am not getting the variant
</h2>

### I keep getting ordinary roe

I leave the correct parents in place and run more cycles. Special breeding is a
weighted chance, not a guaranteed first output.

### All of my eggs are just called Fish Roe

That is normal. I incubate the roe instead of trying to identify the species from
the egg name.

Once it becomes fry, the species becomes useful to me.

### The tank seems to have stopped producing

I check feed first on a normal Fish Tank.

Then I collect any finished output. I do not assume the tank is broken while its
output storage is already occupied.

### I put fry into the tank and production feels slower

I wait for the fry to mature. Fry are using space while growing but are not normal
adult contributors to the breeding cycle.

### I have the right species but the variant still never appears

I count the parents again.

For the full-tank formulas, one missing adult matters:

- 5 Sardines + 1 Catfish means **six adults**;
- Silverlight Eel needs **six Eels**;
- Humpback Salmon needs **six Salmon**;
- Azure Pike needs **six Pike**.

I also remove unrelated fish while troubleshooting. A clean recipe makes it much
easier to see whether the problem is the ratio, feeding, or simply bad rolls.

<h2 id="abundant-collection-route">
  My shortest practical route toward Abundant Collection
</h2>

I do not start by chasing the fanciest fish.

My route is:

1. **Blackback Sardine** — 1 Catfish + 5 Sardines
2. **Nightbone Fish** — 3 Mimicry Skeleton Fish
3. **Crimson Cod** — 3 Cod
4. **Azure Pike** — 6 Pike
5. **Silverlight Eel** — 6 Eels
6. **Terror Fish** — 3 Hoplias + 3 Pike
7. **Leaf Slug** — 3 Aquatic Chameleon Bugs + 3 Grass Carp
8. **Humpback Salmon** — 6 Salmon during/after Salmon Fest
9. **Mist Eel** — 4 Loach + 4 Ricefield Eels in the Large Fish Tank
10. **Abyss Lantern Seabass** — 4 Seabass + 8 Fluorescent Eels in the Large Fish Tank

I keep **Monarch Tuna** as my fallback eleventh target because its parent chain is
more expensive.

That order is not about rarity prestige. It is about avoiding the worst resource
trap: building a 12-slot endgame fish setup before I have finished the variants
that a single cheap six-slot tank can already produce.
