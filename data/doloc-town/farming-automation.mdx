Doloc Town does not jump straight from a watering can to a fully autonomous farm.
The useful automation route comes in layers:

**Manual Sprinkler → Simple Generator → Automatic Sprinkler → batteries and a more
stable power grid → Automation Workbench → Basic Drone → Drone Station → Farming
Drone → specialized Filling, Processing, Gathering, and Logistics Drones.**

I do not rush the last layer as soon as I see it in the tech tree. Early
electricity is worth building when watering is starting to consume too much of the
day. Full drone automation becomes worth the titanium, Engine Cores, batteries, and
Metal Framework only after the farm is large enough that I am repeatedly doing the
same planting, filling, processing, and hauling jobs by hand.

<h2 id="manual-sprinkler">
  Use Manual Sprinklers before electricity becomes worth the cost
</h2>

<AdUnit placement="article_after_first_h2" />

The Manual Sprinkler is the first point where I stop watering every basin one at a
time.

It covers a **7×3 area** and still needs water, so I treat it as a labor-saving
step rather than true automation. Its current recipe at the Equipment Workbench is:

- **2 Copper Ingots**;
- **15 Stone**;
- **3 Resin**.

If I put a Wooden Barrel or another water source close enough, the sprinkler can
draw from that source instead of making me refill it constantly.

<h2 id="simple-generator">
  Build the Simple Generator before placing power-hungry farm equipment
</h2>

Kasia's advice is easy to misread: the Automatic Sprinkler is the upgrade, but the
**Generator comes first**.

The Simple Generator is crafted at the Equipment Workbench with:

- **3 Electrical Wires**;
- **5 Copper Ingots**;
- **1 Old Engine Core**.

It burns ordinary combustible fuel such as **Coal or Wood**.

The Old Engine Core is the material I avoid wasting. I can find one through normal
scavenging and even pull one from trash, so I do not spend unrelated rare materials
trying to force a more advanced power setup before the basic generator has proved
useful.

### When I build it

I build the Simple Generator when at least one of these is true:

- the watering route is taking too much of the morning;
- I already have an Automatic Sprinkler ready to use;
- I am starting to add other electrical farm equipment;
- or I have enough Copper that the 5-Ingot cost will not delay a required tool or
  main-story craft.

If my farm is still tiny, I keep the Copper. Electricity is useful, but it is not a
mandatory day-one expense.

<h2 id="fuel-and-battery">
  Feed the generator first, then add batteries for stability
</h2>

A placed generator is not the same thing as available power.

I add **Coal or Wood** and confirm that the farm's electrical devices are actually
receiving enough power before I troubleshoot the sprinkler itself.

Kasia also points toward the next quality-of-life upgrade: batteries can store
**excess power** and make the grid less fragile.

The early Small Storage Battery uses:

- **2 Copper Ingots**;
- **2 Rubber**;
- **1 Battery**.

I add storage when the farm has enough electrical load that losing power means
watering stops or several devices idle at once. With one sprinkler and a generator
I can keep fed easily, I would rather save the materials.

<h2 id="automatic-sprinkler">
  Upgrade to Automatic Sprinklers when watering time becomes the bottleneck
</h2>

The Automatic Sprinkler is the first farm device I treat as real automation.

It consumes electricity and covers a larger **10×5 area**. The current Equipment
Workbench recipe is:

- **3 Iron Ingots**;
- **2 Electrical Wires**;
- **1 Rubber**.

That larger footprint is why I do not copy the exact layout I used for Manual
Sprinklers. I reposition basins around the powered sprinkler rather than forcing a
new device into an old 7×3 plan.

### If the Automatic Sprinkler says Insufficient Power

I check this sequence:

1. Is a generator actually placed?
2. Does it have Coal or Wood?
3. Is total power demand higher than the current supply?
4. Do I need storage because the grid is unstable?
5. Only after those checks do I question the sprinkler placement itself.

That order saves a lot of pointless rebuilding.

<h2 id="rubber">
  Make Rubber before I commit to electric irrigation
</h2>

Rubber is the material that often delays the move from manual to automatic
watering.

I have two useful production routes:

- **1 Resin → 1 Rubber** in the Resin Press, taking 24 time units;
- **1 Old Tire + 1 Coal → 1 Rubber** in the Garbage Smelter, taking 48 time units.

several industrial recipes, so I reserve the first pieces for the device that
removes the largest repeated chore.

<h2 id="automation-workbench">
  Do not build the Automation Workbench just because the recipe appears
</h2>

Full drone automation belongs to a later material tier.

The Automation Workbench itself needs:

- **1 Titanium Ingot**;
- **2 Steel Ingots**;
- **1 Chip**;
- **5 Electrical Wires**.

That recipe is the reason I do not describe drones as the "next" thing immediately
after the first sprinkler. Titanium changes the opportunity cost.

If I am still short on power, Iron, basic storage, or main-story materials, I finish
those systems first. The Automation Workbench becomes worth it when the farm is
large enough that several manual loops are competing with exploration time.

<h2 id="basic-drone">
  Start the automation tutorial with one Basic Drone, not a fleet
</h2>

The automation tutorial begins by sending me to **Modi**, then asks me to make a
**Basic Drone**.

The Basic Drone costs:

- **1 Titanium Ingot**;
- **1 Engine Core**;

and is made at the Automation Workbench.

I craft exactly one at first.

The tutorial then moves through Cod and into the first farm station rather than
asking me to mass-produce drones immediately. That is the pace I follow even after
the tutorial stops holding my hand.

<h2 id="small-drone-station">
  Build the Small Automated Drone Station for the first real farm loop
</h2>

The first station is the **Small Automated Drone Station**.

Its recipe is:

- **1 Titanium Ingot**;
- **1 Metal Framework**;
- **2 Electrical Wires**;
- **2 Batteries**.

It is an **indoor station** and carries **one automated drone**.

The Metal Framework itself comes from the Assembly Molding Machine using **2 Iron
Ingots + 1 Copper Ingot**, so I prepare that before I spend the Titanium.

This is also tied directly to the automation tutorial: I need to place one Small
Station before the Farming Drone steps continue.

<h2 id="farming-drone">
  Use the Farming Drone to remove crop-care chores first
</h2>

The Farming Drone is the first specialization I care about because it attacks the
most repetitive part of a large farm.

Its job is to combine **watering, fertilizing, and mulching**.

The current recipe uses:

- **1 Titanium Ingot**;
- **1 Engine Core**;
- **1 Farming Automation Component**.

The tutorial makes me prove that the system works instead of merely crafting it:
I use the Farming Drone to **plant 10 times** and **fertilize 10 times** before
moving on.

That is a useful real-world threshold too. If my farm is so small that doing ten
crop-care actions still feels trivial, I probably do not need a large drone
infrastructure yet.

<h2 id="specialized-drones">
  Add specialized drones only after the Farming Drone proves useful
</h2>

After the Farming Drone portion, the tutorial introduces four specialized models.

I think about them by bottleneck:

- **Filling Drone** — use it when machines or containers constantly need inputs;
- **Processing Drone** — use it when I am repeatedly loading production recipes by
  hand;
- **Gathering Drone** — use it when finished goods or harvests keep waiting to be
  collected;
- **Logistics Drone** — use it when the real problem is moving items from an output
  container to the next input container.

Each specialized model uses the same basic shell:

**1 Titanium Ingot + 1 Engine Core + the matching Automation Component.**

I do not build all four because the names are unlocked. I watch the farm for the
chore I keep repeating and automate that one first.

For example, a Grape farm with plenty of raw fruit but idle brewing equipment needs
a processing or logistics solution more than another crop-care drone. A farm where
the basins themselves are the problem still benefits more from Farming Drones.

<h2 id="large-drone-station">
  Upgrade to a Large Drone Station when one drone per station becomes the limit
</h2>

The **Large Automated Drone Station** is an outdoor station that can carry **three
automated drones** and support them moving in and out of buildings.

Its current recipe is:

- **3 Titanium Ingots**;
- **2 Metal Frameworks**;
- **5 Electrical Wires**;
- **5 Batteries**.

That is a major step up from the Small Station.

I do not pay that price just to replace a working single-drone setup. I upgrade
when I have several tasks in the same farm zone and the extra station capacity will
actually keep multiple drones busy.

This is also why the late achievement **Laid-back Farmer**, which asks for **20
automated drones operating at the same time**, is a completion goal rather than a
sensible early automation target.

<h2 id="troubleshooting">
  Fix the bottleneck instead of adding another machine
</h2>

### The Automatic Sprinkler is placed but does nothing

I check generator fuel and total power first. If the device says **Insufficient
Power**, I do not move the sprinkler until I have confirmed the grid.

### I cannot make the first automated station

I check for **Titanium**, **Metal Framework**, Wire, and Batteries. The station is
not part of the same early-material tier as the Simple Generator.

### My Farming Drone exists but automation still feels pointless

I ask what manual task is actually slowing me down. If the farm is still small,
the drone may be solving a problem I do not have yet.

### Processed crops are piling up

I stop adding crop-care capacity. The bottleneck has moved downstream. At that
point I look at Processing, Gathering, Filling, or Logistics instead.

<h2 id="when-to-stop">
  Stop expanding automation when it is no longer saving me time
</h2>

I stop buying more automation equipment when one of three things happens:

- the machines are idle more often than they are working;
- raw materials cannot keep up with processing capacity;
- or the next station costs materials I need for main progression.

A fully automated farm is satisfying, but it is not automatically the best use of
every Titanium Ingot.

My target is much simpler:

> **Automate the chore that is currently stealing time, confirm the new bottleneck,
> then decide whether the next machine is actually worth building.**
