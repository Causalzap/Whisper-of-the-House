Doloc Town is much easier to follow once I stop treating every new machine, request,
and side path as equally urgent. The main story has a clear spine: establish the
farm, open River Valley, unlock movement and combat support, clear the blocked route,
restore the Environmental Modifier, repair Treaty Port, push through the Wetlands,
and then follow the trail into the Old City Ruins.

My main-story route is:

**set up the farm → enter River Valley → join the Guild → get the Jetpack and
Combat Drone → meet the mayor at Treaty Port → upgrade to a Copper Pickaxe →
push deeper into River Valley → follow the Eden site-plan route → restore the
Environmental Modifier → repair Treaty Port → enter the Wetlands → continue to
the Old City Ruins → finish the later Environmental Modifier stages.**

This page owns that **progression order**. When the story turns into a specific
system problem, I switch to the dedicated Owner Page instead of repeating the same
solution here.

<h2 id="starting-farm">
  Build a working farm before spending money on optional upgrades
</h2>

<AdUnit placement="article_after_first_h2" />

The opening farm is not a race to cover every empty tile. I only need enough
infrastructure to keep the story moving without running out of food, money, or
inventory space.

I start by doing four things:

1. clear enough debris to move around the farm;
2. place the **Manual Workbench**;
3. plant and water the starting seeds;
4. unlock **Agriculture Basics** and move toward **Wooden Plant Basins**.

The first Basic Plant Basins are useful for the tutorial, but they are disposable.
That is why I do not spend the opening days building a huge layout out of them.
Once Wooden Plant Basins are available, I would rather expand with something I am
not immediately replacing.

The Manual Workbench is enough for simple early crafts. When the tutorial asks for
something more advanced, I use the **public Equipment Workbench in the Town Hall
lobby** instead of assuming I need to build my own immediately.

### When I buy the first bag upgrade

Inventory pressure becomes real quickly, but I do not spend my last coins on bag
space before the first crop cycle is established.

The first bigger bag costs **500G**. I buy it once I have seeds planted and a clear
way to earn the money back. If I am sitting at 400G and still need seeds or basic
materials, I wait.

That is the rule I use for most early purchases:

> **progression first, convenience second.**

A bigger bag becomes worth it when a full inventory repeatedly forces me to abandon
useful drops. It is not worth delaying the farm or a required tool upgrade just to
get it one day earlier.

Once the farm is stable and I am deciding what is actually worth planting, I use the <Link href="/doloc-town/best-crops" className="font-semibold text-sky-700 underline decoration-sky-300 underline-offset-4 hover:text-sky-900">Best Crops and Profit Calculator</Link> instead of turning the walkthrough into a crop-economy page.

<h2 id="river-valley">
  Wait for River Valley to reopen instead of treating the barricade as a bug
</h2>

River Valley can be temporarily locked while the Guild searches for a missing
person after the landslide. The entrance is visibly barricaded during that stage.

When I hit that lockdown, I stop trying to jump around the entrance or search for
another path. I work on the farm, town tasks, storage, and basic crafting until the
Guild announcement clears the route.

<h2 id="guild-jetpack-drone">
  Join the Guild and unlock double jump before exploring every River Valley branch
</h2>

Inside the reopened River Valley, Orlando offers the Guild route. The entrance test
is simple: defeat **5 Chameleon Bugs** and return.

The important reward is not only Guild access. Before sending me deeper into the
area, Orlando gives me a **Jetpack**, which unlocks **double jump**.

After the Guild test, I follow the shooting-range tutorial and unlock the Combat
Drone route. I do this before spending a long session farming enemies in the
valley. The drone makes the next exploration loop safer and lets me focus on
navigation and resources instead of stopping for every flying threat.

I also resist the urge to buy expensive combat gear immediately. At this stage my
money still has more important jobs: **tool progression, seeds, and inventory
space**.

<h2 id="treaty-port-night">
  Go to Treaty Port at night when the River Valley story tells you to
</h2>

One of the easiest progression cues to miss is the time window for the abandoned
Treaty Port building.

After returning the Plant Analyzer data, I am told to go to the abandoned building
on the **west side of the dock between 6:00 PM and 10:00 PM**.

If nothing happens there during the day, I do not assume the quest is broken. I
come back inside that evening window.

That meeting turns the vague River Valley exploration into a concrete objective:
the blocked waterways have to be cleared so Treaty Port can function again.

<h2 id="copper-pickaxe">
  Upgrade the Pickaxe when the upper River Valley route becomes the blocker
</h2>

The mayor points out the next physical gate: landslide boulders block the upward
route deeper into River Valley.

This is the moment I stop treating tool upgrades as general farm optimization. The
**Copper Pickaxe is now a main-story purchase**.

The Copper Pickaxe upgrade at Merlin's smithy uses the old Pickaxe plus **5 Copper
Ingots** and costs **1,500G**.

If I am short on Copper, I smelt what I already have before buying unrelated
equipment. I also avoid upgrading the Axe or Watering Can first just because they
are available in the same menu.

My decision is simple:

- if a tool only makes chores faster, it can wait;
- if the current main route physically requires that tool tier, I buy it first.

<h2 id="eden-site-plan">
  Follow the Eden site plan until the Environmental Modifier becomes the blocker
</h2>

Once the upper River Valley route opens, I keep following the main investigation
until the Eden **site plan** points me toward the next story machine.

This is where I deliberately keep the walkthrough short.

I pick up scarce materials that are directly on my route, but I do not turn the
trip into a full-area cleanup. Once the investigation reaches the **Core Extractor**
and the Environmental Modifier sequence begins, I switch to the <Link href="/doloc-town/environmental-modifier" className="font-semibold text-sky-700 underline decoration-sky-300 underline-offset-4 hover:text-sky-900">Environmental Modifier Guide</Link> for the Core Sample, rear-panel repair, Small Battery requirement, circuit troubleshooting, and Phase 1.

For this walkthrough, the only progression check that matters is:

> **finish Environmental Modifier Phase 1 before expecting the waterways and Treaty
> Port route to advance.**

That keeps the Core Extractor and Small Battery details on their actual Owner Page
instead of duplicating them here.

<h2 id="treaty-port-repair">
  Repair Treaty Port after Environmental Modifier Phase 1
</h2>

After Phase 1 clears the blocked riverway, I stop searching around the Modifier
cave. The story moves back toward the dock.

The Treaty Port repair sequence sends me back to Doloc Town's dock, then through
the town construction flow before I wait for the repair to finish and return to the
port.

This is another place where I avoid overfarming first. Opening the repaired port
has more progression value than stockpiling another day of common materials.

Once Treaty Port is working again, I treat it as the handoff from the River Valley
chapter into the next large exploration phase.

<h2 id="wetlands">
  Treat the Wetlands as a progression chain, not a free-roam cleanup
</h2>

The Wetlands route has several objectives close enough together that I follow the
quest order instead of exploring every side branch on arrival.

My main-story sequence is:

**reach the Wetlands → follow the main-quest trail → investigate the reservoir →
solve the wastewater-line blocker → retrieve the strange underwater part → take
the Lotus Prototype back into the Environmental Modifier progression.**

I keep this page at the progression level. I do not turn the walkthrough into a
full pipe-repair crafting guide or invent a route I have not verified closely
enough yet.

The practical rule I use is the same one that worked in River Valley: when the
quest changes from exploration to a specific damaged machine, pipe, or required
item, I solve that blocker before spending another day clearing optional branches.

<h2 id="old-city">
  Push the main quest into the Old City Ruins before starting completion cleanup
</h2>

After the Wetlands chain, the story eventually points toward the **Old City Ruins**.

I treat Old City as late-game progression rather than an achievement-cleanup zone.
The main route asks me to prepare for the expedition, restore access and power,
push deeper into the ruins, and then descend underground while following Ponka's
trail.

Later objectives continue through the city's power network. The route includes
restoring power around the warehouse, industrial, and commercial areas before the
Environmental Modifier story advances again.

I do not stop here to finish every reputation track, hidden achievement, fish
variant, or collectible. Those are better cleanup goals after the main route has
opened the late-game systems and areas they depend on.

<h2 id="final-route">
  Follow Ponka's trail into the later Environmental Modifier stages
</h2>

The late main-story chain eventually brings the Environmental Modifier back into
focus.

At that point I keep following the objectives tied to **Ponka**, the **Energy
Core**, the old research location, and the **Reactor Core** rather than returning
to earlier regions looking for an unrelated trigger.

The late progression pattern is:

**Old City investigation → Ponka trail → Energy Core → old research location →
Reactor Core work → later Environmental Modifier stage.**

That is why I do not treat the first Environmental Modifier repair as a one-off
puzzle. It is a system the main story returns to later.

<h2 id="stuck">
  Main-story progression checks I use when nothing seems to advance
</h2>

### River Valley is still barricaded

I wait for the temporary Guild lockdown to resolve. I work on the farm, crafting,
storage, or town tasks instead of trying to bypass the barricade.

### The Treaty Port meeting will not trigger

I return to the abandoned building on the **west side of the dock between 6 PM and
10 PM**. Arriving during the day is not the same as reaching the correct quest
window.

### I cannot get past the boulders in River Valley

I stop searching for a jump route and upgrade the Pickaxe. The Copper Pickaxe is
the intended progression check once the mayor points me toward the blocked upper
path.

### I found the Environmental Modifier but the story still will not advance

I use the <Link href="/doloc-town/environmental-modifier" className="font-semibold text-sky-700 underline decoration-sky-300 underline-offset-4 hover:text-sky-900">Environmental Modifier Guide</Link> rather than repeating the repair here. Finding the machine, repairing the rear circuit, and finishing Phase 1 are separate checkpoints.

### I reached a new region and do not know whether to explore or continue the quest

I follow the named main-story machine, location, or repair objective first. I only
switch to optional exploration when the next story step is waiting on time,
materials, or a scheduled event.

That keeps the route moving without turning every new region into a full completion
sweep.
