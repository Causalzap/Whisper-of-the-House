<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
For early Dew, gather **Wild Berries in The Hills**, buy **Sugar from Kun's Recipe Shop in Global Village**, and cook **Fruit Smoothies**. Each needs 2 Wild Berries, 1 Sugar, and 2 Battery charges. With Sugar costing 5 Dew and a Smoothie selling for 20, you earn 15 Dew above the purchased ingredient cost. Keep crops growing between berry runs, and sell them through your neighborhood Shipping Bin when you need immediate cash.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

Running out of Dew becomes a problem when your next seeds or Building Permit cost more than you have. The fastest choice depends on what's already in your inventory: berries can go straight into Fruit Smoothies, planted crops can be shipped when ready, and harvested vegetables can sometimes earn a little more through Cooking.

Before paying for ingredients, compare the selling price with the value of what you're using up. A 65-Dew dish is not necessarily 65 Dew of new income.

<h2 id="first-dew">
  How Do You Get Your First Dew in Loftia?
</h2>

Your first dependable selling point is the **Shipping Bin in your Neighborhood Island**. You'll encounter it during Hazel's early farming and cooking quests.

The Shipping Bin accepts qualifying crops, cooked dishes, and certain other items. Once you ship them, you receive Dew immediately.

If you haven't finished Hazel's opening tasks, complete those first. They introduce your Garden Bed, Cooking Station, and the process of selling what you've grown. The <Link href="/loftia/beginner-guide">Loftia beginner walkthrough</Link> follows that starting route through the Glider unlock.

Afterward, you have three practical options for earning more:

- Gather free Wild Berries and turn them into Fruit Smoothies.
- Plant inexpensive crops and sell the harvest.
- Cook with crops you already own when the finished dish provides enough additional Dew to justify the extra ingredients and energy.

You can mix these activities. A crop doesn't have to be ready before you leave your island to gather berries, and your Battery can recharge while you handle another objective.

<h2 id="fruit-smoothies">
  How Do You Make Dew With Fruit Smoothies?
</h2>

Fruit Smoothies are a useful starting method because the main fruit ingredient comes from gathering rather than buying seeds.

You need:

- **2 Wild Berries** — gathered in The Hills around Loftia City.
- **1 Sugar** — purchased from **Kun's Recipe Shop** in Global Village.
- **Cooking Station Lv.1** — available through the early cooking progression.
- **2 Battery charges** per Smoothie.

A Fruit Smoothie sells for **20 Dew**. Sugar costs **5 Dew**, leaving 15 Dew above the purchased ingredient cost when you gather your own berries.

<h3 id="smoothie-route">
  Where Do You Get the Ingredients?
</h3>

Begin in **The Hills**, where Wild Berries can be gathered from plants around the area.

Pick up other useful resources along the way if your inventory has room, but keep track of how many berries you actually collect. You'll need two for each Smoothie, so a batch of ten requires twenty berries.

Once you have enough, travel to **Global Village** and visit Kun's Recipe Shop.

Buy one Sugar for every Smoothie you intend to cook. You don't need to buy a large stack immediately. A small batch is safer if you still need Dew for seeds or a Building Permit.

Return to your island, charge your Battery, and interact with your Cooking Station.

<h3 id="smoothie-profit">
  How Much Dew Do You Make From 10 or 20 Smoothies?
</h3>

Using 2 Wild Berries and 1 Sugar per Smoothie:

**10 Fruit Smoothies**

- Gather 20 Wild Berries.
- Buy 10 Sugar for 50 Dew.
- Sell 10 Smoothies for 200 Dew.
- Receive **150 Dew above the Sugar cost**.

**20 Fruit Smoothies**

- Gather 40 Wild Berries.
- Buy 20 Sugar for 100 Dew.
- Sell 20 Smoothies for 400 Dew.
- Receive **300 Dew above the Sugar cost**.

These calculations treat gathered berries as free and do not assign a Dew value to your gathering time or Battery charges. They're useful for deciding how much Sugar to buy, not for measuring guaranteed Dew per hour.

The larger batch also requires more cooking energy. With 2 charges consumed per Smoothie, twenty crafts need 40 charges in total, so plan to recharge repeatedly.

If you're short on both money and ingredients, make a few Smoothies first, sell them, and use that income for the next batch.

This avoids tying up all your Dew in Sugar before you've collected enough berries.

<h2 id="crop-profits">
  Which Crops Are Good for Making Dew Early?
</h2>

Farming is convenient when you want money without making repeated trips around Loftia City.

Buy seeds from **Hazel**, plant them at home, and ship the harvest after it grows. The useful comparison isn't just the crop's selling price: you also need its seed cost and growing time.

<h3 id="cotton">
  Cotton: Cheap Seeds, Small Returns
</h3>

Cotton suits a small starting budget: its seed is inexpensive, and the short growth cycle lets you harvest between brief errands. The return per bed is modest, so use it while saving enough Dew to switch to higher-value crops rather than filling every available space with Cotton indefinitely.

<h3 id="tomatoes">
  Tomatoes: Higher Returns per Garden Bed
</h3>

Tomatoes need more upfront Dew than Cotton, but they return more per planting space. Four Tomato Seeds cost 60 Dew; shipping four Tomatoes brings back 120 Dew. Put at least 60 Dew aside for the next planting before spending the rest on equipment or permits. The comparison assumes a single harvest per seed, with watering delays excluded.

<h3 id="watering">
  Why Are Your Crops Taking Longer Than Expected?
</h3>

The listed growth time assumes the crops can continue growing.

Your plants have a watering status. If they run out of water before they're fully grown, progress stops until you water them again.

This matters when comparing crops. A fifteen-minute plant that sits dry for another fifteen minutes isn't providing its expected return.

Check the watering indicators before leaving your island. If you're about to spend a longer period gathering materials, choose a crop whose watering needs fit that trip, or plan to return before it dries out.

To expand beyond your initial planting space, you'll need additional Garden Bed permissions from Amani. The <Link href="/loftia/beginner-guide">beginner guide</Link> explains the first permit and Garden Bed unlock.

  Should You Cook Crops or Sell Them Raw?
</h2>

Cooking can increase the value of harvested ingredients, but it doesn't always increase that value by very much.

A dish that sells for 65 Dew might look considerably better than a single 30-Dew Tomato. But if that dish requires two Tomatoes, the correct comparison is with the selling value of **both** ingredients.

<h3 id="tomato-soup">
  Is Tomato Soup More Profitable Than Selling Tomatoes?
</h3>

Tomato Soup requires **2 Tomatoes** and sells for **65 Dew**.

If you sell two harvested Tomatoes directly, they bring in 60 Dew. Cooking them into Soup raises the selling value to 65 Dew.

That's only 5 additional Dew for the cooking step, which also uses 2 Battery charges.

If your Battery is nearly empty or you need money immediately to purchase seeds, selling the Tomatoes raw may be more convenient.

If you're also progressing your Cooking skill and have energy available, making the Soup can still be useful. Just don't count the full 65 Dew as extra income created by cooking.

<h3 id="fries">
  Are Fries a Better Money-Making Method?
</h3>

Fries have a higher selling price than Fruit Smoothies, but they also need grown crops and a purchased ingredient.

The recipe uses:

- **2 Potatoes**
- **1 Cooking Oil**
- **Cooking Station Lv.1**

Cooking Oil is available from Kun's Recipe Shop, and the Fries recipe can also be purchased there once available through your progression.

A serving sells for **65 Dew**.

Fries become more convenient when you already have a Potato harvest ready and can afford Cooking Oil without exhausting your Dew balance.

They aren't automatically the best option just because the Shipping Bin shows a larger number.

<h2 id="shipping-bin">
  Where Should You Sell Crops and Cooked Food?
</h2>

For direct income, return to the **Shipping Bin on your Neighborhood Island**.

Open it, select the eligible items you want to ship, and complete the sale. The Dew is awarded immediately.

For instance, keeping a few Tomatoes and Potatoes gives you the flexibility to cook later. Keeping a small selection of other crops may also help when you reach new tasks in Hanging Gardens.

You can still sell the surplus once you've set aside what you need.

<h2 id="vending-machine">
  Can You Sell Items to Other Players?
</h2>

Your neighborhood also includes a **Vending Machine**. Unlike the Shipping Bin, it allows you to list supported items for other Loftians to purchase.

This is useful for surplus items such as clothing and furniture that you don't want to keep.

Choose an eligible item, set its price, and list it through the machine.

A Vending Machine listing isn't the same as immediate Shipping Bin income. Someone else has to buy the item.

That makes it less suitable when you urgently need Dew for seeds, a Permit, or an upgrade. It's better treated as a way to earn something from valuable extras that would otherwise sit in storage.

Check what an item is used for before listing it. Clothing fragments, furniture, and collected materials may have uses beyond their selling value.

<h2 id="spending-dew">
  What Should You Spend Your First Dew On?
</h2>

Keep enough Dew to replace your next batch of seeds before buying an upgrade. With four Tomato Seeds, for example, setting aside 60 Dew means the next planting isn't dependent on finding more salvage to sell. The remainder can go toward whichever constraint is slowing your income: an extra Garden Bed Permit, better energy supply, or an ingredient you need for a profitable dish.

Buying a recipe doesn't help if its ingredients are unavailable, so check the materials first. The purpose of the reserve is simple: your next harvest should already be funded when you collect the current one.

<h2 id="common-money-mistakes">
  Why Are You Still Running Out of Dew?
</h2>

<h3 id="buying-too-many-seeds">
  You Spend All Your Dew Before the Harvest
</h3>

Don't buy an entire field of expensive seeds if doing so leaves you unable to afford another required ingredient or Permit.

Start with the number of plants you can support, harvest them, and reinvest part of the sale.

You can build up to a larger garden without needing to risk your entire balance on one planting.

<h3 id="cooking-energy">
  You Have Cooking Ingredients but Cannot Make the Dish
</h3>

Check the Battery charge before starting a batch.

Fruit Smoothies, Tomato Soup, and Fries each require cooking energy. Having all the ingredients doesn't bypass this requirement.

Recharge at your energy source before returning to the Cooking Station.

<h3 id="selling-ingredients">
  The Finished Dish Is Worth Less Than You Expected
</h3>

If the sale seems disappointing, compare the finished meal with **all** the ingredients you could have shipped separately, then subtract any Sugar, Oil, or other purchased ingredients. The price shown on a recipe is the total sale, not the extra Dew produced by the cooking step. Check the crop-versus-dish comparison above before making another batch.

<h3 id="unsellable-items">
  Your Item Doesn't Appear in the Shipping Bin
</h3>

The Shipping Bin doesn't accept every item in the game.

Check whether the item is eligible for player-to-player sale through the Vending Machine, or whether it's primarily used for crafting, collection, or another activity.

Don't assume every resource is supposed to be converted into Dew.

<h2 id="when-you-unlock-hanging-gardens">
  How Should You Earn Dew After Reaching Hanging Gardens?
</h2>

Your home garden still grows crops while you investigate Hanging Gardens. Keep the routine that fits your play time, but avoid shipping every newly discovered crop: Research Bundles and resident tasks may ask for specific harvests. Check the required contribution first, save those items, and sell the surplus.

If a tool recipe is blocking your next task, identify its <Link href="/loftia/research-and-tool-upgrades">Research requirement</Link> before using up the materials you intended to process or donate.
