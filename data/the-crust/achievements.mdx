<h2
  id="opening"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Let the Opening Clear the Production Achievements for You
</h2>

Several achievements overlap almost perfectly with the route the campaign
already puts in front of you. **Now the Fun Begins** comes from the tutorial,
**A Single Spark** from the first Single Regolith Refinery, and **One Year of
Solitude** simply waits for the first year to pass. Excavating 10,000 cells and
depleting a resource vein also become increasingly likely as the underground
base grows.

I treat those as signals that the factory is progressing rather than jobs to
farm. The same applies to **Smart Moves, Concrete Gains**: the requirement is
three Pressing Modules, but three modules are only useful once Smart Concrete
demand can justify the extra footprint and logistics. Building two idle machines
for the counter while steel, science, or power is still blocking the campaign
works against the rest of the run.

**Ice Breaker** fits a similar pattern. Ice becomes relevant when life support
and habitation expand, so exhausting a vein is better allowed to happen through
real consumption than by forcing unnecessary extraction early.

If the base itself is still the problem, the <Link href="/the-crust/beginners-guide">beginner progression guide</Link>{"."} is the better place to solve steel, research, power, conveyors, and titanium before achievement cleanup starts.

<h2
  id="exploration"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Combine Exploration Achievements With Trips You Already Need
</h2>

The exploration achievements cover very different scales. **Lost and Found**
only asks for the lost convoy, **Charlie on our six** for the Charlie base, and
**Signal in a Bottle** for a magnetic anomaly. **Mad Miner** raises the bar to
five mineral deposits that must be both discovered and developed, while the
achievement named **The Crust** eventually asks for the entire lunar surface to
be scanned for resource deposits.

That last requirement changes the way I schedule exploration. Early Rover trips
already have useful destinations, so I use them to clear nearby discoveries and
open more of the map without turning every excursion into a completion sweep.
The full-surface scan belongs much later, when unexplored territory is the
remaining task rather than the thing distracting me from the next expedition.

This also keeps Rover travel productive. A detour that reveals another deposit
or nearby objective can be worthwhile; crossing large sections of the Moon only
to advance a percentage counter is much harder to justify while the campaign is
still consuming science, power, and vehicles elsewhere.

<h2
  id="money"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Cash on Hand and Company Value Are Two Different Grinds
</h2>

The economy achievements use two measurements that are easy to lump together
even though they reward different states of the company.

**American Dream** requires **1 million credits on balance**, and **I need a
Private Jet** raises the cash requirement to **10 million**. The money has to be
sitting in the account.

**Villy Gates**, **Jeff Bazzos**, and **Ilon Maks** instead track company market
capitalization at **$100 million**, **$500 million**, and **$1 billion**.

A company can be valuable without holding 10 million liquid credits, and a large
cash balance is not the same thing as reaching the market-cap milestones. I
therefore stop treating these five achievements as one continuous money counter.

Credits still pay for practical expansion. Conveyors, electrical infrastructure,
shipments, market purchases, and other logistics continue drawing from the same
balance, so hoarding cash too early can leave the company rich on an achievement
counter and poor everywhere that matters.

I start preserving a million-credit balance once normal production and trade
create a genuine surplus. Ten million comes later. Market capitalization is
allowed to rise alongside the larger company rather than being treated as a
reason to cripple investment.

<h2
  id="expansion-counters"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Ten Vehicles and Ten Fuel Generators Belong to a Base That Can Use Them
</h2>

**Lunar Truck Simulator** needs **10 vehicles** and **Hydrogen tycoon** needs
**10 Fuel Generators**. Neither achievement rewards getting there early.

Vehicles make sense when the expanding lunar operation has enough work for
them. Fuel Generators make sense when they belong in the power plan. If another
vehicle or generator already solves a real need, the achievement counter moves
forward at no extra strategic cost.

The warning sign is buying or building the remaining units only because the
number is close to ten while production, credits, or system capacity are still
tight.

construction because capacity is exhausted, the achievement can wait; the
underlying CPU problem is broader than either vehicle ownership or generator
count.

<h2
  id="hope-2"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Heroes Never Die Means All 12 Hope-2 Survivors
</h2>

**Heroes Never Die** does not unlock simply because you agree to help Hope-2.
Its requirement is explicit: **rescue all 12 survivors**.

That distinction matters because the rescue storyline begins much earlier than
the achievement finishes. Accommodation, restored communications, and the first
rescue steps are progression toward the condition, not substitutes for it.

I treat the Hope-2 branch as one of the runs worth protecting because missing
even part of the survivor requirement leaves the achievement unfinished. The
initial route decision is covered in the <Link href="/the-crust/hope-2-choice">Hope-2 choice guide</Link>{"."}

The detailed rescue execution is a separate problem from choosing the route.
For achievement purposes, the number to remember is **12 / 12**.

<h2
  id="laser"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  The Laser Gun Is a Five-Achievement Chain With Two Different End States
</h2>

The Laser Gun contains the densest cluster of hidden achievements in the list.

The construction sequence begins with:

**Stable Basis** — lay the foundation.

**Plugged In** — install the power supply systems.

**Lock and Loaded** — install the targeting system.

Those three feed into two mutually different event results:

**One shot - One asteroid!** requires the Laser Gun to be finished in time and
humanity to be saved.

**Houston, YOU have a problem** requires the Laser Gun event to fail.

This is the point where one protected save has much more value than another
hour of cleanup. I keep a save before the outcome becomes irreversible, secure
the successful result, then use the earlier state if I also need the failure
achievement.

I also avoid reaching the final stretch with every resource committed somewhere
else. The successful achievement explicitly depends on finishing the Laser Gun
**in time**, so a production queue that looked harmless earlier can become the
wrong bottleneck when the final construction stages arrive.

<h2
  id="silent-assassin"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Silent Assassin Belongs to CRUST's Undercover Assignment
</h2>

**Silent Assassin** is hidden, but its requirement is unusually clear:
**complete CRUST's undercover assignment**.

That wording is useful because it rules out a much broader interpretation. It
does not ask for general loyalty to CRUST, completion of every confidential
objective, or a particular standing with the corporation.

When the undercover assignment itself appears, I give it priority over
experimenting with unrelated story choices until the achievement has registered.
A save before optional decisions around that assignment is useful, but there is
no reason to treat every earlier confidential conversation as though it were
the trigger.

<h2
  id="colonist"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  My mom's friend's son Is a Career Achievement, Not a Housing Achievement
</h2>

**My mom's friend's son** asks for one colonist to reach the **maximum level for
that profession**.

The achievement text does not give a numerical maximum, so I follow the
profession's actual progression rather than assuming an old level cap or
stopping after several promotions.

More importantly, this is not completed by building Living Quarters. Housing
only gets colonists into the base. The achievement needs enough time for one
person to work through a profession until the career itself reaches its maximum.

Its **0.1%** unlock rate also needs context: the achievement was added on
September 9, 2026, immediately before Version 1.0. That percentage is therefore
partly a freshness signal, not evidence that it is inherently the hardest
achievement in the game.

<h2
  id="long-cleanup"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Leave the 50-Year Run and Full-Moon Scan for the Cleanup Phase
</h2>

Two achievements have requirements large enough that they should sit outside
normal progression planning.

**Average Crust Enjoyer** requires **50 in-game years**.

**The Crust** requires the **entire surface of the Moon** to be scanned for
resource deposits.

Neither rewards doing the work early. The years accumulate while every other
system runs, and lunar coverage naturally expands as expeditions and resource
searches take you farther from the starting base.

Once story-sensitive achievements are safe, the Laser Gun outcomes are handled,
and the company no longer needs constant intervention, these become much more
reasonable cleanup objectives. Until then, they are background counters.

The same caution applies to the 0.1% rate for **The Crust**: that achievement was
added in August 2026, so the percentage reflects a comparatively new
full-map objective as well as the scale of the requirement.

<h2
  id="completion-route"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  The 100% Route I Would Use
</h2>

I organize completion around how much a mistake costs rather than around Steam
unlock percentage.

1. **Play the opening normally.** Let the tutorial, refinery, first year,
   excavation, mining, and early production achievements arrive through the
   recovery route.
2. **Fold exploration into real Rover work.** Pick up the convoy, Charlie base,
   anomaly, and mineral discoveries while the Moon is already opening.
3. **Build the economy before preserving huge balances.** Reach the cash and
   capitalization milestones after routine trade can support them.
4. **Keep the Hope-2 survivor requirement visible.** The final condition is
   12 / 12, not merely entering the rescue route.
5. **Protect the Laser Gun branch.** Complete its three construction milestones,
   then use a save that lets the success and failure outcomes be handled without
   rebuilding the entire late game.
6. **Finish story-specific cleanup.** Complete the CRUST undercover assignment
   and any remaining colonist career progression.
7. **Use the mature base for the counters.** Ten vehicles, ten Fuel Generators,
   10 million credits, the largest market-cap target, 50 years, and the complete
   Moon scan are much less disruptive once the campaign-critical infrastructure
   no longer needs every resource.

The tracker is most useful at that point. Instead of revisiting the full list
and trying to remember what happened automatically, I filter what is still
unchecked and finish one remaining category at a time.
