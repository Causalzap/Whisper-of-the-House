<h2 id="hope-2-or-fasm">
  Should You Help Hope-2 or Take the FASM Route?
</h2>

The Hope-2 call is the first decision where I stop asking only, “Which reward is
better?” and start asking what I want the base to become over the next several
objectives.

The station is damaged and heading toward a hard lunar landing. CRUST is already
organizing an evacuation and asks the company to join the search-and-rescue
operation, including providing somewhere to accommodate the people involved.
In return, CRUST offers the equipment and support needed to carry that work
forward.

FASM represents the other pressure on the company. Earth needs supplies, the
contract system needs to be restored, and FASM has negotiated an upfront
payment that can help finance production.

The choice therefore separates two immediate priorities:

**Help Hope-2** if the rescue storyline is the route you want to follow and you
are willing to redirect development toward habitation and communications.

**Keep FASM available** if the base still needs the financial head start and
you want to preserve the production-focused option instead.

For a first campaign where I want to see the Hope-2 thread develop, I take
CRUST's offer. It gives the next several objectives a clear direction. On a run
where cash flow and industrial recovery are the priority, the FASM option has a
much stronger argument than the dialogue initially makes it seem.

<h2 id="accepting-crust">
  Accepting CRUST Changes the Build Order Immediately
</h2>

The largest cost of helping Hope-2 is not a single payment. It is the change in
what the base must build next.

CRUST explicitly warns that accepting its proposal gives the corporation more
influence over how the base develops. **Living Quarters move forward**, while
the expansion of high-tech manufacturing and the investigation surrounding the
meteorite are postponed.

That means resources which looked available for another production expansion a
moment ago now have somewhere else to go.

This is where I become much more conservative with optional construction. A new
production module may still be useful, but the rescue branch is about to ask for
space, power, life support, food, research infrastructure, and additional
logistics close together.

Housing is its own dependency chain rather than something worth compressing
into this decision. The <Link href="/the-crust/living-quarters">Living Quarters guide</Link>{"."} follows that route from the room itself through electricity, ice, water, oxygen, food, work, and medical support.

<h2 id="fasm-tradeoff">
  The FASM Contract Is the Economic Cost of Saying Yes
</h2>

FASM is easy to dismiss as “the money option,” but the timing makes the tradeoff
more important than that label suggests.

The company is still recovering from the disaster when the contract system
begins coming back online. Production needs investment, shipments to Earth are
valuable again, and FASM is willing to provide **prepayment** if that commercial
route is restored.

Accepting CRUST's proposal removes access to that FASM contract.

So the opportunity cost is more than the number shown on a reward screen. You
lose a source of early financing at the same moment the rescue path starts
demanding expensive infrastructure.

That is why I would not choose Hope-2 and then continue expanding the factory as
though the decision had changed nothing. Doing both at full speed leaves the
housing project underfunded while also surrendering the payment that supported
the industrial alternative.

The cleaner approach is to commit resources in the same direction as the
choice:

- Rescue route: reserve capacity for Living Quarters, research, and
  communications.
- FASM route: use the financial opportunity to strengthen production and
  commercial infrastructure.

Neither route needs a perfectly finished base beforehand. What matters is
avoiding a base that is already failing before another expensive branch is
added.

<h2 id="before-choosing">
  Check the Base Before You Commit to Hope-2
</h2>

I make the decision after looking at three underlying systems rather than at
the objective list.

**Basic materials need to be dependable.** Steel, silicon, construction
materials, and their logistics do not need to be luxurious, but I want the base
to replace what new projects consume without constant intervention.

**Research needs to be moving.** The rescue path begins connecting
communications and habitation technology, so weak science income creates long
periods where all the physical resources are ready but the required technology
is not.

**Power needs some margin.** The current production network is not the final
load. Housing, support equipment, research facilities, and new communication
infrastructure arrive on top of what is already running.

If one of those systems is visibly failing, I repair it before starting a large
new build. The <Link href="/the-crust/beginners-guide">beginner progression guide</Link>{"."} covers that recovery stage in more detail.

I also keep a save before making the Hope-2 decision. The immediate consequences
are large enough—especially losing the FASM contract—that replaying from this
point is far easier than rebuilding the opening if I later want to compare the
other route.

<h2 id="after-accepting">
  After Accepting Hope-2, Work on the Base and the Moon in Parallel
</h2>

Once CRUST's proposal is accepted, the campaign stops being a purely local
factory problem.

Communications across the Moon are damaged, and restoring them is necessary for
both wider lunar operations and the Hope-2 rescue. Your base is near the
repeater, so CRUST supplies a **Rover Scout** and sends you into the repair
chain.

From here I divide attention between two tracks.

At home, the base prepares for people. That means keeping the Living Quarters
project supplied without starving the production lines that pay for everything
else.

Outside the base, the Rover works through the communications chain. Once that
progress reaches the Cargo Terminal, navigation itself becomes part of the
problem because the coordinates are approximate rather than a precise marker.
The <Link href="/the-crust/cargo-terminal">Cargo Terminal guide</Link>{"."} covers the Rover and LPS search once you reach that stage.

The useful part of this split is that one track can continue while the other is
waiting. Research can progress while the Rover travels; the Rover can work
off-base while construction drones finish housing infrastructure. I get much
less value from opening a third unrelated expansion project just because some
resources happen to be available.

<h2 id="route-commitment">
  Treat the Choice as a Development Commitment, Not a One-Line Dialogue
</h2>

What makes Hope-2 important is that the consequences start before the rescue
itself.

Accepting CRUST changes the immediate build priorities, removes the FASM
prepayment option, and begins a communications-and-habitation sequence that
pulls the base in a different direction. That is already enough to plan around,
even before every later story consequence is known.

For the rescue route, I consider the opening phase finished once the factory can
support those two new responsibilities at the same time: people at the base and
operations beyond it.

If the base can keep producing while Living Quarters come online and the Rover
is working through the communications chain, the Hope-2 decision has been
absorbed successfully. If every new objective forces another shutdown,
shortage, or abandoned construction project, the problem is no longer the
choice itself—the economy underneath it is not ready for the route yet.
