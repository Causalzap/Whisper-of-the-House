<h2 id="build-living-quarters">
  How to Build Living Quarters That Are Actually Ready for Colonists
</h2>

Living Quarters look like a room-building objective when they first appear, but
the enclosure is only the beginning. By the time the game considers the space
usable, that room has to support electricity, oxygen, water, sleeping, food,
work, and medical care.

That changes how I plan the excavation.

I clear a larger underground area than the first room outline strictly needs
because the later modules take considerably more floor space than a few beds.
The game also recommends keeping the sleeping area away from production, which
is much easier to respect before walls and permanent modules start filling the
available space.

A cramped room may satisfy the first construction step and still become awkward
once the Hydroponic Farm, Kitchen, Science Lab, Medical Block, and life-support
equipment all need somewhere to go. A little unused floor early is cheaper than
reworking the enclosure halfway through the objective.

<h2 id="power-living-quarters">
  Connect the Electronic Board Before Troubleshooting Anything Inside
</h2>

A finished enclosure can still be completely unusable if electricity never
reaches the interior.

Place the **Electronic Board on the wall** and connect it to the main electrical
network. That board is what gives the room access to power; the existence of
solar panels and batteries elsewhere on the base does not mean the quarters are
automatically supplied.

When an interior module appears inactive, I check the board and its network
connection before moving furniture or adding another generator. It is an easy
mistake to read “the base has power” as “the room has power,” even though those
are two separate conditions.

<h2 id="find-ice">
  The Life-Support Chain Starts With Ice, Not Oxygen
</h2>

The next dependency is easy to approach from the wrong end.

The objective talks about making the quarters habitable, so oxygen feels like
the obvious target. In practice, the chain begins with **ice** because both
water and later life-support systems depend on it.

Find an ice deposit and place an **Ice Extractor** on it. The extracted ice then
needs an **Ice Melter**, which converts that supply into water.

That gives the first half of the habitation chain:

**ice deposit → Ice Extractor → Ice Melter → water**

Until water exists, building around later housing requirements only creates more
unfinished modules competing for construction resources.

If the room is powered but the objective is not advancing, this is the first
resource chain I follow. A missing or disconnected water step can make the
housing project look much more complicated than it really is.

<h2 id="oxygen">
  Turn Water Into Oxygen With the Electrolysis Plant
</h2>

Once water is available, research the required oxygen technology and build the
**Electrolysis Plant**.

This is the point where the Living Quarters shift from a powered room to an
actual life-support space. The essential utility chain is now:

**ice → water → oxygen**

Electricity and oxygen are the two basic conditions the room needs before the
rest of the interior facilities matter.

That also gives a useful troubleshooting boundary. Once oxygen is functioning
and the objective has moved on to beds or food infrastructure, continuing to
add Ice Extractors will not solve the new requirement. The bottleneck has moved
farther down the chain.

<h2 id="beds-food-kitchen">
  Beds and Food Come After the Utility Chain Works
</h2>

With the room powered and oxygenated, the next requirements are much more
recognizable as housing:

**beds, a Hydroponic Farm, and a Kitchen.**

Beds provide sleeping capacity. The Hydroponic Farm needs water and produces
food, while the Kitchen gives colonists the facility needed to prepare and use
that food supply.

The sequence matters most when resources are tight. Water infrastructure has
to exist before the farm can contribute anything, and the room needs reliable
power and oxygen before adding extra resident capacity makes sense.

This is where an undersized room usually starts causing trouble. Beds are easy
to scatter into spare tiles, but the Farm and Kitchen compete with later work
and medical modules for the remaining usable floor.

For that reason, I arrange the room around dependencies rather than symmetry:
keep water-fed equipment where it fits cleanly, preserve enough open space for
the final facilities, and worry about perfect organization only after the
objective is secure.

<h2 id="science-lab-medical-block">
  Add the Science Lab and Medical Block Before Calling the Room Finished
</h2>

The last requirements reveal that these quarters are meant to support working
colonists, not simply keep bodies alive.

Build a **Fundamental Science Lab** so incoming colonists can contribute
Fundamental science, then add the **Medical Block** for health support.

Neither building looks like conventional housing, which makes them easy to
underestimate when planning the room. They are still part of the preparation
sequence, and leaving no space for them can force a layout change right at the
end.

I therefore reserve their footprint before filling every free tile with beds.
Sleeping capacity has obvious value, but an extra bed does not replace the
workplace and medical infrastructure required to finish the setup.

<h2 id="living-quarters-stuck">
  If the Living Quarters Objective Is Stuck, Trace the Dependency Chain
</h2>

Most problems here come from one earlier link being incomplete while everything
after it looks visually finished.

Rather than demolishing modules or expanding the room immediately, I check the
system in the order resources actually move through it:

1. Is the room fully excavated with enough space for the required modules?
2. Is the Electronic Board connected to the main electrical network?
3. Has an ice deposit been located?
4. Is the Ice Extractor working?
5. Is the Ice Melter producing water?
6. Has the Electrolysis Plant been researched, built, and supplied?
7. Are beds, the Hydroponic Farm, and the Kitchen complete?
8. Are the Fundamental Science Lab and Medical Block finished?

That sequence narrows the failure quickly. If water is missing, there is no
reason to rebuild the beds. If oxygen is already working, adding another Ice
Extractor is unlikely to move an objective that is now waiting for the Kitchen.

There is one important exception: the game can refuse additional construction
because the **base-wide CPU limit** has been reached. If the required materials
exist but new modules cannot be placed or ordered, check CPU capacity before
treating it as a Living Quarters problem.

CPU is a base-level constraint rather than part of the habitation chain itself,
so once that warning appears I solve the capacity issue separately and then
return to the missing housing requirement.

<h2 id="ready-for-colonists">
  What “Ready for Colonists” Actually Means
</h2>

The room reaches its useful endpoint when the game confirms that it can accept
colonists, not when the walls are finished.

At that point the quarters should have a working combination of:

- an enclosed, powered room;
- ice extraction;
- water production;
- oxygen generation;
- beds;
- a Hydroponic Farm;
- a Kitchen;
- a Fundamental Science Lab;
- a Medical Block.

The dependencies are what matter. Each part answers a different need, and
removing an earlier utility from the chain can undermine modules that appear to
be working farther downstream.

Once the room reaches the **Ready for Colonists** state, I stop expanding it
for the sake of this objective. From there, the important questions shift from
construction to the people themselves: what jobs they take, what skills they
develop, and how the base supports them over the long term.
