<h2 id="find-cargo-terminal">
  The Cargo Terminal coordinates lead to a search area, not the terminal itself
</h2>

The confusing part of this objective is that CRUST gives the Rover coordinates, but those coordinates do **not** behave like a normal clickable destination. I can drive into the correct part of the Moon and still see nothing that looks like a terminal.

That is expected. The coordinates only put the Rover close enough for the **Lunar Proximity Scanner** to take over.

After Hope 2 moves the campaign into this search, I select the Rover and travel toward the indicated area. There is no reason to circle the coordinates looking for a building model or wait for a large waypoint to appear. Once I am near the search zone, the next piece of information comes from the LPS.

If the Cargo Terminal objective has not appeared yet, the blockage is earlier in the campaign rather than on the lunar map. The <Link href="/the-crust/beginners-guide">beginner guide</Link> covers the opening production and research problems that can delay this stage.

<h2 id="lps-scanner">
  Use the LPS twice: first for direction, then for the exact location
</h2>

The LPS becomes much easier to read once I stop treating every pulse as an attempt to reveal the target immediately.

It has two useful states during this search.

When the Cargo Terminal is inside the **outer range**, the scanner gives a direction. That result means I am in the right general region but still too far away for the hidden site to resolve.

After driving farther in that direction, the terminal eventually enters the **inner range**. The next scan can then expose its exact position.

My route through the objective is:

1. Select the Rover after the Cargo Terminal objective becomes active.
2. Travel toward the approximate coordinates.
3. Pulse the LPS once inside the search region.
4. Read the direction from the outer scan.
5. Drive farther in that direction instead of scanning repeatedly from the same spot.
6. Pulse the LPS again once the target is close enough for the inner range.
7. Move onto the revealed Cargo Terminal and investigate it.

The part that usually causes the stall is between steps 4 and 5. A directional scan is not a failed scan. It is telling me that the Rover still has distance to close.

<h2 id="cargo-terminal-not-showing">
  If the Cargo Terminal is not showing, read the scanner before changing anything else
</h2>

A Rover sitting near the coordinates with no visible target can make the objective look bugged, especially because most other lunar locations are easier to identify.

The scanner result tells me whether the search is actually stuck.

If the LPS points toward a direction, I keep moving that way. Repeating the pulse from the same position does not bring the Cargo Terminal into the inner circle; the Rover has to physically close the gap.

If I have no useful search indication at all, I check whether the correct Cargo Terminal objective is active and whether I have actually reached the intended region. Returning to the base, researching unrelated technology, or buying another vehicle does not make the hidden location appear.

Another Rover is not a shortcut here either. The limiting mechanic is the **LPS search**, not transport capacity.

The only reason I interrupt a valid directional search is if the vehicle no longer has enough battery to finish the trip safely.

<h2 id="rover-power">
  Battery can stop the search even when the route is correct
</h2>

The Cargo Terminal search can fail for a completely separate reason: the Rover runs out of power before the target is revealed.

During the light cycle, solar generation gives me more freedom to push farther across the Moon. Once that safety margin disappears, a nearly empty battery can turn a correct LPS route into a long wait.

Before committing to the last stretch, I compare remaining charge with the distance still implied by the scanner. Healthy power is a reason to keep going. A battery already close to empty late in the light cycle is a better reason to wait than to strand the Rover halfway through the search.

This distinction matters because power failure and search failure look similar from a distance: in both cases the Cargo Terminal is still invisible. Only one of them is solved by moving farther.

<h2 id="investigate-cargo-terminal">
  Revealing the Cargo Terminal is only the discovery step
</h2>

Once the inner scan exposes the location, I drive directly onto the site and start the investigation.

The objective does not advance merely because the icon has appeared.

The survey establishes what happened at the facility after the disaster. The terminal attempted to receive Hope 2's distress signal, failed, and eventually shut down.

That information ends the navigation problem. Once the survey resolves, additional LPS scans around the same location are wasted movement because the next requirement is no longer hidden somewhere on the Moon.

<h2 id="after-cargo-terminal">
  The Expedition Center becomes the next real bottleneck
</h2>

Completing the Cargo Terminal investigation sends the campaign back toward base infrastructure.

The important handoff is the **Expedition Center**. Longer operations now need proper support for specialists, drones, equipment, and materials rather than another local Rover scan.

I return to the base after the Cargo Terminal survey and inspect the Expedition Center requirements before spending advanced materials elsewhere. This stage often overlaps with other demands: research may be consuming science, timed contracts can be draining storage, and habitation or production upgrades may already be using the same construction materials.

A commercial order is easy to replace. A missing Expedition Center dependency can stop the story entirely.

So if the next objective is waiting on one expensive component, that component stays out of ordinary contracts until the building is secured. The larger campaign order from this point onward is covered in the <Link href="/the-crust/walkthrough">1.0 story and progression walkthrough</Link>.

<h2 id="terminal-vs-dock">
  Cargo Terminal and Cargo Dock are two different things
</h2>

This is the naming overlap that caused me the most unnecessary checking.

The **Cargo Terminal** on this page is the hidden lunar location discovered with the Rover and LPS. It belongs to the story investigation and leads into the Expedition Center stage.

The **Cargo Dock** is a base building that becomes important later when the **Mobile Drilling Rig (MDR)** is operating outside the colony.

They solve different problems.

The Cargo Terminal answers:

**Where is the hidden story location, and how do I reveal it?**

The Cargo Dock answers:

**How do I bring bulk MDR resources back to the base without manually reassigning the Cargo Truck after every trip?**

If a Cargo Truck is sitting full of mined material and cannot unload the way I expect, searching the lunar map for the old Cargo Terminal will not help. At that stage I am dealing with the Cargo Dock logistics system.

<h2 id="cargo-dock-mdr">
  Later, the Cargo Dock turns MDR hauling into a repeatable route
</h2>

Once the MDR has been recovered, it can mine large amounts of regol ith away from the main base. That solves extraction, but it creates a transport problem immediately afterward.

A **Cargo Truck** carries the mined material back. Building the **Cargo Dock** is what makes that delivery loop much less manual.

After the truck unloads at the dock, it can return to the route connected to the previously selected MDR instead of waiting for me to assign the same journey again. If I want to stop or change that route, I use the transport interface rather than dismantling the dock or sending another vehicle.

That makes the practical sequence:

**recover MDR → assign its mining area → send Cargo Truck → unload through Cargo Dock → let the truck return to the MDR route.**

I only invest in extra hauling capacity after this loop is working. A second truck does little for a route whose unload point or MDR assignment is still wrong.

This also explains why a player can remember “Cargo Terminal” from the earlier story and assume it must be related to later resource unloading. The two systems are close in name, but by the MDR stage the hidden terminal search is already finished.

<h2 id="cargo-terminal-stuck">
  The fastest way to diagnose where the objective stopped
</h2>

When progress stops around this part of the campaign, I use the current screen rather than replaying the whole chain.

**The coordinates are active but nothing is visible:** move into the region and use the LPS.

**The LPS shows a direction:** drive farther before scanning again.

**The terminal icon is visible but the quest has not advanced:** move the Rover onto the site and complete the investigation.

**The survey is complete and nothing else happens on the map:** return to base and work on the Expedition Center.

**Much later, an MDR truck is full and logistics are failing:** that is a Cargo Dock problem, not the original Cargo Terminal search.

Those are separate blockers, and treating them as one long “Cargo Terminal not working” problem is what wastes the most time.
