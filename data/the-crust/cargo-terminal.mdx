<h2 id="find-cargo-terminal">
  How to Find the Cargo Terminal With the Rover
</h2>

The Cargo Terminal objective becomes confusing because the game gives you a
destination without giving you the kind of waypoint most objectives use.

After Hope-2 is stabilized, CRUST sends the terminal coordinates and asks you
to inspect the site so communications with the orbital station can be restored.
The Rover interface gives you a direction to travel, but reaching that general
area does not make a large Cargo Terminal marker suddenly appear on the map.

Nothing is missing at that point. The coordinates are meant to get the Rover
close enough to begin a **Lunar Proximity Scanner** search.

I send the Rover away from the base in the indicated direction, activate the
LPS once I am inside the search area, and then let the scanner tell me where the
next movement should go. The first scan is directional; the final scan is
positional.

In practice, the sequence is:

1. Select the Rover after the Cargo Terminal objective appears.
2. Choose the Cargo Terminal direction.
3. Move out of the base toward the approximate coordinates.
4. Activate the **LPS**.
5. Follow the directional indication from the outer scan.
6. Move farther in that direction.
7. Scan again when the target gets close enough to the inner circle.
8. Reveal the hidden Cargo Terminal.
9. Move onto the site and investigate it.

The important point is that step 3 does not finish the search. The approximate
coordinates only place the Rover in the right part of the Moon.

<h2 id="lps-scanner">
  The Outer LPS Scan Gives Direction; the Inner Scan Gives the Location
</h2>

The two LPS ranges do different jobs, and this is the mechanic that makes the
objective finally make sense.

When the hidden object is only within the **outer scan radius**, the scanner
does not identify the Cargo Terminal itself. Instead, it gives you a direction
to follow. I treat that result as a compass correction: move the Rover farther
toward the indicated side, then pulse the scanner again.

Once the target enters the **inner circle**, the search changes. A new scan can
resolve the hidden object precisely and expose the Cargo Terminal on the map.

So there are really three layers of information:

**Approximate coordinates** put the Rover in the correct region.

**Outer LPS range** tells you which direction closes the distance.

**Inner LPS range** reveals the hidden site.

That distinction is why driving directly to the rough coordinates and waiting
there feels broken. The objective has not failed; you have only completed the
first part of the search.

<h2 id="cargo-terminal-not-showing">
  If the Cargo Terminal Still Is Not Visible, Move Before Scanning Again
</h2>

The most common dead end comes after an LPS scan that seems to accomplish
nothing. The Rover is outside the base, the objective is active, but there is
still no terminal to select.

At that point I look at what the scanner actually returned.

If it is giving me a direction, I move. I do not repeatedly pulse the LPS from
the same position and expect the target to enter the inner circle by itself.
The Rover has to reduce the distance first.

A Version 1.0 run made this problem very obvious: after traveling toward the
Cargo Terminal, the player reached the area and still could not see anything
that looked like the objective. The solution was not another research unlock,
a second Rover, or a return to base. The hidden-object search simply had not
been completed yet.

If the LPS continues to indicate a direction, the search is still alive.

Only three things make me stop and reassess:

- the Cargo Terminal objective is not actually active yet;
- the Rover no longer has enough power to continue safely;
- the scanner gives no useful search state because I am not in the expected area.

If you have not reached the Rover phase at all, the problem is earlier in the
campaign. The <Link href="/the-crust/beginners-guide">beginner progression guide</Link>{"."} covers the production and research recovery that leads into this stage.

<h2 id="investigate-cargo-terminal">
  Finding the Cargo Terminal Does Not Finish the Objective
</h2>

Once the LPS reveals the terminal, navigation is over but the mission is not.

Move the Rover onto the discovered site and run the investigation. Simply
revealing the structure on the map is not enough to advance the objective.

The survey explains what happened to the facility: after the disaster, the
terminal tried to receive Hope-2's distress signal, failed, and shut down.

That result matters because it closes the search phase. Continuing to drive
around the same site or repeatedly scanning it will not repair communications.
The next obstacle is infrastructure for longer-range operations.

<h2 id="after-cargo-terminal">
  After the Survey, Shift Attention Back to the Expedition Center
</h2>

The investigation immediately changes what the campaign needs from you.

Instead of another local search, the next instruction is to build an
**Expedition Center** so the base can support drones and materials over longer
distances. That marks a clean handoff from Rover navigation back to production
and construction.

I return attention to the base as soon as the survey finishes. Before placing
the Expedition Center, I check whether its required materials and research can
actually be sustained without stalling everything already running.

This matters because the Cargo Terminal objective often arrives while several
other systems are active at once. Research may already be consuming science,
contracts can be drawing from storage, and Living Quarters or other
construction may be competing for the same Smart Concrete, steel, or logistics.

The Cargo Terminal itself is no longer the bottleneck once the survey is
complete. If the Expedition Center is now waiting, I direct resources toward
that requirement rather than continuing to invest in exploration equipment that
will not advance the objective.

<h2 id="rover-power">
  Check Rover Battery Before Committing to Another Long Search
</h2>

The Rover introduces a second kind of failure that has nothing to do with the
LPS: **power**.

During sunlight it can rely on solar generation, but after sunset the vehicle
depends on its battery. If that battery reaches zero during a distant trip, the
Rover can be left waiting until sunlight returns.

I check the remaining charge before pushing deeper into the search area,
especially late in the light cycle. If the Rover still has comfortable power,
there is little reason to retreat simply because the terminal has not appeared
after the first scan.

Buying another vehicle does not solve this objective either. The Cargo Terminal
search depends on the Rover's LPS and the approximate-coordinate mechanic; more
transport capacity does not make the hidden site easier to reveal.

When the battery is nearly gone, waiting for a better power window is cheaper
than extending the trip and ending up stranded. When power is healthy, I keep
following the LPS until the target reaches the inner circle and let the search
finish properly.
