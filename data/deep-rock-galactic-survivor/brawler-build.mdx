<GuideQuickAnswer title="What Is the Best Brawler Build in DRG Survivor?">
In **Deep Rock Galactic: Survivor — Knockout**, start with **Crusher** if you want a survivable Brawler using Shockwave Emitter to hit enemies approaching from multiple directions. Choose **Renegade** for a Plasma build using Diffractor Spike, Hellcat Bombardier, Wrist Pulsar, and Ion Stinger. Choose **Enforcer** for stronger melee damage, but compensate for its reduced range with Bonk stacks, Melee Range, and attacks that can reach tougher enemies. Your best option depends on whether you need survival, area coverage, or focused damage.

The Brawler fights differently from the other classes in Deep Rock Galactic: Survivor. Instead of mining with a pickaxe, he uses his fists, and his Bonk ability benefits from the new **[MELEE]** upgrade tag.

That gives the class a reason to improve Melee Damage, Reload Speed, and Range rather than treating every weapon as an unrelated source of damage.

The choice between **Crusher, Renegade, and Enforcer** changes how you use those bonuses. Crusher combines Bonk regeneration with wide shockwave attacks. Renegade uses Bonk to support a mobile Plasma setup. Enforcer converts aggression into additional range but begins with a significant reach disadvantage.

All three can finish difficult dives. They do not become equally comfortable with the same upgrades, however, and a weapon that works well for one Class Mod may leave another struggling against bosses.

## How Does Bonk Work with Melee Upgrades?

Bonk is the Brawler's close-range punching ability.

The Brawler also uses his fists to mine terrain, but mining a rock and successfully hitting an enemy are not interchangeable when judging your Class Mod's combat bonus.

The important interaction is that **[MELEE] upgrades improve Bonk**.

That includes upgrades affecting:

- **Damage:** Makes the punch hit harder.
- **Reload Speed:** Improves how frequently Bonk can be used.
- **Range:** Improves how far the punch can reach.

Because Bonk also activates your Class Mod's special effect, these upgrades can affect survival and mobility as well as direct damage.

### Crusher: Bonk Supports Regeneration

Crusher gains defensive bonuses and receives Life Regen through successful Bonks, with the effect stacking up to its limit.

This creates a reason to land punches on accessible enemies instead of avoiding all close contact.

However, regeneration is not an immediate defense against a dangerous burst of damage. Running into a dense group merely to refresh the effect can cost more health than the bonus is worth.

Use small, isolated enemies when you can safely reach them. Let Shockwave Emitter and your other weapons handle the groups that are too dangerous to approach.

### Renegade: Bonk Provides Temporary Dodge

Renegade gains Dodge stacks from Bonk, with the bonus reaching up to 40% through repeated activations.

The effect is temporary, so the benefit depends on your ability to keep it active while moving through enemies.

Use accessible targets to build Dodge without allowing yourself to be surrounded. If a large enemy is occupying a dangerous position, do not force a Bonk simply to maintain the bonus.

The Plasma build should still be able to deal damage when you move away.

### Enforcer: Bonk Builds Weapon Range

Enforcer begins with reduced weapon range, but successful Bonks increase its range through stacking bonuses.

That means the class performs differently before and after you establish those stacks.

The challenge is obtaining the bonus safely. Bonking smaller enemies can prepare you for a larger fight, but charging directly into an Elite or boss before the range improves can put you in a bad position.

Watch the active bonus indicators during combat. If you repeatedly lose access to your target, prioritize reaching and maintaining useful range rather than stacking more damage.

### Does Every Melee Weapon Hit Count as Bonk?

Do not assume that any hit from a weapon carrying the [MELEE] tag triggers the Brawler's Class Mod effect.

Bonk is the Brawler's punch, while [MELEE] is also a weapon and upgrade category.

The two systems interact because Melee upgrades improve Bonk. The safest way to recognize a successful activation is to watch the relevant Crusher, Renegade, or Enforcer bonus appear or increase after hitting an enemy.

## Best Crusher Build: Shockwave Emitter and Crowd Control

Crusher begins with **Shockwave Emitter**, a weapon that sends out wide shockwaves.

Its main advantage is not simply the damage number. Its wide attacks can clear nearby enemies, creating room to reposition before the next attack.

That is particularly important in Class Mod Mastery or higher-Hazard encounters where you may not have time to move out of every surrounding group.

### Crusher Weapon Setup

A successful Hazard 6 Crusher Class Mod Mastery used the following weapons:

- **Shockwave Emitter:** The primary area-damage weapon in the completed setup.
- **G19 Hammerhead:** A mobile hammer turret that adds continuous damage.
- **Fist Bomb:** An additional source of focused, explosive damage.
- **Supporting weapons:** Extra damage and status effects to handle enemies not cleared by the main shockwaves.

The run's damage recap put Shockwave Emitter well ahead of the other weapons, with roughly 73 million damage. G19 Hammerhead contributed around 22 million, while Fist Bomb contributed around 17 million.

### What Should You Upgrade First on Crusher?

Start by making Shockwave Emitter dependable.

**1. Improve its attack coverage.**

Wider shockwaves make it easier to hit enemies approaching from multiple directions. If an Overclock offers more useful coverage, compare that with the benefit of a narrower but stronger attack.

A larger attack area is especially valuable when enemies are already closing in before the next shockwave fires.

**2. Reduce gaps between attacks.**

Reload Speed helps the weapon attack more frequently. It also supports the Brawler's Bonk ability when gained through the Melee upgrade category.

The goal is to avoid a situation where one shockwave clears a small area but the next swarm reaches you before the following attack.

**3. Build enough survival to use Crusher's advantages.**

Armor, Max HP, and useful healing effects give you more room to recover from mistakes.

Do not rely entirely on Bonk regeneration. Keep enough distance from dangerous groups to avoid losing a large amount of health in one encounter.

**4. Add another weapon that fills a gap.**

G19 Hammerhead provided useful supporting damage in the completed run. Fist Bomb also contributed, although its Overclock choices were still being tested.

If you need more damage against high-health targets, consider a weapon that can reach them while Shockwave Emitter clears the immediate area.

### Which Shockwave Emitter Overclocks Made the Difference?

The Crusher setup combined three important Overclocks:

- **Power Grip:** Improved weapon range and Reload Speed.
- **Elastic Transducer:** Increased damage, Fire Rate, and the shockwave's attack angle.
- **Amped Up:** Greatly increased Fire Rate, Reload Speed, and clip size.

Together, these upgrades made Shockwave Emitter attack more frequently across a wider area.

If enemies consistently reach Crusher between attacks, prioritize better attack coverage and shorter attack intervals. Once the surrounding horde is manageable, invest in additional damage against tougher targets.

## Best Renegade Plasma Build: Burn, Beams, and Focused Damage

Renegade is a good choice when you want several Plasma weapons to work together rather than relying on one large melee attack.

Its starting weapon, **Diffractor Spike**, creates Plasma beams between the deployed spikes and your dwarf.

As you move, those beams can cover different parts of the battlefield. Additional spikes and beams make it easier to damage several enemies without standing directly beside them.

A focused Plasma setup can also use Burn and other status effects to keep damaging enemies while you reposition.

**Wrist Pulsar** can provide additional Plasma coverage.

An optional control-focused direction is to obtain an Overclock that applies Frost, allowing the weapon to slow or freeze enemies while your other weapons continue dealing damage.

In the Plasma setup examined here, the intended Frost option was not obtained during the showcased run.

Treat this as a possible upgrade path rather than a required four-weapon combination that is guaranteed to appear.

If Frost is unavailable, use the offered damage, Fire Rate, or Reload upgrades according to what your build currently needs.

A build that already clears ordinary enemies comfortably may benefit more from stronger focused damage than from another layer of crowd control.

### Ion Stinger — Handle High-Health Targets

**Ion Stinger** helps address a weakness of builds that invest heavily in area damage and status application.

Its combination of focused Plasma damage and a wider spray gives you a way to pressure tougher enemies.

The demonstrated setup favors Central Double Barrel, Hot Plasma, and Piercing-related upgrades.

These options can improve the weapon's ability to contribute meaningful direct damage while the rest of your equipment clears smaller enemies.

If you struggle against an Elite even though the screen is covered in Plasma effects, review Ion Stinger or another reliable focused-damage weapon before adding more area effects.

### Which Stats Matter Most for Renegade?

Prioritize according to the weapons you actually have:

- **Plasma Damage:** Strengthens the shared damage type of the setup.
- **Critical Chance and Critical Damage:** Help weapons that rely on direct hits.
- **Potency:** Improves the application of relevant status effects.
- **Status Effect Damage:** Becomes more useful as your Burn and other effects contribute more damage.
- **Reload Speed:** Helps keep compatible weapons attacking.
- **Movement Speed:** Supports repositioning and makes the temporary Dodge bonus easier to use.

Do not overinvest in status-related stats before acquiring weapons that can apply the effects you intend to build around.

Similarly, a run with multiple strong Plasma weapons may benefit more from shared Plasma upgrades than from an unrelated damage tag that only strengthens one minor weapon.

### What If Renegade Struggles Early?

A successful Hazard 5 Renegade run began with limited weapon options and struggled to damage tougher enemies quickly.

The build improved after obtaining more beams, additional Plasma weapons, and stronger upgrades.

If your first two weapons are underdeveloped, give the primary weapon enough levels to contribute before spreading upgrades across everything available.

You can still complete the run without assembling the exact four-weapon setup above. The important parts are sustained damage, usable coverage, and at least one answer to high-health enemies.

Renegade is especially rewarding when its attacks continue hitting enemies as you move. If your build forces you to stand still to deal damage, reconsider whether your chosen weapons are supporting that strength.

Enforcer is the most demanding of the three Brawler Class Mods to position correctly.

It begins with higher damage but reduced weapon range. Successful Bonks add temporary range, creating a strong incentive to hit smaller enemies before a difficult encounter.

The problem is that you cannot always approach the enemy you most want to damage.

In a completed Hazard 5 Enforcer run, short attack range repeatedly caused trouble against tougher enemies and bosses. Strong damage upgrades helped, but they did not automatically solve the difficulty of reaching a target.

### What Should You Upgrade First on Enforcer?

**1. Solve the range problem.**

Enforcer's reduced starting range is the most important weakness to understand.

Look for Melee Range and other compatible range improvements before investing exclusively in damage.

If your weapon's attack cannot reach a boss, another damage bonus will not help until the range problem is addressed.

**2. Use Bonk stacks before dangerous encounters.**

Bonk smaller enemies when you can do so without taking excessive damage.

Do not run directly into a boss merely to build the range bonus. The initial short reach makes that especially dangerous.

If an enemy cannot be approached safely, rely on weapons that can hit it from your current position until an opening appears.

**3. Consider wider attack coverage.**

A Thunderlash or other weapon that can cover a wider angle may help when several enemies approach simultaneously.

In the Enforcer run, expanding attack coverage was useful because limited range made it difficult to respond to threats arriving from different directions.

When offered an Overclock, decide whether the extra coverage solves a real problem or whether a stronger focused attack would be more helpful.

**4. Improve your survival.**

Bonk encourages close contact, but the enemies still deal contact damage.

Armor, Max HP, and movement are valuable when they allow you to recover from an unsuccessful approach.

A build with excellent damage but no safe way to reach its targets is not finished.

### Why Does Enforcer Struggle with Restricted Weapon Choices?

Enforcer's reduced starting range becomes more punishing when a challenge limits your weapon choices. You may not be able to assemble the same collection of [MELEE] weapons and shared upgrades available in a normal dive.

If your selected weapon cannot reach dangerous targets reliably, prioritize its available Range improvements and use safer opportunities to build Bonk stacks.

## Which Brawler Upgrades Should You Prioritize?

Do not follow the same fixed upgrade order for Crusher, Renegade, and Enforcer.

Start by identifying why your current build is struggling.

<GuideTable
  columns={brawlerUpgradeColumns}
  rows={brawlerUpgradeRows}

### When Is Melee Range Better Than Damage?

Melee Range is valuable when your attacks repeatedly fail to reach the enemy you want to hit.

This is especially important for Enforcer, but it can also help Crusher's Bonk and other close-range weapons.

If enemies are already well within range and your attacks are connecting consistently, more damage or faster attacks may be the stronger choice.

The correct decision depends on whether your immediate problem is reaching the target or killing it after you reach it.

### When Should You Take Reload Speed?

Reload Speed becomes valuable when your weapons spend too long between attacks.

Crusher benefits when Shockwave Emitter's downtime gives enemies room to close in.

Enforcer may benefit when a useful attack does not activate frequently enough to handle surrounding threats.

Renegade can also benefit, but a shared damage or Plasma upgrade may be more valuable when multiple weapons already attack consistently.

Choose Reload Speed because it solves an attack-frequency problem, not simply because it is available.

### Should You Pick More Weapons or Upgrade Existing Ones?

A new weapon is valuable when it fills a missing role.

If your setup has excellent crowd damage but struggles with Elites, adding focused damage can be better than improving an existing area weapon.

If your build already covers the battlefield but its main weapon is approaching a strong Overclock, raising that weapon's level may create a larger immediate improvement.

In both successful Crusher and Renegade runs, concentrated weapon upgrades helped make the core attacks more effective before the final encounters.

Avoid spreading every available upgrade evenly across weapons with no clear purpose.

## Best Gear and Artifacts for Brawler Builds

Gear should reinforce what your chosen Class Mod is trying to accomplish.

You do not need a single mandatory equipment set for all Brawler runs.

### Crusher Gear Priorities

Look for useful combinations of Max HP, Armor, Movement Speed, and damage.

Defensive equipment allows you to take advantage of Crusher's regeneration while remaining capable of escaping a dangerous group.

In the Hazard 6 Crusher Mastery run, additional HP and Armor helped the character survive difficult encounters. Movement Speed also made it possible to reposition after the weapons cleared a narrow route.

Equipment that provides healing or defensive bonuses can be valuable when you are repeatedly losing health between safe opportunities to Bonk.

### Renegade Gear Priorities

Focus on the damage types and secondary effects actually used by your Plasma weapons.

Plasma Damage is a natural starting point. Crit-related bonuses can help focused weapons, while Potency and Status Effect Damage become more useful when your build is applying Burn, Frost, or other relevant effects.

Movement Speed supports the class's evasive playstyle.

Do not take a status-focused item merely because the build contains Plasma weapons. Check whether the weapons you have acquired are currently applying the status effects that item improves.

### Enforcer Gear Priorities

Enforcer benefits from equipment that helps compensate for close-range combat.

Weapon Range, movement, and defensive bonuses can be more important than another small damage increase when your attacks already hit hard.

A strong Thunderlash setup also benefits from being able to keep dangerous targets inside its attack range.

If you consistently lose health while approaching an enemy, improve survivability rather than expecting Enforcer's damage bonus to solve the encounter.

### Which Artifacts Should You Look For?

Evaluate Artifacts according to the run rather than following a fixed ranking.

- **Movement Artifacts:** Useful when you cannot reposition or maintain a safe attack distance.
- **Defensive Artifacts:** Help if repeated close encounters are draining your health.
- **Potency Artifacts:** More attractive when several weapons apply status effects.
- **Reroll-related Artifacts:** Can improve your ability to find upgrades that suit a focused build.
- **Damage Artifacts:** Useful once your weapons already reach targets and cover the threats you need to handle.

For a Plasma build, **Chemist Kit** can support status application when Potency matters.

An Artifact such as **Barley Bulb Juice** can also provide movement benefits, which are useful for any of the three Class Mods when repositioning becomes difficult.

The important question is whether the Artifact improves a weakness in the current run. Picking an item solely because it has a powerful-looking bonus can leave a more urgent problem unsolved.

## Why Is Your Brawler Build Not Working?

Different Brawler builds fail for different reasons. Use the problem you are experiencing to choose what to change.

### Crusher: Enemies Reach You Between Shockwaves

Your Shockwave Emitter may be dealing good damage without attacking often enough or covering enough space.

Prioritize useful Reload Speed, attack coverage, and movement.

Do not stand in the middle of an approaching group waiting for the next shockwave. Reposition until your weapons create another opening.

### Crusher: Bonk Regeneration Is Not Keeping You Alive

Life Regen helps during a sustained run, but it cannot undo a large amount of damage immediately.

Bonk safer enemies and avoid trying to refresh the effect inside a dense horde.

If your health drops too quickly, improve Armor, Max HP, or other survival options before taking more offensive upgrades.

### Renegade: Plasma Looks Strong but Elites Take Too Long

Large numbers of beams and status effects do not automatically guarantee high focused damage.

Check whether you have a weapon capable of pressuring a single high-health target.

Improve Ion Stinger or another suitable focused-damage option rather than adding more area coverage that does not solve the problem.

### Renegade: The Burn and Freeze Combination Is Incomplete

Your build depends on which weapons and Overclocks appear during the run.

If the planned Frost option does not appear on Wrist Pulsar, continue with a useful alternative rather than rerolling away every other valuable upgrade.

A Plasma setup can still work without the exact combination of Burn and Freeze effects.

### Enforcer: You Have Damage but Cannot Hit the Boss

This is a range problem.

Use compatible Range upgrades and safer opportunities to build Bonk stacks.

Do not mistake the inability to reach the target for insufficient damage.

If you must cross a dangerous group to reach the boss, find a route or wait for a safer opening rather than running straight through the enemies.

### Enforcer: Thunderlash Is Not Contributing Enough

Check whether its current range and targeting behavior are allowing it to reach high-health enemies.

If the attack misses important targets because they remain too far away, improve range before adding more damage.

Also consider whether G19 Hammerhead or another secondary weapon is doing more work in the current setup.

### Your Weapons Never Reach Strong Overclocks

You may be spreading levels across too many weapons.

Identify which weapon is responsible for your main damage and which others exist to provide coverage, control, or focused attacks.

Prioritize the upgrades needed to make those roles effective rather than upgrading everything equally.

### You Keep Losing During Higher-Hazard Dives

A stronger build is useful, but Hazard 6 also introduces a different challenge involving enemies carried between stages.

If ordinary encounters feel manageable but later stages become overwhelming, review the <Link href="/deep-rock-galactic-survivor/hazard-6-ultraclock">Hazard 6 progression and survival rules</Link> before assuming that one weapon choice is the entire problem.

## What Should You Do After Your First Brawler Run?

Open the weapon damage report after your next Brawler dive. Compare which weapons received the most upgrades, which ones reached their major Overclocks, and which weapons actually dealt damage to important targets.

If a highly upgraded weapon contributed very little, examine its attack range and targeting behavior before replacing it. A weapon that cannot reach the enemies you need to kill will not become reliable through damage bonuses alone.

Once the loadout performs consistently, test it against <Link href="/deep-rock-galactic-survivor/brood-harvest">Brood Harvest</Link> or a <Link href="/deep-rock-galactic-survivor/hazard-6-ultraclock">higher-Hazard Mastery challenge</Link>.
