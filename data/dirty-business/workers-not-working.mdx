My first Botanist looked broken. I had hired the Worker, placed the required
equipment, added money, and assigned a task, but he still stood around while I
continued doing the growing and packing myself.

The useful clue was not his animation. It was the **Current Status** line in
Worker Management. Once I treated that message as a supply-chain diagnosis, the
setup became much easier to fix.

<h2 id="worker-statuses">
  Botanist status messages at a glance
</h2>

The exact wording can vary, but the diagnosis follows the same rule:

> **Fix the current named blocker, reopen Worker Management, and follow the next
> status until the Botanist reaches a valid waiting state or produces output.**

A new warning after a fix does not mean the first fix failed. It usually means
the Worker has advanced far enough to check the next dependency.

The tutorial asks for two separate objects:

- **Worker Supply Rack:** the input side of the production loop;
- **Worker Product Rack:** the destination for completed output.

I place the Supply Rack beside the growing area and keep the Product Rack on a
clear side where completed boxes can be removed without blocking the Worker's
path.

The Staffing Hub is easier to find by following the tutorial marker than by
searching locked rooms. Once the first Botanical Intern appears, I hire and fund
that one Worker before expanding the setup.

<h2 id="assign-task">
  How I assign a Botanist task that actually starts
</h2>

Hiring and funding the Botanist are not enough. Open Worker Management, select
the Worker, and assign a task that matches the exact strain supplied to the
route.

For the first setup, I used **Pack Up Mermaid Lagoon**.

The name initially sounded like a packaging-only task. Once the complete route
was available, the Botanist also planted the strain, waited for it to grow, and
continued toward the packed output.

My first stable setup is:

> **One Botanist, one strain, one Supply Rack route, and one clear Product Rack.**

I begin with one task. After one complete cycle reaches the Product Rack, I add
more. A queue showing **5/5** means the Worker has assigned work; it does not
prove that the matching strain, supplies, equipment, and output space are
available.

<h2 id="supplies-and-equipment">
  Supplies and equipment the first Botanist needs
</h2>

For the Mermaid Lagoon route, I keep these inputs available:

- an unused Mermaid Lagoon Seed Pack;
- unused Soil Packs;
- Watering Cans with water;
- Plastic Packs for the packing step.

The supplies must be unpacked and placed in the Worker input setup. A Seed Pack
left inside a Store delivery box beside the rack does not count as a usable
Worker input.

The production side also needs:

- empty Weed Pots;
- an active Light covering those Pots;
- the Weed packing station;
- available Product Rack space;
- a clear walking route between each step.

I do not fill the Supply Rack with several strains during the first setup.
Matching one task to one strain removes the easiest source of ambiguity.

<h2 id="follow-status-chain">
  Follow the status chain instead of resetting the Worker
</h2>

A Botanist often reveals one dependency at a time.

A typical troubleshooting chain can look like:

```text
Task assigned
→ matching Seed and Soil available
→ Pot available
→ waiting for plant to grow
→ Plastic Pack required
→ Product Rack required
→ completed output
```

Fixing the Seed does not guarantee immediate output. It allows the Worker to
reach the Soil, Pot, growth, packaging, or output check.

After every change, I reopen Management and read the new Current Status. I do
not keep changing several parts of the room at once because that makes it
impossible to know which correction worked.

<h3 id="task-queue-full">
  The task queue is 5/5, but the Botanist is still idle
</h3>

The queue count only proves that tasks exist.

It does not prove that:

- the supplied Seed matches the task;
- Soil is available;
- a usable Watering Can is present;
- Plastic Packs are available;
- a Pot or packing station is free;
- the Product Rack has space.

In my setup, a full queue still stalled until the correct Mermaid Lagoon route
had its matching supplies.

<h2 id="no-available-equipment">
  Fix No Available Pot, packing station, or output space
</h2>

When the Botanist appears to have every material but still cannot continue, I
check the physical equipment in this order:

1. **Unpacked:** the Pot, station, or rack is not still inside a Store box.
2. **Idle:** the machine or Pot is not in the middle of another incompatible action.
3. **Unoccupied:** a player or another Worker is not using it.
4. **Reachable:** boxes, racks, or furniture are not blocking the walking route.
5. **Relevant:** the equipment belongs to the strain and task currently assigned.
6. **Output available:** the Product Rack has room for the completed box.

Adding more Seeds cannot fix an occupied Pot. Adding more Pots cannot fix a full
Product Rack. The Current Status should determine which side of the route needs
attention.

<h2 id="do-not-take-over">
  Why I stop touching the Worker's assigned equipment
</h2>

A Botanist can yield when I act as if I am taking over the same job.

That creates a misleading loop:

1. I see the Worker standing still.
2. I plant, water, harvest, or pack the assigned batch myself.
3. The Worker continues waiting because I am now performing that step.
4. I conclude that the AI is broken and keep taking over.

After the balance, task, supplies, and equipment pass, I step away from the
assigned Pot and station long enough for the Worker to resume.

I use that time to:

- assemble Weapons;
- refill Printers;
- sort customer boxes;
- complete clean Orders;
- collect payment.

In co-op, one player can maintain Worker inputs while the other handles Orders.
Both players repeatedly interacting with the same assigned equipment makes the
status harder to interpret.

<h2 id="product-rack">
  How I use the Worker Product Rack
</h2>

The Supply Rack feeds the Botanist. The Product Rack is the end of the route.

I keep the Product Rack clear enough that a completed box is immediately
visible. When output appears, I move it to:

- the Order Delivery Elevator when a matching customer is ready;
- a known storage position when waiting for an Order;
- a separate quality-check area when the grade is unclear.

A clear Product Rack helps distinguish three different situations:

- the Worker has not produced anything;
- the Worker completed the batch and is waiting for collection;
- the output area is blocking the next cycle.

Before assigning a Worker-produced box to a customer, I still verify its product,
strain, quantity, and quality. Automation removes labor; it does not remove the
delivery checks covered in the{" "}

I use the least destructive fixes first:

1. remove the Seed Pack from its Store delivery box;
2. place one unused Pack directly on the Worker Supply Rack input;
3. confirm that I am using the Supply Rack rather than the Product Rack;
4. finish the current tablet interaction and place the item again;
5. save, return to the main menu, and reload.

A reload did not repair every tutorial state in my route. More importantly, the
Botanist could still accept tasks and produce afterward.

When Worker Management works but one tutorial checkbox remains stuck, I do not
fire the Worker, buy duplicate racks, or spend **12,000 U-Notes** on another
hire solely to clear that instruction.

I only consider an earlier save when:

- Worker Management never becomes usable;
- no task can be assigned;
- the Botanist cannot consume any valid input;
- the problem survives a normal reload.

<h2 id="when-worker-is-worth-it">
  When the first Botanist is worth hiring
</h2>

The first Botanist is useful when manual Weed repetition is preventing progress
elsewhere.

I fund one when:

- the assigned strain has repeat customer demand;
- Seeds, Soil, water, and Plastic Packs can be restocked reliably;
- the growing and packing area has a clear route;
- the Product Rack can be kept organized;
- the freed time will be used for Weapons, Orders, Acid Disposal, or another
  active system.

I wait before adding another Worker when:

- the first Botanist still stops because inputs are missing;
- the Product Rack is already difficult to manage;
- the daily balance is close to empty;
- cash is needed for essential production equipment;
- current Orders do not consume the additional output.

The **300-U-Note daily fee** is known, but the current route does not establish a
universal payback time. The practical value is the player time released for
another profitable activity.

Higher Botanist tiers cost more and should improve a route that already works;
they do not repair a missing task, empty Supply Rack, occupied Pot, or full
Product Rack.

<h2 id="faq">
  Dirty Business Botanist FAQ
</h2>

### Does Pack Up only make the Botanist bag finished Weed?

No in the working Level 15 route. With the correct strain, supplies, and
equipment available, the Botanist also planted and waited through the growing
stage before packing the output.

### Is Waiting For The Plant To Grow an error?

No. It is a valid waiting state. Check that the crop has light, water, and
visible growth progress, then leave the Botanist alone.

### Why is the task queue 5/5 while the Botanist is idle?

The queue only shows assigned tasks. It does not confirm matching Seeds, Soil,
Watering Cans, Plastic Packs, usable equipment, or Product Rack space.

### Should I fire a Botanist who is not moving?

Check the existing Worker first. The first tutorial hire was free, while the
repeat hire displayed 12,000 U-Notes.

### Can I manually help the Botanist?

Avoid taking over the exact Pot or station the Worker is trying to use. Handle
restocking, Orders, Weapons, and output collection instead.

### How much money should I add to the first Botanist?

The first Botanical Intern charged 300 U-Notes per day. I started with 1,500,
which covered five daily fees, but that is a practical test balance rather than
a required minimum.

### Does this page cover Gunsmiths, Chemists, and Counterfeiters?

No. This page focuses on the Level 15 Botanist and first Worker setup. Other
Worker roles can use the same status-first diagnostic method, but their inputs,
equipment, and normal waiting states are different.
