Reputation becomes the main progression gate in Dirty Business. Cash can be
recovered through another production cycle, but a locked Worker tier, tutorial,
Company, LSD system, or aircraft interaction still requires the correct
Reputation Level.

The most consistent route is not buying every new Store item. It is keeping
compatible production lines active, delivering products with the correct
quantity and quality, and spending only when an unlock removes the bottleneck
that is currently stopping completed orders.

<h2 id="how-reputation-works">
  How Orders turn into Reputation
</h2>

Customer Orders are the most controllable Reputation source in the early and
midgame. The reliable sequence is:

1. produce the requested item;
2. match the requested quantity and quality;
3. close and identify each box;
4. assign the boxes in the Delivery screen;
5. check the penalty preview;
6. complete the clean batch and collect the result.

One Murkwood OG order sample showed:

- **1 Pack: 130 U-Notes and 3 Reputation**;
- **2 Packs: 260 U-Notes and 6 Reputation**;
- **3 Packs: 390 U-Notes and 9 Reputation**.

A clean group of orders using the same sample finished at **1,040 U-Notes and
24 Reputation** with **0% Penalties**.

### When I use large and small orders

I prefer larger compatible orders when the stock is already known and packed,
because they reduce repeated trips between production, Packing Tables, the
elevator, and the tablet.

I still accept a small order when it:

- clears an awkward leftover box;
- uses a product that would otherwise sit idle;
- completes the current Reputation Level;
- does not delay a larger clean batch.

I wait when the requested quality is unclear, the product is scattered across
unknown boxes, or the Delivery preview shows a penalty.

<h2 id="levels-1-5">
  Levels 1–5: keep the Weed loop small and repeatable
</h2>

Before Level 5, the goal is not maximum floor coverage. It is a short loop that
can repeatedly create clean orders:

1. grow one available strain;
2. harvest and pack it;
3. activate only orders supported by current stock;
4. deliver without penalties;
5. restock Seeds, Soil, Watering Cans, Plastic Packs, and boxes.

I avoid filling the room with Pots or buying every optional expansion at this
stage. Level 5 introduces the first major second product line, so cash and floor
space are more useful when they remain available for that tutorial.

In one progression route, Level 5 was reached with about **5,000 U-Notes**
available. The tutorial supplied its starting Weapon equipment in that session,
so I waited for the tutorial order before spending money on the adjacent room.

The working cycle is:

1. water and check the active Weed batch;
2. refill the Weapon Printer;
3. assemble finished parts;
4. pack the product that already has a clean order;
5. return to whichever line is about to stop.

I add another Printer only when Carbon, Polymer, Alloy, assembly space, and order
demand can keep it active. An empty second Printer adds capacity on paper but
does not shorten the route to the next level.

### Level 10: storage before speed when boxes are the problem

Storage Racks unlock at **Level 10**.

In one co-op route, a Double LED Light cost **1,800 U-Notes** and showed a
**35% growth-speed** increase. That did not automatically make it the correct
purchase. When full boxes, empty boxes, filaments, and packing supplies had no
fixed location, storage removed more wasted movement than another growth-speed
upgrade.

I buy the Light first when the room is organized and the production line is
mostly waiting for plants.

I buy storage first when:

- full and empty boxes are mixed together;
- the same boxes are repeatedly reopened;
- the elevator route is blocked;
- supplies disappear into floor clutter.

<h2 id="levels-22-30">
  Levels 22–30: keep every long process moving
</h2>

The next major production system does not arrive until Level 30, so this section
is mainly about removing idle time.

The stable combination is:

- a Botanical Worker maintains one Weed strain;
- Weapon production runs whenever materials are available;
- Acid Disposal fills gaps between the two product lines;
- clean customer batches are submitted as soon as compatible stock is ready.

My action priority is:

1. collect completed output before racks or tables fill;
2. refill any production station that is completely stopped;
3. complete clean orders already supported by stock;
4. refill the Worker route before its current tasks run out;
5. start optional experiments only after the proven lines are moving.

I do not stand beside one slow machine. When Weed is growing, I move to Weapons.
When a Printer is running, I check packing and Orders. When the Worker route is
supplied, I use the free time for Acid Disposal or inventory cleanup.

<h2 id="levels-38-100">
  Levels 38–100: turn the base into a volume operation
</h2>

The current material does not support one verified fastest product from Level 38
to 100. The reliable strategy is to keep several counters and production lines
moving together:

- automate a stable Weed route;
- keep Weapon inputs and assembly supplied;
- use LSD only after its complete processing chain is stable;
- add Companies and aircraft work without allowing the original base to stop;
- prioritize clean Orders already supported by inventory;
- expand the station that has a visible queue instead of buying equal numbers of
  every machine.

<h2 id="buy-now-or-wait">
  Buy the unlock now or wait?
</h2>

I buy immediately when an unlock removes the current blocker:

- Storage when boxes are causing order mistakes;
- a Worker when manual Weed work prevents every other task;
- a Gunsmith when completed Weapon parts are waiting;
- another Distiller when Slug Extract is accumulating;
- more Slug capacity when the Distiller is waiting;
- a stronger Worker when a fully supplied route is limited by movement or
  efficiency.

I wait when the purchase only adds unsupported capacity:

- another Pot without Soil, Seeds, water, and Light coverage;
- another Printer without Carbon, Polymer, Alloy, and assembly capacity;
- another Worker without tasks, balance, and rack supplies;
- a Dealer without repeatable surplus stock;
- a Large Slug Tank while Extract is already queued;
- a new product when existing clean Orders are ready to ship.

The correct purchase converts waiting time into completed output. It is not
automatically the newest item in the Store.

<h2 id="solo-coop">
  Solo and co-op leveling differences
</h2>

In solo, the Level 15 Worker is more valuable because it protects one production
line while the player handles Weapons, Acid, Orders, or LSD. I keep the number
of active manual systems lower until that first automated route works reliably.

In co-op, two players can divide Weed and Weapons earlier, but tutorial purchases
and shared cash need coordination. Before one player orders a second Printer,
extra Tank, or room, both players should confirm that the next required tutorial
purchase is still funded.

The progression priorities remain the same in both modes:

1. keep inputs supplied;
2. prevent output from blocking the station;
3. submit clean compatible Orders;
4. spend on the current bottleneck.

<h2 id="faq">
  Dirty Business leveling and Reputation FAQ
</h2>

### What is the most consistent way to gain Reputation?

Use clean customer Orders supported by products already in stock, and keep
different production timers running in parallel. The current data does not prove
one universal fastest product by Reputation per minute.

### Do larger Orders always give more Reputation?

The Murkwood OG sample scaled from 3 Reputation for one Pack to 6 for two and 9
for three. Check the displayed reward because other products and qualities may
use different values.

### Do penalties reduce Reputation as well as money?

Yes in the captured delivery comparison. The wrong-quality batch finished at 18
Reputation instead of the 24-Reputation clean sample and showed 33.3% Penalties.

### Should I accept only large Orders?

No. Use a small clean Order when it clears leftover stock, finishes the current
level, or prevents a product line from sitting idle.

### Is the first Botanical Intern free?

The Staffing Hub showed a Hiring Cost of 0 and a Daily Fee of 300. It still
needs balance, supplies, tasks, and Product Rack space.

### Is the Level 32 Botanical Recruit automatically better value?

No. It costs 18,000 to hire and 420 per day, compared with 0 and 300 for the
Intern. Upgrade when the functioning route is limited by Worker speed rather
than missing inputs or space.

### Does reaching Level 38 immediately give me a working aircraft?

No. Level 38 opens the next access check, but the current tutorial and location
setup still have to be completed.

### What level does Cash Forging unlock?

The available Early Access screens conflict between Level 40 and Level 48. Check
the current Progression or Tutorial UI in the installed build before planning
the purchase around one fixed level.
