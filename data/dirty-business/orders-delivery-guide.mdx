Dirty Business customer Orders are not completed just by placing a closed box on
an elevator. The physical box must also be assigned to the correct customer in
Delivery, marked for delivery, included in the selected batch, and followed by
payment collection.

The full customer-order chain is:

**Activate Order → Pack Product → Close Box → Order Delivery Elevator → Assign Box in Delivery → Mark for Delivery → Check Penalty → Complete Selected Orders → Collect Payment**

<h2 id="order-errors">
  Dirty Business order errors at a glance
</h2>

Most failed deliveries come from one of three layers:

1. the physical product or box does not match;
2. the box is on the elevator but not assigned in Delivery;
3. the delivery is complete but payment is still waiting.

<h2 id="complete-one-order">
  How I complete one Order step by step
</h2>

I learned the interface with one active Order and one known box. Once that full
chain worked, batching customers became much easier because every failed step
had a clear place to check.

<h3 id="check-order-requirements">
  1. Check product, strain, quality, condition, and quantity
</h3>

Before activating the customer, read every requirement shown on the Order card.

For Weed, check:

- the exact strain;
- the requested quality;
- the required number of Packs.

For Weapons, check:

- the exact Weapon type;
- quantity;
- whether any item is Deformed or otherwise penalized.

For Mixed Orders, treat every line as a separate requirement. Supplying the Weed
portion does not satisfy the Weapon portion, and the Order can continue to show
Missing Packs until every line is assigned.

<h3 id="pack-and-close-box">
  2. Pack the product and close the box
</h3>

Loose Packs are not a completed delivery box.

Place the correct items into an empty box, verify the count, and close it before
moving it to the delivery platform. During the early tutorial, one box could
hold up to **six Packs**, but use the amount required by the active Order instead
of treating a full box as the default.

While learning the interface, keep one product, one strain, and one quality in
each box. This makes the box card and penalty preview much easier to trust.

<h3 id="use-order-delivery-elevator">
  3. Use the correct elevator
</h3>

Dirty Business has two elevators that are easy to confuse:

- **Store Elevator:** receives machines, supplies, packaging, and other Store purchases;
- **Order Delivery Elevator:** holds closed boxes ready for customer assignment.

  </figcaption>
</figure>

Putting the box on the correct platform makes it available to Delivery, but it
does not assign it automatically.

<h3 id="assign-box-to-customer">
  4. Drag the box card onto the customer
</h3>

Open Delivery and find the card representing the box on the elevator.

Drag that box card onto the matching customer Order. This is the step that
separates physical placement from UI assignment.

<figure className="my-8">
  <Image
    src="/images/dirty-business/dirty-business-delivery-drag-box-to-order.webp"
    alt="Dirty Business Delivery screen dragging an elevator box card onto a customer order"
    width={1280}
    height={720}
    className="h-auto w-full rounded-xl"
  />
  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">
    A correct box can sit on the elevator and still count as missing until its
    card is assigned to the intended customer.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

If the customer still shows Missing Packs after the drag, inspect the assignment
and box contents before returning to production.

<h3 id="mark-for-delivery">
  5. Mark the Order for delivery
</h3>

Once the assigned box satisfies the Order, select **Mark for Delivery**.

That status prepares the customer for the outgoing batch. It does not finish the
selected Orders or collect the money.

<h3 id="check-penalty">
  6. Check the penalty before completing the batch
</h3>

Look at the penalty summary before pressing the final completion button.

A clean batch should show:

```text
0% Penalties
```

If the value is above zero, isolate the selected Order causing it before
submitting the batch.

<h3 id="complete-and-collect">
  7. Complete Selected Orders, then collect payment
</h3>

After the selected Orders show the expected boxes and penalty result, choose
**Complete Selected Orders**.

<figure className="my-8">
  <Image
    src="/images/dirty-business/dirty-business-complete-selected-orders.webp"
    alt="Dirty Business Delivery screen showing the Complete Selected Orders action after marking customer orders"
    width={1280}
    height={720}
    className="h-auto w-full rounded-xl"
  />
  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">
    Mark for Delivery prepares the batch. Complete Selected Orders is the next
    action that actually finishes the selected customer deliveries.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

Payment is a separate state. If the products disappear but the balance does not
change, check **Collect Payment** before producing another batch.

<h2 id="missing-packs">
  Why Missing Packs appears
</h2>

`Missing Packs` does not always mean the base lacks enough products. It means
the active customer cannot count every required Pack from the boxes currently
assigned to that Order.

<figure className="my-8">
  <Image
    src="/images/dirty-business/dirty-business-missing-packs-order-error.webp"
    alt="Dirty Business customer order showing the Missing Packs error in the Delivery screen"
    width={1280}
    height={720}
    className="h-auto w-full rounded-xl"
  />
  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">
    Missing Packs is often an assignment or matching problem, not a reason to
    produce more stock immediately.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

I diagnose it in this order.

<h3 id="missing-packs-unassigned">
  1. The box is on the elevator but not assigned
</h3>

Open Delivery and verify that the box card is visibly attached to the intended
customer. Physical placement and customer assignment are separate actions.

<h3 id="missing-packs-reserved">
  2. Another active Order is confusing the assignment
</h3>

When several customers are active, it becomes harder to see which box is meant
for which request.

Remove unrelated active Orders and test the stuck customer with one known box.
If the error clears, the problem was assignment overlap or inventory confusion
rather than production.

<h3 id="missing-packs-product">
  3. The product or strain is wrong
</h3>

A box containing the correct number of Packs can still fail if the customer
requested another Weed strain or product category.

Compare the customer card with the packed item name instead of relying on box
position or color.

<h3 id="missing-packs-quality">
  4. The quality is wrong
</h3>

A Medium Quality Order is not cleanly satisfied by Low Quality Weed.

<figure className="my-8">
  <Image
    src="/images/dirty-business/dirty-business-medium-quality-order-requirement.webp"
    alt="Dirty Business customer order requiring Medium Quality Weed"
    width={1280}
    height={720}
    className="h-auto w-full rounded-xl"
  />
  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">
    Quantity alone is not enough. The packed Weed must also match the requested
    strain and quality.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

Keep differently graded products in separate boxes until their production-side
quality rules are clear. This page treats quality as a delivery requirement
rather than guessing that one Lamp, Soil, or Fertilizer always creates a fixed
tier.

<h3 id="missing-packs-mixed-order">
  5. A Mixed Order still has another missing line
</h3>

A customer requesting two product types can continue to show Missing Packs even
when one line is fully supplied.

Expand the Order card and verify every product line separately.

<h2 id="penalties">
  Quality, Deformed Weapons, and delivery penalties
</h2>

A delivery can be accepted while still losing money and Reputation.

In one Murkwood OG comparison:

- a clean batch returned **1,040 U-Notes and 24 Reputation** with **0% Penalties**;
- a wrong-quality batch returned **842.40 U-Notes and 18 Reputation** with **33.3% Penalties**;
- two mismatched Orders each showed a **-50%** result.

<figure className="my-8">
  <Image
    src="/images/dirty-business/dirty-business-order-penalty-zero.webp"
    alt="Dirty Business clean delivery summary showing 1040 U-Notes, 24 Reputation, and zero penalties"
    width={1280}
    height={720}
    className="h-auto w-full rounded-xl"
  />
  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">
    The zero-penalty summary is the safest final check before submitting the
    selected customer batch.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

That comparison is why I do not send the wrong quality simply to clear floor
space. The batch can lose both cash and progression.

A Deformed Weapon follows the same decision logic. If Delivery accepts it with a
penalty, compare the displayed loss with the value of reprinting it. A product
being technically accepted does not make it a clean delivery.

<h3 id="isolate-penalty">
  How I isolate the Order causing a penalty
</h3>

When the batch shows a penalty above zero:

1. remove the most recently selected Order;
2. check whether the penalty returns to zero;
3. if it does, inspect only that customer's product, quality, condition, and quantity;
4. if it does not, restore it and remove the next selected Order;
5. repeat until the bad Order is isolated.

This is faster than reopening every box in the room.

<h2 id="bug-or-player-error">
  Is the delivery stuck because of a bug or an assignment mistake?
</h2>

Treat it as an assignment mistake first when:

- the box is visible but not attached to the customer;
- another active Order may be confusing the same stock;
- strain, quality, condition, or quantity does not match;
- the previous batch has not been completed or paid;
- the wrong elevator is being used.

Start considering a session or synchronization issue when:

- the host and second player see different customer or box states;
- an assigned box was moved and cannot be reassigned;
- the platform is physically empty but still reported as blocked;
- the same correct setup works only after reopening the tablet.

My recovery order is:

1. let the host control Delivery;
2. remove and reassign the box;
3. clear the elevator platform;
4. close and reopen the tablet;
5. save and reload the session;
6. confirm the game is updated before reporting the issue.

Do not begin by rebuilding the product. Reproducing the same correct box will
not fix a stale assignment state.

<h2 id="elevator-and-payment">
  Fix Elevator Isn't Clear and missing payment
</h2>

<h3 id="elevator-isnt-clear">
  Elevator Isn't Clear
</h3>

Clear both the platform and the surrounding interaction area.

Check for:

- a closed delivery box;
- an empty Store box;
- loose packaging or products;
- a player standing inside the platform;
- a box still attached to an unresolved delivery;
- a Store delivery waiting on the other elevator.

After removing the obstruction, step away from the elevator and reopen the
relevant interface.

<h3 id="completed-no-money">
  The Order completed, but I received no money
</h3>

Check **Collect Payment** before repeating the Order.

1. the Order is no longer waiting in the selected batch;
2. Collect Payment is available;
3. the balance changes after collection.

<h2 id="box-rules">
  The reliable box rule I use for early deliveries
</h2>

For clean early deliveries, use one clearly identified box per active customer.
Keep one product, strain, and quality in that box, assign it only after the
customer is active, and do not move it again before completion.

This avoids ambiguous split-box and excess-quantity behavior while the
interface is still unfamiliar. Multi-box and multi-customer splitting may
behave differently across Early Access builds, so I do not rely on it for clean
Reputation batches.

<h2 id="box-organization">
  The box layout I use
</h2>

A simple storage map makes the Delivery screen easier to trust:

```text
Left Rack
→ Low Quality Weed

Middle Rack
→ Medium or higher Quality Weed

Right Rack
→ Weapons and other finished products

Floor beside Order Delivery Elevator
→ Closed boxes for the current outgoing batch only

Separate rejected area
→ Deformed, wrong-quality, or unidentified products
```

Keep incoming Store purchases out of the outbound area. After a delivery wave,
clear both elevators before opening another large Store shipment or customer
batch.

<h2 id="coop-orders">
  The co-op Order routine I recommend
</h2>

In co-op, one player should own the tablet and Delivery screen for the current
batch.

A clean division is:

- **Player 1:** activates customers, assigns boxes, checks penalties, completes
  the batch, and collects payment;
- **Player 2:** counts products, packs boxes, closes them, and places them on the
  Order Delivery Elevator.

Both players can help production, but they should not independently activate
customers or move already assigned boxes.

Tutorial progress can also be player-specific. If one player's tutorial object
is active, the other player should avoid taking over the same machine or box
until the step registers.

<h2 id="faq">
  Dirty Business Customer Orders FAQ
</h2>

### Why does Dirty Business say Missing Packs when the box is on the elevator?

The box must also be assigned to the correct customer in Delivery. Then verify
product, strain, quality, and quantity.

### Which elevator is used for customer Orders?

Use the **Order Delivery Elevator**. The Store Elevator is for items purchased
from the Store.

### Is Mark for Delivery the final step?

No. Check the penalty, use **Complete Selected Orders**, and then collect the
payment.

### Why did the products disappear without adding money?

Check **Collect Payment** before repeating the Order; delivery and payment
collection are separate steps.

### Do delivery penalties reduce Reputation?

They did in the compared Murkwood OG batches. The clean result showed 24
Reputation, while the wrong-quality batch showed 18 Reputation and 33.3%
Penalties.

### Can I deliver the wrong quality?

Delivery may accept it with a penalty. That can reduce both U-Notes and
Reputation, so wait for the correct quality unless the displayed tradeoff is
intentional.

### What does the red X do?

It removed an activated Order during the early route. Do not treat it as a send
or completion button. Its cancellation consequences still need a separate
controlled test.

### Can one box satisfy multiple customers?

For reliable early deliveries, use one identifiable box per active customer.
Do not depend on split-box behavior for a clean progression batch.

### What should I do when everything matches but Delivery still does not work?

Let the host reassign the box, clear the elevator, reopen the tablet, and reload
the session before producing another box.
