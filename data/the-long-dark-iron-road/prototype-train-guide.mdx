<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
Unlock the locomotive with the **Prototype Train Key** and check its Whale Oil and Water. Light the firebox, build at least **25 PSI**, then release the brake and select **Slow gear** to begin moving.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

The Prototype Train runs on Whale Oil and Water, with steam pressure controlling how long it can maintain speed. Once you unlock it in Track Section Six, the first challenge is getting the engine moving. Farther along the railway, managing pressure becomes a trade-off between clearing heavy obstructions and preserving enough speed control to avoid damaged tracks.

<section id="first-start">

## How Do You Start the Prototype Train?

The Prototype Train is housed in the **Train Shelter in Anna Island Track Section Six**.

You must obtain the **Prototype Train Key** before unlocking the locomotive. The key, Alice McLeod's Journal 01, and nearby recordings are found during the Train Shelter investigation.

If you have not reached that location, follow the [Anna Island walkthrough](/the-long-dark-iron-road/anna-island-walkthrough) through the lighthouse, church, Track Section Five, and the rope climb into Section Six.

You do not need to build the locomotive from scratch. Once it is unlocked, the immediate task is to check its condition and start the engine.

### Check the train's Whale Oil and Water

The locomotive uses two large reservoirs:

- **Whale Oil Tank:** 500 L maximum capacity.
- **Water Tank:** 600 L maximum capacity.

Whale Oil powers the firebox. Water is required for steam generation. Having plenty of one does not compensate for running out of the other.

Open **Train Status** before starting a long journey.

![Train Status showing the Whale Oil and Water reservoirs and the condition of the Brake Shoes, Wheels, Boiler, and Cowcatcher.](/images/the-long-dark-iron-road/prototype-train-fuel-water.webp)

*Train Status shows both reservoirs and all four major components. Check these before leaving a long railway section.*

The example status screen shows **390.38 L of Whale Oil** and **541.23 L of Water** remaining, with the Boiler at 81% and Cowcatcher at 92%.

These are the amounts remaining in that particular train, not guaranteed starting quantities for a new Survival run.

Check your own reservoirs instead of assuming the locomotive is completely full.

### Light the firebox and build pressure

After checking the tanks, enter the locomotive's driving compartment and start the firebox.

The key pressure values in the initial train configuration are:

- **Below 25 PSI:** Not enough pressure to drive.
- **25 PSI or higher:** The locomotive can begin moving.
- **300 PSI:** Normal operating pressure ceiling.
- **Up to 400 PSI:** Overpressure range with additional Boiler risk.

The working firebox generates approximately **7 PSI per second** under normal operating conditions.

If you try to move immediately after lighting it, the train may appear unresponsive. Look at the pressure gauge first.

You only need 25 PSI to begin moving, but a larger reserve is useful before a difficult obstacle.

### Release the brake and select a gear

![Prototype Train driving controls showing the brake engaged, neutral gear, and steam-pressure gauges.](/images/the-long-dark-iron-road/prototype-train-controls.webp)

*This control panel shows Brake ON and Neutral. The train will not begin ordinary forward travel until the controls are changed.*

Once pressure is sufficient:

1. Release the brake.
2. Move the gear selector out of Neutral and into Slow.
3. Allow the train to accelerate.
4. Watch the track ahead and the pressure gauge.

If the train still refuses to move, check these conditions before assuming it is broken.

| What you see | What to check |
|---|---|
| Firebox lit, train will not move | Pressure may still be below 25 PSI |
| Pressure available, train stationary | Brake may still be ON |
| Brake released, no forward movement | Gear selector may be in Neutral |
| Pressure falls rapidly while driving | Fast gear is consuming steam faster than it is generated |
| Train stops at an obstruction | The impact may require more speed or a different interaction |

There is no benefit in repeatedly accelerating against an obstacle when the train lacks sufficient pressure or the brake is still engaged.

<section id="pressure-gears">

## How Do Slow Gear and Fast Gear Affect Steam Pressure?

The two driving gears serve different purposes.

**Slow gear** is for ordinary travel and preserving steam. **Fast gear** is for reaching higher speeds when an obstruction requires a hard impact.

In the initial train configuration, the firebox generates approximately 7 PSI per second while running.

Slow gear consumes **5 PSI per second**, leaving a positive pressure balance of approximately **2 PSI per second** until the normal pressure ceiling is reached.

Fast gear consumes **14 PSI per second**, so pressure falls at a net rate of approximately **7 PSI per second** while the engine continues producing steam.

That difference is significant.

Starting from 300 PSI, you have 275 PSI available before falling below the 25-PSI driving threshold. At a net loss of 7 PSI per second, that represents roughly **39 seconds** of Fast-gear operation.

This is a pressure calculation, not a guaranteed amount of travel time or distance under every condition.

### Use Slow gear for ordinary railway travel

Slow gear reaches a maximum speed of **8** in the train's speed system.

It is sufficient for normal travel and can also reach the speed required for medium-impact obstructions.

Its main advantage is that steam pressure can recover while you drive.

Use Slow gear when:

- The railway ahead is clear.
- You are approaching a junction.
- Visibility is poor.
- You need to preserve pressure for an upcoming obstacle.
- You expect to stop at a refill station or maintenance location.

Selecting Slow does not automatically make an already fast-moving train safe for damaged rails. Check the actual speed and brake early enough to slow down.

### Use Fast gear for heavy obstructions

Fast gear is mainly useful when an obstruction requires **speed 12 or higher**. Build steam pressure before accelerating, then switch back to Slow after the impact.

Because Fast gear consumes steam faster than the firebox replaces it, sustained use can leave the locomotive short of pressure before the next obstruction.

### Use Neutral and the brake deliberately

Neutral is useful when you want the train to coast rather than continue demanding driving power.

The Brake Shoes wear when you use the brake, so avoid unnecessary hard braking after every minor speed change.

However, coasting is not a substitute for slowing down before a dangerous rail section.

If you can see a railway switch, maintenance stop, damaged track, or blind turn ahead, begin reducing speed while you still have room to stop.

The important balance is to avoid both excessive braking and arriving at a hazard too quickly.

</section>

<section id="obstacles">

## How Do You Clear Snow, Gates, and Railway Switches?

Not every obstruction should be handled in the same way.

The train can break some obstacles through impact. Others require a railway switch, a different approach, or an interaction away from the locomotive.

### Snow drifts

Ordinary snow drifts can be cleared at low speed.

They reduce the train's speed slightly, but do not normally justify building a large pressure reserve just for the collision.

There is no advantage in using Fast gear against every patch of snow.

### Medium-impact obstacles

A medium-impact obstacle requires approximately **speed 7**.

Slow gear can reach this threshold.

In the initial configuration, a medium collision damages the Cowcatcher by **2 condition points** and reduces the train's speed.

Approach with enough speed to clear the obstruction, then continue in Slow gear if the next section is safe.

### Heavy-impact obstacles

Larger obstructions may require **speed 12 or higher**.

This requires Fast gear.

A heavy collision damages the Cowcatcher by **4 condition points** in the initial configuration.

If the obstruction does not break, do not repeatedly strike it at the same insufficient speed.

Reverse far enough to obtain a proper approach, rebuild steam pressure, and accelerate again only when the obstruction is intended to be broken by impact.

### How do railway switches work?

Railway switches direct the train between different branches of the track.

They are especially important near maintenance buildings, refueling facilities, and the connection toward the next island.

When approaching a junction:

1. Slow the locomotive and stop before passing the switch.
2. Inspect the switch lever and the track alignment.
3. Move the switch to select the intended branch.
4. Return to the locomotive and approach slowly.
5. Check that the train follows the correct track before accelerating.

The switch indicator shows the branch selected. Confirm the physical rail alignment rather than assuming the lever always points in the direction you want to travel.

At the **Track Section Four** maintenance area, a switch can direct the train into the Repair Depot.

You may need to pass the junction, reverse, or reposition the locomotive to enter the service track correctly.

Do not rush this maneuver. Driving too far past the selected branch can leave you needing to reverse and make the approach again.

### How do you enter the Track Section Four Repair Depot?

The Repair Depot is reached from a railway branch rather than the main track.

At the junction, stop and select the branch leading into the maintenance building.

Then carefully move the train onto the service track.

The surrounding railway also includes a route toward the **next island connection**. That connection is not the same as the maintenance branch and should not be confused with the main route around Anna Island.

The initial Early Access release does not provide access to the next playable island.

If the game tells you the next island will unlock in a later update, you have reached the current content boundary, not a normal snow obstruction.

Return to the available railway route rather than attempting to force the train through it.

<section id="broken-track">

## How Do You Recognize and Repair Damaged Railway Tracks?

Damaged railway is one of the most expensive hazards because it affects the **Wheels**.

A collision can slow the train, but crossing broken rails too quickly can rapidly reduce wheel condition and make every subsequent trip more difficult.

### How can you recognize damaged track?

Inspect the rails ahead, especially after passing an obstruction or transitioning between railway sections.

Damaged track can look **rust-red, twisted, or visibly misaligned** compared with the normal rails.

A harsh scraping or grinding sound can also indicate that the train is passing over a damaged section.

Do not wait until you hear sustained grinding before slowing down.

Some sections place damaged track close to a snow obstruction or near a railway transition. Clearing the obstruction is not proof that the rails immediately beyond it are safe.

A useful approach is to stop at a safe distance, lean out to inspect the track when possible, and walk ahead if the condition is unclear.

### How fast can you cross damaged track?

The initial train configuration uses a speed-dependent wheel-damage system.

- **Below speed 7:** The damage-risk counter does not increase.
- **Medium speed:** Risk accumulates at approximately 0.7 points per second.
- **High speed:** Risk accumulates at approximately 2 points per second.
- **After the damage threshold is reached:** Wheel condition can fall rapidly while the dangerous condition continues.

At higher speeds, the accumulated risk can result in a loss of approximately **5 wheel-condition points per second**.

That makes Fast gear a poor choice for crossing damaged rails.

Slow down **below speed 7 before reaching the damaged section**.

Selecting Slow gear is not enough by itself if the train is still traveling above the safe threshold.

### Can you repair damaged rails without Scrap Metal?

Yes. Damaged track can be repaired using a **Heavy Hammer**.

The initial railway repair rule uses approximately **2 in-game hours** without consuming Scrap Metal. Check the actual repair interaction before committing, since repair time and success can depend on the situation and your skills.

Repairing the track is often more economical than repeatedly damaging your Wheels and paying for locomotive repairs.

Choose between the two options based on your situation:

- **Enough time and a Heavy Hammer:** Repair the track.
- **Unable to repair safely:** Slow down and cross carefully.
- **Already damaged Wheels:** Avoid another high-speed crossing and consider repairing the train before continuing.

### What if the rails still look damaged after repair?

A repaired track may continue to look rough or play an unpleasant sound in some early builds.

Check the actual repair state and whether the train continues losing Wheel condition before assuming the repair failed.

Do not repeatedly spend time on the same repair interaction solely because the rail appearance has not changed.

## How Do You Repair the Wheels, Cowcatcher, Boiler, and Brakes?

The Prototype Train has four major condition-managed components:

- **Wheels:** Affect movement and fuel efficiency.
- **Cowcatcher:** Takes damage when the locomotive breaks obstacles.
- **Boiler:** Required for safe steam operation.
- **Brake Shoes:** Affect the ability to slow and stop the train.

Open **Train Status** to inspect the condition of each component.

You can repair worn train components during a journey. You do not have to reach a Repair Depot every time something needs attention.

The depot is useful because it provides a sheltered maintenance stop and may contain additional equipment and supplies.

### Example: Repairing a Cowcatcher at 92%

![Cowcatcher repair screen showing 8% repair amount, 24 minutes, two Scrap Metal, and a Heavy Hammer.](/images/the-long-dark-iron-road/prototype-train-repair.webp)

*At 92% condition, the repair interface offers only the missing 8%, using 2 Scrap Metal and a Heavy Hammer.*

The interface offers only the missing condition points, so a Cowcatcher at 92% can recover just 8% rather than the full nominal 20%. The material requirement remains 2 Scrap Metal, and the repair takes 24 minutes in the example shown.

A nearly full Cowcatcher may not justify spending the materials immediately. Before repairing, compare its current condition with the number of obstructions you expect to hit on the next stretch.

### Wheels: avoid the expensive low-condition threshold

Wheel condition affects movement efficiency.

The initial performance penalties are:

- **Below 75%:** Maximum speed falls to 90%, and fuel consumption increases by 10%.
- **Below 50%:** Maximum speed falls to 75%, and fuel consumption increases by 15%.
- **At 0%:** Maximum speed falls to 50%, and fuel consumption increases by 25%.

An ordinary repair requires **1 Scrap Metal** and restores up to 20 condition points.

Below 50% condition, the repair cost increases to **2 Scrap Metal**, with a larger nominal repair amount and longer reference time.

Repair Wheels before they deteriorate into the expensive range whenever conditions allow.

Avoid repairing the Wheels and then immediately driving through damaged track at high speed. That defeats the purpose of the repair.

### Brake Shoes: replace them before stopping becomes unreliable

Brake Shoes wear whenever the brake is used.

In the initial configuration, the approximate wear rates are:

- **0.25 condition points per second** under ordinary braking.
- **0.5 condition points per second** when braking above speed 8.

When Brake Shoes reach zero condition, braking effectiveness can fall to approximately **25% of normal**.

This is dangerous when approaching damaged rails, junctions, or an obstacle that requires careful positioning.

A spare **Brake Shoe weighs 0.5 kg** and is used as a replacement rather than repaired with Scrap Metal.

Keep a replacement in the train's cabinet and inspect the brakes before entering a difficult railway section.

Use Neutral and controlled braking to reduce unnecessary wear, but never sacrifice safe stopping distance simply to preserve the component.

### Cowcatcher: plan repairs around upcoming obstacles

The Cowcatcher absorbs damage from collisions.

Medium collisions cost **2 condition points** and heavy collisions cost **4** in the initial configuration.

At low condition, the locomotive can suffer substantially greater speed loss after impact.

Normal Cowcatcher repair costs **2 Scrap Metal**. Below 50% condition, the material cost increases to **4 Scrap Metal**.

This makes unnecessary heavy collisions expensive.

When planning a repair, compare the Cowcatcher's condition with the railway ahead. A clear section does not demand the same preparation as a route with several heavy obstructions.

### Boiler: do not ignore low condition

The Boiler is the most dangerous component to leave badly damaged.

A normal repair uses **2 Scrap Metal**.

Below **25% condition**, the initial repair requirements increase to **3 Scrap Metal**, with a higher nominal condition restoration.

Repair the Boiler before using aggressive pressure settings, especially when its condition is already declining.

### What is the Repair Depot actually useful for?

The Repair Depots in **Track Sections Four and Two** are useful maintenance stops, with indoor shelter and opportunities to search for repair materials.

You can still repair the locomotive away from these buildings when you have the necessary tools and materials. Parking inside a depot does not automatically restore component condition.

The Track Section Four depot contains Wax Cylinder 10 upstairs. Track Section Two holds Cylinders 12 and 15, along with the Whale-Oil Heater. Check the [Wax Cylinder locations](/the-long-dark-iron-road/wax-cylinders) before leaving either depot.

<section id="overpressure">

## What Happens if the Train Goes Above 300 PSI?

Normal operation uses steam pressure up to approximately **300 PSI**.

The initial train rules also allow pressure to rise into an overpressure range, reaching approximately **400 PSI**.

Higher pressure can provide additional performance, but operating above 300 PSI in Fast gear puts the Boiler at greater risk.

In the initial v2.58 configuration, overpressure operation can increase maximum speed by approximately **15%**.

The Boiler also accumulates condition damage while operating in the dangerous pressure-and-gear combination.

The initial configuration includes severe failure checks once Boiler condition becomes too low:

- **Below 50% Boiler condition:** Elevated explosion risk during overpressure.
- **Below 25%:** A substantially greater failure risk.
- **At 0% Boiler condition:** Boiler failure can destroy the locomotive.

These are not normal operating targets.

The useful distinction is that pressure above 300 PSI is not itself a reason to drive faster. The dangerous situation arises when you combine elevated pressure with Fast gear and a vulnerable Boiler.

For ordinary railway travel, preserve Boiler condition and rely on Slow gear.

Even when a heavy obstruction requires Fast gear, approach it with an adequate normal pressure reserve instead of deliberately pushing the Boiler into overpressure.

Destroying the locomotive is a far greater cost than spending a little more time building safe steam pressure.

<section id="refueling">

## Where Can You Refuel the Train with Whale Oil and Water?

The locomotive consumes Whale Oil and Water while the firebox is running.

The initial consumption figures are approximately:

- **0.5 L Whale Oil per minute**
- **0.5 L Water per minute**

At those rates:

| Reservoir | Full capacity | Theoretical continuous burn |
|---|---:|---:|
| Whale Oil | 500 L | 16 hours 40 minutes |
| Water | 600 L | 20 hours |

These figures assume completely full tanks and unchanged consumption.

They are not guaranteed travel times. Long stops, damaged components, and driving conditions affect how useful the remaining resources are.

### How do you know the train is close enough to refuel?

Railway refueling stations use a visual indicator.

**When the train is correctly positioned within the station's refueling range, the indicator turns green.**

This is the most useful way to tell whether you have stopped at the right place.

When approaching a station:

1. Slow down and move alongside the refueling equipment.
2. Watch the station indicator.
3. Stop once the light turns green.
4. Engage the brake.
5. Use the station's fuel or water transfer interaction.

If the refill interaction is unavailable, do not immediately assume the station is empty or broken.

The locomotive may be slightly outside the required position. Reposition it carefully and check the indicator again.

### Which Track Sections have refueling facilities?

Whale Oil and Water facilities are distributed around the railway.

The explored route includes supply opportunities in **Track Sections One, Three, and Five**, along with the major maintenance areas.

The **Track Section Four Repair Depot** has nearby supply facilities.

Track Section Two also provides a maintenance stop with water access.

The important planning rule is to check **both** resources whenever you stop. Not every supply point should be assumed to provide both Whale Oil and Water in equal quantities.

The train can travel a considerable distance on its large reservoirs, but do not wait until one is empty before looking for the next useful station.

### Protect yourself during long refills

Refilling is not always a quick interaction.

Large transfers take in-game time, and the character remains exposed outside the locomotive while completing the process.

A long refill can be dangerous during cold weather or a blizzard, particularly on higher Survival difficulties.

Before beginning:

- Check your warmth and remaining condition.
- Prepare an outdoor fire if the expected transfer will take a long time.
- Consider filling a smaller amount instead of committing to a lengthy transfer while freezing.
- Keep enough food and water available if the refill interrupts a long expedition.

Do not assume the train's warm interior protects you during an outdoor transfer.

If you are already freezing, stabilize your condition before spending another long period operating the supply equipment.

### Can you fill portable containers at a railway station?

Some Whale Oil stations also allow fuel to be transferred into portable containers. A standard Whale Oil Can holds **5 L**, so check that the container is available before leaving the station.

That fuel can also power the [Portable Whale-Oil Heater](/the-long-dark-iron-road#whale-oil-heater), which has a much smaller tank and different consumption settings.

### Should you leave the firebox running while stopped?

The train continues using resources while the firebox is operating, even when you are not making progress along the railway.

Leaving it burning through an extended stop costs Whale Oil and Water.

However, a warm firebox also provides cabin heat and makes steam available when you are ready to depart.

For a brief stop before another obstacle, maintaining pressure may be useful.

For a long shelter stay, consider extinguishing the firebox after accounting for your remaining warmth and whether you will need to depart again soon.

<section id="mobile-shelter">

## Can You Sleep, Cook, and Store Items Inside the Train?

The Prototype Train is more than transportation.

It also provides a mobile shelter for long railway journeys.

This matters because some sections have few convenient buildings, and returning to Port Remnant on foot can be a substantial detour.

### How much warmth does the train provide?

With the firebox fully heated, the cabin can provide approximately **+20°C of warmth** in the initial configuration.

The train can also retain some heat after the firebox is extinguished.

The initial residual-heat rules allow approximately **12 minutes of stored warmth for each hour spent fully heated**, up to a maximum of roughly **3 hours**.

This does not mean that entering the cabin briefly guarantees several hours of warmth after shutdown.

The amount retained depends on how long the train was heated.

Check your character's actual temperature before attempting a long sleep with the firebox extinguished.

The train's shelter function is useful, but extreme weather and insufficient remaining heat can still make a long rest dangerous.

### What equipment is inside the cabin?

The locomotive includes:

- **A bed:** For sleeping when the train is stopped.
- **A cooking position:** Can be used with suitable cooking equipment.
- **A cupboard:** **45 kg of storage capacity**.

![Prototype Train cupboard inventory showing its 45 kg storage limit and stored supplies.](/images/the-long-dark-iron-road/prototype-train-interior.webp)

*The cupboard holds up to 45 kg. It is useful for spare Brake Shoes, Scrap Metal, food, tools, and portable supplies.*

Keep repair materials where you can reach them before the next railway section. A spare Brake Shoe, Heavy Hammer, and Scrap Metal are more useful in the cupboard than bulky supplies you are unlikely to need while driving.

The 45 kg limit makes storage decisions important on longer trips, particularly when you are carrying both survival provisions and replacement components.

### Can you sleep while the train is moving?

No. Stop the train before sleeping or using time-passing interactions.

The cabin is a convenient place to recover during a railway journey, but you still need to manage heat, fuel, and water.

A useful stopping routine is to secure the locomotive, inspect Train Status, decide whether the firebox should remain on, and then rest or organize supplies.

Before leaving again, check the brake, driving gear, pressure, and the condition of the next railway section.

</section>
