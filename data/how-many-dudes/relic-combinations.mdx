The best Relic in **How Many Dudes** is not always the card with the biggest
number.

The useful question is:

> Which choice fixes the problem most likely to end this run before the next
> boss?

If the roster deals enough damage but loses control during the opening, I buy
survivability, Taunt support or interruption. If the front line is stable but
large enemies are surviving too long, I deepen the existing carry. Once I am
close to Round 100 or Round 105, I stop paying for clever long-term ideas and
buy effects that can save the next fight immediately.

This page is therefore not a full Relic database or a universal tier list. It
covers the combinations and three-choice decisions I have actually used inside
successful five-Type routes.

<h2 id="how-i-decide">How I Decide Between Relics During a Run</h2>

Every Relic choice is tied to four checks:

1. **What failed in the previous fight?**
2. **Which boss or difficult matchup is next?**
3. **Is the roster still developing, or am I protecting a winning run?**
4. **Will this effect pay back before the run ends?**

A strong Relic can still be the wrong purchase.

If the front line is losing several Dudes before healing starts, another damage
multiplier does not fix the run. If the roster survives comfortably but spends
too long on every elite enemy, another defensive card can turn safety into a
slow attrition loss.

I also check the five posted Flyers before making a choice. A personal or
Family Relic becomes more valuable when it strengthens a Type I am still
actively recruiting. I rarely invest heavily in a Type that is only occupying
a temporary fifth slot.

<h2 id="warlock-time-package">Warlock and Time: Structure Before Greed</h2>

The Warlock / Time shell creates one of the clearest Relic decisions in the
game.

Warlock buys time through Pact and enemy attention. Time converts that safety
into repeated area damage. The package works only when I know which half is
currently weak.

### Andon Cord, Shadowy Funnel or Time Sink?

During the Warlock / Time clear, the Relic Stash offered:

- **Andon Cord**
- **Shadowy Funnel**
- **Time Sink**

#### When I take Andon Cord

Andon Cord triggers when any Dude gets Knocked Out:

- all Dudes gain **90% Toughness for 2 seconds**;
- it has a once-per-battle global cooldown.

I take it when one early Knockout is turning into five more.

The Relic does not prevent the first failure. It stops the failure from
cascading through the roster. That makes it useful when the team is generally
strong enough to recover but needs a brief window for healing, Taunt or Pact to
settle.

I value it less when the same front-line Type is being erased repeatedly. In
that case I need a permanent defensive solution rather than one emergency
pause.

#### When I take Shadowy Funnel

Shadowy Funnel gives Warlock **5% Crit Chance**. When Warlock lands a Direct
Crit, **25% of his Stored Pact damage** is expelled onto nearby enemies and
split among them.

I choose it only when two conditions are already true:

- Warlock is storing meaningful Pact damage;
- the build has enough Crit support for Direct Crits to happen reliably.

This is not the card I use to rescue a weak Warlock. It is the card I use to
turn a stable Warlock into a damage contributor.

If the opening is still collapsing, Shadowy Funnel is usually too ambitious.

#### When I take Time Sink

Time Sink triggers when a Dude Deflects:

- that Dude gains **3% Attack Speed for 5 seconds**;
- the effect stacks up to **5 times**.

Despite the name, this is not automatically a Time Dude Relic.

I take it when the roster already has enough Deflection for the trigger to
occur repeatedly. Shield Belt can make that condition much easier later. I
skip Time Sink when Deflection is nearly absent, because a strong ceiling does
not matter if the trigger rarely activates.

### Time Trigger is a separate Merchant purchase

Later in the same route, Time Trigger appeared separately in the Merchant
shop. It was not part of the earlier Relic Stash choice.

I buy Time Trigger when Time Anomalies are already doing real work.

The card is a scaling purchase. It becomes better when:

- the roster survives long enough for repeated Anomaly cycles;
- crowded fights give the stored damage useful targets;
- Time is already one of the team's main damage Types.

I do not buy it merely because Time Dude is present. If the build still needs
front-line stability or recovery, the expensive Merchant purchase can delay a
more urgent fix.

<h2 id="general-franken-package">
  General and Franken: Protect the Carry Instead of Rebuilding It
</h2>

Once a damage Type has clearly become the carry, I stop trying to reinvent the
roster.

In the Cyborg / Samurai / Assassin / Franken / General route, the late Relic
job was straightforward:

- let General make the opening more predictable;
- let Franken keep the core alive;
- add roster-wide defense;
- preserve the damage Types already winning the fights.

### Heroic Medal

Heroic Medal is a General Relic I value when Tactical Command is already part
of the front-line plan.

  </figcaption>

I take it when General is already organizing enemy attention effectively. I do
not add General late only because Heroic Medal appears.

The Relic deepens an established plan; it does not create a complete front line
from nothing.

### Shield Belt

Shield Belt gives **all Dudes 15% Deflection**.

  <Image
    src="/images/how-many-dudes/how-many-dudes-shield-belt-deflection.webp"

    receives the defensive value immediately.

This is the kind of card whose value rises as the run gets closer to the final
bosses.

Early, I may prefer a Relic that establishes the carry. Late, I would rather
reduce chip damage across the five core Types than begin another narrow scaling
engine.

Shield Belt also makes Deflection-triggered effects such as Time Sink more
credible. I still do not buy the trigger first and hope the Deflection appears
later.

### Copper Bolts

Copper Bolts gives Frankendude **30% Attack Speed**. When he deals Direct
Damage, he has a **20% chance** to gain **1% Regeneration for 3 seconds**,
stacking up to **6 times**.

If Franken is a temporary healer and the current shop must solve an immediate
boss problem, I pass. If Franken is already a core support Type, the Attack
Speed and Regeneration trigger strengthen something the roster depends on.

<h2 id="consumable-package">
  The Consumable Package I Want Near Round 100 and Round 105
</h2>

Consumables are not secondary to Relics late in the run. They often have more
value because their effect can change the next battle immediately.

My preferred final-checkpoint package is:

- Health Potion;
- Dude Juice;
- Bottled Lightning;
- Meteor.

### Health Potion

Health Potion heals the most injured half of the roster for **50% of Max HP**,
and overhealing becomes Temporary Health.

I buy it when several core Types are entering fights injured but not yet
Knocked Out. It is especially useful after a damaging elite battle when the
next important fight is too close for slow recovery.

I skip extra copies when the actual problem is missing bodies rather than low
HP. That is Dude Juice territory.

### Dude Juice

Dude Juice has different values depending on where I use it:

- at the Ranch, it revives **10 Dudes**;
- during battle, it revives **5 Dudes at full HP**.

I do not use it during battle for one random Knockout unless that exact Dude is
holding together the front line or healing engine.

I wait until the revive restores a meaningful fighting group. Between fights,
I use the Ranch version when ten recovered Dudes will return the five-Type core
to a usable state.

### Bottled Lightning

The captured version deals **11,500 damage**, splits the damage among enemies
struck and stuns them for **1 second**.

I value the stun more than the headline damage when the fight opens badly.

It gives Taunt, Pact, healing and area effects time to begin working. It is not
a guaranteed boss shutdown, so I do not enter Round 105 assuming one cast will
solve the G.O.A.T.

### Meteor

Meteor deals **300 damage** to enemies in a chosen location in the captured
card, with damage increasing each round. Struck Dudes are thrown but unharmed.

<h2 id="cave-package">Cave Painting and the High-HP Package</h2>

Cave Painting gives all Dudes **1% Max HP for every Cave Dude**, stacking up to
**15 times**.

  <Image
    src="/images/how-many-dudes/how-many-dudes-cave-painting-15-percent-max-hp.webp"
    alt="How Many Dudes Cave Painting giving one percent Max HP per Cave Dude up to fifteen stacks"
    width={536}

    className="mx-auto h-auto w-full max-w-md rounded-xl"
  />
  <figcaption className="mx-auto mt-3 max-w-md text-sm text-slate-400">
    Cave Painting is excellent after the Cave route is committed because one
    Type's recruitment depth becomes roster-wide survivability.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

I do not draft Cave Dudes solely because the Relic appeared.

I buy Cave Painting when Cave is already one of the five planned Types and I
expect to recruit enough copies for the bonus to become meaningful.

Once the team approaches the 15-stack cap, the decision changes. Another Cave
Dude no longer adds unlimited Relic value, so I return to the roster's current
problem:

- Does it need more damage?
- Is healing arriving too slowly?
- Is the next boss likely to demand a consumable?
- Are too many non-Cave support Types missing?

A scaling Relic is successful when it improves the whole route. It becomes a
trap when I keep forcing the same decision after the useful cap is already
secured.

<h2 id="purchase-timing">What I Buy Early, in the Middle, and Late</h2>

### Early: build the engine

Before Round 60, I am willing to buy:

- personal Relics for the planned carry;
- Family Relics that support several of the five Types;
- Time Trigger when Time is already becoming central;
- Cave Painting when the Cave route is committed;
- economy that can repay before the decisive corridor.

### Middle: fix the proven bottleneck

From roughly Round 60 to Round 80, I buy based on what the previous fights
showed.

If the opening is unstable, I prioritize Andon Cord, Heroic Medal, Shield Belt
or interruption.

If the roster survives but cannot close elite fights, I deepen the established
damage engine.

If the carry is strong but the support Types keep disappearing, I buy recovery
instead of another carry Relic.

### Late: protect the result

After Round 80, my rules become much stricter.

I lower the value of:

- slow economy;
- Relics for barely supported Types;
- narrow effects that need many future battles;
- a new synergy that does nothing for the next boss.

I raise the value of:

- Shield Belt;
- Health Potion;
- Dude Juice;
- Bottled Lightning;
- Meteor;
- replacements for missing core Dudes.

The closer I am to Round 100 or Round 105, the more every purchase must justify
itself immediately.

<h2 id="shop-check">My Five-Second Shop Check</h2>

When a shop opens, I use this order:

1. **Is a core Type missing too many Dudes?**
2. **Did the previous fight fail during the opening or because it lasted too
   long?**
3. **Does this Relic have a trigger the current build can activate reliably?**
4. **Will it pay back before the next boss?**
5. **Would a consumable solve the same problem more safely?**

That keeps the page useful in the middle of a run.

I do not need to remember one permanent Relic ranking. I only need to identify
the next real failure and buy the effect that addresses it without creating a
new unsupported plan.
