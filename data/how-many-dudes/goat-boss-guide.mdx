Round 100 is not the end of **How Many Dudes** Tier 1.

The real final check arrives at **Round 105**, where the G.O.A.T. enters with
**250 Rams**, a battle modifier, and enough burst damage to erase a roster that
looked comfortable one round earlier.

The exact modifier and final shop offers changed between my clears, so I do not
use one fixed-seed solution. I use the same preparation rule every time:

- repair the roster immediately after Round 100;
- use Rounds 101–104 as a protected corridor;
- buy only effects that matter before or during Round 105;
- enter with wave clear, sustained healing, a working frontline, Dude Juice,
  and at least one immediate combat item.

<h2 id="round-105-setup">What Round 105 Actually Throws at Me</h2>

The Tier 1 final battle consistently gave me the same basic problem:

- the G.O.A.T.;
- **250 Rams**;
- a modifier that can weaken the roster's normal control plan;
- repeated heavy attacks after the Rams are already disappearing.

The Rams are not harmless background enemies. They fill the field, interfere
with targeting and keep damage spread across the roster while the G.O.A.T.
prepares heavier attacks.

I therefore split the fight into two phases:

1. clear enough Rams for the roster to regroup;
2. switch fully to the G.O.A.T. after the field is under control.

### The modifier is not fixed

Across the completed clears, one Round 105 fight displayed
**Resilient / Crit Avoidance**, while another gave enemies
**25% Taunt Resistance**.

Before pressing Start, I check:

- Can I remove a large group quickly?
- Can the frontline survive while Rams are still active?
- Does healing continue through repeated damage?
- What will I use after the first meaningful collapse?
- Does the modifier weaken Taunt, Crit or another major part of this roster?

That check matters more than chasing one perfect Relic combination.

<h2 id="round-100-to-104">How I Use Rounds 101–104</h2>

Round 100 is the last major checkpoint with room to correct the run.

The Gorilla result tells me whether the damage engine works, but it does not
guarantee the final corridor is safe. Rounds 101–104 can still remove several
core Dudes, especially when the forced battles use Separator or another
unfavorable modifier.

### Restore the roster before buying a luxury Relic

My first priorities after Round 100 are:

1. identify which core Type was depleted;
2. recruit or train that Type;
3. restore Dude Juice;
4. buy immediate defense or recovery;
5. skip delayed value.

A strong damage result is not permission to start another side project. If the
carry already works, the final shops should protect it.

### The final Relic must matter immediately

A late Relic earns the purchase only when it does at least one of these:

- prevents the frontline from collapsing;
- strengthens the established carry;
- improves the healer or recovery engine;
- helps clear the Rams;
- gives value during the opening seconds.

I skip:

- economy that pays back after the run;
- a new 15-round Business investment;
- narrow Relics for unsupported Types;
- an 11-round Scavenger loan that returns after Round 105.

### Shield Belt and Heroic Medal

In one Cyborg clear, the final shop offered **Shield Belt**, **Heroic Medal**,
and a Health Potion before Round 105.

Shield Belt gives **all Dudes 15% Deflection**. Heroic Medal strengthens General
and lets Tactical Command target an additional Dude.

These are late-run structure purchases:

- Shield Belt gives the entire roster immediate defensive value;
- Heroic Medal improves a General plan that is already working;
- neither requires ten more rounds to become useful.

I do not force General into the roster because Heroic Medal appears. I buy it
when General is already one of the five core Types.

### Treat Rounds 101–104 as a protected corridor

In one clear, the final battles repeatedly used **Separator**, spawning Dudes
far apart.

A short opening delay or Stasis effect was valuable because it gave isolated
Dudes time to regroup before taking full pressure.

This is the kind of effect I want late: something that solves a problem the
next forced battle can actually create.

I do not Restock repeatedly for a theoretical perfect combination when the shop
already offers:

- opening control;
- roster-wide Deflection or Toughness;
- Dude Juice;
- Health Potion;
- a useful Meteor interaction;
- a Relic for the real carry or healer.

<h2 id="goat-fight-route">The Fight Order I Use Against the G.O.A.T.</h2>

The battle becomes easier to read when I stop treating all 251 enemies as one
target.

### Step 1: clear space before chasing boss damage

I open with Meteors or other area damage on the thickest Ram groups.

I do not place every Meteor directly on the G.O.A.T. at the start. The boss is
dangerous, but the Rams are what prevent the roster from stabilizing.

My opening goals are:

1. reduce the number of incoming attacks;
2. give Taunts a smaller group to control;
3. let healing respond to meaningful damage instead of constant chip;
4. create room for melee Dudes to reach the boss.

When a Meteor or another effect applies Vulnerability, I use one application
during the crowded opening and keep another for the G.O.A.T.-only phase when
possible.

### Step 2: watch the frontline recovery pattern

The first clear nearly failed because I waited too long to use Dude Juice.

The Warlocks were still distributing damage, so the roster looked recoverable.
Then a G.O.A.T. Stomp pushed several Types into danger at the same time.

I use Dude Juice when:

- the tank group remains Knocked Out after healing triggers;
- the same healer group keeps falling;
- the boss reaches unprotected damage Types;
- roster health continues falling after most Rams are gone;
- multiple core roles are missing while enough fight remains for the revival
  to matter.

I do not wait until almost every Dude is gone. Returning Dudes are safer while
some frontline structure still exists.

### Step 3: use Health Potion after real damage lands

I normally save the Health Potion through the opening unless the build is
already collapsing.

Once the Rams are mostly gone, the fight changes from wave clear to repeated
boss damage. That is when healing the most injured half of the roster can give
the frontline enough time to reset and the damage engine enough time to finish.

Using the Potion while everybody is healthy wastes its best window.

### Step 4: do not rely on Bottled Lightning to stun the boss

Bottled Lightning is still useful against Rams or during the approach to Round
105.

I do not build the final plan around it stopping the G.O.A.T.

Bottled Lightning did not stun the G.O.A.T. in this clear, so I do not treat
its stun as guaranteed boss control. I treat that as a tested warning, not proof that every
version of the G.O.A.T. is always immune.

My safer control package is layered:

- opening delay or Stasis;
- Toughness, Block or Deflection;
- Taunt where the modifier allows it;
- healing that continues during a long fight;
- Dude Juice after a meaningful collapse.

### Step 5: switch fully to the G.O.A.T.

Once the Rams are gone, I stop spreading damage.

I move the remaining Vulnerability, healing and active items onto the boss
phase. The G.O.A.T. can still dash and Stomp, but the roster becomes easier to
stabilize when support effects are no longer divided across 250 smaller
enemies.

This is also when sustained scalers overtake short opening bonuses:

- Time Anomalies;
- Cyborg Extermination;
- Cave Dude Bunga damage;
- Vampire Lifesteal;
- repeated Crit or stored-damage effects.

<h2 id="winning-roster-check">What a Round 105 Roster Still Needs</h2>

Three different five-Type teams completed Tier 1, so I do not treat one exact
lineup as mandatory.

The result-screen numbers in this section come from individual successful
Round 105 clears. They show that the damage and recovery engines remained
active, not that every build must reach the same totals.

The successful rosters shared four jobs.

### A frontline that survives imperfect control

Warlock can distribute damage and Taunt. General can direct pressure toward a
durable Type. A Cave-based team can replace normal Taunt structure with Max HP,
healing, Lifesteal and HP equalization.

The method changes, but the G.O.A.T. still needs something standing between it
and the primary damage engine.

### Healing that continues after the opening

One-time recovery is not enough for a fight with 250 Rams and repeated Stomps.

The successful routes used combinations such as:

- Chef and Mutant;
- Franken;
- Cleric, Medic, Ghost and Vampire Lifesteal.

I want healing that remains active after the first burst rather than one narrow
trigger that can fail once and disappear.

### Damage that grows during a long fight

The Cyborg clear finished Round 105 with approximately **304k Cyborg damage**
and **62k Franken healing**.

### Enough wave clear to reach the boss phase

A single-target roster can look excellent against Gorillas and still struggle
when 250 Rams occupy the field.

Before Round 105, I want at least one dependable area tool:

- Time Anomalies;
- Cleave;
- Meteor;
- chained attacks;
- Skeledudes and on-Knockout triggers;
- another repeatable AoE source.

Its first job is not to burst the G.O.A.T. It is to create the space required
to begin the real boss phase.

<h2 id="common-failures">The Mistakes That Almost Ended My Clears</h2>

### Entering Round 101 without repairing the roster

Round 100 is not the time to celebrate and ignore missing core Dudes. I repair
the exact role that was depleted before chasing another Relic.

### Saving Dude Juice until the roster is nearly empty

I now use it when the frontline no longer resets between attacks, not when only
a handful of Dudes remain.

### Buying more damage while defense is already failing

Another damage Relic does not solve a roster that cannot survive one Stomp.

At the final shop, I choose the effect that addresses the current failure:
Deflection, Toughness, healing, recovery or a direct upgrade for the existing
carry.

### Focusing the G.O.A.T. while most Rams are active

That keeps incoming damage high, interferes with targeting and spreads healing
across too many small hits.

I clear space first, then commit to the boss.

### Assuming the normal Taunt plan works unchanged

Taunt Resistance can weaken the exact opening the roster has relied on for the
previous 100 rounds.

I read the modifier and add a second defensive layer.

### Treating Bottled Lightning as guaranteed control

It failed to stun in one clear. I still use it when it helps against Rams, but
the entire strategy never depends on that stun.
