To unlock **Tier 2 in How Many Dudes**, I focus on one requirement:

> **Earn Gold Stars with five Dude Types.**

Discoveries, Ensemble, Silver Stars and Pocket Dice all improve the account, but
they do not replace that requirement. The fastest route is therefore a complete
Tier 1 win with one stable five-Type roster, not repeated partial runs or an
early detour into Endless.

<h2 id="how-tier-2-unlocks">How Tier 2 Actually Unlocks</h2>

The post-run systems are easy to mix together because several rewards appear
after the same successful run.

The result explanation I saw separated them like this:

- a regular Star can be awarded for first-time progress;
- getting beyond **Round 60** can award a **Silver Star**;
- beating the standard run can award a **Gold Star**.

The Emperor Dude then gives the important Tier 2 instruction:

> Earn Gold Stars with five Dude Types to advance to Tier 2.

That means a Round 60 result is useful account progress, but it is not the
finish line for this route. I need the full Tier 1 completion.

<h2 id="fastest-route">The Fastest Route From Tier 1 to Tier 2</h2>

### 1. Lock the five-Type plan before the late game

By roughly Rounds 15–25, I want to know what each Type is doing.

The exact Dudes can change, but the roster still needs:

- a way to control enemy attention;
- healing that starts before the roster collapses;
- sustained damage for large enemies;
- area damage or control for crowded screens;
- enough recovery to enter the next boss in usable condition.

I do not need every slot perfected by Round 20. I only need a clear direction.

If I am still adding unrelated Types after Round 40, the run usually has two
problems:

1. the original core is not being recruited deeply enough;
2. the personal and Family Relic pool keeps expanding.

### 2. Stop drafting for novelty

A new Dude can look exciting without helping the current Tier 2 route.

Before adding another Type, I ask:

- Which current job is missing?
- Will this Type replace one of the planned five?
- Does it improve the next boss, or only create another Relic branch?
- Can I recruit enough copies for it to matter before Round 100?

If the answers are unclear, I deepen the five Types I already have.

Discovering one more card is not worth making the winning roster less
consistent.

### 3. Push past Round 60

Round 60 is an important checkpoint because it can award Silver-Star progress.
It is also where a Modifier such as Separator can expose weak spacing, healing
range or Taunt coverage.

But I do not cash out mentally at Round 60.

Once the roster is stable, my priorities change:

- I stop buying slow economy pieces that will not repay before the next boss;
- I avoid enemy Types that have already caused severe losses;
- I save immediate recovery and control for the decisive checkpoints;
- I replace missing core Dudes instead of starting a sixth build.

<h2 id="what-does-not-unlock-tier-2">
  What Does Not Replace the Tier 2 Requirement
</h2>

Several systems are valuable, but none of them substitutes for five Gold-Star
Dude Types.

### Discoveries

Discoveries expand account progression and unlock milestone rewards. They are
worth collecting when they fit the run.

I do not add a weak or unsupported Type only to increase the discovery count
during a promising Tier 1 clear. The completed win is the higher-value result.

### Silver Stars

Silver Stars are useful progress and can unlock permanent starting rewards, but
the Tier 2 instruction specifically asks for Gold Stars.

A run that reaches Round 60 and stops is therefore progress toward a stronger
account, not completion of the Tier 2 route.

### Endless

Endless starts after the standard clear, but the prompt states that it **does
not grant credit toward Collectibles**.

I use it to test late scaling or see how a build eventually fails. I do not use
it as the efficient route for Tier progression.

<h2 id="common-delays">What Usually Delays the Unlock</h2>

### Adding too many Types

The roster may contain many Dudes while only three Types have enough copies and
Relics to contribute.

For Tier 2, I would rather have five clearly supported Types than a collection
of half-built ideas.

### Treating Round 60 as the goal

Silver Stars matter, but the Tier 2 gate asks for Gold Stars. When the build is
stable after Round 60, I keep pushing.

### Spending for value that arrives too late

A 15-round investment or an 11-round Relic loan can be excellent early and
irrelevant near Round 100.

As the final bosses approach, I buy what helps immediately:

- replacements for the five core Types;
- Dude Juice;
- Health Potion;
- roster-wide defense;
- area control for packed fights.

### Continuing into Endless automatically

After a successful clear, it is tempting to keep the same strong roster going.

I only do that when testing is the goal. When account progression is the goal,
I record the completed run and start the next run that can add new Gold-Star or
Silver-Star progress.

<h2 id="when-to-enter-tier-2">When I Stop Farming Tier 1</h2>

I move forward when three things are true:

1. the account has the required five Gold-Star Dude Types;
2. I understand which five jobs made the winning roster work;
3. another Tier 1 run would mainly repeat known decisions instead of solving a
   current progression requirement.

I do not wait for every discovery, every achievement or every Relic.

Those are separate completion goals. Tier 2 is the next learning step once the
five-Type Gold-Star requirement is satisfied.

Before entering, I make sure I can answer:

- Which Type held enemy attention?
- Which Type prevented attrition?
- Which Type carried sustained damage?
- What cleared crowded screens?
- Which matchup nearly ended the run?
- Which late purchase actually saved it?

That gives me a useful baseline for Tier 2 instead of entering with only the
knowledge that one lucky roster happened to win.

The short route is straightforward: build five Types with real jobs, complete
Round 105, apply the Gold-Star progress, collect Discovery and Silver-Star
rewards along the way, and leave Endless for testing rather than progression.
