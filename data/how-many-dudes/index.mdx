A good **How Many Dudes** run is not about recruiting the most characters. It is
about building five Dude Types that cover the jobs the roster actually needs,
then changing my decisions as the next boss gets closer.

My successful Tier 1 runs did not use one mandatory team. They did share the
same structure:

- somebody controlled enemy attention;
- somebody kept injured Dudes alive;
- at least one Type scaled into sustained damage;
- the roster had a way to clear crowded screens;
- and I stopped gambling on long-term value once the run was close to Round 100.

> **Testing note:** Run-result numbers such as damage, healing, roster size,
> and observed timing come from individual successful Tier 1 runs. I use them
> as reference points for build growth and role balance, not as minimum
> requirements that every seed or roster must match. Fixed card values and
> unlock conditions are stated separately as game rules.

My results improved when I stopped judging each screen alone and started
asking what the roster was missing, which checkpoint was next, and whether the
current choice would matter before I reached it.

<h2 id="start-here">How I Build the Run From the First Flyers</h2>

A run moves through the same repeating decisions:

- post Flyers and recruit the Dudes they attract;
- choose a battle;
- collect cash and a recruit after winning;
- buy or restock Relics and consumables;
- decide whether Business Dude, Scavenger Dude or another visitor is worth the
  timing;
- reach the next boss with a usable roster.

Every choice changes later choices. A new Flyer changes the Relic pool, an
expensive Relic removes recovery money, and a richer fight can cost enough core
Dudes to make its payout meaningless.

That is why I establish a five-Type identity early and judge everything else
against it.

<h2 id="five-jobs">The Five Jobs I Want the Roster to Cover</h2>

I do not need exactly one Dude Type for each job. One Type can cover two jobs,
and several Types can share the same responsibility. I only need to know that
none of these areas has been ignored.

### Front-line control

High Max HP alone does not create a front line. I look for Taunt, Pact,
Toughness, Deflection, Block, Evasion, damage redirection or repeatable
Temporary Health that gives the rest of the roster time to work.

Warlock and General showed me two different solutions:

- Warlock absorbed and delayed pressure through Pact while repeatedly pulling
  attention;
- General used Tactical Command to make a Dude of the Type immediately below
  him on the Roster Taunt nearby enemies.

That second ability is why I treat roster order as part of the build. Moving a
Type above or below General can change who receives the command.

### Healing that arrives early enough

A large healing total can still arrive too late. I want recovery to begin before
several core Dudes are already Knocked Out, and I stop adding healers once the
roster survives comfortably but cannot finish fights fast enough.

If the roster survives comfortably but takes too long to finish a battle, I
stop adding sustain and invest in damage.

### Sustained damage

Some builds erase small enemies and then stall against one durable target.

I want at least one Type whose damage keeps improving as the run continues.
Cyborg, Cave, Ranger, Samurai, Time and Warlock all filled this role in
different completed runs.

I use result screens as a direction check, not a required threshold: by the late
middle game, the intended carry should be separating from the support Types.

### Crowded-screen damage

Round 100 and Round 105 are not clean one-target tests.

Time Anomalies, Cleave, Meteors and other area effects create room for the
front line to function. A roster with excellent single-target damage can still
collapse while dozens of smaller enemies block movement and spread incoming
pressure across the team.

### Recovery between important fights

Recovery is the fifth job even when it comes from Dude Juice, Health Potion,
Ranch recovery, replacements, a defensive Relic or simply refusing the matchup
that caused the previous collapse. I check which Type is missing, not only the
total roster size.

<h2 id="core-mechanics">The Combat Mechanics I Check Before Choosing a Relic</h2>

The Dudex explains the formal definitions. During a run, these distinctions
change my decisions most often.

### DPS and Direct Damage are not the same label

DPS is a useful baseline for damage and for abilities whose power scales from
it. **Direct Damage** is a specific trigger named in many ability and Relic
descriptions.

If a Relic activates when a Dude deals Direct Damage, I do not assume every
damage-over-time tick, summon or secondary effect will activate it. I read the
exact tooltip before building around the interaction.

### Taunt controls targets; it does not make the Dude immortal

Taunt makes affected enemies focus on the Taunting Dude. It creates time for
the rest of the roster, but it can also concentrate fatal pressure on one weak
front line.

Before adding more Taunt, I check whether the target also has enough Max HP,
Toughness, Block, Deflection, healing or Pact support to survive the attention.

### Pact delays a problem rather than deleting it

In my Warlock runs, the purple Pact bar showed incoming pressure being carried
over time instead of landing as one immediate burst.

The delay gives healing time to work, but it is not free defense. If recovery
falls behind, the stored damage still catches up.

### Block, Evasion and Deflection are separate build hooks

I do not treat every defensive stat as interchangeable.

Different Relics react to **Block**, **Evasion** or **Deflection** separately.
A defensive-looking option is not interchangeable when the build needs one
specific trigger.

### Temporary Health is a timed buffer

Temporary Health protects real HP, but its duration matters. Some sources last
only a few seconds, while others remain until depleted.

I value short-duration Temporary Health when it appears repeatedly during the
dangerous part of the fight. A large buffer that arrives after the front line
has already collapsed does not solve the opening.

### Knocked Out Dudes are a route-planning problem

When a core Dude is Knocked Out, I do not automatically spend Dude Juice.

I wait until a revive restores a meaningful group or a Type the build cannot
function without. One missing support Dude and eight missing core Dudes are not
the same recovery problem.

<h2 id="relic-pool">How Flyers and Families Change the Relic Pool</h2>

This is the most important system I originally underestimated.

Posting a Flyer adds more than that Dude Type to the run:

- each Dude Type has **7 personal Relics**;
- each Dude Family has **8 Family Relics**;
- posting the Flyer adds that Dude's personal Relics and the Family Relics to
  the available pool;
- the same Relic only enters the pool once.

This creates a trade-off between roster diversity and Relic consistency.

### A focused Family keeps the pool narrower

If five posted Dudes all belong to one Family, the pool contains:

- 35 personal Dude Relics;
- 8 Relics from that one Family.

The target Relic is not guaranteed, but the Family side of the pool is smaller.

### Five Families create far more possible Relics

If the five Flyers come from five different Families, the pool contains:

- the same 35 personal Dude Relics;
- 40 Family Relics.

That gives access to more effects but makes one specific personal Relic harder
to find. I use Family diversity only when the five Types already form a complete
team or I deliberately want the broader pool.

### Pre-Posted Flyers change the opening shop

Daily Dude and Rematch begin with all five Flyers pre-posted. Ensemble lets me
choose how many Flyers are pre-posted.

Every pre-posted Flyer contributes its Relics immediately. If an Ensemble plan
depends on one key personal Relic, I prefer a small partial team; pre-posting all
five opens the full pool before the first shop.

My practical Relic Pool rules are:

1. post toward a complete five-Type team, not five unrelated high-rarity Dudes;
2. keep the early pool narrow when the build needs one defining Relic;
3. add another Family only when the new Type fills a real roster job;
4. stop rerolling for one dream Relic if the current shop can solve the same
   problem another way.

<h2 id="round-route">How My Priorities Change Across Tier 1</h2>

### Rounds 1–40: build the engine

I solve the missing jobs, deepen the five core Types and buy scaling Relics
that still have time to pay back.

This is the safest period for a 15-round Business investment or 11-round
Scavenger loan, provided the roster can survive without the cash or Relic.

### Rounds 41–80: fix what the fights are proving

By now I stop evaluating the build from the draft screen and start using battle
results.

If the opening is unstable, I improve control. If the roster survives but cannot
close fights, I improve the carry. If one enemy class repeatedly causes losses,
I stop selecting it for a slightly larger payout.

### Rounds 81–105: protect arrival

Late purchases need to matter immediately.

I prioritize recovery, roster-wide defense and fight control. I lower the value
of long economy cycles, unsupported new Dude Types and narrow Relics that need
many more rounds to become useful.

Round 100 is the elite-wave check, Rounds 101–104 are recovery, and Round 105 is
the G.O.A.T. finish.

<h2 id="progression">What the First Full Clear Unlocks</h2>

Stars and Discoveries are connected to account progression, but they are not
the same requirement.

The result screen distinguishes normal Stars, Silver Stars and Gold Stars. For
Tier 2, the important instruction is to earn **Gold Stars with five Dude
Types**.

Discoveries have separate milestone rewards. A completed route can also unlock
Ensemble and additional starting Pocket Dice. Endless is useful for testing,
but its prompt warns that it does not grant Collectible credit.

<h2 id="final-check">My Check Before Every Important Fight</h2>

Before clicking **Fight**, I check four things:

1. **Core Type counts:** is the healer, front line or carry missing too many
   Dudes?
2. **Previous matchup:** has this enemy already caused losses the build could
   not recover from?
3. **Immediate recovery:** can the inventory rescue one bad opening?
4. **Rounds remaining:** will the investment, loan or scaling Relic pay back
   before the next boss?

The closer I am to Round 100 or Round 105, the less patience I have for a choice
that only improves a hypothetical future version of the roster.

That is the route that made the game consistent for me: control the Flyer and
Relic pools, build five Types with real jobs, let each battle expose the next
problem, and stop risking a winning run for value that arrives too late.
