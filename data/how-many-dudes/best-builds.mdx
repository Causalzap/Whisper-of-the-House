I do not look for one universal team that wins every **How Many Dudes** run.

The available Dude Types, Relics, modifiers, and enemy choices change too much.
What I look for is a five-Type roster that solves the same five problems before
the run becomes expensive to repair:

- one group that can absorb or redirect pressure;
- healing that continues through a long fight;
- enough area damage to remove swarms;
- one damage engine that keeps scaling against bosses;
- a way to recover after several Dudes are Knocked Out.

Three different teams met those checks and completed Tier 1:

- **Warlock / Chef / Time / Mutant / Ranger** was the safest and easiest to
  diagnose;
- **Cyborg / Samurai / Assassin / Franken / General** produced the strongest
  verified final-boss damage;
- **Cave / Cleric / Vampire / Ghost / Medic** proved that a sustain-heavy team
  can win without a normal Taunt tank.

> **Testing note:** The damage, healing, roster-size, and checkpoint numbers
> below come from individual successful Tier 1 runs. They show how each role
> contributed inside those clears, but they are not minimum requirements that
> every build must match.

<h2 id="build-checkpoints">How I Know a Build Is Worth Continuing</h2>

I make the first serious decision before the Round 20 boss.

The roster does not need every perfect Relic yet, but I need clear answers to
four questions.

### Who is supposed to receive the first heavy attacks?

A named tank is not mandatory. An unmanaged opening is the problem.

The Warlock team uses Taunt and stored Pact damage. The Cyborg team places
Cyborg directly below General so Tactical Command directs pressure toward the
same group receiving defensive upgrades. The Cave team replaces a classic
frontline with Cleric pulses, Ghost HP equalization, Vampire Life Steal, and
shared Max HP.

If I cannot explain who is supposed to be hit, the fifth Type is not solved.

### Does healing arrive before the second heavy hit?

I watch the health bars, not only the result screen.

A working engine lets the frontline drop, recover, and drop again in a
repeatable rhythm. A failing engine leaves more unconscious Dudes after every
ordinary battle.

Dude Juice can repair one bad result. It cannot replace missing sustain for the
rest of the run.

### Can the team clear a swarm and one durable target?

The roster needs both.

Time Anomalies, Mutant Floofballs, Samurai dashes, Assassin Toxin, Cave attacks,
Cleave, and other Relics can clear groups. The team still needs damage that
continues after the small enemies disappear.

That is where Cyborg and Cave were easiest to read. The Warlock route revealed
its final carry later, when Time became the Round 105 MVP.

### Can I identify the current bottleneck?

I never recruit all five Types evenly.

I add Dudes based on the previous fight:

- Burger storage empties while Warlocks remain injured: add Chefs;
- Warlocks stay healthy while enemies live too long: add Time, Ranger, or
  Mutant;
- Cyborgs fall before Extermination stacks build: add Cyborg bodies, Franken
  healing, or defense;
- the Cave roster heals comfortably but kills too slowly: add Cave or Vampire;
- ordinary fights remove several core Dudes: take safer enemies and rebuild
  before increasing the carry.

<h2 id="warlock-chef-build">
  Build 1: Warlock, Chef, Time, Mutant, and Ranger
</h2>

This is the first team I choose when I want the most forgiving Tier 1 route.

It does not depend on one fragile carry:

- **Warlock** absorbs pressure through Taunt and delayed Pact damage;
- **Chef** prepares Burgers for repeatable healing;
- **Mutant** adds hybrid healing and damage through Floofballs;
- **Time** supplies late area scaling;
- **Ranger** contributes steady ranged pressure while the engine stabilizes.

The real strength is not simply “a lot of healing.” Warlock turns immediate
burst into damage spread over time, giving Burgers, Regeneration, Temporary
Health, and Floofballs time to work.

### My recruitment order

The tested route was:

1. **Time** for Anomalies and early swarm control;
2. **Ranger** for reliable ranged damage;
3. **Mutant** as the first hybrid support Type;
4. **Warlock** for pressure control and the frontline;
5. **Chef** for repeatable Burger healing.

The order is less important than the final job coverage. I do not force this
exact sequence when Warlock or Chef appears earlier.

### When I add Chefs

I add Chefs when Warlocks consume Burgers faster than the team replaces them.

That usually appears as a specific pattern:

- Warlocks survive the opening;
- the Burger supply empties;
- stored Pact damage keeps draining them;
- the next large hit arrives before the Burger meter recovers.

I do not wait until Warlocks begin falling every round.

In the completed run, I brought the Chef count to roughly **10**. Once Burgers
remained available through normal fights, I stopped adding Chefs automatically
and returned to Warlock, Time, and other damage.

More healing has diminishing value once the roster already exits fights near
full HP.

### When I add Warlocks

I add Warlocks when pressure is spreading beyond the current Taunt group or the
existing Warlocks are storing too much damage per body.

More Warlocks can improve three things at once:

- more enemy attention is controlled;
- Pact damage is distributed across a larger group;
- the roster has more time for Burger and Mutant healing to work.

I stop when the frontline is stable and the real problem becomes fight length.

### Relic priorities

I prioritize Relics that extend the time between a heavy hit and an actual
Knockout:

- Evasion, Deflection, Toughness, or Regeneration on Warlocks;
- overhealing converted into Temporary Health;
- Chef Attack Speed after Burger healing is established;
- stronger Food effects;
- Warlock redirection or Pact support;
- Time and Mutant damage after the frontline is reliable.

I do not keep buying generic Taunt effects after a large Warlock group already
controls enough enemies.

### What makes me abandon this build

I abandon or pivot when:

- Warlock never receives enough defensive support;
- Chef appears too late to establish reliable Burgers;
- the roster stays alive but has no real swarm or boss damage;
- every large enemy group bypasses the Taunt line;
- I keep adding healing while Time, Ranger, and Mutant remain too weak to end
  fights.

<h2 id="cyborg-build">
  Build 2: Cyborg, Samurai, Assassin, Franken, and General
</h2>

This was the strongest demonstrated damage build of the three.

It came from a Daily Dude roster, so I treat the five Types as a tested synergy
package rather than a guaranteed normal-run draft.

The team works because each Type protects the Cyborg engine:

- **Cyborg** is the main scaling carry;
- **Samurai** supplies mobile secondary damage;
- **Assassin** reduces enemy Attack Power and adds Toxin support;
- **Franken** is the sustain engine;
- **General** directs enemy attention toward the chosen frontline Type.

Cyborg direct damage has a chance to grant all Cyborgs a stacking Attack Power
bonus for the rest of the battle. The important requirement is therefore
uptime: enough Cyborgs must remain alive and attacking for the stacks to become
meaningful.

### My recruitment order

The tested structure was:

1. **Cyborg** as the main carry;
2. **Samurai** as secondary damage;
3. **Franken** as the healer;
4. **General** as the pressure controller;
5. **Assassin** for Toxin and enemy Attack Power reduction.

### Why Cyborg sits below General

General commands the Dude Type directly below it to Taunt nearby enemies.

I place Cyborg there because the same Type receives my strongest defensive
Relics and must remain active for Extermination to scale.

This creates the build's biggest strength and its biggest risk:

- the main carry also becomes the practical frontline;
- if Cyborg survives, damage accelerates;
- if Cyborg collapses, both pressure control and the main damage engine fail at
  once.

I do not place Franken in that slot. Franken spends current HP to heal injured
Dudes, so forcing it to absorb the main enemy pressure works against its support
role.

### What I buy first

My first priorities are:

- an opening delay such as Stasis;
- defensive effects on Cyborg;
- Cyborg-specific survival and Extermination support;
- Franken healing or Regeneration;
- Samurai damage as the second line;
- Assassin upgrades when they improve Toxin, Vulnerability, or enemy debuffs;
- General Relics that improve Tactical Command.

I stop buying broad low-impact bonuses once the run offers strong Cyborg Relics.
At that point, another Cyborg body or direct Cyborg upgrade usually gives more
value.

### When I increase Franken

I add Franken when the Cyborg group survives the first hit but cannot recover
before the next one.

Franken is not the carry and not the tank. Its value is keeping the carry and
other core Types active long enough for their scaling to matter.

In the final clear, Franken produced approximately **62k healing**, which is why
I treat it as a core engine rather than a disposable fifth Type.

### When I stop forcing more Cyborgs

I stop automatically adding Cyborgs when:

- the frontline is already dense enough;
- healing cannot keep pace with the current Cyborg count;
- Samurai or General has been depleted;
- the run needs Dude Juice more than another carry body;
- ordinary fights are winning but leaving the support shell too weak.

A larger carry group does not help if the roster around it cannot deliver the
carry to the boss.

### One Successful Run at Rounds 100 and 105

The Cyborg clear reached Round 100 with approximately:

- **202k Cyborg damage**;
- **104k Samurai damage**;
- heavy healing from Franken.

By Round 105, the same roster included:

- 35 Cyborgs;
- 30 Samurai;
- 32 Franken;
- 17 Assassins;
- 15 Generals.

The final result showed approximately:

- **304k Cyborg damage**;
- **111k Samurai damage**;
- **106k Franken damage**;
- **62k Franken healing**;
- **68k damage prevented by Cyborg**.

<h2 id="cave-cleric-build">
  Build 3: Cave, Cleric, Vampire, Ghost, and Medic
</h2>

This was the slowest and least conventional clear, but it proves that Tier 1
does not require a normal Taunt tank.

The five roles are:

- **Cave** as the main direct-damage group;
- **Cleric** as the primary area healer;
- **Vampire** as secondary damage and Life Steal support;
- **Ghost** as HP equalization and hybrid recovery;
- **Medic** as emergency single-target healing.

The team survives because several systems overlap. Its health bars can drop
sharply before Life Steal, Divine Pulse, Ghost equalization, and Triage begin
correcting the damage.

### My recruitment order

The tested order was:

1. **Ghost** for hybrid recovery;
2. **Medic** because the opening roster needed healing;
3. **Cleric** for repeatable area healing;
4. **Cave** for the missing direct-damage engine;
5. **Vampire** for Life Steal and secondary damage.

### How I recruit after the five Types are locked

My priority becomes:

1. add Clerics while Divine Pulse is the main reason health recovers;
2. add Cave Dudes when fights last too long;
3. add Ghosts when equalization and hybrid output are falling behind;
4. add Vampires after Life Steal Relics begin scaling;
5. add Medics only when Triage has a specific improvement or another healer is
   unavailable.

This is not balanced recruitment.

By Round 100, the roster had reached **30 Clerics** and only **12 Medics**.
Three Medics were already unconscious before the boss, but the main Cleric,
Cave, and Vampire engines were intact.

That is the correct way to judge this team: restore the engine, not every empty
slot.

### The Relics that change the build

The most important breakpoints were:

- **Clerical Error**, which converted part of raw Divine Pulse healing into
  stored direct damage;
- Vampire damage and Life Steal support;
- **Cave Painting**, which gave all Dudes 1% Max HP per Cave Dude, capped at 15
  stacks;
- effects that let Ghost equalization or Vampire healing reach more injured
  targets.

### When I refuse a good-looking Relic trade

I reject a loan or trade when it removes a core Cave or Vampire Relic to improve
a low-population Medic package.

I accept it only when:

- the roster can survive the temporary loss;
- the returned Relics strengthen a main engine;
- the return arrives before the checkpoint I am building toward.

<h2 id="which-build-to-use">Which Build I Choose From a New Draft</h2>

I choose according to the first three useful Type offers and the Relics already
entering the pool.

### I choose Warlock when the frontline and healing pieces appear early

Warlock plus Chef is the easiest of these three structures to diagnose. The
Burger supply and stored Pact damage bars show whether the build needs more
Chefs, more Warlocks, or more damage.

I do not force the route when Warlock arrives without healing or when Chef
arrives after the roster has already committed to a different sustain engine.

### I choose Cyborg when I can protect the carry

Cyborg needs:

- a real healer;
- a controlled frontline or enough defense;
- replacement Cyborg bodies;
- time for Extermination to stack.

General plus Franken is the tested version. I do not turn Cyborg into the tank
when the run has no defensive Relics, no healing, and no replacement plan.

### I choose Cave and Cleric when sustain Relics begin overlapping

I commit when the pool begins offering combinations of:

- Divine Pulse support;
- Life Steal;
- healing converted into damage;
- Cave Max HP scaling;
- Ghost equalization;
- Vampire damage.

This route feels less stable early, but it becomes difficult to break once
several recovery systems are active.

### I abandon the build that cannot solve its main problem

After Round 20, I stop assuming every draft will become good later.

I abandon or redirect a roster that still has one of these failures:

- no answer to the opening pressure;
- healing that never catches up;
- excellent sustain but no finishing damage;
- one strong carry that disappears every difficult fight;
- too many special Types competing for recruitment and Relics.

The best build is not the one with the most impressive isolated card. It is the
five-Type structure whose jobs remain visible from the early acts through the
final result screen.
