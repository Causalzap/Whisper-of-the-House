Reaching **Round 100 in How Many Dudes** is not mainly about finding one broken
Dude Type.

It is about keeping the same five-Type plan functional while enemy counts,
modifiers and recovery costs keep climbing. My successful routes used different
teams, but the decision pattern stayed consistent:

- establish the five jobs early;
- invest only while there is time for the money or Relic to return;
- treat repeated knockouts as a build warning;
- stop choosing risky fights for small payout gains;
- enter Round 100 with one working damage engine, one working recovery engine
  and enough emergency resources to survive a bad opening.

In the completed runs I checked, Round 100 used the same core enemy group:
**30 Silverback Gorillas, 2 Diamondback Gorillas and 1 Toddler**. The modifier
was not fixed. One clear rolled Frenzied, while another used a
piercing-focused modifier.

<h2 id="route-overview">What Round 100 Actually Tests</h2>

Round 100 exposes rosters that have only one strength:

- damage without recovery;
- recovery without enough finishing power;
- a strong carry with no protection;
- a full roster that cannot replace Knocked Out core Dudes;
- a rich economy that was never converted into combat power.

I do not use one universal damage number as the readiness test. Different
Types report damage differently, and some builds win through Taunt, Temporary
Health, Life Steal or repeated healing rather than one huge result-screen
number.

The checkpoint numbers and route examples below come from individual successful
runs. I use them to compare whether a build is improving across its own route,
not as universal Round 80 or Round 100 requirements.

I use three checks instead.

### The team can recover during an ordinary fight

If every health bar only moves downward until the battle ends, the roster is
not sustainable. Winning one fight does not matter if the next fight begins
with the healer, frontline or carry already depleted.

### The damage engine survives after the first wave

Area damage can erase small enemies and make the build look finished. Round 100
still has two Diamondback Gorillas after the dense Silverback pack begins to
disappear.

I need sustained damage that continues after the first burst.

### The five core jobs reach the checkpoint

Four missing secondary Dudes can be acceptable. Losing most of the primary
healer or carry is different.

Before Round 100, I care more about **which Types are missing** than the total
number of empty roster slots.

<h2 id="rounds-1-40">Rounds 1–40: Establish the Team and Time the Economy</h2>

By Round 20, I want answers to four questions:

1. Which Type receives or redirects the opening pressure?
2. Which Type restores health during combat?
3. Which Type clears a crowded screen?
4. Which Type continues damaging one durable target?

The exact answers can differ. What matters is that the five main Types are
moving toward distinct jobs rather than becoming five versions of the same
damage plan.

I spend early Pocket Dice when a missing job appears. I do not spend them only
to replace a useful damage Type with a more exciting damage Type.

Repeated knockouts in easy fights are the first warning. One bad early Bee
fight can be repaired with Dude Juice, but if the same pattern returns, the
problem is not the lack of another consumable. The roster needs earlier control,
better healing or more Dudes in the Type that is being depleted.

### When I buy Dude Juice early

I buy it when:

- several useful Dudes are already Knocked Out;
- a boss is close;
- the missing group contains a core Type;
- the next recruit cannot repair the roster by itself.

I do not automatically revive one Regular Dude. Early money still has to create
the team, and an unnecessary recovery purchase can cost the Relic or recruit
that prevents the next ten knockouts.

### Business Dude: count 15 rounds forward

The Baller Package takes the current cash and returns double the investment
after **15 rounds**.

I invest heavily only when the previous fights were controlled and I am not
already missing a required purchase.

I skip the all-in option when:

- the frontline is collapsing before healing begins;
- several core Dudes are Knocked Out;
- I have no Dude Juice before a boss;
- the next rounds are already producing escalating losses.

A large return does not help if the run ends before it arrives.

### Scavenger Dude: count 11 rounds forward

The Relic loan lasts **11 rounds** and returns the borrowed Relic with a bonus.

That can be excellent early. It can also remove the exact card that currently
keeps the roster alive.

I check two things:

1. Will the Relic return before the checkpoint I am preparing for?
2. Can the roster function during the 11 rounds without it?

A loan started around Round 26 can return before Round 40. A loan started around
Round 85 can still return before Round 100. A new loan after Round 90 does not
help with the Round 100 fight, so I do not hand over a core defensive or damage
Relic there.

<h2 id="rounds-41-60">Rounds 41–60: Repair Repeated Losses Before Separator</h2>

By Round 40, the five-Type identity should already be established. Rounds 41–60
are for strengthening whichever job is falling behind.

I use the previous result screen and the health bars:

- if damage Types survive while the front line disappears, I add frontline
  bodies or defense;
- if the front line survives but never returns to safe HP, I add healing;
- if everybody survives while enemies remain active too long, I add damage;
- if ordinary fights repeatedly remove several Dudes, I stop taking the
  highest-payout route.

This is also where I stop confusing recovery spending with wasted money. Buying
Dude Juice and choosing easier enemies can prevent one bad battle from becoming
three weaker battles in a row.

### Prepare for the Round 60 modifier

One successful route rolled **Separator** at Round 60. The roster spawned far
apart while Grizzly Bears and Ravens attacked immediately.

My response is:

1. use early control or area damage;
2. let isolated Dudes move back toward the main group;
3. watch which core role is being removed;
4. revive only when the missing role prevents stabilization.

The exact consumable is not mandatory. The lesson is to solve the opening
positioning failure before it becomes a full-roster collapse.

Round 60 also marks useful Silver-Star progress, but I treat it as proof that
the build can continue, not as the end of the Round 100 route.

<h2 id="rounds-61-80">Rounds 61–80: Compare Payout With Recovery Cost</h2>

This is where risky battles begin to look more profitable than they really are.

A harder fight may pay slightly more, but the true cost includes:

- missing core Dudes;
- Dude Juice;
- a weaker next battle;
- a lost investment or Relic-loan window;
- entering the boss with the wrong roles depleted.

In one route, the harder option paid only about **$90 more** and removed enough
Dudes to force another recovery purchase. The payout difference did not cover
the damage done to the next rounds.

I choose the safer battle when:

- a boss is within two or three rounds;
- the last fight already caused meaningful losses;
- Ravens, Honey Badgers or another proven bad matchup appears in large numbers;
- trained Dudes or loaned Relics have not returned;
- only one Dude Juice remains.

### Use Round 80 as a trend check, not a universal number check

The Cyborg route reached Round 80 with approximately **77k Cyborg damage**,
while Franken carried the healing through a 25% Piercing boss.

The useful question is not whether every build has 77k damage.

I ask:

- Is the main damage Type separating from its earlier results?
- Is the healer keeping pace with the larger enemy groups?
- Does the roster finish the boss with the same five jobs still active?
- Did the win require more recovery than the next 20 rounds can support?

If the carry is scaling but the rest of the roster keeps shrinking, I stop
buying secondary damage Relics and return to defense, healing and core
replacements.

<h2 id="rounds-81-99">Rounds 81–99: Change the Goal From Profit to Arrival</h2>

After Round 80, the goal is no longer to extract maximum value from every
choice.

The goal is to reach Round 100 with the engine intact.

An ordinary Round 82 fight removed enough Dudes in one route to create a
recovery spiral. That is the point where a technically successful fight becomes
a warning.

My recovery loop is:

1. choose a safer enemy group;
2. recruit the Type that was just depleted;
3. use training when Regular Dudes can become missing roles;
4. buy Dude Juice before optional Relics;
5. let active investments and Relic loans return;
6. reassess after one clean battle.

I do not call the roster recovered because it won once. I want a fight where
the core Types stop disappearing and the team exits without needing another
immediate revive.

### Avoid the matchup that already countered the build

During this route, the Round 94 choices included a large Raven group. I rejected
that matchup and chose a Tiger-and-Gorilla fight instead. The safer route
reached the final shop with the core intact.

### Spend the final shops on immediate value

By the mid-90s, I prioritize:

- enough Dude Juice to repair one bad pre-boss fight and the boss itself;
- a Health Potion when several core Dudes are injured;
- roster-wide survivability;
- a direct upgrade for the established carry or healer;
- one control or area-damage tool for the Gorilla pack;
- replacements for missing main Types.

I do not begin a new 15-round Business investment, and I do not hand over a
critical Relic for an 11-round loan that returns after Round 100.

<h2 id="round-100-boss">Round 100: Beat the Gorilla Pack</h2>

The completed runs I checked used:

- **30 Silverback Gorillas**;
- **2 Diamondback Gorillas**;
- **1 Toddler**.

The modifier can change.

In one completed run, Round 100 rolled Frenzied, which gives enemies
**2% Attack Power every second**. That turns the fight into a timer: a
sustain-heavy roster can still lose if it allows the gorilla pack to remain
active for too long.

### My opening order

I do not focus attention on the Toddler. It disappeared quickly in multiple
clears.

I open by reducing the dense Silverback group.

When a Meteor interaction can apply Vulnerability, I place it where it catches
the largest cluster at the start. The point is to improve the damage from the
whole roster while the screen is still crowded.

After the Silverbacks begin disappearing, I watch the two Diamondbacks and the
core roster bars. This is where sustained damage matters. A build that only
frontloads one explosion can stall while Frenzied continues stacking.

### When I use Dude Juice

I do not revive only because the roster is not perfectly full.

One successful clear entered the fight four Dudes short: three Medics and one
Ghost. The Cave, Cleric and Vampire core was still functioning, so I kept the
charge and completed Round 100.

I use Dude Juice when the missing Dudes stop the engine from working:

- the frontline group is collapsing;
- the healer count is too low for health to rebound;
- the primary damage Type is disappearing before the Diamondbacks die;
- several core roles are down while the fight is still early.

That is more reliable than using an arbitrary missing-Dude count.

<h2 id="final-check">My Final Check Before Starting Round 100</h2>

I start the fight when I can answer yes to most of these:

- Is the established carry still present in meaningful numbers?
- Does the roster recover during ordinary battles?
- Can the team continue damaging the Diamondbacks after the first wave?
- Is the primary healer or sustain engine intact?
- Do I have Dude Juice for a real core-role collapse?
- Do I have one immediate control, healing or area-damage option?
- Have I stopped all investments and loans that return too late?
- Am I avoiding a last optional fight that has already countered this roster?

Round 100 becomes much more consistent when I treat it as the end of a
20-round preparation corridor rather than one isolated boss.
