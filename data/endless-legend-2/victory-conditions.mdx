<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
Check both objectives before choosing an Awakening path. **Worship** needs **120 Population and 7 Monuments**, **Impress** needs its Science objective plus controlled pacified Villages, and **Master** needs controlled Territories plus **6 Doomwraiths**. After both objectives are complete, keep them satisfied for **10 turns**.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

The numeric values below reflect the September 2026 1.0 balance. Costs that scale with game speed are labeled when they come from a specific run.

ENDLESS Legend 2 has three Awakening paths — **Worship, Impress, and Master** — plus **Conquest** and **Score**.

The three Awakening paths work differently from a single-stat victory. Each one asks the Empire to finish two objectives at the same time, so a nearly complete requirement can hide a much slower second half.

A Worship Empire may already have 120 Population while several Monuments are unfinished. Impress can finish its research while the remaining Villages sit inside foreign land. Master can spread across a large part of Saiadha and still have too few controlled Doomwraiths.

The Victory screen is therefore most useful when read as two separate progress problems rather than one percentage bar.

  What are the victory conditions in ENDLESS Legend 2?
</h2>

The numbers are not equally fixed.

**Worship's 120 Population and 7 Monuments are fixed**, and the current Doomwraith requirement for Master is **6**. The Science side of Impress is also fixed at **2 Era VI technologies**.

The number of controlled pacified Villages required for Impress and the number of Territories required for Master depend on the generated world and match setup, so those two values should be read directly from the current Victory screen.

Both objectives on Worship, Impress, or Master are mandatory. Completing only Population, Science, or Territories does not start the final victory timer.

<h2 id="worship">
  How does the Worship victory work?
</h2>

Worship requires:

- **120 Empire Population**
- **7 Monuments to the Lost**

The two counters usually advance at very different speeds.

Population can accelerate sharply once several Cities are developed, while a Monument still has to occupy a construction queue and consume its required resources. Each Monument to the Lost currently costs **5,000 Industry, 35 Hyperium, and 5 Thalitine**. Reaching the late game with seven suitable Cities but an empty strategic-resource stockpile can therefore delay Worship just as effectively as low Industry.

The safest Worship plan is to start the slow construction side while population is still climbing.

A mature City that already has its essential economy and defenses is a much better Monument site than a new settlement still waiting for basic districts. Once Worship looks likely, I also stop treating Hyperium and Thalitine as spare upgrade materials and reserve enough for the remaining Monuments.

Population does not need the same kind of early commitment.

A developed Empire can add several population points while the Monuments are being built, and factions such as the <Link href="/endless-legend-2/last-lords">Last Lords</Link> can close that gap particularly quickly because Dust can accelerate their population economy.

That does not make uncontrolled growth free. Pushing toward 120 while Cities are already short on vocation slots can create Destitute population, Shanties, and falling Approval. If the counter is moving but City output is collapsing, repair the <Link href="/endless-legend-2/approval">Approval and population problem</Link> before forcing the last few population points.

<h2 id="impress">
  How does the Impress victory work?
</h2>

Impress combines a fixed Science objective with map-dependent Village control.

The current Science target is **2 Era VI technologies**.

The second objective asks for a required number of **pacified Minor Faction Villages located inside Territories you control**. That number changes with the generated map rather than using one universal value.

This distinction makes ownership more important than the history of the Village.

A pacified Village inside another Empire's Territory does not advance your current objective. If you take control of the surrounding Territory, that Village can become part of the count; if you lose the Territory later, the same progress can disappear.

That makes the Village half progressively harder to repair as borders solidify.

Early in the campaign, another useful Village may only require exploration, pacification, and enough Influence to secure its Territory. Late in the campaign, solving the same shortage may require taking established land from another Empire.

The Parlay, Bribe, Assimilation, and Protectorate decisions themselves are covered in the <Link href="/endless-legend-2/minor-factions">Minor Factions guide</Link>. For Impress, the number that matters is how many pacified Villages actually sit inside land you control.

A research-heavy Empire can finish its two Era VI technologies and still be far from winning if the Village map is poor. The reverse is also possible: broad expansion can secure enough Villages long before the Science objective finishes.

When Impress becomes a serious option, I count the Villages first. Research can continue inside existing borders; an unclaimed Village may not remain available for long.

<h2 id="master">
  How does the Master victory work?
</h2>

Master requires:

- the map-dependent number of **controlled Territories**
- **6 controlled Doomwraiths**

The older Fortress requirement is no longer part of Master.

The Doomwraith objective is especially important because simply owning more land does not substitute for it. A huge territorial Empire can finish one half of Master while leaving the other almost untouched.

Choosing Master also gives access to the mechanics used to control Doomwraiths, so this part of the victory cannot be completely prepared before committing to the path.

Doomwraiths are not passive counters waiting to be collected. They settle around the world and apply corruption to settlements. If left unresolved, their corruption can become increasingly disruptive and can destroy Minor Faction Villages, which means the same Doomwraith can interfere with City output or even remove Villages that matter to an Impress plan.

That creates an unusual late-game choice: a Doomwraith that would normally be removed as a threat may instead be something a Master Empire wants to control.

The Territory side is more straightforward, but I still avoid filling the counter with weak land too early. Regions containing strategic resources, Minor Faction Villages, useful chokepoints, strong City space, or connections between existing holdings improve the Empire while also advancing Master.

Tidefall can suddenly add another set of candidates. When the sea is close to receding, keeping enough Influence and army movement available to inspect the new coast can produce a better Territory than rushing the final marginal Camp beforehand. The changing-map decisions are covered in the <Link href="/endless-legend-2/tidefall">Monsoon and Tidefall guide</Link>.

<h2 id="choose-path">
  Which Awakening path should you choose, and can you switch later?
</h2>

The useful comparison is not which path has the highest completed percentage. It is how expensive the unfinished requirement will be.

**Worship** is easier to continue when population is already healthy and several productive Cities can carry Monument construction without freezing the rest of the Empire.

**Impress** becomes practical when late Science is already strong and enough Village-heavy Territories are inside, or realistically within reach of, your borders.

**Master** benefits an Empire that is already wide, has strong Influence income, and can defend enough fronts to pursue Territory and Doomwraith control together.

Path selection also activates path-specific traits and mechanics, so the choice affects the Empire before the final timer appears.

The choice is not permanent.

You can change Awakening paths from the Victory screen, and switching does not erase Cities, Science, Population, Villages, Territories, or armies already earned. What changes is the pair of objectives being pursued and the path-specific effects that remain active.

A late switch therefore makes sense when the Empire has accidentally completed most of another route.

For example, a Master campaign may discover that its expansion has also placed enough pacified Villages inside its borders while Science is nearly finished. Moving to Impress can be faster than forcing the remaining Master objective.

The expensive switch is the opposite: abandoning seven nearly completed Monuments for a Master path when the Empire owns little Territory. The progress remains in the world, but it no longer solves the active victory condition.

<h2 id="countdown">
  Why does the game continue after both objectives are complete?
</h2>

Completing both objectives starts a **10-turn maintenance period** rather than ending the campaign immediately.

Conditions that depend on the current Empire state can move backward during those ten turns.

For Worship, Population can fall.

For Impress, losing a Territory can remove its controlled pacified Villages.

For Master, losing land can reduce the Territory count, while the Doomwraith side also has to remain satisfied.

That is why I prefer a small buffer rather than finishing on the exact minimum whenever the economy can support one. Sitting at exactly 120 Population leaves Worship vulnerable to a single loss, while an exposed border Territory can be a fragile way to maintain Impress or Master.

The final ten turns also change military priorities.

An army that would normally begin another distant conquest is often more useful protecting a Village cluster, a vulnerable frontier, or the Cities carrying Worship's population and Monument investment.

Rival progress does not stop because your timer started.

When another Empire receives an imminent-victory warning, the fastest response is to inspect which half of its route can still be changed from outside. Territory and Village control are directly contestable. Finished Science is much harder to undo without attacking the Empire itself.

A small territorial strike that breaks a victory condition can buy more time than starting a general war with no specific target.

<h2 id="conquest-score">
  How do Conquest and Score victories work?
</h2>

**Conquest** ignores the Awakening objective system.

You win by becoming the **last remaining Empire**, and an Empire is eliminated when it loses its last settlement.

That sounds simpler than Worship, Impress, or Master, but simplicity does not guarantee speed. On a large map, finding, reaching, besieging, and taking every surviving capital can consume far more turns than finishing an Awakening route that is already close.

When the last opponent is half a world away and the seventh Monument has only a few turns remaining, conquest cleanup is rarely the shorter job.

The reverse can happen when the military position is overwhelming and the remaining Awakening objective depends on Villages or Territories that are harder to acquire than enemy Cities.

**Score** resolves at the configured turn limit if no other victory has already ended the game.

The Victory screen shows the score standings and remaining turns. A strong lead can make preservation more valuable than forcing another risky victory condition, while a large deficit means waiting for the clock is not a rescue plan.

Score is therefore something to watch throughout the late game rather than a separate project to begin on the final turn.

<h2 id="victory-achievements">
  Which achievements require specific victory paths?
</h2>

Three achievements correspond directly to the Awakening paths:

- **Red Sky at Night** — win through Master
- **Saiadha Reborn** — win through Worship
- **The End of Magic** — win through Impress

Conquest has its own elimination achievement as well.

Because Worship, Impress, and Master are separate victory outcomes, completing all three naturally means winning multiple campaigns rather than trying to satisfy all three paths in one save.

The other faction, difficulty, combat, and unusual-condition goals are listed in the <Link href="/endless-legend-2/achievements">59-achievement checklist</Link>.
