<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
Use **Industry** to get the capital functioning, then treat Dust as a flexible reserve for population, army recovery, and urgent development. Buy population only when the City has a useful job and enough Approval to absorb it, and scout Minor Faction Villages early because **Lord's Estates** make some regions much more valuable to the Last Lords.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

Last Lords can turn Dust into results that other factions have to wait several turns to obtain.

A treasury can become another population unit, restore a damaged army, accelerate construction, or support equipment and expansion. The strength of the faction is not simply earning a lot of Dust. It is being able to choose which delay disappears first.

That flexibility also creates the main risk. Population, military recovery, infrastructure, and faction progression can all begin asking for the same resource at once. A rich Empire can still make a bad decision if it spends heavily on the wrong problem just before a more important one appears.

<h2 id="opening">
  What should Last Lords prioritize in the opening?
</h2>

The capital needs enough **Industry** before the Dust economy can do much useful work.

A settlement with strong Dust but very slow construction still waits too long for districts and improvements that cannot simply be ignored. I therefore value a starting region with workable production, reasonable district space, useful resources, and access to Minor Faction Villages rather than choosing the highest immediate Dust total.

In one opening, **Workshop** was an important early research choice because the capital needed production before the faction's stronger Dust tools could accelerate anything meaningful.

That is not a fixed build order. If the starting region already has strong Industry and weak Science or Approval, the research priority can change. The important part is getting the City to the point where Dust purchases are accelerating a functioning economy rather than compensating for one that never developed.

Minor Faction access also matters earlier than it does for many other factions. A nearby Village can later support Lord's Estates, so I scout far enough to know whether the next expansion is buying ordinary terrain or opening a useful part of the faction economy.

<h2 id="population-and-approval">
  When should you buy population with Dust?
</h2>

Last Lords can turn Dust directly into population instead of relying entirely on normal growth.

That makes population one of the fastest ways to convert a strong treasury into more output, but the purchase only pays off when the new worker can be used immediately.

Before buying another population unit, I check three things:

1. **Is there an open vocation slot that matters?**
2. **Does the City have enough capacity for another population unit?**
3. **Can its Approval absorb the additional pressure?**

A worker entering a useful Artisan or Scribe slot can immediately strengthen a weak yield. A worker entering a full City may instead create unemployment, capacity pressure, or another Approval problem.

That distinction is especially important for Last Lords because their economic growth can outrun City stability.

Several population interactions carry negative Approval, so the faction can move from productive to unstable much faster than an Empire waiting for normal population growth. Once Approval is already cutting Food or Industry, adding another worker usually makes recovery harder rather than faster.

The complete relationship between vocation slots, Destitute population, Shanties, production penalties, and rebellion is covered in the <Link href="/endless-legend-2/approval">Approval guide</Link>.

A strong treasury therefore does not automatically justify another population purchase.

I buy population when the Empire has a specific yield shortage that another worker can solve. If the real problem is Approval, Science, or missing infrastructure, the Dust stays available for something else.

This becomes particularly useful late in a **Worship** campaign. Last Lords can close a population gap much faster than many factions, but reaching the population requirement does not complete the separate Monument requirement. The current objectives are covered in the <Link href="/endless-legend-2/victory-conditions">victory conditions</Link>.

  How should Last Lords use Minor Factions and Lord's Estates?
</h2>

Minor Faction Villages have extra strategic value for the Last Lords because they can support **Lord's Estates**.

An Estate can contribute Dust and population capacity, making the Village part of long-term City development rather than merely a source of normal Minor Faction benefits.

That can change which Territory is worth claiming.

A region with average terrain may still be attractive if its Village supports a useful Estate network, population source, or faction objective. At the same time, a Village does not automatically make a weak region good. Influence still has to secure the land, the border still has to be defended, and any new population still needs jobs and Approval.

By the midgame I look at Village clusters as a connected package:

- access to Minor Faction population;
- Lord's Estate value;
- Dust;
- population capacity;
- strategic position;
- and any faction progression that also requires pacified Villages.

The general decisions around Parlay, Bribe, Assimilation, Protectorates, and Minor Faction units are covered in the <Link href="/endless-legend-2/minor-factions">Minor Factions guide</Link>.

For the Last Lords, the additional question is whether the Village improves the Dust-and-population economy enough to justify controlling the surrounding region.

<h2 id="army-recovery">
  How should Last Lords use Dust for army healing?
</h2>

Paid recovery changes which battle damage is acceptable.

A surviving unit at low Health can often be restored with Dust after the fight. A dead unit has to be replaced completely and may take valuable experience with it.

That makes unit survival the important breakpoint.

I am willing to trade Health for a useful victory when the army can keep its experienced units alive. I am much less willing to lose a unit simply because the faction can spend Dust after combat.

Recovery also has practical limits. In one siege sequence, the damaged army had to pull back toward controlled land before proper recovery became available. A force deep in hostile territory therefore cannot assume that the treasury instantly erases every mistake.

This matters when faction quests point toward Fortresses or other difficult battles.

One Last Lords force reached a Fortress at roughly **1,200 army strength** against about **1,700** on the defending side. Rather than treating the quest marker as an instruction to attack immediately, the army delayed the fight and improved first.

Those numbers are not a universal Fortress requirement. Levels, equipment, unit composition, terrain, and manual battle decisions change the result.

The useful rule is simpler: Dust healing is excellent at repairing **survivable** damage. It does not solve a battle where the army is too weak to keep its units alive.

<h2 id="faction-quest">
  What can block the Last Lords faction quest?
</h2>

Several Last Lords quest steps depend on progression outside the visible quest location.

**Feudalism** becomes an early research gate. If the resurrection sequence stops advancing and the next action is unavailable, I check the technology tree before assuming that a location or interaction was missed.

The first **Marquis Galardi** resurrection can also look like a failure state when it is not.

Galardi's restored body can deteriorate and die, but his original soul record remains available. The sequence can continue through another resurrection rather than requiring a restart.

Later progression can involve the map as well. One step required the Empire to maintain at least **three pacified Village sites for a turn**. If the Empire has only two available, the next part of the faction story is effectively waiting on scouting and Minor Faction access.

That is another reason I want to know where nearby Villages are before the quest explicitly asks for them.

Galardi remains useful after the immediate resurrection objective. An achievement also asks the **Marquis Galardi Army** to win battles, so manageable neutral or Minor Faction fights are better opportunities than starting an unnecessary major war solely for that requirement.

The remaining hidden and faction-specific requirements are listed in the <Link href="/endless-legend-2/achievements">59-achievement checklist</Link>.

<h2 id="roundup">
  When does Roundup become dangerous?
</h2>

Later Last Lords progression can convert outside population into native Last Lord population more aggressively.

That can be economically attractive because native population fits the faction's Dust-centered system, but **Roundup** adds a stability risk.

Repeated use can create uprising pressure.

One quest sequence required multiple Roundups, and a Territory still rebelled even after the most unstable regions were avoided. That makes Approval something to check before beginning repeated conversions rather than after the warning appears.

I do not spread Roundup across several already fragile Cities simply because conversion is available.

The safe order is to stabilize the City first, make sure vocation capacity exists for the population that will remain, then decide whether the conversion benefit is worth the additional risk.

A later faction outcome can replace Roundup with **Conscription**, retaining the broad population-conversion function without the same uprising chance.

Because that change depends on faction progression, I do not plan the early economy as though Conscription is already available.

<h2 id="primordial-lords">
  Where should you use Primordial Lords?
</h2>

Primordial Lords are better treated as economic multipliers than emergency repair tools.

A weak City with poor infrastructure does not suddenly become strong because a premium economic piece arrives there. A developed City already producing large amounts of Dust, Industry, Science, or another valuable yield has much more to multiply.

That changes placement.

I build weak settlements with ordinary infrastructure first. Primordial Lords go where the existing economy is already good enough for their effect to matter.

The same logic applies to the faction's broader Dust economy.

Spending Dust to make a strong system faster is usually more efficient than repeatedly paying to compensate for a City whose basic problems were never fixed.

<h2 id="research-and-late-game">
  What should Last Lords research, and what changes once the Empire is rich?
</h2>

Research matters most where Dust cannot substitute for the missing system.

The capital needs enough Industry to function. **Feudalism** matters for faction progression. Approval technologies become increasingly valuable as purchased population adds pressure. Additional Minor Faction options can matter when Lord's Estates and useful Villages are available. Military research becomes important when paid healing is preserving units that are still fighting with outdated equipment.

Science itself is the easiest weakness to hide.

Dust can solve population, healing, and construction so quickly that the Empire feels powerful while its research rate falls behind. Eventually a unit, improvement, or quest needs a technology that cannot be purchased directly from the treasury.

I therefore use Dust to accelerate what the Empire has already unlocked rather than treating it as a replacement for Science.

The reserve itself changes with the campaign.

Before a dangerous battle, I spend less on ordinary construction because surviving units may need immediate recovery. During a peaceful stretch with stable Cities and healthy armies, more of the treasury can safely go into population, buildings, equipment, or extractors.

As the Empire grows, the number of possible Dust demands increases at the same time:

- more Cities can use population;
- more armies can need recovery;
- equipment becomes more expensive;
- new specialists need vocation capacity;
- and Approval or Science can become the real bottleneck even while the treasury keeps rising.

The strongest Last Lords position is therefore not the one with the largest number of Dust displayed.

It is the one where enough Dust remains available to solve the **next important problem**, while the problems Dust cannot solve — Approval, Science, job capacity, territorial access, and technology gates — are already under control.
