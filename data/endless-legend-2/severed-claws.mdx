<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
Scout nearby Minor Factions before committing your expansion. **Defeating a Village gives you one unit of that Minor Faction at the Capital; rebuilding the Village then adds one matching population.** Keep growing the single City through attached Territories, use Burrows to shorten reinforcement routes, and avoid wasting experienced units because Severed Claws do not replace battle losses through Larvae.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

Severed Claws keep the Necrophage one-city foundation but replace much of the hive economy with people and soldiers taken from Minor Factions. Their own population grows slowly, they do not receive the usual Larva reinforcement loop after battles, and the Villages around the starting region can determine both the workforce and the army for much of the campaign.

The important part is that destroying and rebuilding a Village are **not competing rewards**. The military unit arrives when the Village is defeated. Rebuilding that same Village is the next step that brings its population into the City.

<h2 id="how-severed-claws-work">
  How do Severed Claws work?
</h2>

Four rules shape most early decisions.

**There is only one City.** New regions strengthen the same capital through attached Territories instead of becoming a normal chain of independent Cities. Burrows help that increasingly wide Empire move armies between distant regions.

**Minor Faction Villages provide military units.** Every defeated Village grants **one unit of that Minor Faction's type**, and the new unit appears at the Severed Claws Capital rather than beside the army that fought the Village.

**Rebuilt Villages provide population.** Rebuilding a conquered Village grants **one population of that Minor Faction's type**. Leveling rebuilt Villages can provide additional population later.

**There is no normal Necrophage Larva cycle.** Native units must be produced at the Capital, while outside troops come from defeated Minor Faction Villages. Winning another battle does not automatically replenish the force that fought it.

That combination makes scouting unusually important. A region with an excellent Village can be more valuable than a neighboring region with slightly better raw yields because the Village may contribute both a useful worker type and a military role the native roster is missing.

<h2 id="starting-area">
  What should you scout before choosing your expansion direction?
</h2>

The capital still needs workable Food, Industry, and enough usable space for districts, but I do not judge the opening from terrain alone. Before committing heavily to one direction, I uncover enough nearby regions to see which Minor Factions are actually available.

I want to know what unit each Village provides, what its population does inside the City, and how difficult that region will be to connect to the capital. A nearby Village is especially valuable when both rewards solve something I already need: perhaps its unit fills a missing battlefield role while its population performs a vocation better than the Severed Claws population currently working there.

Several Villages can also overlap badly. If every nearby Minor Faction offers roughly the same military role or the same kind of worker, pushing toward another region can be worth the extra distance.

This is why I avoid locking the opening into a rigid expansion route before scouting. The City itself is permanent, but the population mix and army composition are partly determined by what the map puts around it.

<h2 id="village-cycle">
  How does the Severed Claws Village cycle work?
</h2>

The sequence is:

**find the Village → defeat it → receive one unit at the Capital → rebuild the Village → receive one matching population.**

The military reward comes first. Defeating a Minor Faction Village grants **one unit of that faction's type**, but the unit appears back at the Capital. If the attacking army is several Territories away, that reinforcement still needs time to reach the front.

Rebuilding changes the same conquest into an economic reward. Once the destroyed Village is rebuilt, **one population of that Minor Faction's type** joins the Severed Claws City. Rebuilt Villages can then be developed further for additional population.

Because the rewards arrive at different stages, I do not treat the choice as “unit or population.” I first decide whether the Village is worth attacking. Once the unit has already been gained, the next question is whether that population is useful enough to justify rebuilding and developing the Village.

A strong battlefield unit can make the initial conquest worthwhile even if the population is unremarkable. The reverse can also happen: the free unit may be little more than another body, while the population becomes valuable for the rest of the campaign because it fills a weak vocation in the capital.

Severed Claws also approach early Minor Factions differently from most Empires. They cannot Parley or Bribe them, and they cannot establish Protectorates before **Era 3**. The normal relationship between pacification, Assimilation, and Protectorates is covered in the <Link href="/endless-legend-2/minor-factions">Minor Factions guide</Link>.

<h2 id="population">
  How does Severed Claws population grow?
</h2>

The faction has two population sources that behave very differently.

**Native Severed Claws population can still grow through Food, but it grows slowly.** That growth remains important because native population helps determine how much captured population the single City can support.

**Minor Faction population does not simply reproduce by itself.** The reliable way to expand that part of the workforce is to conquer and rebuild more Villages, then continue developing the Villages that are worth keeping.

That makes the native population more than another set of workers. Even when Minor Faction specialists eventually handle many of the best economic jobs, continued native growth gives the City more room to absorb the populations being brought in from outside.

I therefore judge a rebuilt Village by what its population actually adds. A specialist that fixes weak Science, Industry, Dust, or another useful vocation can affect the capital for the rest of the game. Another population type may only duplicate jobs that are already well covered.

The result is a City whose final workforce can look very different from one map to another. One campaign may discover excellent economic specialists around the starting region, while another gets populations that matter more for their accompanying military units than for their jobs.

<h2 id="communal-habitation">
  Why is Communal Habitation different for Severed Claws?
</h2>

Communal Habitation does not perform the same job here that it does in a normal Empire.

For Severed Claws, it does **not** provide the usual population slots. Instead, it accelerates native population growth.

That matters because additional Severed Claws population feeds back into the one-city economy: more native population provides room for more captured population and also reduces District costs. Faster growth can therefore help the capital keep expanding rather than merely adding another worker to an existing vocation.

The useful sequence is closer to:

**grow native population → support more captured population → reduce District costs → keep developing the single City.**

I still do not build Communal Habitation automatically. Faster growth is useful when the City has jobs and economic room for the next population point. If Approval is already deteriorating or useful vocation slots are exhausted, accelerating growth can make that problem arrive sooner.

When Destitute population, Shanties, or low Approval become the real bottleneck, I stop solving the problem through growth and deal with the actual City condition instead. The relationship between vocation capacity, Shanties, production penalties, and rebellion is covered in the <Link href="/endless-legend-2/approval">Approval guide</Link>.

<h2 id="single-city">
  How should a one-city Severed Claws empire expand?
</h2>

Every new Territory has to improve the same capital, so expansion is less about founding another production center and more about deciding what the existing City still needs.

I give priority to regions that provide at least one concrete advantage:

- a Minor Faction Village whose unit or population I want;
- strategic or luxury resources;
- strong district space;
- access to another valuable region;
- or a position that shortens or protects an important frontier.

This can create an Empire that covers a large amount of land without ever becoming a normal multi-city network. The capital accumulates the economic value while armies operate across an increasingly long territorial footprint.

**Burrows** are what make that structure practical.

Without them, an army on one side of the Empire may lose several turns simply traveling back toward another threatened region. A useful Burrow connection turns distant attachments into locations that can actually reinforce one another.

I still avoid claiming disconnected land merely because Burrows exist. A remote Territory surrounded by enemies can create a new front faster than the army can defend it, and fast travel cannot compensate for arriving with too few units.

<h2 id="army">
  How should you build and replace a Severed Claws army?
</h2>

Minor Faction troops are not just temporary reinforcements. They can directly improve the faction's combat scaling through **Exploiter**:

- **+1 Might per Minor Faction unit in the army**
- **+1 Intuition per Minor Faction unit in the army**

I still choose units for what they actually do in battle. Three Minor Faction troops filling the same role do not become a balanced army simply because they improve Exploiter. The strongest Village network gives me different tools: a frontline body, sustain, range, mobility, or another capability the native roster cannot provide efficiently.

Replacement is the larger strategic problem.

Severed Claws do not generate Larvae after successful battles. Native replacements must be constructed at the Capital, and every unit gained from a defeated Village also begins there. An army fighting several Territories away can therefore win a battle and still become weaker for the next one.

Burrows reduce the travel problem, but they do not erase the cost of experienced casualties. Before committing to a long war, I check how the next unit will reach the front if the first two battles go badly rather than judging the campaign only from the strength of the opening army.

<h2 id="ever-fighting">
  How does Ever Fighting change war decisions?
</h2>

Severed Claws are designed to remain comfortable in situations that would make many other Empires want peace.

**Ever Fighting** makes their declared wars justified and gives their population an **Approval bonus while the Empire is at war**. At the same time, the faction gives up several peaceful tools:

- no diplomatic Technologies;
- no Parley with Minor Factions;
- no Bribe with Minor Factions.

That does not make an unnecessary war profitable. I still want the conflict to open access to something the single City needs: a useful Village, another Territory, a strategic resource, or a route that improves the shape of the Empire.

The Approval benefit makes staying at war less damaging internally, but it does not replace dead soldiers. A fight that gains nothing while destroying an experienced army can leave the faction waiting on replacements from the Capital with no Larva economy to repair the loss.

<h2 id="combat">
  How do you use the extra attack after a kill?
</h2>

Severed Claws reward planning the finishing blow instead of automatically killing the weakest target with whichever unit acts first.

When one of their units destroys an enemy, that attacker can attack again during the same turn. The value of the first kill therefore depends partly on where that unit will stand afterward and whether another useful target remains within reach.

If one enemy is already close to death while a more dangerous target is nearby, I try to let the unit with the best follow-up position claim the kill. Another attacker can soften the first enemy without finishing it, leaving the final blow for the unit that can immediately convert the extra action into pressure on the second target.

Movement is often the limiting factor. An attacker that earns another attack but finishes too far from every worthwhile target has gained very little. I work backward from the second action: decide what I want to hit next, then choose which unit should take the first kill.

That sequencing matters even more here because keeping experienced units alive has long-term value. A good chain attack is not only extra damage; it can shorten a fight and reduce the number of replacements that later have to travel from the Capital.

<h2 id="severed-claws-vs-necrophage">
  Severed Claws vs Necrophage: what actually changes?
</h2>

Both factions can build one enormous City across many attached Territories, but the resources feeding that City are fundamentally different.

**Necrophage** use warfare to generate Larvae and Corpses. Successful battles help replenish and develop the same faction that fought them, so the army and internal economy continually feed one another.

**Severed Claws** lose that Larva replacement cycle. Instead, the surrounding Minor Factions become part of the faction's growth: defeating their Villages supplies outside military units, rebuilding those Villages adds population, and native Severed Claws population grows slowly alongside that captured workforce.

That difference changes what I look for on the map. Necrophage can rely more heavily on their own recurring war economy. Severed Claws need to care much more about which Minor Factions are nearby, which Villages are worth developing, and how far new units will have to travel from the Capital.

The shared one-city structure may look familiar, but the campaign becomes much less predictable. The population mix, army roster, expansion direction, and even the value of neighboring Territories can all change according to the Minor Factions the map provides.
