<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
Choose your faction before copying any opening, get the capital stable before adding another weak frontier, and keep enough **Influence and army strength** to use the land your scouts reveal. When Monsoon begins, prepare for Tidefall; once the Empire is established, start comparing victory paths before one objective becomes expensive to recover.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

ENDLESS Legend 2 does not reward one opening copied across every faction.

Kin of Sheredyn can turn a compact capital into their strongest asset. Order of Zelevas want Territory to keep changing hands. Last Lords can solve delays with Dust. Sandshapers may prefer a dry Ruin region over land that looks fertile to everyone else.

The easiest way to read a campaign is to start with what your faction needs, then identify the resource or system currently preventing the next useful action.

<h2 id="first-30-turns">
  What should you do in the first 30 turns?
</h2>

The opening is less about hitting a fixed turn-by-turn build order and more about preventing one part of the Empire from falling far behind the others.

Scout long enough to understand the starting region, then settle before searching for a better capital turns into wasted movement. Once the City exists, watch whether construction, population, Influence, research, and the army are developing at roughly compatible speeds.

If buildings take too long, more Industry may be the answer. If scouts have already found excellent land but the next Camp is unaffordable, Influence matters more. If population is arriving without useful vocation slots, stop treating growth as automatically good. If another border would leave the only army unable to defend both sides, the Empire is not ready for that expansion yet.

The <Link href="/endless-legend-2/beginner-guide">first 30 turns guide</Link> covers first-City timing, scouting, the first Camp, early Dust spending, research, army strength, and how to recognize an opening that has started to stall.

<h2 id="factions">
  Which ENDLESS Legend 2 faction should you play?
</h2>

There are **eight playable factions in 1.0**, and they change what counts as a good start.

For a first campaign, **Kin of Sheredyn** are the easiest place to learn Cities, population, districts, Influence, and combat without being pushed immediately into an unusual economy.

**Order of Zelevas** suit a campaign built around battles and territorial momentum. **Last Lords** turn Dust into population, recovery, and immediate economic tempo. **Necrophage** make warfare part of the economic engine rather than a separate expense.

**Severed Claws** depend heavily on which Minor Faction Villages appear nearby. **Aspects** get much more from diplomacy and Protectorates. **Tahuks** turn Science into both technological progress and an active resource. **Sandshapers** reshape the map through Ruins, Sand, Wishes, and later teleportation.

The <Link href="/endless-legend-2/factions">all 8 factions comparison</Link> is the fastest way to compare their playstyles, main resources, and pressures.

If you have already chosen one of the factions with a more unusual progression loop, continue with <Link href="/endless-legend-2/kin-of-sheredyn">Kin of Sheredyn</Link>, <Link href="/endless-legend-2/order-of-zelevas">Order of Zelevas</Link>, <Link href="/endless-legend-2/last-lords">Last Lords</Link>, <Link href="/endless-legend-2/severed-claws">Severed Claws</Link>, or <Link href="/endless-legend-2/sandshapers">Sandshapers</Link>.

<h2 id="empire-problems">
  What should you fix when the Empire stops moving forward?
</h2>

Most slow midgames look like several problems arriving at once, but one of them usually appeared first.

**Population and Approval:** more population is only useful while Cities have jobs, capacity, and enough stability to support it. Destitute population, Shanties, falling Food or Industry, and rebellion are signs that growth has outrun the City. The recovery sequence is covered in <Link href="/endless-legend-2/approval">Approval and population management</Link>.

**Influence:** good land is useless if the Empire cannot afford the Camp, attachment, City, or faction action needed to secure it. When expansion repeatedly waits on Influence, adding more targets to the map only increases the backlog.

**Army strength:** every new frontier creates another place that may need protection. If one battle forces several turns of rebuilding, territorial growth has probably moved ahead of the military.

**Dust:** this can be an ordinary reserve or a core faction resource. Order can turn it directly into armies, while Last Lords use the same treasury for population and recovery. Spending it on routine acceleration can be expensive when the faction needs it for something only Dust can solve.

**Science:** an economy can look strong while research quietly falls behind. Dust, population, and Territory cannot replace a Technology that unlocks the unit or infrastructure the Empire now needs.

When two or three systems appear broken at once, I fix the earliest constraint instead of opening another front.

<h2 id="minor-factions">
  When is a Minor Faction worth changing your expansion plan for?
</h2>

A Minor Faction Village can make an otherwise average Territory much more important.

Population, recruitable units, Notables, assimilation effects, and Protectorates can all alter what the Empire is capable of doing. Several playable factions push that relationship even further: Last Lords can build Lord's Estates around Villages, Severed Claws take population and troops from them, and Aspects build much more of the campaign around diplomatic relationships.

That means the first Village discovered is not automatically the one to build around, but it should be inspected before the next large Influence commitment.

The <Link href="/endless-legend-2/minor-factions">Minor Factions guide</Link> covers Parlay, Bribe, Attack, Assimilation, Protectorate slots, population, units, village scaling, and the faction rules that change those choices.

<h2 id="tidefall">
  What should you do before Monsoon ends and Tidefall begins?
</h2>

Monsoon is the warning that the strategic map is about to change.

Temporary bridges, Curiosities, vision, battle conditions, and Minor Faction behavior can change during the storm. The larger consequence arrives afterward when **Tidefall exposes new land**.

A coastline that looked unimportant can reveal resources, Ruins, new Territories, Fortresses, or a route toward another Empire. That is why I avoid committing the last mobile unit and the last large block of Influence immediately before the coastline changes.

Once Tidefall happens, scout the exposed land before assuming yesterday's best expansion target is still today's.

The <Link href="/endless-legend-2/tidefall">Tidefall and Monsoon guide</Link> covers temporary bridge danger, Monsoon effects, positioning before Tidefall, newly exposed land, and later weather cycles.

<h2 id="victory">
  Which victory path should you work toward?
</h2>

ENDLESS Legend 2 has three Awakening paths: **Worship, Impress, and Master**, alongside Conquest and the eventual Score result.

The useful question is not simply which path currently shows the most progress.

Look at the objective that will be hardest to finish.

A population-heavy Empire may appear close to Worship while still being far behind on Monuments. A research leader can look perfect for Impress until the required pacified Villages turn out to be controlled by another Empire. A wide Empire may already satisfy much of Master but still need to solve the Doomwraith side of the path.

Once one route clearly matches what the Empire has already built, start protecting that advantage instead of waiting until the final turns to discover that the second objective is still far away.

The <Link href="/endless-legend-2/victory-conditions">victory conditions guide</Link> covers Worship, Impress, Master, Conquest, Score, path switching, rival warnings, and the final maintenance period.

<h2 id="achievements">
  Which achievements need planning before the campaign ends?
</h2>

ENDLESS Legend 2 has **59 achievements**, but many do not need to shape every turn.

Long battle, Curiosity, Dust, treaty, and turn counters can advance naturally across multiple games. The achievements worth remembering early are the ones tied to a particular faction, quest outcome, victory path, difficulty setting, unusual combat trigger, or exact Empire state at the end of a turn or game.

That includes the three Awakening victories, faction requirements such as Kin Chosen or Last Lords Lord's Estates, and setups where several conditions must exist simultaneously.

The <Link href="/endless-legend-2/achievements">59-achievement checklist</Link> includes all requirements and separates ordinary progression from the goals that benefit from a deliberate campaign setup.

<h2 id="where-next">
  What should you focus on once the opening is stable?
</h2>

By midgame, the Empire should have a recognizable reason for becoming stronger.

Kin should be getting real value from a developed capital rather than adding mediocre Cities. Order should be turning battles into Territory without exhausting the Dust needed to defend it. Last Lords should be spending aggressively without letting jobs, Approval, or Science fall behind.

Necrophage should be gaining resources from warfare. Severed Claws should have a useful reason for the Villages they are exploiting. Aspects should be getting meaningful value from Minor Faction relationships. Tahuks should have enough Science to use faction powers without losing control of their Cities. Sandshapers should be moving from isolated Ruins toward a functioning network.

At the same time, the map is becoming less forgiving. Tidefall can create new borders, rivals begin competing for the same regions, Minor Factions become strategically important, and victory objectives start competing for Influence, army turns, and economic attention.

At that point, I stop asking which move gives the largest immediate number.

I look for the next action that strengthens the Empire **without creating a larger weakness somewhere else**.
