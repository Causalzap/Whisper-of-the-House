<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
Build the Kin capital around **Works, Keeps, and high district levels**. Keep the Fortification that matters for **Strength of Our Zeal inside the capital region**, preserve enough Influence for expansion, and let native Kin population handle Industry while other populations cover jobs they perform better.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

Kin of Sheredyn are strongest when several systems reinforce the same City.

Works provide the Industry needed to construct larger districts. Keeps improve adjacency while adding Fortification. That Fortification advances **Strength of Our Zeal**, which unlocks Holy Commandments and expands access to the **Chosen**. Native Kin population then feeds the production economy that keeps the entire cycle moving.

The faction can defend extremely well, but the point is not to sit behind walls forever. A developed capital turns those walls into military and economic tools that can be used across the rest of the map.

<h2 id="capital-layout">
  How should you build the Kin of Sheredyn capital?
</h2>

Kin begin with **Workshop** and **Stonework**, so Works and Keeps are available immediately.

That makes the starting City layout unusually important.

I want good terrain for the first Works, but I also want enough clean space around the City Hall to support later adjacency. Kin districts can reach **level 3**, and a **Keep counts as two districts when contributing adjacency toward district levels**.

This is why a slightly better starting yield is not always worth sacrificing the shape of the capital.

A Works placed on strong Industry terrain helps immediately. A Keep placed beside several important districts can then improve their level progression while also claiming nearby Foundations without the usual Influence purchase.

Every Kin district also provides **25 Fortification per district level**, so the City is already moving toward faction progression while its economy develops.

The opening usually works better when the first strong Works comes before several Keeps. Keeps are expensive enough that constructing too many before the capital has real Industry can slow population support, improvements, and the rest of the district plan.

In my Fast-speed opening, the difference between those early investments was already large:

- **Works:** 45 Industry
- **Keep:** 113 Industry
- **Keep Fortification:** 525 in that position

That made the Keep a deliberate military and layout investment rather than something I placed automatically whenever it was available.

A practical early layout therefore has three goals at once:

- put Works where the terrain actually supports Industry;
- use Keeps where their double adjacency improves several useful districts;
- leave enough flexible Foundations for level-3 development and later Kin structures.

Filling every available Foundation immediately can make the City larger without making it better.

<h2 id="strength-of-our-zeal">
  How does Strength of Our Zeal work?
</h2>

**Strength of Our Zeal is driven by Fortification in the capital region.**

That geographic restriction matters.

Fortification built in attached Territories can still defend those locations and improve their Cities, but it does not replace Fortification inside the region containing the Kin capital when advancing this faction progression.

Keeps are especially effective here because they combine several jobs that Kin already want done: district adjacency, Fortification, Foundation control, and capital defense.

As Strength of Our Zeal rises, it unlocks **Holy Commandments** and increases access to **Chosen** units.

Holy Commandments turn the capital's defensive investment into active powers rather than leaving Fortification as a purely passive number. Their use is connected to Kin Cities and Chosen, so the faction's strongest central City eventually supports operations well beyond its own walls.

That is the real payoff for concentrating Fortification correctly. A fortified capital is not only harder to capture; it becomes the source of additional tools and elite military power.

<h2 id="population-economy">
  Where should Kin population work, and what limits their growth?
</h2>

Native Kin population is naturally suited to **Artisan** work.

When possible, I let other populations cover jobs such as Food or Science while Kin remain on Industry. Minor Factions are especially useful for this because the right population can fill a vocation that would otherwise force native Kin away from their strongest role.

The <Link href="/endless-legend-2/minor-factions">Minor Factions guide</Link> covers the full pacification, population, and Protectorate decisions. For Kin, the main benefit is specialization: outside populations can cover weaknesses while Kin keep producing the Industry needed for Works, Keeps, units, and later districts.

Native Kin population also reaches important empire-wide thresholds:

- at **5 Kin population**, **Military Press** becomes available;
- at **15 Kin population**, Kin gain additional Industry outside the Artisan role;
- at **30 Kin population**, Cities containing Kin gain a broader Industry benefit.

Growth still has to fit the City's vocation capacity.

Kin cannot build the normal **Forum**, removing one common source of Approval and Influence support. Upgraded Kin districts help compensate by producing Influence, while **Warrior Honor** allows military districts to contribute Approval.

That keeps district levels valuable even when the capital is not under attack.

It does not remove population pressure completely. An Artisan-heavy City can become productive enough to grow faster than its Approval or vocation slots can comfortably support. When Destitute population or Shanties begin appearing, the recovery rules are the same ones covered in the <Link href="/endless-legend-2/approval">Approval guide</Link>.

Influence needs similar restraint.

Kin still spend it on Camps, Territories, and other expansion actions while also having faction-specific uses. **Divine Service**, for example, can create Divine population; its first use costs **50 Influence**, with the cost increasing by another **50** for subsequent uses.

That can be an excellent conversion when the Empire is staying compact for several turns. It is much harder to justify when the same Influence is needed to secure an important neighboring region.

<h2 id="chosen">
  How should you use Chosen in battle?
</h2>

Chosen become much stronger when the army preserves the Kin formation mechanics around them.

**Stand United gives Kin units 8 Shield for each adjacent friendly unit.**

That encourages compact formations where several units protect one another rather than scattering across the battlefield.

Chosen can then convert part of that defense into offense. One of their effects adds damage equal to **50% of their Shield**, so Shield accumulated before an attack can significantly increase their damage.

An isolated Chosen loses much of that advantage.

I prefer to move the formation so that the Chosen reaches its target with friendly units still beside it. Defender effects can add even more Shield before the attack, increasing both survival and damage.

They also contribute to pressure against fortified positions, so they are useful before a siege turns into a normal field battle.

The army still needs range.

A group made entirely of durable melee units can spend too many turns chasing Archers or breaking formation to reach them. Chosen work better as the center of the line while ranged support removes targets that would otherwise pull the formation apart.

<h2 id="divine-monument">
  Where should you place a Divine Monument?
</h2>

The **Divine Monument** rewards district planning that began much earlier in the campaign.

It can be built once per Territory and gains different benefits from the districts placed directly around it:

- **8 Influence per adjacent district level**
- **6 Food per adjacent Farm**
- **4 Industry per adjacent Works**
- **6 Dust per adjacent Merchant House**
- **4 Science per adjacent Laboratory**
- **5 Approval per adjacent Communal Habitation**

There is no single ideal ring around every Divine Monument.

A production-heavy capital naturally values adjacent Works. A City struggling with Approval can get more from Communal Habitation. Another Territory may be better used as a Science or Dust cluster.

What matters is preserving a location where several useful districts can surround the Monument.

This is another reason not to treat early Foundation placement as disposable. A Works chosen for good terrain in the opening can later become one part of a much stronger Divine Monument cluster.

<h2 id="research">
  Which technologies should Kin prioritize?
</h2>

Because Kin already start with Workshop and Stonework, the first research choices do not need to unlock the faction's basic Works-and-Keeps plan.

Research is more valuable when it repairs whichever resource the capital cannot produce efficiently yet.

**Low Science** is one of the more common problems. A strong Industry start can still crawl through technologies if the capital never adds meaningful Science.

**Dust** can become the next shortage once army upkeep rises.

**Influence** matters whenever expansion is waiting on Camps or attached Territories, and it competes directly with faction actions such as Divine Service.

**Approval** becomes urgent if Artisan-heavy growth begins pushing the capital into production penalties.

The early technology tree contains several direct fixes, including flat Dust, flat Influence, Approval support, and Fortification improvements. One Fortification option adds **300 Fortification to the City Hall**, **200 to the Ministry**, and reduces **Keep Industry cost by 20%**, making it particularly valuable when more Keeps are about to enter the queue.

A rigid research order misses the point of Kin's opening.

If Science is the only resource preventing progress, another military unlock does little. If Influence is delaying the next Territory, improving production again may simply create more things the Empire cannot place.

When the starting region is weak, this becomes even more important. A poor resource roll does not require forcing an aggressive expansion into mediocre land. The capital can continue improving its district geometry, Fortification, and basic yields while scouts look for a Territory that is actually worth the Influence.

<h2 id="expansion">
  How wide should Kin of Sheredyn expand?
</h2>

Kin are designed to get more out of fewer Cities.

They have **one less City capacity than a normal faction**, but each City can support **one additional attached Territory**.

That makes attachment particularly valuable.

A region with strategic resources, a useful Minor Faction Village, strong district space, or an important defensive position does not automatically need its own production queue. Attaching it to an established Kin City can strengthen an economy that already has high district levels and faction infrastructure instead of creating another City that needs to build everything from scratch.

Influence is another reason not to turn every Camp into a City.

The general choice between Camps, attached Territories, and new Cities is covered in the <Link href="/endless-legend-2/beginner-guide">first 30 turns</Link>. Kin change the weighting: strengthening an existing City is often the intended path rather than a compromise.

A useful expansion should answer at least one concrete need — resources, population, military position, district space, or access to the next region.

If it does not, waiting for a better Territory is often stronger than increasing the Empire's footprint simply because another Camp can be placed.

The Kin economy becomes dangerous once the same developed Cities are producing enough Industry to build quickly, enough Fortification to advance Strength of Our Zeal, enough Influence to keep expanding, and enough military power to send Chosen beyond the capital. The faction does not need to match a wide Empire City for City when each existing City is becoming substantially more productive.
