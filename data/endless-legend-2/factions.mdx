<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
ENDLESS Legend 2 has **8 playable factions** in 1.0. **Kin of Sheredyn** are the most straightforward first campaign; choose **Order** for expansion, **Last Lords** for a Dust economy, **Necrophage** for constant warfare, **Severed Claws** for Minor Faction armies, **Aspects** for diplomacy, **Tahuks** for Science, or **Sandshapers** for Ruins and terraforming.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

Faction choice changes much more than a passive bonus in ENDLESS Legend 2.

The same region can be excellent for one Empire and awkward for another. Some factions want a small number of highly developed Cities, while others gain power by controlling more Territory. Some turn Dust into armies or population; others build around Minor Factions, Science, warfare, or the physical shape of the map.

For a first campaign, **Kin of Sheredyn** are the easiest faction to read because their strength grows through visible City development, Fortification, and compact armies. After that, the better choice depends on which part of the game you want to manage most often.

<h2 id="kin-of-sheredyn">
  Kin of Sheredyn – build fewer Cities much further
</h2>

Kin of Sheredyn favor a compact Empire where City quality matters more than quickly filling the map.

Their economy naturally connects **Industry, district development, Fortification, Strength of Our Zeal, and Chosen**. They also operate with a lower City limit than most factions, making a strong capital and well-chosen attached Territories more important than simply founding another settlement whenever good land appears.

That makes the Kin a useful first faction because most of their important decisions are visible: where districts go, whether the capital has enough Industry, how Fortification is developing, and whether expansion is improving the existing Cities or merely adding more obligations.

Their battlefield style follows the same principle. Kin units benefit from staying organized around one another rather than scattering across the map.

The <Link href="/endless-legend-2/kin-of-sheredyn">Kin of Sheredyn guide</Link> covers their capital layout, Strength of Our Zeal, population economy, Chosen, Divine Monument, research, and expansion.

<h2 id="order-of-zelevas">
  Order of Zelevas – turn battles into more Territory
</h2>

Order of Zelevas take several ideas shared with the Kin and point them outward.

Their faction progression depends on **controlled Territories**, while victories can generate Influence that helps finance the next claim. Their armies are recruited instantly with **Dust**, which lets the Empire react quickly when a new frontier becomes dangerous.

That creates a much faster territorial rhythm than the Kin.

A good Order position has enough Influence to claim useful land, enough Dust to replace or expand the army, and enough military strength to defend what was just taken. When any one of those falls behind, the map can become larger without the Empire actually becoming stronger.

Keeps, Chosen, Holy Commandments, and later territorial rewards all reinforce the same expansion loop.

The <Link href="/endless-legend-2/order-of-zelevas">Order of Zelevas guide</Link> covers Loyalty to the Emperor, Dust recruitment, Keeps, combat, and how to keep a wide Empire connected.

<h2 id="last-lords">
  Last Lords – use Dust to remove waiting
</h2>

Last Lords make the treasury unusually flexible.

Dust can accelerate population, restore damaged armies, support development, and solve several problems that another faction would have to wait through. That gives the Last Lords excellent control over timing but also makes overspending more dangerous.

The key question is rarely whether a purchase is affordable.

It is whether spending on population now leaves enough Dust for military recovery, whether another worker has a useful vocation, and whether the City can absorb the additional Approval pressure.

Minor Faction Villages also matter because **Lord's Estates** can turn them into part of the Last Lords population and Dust economy.

The <Link href="/endless-legend-2/last-lords">Last Lords guide</Link> covers population purchases, Approval, Lord's Estates, army recovery, faction progression, Roundup, and Primordial Lords.

<h2 id="necrophage">
  Necrophage – warfare supplies the next war
</h2>

Necrophage are built around a **single City with many attached Territories** and an economy that expects the army to keep fighting.

Winning battles produces **Larvae**, while Corpses feed other parts of the faction's development. Military success therefore does more than remove enemies: it creates resources that help sustain the next phase of the campaign.

That changes what a quiet turn costs.

Another faction may be comfortable developing Cities while avoiding unnecessary fights. Necrophage lose part of their natural momentum when the army spends too long without producing Larvae or Corpses.

Their one-City structure also changes expansion. New regions are valuable because they strengthen the central Empire through attached land, resources, movement, and military access rather than because every good Territory should become another independent City.

Minor Faction diplomacy is much more restricted as well, pushing the faction toward military solutions where another Empire might use peaceful relationships.

Necrophage are a strong fit when you want warfare to be part of the economy rather than something the economy pauses to support.

<h2 id="severed-claws">
  Severed Claws – let the nearby Villages define the Empire
</h2>

Severed Claws also build around one City, but their growth does not follow the Necrophage Larva loop.

Their most important external resource is the **Minor Faction Village**.

Destroying a Village can provide that faction's military unit, while rebuilding a pacified Village can add its population to the capital. The result is an army and population mix shaped by whichever Minor Factions actually appeared nearby.

That makes scouting especially important.

One map may offer populations that cover several economic weaknesses and units that complement each other. Another may produce several Villages with overlapping roles, forcing the campaign in a different direction.

Their relationship with Minor Factions is much more aggressive than standard Protectorate play, and their combat mechanics reward keeping offensive momentum once enemies begin falling.

The <Link href="/endless-legend-2/severed-claws">Severed Claws guide</Link> covers the Village cycle, population, the one-City economy, Ever Fighting, and their kill-based combat sequencing.

<h2 id="aspects">
  Aspects – build power through Minor Faction relationships
</h2>

Aspects put **Influence, Protectorates, Coral, and diplomacy** much closer to the center of the campaign.

Minor Factions are particularly important because Aspects can assimilate them without the normal Influence payment and can support more of those relationships than a typical Empire.

That means scouting is partly about identifying what the surrounding peoples can add.

A useful Protectorate can supply population, recruitable units, Notables, and an empire-wide effect. Those outside strengths matter more when the native faction does not want to solve every military or economic role by itself.

Coral gives the map another layer, while Influence supports both political relationships and faction actions.

The result is an Empire that often becomes stronger by connecting several different external advantages rather than concentrating everything into one native military or production engine.

The standard village choices, population effects, assimilation bonuses, units, and Protectorate limits are explained in the <Link href="/endless-legend-2/minor-factions">Minor Factions guide</Link>.

<h2 id="tahuks">
  Tahuks – turn Science into an active resource
</h2>

Tahuks make Science more than a race through the Technology tree.

**Observatories** make mountains and ridges especially valuable, producing Science during normal conditions and changing behavior during Monsoon. The faction can then use Science through powers associated with the **Holy Oculum**, creating a choice between saving research for Technologies and spending some of that advantage on an immediate effect.

That makes the faction's strongest resource surprisingly easy to misuse.

Being ahead in research does not automatically mean every Science-powered action is worth paying for. The effect has to matter more than whatever Technology is being delayed.

Tahuk powers can also create **Called** population, while Festivals and other population interactions can increase pressure on City management and Approval.

A Tahuk Empire can therefore be technologically dominant while still struggling at home.

Monsoon also affects their Observatory economy, so weather has direct economic consequences rather than acting only as a map event. The full weather cycle is covered in <Link href="/endless-legend-2/tidefall">Tidefall and Monsoon</Link>.

<h2 id="sandshapers">
  Sandshapers – change the land instead of accepting it
</h2>

Sandshapers break many of the terrain instincts that work for the rest of the roster.

Their Food economy relies heavily on **Sand**, nearby **Ruins** shape settlement value, and water can make an otherwise attractive region much less useful.

The early campaign revolves around finding Buried Ruins, Raising them for permanent value and Ancient Wisdom, then deciding where Wishes and Restorations are worth additional Dust.

Later, Restored Ruins can become long-distance movement points, turning the network into part of both the economy and military map.

Resource-heavy Territories are also unusually attractive because the faction can connect extraction with Food and other bonuses.

Monsoon hurts the Sandshapers more through Storm Cursed, while Lands of the Past helps them anticipate the land revealed by Tidefall.

The <Link href="/endless-legend-2/sandshapers">Sandshapers guide</Link> covers first-City placement, Sand Food, Ruin progression, Wishes, teleportation, combat, and Tidefall planning.

<h2 id="rogue-factions">
  What are Rogue Factions in ENDLESS Legend 2?
</h2>

ENDLESS Legend 2 has two Rogue Factions:

- **Order of Zelevas**, connected to the Kin of Sheredyn
- **Severed Claws**, connected to Necrophage

They reuse parts of the related faction's foundation but replace the system driving the campaign.

Kin and Order both use Chosen and Holy Commandments, yet their strategic direction is almost opposite. Kin gain strength by concentrating development and Fortification, while Order gain strength through Territory and aggressive expansion.

Necrophage and Severed Claws both work around one central City and attached Territories, but their replacement economies differ. Necrophage turn warfare into Larvae and Corpses. Severed Claws draw population and military units from Minor Faction Villages.

The shared foundation makes the relationship recognizable without making the two factions interchangeable.

<h2 id="which-faction">
  Which faction should you choose first?
</h2>

For a first full campaign, **Kin of Sheredyn** are the safest place to learn how Cities, population, Influence, districts, and combat fit together without adding too many unusual rules at once.

If you already know what kind of campaign you want, narrow the choice by the system you want to spend the most time managing. **Order of Zelevas** and **Necrophage** both reward frequent warfare, but Order turn victories into territorial momentum while Necrophage need combat to keep their replacement economy moving. **Last Lords** and **Tahuks** are better fits when you want one resource — Dust or Science — to create unusual strategic choices throughout the campaign.

Choose **Aspects** or **Severed Claws** when Minor Factions should have an unusually large effect on how the Empire develops. Aspects build those relationships through diplomacy and Protectorates; Severed Claws turn nearby Villages into population and military options through a much more aggressive system.

**Sandshapers** are the most distinct choice when you want the map itself to be part of the puzzle. Their Ruins, Sand, Wishes, water restrictions, and later teleport network make settlement and expansion work differently from the normal economy.

Once the faction is chosen, evaluate the starting map through its mechanics rather than comparing every opening with the same Food, Industry, and City-count expectations.
