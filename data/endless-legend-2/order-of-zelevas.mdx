<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
Keep a real **Dust reserve** for instant military recruitment, take winnable battles that feed **Influence**, and spend that Influence on Territories that improve the Empire instead of expanding in a straight line. Territory control raises **Loyalty to the Emperor**, eventually unlocking more Chosen, stronger powers, and the removal of the City Cap.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

Order of Zelevas turns conquest into an economic cycle.

The army wins battles, those victories help produce the Influence and spoils needed for the next stage of expansion, and each controlled Territory advances **Loyalty to the Emperor**. Meanwhile, military recruitment bypasses the normal Industry queue entirely: Order units are bought instantly with Dust.

That combination makes the faction fast when all three resources stay in balance. Dust without Influence leaves an army with nowhere useful to expand. Influence without military reserves creates borders that cannot be defended. Territory without roads, Cities, or economic value can advance Loyalty while making the Empire harder to hold together.

<h2 id="order-loop">
  How should you play the Order of Zelevas?
</h2>

The basic rhythm is:

**win useful battles → gain resources and Influence → secure useful Territory → raise Loyalty → use the larger Empire to support the next campaign.**

Battle victories provide bonus Influence, with the amount improving as the technological eras advance. The faction also benefits from battle spoils and starts with tools that make fighting part of the economy rather than a pure military expense.

The useful fights are not necessarily wars against another major Empire.

Neutral armies, hostile quest forces, and other manageable battles can generate momentum without committing the Order to a front the treasury cannot yet sustain. A battle that leaves the main army nearly destroyed may technically be a victory while still delaying the next claim because Dust has to rebuild the force first.

Expansion works best when each new Territory gives the army another reason to be there: a resource, a valuable border position, access to another region, or land that strengthens an existing City.

<h2 id="loyalty">
  How does Loyalty to the Emperor work?
</h2>

The Order replaces the Kin's Fortification-based progression with **controlled Territories**.

Each controlled region advances **Loyalty to the Emperor**, unlocking military and expansion rewards as the gauge rises.

At **5 controlled regions**, Chosen become available.

At **9 regions**, the Order gains further Chosen capacity together with additional specializations and faction powers.

The final Loyalty tier changes the Empire much more dramatically:

**the City Cap is removed entirely.**

That does not mean the Order should immediately turn every Camp into another City. It changes the restriction, not the cost of expansion. Influence is still limited, new Cities still need infrastructure and population, and every independent settlement adds another location that may need defense.

What disappears is the hard ceiling.

An excellent Territory no longer has to remain attached simply because the Empire has run out of City capacity. By that stage, the decision becomes whether the land is good enough to support another City and whether the economy can afford to develop it.

Loyalty also unlocks **Holy Commandments** and more ways to use Chosen, so Territory control is doing more than increasing the size of the map. Expansion steadily widens the faction's military toolkit.

<h2 id="dust-economy">
  How much Dust should the Order keep for its army?
</h2>

Order military units are recruited **instantly with Dust** instead of being produced with Industry over several turns.

This is one of the faction's strongest advantages because a City can keep constructing infrastructure while the treasury produces soldiers on demand. It is also the easiest way to misread the economy.

A large Dust balance is not necessarily spare money.

Part of it is an army that has not been purchased yet.

If a new Camp creates another exposed border, a replacement unit can appear immediately as long as the treasury can afford it. If the Dust was spent on marginal buyouts one turn earlier, Rapid Deployment provides no protection at all.

The faction's native population supports this economy naturally. Order population performs well as **Artisans**, combining Industry with additional Dust. The **Merchant House** is also available from the start, allowing the City to build toward stronger Dust income early rather than waiting for a later era.

I spend more freely when the army is healthy, nearby rivals are quiet, and the next territorial claim is still several turns away. Spending slows down when another Empire approaches an important Camp or when one bad battle could require several immediate replacements.

Army wipes are especially dangerous. The faction can recover faster than most Empires, but only if enough Dust still exists to rebuild.

The same reserve rule applies to **Chosen** on a larger scale. A first Chosen can consume well over a thousand Dust; one early purchase cost **1,350 Dust**. Buying it is much easier to justify when the ordinary frontline is already covered than when the purchase empties the treasury while another border still needs basic soldiers.

<h2 id="city-planning">
  How should Order Cities use Keeps and district levels?
</h2>

The Order retains Keeps, but it does not copy the Kin's City layout.

Unlike Kin of Sheredyn, Order districts do **not** reach level 3. They stop at the normal level-2 ceiling, while the faction compensates by making districts easier to level around important centers such as the City Hall and Ministries.

That means I do not build an Order City around the same dense level-3 adjacency puzzle used by the <Link href="/endless-legend-2/kin-of-sheredyn">Kin of Sheredyn</Link>.

The City Hall and Ministries act as useful anchors, and the surrounding tiles can be chosen more aggressively for the yields the Empire currently needs.

Keeps have an additional military reward:

**every completed Keep creates a free Cavalry unit.**

That makes a good Keep unusually efficient. The City gains a military district while the Empire gains another mobile unit without paying the normal Dust recruitment cost.

Those Cavalry units are useful well beyond direct combat. They can reveal nearby Territory, collect Curiosities, reach future Camp locations, watch a rival border, reinforce a battle, or be ready near a coast before Tidefall.

The free unit does not make a bad Keep location good, however. The district remains part of the City after the Cavalry rides away. I still choose the tile for the long-term City first, then treat the unit as additional value.

Population growth needs the same attention.

Territorial warfare can easily pull attention away from the home Cities while population quietly fills every vocation slot. Once workers become Destitute, Shanties and Approval penalties can begin undermining the Industry and Dust economy supporting the army.

When that starts happening, the detailed recovery sequence is in the <Link href="/endless-legend-2/approval">Approval guide</Link>. For the Order, the main danger is simply noticing the crisis several turns late because the army is operating far from home.

<h2 id="combat">
  How should Order armies use Into the Breach and Chosen?
</h2>

The Order rewards aggressive contact rather than the Kin's tightly packed friendly formation.

**Into the Breach gives 12 Shield for each adjacent enemy unit when attacking or defending.**

That makes a frontline unit harder to punish when it enters contact with several targets, but the Shield should not be mistaken for permission to get surrounded without support. Three adjacent enemies may provide a large Shield gain while also creating three sources of incoming damage.

The best use is to enter fights the rest of the army can finish.

A Legionary can hold the center while ranged units and the Hero attack from safer positions. If the Shield absorbs retaliation while nearby allies remove the enemies generating that pressure, the affinity is doing real work.

Chosen amplify the same idea.

One early Chosen displayed **200 Health**, **10 Armor**, and **Juggernaut**, making it a durable siege and frontline unit. Its more important synergy is **Righteous Wrath**:

**bonus Damage equals 50% of its Shield.**

Into the Breach can therefore feed the Chosen offensively as well as defensively. Additional Shield from other abilities raises Righteous Wrath's damage again.

Chosen also change how Holy Commandments travel with the army.

Several Order powers normally depend on controlled Territory, while the presence of a Chosen can extend where those abilities are usable. **Rejuvenation**, for example, can restore an army's Movement and ability to attack, turning a successful action into another move when the positioning allows it.

That makes Chosen valuable for tempo, not just raw combat statistics. Territory unlocks them, Dust buys them, and then they help the Order project its faction powers beyond the safest parts of the Empire.

<h2 id="expansion">
  Which Territories should the Order claim, and how do you keep them connected?
</h2>

Loyalty rewards quantity, but the Empire still has to live with the land after it is claimed.

A resource-rich backfill can be stronger than another forward Camp even though both increase the controlled-Territory count. The backfill may connect existing holdings, secure a strategic deposit, and shorten a vulnerable border at the same time.

Before spending Influence, I look for land that does at least one useful job: blocking a rival, securing a strategic or luxury resource, connecting the current border, adding a valuable Minor Faction Village, or giving an existing City better attached land.

Battle Influence helps finance those claims, but battles cannot be the Empire's only Influence source. There will be quiet turns and moments when a Camp has to be placed before another army can conveniently be fought.

Passive Influence therefore matters alongside combat income. Infrastructure such as a Scriptorium can keep expansion moving during the turns between victories instead of forcing every territorial decision to wait for the next battle.

As the Empire spreads, movement becomes another resource.

Trading Posts and their road network reduce the turns needed to shift armies between fronts. Once the Order controls a long chain of Territories, shaving several turns off reinforcement travel can matter more than another small yield improvement.

This is especially noticeable after **Tidefall**.

Newly exposed land can create unclaimed corridors between established Empires, and the Order gets extra value from reaching those regions quickly because another controlled Territory also advances Loyalty. I keep fast units near important coastlines when the sea is about to recede, scout before committing Influence, then favor regions that combine faction progress with resources, connections, or border control.

The broader weather and coastline rules are covered in the <Link href="/endless-legend-2/tidefall">Tidefall and Monsoon guide</Link>.

Once the highest Loyalty tier removes the City Cap, the same principles still apply. The Empire can found more Cities, but it still needs Dust to defend them, Influence to claim them, roads to connect them, and enough economic value to justify the extra frontier.

The Order becomes strongest when territorial growth increases military power faster than it increases the cost of holding the map.
