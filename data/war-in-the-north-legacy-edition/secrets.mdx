The biggest mistake I made with Secrets in **War in the North: Legacy Edition**
was treating them like 25 fixed collectibles.

The **Seeker** achievement asks me to discover **25 Secrets in one
playthrough**, but the campaign contains more Secret opportunities than I
actually need.

That changes my route completely.

I do not try to clear every hidden wall in the game.

I build a strong early total, verify that each Secret actually registered, and
use later areas only when something in my first route did not count.

The confirmation I trust is simple:

**I wait for the game to show `New Secret Found!`.**

A hidden chest, Ranger cache, or side room that looks secret is not enough by
itself.

<h2 id="what-counts-as-secret">
  What actually counts as a Secret
</h2>

This matters more than memorizing a list of chests.

A hidden chest is **not automatically a Secret**.

Early in Fornost, for example, Eradan can find tucked-away Ranger supplies that
look exactly like the sort of thing I would expect to count.

Some do.

Some do not.

The only confirmation I trust for Seeker is the game's own registration
message.

Secrets can also overlap with other objectives.

A quest item or collectible can count as a Secret even though its main purpose
belongs to another quest.

When that happens, I count the Secret here but leave the actual quest route to
its own Owner Page.

That is especially important for the Cult of the Lidless Eye Scrolls.

<h2 id="fornost-secrets">
  Fornost — my first 15 Secrets
</h2>

Fornost is where I do most of the work for Seeker.

I count the city by section instead of trying to remember one giant list:

**Main Gate → Battlements → Outer Wards → Inner Wards.**

If all fifteen triggers register, I leave Fornost at **15/25**.

<h3 id="fornost-main-gate">
  Main Gate — Secrets 1–5
</h3>

### 1. Eradan — Ranger cache beside the first courtyard

After the opening fights, I reach the courtyard with enemies attacking from the
stairs and ruined buildings.

I do **not** run through the newly opened gate immediately.

I switch to **Eradan** and check the area to the **right of the stairs**.

His Ranger trail leads into the ruined building and toward the first hidden
cache I use on this route.

This is the basic rhythm I use for the rest of Fornost:

**finish the fight → check the room → then advance.**

### 2. Farin — cracked wall on the opposite side

I stay in the same courtyard and switch to **Farin**.

The building on the opposite side contains damaged stone he can break.

I open the wall and check the room behind it before leaving the courtyard.

This one is easy to skip because the main objective has already opened the path
forward.

That is exactly when I turn around.

### 3. Andriel — hidden wall after the floor collapses

After passing through the gate, I eventually enter a building where the floor
collapses.

Once I land in the lower corridor, I stop before following the obvious exit.

I switch to **Andriel** and inspect the walls.

One section responds to her and opens another hidden area.

The important decision is not the wall itself.

It is recognizing that the floor collapse **feels like a transition**, which
makes me instinctively look for the way out instead of checking the room I just
entered.

### 4. Farin — second breakable wall in the lower corridor

I continue through the same underground route.

Before leaving it, I switch back to Farin and check the damaged stone farther
along.

Another wall can be broken open.

This is where I start treating cracked masonry as a gameplay tell rather than
background decoration.

### 5. Eradan — cache in the two-level chamber

Later I reach a large chamber with a lower central floor and upper walkways.

After the enemies are dead, I do not leave as soon as the next route opens.

I go to the **upper level opposite the way I entered** and check with Eradan.

Another Ranger cache is hidden there.

If every trigger worked, my Main Gate total is now **5**.

### 9. Eradan — Ranger cache beside the Tome room

<h3 id="fornost-inner-wards">
  Inner Wards — Secrets 11–15
</h3>

The Inner Wards are where I slow down the most.

Several Secrets sit close to combat arenas, and some hidden spaces contain a
second character-specific Secret behind the first one.

That makes this section much easier to undercount than it looks.

### 11. Andriel — hidden wall back toward the mounted crossbow

After the large courtyard battle, a gate opens at the rear.

I do not immediately pass through it.

I turn around and move back toward the mounted crossbow section.

With Andriel, I inspect the wall there and open the hidden room.

### 12. Eradan — Ranger cache inside Andriel's room

I stay **inside the room Andriel just opened**.

Before leaving, I switch to Eradan.

There is another Ranger cache hidden inside that newly accessible area.

This is one of the most important habits in the entire Secret route:

**when one character opens a hidden room, I check the room again with the other
two before I assume it is finished.**

### 13. Eradan — cache in the Sapper room

I continue until Goblin Sappers begin dropping into the next combat area.

I clear the fight first.

Only after the explosions stop do I search the room with Eradan.

Another Ranger cache is hidden here.

I do not try to inspect walls while Sappers are still entering the room.

There is no reason to trade health for a Secret that will still be there after
the fight.

### 14. Everyone — Gilded Chest after the jammed gate defense

The next major fight centers on a gate that jams while I am trying to open it.

I survive the attack and let the gate finish opening.

Before I pass through, I backtrack toward the direction the Sappers originally
came from.

Eradan can see a trail through the vegetation leading toward a **Gilded
Chest**.

Despite the Ranger trail pointing toward it, this Secret is not something I
treat as Eradan-only.

The registration is what matters.

### 15. Farin — wall beside the collapsed archer tower ramp

Later I become trapped between gates and survive another attack.

Once the Sons of Elrond reopen the route, I climb toward the archer tower.

I destroy the explosive section so the ruined tower creates a ramp.

Before crossing it, I switch to **Farin** and inspect the wall on the **left
side of the ramp**.

He can break it open.

If all fifteen Fornost triggers registered, I leave the city at **15/25**.

<h2 id="barrow-downs-secrets">

I do not turn the Barrow-downs into a full collectible sweep.

My goal is another **9 Secret triggers**, taking the route from 15 to a possible
**24/25**.

The main mistake here is following the Ranger trail too aggressively.

Several Secrets sit on short side paths or inside tombs where the main route
keeps trying to pull me forward.

<h3 id="barrow-three-tombs">
  The three opened tombs — Secrets 19–23
</h3>

This is the section where I deliberately stop treating one hidden room as the
end of the search.

After the long fight in the clearing, three tomb routes become available.

Inside each tomb, I finish the combat first and then check the walls with the
characters who can open them.

### 19. Farin — first tomb

In the first opened tomb, I descend into the circular room.

Once it is safe, I switch to Farin and inspect the walls.

He can break open one hidden section.

### 20. Andriel — first tomb

I stay in the same circular room.

Before leaving, I switch to Andriel.

She has a separate hidden interaction here.

I check both characters before exiting because getting one `New Secret Found!`
message does not mean the entire room is finished.

### 21. Andriel — second tomb

In the second tomb, I reach another circular chamber with **two passages
continuing deeper**.

Before choosing either passage, I check the chamber itself with Andriel.

One wall hides another Secret area.

### 22. Farin — third tomb after the Barrow-wight

After defeating the Barrow-wight and entering the third tomb, I continue until
the lower fight is over.

I switch to Farin before taking the normal exit.

He can break through another wall.

### 23. Andriel — the hidden area inside Farin's hidden room

This is the Secret I am most likely to miss on the whole early route.

After Farin opens his hidden room, I **do not leave it**.

I switch to Andriel and inspect the newly revealed space again.

There is another hidden area **inside the first hidden area**.

<h2 id="seeker-not-unlocking">
  What I check if Seeker does not unlock
</h2>

If I believe I reached 25 but Seeker is still locked, I do not immediately
reload a chapter.

I check four things.

### 1. Did the game actually register the Secret?

I stop counting ordinary hidden loot.

A chest sitting behind rubble is not proof that Seeker advanced.

I only trust the places where the game actually registered the discovery.

### 2. Did I leave a nested room too early?

The Inner Wards and Barrow-downs both contain spaces where one character opens
the first hidden route and another character can reveal something deeper
inside.

If I remember seeing only one registration in a room where I switched
characters, that is one of the first places I suspect.

### 3. Did I notice the clue but use the wrong character?

A cracked wall is not a Secret until Farin actually opens it.

A Ranger trail is not complete because I saw it.

An Andriel wall does not count because I walked past it with Eradan.

If I recognized the clue but never completed the interaction, I still need the
Secret.

### 4. Can I replace the missed trigger later?

Usually, yes.

The Ettenmoors, Mount Gundabad, Mirkwood, and Grey Mountains all give me more
opportunities.

That is why I do not restart a clean campaign over one uncertain early trigger.

My preferred Seeker route is not built around perfection.

It is built around **redundancy**:

**get as many as possible in Fornost, push the total into the twenties in the
Barrow-downs, use Amon Gorthad as the early 25th target, and let the rest of the
campaign cover any mistake.**

That is much easier than trying to memorize every Secret in War in the North
before I have even reached Rivendell.
