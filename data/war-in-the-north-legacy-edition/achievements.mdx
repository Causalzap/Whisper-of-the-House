I do not approach the **46 achievements in War in the North: Legacy Edition**
as 46 separate jobs.

The real 100% route is much simpler once I separate three different kinds of
requirements:

**things I must finish inside one campaign run, things I can plan around a
specific story moment, and the three difficulty clears that form the long-term
progression.**

That last part matters more than it first appears.

I plan a full achievement run around **three campaign clears**:

**Normal → Heroic → Legendary.**

Normal opens Heroic. Finishing Heroic opens Legendary.

When I replay the campaign, my character progression comes forward with me, so
the later runs are not three completely fresh starts.

But I do **not** treat achievements explicitly marked **"in a single
playthrough"** as lifetime counters.

If the achievement says one playthrough, I finish that requirement inside one
campaign run.

<h2 id="how-many-playthroughs">
  How many playthroughs I plan for 100%
</h2>

I plan for **three full campaign clears**.

| Run | Difficulty | What I use it for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Normal | Missables, Secrets, quests, Scrolls, counters, co-op, Eagle Savior |
| 2 | Heroic | Heroic clear, build refinement, remaining cleanup |
| 3 | Legendary | Legendary clear and final achievement cleanup |

I start on Normal rather than Easy because **Victorious in Battle** requires at
least Normal and finishing Normal is what moves the difficulty progression
forward.

After that, Heroic becomes the next campaign milestone.

Legendary comes after completing Heroic.

This is why I do not describe Heroic and Legendary as optional difficulty
experiments in a 100% run.

They are part of the route.

That changes the purpose of the second and third runs.

My first Normal run is where I learn the routes and secure the easy-to-miss
content.

Heroic is where the developed build starts doing more of the work.

Legendary is where the gear decisions I made across the earlier runs matter

<h3 id="single-playthrough-does-not-mean-lifetime">
  What I refuse to assume carries over
</h3>

Several achievements explicitly say **in a single playthrough**.

I take that wording literally.

If I finish Normal with 12 completed quests and then begin Heroic, I do not
plan the Heroic run around the assumption that only three more will finish
**Many deeds, great and small**.

The same principle applies to:

- enemy kills;
- headshot kills;
- Beleram summons;
- war-machine kills;
- crafted potions;
- Secrets;
- gilded treasure chests;
- and the other requirements explicitly tied to one playthrough.

That is why my first serious completion run is deliberately thorough.

I want as many of those counters finished before the credits as possible.

<h2 id="fornost-achievements">
  Fornost — where I start the counters instead of rushing
</h2>

Fornost is where my achievement run starts behaving differently from a normal
story playthrough.

I am already fighting large enemy groups, meeting Beleram, using mounted war
machines, opening optional paths, and collecting early Secrets.

That gives me several counters I can begin naturally rather than turning them
into cleanup later.

### Where there's life, there's hope

This only requires me to **revive a fallen ally**.

I do not deliberately let somebody die for it.

The first time a companion genuinely goes down, I make the revive instead of
assuming the AI or another player will handle it.

### Foe-hammer and Bane of Mordor

**Foe-hammer** needs **200 enemy kills in one playthrough**.

**Bane of Mordor** raises the target to **600**.

I treat 200 as a progress marker.

It is not permission to start avoiding every optional fight.

Across one full completion-focused Normal run, 600 is much easier if I simply
keep fighting through the natural route instead of leaving all remaining kills
for one artificial grind.

### Friend to the Eagles and Swift-winged Warrior

Freeing **Beleram** is tied to **Friend to the Eagles**.

Once he is available, I start using him naturally in suitable outdoor fights.

**Swift-winged Warrior** requires **10 Beleram summons in one playthrough**.

Ten is low enough that I do not hoard every summon for an imagined emergency.

I would rather finish the counter comfortably before the final chapters.

### War-machinist

This is one of the counters I pay the most attention to.

**War-machinist requires 150 enemy kills with war machines in one
playthrough.**

Whenever the game puts me on a mounted weapon and continues feeding enemies
into the firing lane, I stay on it.

I do not fire five shots, jump off because normal combat feels better, and then
expect 150 kills to appear by accident.

Fornost gives me early progress.

Later siege encounters can add much more.

My job is to stop throwing the early opportunities away.

forget because they look like optional dialogue.

<h2 id="combat-achievements">
  Combat achievements I build into normal progression
</h2>

The combat achievements become tedious mainly when I ignore their mechanics
until the end.

I would rather recognize the right situation while I am already fighting.

### Sudden Fury

I need **3 critical hits within 10 seconds**.

I attempt this when several enemies are available and Hero Mode is already
working in my favor.

I do not stop to loot or break the flow once the opportunity starts.

### Relentless

**Relentless** requires a **50-hit streak while in Hero Mode**.

My biggest problem here is usually not raw damage.

It is interrupting my own streak.

When I am attempting it, I avoid:

- opening loot;
- chasing one distant archer;
- pausing between targets;
- and taking unnecessary risks that break Hero Mode.

Dense enemy groups are where I want to do it.

### Now for wrath, now for ruin!

This requires **4 simultaneous kills**.

I save strong area attacks for a real cluster instead of spending them as soon
as two enemies stand beside each other.

Four weak enemies in one group are more useful than one elite target for this
achievement.

### Like a Thunderbolt and Fell-handed

These are single-hit damage checks:

**Like a Thunderbolt** — deal **3,000 damage with one ranged strike**.

**Fell-handed** — deal **1,500 damage with one melee strike**.

I do not rebuild an early character just to force these.

Damage grows naturally through levels, skills, weapons, bonuses, sockets, and
later equipment.

Once I am already approaching the threshold, I deliberately attempt the hit.

### Keen-eyed Marksman

This requires **50 headshot kills in one playthrough**.

With Eradan, I begin aiming deliberately at ordinary humanoid enemies early.

Fifty across a campaign is manageable.

Fifty remembered near Carn Dûm becomes a grind.

I therefore treat headshots as a normal combat habit rather than an endgame
project.

### Living Shield

**Living Shield** requires absorbing **25,000 damage during one level**.

I leave this until my character can actually survive the experiment.

I do not force it with weak early armor and poor recovery.

Once my health, defenses, and healing options are more stable, I can spend one
level deliberately taking more pressure without turning every room into a
reload.

<h2 id="gear-skill-achievements">
  Gear and skill achievements — I plan them without hijacking the build
</h2>

Several achievements are really small build-management checks.

### Gem-studded

**Gem-studded** only requires inserting an elfstone into an item.

I wait until I have gear I expect to keep for more than a few minutes.

I do not burn a useful stone solely to socket disposable starting equipment.

The deeper socket and jewel decisions belong to the Weapons and Mithril guide.

### Well-arrayed

**Well-arrayed** asks me to equip every part of an armor set.

This changes how I treat matching pieces.

If I already have several parts of the same set, I stop automatically selling
one just because its individual stats are a small downgrade.

I hold the partial set until I know whether I can complete it.

### War-hardened, Battle-master, and Warrior Exemplar

The skill-tree achievements are:

**War-hardened** — put at least one rank into a tier 3 skill.

**Battle-master** — unlock every active-cast ability in one character's skill
tree.

**Warrior Exemplar** — on one character, unlock every skill that modifies War
Cry, Sanctuary, or Evasion.

These are why I do not scatter points across whichever icon looks interesting
at the time.

Once I choose the character I want to use for those requirements, I make sure
my normal build progression is moving toward them.

<h2 id="level-coins-potions">
  Level, coins, and potion achievements
</h2>

**Defender of the North** unlocks at **level 10**.

**Champion of the North** unlocks at **level 20**.

I do not separately grind levels during the first half of the game.

Optional quests, normal combat, and exploration already give me reasons to keep
progressing.

### Dragon-hoard

For **Dragon-hoard**, I need to amass **25,000 coins**.

That makes me less willing to buy tiny shop upgrades.

If a weapon meaningfully changes my damage or fixes a real build problem, I
buy it.

If the difference is marginal and my current equipment is already handling the
area, I keep the money.

I also do not keep using an inferior weapon forever just because I want the
coin achievement.

The goal is to stop wasting money, not stop improving the build.

### Herb-master

**Herb-master** requires **15 crafted potions in one playthrough**.

I solve this by actually using the crafting system while I progress.

I gather ingredients naturally and craft instead of selling everything.

Fifteen is low enough that I do not need a dedicated late-game herb grind if I
start using the mechanic early.

<h2 id="heroic-run">
  Run 2 — what changes on Heroic
</h2>

After Normal, my Heroic run has a different purpose.

I already know:

- which hubs contain important conversations;
- where optional routes branch away from the story;
- which fights deserve my strongest consumables;
- which weapons are worth keeping;
- and which mechanics I no longer need to force because their achievements are
  already complete.

That lets me play much more directly.

The main goal is **Hero of Legend** for completing Heroic.

I also use this run for any cleanup that benefits from my stronger character
but does not need to wait until Legendary.

What I do **not** do is assume an unfinished single-playthrough counter can be
completed by adding a few actions from Normal to a few actions from Heroic.

If I still need one of those achievements, Heroic becomes a fresh opportunity
to complete its full requirement during that campaign.

<h2 id="legendary-run">
  Run 3 — Legendary is the finish line, not another blind completion run
</h2>

Completing Heroic opens the final campaign difficulty I need.

My Legendary target is **Against All Odds**.

By this point, I want the run to be much cleaner than Normal.

I am not trying to re-check every optional conversation simply because it
exists.

I already used the first run to remove the dangerous missables.

Now I can concentrate on:

- the Legendary clear;
- a mature character build;
- high-end gear;
- and whatever achievements genuinely remain.

This is also where specialized equipment becomes much more useful.

If I have built strong Orc, Undead, or other enemy-specific weapons across the
earlier progression, I use them when their matchup actually matters rather
than carrying one generic weapon through every stage.

The detailed investment rules stay in the Weapons and Mithril Owner Page.

<h2 id="my-final-100-percent-check">
  My final 100% check
</h2>

Before I consider the achievement route finished, I ask myself three separate
questions.

**Did I finish the one-playthrough requirements inside a complete campaign
run?**

That includes the counters, quests, Secrets, and other achievements whose
wording explicitly limits them to one playthrough.

**Did I remove the true missables while I was already standing near them?**

That includes conversations such as Frodo, optional rescue content such as
Glorhirin, the completed Lidless Eye investigation, and the no-Beleram final
fight.

**Did I finish all three difficulty milestones?**

Normal.

Heroic.

Legendary.

If all three answers are yes, I am no longer relying on random cleanup.

I have treated the achievement list as a route through the game instead of 46
unrelated tasks.

That is the difference between my 100% run and simply opening an achievement
list after the credits.
