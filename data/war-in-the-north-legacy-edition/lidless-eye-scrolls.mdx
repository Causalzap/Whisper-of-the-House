The **Cult of the Lidless Eye** is one side quest I never leave for endgame
cleanup in **War in the North: Legacy Edition**.

There are **7 Sorcerer's Scrolls** across the campaign. Two are inside chests,
while the others come from specific sorcerers or bosses.

The dangerous part is not understanding what the quest wants.

It is walking away after killing the correct enemy without actually collecting
the dropped Scroll.

My rule is simple:

**I collect each Scroll before crossing the next major checkpoint, and I speak
to Elrond on my first Rivendell visit after finding Scroll 1.**

<h2 id="scroll-1-fornost">
  Scroll 1 — Fornost Battlements
</h2>

The first Scroll comes during the section where Beleram needs me to clear the
war machines from the walls of Fornost.

After freeing Beleram, I eventually climb onto the **Battlements** with the
objective to destroy the mounted war machines.

I do not rush from one siege weapon to the next.

At the **first war machine**, there is a Goblin Sorcerer mixed into the fight.

I kill him and then deliberately walk over to his body to collect the drop.

This is the first place where the quest can quietly go wrong.

**Killing the sorcerer is not the same thing as collecting the Scroll.**

There are enemies, loot, and a siege objective all competing for my attention.
I make sure the item is actually picked up before I leave the Battlements.

<h2 id="start-cult-lidless-eye">
  Speak to Elrond after Scroll 1
</h2>

When I first reach Rivendell, I do not leave for the Ettenmoors as soon as the
main route becomes available.

I speak to **Elrond** about the Scroll I found at Fornost.

He identifies the writing as **Black Speech** and explains that it teaches dark
sorcery. More importantly, he discovers that this is only **one of seven**
works.

That leaves **six Scrolls still in enemy hands**.

This is the moment I treat the Cult of the Lidless Eye investigation as active.

If I found Scroll 1 but have not yet discussed it with Elrond, I do not head
north.

<h2 id="scroll-2-coldfells">
  Scroll 2 — Ettenmoors, Coldfells
</h2>

Scroll 2 is the first one that is easy to miss because it does **not** come from
an obvious quest enemy.

In the Coldfells I eventually fight through a large Orc group that includes a
Troll, then reunite with **Beleram**.

After that conversation, I continue until the path runs beside a **stream**.

The Scroll chest is down by that water, but I cannot simply step directly onto
it from the main path.

I take the route **up the left side first**, clear the enemies ahead, continue
until I can safely drop down from the higher rocks, and then turn back along the
stream.

That backtrack leads me to the chest containing Scroll 2.

The visual trap here is obvious once I know it:

the main path keeps pulling me forward, while the Scroll requires a short
**forward → drop down → backtrack along the water** loop.

If I see the stream and immediately keep climbing away from it, I stop and
check this chest before progressing.

<h2 id="scroll-3-high-moors">
  Scroll 3 — Ettenmoors, High Moors
</h2>

The third Scroll returns to an enemy drop.

Once I enter the **High Moors**, I push through the first fights until the route
reaches a **small natural stone bridge**.

A group of Orcs and Goblins is defending the crossing.

The enemy I care about is the **sorcerer** among them.

I kill the sorcerer, clear enough pressure around me that I can safely loot, and
then check the body before moving past the bridge toward the tunnels.

This is another location where I do **not** trust the fact that the enemy died.

A spellcaster can fall in the middle of a crowded fight while I am already
chasing the next target.

I physically return to the corpse and collect the drop.

By the time I leave the Ettenmoors, I expect to have **3 of the 7 Scrolls**.

<h2 id="scroll-4-mount-gundabad">
  Scroll 4 — Mount Gundabad
</h2>

Scroll 4 is the one I consider the easiest chest Scroll to walk past.

It appears fairly late in **Mount Gundabad**, after I have already been dealing
with the Orc war machinery.

The important landmark is the section with the **two towers and their huge
chains**.

I move up through the first tower, cross to the second tower, and begin
descending.

The mistake is following the obvious progression route as soon as I reach the
bottom.

Instead, when I arrive at the bottom of the **second tower**, I look at the
doors before moving on.

The normal route and checkpoint are off to one side.

I go through the **opposite opening toward the dead-end exterior platform**.

There is a chest there.

Scroll 4 is inside.

My decision point here is simple:

**if I can see the route that clearly continues the level, I check the other
door first.**

Mount Gundabad is already pushing me to hurry after the machinery sequence, so
this is exactly the kind of optional room that is easy to abandon when the
story feels urgent.

I do not leave the mountain until I know I have Scroll 4.

<h2 id="scroll-5-wulfrun">
  Scroll 5 — Wulfrun in Mirkwood
</h2>

Scroll 5 is mechanically straightforward but still dangerous for one reason:
the game immediately gives me another reason to move.

When I arrive in **Mirkwood**, the opening sequence leads straight into the
fight with **Wulfrun**.

I defeat him, but I do not immediately follow Beleram or start searching for
Radagast.

I walk over Wulfrun's body and collect his drop first.

**boss dead → loot body → verify Scroll → only then follow the new objective.**

The campaign moves quickly from Wulfrun into the search for Radagast, and I do
not let that transition distract me from the drop.

At this point I should be at **5/7**.

<h2 id="scroll-6-urgost-lair">
  Scroll 6 — the sorcerer near the gate in Urgost's Lair
</h2>

The final two Scrolls are both on the route toward **Urgost**, which is why I
treat this entire section as my final collection checkpoint.

After traveling through the Grey Mountains, I eventually pass through a
mountain section and emerge into a more open area.

The landmark I look for is a **stone bridge with water nearby**, followed by a
large entrance on the far side.

There are several enemies here, including sorcerers.

One of those sorcerers carries Scroll 6.

I cross into the fight, kill the spellcasters, and check their drops **before
entering the large gate and continuing deeper**.

This is not the place where I start wondering whether an earlier Scroll was

Before I continue, I expect to be at **6/7**.

If I am not, I check the current battlefield first. An enemy-drop Scroll is
much easier to overlook on the ground than a chest that I knowingly opened.

<h2 id="scroll-7-carn-dum-captain">
  Scroll 7 — Carn Dûm Captain before Urgost
</h2>

The seventh and final Scroll comes at the end of the route through Urgost's
Lair.

Before the actual meeting with the dragon, I fight the **Carn Dûm Captain**.

He uses sorcery and is backed by other enemies, so during the fight my attention
is naturally on staying alive rather than watching where every enemy falls.

Once the Captain is dead, I stop.

I do not walk into the next checkpoint.

I loot his body and collect Scroll 7.

I do not assume defeating the final carrier automatically finishes the

There is still one step left.

<h2 id="return-scrolls-elrond">
  Return all 7 Scrolls to Elrond
</h2>

After the Urgost sequence gives me access to travel again, I go to
**Rivendell** before getting absorbed in the next major crisis.

I speak to Elrond.

He explicitly recognizes that I recovered **all six of the remaining Scrolls**
after the original Fornost discovery.

sorcery and gives me a reward from Rivendell's relics.

That conversation is what closes the investigation and satisfies
**The Lidless Eye** achievement requirement.

For my 100% run, I therefore think of the sequence as:

**7/7 collected → return to Rivendell → speak to Elrond → quest complete.**

Not simply:

**7/7 collected → done.**

<h2 id="missing-lidless-eye-scroll">
  What I check if I am missing a Scroll
</h2>

If I reach the late game with the wrong total, I first separate the seven
locations into two types.

The chest Scrolls are **Coldfells** and **Mount Gundabad**.

The others come from specific enemies or bosses.

That tells me where the most likely mistake happened.

If I remember opening both special chests but my total is short, I think back to
the enemy drops: the Fornost sorcerer, High Moors sorcerer, Wulfrun, the
sorcerer near Urgost's gate, and the Carn Dûm Captain.

Those are the places where killing the correct enemy can feel like success even
though the item was still lying on the ground.

I also verify that I actually spoke to Elrond after the first Scroll.

The safest run is not one where I plan to repair mistakes later.

It is one where I use each region as a hard checkpoint:

**1 before leaving Fornost, 3 after the Ettenmoors, 4 after Gundabad, 5 after
Wulfrun, and 7 before meeting Urgost.**

That is enough structure to keep the quest under control without turning the
rest of the campaign into a collectible hunt.
