I treat **War in the North: Legacy Edition** as a chain of staging points
rather than one continuous dungeon crawl.

The route that keeps me moving cleanly is:

**Bree → Fornost → Sarn Ford → Barrow-downs → Rivendell → Ettenmoors →
Rivendell → Mount Gundabad → Nordinbad → Mirkwood → Grey Mountains →
Nordinbad → Carn Dûm.**

Most of the time I am not stuck because I cannot win a fight.

I am stuck because an area looks finished when one wall, war machine, gate,
conversation, or return trip is still blocking the next objective.

My rule is:

**finish the current objective → confirm where the story wants me to return →
prepare at the hub → then commit to the next region.**

<h2 id="fornost-war-machines">
  Clear every Fornost war machine before pushing toward the Citadel
</h2>

Beleram explains that the siege weapons are preventing him from operating
freely.

I take the stairs and passages upward and clear the wall.

The mistake is destroying the machines I can immediately see and assuming the
objective is finished.

I keep moving along the Battlements until **every required war machine is
destroyed**.

If the route still has not advanced, I scan the wall again before assuming the
quest is broken.

After the wall is secure, I follow the fighting deeper into the city.

The Sons of Elrond are heading toward the **Citadel**, so that becomes my
navigation rule whenever the streets split:

**inward and upward.**

I do not search every side street for the main objective.

Locked gates, mounted crossbows, and combat arenas can make this stretch look
more complicated than it is.

When a gate refuses to open, I check the immediate area for the fight, lever, or
alternate path the game still expects me to finish.

<h3 id="tharzog">
  Tharzog — clear the room before I tunnel the boss
</h3>

The Citadel eventually puts me against **Tharzog** and his guard.

This is where I stop playing the fight like a simple damage race.

I remove smaller enemies when they are making it impossible to read Tharzog's
movement.

His wide melee pressure is much easier to avoid when I am not simultaneously
being hit from several directions.

I also stop attacking through every animation.

If he is beginning a wide swing, I would rather dodge, reset my position, and
resume than trade a large chunk of health for one extra hit.

I use healing when I actually need to recover from a mistake.

I do not burn it simply because the boss has a large health bar.

Once the Citadel confrontation is finished, Fornost is done.

And this is where I make the first major route decision people often remember
incorrectly:

**I do not go directly to Rivendell.**

<h3 id="bargrisar">
  Bargrisar — attack in stages instead of hitting the giant anywhere
</h3>

Bargrisar is huge enough that my normal "stay on the target and keep swinging"
habit works badly.

I start at his **legs**.

His stomp and ground attacks can knock me down and take a large amount of
health, so I do not finish one extra combo just because I am already beside
him.

When he starts committing to a heavy movement, I get out.

After enough pressure on the legs, I get the opportunity for a critical attack
that brings him down.

That changes the target.

I use the opening to attack his **arms** rather than going back to the legs out
of habit.

Another successful critical sequence exposes the **head**, which is where I
want my strongest damage.

So the fight I remember is:

**legs → critical → arms → critical → head.**

That makes the boss much less confusing than treating his entire model as one
giant health bar.

I also save healing for mistakes.

Bargrisar has enough health that panic-healing every small hit is a good way to
run low before I have reached the best damage windows.

Once he falls, I let the sequence finish.

Then I **return to Rivendell**.

I do not keep walking farther north looking for Agandaûr.

The evidence gathered in the Ettenmoors is what points the next stage toward
**Mount Gundabad**.

<h2 id="mount-gundabad">
  Return to Rivendell, then follow the new lead to Mount Gundabad
</h2>

Back in Rivendell, I finish the main conversation before doing anything else.

The trail now points toward Mount Gundabad.

This is where I make sure I have enough healing and equipment I actually trust
for a long combat section.

I do **not** rebuild my entire loadout merely because the next destination
sounds dangerous.

If my current setup is already working, I keep it.

If something genuinely needs replacing, this is the time to fix it before the
mountain locks me into another extended route.

Inside Gundabad, the campaign eventually connects me with the dwarves trying to
activate an **ancient defensive weapon**.

That objective changes how I fight.

I am not there to chase every Orc around the arena.

I am there to keep the weapon functioning long enough to complete its job.

Enemies reaching the objective matter more than enemies I could pursue for one
more kill.

Once the weapon succeeds, the mountain begins to collapse.

<h2 id="mirkwood">
  Mirkwood — defeat Wulfrun, then push toward Radagast
</h2>

Mirkwood changes the way I navigate again.

The forest canopy makes Eagle scouting much less useful, so I continue
**on foot**.

The first important combat stop is **Wulfrun**.

<h3 id="wulfrun">
  Wulfrun — stop chasing every teleport
</h3>

Wulfrun is a Sorcerer, so I do not expect him to stand in one place and trade
melee attacks.

He can teleport, throw ranged dark-energy attacks, and use a wall of fire to
control the space around him.

The mistake is chasing him blindly every time he disappears.

I re-center first.

If a fire wall is blocking the direct route, I do not walk through it simply to
keep attacking.

I reposition, reacquire Wulfrun, and use ranged pressure whenever he creates
distance.

When he appears close enough for safe melee, I take the opening.

When he teleports again, I reset instead of continuing to swing at empty space.

That rhythm keeps me from losing more health to positioning than to the boss
himself.

Once Wulfrun is down, I continue deeper into Mirkwood toward Radagast.

There are optional objectives around the forest, but their complete routes
belong to the Side Quests and Achievement pages.

For the main story, I keep moving toward the spider threat.

<h3 id="saenathra">
  Saenathra — I fight from the sides and clear the spider swarm
</h3>

The route eventually reaches **Saenathra**, the giant spider holding Radagast.

This is one fight where I do **not** want to stay glued to the boss.

Saenathra can punish me from the front, leap onto a target, grapple a character,
and use her stinger behind her.

That makes both "stand directly in front and trade" and "sit behind her forever"
bad habits.

I prefer her **sides**.

When she turns to face me, I move again rather than insisting on finishing the
combo.

When she leaps, I get out of the landing area.

When she calls in smaller spiders, I clear enough of the swarm that I can read
the boss again.

I do not let ten small enemies chip away at the party while I stare only at
Saenathra's health bar.

The fight becomes much cleaner when I think of it as:

**side pressure → move when she turns → clear the swarm → return to the boss.**

Once Saenathra is defeated, I free **Radagast**.

stay neutral, and **Carn Dûm** can become his once Agandaûr is removed.

<h2 id="nordinbad-siege">
  Defend Nordinbad in objective order
</h2>

The Nordinbad siege is one of the easiest battles in the campaign to make harder
than necessary.

There are too many enemies to treat it as one giant kill-everything arena.

I follow the threat to the objective.

First come the **siege towers**.

I use the dwarven defenses to deal with them instead of leaving the proper
weapons unused while I attack everything manually.

Then I watch for **sappers**.

Once they start running toward the inner door, they become my priority.

I stop chasing ordinary enemies and intercept the targets that can actually
damage the objective.

After that, heavier enemies and Trolls push the defense closer to the doors.

That conversation confirms the final route.

Agandaûr was not personally leading the siege.

He is still at **Carn Dûm**.

Beleram is badly wounded, so I also stop planning around Eagle transport for
the final trip.

Before leaving, I take the offered dwarven heirloom, repair what needs
repairing, restock healing, and commit to the endgame.

<h3 id="agandaur">
  Agandaûr — conserve resources before the bridge phase
</h3>

The first mistake I avoid is assuming every pause in the fight means the boss
is almost finished.

Agandaûr changes the encounter as it goes.

When another major enemy enters the fight, I stop tunneling Agandaûr and remove
the immediate pressure before trying to force more boss damage.

Once that threat is gone, I return my attention to him.

Smaller enemies can also enter the arena.

I kill enough of them to keep the battlefield manageable, but I do not spend
every cooldown chasing the weakest target while Agandaûr is still the real
threat.

His forward pressure and magical attacks make greedy melee expensive.

I take the clean openings.

I do not trade health simply because I want one more combo.

Eventually his health reaches the point where he retreats.

That is **not the ending**.

He runs toward the bridge.

I follow him.

<h3 id="agandaur-bridge">
  Agandaûr on the bridge — back off when he rises, shoot when he hovers
</h3>

The bridge phase changes the rhythm again.

When Agandaûr rises from the ground, I back away instead of trying to hit
through it.

The movement signals an electrical shockwave around him.

Staying close for one more melee attack is not worth taking the hit.

When he stays airborne, I switch to **ranged damage**.

I do not stand underneath him waiting for melee range to return when I can keep
pressure on the boss safely from a distance.

The other attack I respect is his straight-line lightning pass.

When he takes off for that charge, I move out of the lane instead of trying to
tank it.

So my bridge rhythm becomes:

**back away from the rise → use ranged pressure while he hovers → leave the
lightning line → return to damage when he lands.**

That is much safer than treating the final phase like the earlier arena.

I also keep enough healing available for this section.

Burning every potion trying to rush the first phase only makes the bridge harder.

Once Agandaûr finally falls here, the northern campaign is over.
