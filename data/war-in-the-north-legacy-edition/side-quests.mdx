I do **War in the North: Legacy Edition** side quests alongside the main
campaign.

I do not save them for a giant cleanup session near the end.

That works better because most of these quests already line up with places the
story is sending me anyway.

The real problems are usually smaller:

**I leave a hub before accepting the quest.**

**I sell or ignore an item that looked like ordinary loot.**

**I collect what I need but forget to hand it in when I return.**

The achievement **Many deeds, great and small** requires **15 completed quests
in one playthrough**, so I use the fifteen named quests below as my running
route instead of trying to reconstruct the counter later.

<h2 id="bree-side-quests">
  Bree — finish the short quests before Fornost
</h2>

Bree is small enough that I deal with its optional work immediately.

I do not grind levels here.

I do not buy an entire new equipment set before the first major combat area.

I use Bree to open the quests that either finish inside town or change how I
handle the loot coming out of Fornost.

<h3 id="rowlies-gift">
  Rowlie's Gift
</h3>

This is the first side quest I remove from my checklist because the entire route
stays inside Bree.

Rowlie gives me his mother's locket for **Idona**, but the chain is broken.

I go to the blacksmith first.

The repair costs only **one silver penny**.

That is cheap enough that I never delay the quest just to preserve money.

From the smithy, I take the repaired locket to **Idona**.

She makes it clear that Rowlie still has her heart.

I do not stop there.

The final step is going **back to Rowlie** with her answer.

That return conversation is what closes the loop for me.

This is the kind of quest I finish before leaving because every step is already
within a few minutes of the starting NPC.

<h3 id="forewarned-is-forearmed">
  Forewarned is Forearmed
</h3>

A Bree resident is worried that the town is not preparing seriously enough for
the war.

The practical problem is weapons.

The dwarven merchant does not believe peaceful Breelanders are likely customers,
so I speak to him and push the argument that the town may need arms even if it
never needed them before.

Once he agrees to supply Bree, I report back.

I finish this conversation chain before Fornost because it sets up the next
quest and changes what I do with spare weapon drops.

<h3 id="ottos-arsenal">
  Otto's Arsenal
</h3>

This is one of the first quests where the real decision is not **where to go**.

It is **what not to get rid of**.

Once Otto's Arsenal is active, I stop automatically disposing of every spare
weapon I pick up in Fornost.

I still keep my real combat gear.

I do not sacrifice something I am actively using just to make the quest counter
move faster.

Instead, ordinary weapons that I would otherwise sell become useful turn-in
items.

That makes Fornost do double duty:

I progress the main story while collecting spare arms for Bree.

When I get a convenient return opportunity, I hand them in instead of carrying
a pile of low-value weapons farther into the campaign.

<h2 id="fornost-side-quests">
  Fornost — keep the three quest items that look like ordinary loot
</h2>

Fornost contains several optional items that are easy to mentally classify as
random treasure.

I do not turn the whole city into a second collectible walkthrough.

I simply recognize the items that start or advance another quest and make sure
they survive the trip out of Fornost.

<h3 id="work-of-westernesse">
  Work of Westernesse
</h3>

While clearing Fornost, I keep the old weapon components associated with
Westernesse.

I do not need to understand the final weapon while I am still fighting through
the ruins.

My job here is only:

**find the components → keep them → take them to a proper smith later.**

Once I reach Rivendell, I bring the assembled material to **Angmir**.

<h2 id="sarn-ford-barrow-downs">
  Sarn Ford and Barrow-downs — stack both quests into the same trip
</h2>

This is one of the best places in the campaign to avoid backtracking.

After Fornost, the story already sends me back through **Sarn Ford**.

Before going north into the Barrow-downs, I make sure I have both relevant
objectives active:

**find the missing Rangers**

and

**find Athelas for the Ranger suffering from the Black Breath.**

Both belong on the same expedition.

<h3 id="athelas">
  Athelas
</h3>

At Sarn Ford, a wounded Ranger is dying even though the visible injury does not
look severe enough to explain it.

The real problem is the **Black Breath**.

Salanna remembers a remedy involving **Athelas**, or kingsfoil, and points me
toward land where the herb may still grow.

That matters because I am already about to enter the Barrow-downs.

I accept the quest **before** leaving Sarn Ford.

Then I collect the Athelas while following the normal northward route.

At the first practical return, I bring it back to Salanna.

<h3 id="miruvor">
  Miruvor
</h3>

I accept **Miruvor** before leaving for the Ettenmoors because its requested
materials can be collected while I am already progressing through that region.

I do not stop the entire expedition to force the materials immediately.

I keep the quest active and collect them as the route gives me the opportunity.

When I have what Arwen asked for, I bring the materials back on the next
Rivendell visit.

She rewards me with **Miruvor**, a strong restorative.

That is where this Side Quest page stops.

I do not turn one quest reward into a general potion-ranking section.

<h3 id="elf-stones">
  Elf-stones
</h3>

I also start **Elf-stones** in Rivendell.

This quest behaves differently from a fixed-location objective because the
colored beryl stones come from containers rather than one single landmark I can
walk directly toward.

That changes the way I play the Ettenmoors.

I break containers that are already on my route.

I do **not** spend a long time retracing cleared rooms because one particular
stone has not appeared yet.

If I have the required stones when I return to Rivendell, I hand them in.

If I do not, I leave the quest active and continue normal looting.

That is the right place for patience.

Trying to force an item-dependent quest like a fixed collectible route only
creates unnecessary backtracking.

<h2 id="truesilver">

</h2>

This is when I accept **Truesilver**.

I do not start inventing a separate Mithril-farming expedition.

The campaign is already about to take me toward **Mount Gundabad**.

That is where I keep my eyes open.

The useful route is hidden behind stone rather than sitting directly on the
main corridor, so I pay attention to the same kind of suspicious rock I am
already checking with Farin.

Once I have the Mithril and the campaign gives me the opportunity to return, I
bring it back to Arwen.

<h2 id="nordinbad-side-quests">
  Nordinbad — claim the free reward and accept the Black Pearl quest

After Mount Gundabad, Nordinbad becomes the next staging hub.

I do not immediately leave for Mirkwood.

There are two optional stops I handle first.

<h3 id="gorins-gratitude">
  Gorin's Gratitude
</h3>

Gorin rewards the Fellowship for what happened at Mount Gundabad.

The important detail is that the reward does not simply appear in my inventory.

He directs me to his **steward**.

I follow through and claim it before leaving Nordinbad.

There is no cost.

There is no long side route.

Skipping it because the Mirkwood objective is already active is simply leaving
a reward behind.

<h3 id="gem-no-mine-can-yield">
  A Gem No Mine Can Yield
</h3>

The dwarven smith asks for a **Black Pearl**.

This is not the kind of quest where I immediately abandon the main story and
farm one room until the item appears.

I accept it before going to Mirkwood.

Then I keep opening containers and looting while progressing normally.

When a Black Pearl finally drops, I remember that the quest is now ready for
the next Nordinbad return.

<h2 id="side-quest-missables">
  The six checkpoints I use so I do not leave quests unfinished
</h2>

I do not try to remember fifteen quest descriptions every time I load the save.

I remember six campaign transitions.

### Before Fornost

I finish **Rowlie's Gift** and make sure the other Bree quest chains are active.

### Before leaving Fornost behind

I make sure the important optional pickups are still accounted for:

**Westernesse components, Mysterious Aged Tome, first Lidless Eye Scroll.**

I do not need to finish their later steps yet.

I only need to avoid leaving Fornost without the items that start them.

### Before entering the Barrow-downs

At Sarn Ford, I accept both:

**Athelas**

and

**The Last Sons of Cardolan**.

One trip north should advance both.

### Before leaving Rivendell for the Ettenmoors

I hand in the items I brought out of Fornost.

Then I finish the short hub quests and accept the Ettenmoors collection work.

### Before Mount Gundabad

On the return from the Ettenmoors, I finish anything already ready to hand in
and accept **Truesilver**.

That lets the Mithril objective ride alongside the main Gundabad route instead
of becoming a separate expedition.

### Before leaving Mirkwood behind

I make sure I have:

- accepted the Black Pearl quest before entering the region;
- freed Glorhirin;
- spoken to Glorhirin again after his storyline resolves;
- and handled any ready Nordinbad turn-in when the story returns me there.

That is the whole system I use.

The fifteen quests stop feeling like fifteen unrelated errands because the
campaign is already carrying me through every important region.

My actual job is much smaller:

**accept the quest before the relevant trip, recognize the item when it appears,
do not throw away what still matters, and use every hub return as a hand-in
checkpoint.**
