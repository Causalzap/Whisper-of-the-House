<h2 id="progression-order">
  What is the RuneScape: Dragonwilds progression order?
</h2>

Dragonwilds works best when progression and gear upgrades happen together. The next region often contains the resources needed for the next equipment tier, so waiting until every slot is perfect before crossing a regional boundary usually creates unnecessary grinding.

The main sequence is:

**Brynmoor → Ghornfell → Dragon Slayer / Velgar → Fellhollow → Imaru → Dowdun Reach → Black Knight Titan → Umbral Sands → Fuzan → Scorned Wilderness → Queen Kuldra**

Side quests, Agility courses, building, skill training, and resource runs fit around that route. I finish nearby objectives when they support what I am already doing, but I do not clear every optional activity before moving to the next major region.

For a fresh character, the first goal is simply making exploration sustainable. The <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/beginner-guide">beginner guide</Link> covers Rest, food, water, crafting recipes, combat progression, Power Level, Runes, and storage. Once the opening shelter becomes cramped, the <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/starter-base">starter base guide</Link> shows how to turn a practical Temple Woods location into a longer-term crafting, farming, storage, and processing base.

Travel also becomes a separate resource problem once the map opens up. Rather than repeatedly moving the whole base, I extend the Lodestone network toward the region I am actually using. The <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/fast-travel">fast travel guide</Link> covers Vault Cores, Lodestone construction, Law Rune costs, carried-weight scaling, and when another forward teleport point is worth building.

If the world is being shared with friends, it is worth deciding the multiplayer setup before everyone spreads out. The <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/crossplay-coop">crossplay and co-op guide</Link> covers Crossplay, Invite Codes, hosted-world player limits, Dedicated Servers, and moving an existing world onto a server later.

<h2 id="dragon-slayer">
  When should I fight Velgar?
</h2>

**Velgar closes the early Brynmoor and Ghornfell stage.**

Dragon Slayer does not send you straight into the boss. The Challenge Horn requires materials including a **Dragon Tooth** and **Bloodwood Sap**, so reaching Velgar already assumes that the character has moved beyond the opening gathering loop and can survive longer trips away from the first shelter.

I treat poison preparation as the important readiness check rather than waiting for another complete armor tier. Food, repaired equipment, usable combat Runes, and an Anti-poison supply make a larger difference than delaying the fight to finish every nearby activity.

If the Challenge Horn, poison pressure, elemental damage, or Velgar's liftoff attacks are stopping the kill, follow the <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/dragon-slayer">Dragon Slayer and Velgar walkthrough</Link>.

Turning in Velgar's head completes Dragon Slayer and moves the main route toward **Fellhollow**.

<h2 id="fellhollow">
  What should I do after Velgar?
</h2>

Fellhollow is where the gear that felt comfortable around Velgar can start showing its age.

The region introduces stronger undead enemies, Soul-related objectives, Astral traversal, **Granite**, **Coal**, and the progression toward **Steel**. Entering with the equipment used for Velgar is reasonable; staying on that tier after ordinary enemies begin draining most of the healing and weapon durability is not.

A hard-hitting Necromancer nearly deleting the health bar tells me more than the region label does. Once routine encounters start costing too much, I stop forcing the quest marker and get the next production step running before returning.

<h2 id="astral-form">
  Where do I go when the Fellhollow route looks blocked?
</h2>

Some Fellhollow routes only make sense in **Astral Form**.

One path initially looked like a dead end because the continuation was not obvious in the normal state. Switching into Astral Form exposed the route instead of requiring a second entrance somewhere else on the map.

Granite is only the station requirement. Once those 12 pieces are secured, continuing to farm Granite does not solve the next bottleneck. **Iron and Coal** become more valuable because they produce the Steel gear and tools that make Fellhollow combat more comfortable.

I also upgrade in the order that removes the current blocker. A stronger weapon or required tool can matter immediately; replacing every defensive slot at the same time can wait until the materials are available.

The region eventually leads to **Imaru** through the Withering Heights progression. Defeating Imaru opens the next major stage in **Dowdun Reach**.

<h2 id="dowdun-reach">
  Where do I go after Imaru?
</h2>

The next major objective is **Black Knight's Fortress**, ending with the **Black Knight Titan**.

At this point the route is easier to manage when the main quest stays central. I gather the regional materials needed for the current equipment tier and take Secondary Quests when they sit naturally along the same path, rather than turning every dungeon or optional encounter into a prerequisite for the Titan.

The Black Knight Titan is the milestone that matters for the main sequence. Once that fight is finished, the next large destination is **Umbral Sands**.

<h2 id="umbral-sands">
  What should I do in Umbral Sands?
</h2>

Umbral Sands is the final large regional stage before the 1.0 endgame, with **Fuzan** as its main boss.

The useful priority is getting the equipment, supplies, and quest progress needed to defeat Fuzan. The region also contributes to the Secondary Quest completion requirements, but those objectives do not need to delay the boss when the character is already ready to continue.

Defeating **Fuzan** is the important progression gate. Once he is down, the route toward the **Scorned Wilderness** opens.

<h2 id="scorned-wilderness">
  Where do I go after Fuzan?
</h2>

After Fuzan, the earlier one-region-one-boss rhythm changes. The **Scorned Wilderness** is a multi-stage endgame run rather than another short regional step.

The entrance is through the **Dragon Gate northeast of the Fractured Plains**. Beyond the gate, Commander Zilyana sends you through extraction points, elemental Anima mechanics, combat rooms, traversal sections, breakable walls, and puzzles before Queen Kuldra becomes accessible.

Late-game materials such as **Runite, Luminite, Black Dragon Hide**, and other new resources appear along the route. That means there is no need to arrive with every Rune item already finished; the raid itself contributes to the gear improvement that happens before the final boss.

The <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/scorned-wilderness">Scorned Wilderness walkthrough</Link> covers the Dragon Gate, Zilyana staging area, Dragontail Vines, **Fragments of Ghornfell**, poison maze, Shattered Stronghold, Cataclysm Keep, extraction points, and the **Air → Water → Earth → Fire** sequence.

<h2 id="kuldra">
  Do I need full Rune armor before Kuldra?
</h2>

No.

Strong Tier 7 equipment that has already carried you through the Scorned Wilderness can be enough to reach the final fight, especially if useful Rune weapons or raid upgrades have already dropped along the way. A missing Rune armor piece is not, by itself, a reason to leave the checkpoint and rebuild the entire loadout.

Before entering Kuldra's Sanctum, I use the checkpoint to repair equipment, bank raid materials, refill food, restock resistance supplies, and fix whichever weakness was actually causing problems on the previous section.

Queen Kuldra is then a multi-stage fight built around Anima, the Harmonic Resonator, beam avoidance, elemental dragons, and the final Heart sequence. The <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/kuldra">Kuldra boss walkthrough</Link> covers those mechanics, the escape, first-clear rewards, and repeat Dragon equipment drops.

<h2 id="combat-choice">
  What combat style should I use while progressing?
</h2>

One dependable combat style is more useful than three styles that are all underdeveloped.

I keep most early combat experience concentrated in whichever style is carrying normal fights, then bring another tool for situations the main weapon handles badly. A melee setup can keep a bow for pulls and awkward targets; a ranged setup can still use Magic for elemental damage, movement, or utility.

The choice becomes much easier once ordinary enemies start showing what the build lacks. If the damage is fine but positioning is dangerous, a bow or spell may solve the encounter without requiring a full respec. If everything is taking too long to kill, the problem is more likely the primary skill or gear tier.

Weapon progression, armor mixing, Power Level, the Special Meter, parries, neutral evades, and early Rune use are covered in the <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/beginner-guide">beginner guide</Link>.

<h2 id="version-1">
  What changed for returning players in Dragonwilds 1.0?
</h2>

The biggest progression change is what happens after Fuzan. **Scorned Wilderness and Queen Kuldra extend the main route**, while Rune equipment and repeatable Kuldra rewards add another endgame gear stage.

Combat also feels different immediately. Weapon special attacks now use their own meter, successful parries create stronger counter opportunities, and movement during combat is more flexible. Base management has changed as well, particularly through additions such as the Storage Ledger.

Travel deserves another look on an older save too. Lodestones remain useful, but long routes become much easier to manage once placement, Vault Core supply, and Law Rune cost are treated as part of progression rather than as an afterthought. The <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/fast-travel">fast travel guide</Link> covers the current network mechanics.

Multiplayer has expanded through Crossplay, Invite Codes, and Dedicated Servers. The <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/crossplay-coop">crossplay and co-op setup</Link> covers the current player limits, server configuration, migration, and connection issues.

Before heading straight into the new endgame on an older save, I spend a few minutes checking controls, storage, travel access, and equipment. That short reset is usually cheaper than discovering the changed systems in the middle of the next dangerous area.

<h2 id="after-kuldra">
  What should I do after beating Kuldra?
</h2>

The first Kuldra kill is where progression stops being a single straight line.

Repeat clears can produce more Dragon equipment through **Queen Slayer Packs**, while the rest of Ashenfall may still contain unfinished Secondary Quests, Agility courses, spell and ability challenges, and long skill grinds.

There are **50 achievements** in the current list. Completion includes the major boss milestones, three Secondary Quests in each of five regions, Gold medals across the Agility courses, specific combat and utility conditions, and **12 skills at Level 99**.

The <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/achievements">all 50 achievements checklist</Link> is the easiest way to separate goals already completed during normal progression from the ones that still require deliberate cleanup.

Kuldra can also be farmed for additional Dragon drops. A completed raid resets its extraction points, so another Queen run starts by moving through the Scorned Wilderness again rather than spawning directly outside the boss arena.
