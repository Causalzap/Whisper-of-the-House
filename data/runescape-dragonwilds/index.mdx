<h2 id="where-to-start">
  Where should you start in RuneScape: Dragonwilds?
</h2>

The early game becomes much easier once survival stops interrupting progression. If every expedition ends because Rest is low, food is gone, equipment is damaged, Runes are missing, or the inventory is full, travelling farther usually creates longer recovery runs rather than meaningful progress.

The <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/beginner-guide">RuneScape: Dragonwilds beginner guide</Link> covers the systems I would stabilize first: Rest, food and water, recipe unlocks, Power Level, combat choices, Runes, storage, and the point where the character is ready to stay away from the opening area for longer.

The first shelter also does not need to become a giant permanent build. Once benches, storage, farming, and processing start competing for the same small floor, move into something more practical. The <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/starter-base">base locations and starter layout guide</Link> compares the early Temple Woods setup with later places to build and explains when expanding makes more sense than relocating.

<h2 id="main-progression">
  What is the main Dragonwilds progression order?
</h2>

The broad 1.0 route is:

**Brynmoor → Ghornfell → Dragon Slayer / Velgar → Fellhollow / Imaru → Dowdun Reach / Black Knight Titan → Umbral Sands / Fuzan → Scorned Wilderness → Queen Kuldra**

I would not clear every Secondary Quest, Agility course, skill milestone, and resource node before entering the next region. New areas introduce the materials and enemies that reveal what the character actually needs next, so over-farming the previous biome can spend a lot of time solving problems you do not have yet.

The <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/progression">Dragonwilds progression guide</Link> follows the region order in detail, including the major gear changes, boss handoffs, Fellhollow transition, Steel timing, and where to go after each important fight.

<h2 id="dragon-slayer">
  What happens during Dragon Slayer?
</h2>

Dragon Slayer is the first long sequence that combines exploration, gathering requirements, multiple regions, and a major boss at the end. The quest follows Cathan's trail through the Goblin Castle and swamp, continues into Fractured Plains, then sends you out for the materials needed to craft the **Challenge Horn** before Velgar can be fought properly.

The <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/dragon-slayer">Dragon Slayer quest walkthrough</Link> follows that route from the first objectives through Cathan, the Challenge Horn, Velgar, and the final turn-in.

After Velgar, there is little reason to stay behind repeating early farming unless a specific upgrade still matters. Progression moves into Fellhollow, where the next equipment and traversal problems begin replacing the ones from Brynmoor and Ghornfell.

<h2 id="base-and-travel">
  Should you move your base as the map gets larger?
</h2>

Usually not.

A useful early base can remain valuable for a long time if storage, crafting, farming, and outdoor processing still work efficiently. When the only problem is distance, I prefer extending the Lodestone network toward the regions I keep revisiting rather than dismantling the entire workshop and rebuilding it farther away.

Later locations such as Fellhollow and Umbral Sands become more attractive once most of the character's activity has genuinely shifted into those parts of Ashenfall. Until then, relocation can cost more time than it saves.

For building decisions, use the <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/starter-base">base locations and starter layout guide</Link>. For Vault Cores, Law Rune costs, travel weight, and forward Lodestone placement, use the <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/fast-travel">fast travel guide</Link>.

<h2 id="after-fuzan">
  Where do you go after Fuzan?
</h2>

Fuzan leads into the largest late-game transition in 1.0. The next route passes through the **Dragon Gate northeast of the Fractured Plains** and enters the **Scorned Wilderness**.

This area is less direct than the earlier regional progression. Instead of entering a biome and moving toward one obvious boss objective, the route mixes extraction points, Anima mechanics, combat rooms, traversal hazards, environmental damage, and puzzles before Queen Kuldra becomes available.

The <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/scorned-wilderness">Scorned Wilderness walkthrough</Link> follows the Dragon Gate, Commander Zilyana, extraction points, Dragontail Vines, Fragments of Ghornfell, the poison maze, Shattered Stronghold, Cataclysm Keep, and the route into the final encounter.

<h2 id="queen-kuldra">
  What should you prepare before Queen Kuldra?
</h2>

By the time you reach Kuldra, navigation is no longer the main problem. The question is whether the equipment, healing, resistance setup, and supplies can survive the fight itself.

I use the final checkpoint to repair everything, sort what came out of the Scorned Wilderness, refill food, and fix the weakness that caused the previous attempt to fail. One missing armor piece does not automatically mean the entire late-game route needs to be repeated from the beginning.

The <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/kuldra">Queen Kuldra boss guide</Link> covers the fight phases, Harmonic Resonator, beam attacks, elemental dragons, Heart sequence, escape, first-clear rewards, and repeat clears.

<h2 id="combat">
  Do you need to level every combat style equally?
</h2>

No. Progression is smoother when at least one combat style is strong enough to kill ordinary enemies reliably.

I still keep access to other weapons or spells for awkward targets, pulling enemies, movement, or elemental utility, but I do not try to keep Attack, Ranged, and Magic perfectly even. If every normal enemy in a region takes too long to kill, the main combat skill or equipment tier probably needs attention. If only one encounter is causing trouble because of range or positioning, changing tools can be enough.

The <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/beginner-guide">beginner guide</Link> covers early combat choices, Power Level, armor, parries, neutral evades, the Special Meter, and Rune use in more detail.

<h2 id="coop">
  How does co-op work in Dragonwilds 1.0?
</h2>

Dragonwilds supports crossplay across PC and the current console versions. A normal hosted world supports **4 players total**, while a **Dedicated Server supports up to 6** and can remain available without the original host being online.

Crossplay does not move saves between platforms. If the group needs Invite Codes, platform compatibility, a persistent server, world migration, or help joining after an update, the <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/crossplay-coop">crossplay and co-op guide</Link> covers the current multiplayer setup.

<h2 id="returning-players">
  What should returning players check in 1.0?
</h2>

The biggest route change for an older save is what happens after Fuzan. Progression now continues through the **Scorned Wilderness** and ends with **Queen Kuldra**, rather than stopping around the earlier endpoint.

Before jumping directly into that content, I would spend a few minutes checking the things most likely to create avoidable friction: controls, equipment condition, food, storage, travel access, and the current combat setup. Weapon specials use their own meter, parries create stronger counter opportunities, movement has changed, and the Storage Ledger can make an older base much easier to manage.

That short check is usually enough to reveal what needs fixing without turning a returning save into another early-game grind.

If you are unsure where an older character fits into the current route, the <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/progression">progression guide</Link> is the quickest place to rejoin.

<h2 id="achievements">
  Should you chase achievements during progression?
</h2>

Most achievement progress happens naturally, but I would not interrupt the main route every time another requirement becomes possible.

Boss kills and regional Secondary Quests fit well alongside normal progression. Agility Gold medals, unusual combat conditions, long skill grinds, and other cleanup become much easier once travel and equipment are no longer limiting the character.

There are **50 achievements** in the current list. The <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/achievements">all 50 achievements checklist</Link> separates objectives that usually happen during normal play from the ones that need a deliberate setup.

<h2 id="after-kuldra">
  What can you do after beating Queen Kuldra?
</h2>

There is no single mandatory region after the first Kuldra clear. At that point, I choose the next goal by what is still unfinished on the save: repeat Kuldra runs for additional Dragon equipment, remaining Secondary Quests, Agility courses, achievement cleanup, missing abilities, base projects, or longer skill goals.

Repeat Kuldra runs send you back through the Scorned Wilderness rather than placing the next attempt directly outside the arena, so keeping late-game supplies and useful travel points ready saves a lot of repeated preparation.
