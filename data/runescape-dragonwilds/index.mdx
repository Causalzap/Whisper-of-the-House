<h2 id="where-to-start">
  Where should you start in RuneScape: Dragonwilds?
</h2>

The first few hours are easier once I separate survival problems from progression problems. When Rest, food, water, repairs, Runes, or inventory space are still interrupting every trip, pushing farther across Ashenfall usually creates longer recovery runs rather than useful progress.

The <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/beginner-guide">RuneScape: Dragonwilds beginner guide</Link> covers that opening stage: keeping Rest high, making food and water routine, unlocking recipes through actual materials, choosing a combat style, understanding Power Level, using Runes without wasting them, and deciding when the character is ready for longer expeditions.

A cramped starter shelter also does not need to become the permanent center of the save. Once crafting stations, farming, processing, and storage are competing for space, the <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/starter-base">starter base guide</Link> shows how I turn a practical Temple Woods location into a base that can support the rest of the journey.

<h2 id="main-progression">
  What is the main Dragonwilds progression order?
</h2>

The broad 1.0 route is:

**Brynmoor → Ghornfell → Dragon Slayer / Velgar → Fellhollow / Imaru → Dowdun Reach / Black Knight Titan → Umbral Sands / Fuzan → Scorned Wilderness → Queen Kuldra**

I do not finish every Secondary Quest, Agility course, skill grind, or resource node before moving on. The main route works better when the next region is allowed to introduce the materials and enemies that tell me what needs upgrading next.

For the full region order, gear-upgrade points, Fellhollow transition, Steel timing, boss handoffs, and where to go after each major fight, use the <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/progression">Dragonwilds progression guide</Link>.

<h2 id="dragon-slayer">
  What should you do when Dragon Slayer becomes the next objective?
</h2>

Dragon Slayer is the first point where I expect the early survival setup to prove that it can support a real boss attempt. By then I want the trip away from the base to feel routine rather than dangerous before the fight has even started.

The actual quest preparation and Velgar fight have several separate failure points, including the Challenge Horn, poison pressure, elemental choices, and the boss's aerial attacks. The <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/dragon-slayer">Dragon Slayer and Velgar walkthrough</Link> covers that sequence from preparation through the quest turn-in.

After Velgar, the next important change is not another round of early-game farming. The route continues into Fellhollow, where the next equipment and traversal problems begin.

<h2 id="fast-travel">
  When is it worth building more Lodestones?
</h2>

Travel starts costing enough time to notice once the active quests and material runs spread beyond the opening areas. I keep the main base where it is useful, then extend the Lodestone network toward regions I expect to revisit rather than moving the entire settlement every time the story advances.

That decision gets more important when repeated boss attempts or crafting trips start sending me across the same long route. The <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/fast-travel">fast travel guide</Link> covers Lodestone construction, Vault Cores, Law Rune costs, inventory-weight scaling, and the points where another forward destination begins saving more than it costs.

<h2 id="scorned-wilderness">
  What should you do after Fuzan?
</h2>

Fuzan is the handoff into the 1.0 endgame. From there the route moves through the **Dragon Gate northeast of the Fractured Plains** and into the Scorned Wilderness.

This section is different from the earlier regional loop. Instead of entering a biome and heading toward one straightforward boss objective, the Scorned Wilderness strings together extraction points, Anima mechanics, combat rooms, traversal, environmental hazards, and puzzles before Queen Kuldra becomes accessible.

The <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/scorned-wilderness">Scorned Wilderness walkthrough</Link> covers the Dragon Gate, Commander Zilyana, extraction points, Dragontail Vines, Fragments of Ghornfell, the poison maze, Shattered Stronghold, Cataclysm Keep, and the route that eventually opens the final encounter.

<h2 id="kuldra">
  What should you do when you reach Queen Kuldra?
</h2>

Reaching Kuldra means the navigation problem is largely finished; the remaining question is whether the current equipment, supplies, and resistance setup can survive the boss itself.

I use the final checkpoint to repair, sort the materials collected through the Scorned Wilderness, refill food, and correct the weakness that actually caused the previous failure. A missing late-game armor piece does not automatically mean the entire route needs to be abandoned and repeated from the base.

The <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/kuldra">Queen Kuldra boss guide</Link> covers the fight phases, Harmonic Resonator, beam attacks, elemental dragons, Heart sequence, escape, first-clear rewards, and repeat clears.

<h2 id="combat">
  Which combat style should you focus on?
</h2>

A character progresses more smoothly when one combat style is strong enough to handle ordinary fights consistently. I still carry tools from other styles for awkward targets, pulls, movement, or elemental utility, but I do not try to keep Attack, Ranged, and Magic perfectly even from the beginning.

The useful signal is what normal enemies are doing to the build. Slow kills across an entire region usually point toward the main combat skill or equipment tier. A single enemy that is dangerous because of range or positioning may only need a different weapon or spell for that encounter.

The <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/beginner-guide">beginner guide</Link> goes deeper into combat progression, Power Level, armor choices, parries, neutral evades, the Special Meter, and early Rune use.

<h2 id="coop">
  What should you decide before starting co-op?
</h2>

Multiplayer works better when the world setup is settled before everyone begins building in different directions. Crossplay, Invite Codes, hosted worlds, Dedicated Servers, player limits, and world migration all affect how the group reconnects later.

The <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/crossplay-coop">crossplay and co-op guide</Link> covers the current multiplayer setup, including what works between platforms and how to move an existing world onto a Dedicated Server when a hosted session is no longer enough.

<h2 id="returning-players">
  What changed for returning players in Dragonwilds 1.0?
</h2>

The largest progression addition comes after Fuzan: the route now continues through the **Scorned Wilderness to Queen Kuldra**, with new late-game materials and repeatable rewards beyond the previous endpoint.

Combat and base management may also feel different on an older save. Weapon specials use their own meter, parries create stronger counter opportunities, movement is more flexible, and systems such as the Storage Ledger change how much time I spend sorting the base between trips.

Before pushing directly into the new endgame, I spend a few minutes checking controls, storage, equipment condition, food, and travel access. That small reset is usually enough to expose anything that no longer works the way I remember without turning the return into another early-game grind.

For the exact point where an older save should rejoin the main route, use the <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/progression">progression guide</Link>.

<h2 id="achievements">
  Should you work on achievements during the main story?
</h2>

Most achievement progress fits naturally around normal play, but I do not stop the main route every time a new requirement becomes available. Boss kills and regional Secondary Quests line up with progression, while other goals make more sense as deliberate cleanup once travel and equipment are no longer limiting the character.

There are **50 achievements** in the current list, covering bosses, regional quests, combat and utility conditions, Agility Gold medals, and long skill grinds. The <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/achievements">all 50 achievements checklist</Link> makes it easier to separate objectives already completed naturally from the ones that still need a specific setup.

<h2 id="after-kuldra">
  What should you do after beating Queen Kuldra?
</h2>

After the first Kuldra clear, there is no single mandatory region waiting next. I choose the next goal by what still matters on that save: repeat Kuldra runs for more Dragon equipment, unfinished Secondary Quests, Agility courses, achievement cleanup, missing abilities, base projects, or longer skill goals.

Another Queen run sends the character back through the Scorned Wilderness rather than placing the next attempt directly outside the boss arena, so I keep useful endgame supplies and travel access ready when farming repeat rewards.
