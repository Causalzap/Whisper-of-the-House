<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
The main RuneScape: Dragonwilds route is **opening survival → Dragon Slayer / Velgar → Fellhollow / Imaru → Dowdun Reach / Black Knight Titan → Umbral Sands / Fuzan → Scorned Wilderness → Queen Kuldra**. Do not wait for a perfect gear set before entering each new region. Move forward while ordinary enemies remain manageable, then upgrade when the next material tier starts appearing or normal fights begin draining most of your food and durability.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

<h2 id="progression-order">
  What is the RuneScape: Dragonwilds progression order?
</h2>

Dragonwilds progression makes more sense when I follow the region that supplies the materials for the next step instead of trying to finish every upgrade before crossing its border.

The major sequence is:

**Brynmoor / Ghornfell → Dragon Slayer / Velgar → Fellhollow → Imaru → Dowdun Reach → Black Knight Titan → Umbral Sands → Fuzan → Scorned Wilderness → Queen Kuldra**

Side quests, skill training, Agility courses, building, and resource farming sit around that route rather than replacing it. I complete useful objectives when they are already close to where I am travelling, but I do not empty an entire region before allowing myself to move on.

A character that is still struggling with Rest, food, Runes, Lodestones, Power Level, combat styles, or storage will get more value from the <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/beginner-guide">beginner guide</Link> before pushing the later regions.

If you are starting with friends or mixing PC and console players, set up the world first. The <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/crossplay-coop">crossplay and co-op guide</Link> covers Crossplay, Invite Codes, four-player hosted worlds, six-player Dedicated Servers, and moving an existing world to a server later.

<h2 id="dragon-slayer">
  When should I fight Velgar?
</h2>

**Velgar is the first major boss checkpoint.**

Dragon Slayer makes you move beyond basic gathering because the Challenge Horn requires materials such as a Dragon Tooth and Bloodwood Sap. By the time those ingredients are obtainable, the character should already have a working food loop, repaired equipment, access to combat Runes, and tools beyond the earliest starter tier.

I would not delay the fight just to build an oversized base or complete every nearby activity. Velgar can be beaten without a huge intermediate gear grind, and the most important preparation issue in my attempts was poison rather than one missing armor tier.

If the Challenge Horn, poison, Air damage, or liftoff attacks are stopping the kill, use the <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/dragon-slayer">Dragon Slayer and Velgar walkthrough</Link>.

After Velgar's head is turned in, Dragon Slayer is complete and the route moves toward **Fellhollow**.

<h2 id="fellhollow">
  What should I do after Velgar?
</h2>

Fellhollow was the first region where an equipment weakness became obvious before the boss itself.

The region brings stronger undead enemies, Soul-related objectives, Astral traversal, Granite, Coal, and the move toward **Steel**. You can enter with the equipment that carried you through Velgar, but the longer that old tier remains unchanged, the more ordinary encounters start eating through healing and durability.

A Necromancer or another hard-hitting enemy nearly deleting the health bar is a better warning than the region name itself. At that point I stop forcing the quest marker and get the next production step running.

<h2 id="astral-form">
  Where do I go when the Fellhollow route looks blocked?
</h2>

Some Fellhollow paths only become usable through **Astral Form**.

One route initially looked like a dead end because the lower passage did not appear traversable in the normal state. Entering Astral Form exposed the continuation without requiring a second entrance somewhere else on the map.

<h2 id="steel-upgrade">
  When should I make Steel gear?
</h2>

The important Fellhollow production step is the **Blast Furnace**, which required **12 Granite** in this progression.

Granite is only the station requirement. Producing Steel still needs enough **Iron and Coal**, so collecting far more Granite than the furnace requires does not solve the next bottleneck.

Once I have the 12 Granite, my gathering priority changes. Iron and Coal become more valuable because they turn the new station into actual equipment.

I also do not wait until every slot can be replaced together. A stronger weapon or required tool can remove the immediate blocker first; defensive pieces can follow as the materials accumulate.

Fellhollow ultimately leads to **Imaru** through the Withering Heights progression. After Imaru is defeated, continue into **Dowdun Reach**.

<h2 id="dowdun-reach">
  Where do I go after Imaru?
</h2>

The next major objective is **Black Knight's Fortress** in Dowdun Reach, ending with the **Black Knight Titan**.

This is a good stage to keep the route focused. Follow the main quest, collect the materials that support the area's equipment step, and take convenient Secondary Quests when they are already close to your path.

There is no need to treat every dungeon or optional encounter you discover as mandatory before fighting the Titan.

The Black Knight Titan is the milestone that carries the main sequence forward. Once that fight is complete, move into **Umbral Sands**.

<h2 id="umbral-sands">
  What should I do in Umbral Sands?
</h2>

Umbral Sands is the final large regional stage before the 1.0 endgame, with **Fuzan** as its major boss.

The immediate priority is to get the equipment and quest progress needed to defeat Fuzan rather than stopping for a full completion sweep. Umbral Sands also has a three-Secondary-Quest achievement, but those objectives can be finished when they fit naturally around the region instead of delaying the boss indefinitely.

Defeating **Fuzan** is the important gate because it opens the route toward the **Scorned Wilderness**.

<h2 id="scorned-wilderness">
  Where do I go after Fuzan?
</h2>

After Fuzan, the game stops following the earlier one-region-one-boss rhythm and shifts into the multi-stage **Scorned Wilderness** run.

The entrance is reached through the Dragon Gate northeast of the Fractured Plains. Beyond it, Commander Zilyana sends you into a sequence of extraction points, elemental Anima mechanics, combat rooms, breakable walls, traversal sections, and puzzles before Kuldra becomes accessible.

Runite, Luminite, Black Dragon Hide, and other late-game materials also begin appearing through this route, so you can improve equipment while progressing rather than arriving with every Rune item already crafted.

The <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/scorned-wilderness">Scorned Wilderness walkthrough</Link> covers the Dragon Gate, Zilyana staging area, Dragontail Vines, Fragments of Ghornfell, poison maze, Shattered Stronghold, Cataclysm Keep, and the **Air → Water → Earth → Fire** puzzle.

<h2 id="kuldra">
  Do I need full Rune armor before Kuldra?
</h2>

No.

If strong Tier 7 equipment has already carried you through the Scorned Wilderness and you have picked up useful Rune weapon upgrades along the way, reaching the final checkpoint without a complete Rune armor set is not a reason to abandon the attempt.

Use the checkpoint to repair, bank raid materials, refill food and resistance supplies, and fix any obvious weakness before entering Kuldra's Sanctum.

Queen Kuldra then becomes a separate four-stage fight built around Anima, the Harmonic Resonator, a dangerous beam attack, and the final Heart phase. The <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/kuldra">Kuldra boss walkthrough</Link> covers those mechanics and the Dragon rewards from the kill.

<h2 id="combat-choice">
  What combat style should I use while progressing?
</h2>

Keep one combat style strong enough to be dependable, then carry another answer for enemies that make the primary style awkward.

That can mean a melee character carrying a bow to pull enemies, or a ranged character keeping useful magic available for elemental damage and utility. The important part is not splitting early experience so widely that Attack, Ranged, and Magic all remain underdeveloped.

Weapon progression, armor mixing, the Special Meter, parries, neutral evades, Rune management, and Power Level are covered in the <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/beginner-guide">beginner guide</Link>.

<h2 id="version-1">
  What changed for returning players in Dragonwilds 1.0?
</h2>

The largest progression change is what happens after Fuzan.

**Scorned Wilderness and Queen Kuldra extend the main route**, while Rune equipment and repeatable Kuldra rewards add another endgame gear step. A save that previously felt finished therefore has a substantial new destination rather than only extra side activities.

Combat also behaves differently enough to notice immediately. Weapon special attacks now use their own meter, parries are more rewarding, and base management is easier with additions such as the Storage Ledger.

Multiplayer changed as well. Crossplay can be enabled when hosting, friends can use Invite Codes, and Dedicated Servers can support larger persistent groups. The <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/crossplay-coop">crossplay and co-op setup</Link> covers the current multiplayer options and server requirements.

If you are returning on an older save, spend a few minutes checking the combat controls, storage setup, and current equipment before walking directly into the new region.

<h2 id="after-kuldra">
  What should I do after beating Kuldra?
</h2>

After the first Kuldra kill, progression becomes much less linear.

Repeat clears can produce additional Dragon equipment through Queen Slayer Packs, while the rest of the world still contains Secondary Quests, Agility courses, spell challenges, and skill levels that may be unfinished.

The current achievement list contains **50 achievements**. Completion includes five boss kills, three Secondary Quests in each of five regions, Gold on every Agility course, specific spell and ability conditions, and **12 Level 99 skills**.

Use the <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/achievements">all 50 achievements checklist</Link> to see what normal progression already completed before starting the long skill grind.

Kuldra can also be repeated for more Dragon drops, but finishing the raid resets its extraction points. Later runs therefore begin through the Scorned Wilderness again rather than starting directly outside the boss arena.
