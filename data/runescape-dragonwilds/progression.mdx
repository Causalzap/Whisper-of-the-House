<h2 id="progression-order">
  What is the RuneScape: Dragonwilds progression order?
</h2>

Dragonwilds does not expect you to finish every side activity before entering the next region. The useful rhythm is to push the main route until the new area reveals either the next story gate or the material tier needed to keep going.

The current 1.0 sequence is:

**Brynmoor → Ghornfell → Dragon Slayer / Velgar → Fellhollow → Imaru → Dowdun Reach → Black Knight Titan → Umbral Sands → Fuzan → Scorned Wilderness → Queen Kuldra**

I treat side quests, Agility courses, skill training, base work, and resource runs as support for that route rather than prerequisites for every regional transition. A nearby objective is worth finishing when it gives me something I already need; clearing an entire biome before crossing its border usually adds more gathering than useful progress.

A fresh character should first reach the point where an ordinary expedition can leave home and return without collapsing because of Rest, thirst, broken equipment, or a full backpack. The <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/beginner-guide">beginner guide</Link> covers those early systems. Once the first shelter is too cramped for normal crafting and storage, the <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/starter-base">starter base guide</Link> handles the longer-term base setup.

<h2 id="velgar">
  When should you leave the early game and fight Velgar?
</h2>

**Velgar is the first major progression checkpoint.**

Dragon Slayer already pushes the character beyond the opening survival loop before the boss begins. By the time I am gathering the materials needed to finish that quest chain, I want food and water preparation to be routine, the main weapon repaired, enough combat Runes for the trip, and a combat style that has actually been receiving experience.

I do not delay Dragon Slayer just because every nearby activity is still unfinished. A giant base, perfect secondary skills, and a completely cleared region are not required to move the main route forward.

The useful question is whether the character can reach the fight with supplies still intact. When the journey itself is already consuming most of the healing or durability, I repair and upgrade before another attempt. When travel and normal enemies are stable, I stop farming and finish the quest.

The Challenge Horn, Velgar's attacks, poison preparation, damage choices, and the boss fight itself are covered in the <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/dragon-slayer">Dragon Slayer and Velgar walkthrough</Link>.

Once Velgar is dead and Dragon Slayer has been turned in, the next place I focus on is **Fellhollow**.

<h2 id="fellhollow">
  What should you do after Velgar?
</h2>

Fellhollow was the first stage where old equipment could still get me into the region but no longer guarantee that normal exploration would stay efficient.

Stronger undead enemies, Soul-related progression, Astral traversal, **Granite**, **Coal**, and the move toward **Steel** all arrive close together. I enter with the setup that beat Velgar rather than waiting outside for another complete armor set, then let the first few fights tell me whether that equipment is still doing its job.

A normal enemy nearly emptying the health bar is a better warning than the region name. When routine fights start demanding boss-level healing, repeated repairs, or several retries just to reach a quest marker, I return to production instead of forcing another identical run.

That is also why I do not automatically interpret every Fellhollow death as a bad dodge. Sometimes the route is correct and the equipment step is what has fallen behind.

<h2 id="astral-form">
  Where do you go when a Fellhollow path looks blocked?
</h2>

Some routes that appear to end in Fellhollow continue through **Astral Form**.

One path looked like a dead end in the normal state. Searching the surrounding area for another entrance did not solve it; changing into Astral Form exposed the continuation on the route I was already standing beside.

This is where gathering priorities can easily go wrong. Granite gets the station built, but a chest full of extra Granite does not make Steel bars. Once I have the required 12, **Iron and Coal** matter more because they turn the new station into the equipment I actually came back to make.

I upgrade whatever removes the current blocker first. When damage is the issue, a weapon can be a better first craft than replacing every armor slot. When normal hits are taking enormous chunks of health, enough defensive improvement to survive the route comes before filling out a perfectly matched set.

There is no benefit in sitting at home until every Steel piece is finished when a smaller upgrade already makes Fellhollow stable again.

The main regional progression eventually leads through **Withering Heights** to **Imaru**. Once Imaru is defeated, I leave Fellhollow behind and start working through **Dowdun Reach**.

<h2 id="dowdun-reach">
  Where do you go after Imaru?
</h2>

The next major route is **Black Knight's Fortress**, with the **Black Knight Titan** as the milestone that closes this stage.

Dowdun Reach gives plenty of reasons to leave the main path, so I keep the active progression objective central. Regional materials that improve the equipment I am actually using are worth gathering. Secondary Quests sitting beside the route are easy to justify. A distant optional dungeon is not automatically something I need to clear before fighting the Titan.

The Black Knight Titan is the important handoff. Once that fight is finished, I stop treating Dowdun Reach as the next mandatory destination and move toward **Umbral Sands**.

<h2 id="umbral-sands">
  What should you do in Umbral Sands?
</h2>

Umbral Sands is the last large regional stage before the 1.0 endgame route, and **Fuzan** is the boss that determines when I am ready to leave it.

I use the same rule that worked in Fellhollow: enter the region, let its materials and enemies expose the next useful equipment step, and upgrade enough to keep normal travel reliable. I do not delay Fuzan for a full completion sweep when the character is already surviving the regional route comfortably.

Secondary Quests still matter for completion and achievements, but they do not all need to happen before the boss. When the main setup can reach Fuzan with supplies intact, I finish the regional fight first.

Defeating Fuzan changes the route completely. The next destination is no longer another conventional biome with a single short boss path; it is the **Scorned Wilderness**.

<h2 id="scorned-transition">
  Where do you go after Fuzan?
</h2>

After Fuzan, head to the **Dragon Gate northeast of the Fractured Plains**.

The gate is the transition into the 1.0 endgame. Once the earlier regional progression has been completed, the route opens and the objective sends you into the Scorned Wilderness.

I prepare differently here because the structure changes. Scorned Wilderness is a long sequence of extraction points, elemental Anima mechanics, combat rooms, traversal sections, and puzzles rather than another short trip to a regional boss.

It also contains late-game materials, including **Runite and Luminite**, so I do not treat a complete Rune loadout as an entry requirement. The route itself contributes materials and upgrades before the final encounter.

<h2 id="kuldra">
  When are you ready to continue to Queen Kuldra?
</h2>

The Scorned Wilderness checkpoints are what I use to decide whether the final push is still working.

A bad room does not automatically mean the whole build needs replacing. I repair, refill food and resistance supplies, bank materials when the checkpoint allows it, and fix the weakness that actually caused the previous failure. Leaving the entire endgame route to rebuild every slot is a much larger detour than replacing one weak link.

Once the Scorned Wilderness route is complete and the path to Kuldra is open, the remaining problem is the boss itself rather than regional navigation.

The <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/kuldra">Queen Kuldra boss guide</Link> covers the gear decision at the final checkpoint, the Harmonic Resonator, elemental phases, beam avoidance, the Heart, escape sequence, and rewards.

<h2 id="combat-progression">
  Should you level one combat style or all three?
</h2>

During normal progression, one reliable combat style is more useful to me than three underdeveloped ones.

Attack, Ranged, and Magic improve through use, so switching constantly can leave every option behind the equipment tier I am finding. I keep most routine combat on the style that is carrying the character, then maintain another tool for encounters that create a specific positional problem.

A melee character can still carry a bow to separate enemies from a pack. A ranged character can still use Magic for utility or an elemental answer. That flexibility does not require keeping every combat skill equally levelled.

When damage across all ordinary encounters becomes slow, I look at the main skill and equipment tier. When damage is fine but one enemy is dangerous to stand beside, changing the tool can solve the encounter without rebuilding the character.

The <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/beginner-guide">beginner guide</Link> covers the underlying combat-skill system, Power Level, parries, evades, Special Meter, and early Rune use.

<h2 id="travel">
  When should you extend your Lodestone network?
</h2>

Regional progression becomes much less expensive once I stop treating every long trip as something that has to begin at the original base.

I add a forward Lodestone when I know I will return to the same region repeatedly for quests, materials, or boss attempts. I do not build one simply because I reached a distant landmark once; the Vault Core and travel resources are more useful when the destination is becoming part of the normal route.

That becomes increasingly important from Fellhollow onward because returning home for Steel production, repairs, food, or storage can otherwise turn one failed attempt into a long recovery trip.

For Vault Cores, construction requirements, Law Rune costs, carried-weight scaling, and placement decisions, use the <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/fast-travel">fast travel guide</Link>.

<h2 id="returning-players">
  Where should returning players continue after Dragonwilds 1.0?
</h2>

The important route change in 1.0 happens **after Fuzan**.

An older save that had already finished the previous regional progression can continue toward the Dragon Gate and the Scorned Wilderness rather than searching the earlier regions for another normal quest chain. From there the route leads through the endgame sequence to Queen Kuldra.

I still spend a short preparation stop checking equipment, supplies, storage, and travel access before leaving. That is enough to catch an outdated setup without turning the return into a full restart.

<h2 id="after-kuldra">
  What should you do after beating Queen Kuldra?
</h2>

The first Kuldra clear is where the straight regional progression ends.

From there I choose according to what is still unfinished: another Kuldra run for Dragon equipment, regional Secondary Quests, Agility Gold medals, spell and ability challenges, or longer skill goals.
