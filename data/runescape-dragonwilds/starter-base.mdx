<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
Do not turn the first quest shelter into a huge permanent build. Once storage and crafting stations start crowding it, move to **flat ground with nearby water and room for outdoor production**. The lake north of the starting area in **Temple Woods** is a strong first long-term location, while the Bramblemead cliff, Fellhollow, and later Umbral Sands can support larger bases as progression moves outward. Keep storage and frequently used benches close together, leave smelting and campfires outside, put farms directly on open ground, and expand only when a real space or travel problem appears.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

<h2 id="when-to-build">
  When should I replace the first shelter?
</h2>

The shelter you build during the opening progression only needs to keep a bed covered and give you enough room to survive the first few crafting steps. I would leave it alone while it still handles a bed, several chests, food, repairs, and the earliest benches without making every trip home annoying.

The point where I start a proper base is when preparation itself becomes the problem. If new crafting stations are being dropped outside because there is nowhere sensible to place them, chests are blocking movement, or I am constantly moving materials from one side of a cramped room to the other, the shelter has already outlived its useful size.

A first long-term base does not need to be an endgame fortress. The layouts I used had enough room for the major early workstations and continued functioning through several equipment tiers. That is a better target than spending a large pile of wood and stone on empty rooms before the stations that would fill them even exist.

<h2 id="location-factors">
  What makes a good base location?
</h2>

I care more about the terrain and the route around the base than about finding one perfect coordinate. A useful location gives me **flat building space, water, outdoor ground, nearby early resources, and enough empty room to add another production area later**.

Flat ground matters immediately. Uneven terrain turns every extension into a foundation problem, and a slope that looks harmless when the house is small becomes much more frustrating once storage, a farm, furnaces, and a Lodestone all need space.

Water is also worth keeping nearby. Fishing stays convenient, and a lakeside base naturally gives the building one side that does not need to become another cluttered work area. I would rather walk a little farther to one resource node than spend the next twenty hours rebuilding around bad terrain.

Travel becomes less important once the Lodestone network is working. If the only problem with an established base is that later regions are farther away, the <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/fast-travel">Lodestone and fast-travel route</Link> is usually a cheaper solution than dismantling an entire workshop.

<h2 id="best-starter-location">
  Where is a good starter base location?
</h2>

For the first substantial base, the **lake just north of the starting area in Temple Woods** gives you most of what matters without forcing a long relocation early.

The ground beside the water is flat enough for a useful central structure, there is room to put processing equipment outside, and the open land around the house leaves space for farming and later extensions. Water stays close enough for fishing, while the base is still near the part of the map where most early trips begin.

<h2 id="other-base-locations">
  Are there good base locations outside Temple Woods?
</h2>

Yes. The useful base spots change as the character moves farther from the opening regions, and several later locations support much larger builds.

One early alternative is the **cliff in Bramblemead Valley above the Wise Old Man**. The top has a broad flat area that can take a much larger foundation than the first shelter, and it works well for a communal or oversized early base. A Lodestone can be incorporated directly into the property, while the open ground below leaves room for a separate farm.

Farther into progression, there is a very flat area in **Fellhollow near Silverthorn Keep**. I used that space for a much larger base with indoor storage and crafting, an upper living area, a rear balcony, a large farm, and a separate Lodestone structure. If you already know the starter house is too small and want a bigger midgame home, the terrain there is much easier to work with than forcing a large build onto a cramped early slope.

**Umbral Sands** can support another full relocation later. One build there had enough flat room for a large open storage center, furnaces, cooking, crafting rooms, an armory, and continued expansion. At that point, though, the reason to move is no longer that the opening base was badly placed. The character is simply spending enough time in later regions that establishing another major working area begins to make sense.

I would not try to build permanent bases in every biome. A good home base plus forward Lodestones handles ordinary travel much more efficiently than repeatedly recreating the same workshop.

<h2 id="base-size">
  How big should a starter base be?
</h2>

There are two practical sizes depending on how much building you want to do.

A compact build can begin with a **3 × 5 foundation core** and add exterior processing areas as new stations appear. That footprint is already enough to create a functional workshop without spending much time or material on decorative empty space.

If you want the first long-term house to have separate storage, crafting, cooking, and room to shape the exterior properly, a **5 × 7 main floor** is much more comfortable. Odd-number dimensions also make it easier to center doors, windows, roof lines, and interior sections.

Neither size is a rule. They are useful references for avoiding the two common extremes: building a box that becomes full almost immediately, or laying down an enormous foundation that consumes resources while most of the floor remains empty.

I prefer to leave one or two sides capable of growing. When another bench unlocks, I can extend the correct work area instead of redesigning the whole house.

<h2 id="building-materials">
  Should I build the starter base from basic wood or oak?
</h2>

Basic wood is enough for a simple one-floor base. The problem appears when the structure becomes taller or the roof starts spanning larger sections.

The building system tracks structural support, and stronger materials allow more ambitious vertical construction before instability stops placement. If the plan includes a taller roof, second level, large balcony, or long unsupported sections, moving into **oak** gives much more freedom than forcing the whole structure to work with the earliest materials.

Oak does cost more time because logs and processed materials have to be gathered in larger quantities. I would not delay a functional base just to replace every early wall with oak. Build the useful parts first, then spend better materials where the height or support actually benefits from them.

For large roofs, pillars and beams are not only decoration. Once a span stops accepting another piece, adding a real support point can be the difference between finishing the roof and rebuilding half of it.

<h2 id="indoor-outdoor">
  Which crafting stations should stay inside?
</h2>

A good layout separates the things I use constantly from equipment that benefits from open space.

The main indoor workshop is where I keep storage and the benches involved in the normal gear loop. Crafting, armor, fletching, blacksmithing, jewelry, Rune-related work, tanning, and spinning all become easier when the materials and the benches consuming them are only a few steps apart.

The mistake is trying to force every station under the same roof.

Smelting works much better with a dedicated **outdoor production wing**. A side platform several foundations deep gives the furnaces and other bulky equipment enough room without turning the middle of the house into an obstacle course.

<h2 id="storage">
  How much storage should I plan for?
</h2>

Much more than the first few hours suggest, but I still would not build a warehouse on day one.

One compact arrangement held **16 chests** in the main storage section while leaving room to increase the capacity later. That was enough to keep early materials organized without allowing the storage room to consume the whole house.

<h2 id="campfires-and-farming">
  Where should campfires and farming go?
</h2>

Both systems can break an otherwise tidy floor plan if you leave them until the end.

A **campfire cannot sit under a normal roof**, so I reserve an uncovered cooking corner before finishing the building. Two campfires are more practical than one when several foods are being processed, and putting them along the outside edge keeps the center path clear.

Farming has a different restriction: **crop plots need actual ground rather than foundations**, and the crop area cannot simply be sealed beneath a normal roof.

That means the farm should sit beside the building from the beginning instead of being squeezed into whatever patch remains after the house is finished.

<h2 id="workstation-layout">
  What should fit in a long-term starter base?
</h2>

By the time the first long-term base is fully operating, it can handle most of the early and midgame preparation without becoming enormous.

Inside, the tested setup had room for the **Jewelry Bench, Rune-related equipment, Crafting Bench, Armorer Bench, Fletching Bench, Blacksmith's Bench, Tannery, Spinning Wheel, storage, Grill, and Cooking Range**.

The larger outdoor work area handled the main **smelting stations**, along with the Loom, Pottery Wheel, Brewing Cauldron, campfires, and other equipment that did not need the most convenient indoor space.

<h2 id="roof">
  Is Eye of Oculus useful for building?
</h2>

It becomes much more useful once the walls and roof are high enough that normal placement starts fighting the camera.

With **Eye of Oculus**, I can move around the upper structure and see roof angles, beams, support walls, and snap points directly instead of building temporary stairs every time another piece needs to be placed.

That is particularly helpful when several roof angles meet. A piece that looks aligned from the ground can still be attached to the wrong snap point, and long sections eventually need enough structural support underneath them to remain stable.

I finish the floor plan first. Doors, storage, outdoor production, and the route between work areas matter more than perfect roof geometry. Once the functional layout is settled, the roof is much easier to finish without having to tear it apart again.

<h2 id="lodestone-space">
  Where should the Lodestone go?
</h2>

I leave a small clear pad at the back or side of the property rather than placing the Lodestone in the middle of the workshop.

That keeps the teleport arrival point out of the storage and crafting lanes while still making it easy to enter the base. On a larger property, it can even sit in a small separate structure without hurting the workflow.

Do not fill every nearby patch with crops, campfires, or processing equipment before reserving that space.

The exact Vault Core requirements, travel costs, free connections, and forward Lodestone placement are covered in the <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/fast-travel">fast-travel route</Link>. For the base itself, all that matters is leaving a sensible arrival point before the property becomes crowded.

<h2 id="when-to-expand">
  When should I expand instead of moving?
</h2>

I expand for **space problems** and move for **location problems**.

If the furnaces need another strip of foundations, add one. If storage is full, extend the storage wall or add another section. If a newly unlocked bench finally makes the indoor workshop too tight, open one side of the building and increase the floor plan.

None of those problems justify rebuilding everything somewhere else.

Relocation starts making more sense when the terrain itself prevents useful expansion or when most of the character's activity has permanently shifted far beyond the opening regions. That is when locations such as Fellhollow or Umbral Sands become more attractive than endlessly enlarging the first house.

Even then, I do not move simply because the map has opened another biome. A working base plus forward Lodestones remains efficient for a long time.

The test I use is the routine after returning from an expedition:

**unload → process materials → repair or craft → cook and restock → leave again.**

If that cycle still takes place in one compact route, the base is doing its job. I would put the next batch of resources into equipment and continue the <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/progression">RuneScape: Dragonwilds progression route</Link> until the layout or location creates a real reason to change it.
