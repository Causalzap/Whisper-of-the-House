<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
Keep the tiny early shelter until storage and workstations start fighting for
space. For a first long-term base, look for **flat ground beside water**; the
lake just north of the starting area in **Temple Woods** works well. Build a
compact indoor workshop, leave an uncovered side for smelting and campfires,
keep farming plots on actual ground, and leave room to expand instead of
building the finished endgame base immediately.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

<h2 id="when-to-build">
  When should I build a real starter base?
</h2>

The shelter that gets you through the opening hours does not need to become
your permanent home.

I would keep it while it still handles the basics: a claimed bed, a few
containers, food preparation, repairs, and the first crafting stations. The
point where I rebuild is when normal preparation starts turning into inventory
management — chests are blocking walkways, new stations have nowhere sensible
to go, and I am moving equipment outside simply because the room is full.

That is early enough to matter, but late enough that I already know which
systems I actually use.

A practical starter base can hold the major stations from roughly **Gear Level
1 through Gear Level 4**, with room for some early Gear Level 5 equipment as
well. That gives it a much longer life than the first quest shack without
forcing me to build an oversized fortress before I have the materials or
stations to fill one.

The goal is a working base I can keep returning to while progression moves
through the first several regions, not a final showcase build.

<h2 id="best-location">
  Where is a good starter base location?
</h2>

A strong early location is the **lake just north of the starting area in
Temple Woods**.

What I want from the terrain matters more than finding one magical map
coordinate:

- enough **flat land** that foundations are not constantly fighting the slope;
- a **body of water** close enough that water access and fishing remain
  convenient;
- open ground beside the house for farming and stations that should stay
  outside;
- enough unused space behind or beside the building to add another utility
  area later.

I would not restart an established base purely because another spot looks a

the layout you need, or when progression has moved so far away that you
actually want a new permanent working area.

Long travel by itself is cheaper to solve with the
<Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/fast-travel">
  Lodestone and fast-travel network
</Link>
than by carrying an entire workshop across the map.

<h2 id="base-size">
  How big should the starter base be?
</h2>

The tested layout begins with a **3 × 5 rectangular foundation core** before
adding side spaces and exterior work areas.

That is a useful scale reference, not a requirement to copy every wall.

What matters is that the central structure is large enough for:

- a storage wall;
- the crafting stations used constantly;
- cooking;
- gear and weapon production;
- a clear route through the room;
- doors leading directly toward outdoor processing and farming.

Starting with a compact footprint also makes expansion much easier to judge.
When a new station arrives, I can tell whether it belongs inside, outside, or
whether one side of the base needs another foundation strip.

Building twice as large at the start usually creates empty floor space while
consuming materials that would be more useful on tools, stations, storage, and
progression gear.

<h2 id="indoor-outdoor">
  Which crafting stations should go inside and which should stay outside?
</h2>

This is where the layout stops being cosmetic.

Not every production station fits comfortably under the same roof. A good
starter base deliberately reserves an **outdoor production wing** instead of
trying to force everything indoors.

One working arrangement keeps the major smelting equipment outside. An
exterior platform about **three foundations deep** was enough to create useful
room for the smelting side of the build, along with other bulky stations.

I keep the stations I touch constantly while gearing up closer to the central
storage area. Crafting, armor, fletching, blacksmithing, jewelry, Rune-related
work, tanning, and spinning all benefit from being in the same compact working
zone.

Outside, there is more freedom for smelting equipment, the Pottery Wheel,
Brewing Cauldron, Loom, and other stations that either need open space or do
not need to occupy the most convenient indoor positions.

The important part is the path between them. Ore coming out of storage should
not require a lap around the house to reach the furnace, and finished bars
should not be several rooms away from the bench that uses them.

<h2 id="campfires">
  Why won't my campfire fit under the roof?
</h2>

A **campfire cannot be placed underneath the roof**, so leave part of the
cooking area exposed before finishing the building.

Two campfires worked better than one in the starter layout. Cooking can take
long enough that having a second fire lets another stack process without
turning one corner of the base into a queue.

I like putting them near the edge of the outdoor working area rather than in
the middle of the main route. They stay easy to reach without forcing me to
walk around fires every time I move between storage and the door.

This is also a good reason to plan the exterior zones before finishing every
roof and wall. It is much easier to leave one useful corner uncovered than to
discover later that the cooking setup has nowhere legal to go.

<h2 id="farming">
  Where should I put farming plots?
</h2>

Farming needs real ground.

**Farming plots cannot be placed on foundations**, so the farm should be
planned beside the structure rather than as another room built on the same
floor.

There is a second restriction that changes the design: **you cannot cover the
crops with a normal roof**.

That means a decorative enclosed greenhouse can still use pillars and low
walls around the plots, but the growing area itself needs to remain open
above.

The tested farm uses rows **three plots wide**, which works well with the
watering magic used as farming improves. I would rather keep those rows clean
and reachable than squeeze in one extra plot that makes every harvest awkward.

This is another reason flat ground around the house matters. A large building
on the only level patch can leave the farming area fighting slopes and snap
points for the rest of the game.

<h2 id="storage">
  How much storage should I leave room for?
</h2>

More than the first few hours suggest.

One compact starter layout fit **16 chests** in its main storage section, with
room to add additional layers later if the collection outgrew that setup.

I would still avoid building an enormous storage hall before I need it. The
better approach is to leave vertical or side expansion available.

The central position matters more than raw chest count. Materials constantly
move between storage and crafting stations, so putting every chest at the
furthest edge of the property adds friction to every repair and crafting
session.

Once you have the **Storage Ledger**, the storage workflow changes again. The
<Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/beginner-guide">
  beginner guide
</Link>
covers how the Ledger deposits matching stacks and how nearby crafting uses
stored materials. For the physical layout, the useful takeaway is simple:
keep the main storage zone near the stations you actually use.

<h2 id="workstation-layout">
  What should actually fit in the finished starter base?
</h2>

The completed layout gives a useful reality check for how much space the first
long-term base needs.

Inside, it can accommodate the main crafting and gear loop: **Jewelry Bench,
Mystic equipment, Rune-related stations, Crafting Bench, Armorer Bench,
Fletching Bench, Blacksmith's Bench, Tannery, Spinning Wheel, storage, Grill,
and Cooking Range**.

Outside, the layout keeps the major **smelting stations**, along with the Loom,
Pottery Wheel, campfires, Brewing Cauldron, and other equipment that fits the
exterior workflow better.

The significant omission is the later **advanced stations**. Trying to reserve
space for every endgame station during the first major build makes the starter
base much larger than it needs to be.

That is why this size works well for me: it carries the character through the
first several equipment tiers, but still gives a clear reason to expand once
advanced production becomes a real part of the routine.

<h2 id="roof">
  Is the Eye of Oculus worth using while building the roof?
</h2>

Yes. Once the walls are high enough that normal placement becomes awkward,
**Eye of Oculus** makes roof work much easier.

Instead of repeatedly climbing temporary stairs or trying to reach snap points
from the floor, I can move around the upper structure and see the beams,
supports, and roof angles directly.

That matters because structural stability starts becoming visible on the
larger roof sections. Some long spans eventually refuse placement until a wall
or beam provides another supported snap point.

I would finish the functional footprint first, make sure the outdoor areas and
doors are where I want them, then use Eye of Oculus for the roof rather than
perfecting the roof while the workstation layout is still changing.

A beautiful roof over a room that later needs another doorway is just extra
work.

<h2 id="lodestone-space">
  Where should the Lodestone go in the base?
</h2>

Leave a small clear pad on the edge or back side of the property.

The Lodestone does not need to occupy the center of the workshop. Putting it
slightly behind the main structure keeps the teleport arrival point out of the
crafting lanes while still making the base easy to enter.

I only reserve the **space** here. Vault Core farming, the exact Lodestone
materials, Law Rune travel costs, weight scaling, free travel connections, and
forward placement are all part of the
<Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/fast-travel">
  fast-travel route
</Link>

For the base itself, the decision is simply to avoid discovering later that
every open patch near the house has been filled with crops or processing
stations.

<h2 id="when-to-expand">
  When should I expand the base instead of rebuilding it?
</h2>

Expansion is usually enough while the existing base still has a good workflow.

Add another exterior strip when the smelting area fills. Raise or extend the
storage section when 16 chests are no longer enough. Open another wall when
advanced stations finally become part of normal crafting.

A full relocation makes more sense when the terrain itself is now limiting the
build, or when you deliberately want to establish a new permanent center much
deeper into progression.

Until then, a stable base plus forward Lodestones is cheaper than repeatedly
moving everything you own.

The build is finished when I can come home from an expedition and move through
the same short cycle without fighting the layout:

**unload → process materials → repair or craft → cook and restock → leave
again.**

Once that loop is smooth, more building is optional. I would put the materials
back into gear and continue the
<Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds">
  main RuneScape: Dragonwilds progression
</Link>
until a new station or storage problem gives me a concrete reason to expand.
