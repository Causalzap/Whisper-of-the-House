<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
Clear an early **Vault**, bring back at least **1 Vault Core**, then build a
Lodestone with **32 Stone, 20 Rune Essence, and 1 Vault Core**. Keep some Law
Runes in reserve, and place the next Lodestone near the route you are actually
repeating rather than walking back to your main base every time.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

<h2 id="unlock-fast-travel">
  How do I unlock fast travel in RuneScape: Dragonwilds?
</h2>

The first real obstacle is getting a **Vault Core**.

Lodestones are player-built fast-travel points. One requires:

- **32 Stone**
- **20 Rune Essence**
- **1 Vault Core**

Stone and Rune Essence are easy enough to replace during normal exploration.
The Vault Core is the material I plan around, because the same resource takes
me into a Vault and competes with other useful progression and base upgrades.

There is no reason to wait until you can build a huge network. The first useful
expansion is simply another point near somewhere you already expect to revisit.

I usually leave the established base alone, carry the materials for one
Lodestone into the next region, and place it once the walk back has become an
obvious waste of time.

<h2 id="vault-cores">
  Where do I get Vault Cores?
</h2>

**Vaults are the repeatable source of Vault Cores.**

A Vault can contain more than one. Near the later part of the run, check the
rooms around the lever that opens the boss route. A Core can appear around
that section, and another can be found around the boss room. A typical Vault
can provide roughly **two to three Vault Cores**.

Inventory space matters more here than it first appears. A **Vault Core weighs
50**, so collecting several after spending the run filling every spare slot
with ordinary materials can immediately push the character over the comfortable
carry limit.

That changed how I approach repeat Vault runs. Food, water, equipment, and
enough free capacity for the Cores come first. Stone and low-priority drops can
wait.

Once I already know the route, I also stop treating every return visit as a
full dungeon clear. Reaching the Core efficiently matters more than spending
extra durability and supplies fighting enemies that are no longer blocking the
resource I came for.

<h2 id="where-to-place-lodestones">
  Where should I place Lodestones?
</h2>

A useful Lodestone removes a trip I was already making repeatedly.

The placements that keep paying for themselves are usually:

1. **The main base** — for storage, crafting, repairs, food, and Rest.
2. **The current progression region** — once the walk from home becomes
   repetitive.
3. **A Vault or important farming route** — when I already know I will return.
4. **Near a free world-travel connection** — so the free leg covers most of
   the distance before I spend Law Runes.

I do not relocate an entire base simply because progression has moved several
minutes farther away. Chests, farms, crafting stations, processing equipment,
and stored materials take much longer to move than one Lodestone takes to
build.

That decision becomes even easier once the first long-term base is working
well. The
<Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/starter-base">
  starter base layout
</Link>
is much more useful as a stable crafting center while the fast-travel network
moves outward with progression.

On one later route, the trip back toward the established base was already
showing a **14 Law Rune** cost. That still was not enough reason to dismantle
the whole base. It was a reason to improve the network.

The same principle continues as the main route moves into later regions. The
<Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/progression">
  Dragonwilds progression guide
</Link>
shows where the major regional handoffs happen, which helps me decide when a
forward Lodestone is likely to keep paying for itself instead of serving one
temporary trip.

A Lodestone setup that feels perfect in the early regions will eventually be
poorly positioned for where you are actually playing.

<h2 id="law-rune-cost">
  Why does Lodestone fast travel cost more Law Runes sometimes?
</h2>

Lodestone travel does **not** have one fixed Law Rune price.

The two variables I have seen change it are:

**distance and carried weight.**

With a light inventory, two example destinations cost **3** and **6 Law
Runes**. After loading up to roughly **345 carry weight**, those trips rose to
**6** and **13 Law Runes**.

That is enough of a difference that I unload before a long teleport.

Stone, logs, ore, duplicate equipment, and anything already stored at the
destination should not ride along simply because there is room. Carrying
unnecessary weight turns convenience into a recurring Rune tax.

The effect becomes much harsher over long distances. One direct return trip
reached **37 Law Runes**.

A displayed cost that high is a signal to look at the route before pressing
Teleport. There may be a free connection that handles the expensive middle of
the journey, leaving only a short paid jump at the end.

Other runs showed **9** and **14 Law Rune** teleports as progression moved
farther from the original base. Those numbers are useful examples, not fixed
prices for specific destinations.

<h2 id="burrow-fast-travel">
  How do the free burrow fast-travel points work?
</h2>

The world also has a **burrow travel network** that can cover long distances
without consuming Law Runes.

It becomes useful once you have discovered more than one connection. Rather
than teleporting directly from a distant region all the way home, I use the
free network for the largest possible section of the journey and then make a
short Lodestone jump from whichever exit is closest to my destination.

That matters most once a direct Lodestone trip starts costing double-digit
Law Runes.

It also changes where a Lodestone is worth building. A point near a burrow
does not need to sit directly beside every quest or resource. The free route
has already done much of the movement for you.

I still activate these connections when I encounter them even if I do not need
the route immediately. Their value grows as the explored map gets larger.

<h2 id="law-runes">
  How do I stop running out of Law Runes?
</h2>

The awkward stage comes after Lodestones become useful but before Law Runes
are easy to replace.

**Law Rune crafting unlocks at Runecrafting level 50.**

Before that point, I treat every stack as both travel currency and a reserve
for getting out of trouble. Burning the last few Runes on convenience feels
fine until the next objective ends several regions away from home.

A real failure state looks like this:

You reach a new area, spend several teleports moving equipment and supplies,
then discover that the remaining Law Rune stack is too small for the return
trip you actually care about.

I now check Law Runes before leaving in the same pass where I check food,
water, repair state, and inventory capacity.

World Rune sources are also worth remembering. A useful Law Rune source near
a Lodestone or a route you revisit regularly becomes part of the travel
network itself: collect there whenever the resource has refreshed instead of
waiting until the stack is nearly empty.

After **Runecrafting 50**, the situation gets easier because Law Runes are no
longer purely something you must find. It still does not make a badly placed,
high-cost network efficient. Distance and weight continue to determine how
quickly the supply disappears.

<h2 id="home-teleport">
  Is Home Teleport the same as using a Lodestone?
</h2>

No.

**Home Teleport** is the emergency return option added in 1.0. It sends you to
your **claimed bed**, costs **15 Law Runes**, and has a **900-second / 15-minute
cooldown**.

That makes its role different from a Lodestone.

Lodestones are for repeated movement between useful points. Home Teleport is
for the moment when the expedition is finished and all I really want is one
reliable trip back to the claimed-bed base.

The cooldown is why I do not build my normal travel routine around it. It
cannot replace repeated runs between a base, Vault, resource route, and current
quest region.

It is still useful when returning with a full load. Lodestone cost can climb
as carried weight rises, while Home Teleport gives me a predictable way to end
an expedition and return directly to the bed rather than continuing deeper
into the network.

<h2 id="vault-core-priority">
  Should I spend my next Vault Core on another Lodestone?
</h2>

A Lodestone is not automatically the best use of every Core.

Vault Cores compete with other useful construction and progression needs, so I
look at how much time the missing teleport point is actually costing.

One annoying walk does not justify another Lodestone. Repetition does.

I build the next one once one of these starts happening regularly:

- the same quest road is being run again and again;
- the same Vault is being revisited for more Cores;
- valuable materials repeatedly have to cross a dangerous stretch;
- the nearest useful Lodestone makes every trip unnecessarily expensive;
- death recovery requires another long run through an area already cleared.

At that stage the Core is no longer buying convenience. It is removing a
recurring piece of wasted time.

There are also moments where I keep the Core. A workstation, utility upgrade,
or another progression requirement that solves an immediate block matters more
than arriving five minutes faster.

The teleport network should support progression, not consume the resource that
progression currently needs.

<h2 id="forward-network">
  When should I build a new Lodestone instead of moving my base?
</h2>

Long travel alone is usually a reason to build forward, not a reason to move
home.

The pattern that has worked best for me is:

**stable main base → push into the next region → place a forward Lodestone once
the route becomes repetitive → keep moving the network as progression moves.**

That becomes particularly noticeable after a bad death. A gravestone deep in
a dangerous region is far more annoying when the nearest teleport point is
back near home. A Lodestone built near the current frontier turns the recovery
into a short run instead of repeating the entire journey.

I only start thinking seriously about relocating the base when the **base
itself** has become the limitation — bad terrain, poor expansion space, or a
deliberate decision to establish a new long-term crafting center.

Otherwise the existing base can keep doing its job while Lodestones handle
distance.

The late-game Scorned Wilderness is a special case. Once the route reaches
Commander Zilyana and the raid structure takes over, follow the
<Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/scorned-wilderness">
  Scorned Wilderness walkthrough
</Link>
for its extraction points and internal progression instead of treating the
whole raid like another normal overworld commute.

For ordinary exploration, I keep adding Lodestones only when I can answer one
question clearly:

**Which repeated trip is this point removing?**

When there is no good answer, I keep the Vault Core.
