<h2 id="gear">
  Do I need full Rune gear to beat Kuldra?
</h2>

No. A first clear reached Kuldra in strong **Tier 7 armor**, after entering the raid with weapons such as the Abyssal Whip, Dorgeshuun Crossbow, and Necromancer Staff and upgrading the most useful weapon slots to a **Rune Sword and Rune Crossbow** along the way. Full Rune armor was not required.

That makes the final extraction point a place to fix clear weaknesses rather than a reason to craft every Rune piece available. If the raid has already carried your current armor all the way to Kuldra, a meaningful weapon upgrade, repaired gear, and enough consumables for the boss can be a better use of the materials than delaying the fight for a complete matching set.

The route through the stronghold, extraction points, and elemental puzzle system is covered in the <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/scorned-wilderness">Scorned Wilderness walkthrough</Link>. Once you are already outside Kuldra's arena, bank the Runite, Luminite, hides, and other raid materials you do not need in combat, then make sure you have:

- repaired combat gear;
- a reliable ranged weapon for the elemental dragons and Heart;
- **Anti-poison** for the final phase;
- **Antifire** for the dragon-heavy outdoor phases;
- enough healing food for several transitions;
- any Whetstone or combat infusion you actually intend to use.

The fight changes shape several times, so carrying only a close-range weapon makes the later sections less forgiving than they need to be.

<h2 id="resonator">
  How does the Harmonic Resonator work?
</h2>

The first three phases repeat the same core objective while Kuldra attacks in the background:

**gain the required Anima → power the pylon → create an Anima Orb → load the Harmonic Resonator → fire at Kuldra.**

In single-player, one loaded Resonator gave me **three shots**, and each successful hit dealt **9,999 damage**. 

The dangerous mistake is treating the machine like a safe interaction. Kuldra continues attacking while you charge, load, and aim it. If the main head begins a high-damage attack while I am using the Resonator, I leave the interaction immediately and reposition. Losing a few seconds costs far less than taking the beam while locked into the mechanic.

Once the Resonator is loaded and the arena is calm enough to aim, fire the available shots promptly rather than leaving the cannon charged while more hazards build around you.

<h2 id="hyper-beam">
  How do I avoid Kuldra's beam attack?
</h2>

Kuldra throws out poison, fire, ice, and other area pressure during the outdoor phases, but the **main head's beam** is the attack that changes my priorities fastest. It hits much harder than the ordinary ground effects and can punish the extra second spent finishing a Resonator interaction.

The arena provides solid cover. **Green crystals** and other large objects can block the line between you and the beam, so I position with one of those nearby whenever possible. If I already have open space, moving fully out of the firing line also works.

Normal elemental zones leave more room for judgment. The beam does not. When both Kuldra and the Resonator demand attention at the same time, survive the beam first and finish the mechanic afterward.

<h2 id="phase-one">
  What do I do in Kuldra Phase 1?
</h2>

Phase 1 introduces the complete Resonator loop with relatively few moving parts.

A **blue dragon** enters the arena and supplies **Water Anima** when killed. Earth is also available for the other part of the setup. The dragon is part of the objective rather than optional pressure; ignoring it leaves you without the element needed to progress.

At roughly **75% health**, Zilyana creates a protective barrier. Use that transition to heal and refresh any consumable buff that needs replacing. It is a much safer place to reset than the opening seconds of the next arena. 

<h2 id="phase-two">
  How do I open the Orb Dispenser in Phase 2?
</h2>

Phase 2 begins with the **Anima Orb Dispenser behind a locked gate**.

A **red dragon hatchling** enters the fight. Kill it for **Fire Anima**, take that charge to the required pylon, and activate it to open access to the dispenser. Once the gate is open, return to the same cycle: generate the Orb, load the Resonator, and fire at Kuldra.

The mechanic is familiar by this point, which is exactly why it is easy to get greedy. Kuldra is still attacking during every interaction. Keep enough space to read the main head rather than standing beside the machine waiting for the last possible second to dodge.

Zilyana provides another protected transition when Kuldra reaches roughly **50% health**. Heal there instead of carrying avoidable missing health into Phase 3.

<h2 id="phase-three">
  How does Kuldra Phase 3 work?
</h2>

Phase 3 combines both elemental dragon types in the same arena.

You now have **red and blue dragons**, supplying Fire and Water Anima for multiple pylons. The objective remains familiar, but there are more enemies and more competing targets around the Resonator.

The **Orb Dispenser platform near a green crystal** gave me the cleanest position from which to organize the phase. The crystal provides beam cover, while a Crossbow or Staff can reach the elemental dragons without chasing them across the whole arena.

Kill the dragons required for the current charges, power the Water and Fire pylons, generate the Orb, and keep feeding Resonator shots into Kuldra.

When Kuldra finally falls to the ground, do not relax yet. The health bar remains, and a new bridge opens toward the body.

<h2 id="inside-kuldra">
  What do I do after Kuldra collapses?
</h2>

Cross the bridge and approach Kuldra's head. An interaction appears that lets you **enter Kuldra**, which starts the real final phase.

<h2 id="heart-phase">
  What is the safest way to destroy Kuldra's Heart?
</h2>

The Heart supports two very different approaches, and the better one depends on what you still have left after the first three phases.

The safer route is **ranged damage from farther back**. From a good rear position, the enemies around the Heart have a harder time reaching you. Damage takes longer, but that trade is attractive when healing food or potions are running low after the outdoor fight. 

Moving close to the Heart kills it faster, but now you are fighting beside **Heart Worms** while poison builds around the arena.

This is the phase where I want an **Anti-poison dose still available**. A Water-enchanted weapon can also help at close range because its passive healing offsets part of the sustained damage. 

The Heart becomes more aggressive as its health falls: the beating speeds up, attacks arrive more often, and poison pressure increases until the hazard can begin following your position.

That escalation is not a reason to race the final chunk of health. If melee pressure starts stacking faster than you can control it, move away, clear the poison problem, restore health, and return from range rather than forcing the last few hits.

<h2 id="escape">
  Do I have to escape after destroying Kuldra's Heart?
</h2>

Yes. Destroying the Heart is followed by an instruction to **get out**.

Turn around and retrace the route used to enter. There is nothing else to wait for inside, and lingering only delays the actual kill.

Once you escape, Kuldra dies and you are returned to **Commander Zilyana** for the rewards.

<h2 id="rewards">
  What do you get for beating Kuldra?
</h2>

The first completion awards the **Regicide Reward Pack**, which guarantees:

- **Dragon Kite Shield**
- **Dragon Slayer Cape**

The Dragon Slayer Cape gives **+2 Melee Defence, +2 Ranged Defence, and +2 Magic Defence**. It is a prestige reward with modest defensive stats rather than an automatic replacement for every cape you may already be wearing.

The Dragon Kite Shield is the more immediate equipment upgrade and arrives as a high-tier shield after the first victory.

Kuldra also drops a **Queen Slayer Pack** containing a random rare reward. First-clear examples produced pieces such as a **Dragon Platebody** and **Dragon Platelegs**, while later kills can produce other Dragon equipment rather than the same fixed item every time.

That randomness is important if you are deciding whether one victory is enough. Killing Kuldra once guarantees the first-clear items; filling out the wider Dragon equipment set requires repeated runs and luck.

<h2 id="farm-kuldra">
  Can I farm Kuldra for more Dragon gear?
</h2>

Yes. Kuldra can be killed again for additional **Queen Slayer Packs**, but completing the raid resets the Scorned Wilderness checkpoints. The next attempt therefore begins from the raid route again rather than teleporting directly to Kuldra.

Repeat clears are still much faster once the route is familiar. You already know which enemies can be skipped, where the breakable walls are, how the Anima rooms work, and where each extraction point sits.

After the first victory, the equipment decision also changes. Rune gear remains useful, but random Dragon drops can begin replacing pieces before a complete Rune set is finished. I would spend raid materials on upgrades that solve an immediate weakness, then check what the Queen Slayer Pack actually gives before committing the rest.

Later packs have produced rewards such as **Dragon Platelegs** and **Kuldra's Wand**, but there is no guarantee that the next pack contains the exact slot you want.

Before another run, restock healing and resistance supplies first. Once the route and boss mechanics are familiar, arriving at the Heart with too little food or no Anti-poison is a much more avoidable failure than any of the puzzles on the way there.
