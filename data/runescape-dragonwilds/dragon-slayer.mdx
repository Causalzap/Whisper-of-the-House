<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
Start **Dragon Slayer** by finishing the opening tasks for the Wise Old Man, then build a shelter and claim a covered bed in Bramblemead. The quest sends you through the Goblin Castle, the swamp, Fractured Plains, Bloodblight Swamp, and Stormtouched Highlands before you can craft the **Challenge Horn** and summon Velgar. Bring repaired gear, strong healing food, **Anti-poison**, and enough ammunition or combat Runes for more than one attempt. After Velgar dies, take **Velgar's head** from the quest-item tab back to the Wise Old Man to finish the quest.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

<h2 id="start-dragon-slayer">
  How do I start the Dragon Slayer quest?
</h2>

Dragon Slayer begins after the opening tutorial sequence brings you back to the **Wise Old Man in Bramblemead Village**. Once the earlier tasks are out of the way, he gives you the main quest and immediately asks you to establish a proper place to live.

The first requirement is simple but easy to overcomplicate: build a shelter and **claim a bed**. The bed has to be underneath a roof before the game will let you claim it. You do not need to build the permanent base you intend to use for the rest of the game just to clear this objective. A small covered room is enough.

If you are already ready to build something more permanent, the <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/starter-base">starter base location and layout</Link> covers the early locations, workspace placement, storage, farming, and when it starts making sense to move.

Once the bed is claimed, return to the Wise Old Man. He next asks for a **deer antler** so he can track Cathan. This is also the point where the quest starts nudging you toward better early combat equipment rather than letting you continue indefinitely with the tutorial loadout.

<h2 id="deer-antler">
  How do I get the deer antler and continue the quest?
</h2>

You can kill a deer with a bow, magic, or whatever reliable ranged option you already have. If the bow is still locked, gather flax, use the spinning wheel to make **Coarse Thread**, and continue the early crafting chain until the bow recipe becomes available.

Bring the antler back to the Wise Old Man. His tracking spell points you toward the goblins and the ruined castle where Cathan was last seen.

Before leaving Bramblemead, this is a good time to stop treating gear upgrades as optional. The castle route contains enough enemies that entering with underpowered weapons turns a simple investigation into repeated food consumption. The quest also introduces the **Life Ward** around this stage, giving you an additional defensive buffer once you have completed the associated step.

You do not need to grind far beyond the region, but getting your available **Power Level 2** equipment into usable condition makes the next section noticeably cleaner.

<h2 id="cathan-journal">
  Where is Cathan's Journal?
</h2>

Head to the **Goblin Castle** and work your way upward through the interior. Rats and goblins fill the route, but there is no special puzzle required to reach the objective. Keep climbing until you reach the upper part of the castle.

**Cathan's Journal is sitting on a box near the top.**

Reading it triggers the next major event: **Velgar appears for the first time**.

Do not burn food, Runes, or weapon durability trying to turn this encounter into the final boss fight. At this stage, Velgar is not a normal damage check. You are not meant to kill him here. After the encounter progresses and he leaves, the quest points you toward the **blue-flame door in the swamp**.

That changes the objective from chasing Velgar directly to finding out what happened to Cathan.

<h2 id="blue-flame-door">
  What do I do at the blue-flame door in the swamp?
</h2>

Before making repeated long runs into the swamp, I would unlock **Lodestone travel** if you have not already done so. The distances become large enough during Dragon Slayer that walking the same route after every death wastes more time than the vault detour needed to open fast travel.

Once you reach the swamp objective, enter the nearby vault and clear it. At the far end you can speak with **Spitfoot**, the wounded goblin who provides more information about Cathan.

After that conversation, return to the Wise Old Man.

The next destination is **Fractured Plains**. The game can send you there before you feel completely prepared for its enemies, because many of the materials required for the next equipment step are found inside the new region itself. You do not need to stand in the previous biome farming endlessly before crossing the border.

<h2 id="cathan-ghost">
  Where do I find Cathan in Fractured Plains?
</h2>

Once you enter Fractured Plains, the quest points toward **Velgar's Tower**.

Put a Lodestone somewhere useful before committing to repeated runs into the area. This part of Dragon Slayer introduces stronger enemies and is also where failed attempts start becoming expensive if your nearest return point is several minutes away.

At the tower you find **Cathan's ghost**.

Speaking with him explains what happened and finally gives you the path to forcing a real fight with Velgar. You cannot simply walk into the tower and attack the dragon whenever you want. You first need to make a **Challenge Horn**.

The horn is also where Dragon Slayer changes from a mostly linear investigation into a resource and progression check. Its ingredients send you into two more dangerous regions, and both of the important materials require upgraded gathering tools.

<h2 id="challenge-horn-materials">
  How do I craft the Challenge Horn?
</h2>

The important ingredients are a **Dragon Tooth**, **Bloodwood Sap**, and **five Bronze Bars**. You should also have the required smithing setup available back at your base.

The two quest materials are the parts most likely to stop progress.

### Dragon Tooth

Dragon Teeth are harvested from the enormous dragon remains in **Stormtouched Highlands**. They are not ordinary mining nodes scattered around the ground.

You need an **Iron Pickaxe** before the exposed teeth can be harvested.

If the tooth refuses to harvest, do not spend time searching for a different skeleton first. Check the pickaxe.

I would also take more than the minimum amount if the route is already safe for you. A failed Velgar attempt can force another Challenge Horn, and repeating the Highlands trip just because you collected exactly one quest material is avoidable.

### Bloodwood Sap

**Bloodwood Sap** comes from the large Bloodwood tree inside the castle in **Bloodblight Swamp**.

This time the gathering check is an **Iron Axe**.

Bloodblight itself is dangerous before Velgar ever enters the picture. Have Anti-poison available before moving deep into the region, and place a Lodestone somewhere near enough that a death does not turn into another full cross-map run.

You can also use this trip to collect the herbs and materials needed for better healing and later equipment instead of entering the swamp only for a single drop.

### Returning home

Once you have the Dragon Tooth, Bloodwood Sap, and Bronze Bars, go back to your base and use the smithing setup to make the **Challenge Horn**.

The horn appears with your special quest items rather than behaving like ordinary inventory clutter, so do not assume the craft failed just because it is not sitting beside your food and tools.

Do not immediately run from the anvil back to Velgar just because the horn is finished. This is the last convenient point to repair gear, cook food, restock ammunition, and replace anything you burned while gathering the materials.

<h2 id="before-summoning-velgar">
  What should I bring before summoning Velgar?
</h2>

The Challenge Horn is where I stop thinking about exploration and prepare for a boss attempt.

My first fights showed that simply wearing a higher-looking armor set was not enough to make Velgar comfortable. The supplies around the armor mattered more. I would go in with the primary weapon fully repaired, a complete stack of healing food, enough arrows or combat Runes to survive a long attempt, and **Anti-poison ready to use**.

If you are using ranged weapons, bring more ammunition than the optimistic estimate. One successful 1.0 attempt ran low on **Barbed Iron Arrows** near the end, and those arrows were doing enough work that losing them earlier could easily have changed the result.

The same principle applies to food. A failed attempt with weak healing was not failing because every individual attack was unavoidable. The problem was that the food could not recover enough health between mistakes. Better healing changed the fight without requiring a complete rebuild of the character.

If you are playing co-op, also watch the firing line. One player standing directly beside Velgar while another attacks through the same space can create unnecessary friendly-fire problems depending on the setup.

<h2 id="summon-velgar">
  How do I summon Velgar?
</h2>

Return to **Velgar's Tower** with the Challenge Horn and finish all preparation before interacting with the summon point.

Buff first. Repair first. Put the food and Anti-poison where you can actually reach them under pressure.

Then place the Challenge Horn to start the real encounter.

If you crafted or gathered enough material for more than one horn, a failed attempt is much less punishing. If you made only one, do not waste the opening seconds reorganizing the hotbar after Velgar is already active.

<h2 id="best-damage">
  What damage works best against Velgar?
</h2>

Among the spells I used, **Air produced the strongest immediate single-target damage**.

Water was noticeably weaker as direct damage. Fire still had value because of its continuing pressure, but Air moved Velgar's health bar faster and reached **51-damage criticals** without requiring a large additional gear grind.

That does not make magic mandatory.

Melee works, and ranged weapons can also finish the fight. The choice changes the positioning problem more than the basic objective.

A melee player stays close enough to deal with bites, swipes, and close-range movement. At range, backing away too aggressively can turn the fight into a constant exchange of projectiles and movement attacks. With magic, I preferred a middle distance: enough room to react, but not so far away that every attack became another chase across the arena.

The later 1.0 kill also showed how effective a good ranged setup can be. **Barbed Iron Arrows** remained useful deep into the fight, particularly once the goal changed from experimenting with damage types to simply finishing the remaining health safely.

<h2 id="poison-resistance">
  Do I need Anti-poison for Velgar?
</h2>

I would bring it even if you normally trust your dodging.

Poison can turn a manageable mistake into a failed attempt because it keeps consuming health after the original hit is already over. During one early fight, poison removed enough health that food was being spent just to stabilize the status damage rather than saving healing for Velgar's actual attacks.

After that wipe, we did not replace every piece of armor. We repaired what was damaged, refilled food, added poison protection, and tried again.

That was enough to make a substantial difference.

The Anti-poison used during this progression provided a **120-second protection window**, so do not drink it while still standing at the base rearranging supplies. Use it close enough to the pull that the protection covers the part of the fight where Velgar becomes difficult to control.

<h2 id="liftoff">
  How do I survive Velgar's airborne attacks?
</h2>

Velgar becomes much more dangerous after taking off.

The airborne sequence can layer several lines of damage across the arena. This is where a fight that looked stable a few seconds earlier can collapse, especially if another player has already died or you enter the transition without stamina.

The resource I protect here is **stamina**.

Do not empty the bar chasing Velgar, sprinting during quiet moments, or squeezing in one more roll that is not needed. Once the ground lines begin arriving, enough stamina has to remain for several consecutive movements.

Dodging **forward through an incoming line** worked better for me than trying to outrun the attack sideways after it had already spread across the floor. Moving through the danger reduces the distance you need to cover and puts you on the safe side faster.

This is also a poor moment to greed another spell or melee hit. Stop attacking, clear the movement sequence, then resume once Velgar gives you a stable opening again.

<h2 id="failed-attempts">
  Why does the Velgar fight keep falling apart?
</h2>

The most useful attempts were the failures where Velgar was already reaching half health or lower.

If you can consistently reach that point, the answer is not automatically “farm a complete new armor tier.”

The problems I would fix first are much smaller:

* repair anything that entered the fight damaged;
* replace weak food with healing that can actually recover from a bad exchange;
* carry Anti-poison instead of trying to heal through the status damage;
* bring enough arrows or Runes that the fight cannot outlast the supply;
* save stamina for liftoff rather than spending it on extra pressure;
* stop switching damage types once one option is already working reliably.

One failed attempt became unwinnable because poison, a player death, and the airborne pattern all stacked together. Another failed because the available food simply did not heal enough. Those are very different problems from entering the arena with damage so low that Velgar's health barely moves.

That distinction matters.

If ordinary attacks are deleting most of your health and your best hits barely change Velgar's bar, upgrade first. If you are already reaching the second half regularly, tightening the preparation and movement can save far more time than farming an entirely new equipment set.

<h2 id="successful-attempt">
  What changed on the successful Velgar kill?
</h2>

The winning attempts were not built around one enormous upgrade.

The fight improved after the setup became more consistent: repaired equipment, stronger healing, poison protection, a reliable damage plan, and enough stamina left for the aerial phase.

With magic, Air took larger chunks from Velgar's health than the weaker direct-damage options I had tested earlier. In the later ranged attempt, Barbed Iron Arrows provided enough sustained damage that running low on them near the end became one of the biggest remaining risks.

By the final quarter of Velgar's health, experimentation stopped being useful. That is the point to stay with the strongest attack that has already proven reliable, keep enough stamina for another liftoff, and avoid turning a nearly finished fight into another corpse run.

<h2 id="after-velgar">
  What do I do after beating Velgar?
</h2>

Dragon Slayer does not finish when Velgar hits the ground.

Open the **quest-item tab** after the kill and take **Velgar's head**. The objective then sends you back to the **Wise Old Man outside Velgar's lair**.

Hand over the head to complete Dragon Slayer.

Defeating Velgar also completes the **Dragon Slayer** achievement; the remaining requirements are listed in the <Link href="/runescape-dragonwilds/achievements">all 50 achievements checklist</Link>.

The Wise Old Man's dialogue also makes it clear that Velgar is not the end of the wider dragon threat. **Queen Cauldra** is still ahead, but there is no reason to keep farming Velgar just because Dragon Slayer is complete.

From here, move into the next region and continue building the character rather than repeating an early progression boss.
