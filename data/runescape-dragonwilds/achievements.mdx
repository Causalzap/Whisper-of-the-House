<h2 id="all-achievements">
  How should I approach all 50 achievements?
</h2>

Trying to complete the list in menu order creates unnecessary backtracking. The first hours already make you mine ore, chop trees, cook food, craft weapons and Runes, build a shelter, fish, farm, enchant equipment, and fight the regional bosses. Those actions cover a large part of the list without any special setup.

The requirements worth remembering early are the ones that become annoying if left until everything else is finished. Complete nearby Secondary Quests while you are already travelling through their region, use unfamiliar spells when their required setup appears naturally, and keep working through Agility courses instead of leaving every Gold medal for one final session.

The Level 99 achievements are different. Normal play feeds experience into almost every skill, so I let the main progression do as much of that work as possible before deliberately grinding the remaining levels.

<h2 id="early-achievements">
  Which achievements should unlock during normal progression?
</h2>

Thirteen of the requirements are basic actions that naturally happen while building a functioning character:

- **Swift Pick** — mine an ore
- **One Misthalin Star** — cook a food item
- **Chop Chop** — chop down a tree
- **Stab, Slash, Crush!** — construct a weapon
- **Crafting to Your Own Rune** — craft a Rune
- **Sheltered** — build a base and claim a bed
- **Not today!** — parry an attack
- **Fred would be proud** — plant a seed
- **Honed Edge** — enchant a weapon
- **Strike True** — hit an enemy with a trick shot
- **Winner Winner Fish Dinner** — eat fish-based food
- **Catch of the day** — catch a fish
- **Singed!** — hit an enemy with fire magic

There is little value in stopping progression to force most of these. Mining, Woodcutting, cooking, building, and fishing arrive naturally.

**Strike True** is the one I keep in mind because an ordinary ranged hit is not enough; the hit needs to count as a **trick shot**.

<h2 id="ability-achievements">
  Which achievements need a specific spell or ability setup?
</h2>

This group causes more cleanup because the numbers and combinations are precise:

- **You Axed For It** — cut down **3 trees** with one Axtral Projection
- **Mend it like...** — repair **3 items** with one Magical Mending
- **It's all Mine!** — break **4 ore deposits** with one Rocksplosion
- **All Buffed Up** — have food, infusion, and potion buffs active together
- **Eye can see my house from here!** — build while using Eye of Oculus
- **I'll Just Watch** — make a confused enemy defeat another enemy
- **Sharp and Shiny** — have a weapon enchanted and honed with a Whetstone at the same time
- **No Runefunds!** — cast Runes to Rune Essence
- **Two Misthalin Stars** — make an iconic meal
- **Gotta Patch 'Em All** — harvest **24 plots with one Uproot cast**
- **Master Huntsman** — hit a snared enemy with an enhanced arrow
- **Frenzied** — cast Fishnado on a frenzied fishing spot
- **Doric's Fire Fried Chicken** — kill a chicken with a fireball

Several are easier once the setup is prepared before casting.

For **All Buffed Up**, have all three consumables ready first. Use the food buff, infusion, and potion close together so the first effect is still active when the third one lands.

**Sharp and Shiny** works the same way: the magic enchantment and Whetstone effect need to overlap on the weapon rather than being applied in separate fights.

### Eye can see my house from here!

Activating Eye of Oculus by itself does not satisfy the requirement. **Place a building piece while controlling the Eye.**

I clear this during an actual base expansion. There is no need to construct something elaborate; the achievement cares that a building action happens through the Eye.

### Gotta Patch 'Em All

The key number is **24 plots in one cast**.

Repeated smaller casts do not match that requirement. Prepare at least 24 harvestable plots together, wait until they are ready, then use Uproot once across the full group.

### Master Huntsman

Two conditions have to meet on the same attack: the target must already be held by a **snare**, and the projectile that hits it must be an **enhanced arrow**.

A normal arrow into the snare misses half of the condition. An enhanced arrow fired before the target is trapped does the same.

### Doric's Fire Fried Chicken

The chicken must actually **die to the fireball**.

If another weapon or damage source finishes it after the fire hit, repeat the attempt and make the fireball the killing blow.

- **Black Knight's Fortress** — defeat the Black Knight Titan

No extra achievement condition is attached to the first four beyond winning the fight.

<h2 id="regional-quests">
  How many Secondary Quests do I need in each region?
</h2>

Five achievements require **three Secondary Quests in a specific region**:

- **Helping around the farm** — complete 3 in Brynmoor
- **Bark at the moon** — complete 3 in Ghornfell
- **Don't fear the Reaper** — complete 3 in Fellhollow
- **Mages & Knights & Demons, oh my!** — complete 3 in Dowdun Reach
- **Icthlarins Bigger Helper** — complete 3 in Umbral Sands

The number to remember is **three**, not every optional objective in the region.

That makes these good achievements to work on while travelling through the main route. When a Secondary Quest starts near a location I am already visiting and does not demand a large detour, I finish it then. Leaving all five regional sets until the end means reopening several areas purely for quest cleanup.

Once the third qualifying quest registers in a region, there is no achievement reason to finish additional Secondary Quests there unless you want their normal rewards.

<h2 id="agility-achievements">
  How do the Agility medal achievements work?
</h2>

Agility contributes three different requirements:

- **Medal Winner** — earn your first Gold medal
- **Medal Collector** — earn Gold on every Agility course
- **Amazingly Acrobatic** — reach Level 99 Agility

The first two are course-performance goals. Level 99 is a separate skill requirement, so earning every Gold does not replace the experience grind.

A completed Gold run I used as a reference finished in **00:56.07**, but that is a result from that specific course, not a universal Gold threshold for every course.

The time I lost during repeated attempts usually came from missed pickups, clipping a corner, or turning one bad landing into several seconds of recovery. Once the basic route is memorized, cleaning up those mistakes is more consistent than forcing a risky shortcut into every section.

For **Medal Collector**, treat each course separately. A Gold already earned on one course does not reduce the requirement on another.

<h2 id="level-99-achievements">
  Which skills have a Level 99 achievement?
</h2>

Twelve skills need to reach **Level 99**:

- **The School of Jewels** — Artisan
- **Melee Master** — Attack
- **Absolute Planker** — Construction
- **Dionysius' Disciple** — Magic
- **Everything Is Oresome** — Mining
- **Granny's Favourite Farmer** — Farming
- **Three Misthalin Stars** — Cooking
- **Thrill of the Hunt** — Ranged
- **Altar Native** — Runecrafting
- **Amazingly Acrobatic** — Agility
- **Everything Is Treemendous** — Woodcutting
- **Maximum Effishiency** — Fishing

These are the requirements that turn a finished boss run into a long completion project.

I do not push a single skill to 99 at the start unless I specifically want that skill. Mining, Woodcutting, Cooking, Runecrafting, combat, Construction, Farming, and Fishing all receive experience from activities already needed during progression. Let those levels accumulate while unlocking better tools, spells, gear, and gathering methods.

The difference becomes substantial later. Spending hours repeating an early gathering action only to unlock a faster spell or stronger tool afterward is time that cannot be recovered.

<h2 id="completion-order">
  What is the fastest order for cleaning up the achievements?
</h2>

After the main boss route is finished, I separate the remaining work by what can be combined.

First, check the five **Secondary Quest** achievements. Finishing those regional sets before leaving each area is ideal, but any missing third quest is usually quicker to solve than starting a Level 99 grind.

Next, clear the one-off mechanic requirements: Axtral Projection, Magical Mending, Rocksplosion, the three simultaneous buffs, Eye of Oculus building, a confusion kill, Sharp and Shiny, Uproot, Master Huntsman, Fishnado, and Doric's Fire Fried Chicken. These are short once the correct ability and setup are available.

Agility comes after that. Finish any courses still missing Gold, then look at the actual Agility level rather than assuming the medals finished the skill achievement.

The last stage is the twelve Level 99 requirements. At that point the checklist makes it easy to see which skills have already benefited heavily from normal play and which still need a deliberate grind.

A finished run therefore tends to narrow naturally:

**normal actions and bosses → regional Secondary Quests → specific spell and ability setups → remaining Gold medals → Level 99 skills**

That order avoids spending hours on a skill grind while easier achievements are still waiting on one cast, one boss kill, or one nearby quest.
