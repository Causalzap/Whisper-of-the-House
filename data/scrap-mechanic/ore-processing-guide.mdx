<h2 id="universal-connection-check">
  The Input and Output Check I Run Before Troubleshooting Anything Else
</h2>

The most common fault in my Mining Hub machines was not missing power.

It was an input, output, Vacuum Tube, or Pump facing the wrong direction.

I use the same test before troubleshooting the Crushbot, Prospector, or complete
automation line:

1. disconnect the large storage network;
2. place one test item into the machine manually;
3. watch which side accepts it;
4. wait for the full cycle;
5. identify the exact point where the result appears;
6. reconnect one input chest and one output chest;
7. test one more item before filling either chest.

This establishes the machine direction once.

Later sections only describe problems unique to that machine rather than
repeating the complete input-and-output explanation.

A pipe that visually touches a port is not proof that the item is moving in the
correct direction.

<h2 id="first-crushbot">
  How I Use the Crushbot for the First Time
</h2>

For the first batch, I process one Ore chunk manually.

I place the Crushbot on a stable surface, pick up the mined Ore, and interact
with the machine while carrying it.

Dropping the chunk beside the Crushbot does nothing.

I collect the first result by hand before connecting storage.

That lets me read the item label and decide whether it belongs in direct
processing storage or the Residual Ore chest.

<h2 id="crushbot-not-working">
  Why My Crushbot Is Not Taking the Ore
</h2>

### The Ore is only sitting beside the machine

I pick the chunk up and insert it through the interaction point.

The Crushbot does not automatically collect loose Ore from the floor unless I
have built a functioning collection system.

### I am feeding Residual Ore back into the Crushbot

The Crushbot accepts the physical Ore produced by the Plasma Drill.

Residual Ore is already a later processing result. I move it to the Prospector
rather than cycling it through the Crushbot again.

### Manual input works, but the input chest does not

That means the Crushbot itself is working.

I inspect:

- Vacuum Tube direction;
- Pump direction;
- whether the chest is connected to the actual input side;
- whether the chest contains the same item used in the successful manual test;
- whether a switch or button is activating the expected connection.

I return to the universal one-item test before replacing the Crushbot.

The most important split is:

- **direct processed material:** keep it labeled and send it to the matching
  Mining Hub intake when required;
- **Residual Ore:** wash it in the Prospector before deciding where it belongs.

<h2 id="prospector-bot">
  How the Prospector Bot Works
</h2>

The Prospector washes Residual Ore and reveals the material hidden inside it.

It requires water.

The machine can be supplied manually or connected to a Water Container, and its
rear ports provide separate material input and output connections.

My first test is deliberately small:

1. fill or connect the Water Container;
2. verify the input and output symbols;
3. insert one Residual Ore item;
4. wait for one complete wash cycle;
5. confirm that water is consumed;
6. confirm that a revealed material reaches the output.

A mixed result is normal.

The Prospector does not guarantee one specific material from every Residual Ore
item.

<h2 id="prospector-materials">
  Materials I Confirmed From the Prospector
</h2>

The revealed output in my run included:

- **Metal Block 1**;
- **Chemical**;
- **Quartz**;
- **Sand**;
- **Crimsonite**.

I handle them differently.

### Metal Block 1

I send Metal into the active machine, vehicle, and structural crafting queues
rather than depositing every piece immediately.

Mining upgrades and processing infrastructure can consume large amounts of
Metal, so selling or converting all of it can create another surface-resource
grind.

### Chemical

I reserve Chemical for systems that already consume it, especially Battery
crafting and Sticky Wheel operation.

If the mining vehicle uses Sticky Wheels, Chemical output can directly reduce
the cost of the next underground trip.

### Quartz

I store Quartz separately.

It appears in machine and progression recipes, so I avoid mixing it into a
general output chest where it is difficult to count.

### Sand

I keep Sand in its own labeled stack until a current recipe or Mining Hub intake
requires it.

The important decision is not to treat every revealed material as immediate
Vault value.

### Crimsonite

Crimsonite appeared as a named Prospector result in my run.

I preserve it until its current recipe, station, or progression use is clear
rather than consuming a rarer output experimentally.

<h2 id="prospector-not-working">
  Why My Prospector Bot Is Not Working
</h2>

I run the universal input-and-output test first.

After direction is confirmed, the Prospector has four additional failure points.

### The Water Container is connected but empty

A connected empty container still leaves the Prospector unable to wash Residual
Ore.

I check the actual water level instead of treating the visible pipe as proof of
supply.

### The Residual Ore is beside the intake rather than inside it

Loose items near a port are not the same as items moving through the input.

For manual testing, I insert one item directly. For automation, I use an input
buffer chest.

### The control will not connect

In my automated build, one control connection refused to register while the
machines merely looked close together.

Placing the controls and processing machines on a small continuous Concrete
foundation removed the ambiguity.

I treat this as a diagnostic from my build, not a rule that every Mining Hub
machine requires a giant shared floor.

### The Prospector is working, but no result appears

I wait for a complete cycle and inspect the output chest.

A mixed Prospector result needs several free storage slots. A chest with many
nearly full stacks can run out of usable space earlier than expected.

### The input chest remains full

If the Prospector is cycling continuously, this is a throughput problem.

If the Prospector is idle, it is still a connection, water, or input problem.
Adding another machine will not fix it.

<h2 id="confirmed-numbers">
  Ore Processing Numbers I Could Actually Confirm
</h2>

I keep verified values separate from estimates.

The confirmed numbers from this route are:

- one deposited valuable was worth **2,500 Credits**;
- that deposit completed the first **10,000 Quota**;
- reaching 10,000 unlocked the next Access Card and mine-floor progression;
- a later active Quota increased to **100,000**;
- one Prospector eventually became too slow;
- I expanded from **1 Prospector to 3 Prospectors**.

The following values were not reliably captured:

- a fixed amount of Residual Ore required for one revealed material;
- a guaranteed Prospector output ratio;
- exact water consumed per Residual Ore cycle;
- one universal Credit value for every Ingot;
- one universal material amount required to form every Ore Ball.

I therefore do not turn one observed deposit into a rule such as:

> Every Ingot is worth 2,500 Credits.

The screenshot proves one accepted valuable and one Quota change, not a global
price table.

The Mining Hub has fixed intake stations for accepted processed materials.

I deliver the correct batch to its matching station using a container, Vacuum
Pump, or controlled manual setup.

When the required batch is accepted, the Hub forms a large Ore Ball on the
upper platform.

If no Ball appears, I check:

- the item label matches the intake;
- the material left the input chest;
- the Pump faces the correct direction;

- I am watching the correct Ball platform.

I test one material type at a time.

Feeding several unrelated items into one intake makes it much harder to
identify what the station is still missing.

<h2 id="caster-route">
  How I Move the Ore Ball to the Caster
</h2>

The Mining Hub uses colored transport lines and sloped transfer points.

I navigate one transfer at a time rather than trying to memorize the complete
Hub as one route.

In my first run:

- the **turquoise line** helped me reach the upper Ball platform;
- the **red line** led toward the Caster side;
- the other colored lines connected intermediate ramps and transfer points.

My movement sequence is:

1. identify the Ball's current platform;
2. find the next marked ramp;
3. push or strike the Ball toward that ramp;
4. follow it to the next stop;
5. repeat until it enters the Caster.

When the Ball rests against the lip of a ramp, I use one controlled Hammer hit.

Repeated full-strength hits can knock it away from the intended transport line
and create more recovery work.

  How the Caster Produces Ingots

Once the Ball enters the Caster, the Hub handles the final conversion.

I wait beside the output area rather than standing below the moving Ball route.

The Ingots are heavy physical objects.

A large load compressed my suspension and made the vehicle much less stable
than an ordinary inventory run.

My hauling setup uses:

- a low loading platform;
- strengthened suspension when the vehicle begins sitting too low;
- weight distributed across the complete bed;
- no unnecessary mining equipment on the hauling trip;
- enough Fuel or Batteries for the full route;
- slower turns and braking.

I prefer a simple low trailer over rebuilding the entire mining vehicle around
Ingot transport.

The mining rig needs drill clearance and traction. The hauling platform needs a
low center of gravity and predictable weight distribution.

Those are different jobs.

The current bottleneck decides the next step.

This replaces several nearly identical “when I do X” checklists.

The key distinction is:

- **broken line:** fix direction, water, control, or storage;
- **slow line:** add capacity;
- **empty line:** return to mining;

<h2 id="automation-layout">
  How I Build the Automated Processing Layout
</h2>

I build the first automatic line on a compact Concrete frame near water and the
Mining Hub elevator.

The frame keeps the controls easy to identify and leaves enough space behind
each machine for the ports.

My build order is:

1. place the Water Container;
2. place the first Prospector with access to both rear ports;
3. add an input buffer chest;
4. add an output chest with several free slots;
5. connect the water line;
6. connect the Residual Ore input;
7. connect the revealed-material output;
8. add the switch, button, or Pump control;
9. test one Residual Ore item;
10. expand only after that item reaches the output chest.

<h2 id="automation-not-working">
  Why My Automated Ore Processing Line Is Not Working
</h2>

### The Water Container is connected, but nothing is washing

Check the actual water level.

A correct pipe connected to an empty container still provides no water.

Then confirm that the line reaches the Prospector's water connection rather
than a material port.

### The switch or button will not connect

Place the control on the same continuous structure as the parts it needs to
control.

A small Concrete bridge fixed this problem in my build.

I test this before replacing every Vacuum Tube.

### The input chest is full, but nothing enters the Prospector

Run the universal direction check again.

Then confirm:

- the chest contains Residual Ore;
- the Pump points away from the chest and toward the Prospector;
- the input port is not being used as the output;
- the Prospector has water;
- the machine is not blocked by its current cycle.

### The item enters, but no result reaches storage

Watch one complete wash cycle.

Then inspect:

- the first output tube;
- missing corners or T-pieces;
- the connection to the output chest;
- available chest slots;
- whether the result dropped onto the floor.

Mixed Prospector outputs can fill several partial stacks, so total empty space
is less useful than having several completely free slots.

### The line works, but the backlog grows

That is not a connection failure.

It means extraction is delivering Residual Ore faster than the current
Prospector capacity can wash it.

Only after water and output storage remain stable do I add another Prospector.

<h2 id="when-to-return">
  When I Leave the Processing Area and Return to the Mine
</h2>

I first clear any progression value already waiting:

- move an available Ore Ball;
- collect finished Ingots;
- deposit accepted valuables;
- check whether the Vault crosses its target;
- confirm whether a new Access Card or floor unlock appears.

Only then do I decide whether another mining trip is needed.

I return underground when:

- no finished Ingot is waiting;
- no Ore Ball is ready;
- the processing line is stable;
- water and output storage have capacity;
- the current Vault balance still needs more value.

This prevents unnecessary vehicle upgrades.

After depositing the existing batch, I may discover that the next step requires
only one short mining run rather than another Plasma Drill, another Prospector,
or a completely new hauler.

<h2 id="faq">
  Ore Processing Edge Cases
</h2>

### Does each Prospector need its own Water Container?

No. Multiple Prospectors can draw from the same connected water network.

They still need enough shared water for every active wash cycle, and each
machine needs a clear material input and output path. If the first Prospector
works but the additional Bots remain idle, I check their individual material
connections before adding another Water Container.

### Should I keep rare Prospector materials or send everything to the Vault?

I keep at least one labeled stack of a newly revealed material until I know its
current crafting or progression use.

Metal, Chemical, Quartz, Sand, and Crimsonite do not all have the same immediate
value to my build. Depositing or consuming every new material can leave me
short when the next machine, vehicle upgrade, or Station asks for that exact
resource.
