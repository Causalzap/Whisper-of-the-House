Mining Site 01 is where Scrap Mechanic 1.0 changes from ordinary surface
exploration into underground vehicle engineering.

My first Plasma Drill could break through the mine wall, but the vehicle itself
was not ready for a real mining trip.

The fixed drill cut beneath its own front wheels, ore ejected under the chassis,
and the vehicle became trapped in the hole it created. The real progression was
not simply adding more drills—it was fixing aiming, collection, traction, and
power problems in the right order.

<h2 id="what-to-bring-underground">
  What I Bring Into the Mine
</h2>

A mining vehicle alone is not enough.

My practical first-trip loadout is:

- at least one working Plasma Drill;
- a Battery Container or secure Battery supply;
- enough Batteries for drilling and the return trip;
- a storage chest or Ore Collector connection;
- a Spud Gun or Shotgun;
- more ammunition than a peaceful resource trip would require;
- healing food in an immediately accessible slot;
- a Lift;
- spare blocks for ramps and recovery;
- a vehicle Bed or nearby respawn point;
- a beacon for the elevator;
- Chemicals when Sticky Wheels are installed.

On a later four-drill Electric Engine run, I entered with about **25 Batteries**
and still treated power as a real limit.

One fixed drill uses far less power than four drills plus an Electric Engine, so
I scale the Battery supply with the vehicle rather than using one permanent
number for every build.

I keep one emergency food and ammunition stack outside the vehicle's main
storage system.

If the mining chest fills, disconnects, or becomes trapped under the vehicle, I
still need enough supplies to fight and reach the elevator.

<h2 id="underground-combat">
  Underground Combat and Minerbot Preparation
</h2>

The mine is not a combat-free resource zone.

My first major underground access encounter gave me Potatoes shortly before an
ambush. That encounter included Totebots and Tapebots.

The Shotgun worked well when several enemies entered the same narrow approach.

My combat procedure is:

1. switch off the Plasma Drill;
2. move the vehicle away from the direct enemy approach;
3. leave enough space to retreat behind the chassis;
4. fight from a cleared tunnel rather than an unexplored opening;
5. remove ranged enemies before returning to the drill controls;
6. inspect the suspension, storage, and drill mounts before mining again.

The vehicle is useful as cover, but I avoid fighting directly beside exposed
Batteries or the ore chest.

A damaged storage connection can turn a successful fight into a long search for
dropped resources.

I also do not treat the Plasma Drill as the vehicle's main weapon.

In my tests, the drill did very little damage to Bots compared with a proper
Spud weapon. Its main jobs are:

- removing terrain;
- opening routes;
- extracting Terramass.

Deeper underground progression also introduces Minerbots, and the achievement
list includes:

`Minerbot Junkyard — Destroy 100 Minerbots`

That is another reason to keep a ranged weapon available even when the current
floor appears quiet.

A drill mounted low on the front of the vehicle cannot replace a weapon against
enemies that do not remain directly in front of the mining beam.

I progress the Minerbot counter during normal underground exploration rather
than forcing a separate 100-kill grind before the deeper floors are available.

<h2 id="first-plasma-drill">
  How the Plasma Drill Extracts Terramass
</h2>

The first mining tutorial explains the extraction loop:

1. aim the Plasma Drill at valuable Terramass;
2. keep the beam on valid material;
3. let the drill's collection meter fill;
4. wait for the collected ore to eject;
5. take the ore by hand or collect it automatically;
6. bring the ore to a Crushbot for processing.

<h2 id="valid-terramass">
  How I Tell Whether the Drill Is Hitting Valid Ore
</h2>

I watch the **collection meter on the Plasma Drill**.

When the beam is extracting valid Terramass:

- the meter begins filling;
- continued contact advances it;
- the material contributes toward an ore ejection;
- the vehicle is doing more than reshaping the tunnel.

When the meter remains still:

- the beam may be hitting ordinary terrain;
- the target may be outside the useful drill area;
- the deposit may already be exhausted;
- another drill may be taking the same small resource point;
- the vehicle may have shifted away from the colored material.

I stop holding the trigger when the meter does not move.

Continuing to cut ordinary rock only creates a larger hole, wastes Batteries,
and increases the chance that the vehicle falls into its own excavation.

My fastest diagnostic is:

1. stop the vehicle;
2. center one drill on a clearly colored deposit;
3. activate only that drill;
4. watch the meter for several seconds;
5. reposition when no progress appears.

This is more reliable than judging the target only by particle effects.

<h2 id="ore-ejection">
  Why the Drill Is Full but No Ore Is in My Chest
</h2>

A full Plasma Drill ejects a physical piece of ore.

  <Image
    src="/images/scrap-mechanic/scrap-mechanic-plasma-drill-ore-ejection.webp"
    alt="Scrap Mechanic Plasma Drill ejecting collected ore beside the mining vehicle"
    width={550}
    height={332}
    className="mx-auto h-auto w-full max-w-2xl rounded-xl"
  />
  <figcaption className="mx-auto mt-3 max-w-2xl text-sm text-slate-400">
    Filling the drill meter creates an ore object; the ore still has to reach
    my inventory or collection system.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

The ejected piece can land:

- in front of the vehicle;
- beside the drill;
- under the chassis;
- against a wheel or suspension arm;
- on the newly cut slope;
- outside the Ore Collector's pickup area.

This creates a common false conclusion:

> The drill filled, but it did not produce anything.

The ore may already exist underneath the vehicle.

My recovery steps are:

1. switch off the drill;
2. reverse onto stable terrain;
3. inspect beneath the front axle;
4. check both sides of the drill mount;
5. pick up the ore manually when the collector missed it;
6. move the collector closer to the normal ejection path;
7. test one full meter before resuming a long mining pass.

I avoid continuing while the first ejected piece is still trapped beneath the
vehicle.

Additional ore and broken terrain make the obstruction harder to inspect.

If I need to remove or reposition a loaded Plasma Drill, I empty it before
detaching the assembly. The interaction prompt in my run used `U`, but that
input may differ with custom controls.

<h2 id="fixed-drill-problems">
  Why a Fixed Plasma Drill Digs the Vehicle Into the Ground
</h2>

My first Plasma Drill was fixed to the front of the vehicle.

It could open the first tunnel, but its aiming angle depended almost entirely on
the chassis.

When the front wheels moved onto soft or newly removed terrain, the drill tilted
downward. It then removed the ground beneath the same wheels that were carrying
it.

The result was a self-reinforcing loop:

<div className="my-6 overflow-hidden rounded-2xl border border-slate-200 bg-slate-50 px-5 py-4 dark:border-slate-700 dark:bg-slate-900">
  <p className="m-0 font-mono text-sm leading-7 text-slate-700 dark:text-slate-200">
    Drill angles downward
  </p>
  <p className="m-0 font-mono text-sm leading-7 text-slate-700 dark:text-slate-200">
    → ground disappears
  </p>
  <p className="m-0 font-mono text-sm leading-7 text-slate-700 dark:text-slate-200">
    → front wheels fall
  </p>
  <p className="m-0 font-mono text-sm leading-7 text-slate-700 dark:text-slate-200">
    → drill points farther downward
  </p>
  <p className="m-0 font-mono text-sm leading-7 text-slate-700 dark:text-slate-200">
    → vehicle digs a deeper hole
  </p>
</div>

I recover from that situation in this order:

1. release the drill control immediately;
2. reverse before the front axle drops farther;
3. straighten the wheels;
4. place the vehicle on the Lift when reversing fails;
5. use temporary blocks to create a shallow ramp;
6. approach the deposit from a higher and flatter angle;
7. remove the recovery blocks only after the chassis is stable.

The worst response is to keep drilling forward and hope the vehicle eventually
levels itself.

The drill removes the support faster than the wheels can climb out.

For early tunnels, I work from the upper edge of a deposit and shave material
away gradually.

Driving directly into the bottom of a wall encourages the beam to cut downward.

I bought **Turret Seat Level 1 for 2,000 Credits** from the Axobot store.

<h2 id="turret-clearance">
  Test the Turret Before Driving Back Underground
</h2>

My first Turret Seat mount sat too low.

At some angles, the Plasma Drill or its supporting frame collided with the
front suspension.

I test three positions before loading the elevator.

### Maximum upward aim

The drill must clear:

- the Turret Seat frame;
- the top of the vehicle;
- storage connections;
- pipes behind the seat.

### Straight forward

The center position must not rest against:

- the front suspension;
- the chassis edge;
- the Battery Container;
- a decorative guard.

### Maximum downward aim

The drill should reach a lower deposit without striking:

- the front wheels;
- suspension springs;
- the vehicle floor;
- the Ore Collector.

When one angle collides, I raise the complete turret assembly by at least one
block or move the suspension below the turret base.

Removing the suspension entirely may fix one collision while making the vehicle
far less stable underground.

<h2 id="sticky-wheels-electric-engine">
  Sticky Wheels and the Electric Engine
</h2>

Sticky Wheels became useful when the mine stopped behaving like a flat tunnel.

They helped the vehicle hold position on:

- steep slopes;
- curved walls;
- uneven cut surfaces;
- routes where ordinary wheels kept sliding away from the deposit.

  <Image

  <figcaption className="mx-auto mt-3 max-w-2xl text-sm text-slate-400">
    Sticky Wheels let the mining rig hold angles that ordinary wheels could not
    maintain.
  </figcaption>

They also introduced a new resource dependency: **Chemicals**.

When sticky mode is off, the wheels can feel much more slippery than normal
wheels on ordinary ground.

The most practical arrangement for my vehicle was:

- normal wheels for predictable driving;
- Sticky Wheels where extra grip mattered;
- a dedicated switch for sticky mode;
- enough Chemical storage for the planned mining trip.

I avoid activating Sticky Wheels continuously on flat terrain.

They are a positioning tool, not a replacement for normal handling.

The Electric Engine became attractive for a different reason.

The mining vehicle already needed Batteries for its Plasma Drills. Carrying a
separate large Fuel supply for the engine created another container, another
resource check, and another reason to abandon a run early.

With an Electric Engine, I can plan around one shared power resource.

The downside is that driving and drilling now consume the same reserve.

I separate my Batteries into:

- an active mining supply;
- a protected return supply.

Once the active supply is exhausted, the trip is over even when the storage
chest still has room.

<h2 id="multiple-plasma-drills">
  Why Four Plasma Drills Did Not Give Four Times the Ore
</h2>

Four Plasma Drills looked much faster when clearing a wide section of terrain.

They did not produce four times the useful output when all four beams were
fighting over one small resource point.

<figure className="my-8">
  <Image
    src="/images/scrap-mechanic/scrap-mechanic-four-plasma-drills-overlap.webp"
    alt="Scrap Mechanic four Plasma Drills overlapping on the same underground mining area"
    width={522}
    height={378}
    className="mx-auto h-auto w-full max-w-2xl rounded-xl"
  />
  <figcaption className="mx-auto mt-3 max-w-2xl text-sm text-slate-400">
    Four drills clear terrain quickly, but overlapping all four on one small
    deposit does not create four independent resources.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

The first test produced a warning sign:

- terrain disappeared quickly;
- all four beams were active;
- the vehicle had not produced a single ore chunk;
- the drills were competing for the same limited material.

I use the four-drill assembly in two configurations.

### Spread mode

I move the drills apart when:

- the mining face is wide;
- several colored areas are visible;
- I want to clear a broad tunnel;
- each drill can contact a different part of the deposit.

### Converged mode

I bring the drills together when:

- the tunnel is narrow;
- one large valid deposit fills the center;
- the outer drills would otherwise miss;
- the vehicle needs a compact profile.

More active beams do not automatically mean more collected ore.

I judge the arrangement by:

- collection-meter movement;
- actual ore ejections;
- Battery use;
- whether each drill is contacting useful material.

Upgrade cost also matters.

The first Plasma Drill upgrade used **5 Multicomponent Kits**, while the next
upgrade step asked for **20**.

For my progression, upgrading several drills once was more practical than
immediately pushing one drill into the much more expensive second upgrade.

<h2 id="when-to-return">
  My Hard Return Triggers
</h2>

I return to the Mining Hub before the vehicle becomes stranded.

The trip ends when any of these conditions appears:

- the protected Battery reserve is being used;
- the ore chest is full;
- ejected ore is accumulating beneath the chassis;
- the turret mount or front suspension is damaged;
- the vehicle can no longer reverse out of its own excavation;
- the Sticky Wheels are nearly out of Chemicals;
- I have lost the elevator direction without placing a beacon;
- ammunition is too low for another underground encounter;
- the current tunnel produces no collection-meter progress;
- the next useful upgrade requires processing the ore already collected.

A half-full chest returned safely is worth more than a full chest attached to a
vehicle with no Batteries.

Before leaving a branch, I point the vehicle toward the elevator beacon and
confirm that it can climb the return route without drilling.

The trip back should not depend on cutting a new tunnel while the power supply
is already low.

<h2 id="troubleshooting">
  Plasma Drill and Mining Vehicle Troubleshooting
</h2>

### The Plasma Drill is firing, but its meter is not moving

The beam is probably hitting ordinary terrain, an exhausted deposit, or the
wrong part of the mining face.

Stop the vehicle, activate one drill, and test a clearly colored Terramass area.

Visual destruction alone does not prove that valuable ore is being collected.

### The drill filled, but I cannot find the ore

Reverse the vehicle and inspect underneath the front axle.

The ejected ore may be trapped beneath the chassis, against a suspension arm,
or outside the Ore Collector's pickup range.

### My Ore Collector is connected, but ore still falls on the ground

Test the exact ejection path.

The collector may be connected correctly but mounted too far from the place
where the Plasma Drill ejects its ore.

Move it closer and fill one drill meter before starting another long run.

### My vehicle keeps digging into the floor

The fixed drill is angled below the front wheel contact point.

Stop drilling, reverse, use the Lift or temporary blocks to recover the chassis,
and approach the deposit from a higher angle.

A Turret Seat is the permanent solution when this happens repeatedly.

### The Turret Seat will not aim downward

The drill frame is probably colliding with:

- the front suspension;
- the vehicle floor;
- a Battery Container;
- the Ore Collector;
- a nearby pipe.

Raise the entire turret assembly and repeat the upward, level, and downward
clearance tests.

### Sticky Wheels are sliding everywhere

Check whether sticky mode is active.

Sticky Wheels handle poorly as ordinary wheels when their grip is not engaged.
Use them only for slopes and surfaces where the extra traction is needed.

### Sticky Wheels stopped working underground

Check the Chemical supply and its connection.

The vehicle can still roll after Chemicals run out, which makes the problem look
like a traction or switch failure rather than an empty resource container.

### My four drills are active, but ore is appearing slowly

The beams may be overlapping on the same small deposit.

Spread the drills across separate parts of the mining face and compare their
collection meters.

### I cannot find the elevator again

Follow the beacon placed at the elevator before mining.

Without a beacon, stop drilling and move upward through existing tunnels rather
than creating another uncontrolled branch.

### Deeper Mining Site floors are still locked

Continue increasing the required Vault or Quota progress.

Mining access, ore processing, and Vault progression are connected systems.
Removing deposited value can delay the next floor unlock.

### Bots destroyed part of my mining vehicle

Clear the area before rebuilding.

Repair these components first:

1. driver or Turret Seat;
2. wheels and suspension;
3. engine and power supply;
4. drill mount;
5. storage and collector;
6. optional armor or decoration.

A working return vehicle matters more than restoring the complete original
shape underground.

<h2 id="faq">
  Scrap Mechanic Mining FAQ
</h2>

### Where is Mining Site 01?

Mining Site 01 is revealed on the planetary survey map during the story
Warehouse route. After the objective updates, follow the marked route to the
Mining Hub entrance and use the Mining Access Card.

### Is Excavation Island the same as Mining Site 01?

Some community searches use Excavation Island or mining island for the broader
area. The in-game story map in my route labels the destination **Mining Site
01**.

### Do I need a special vehicle for the first mine?

One normal vehicle with a fixed Plasma Drill can enter and collect early ore,
but independent drill aiming, storage, and stable traction make later trips
much easier.

### How do I know whether I am mining Terramass?

Watch the Plasma Drill collection meter. Valid Terramass advances the meter.
Ordinary terrain can disappear without producing meter progress or valuable
ore.

### Why is my Plasma Drill not dropping ore?

A full drill ejects a physical ore object. Check beneath the vehicle and around
the drill mount before assuming nothing was produced.

### Should I buy the Turret Seat immediately?

A fixed drill is enough for the first test. The Turret Seat becomes the priority
when poor aiming and downward digging repeatedly slow the run.

### Why does my Turret Seat hit the suspension?

The turret is mounted too low or too close to the front axle. Raise the turret
assembly and test maximum upward, straight, and maximum downward aim before
returning underground.

### Are Sticky Wheels required for mining?

They are not required for the first flat tunnel. They become valuable on steep,
curved, and uneven surfaces where normal wheels cannot hold the vehicle in
position.

### Should I use a Fuel Engine or Electric Engine?

A Fuel Engine is fine for the first rig. An Electric Engine simplifies
logistics once the vehicle already carries a large Battery supply for several
Plasma Drills.

### Are four Plasma Drills four times faster?

Not automatically. Four drills clear a wide face quickly, but overlapping all
four on one small deposit makes them compete for the same material.

### Do I need weapons in the mine?

Yes. Underground routes include combat encounters, and the Plasma Drill is not
a reliable replacement for a Spud weapon. Bring ammunition and healing food
with the mining supplies.

### What should I do with mined ore?

Collect the ejected ore, return it to the Mining Hub, process it through the
Crushbot and later systems, and use the resulting value or Ingots for upgrades
and deeper progression.
