<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
The **Baikonur Campaign** ends when two Soyuz spacecraft successfully connect in Earth orbit. To reach that objective, complete the first R7 test flight, place Sputnik into orbit, return the Vostok cosmonaut safely to Earth, and finish the first crewed Soyuz mission. For the final rendezvous, launch one Soyuz, target its orbit with the second, and complete the spacecraft connection. The campaign does not finish when the second Soyuz merely reaches space.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

Baikonur begins with a functioning Soviet city and a prepared launch area, but the facilities needed to manufacture and assemble rockets are still incomplete. The opening missions focus on getting that industrial system working.

Later objectives depend on what happens after liftoff. Sputnik must remain in orbit, Vostok and Soyuz require safe returns, and the last mission demands an orbital connection between two spacecraft. Each new mission introduces a different condition that must be satisfied before the campaign advances.

## What Are All the Baikonur Campaign Missions?

The campaign progresses through six main stages.

You do not need to establish a completely new republic before beginning this sequence. The campaign provides a functioning starting area, industrial infrastructure, research facilities, and a prepared launch location.

However, you must still construct several space-industry facilities and complete the required research.

You also need to distinguish between a **successful launch** and a **completed mission**. The Test Rocket, Sputnik, Vostok, and Soyuz objectives have different conditions.

## How Do You Complete Preparation for Space?

The opening stage establishes the buildings required for your first rocket.

Baikonur already has a population, supporting infrastructure, a Space University, and a designated launch area.

Start by following the introductory objective rather than expanding the city in every direction. The space facilities are the immediate priority.

### Step 1: Build the Rocket Parts Manufacturing Plant

The first construction objective asks you to build a **Rocket Parts Manufacturing Plant**.

Check the proposed location and make sure the building has access to workers, materials, and suitable transportation.

Rocket components are large and cannot be moved through ordinary factory connections. Leave enough room for the vehicle routes needed later.

Once construction is complete, open the building and check the available rocket component blueprints.

### Step 2: Build Mission Control

The campaign then requires a **Mission Control Center**.

This building handles launches and spacecraft monitoring.

Place it where its workers can reach it reliably. The campaign recommends an area accessible to qualified personnel, so checking the existing passenger network and nearby university district is useful.

The objective should advance when the required facility is constructed.

Do not mistake Mission Control for the Rocket Assembly Hall. The two buildings serve different purposes.

### Step 3: Manufacture the first rocket components

Return to the Rocket Parts Manufacturing Plant.

The early R7 program uses separate booster and core components. Follow the current production objective and select the specified blueprints.

For the first test rocket, you will ultimately need:

- One **R7 test - core RD108**.
- Four **R7 - booster RD107** components.
- One **R7 testing rocket fairing**.

Check the Assembly Hall's selected rocket requirements before producing the fairing or repeating a part.

The manufacturing objective may track a specific production action or completion condition. If you already have finished components but the mission has not advanced, inspect the current objective rather than automatically manufacturing another complete batch.

### Step 4: Research Rocket Engine Testing

The next important research is **Rocket Engine Testing**.

Open the Space University and begin the required research.

Once it finishes, construct a compatible Rocket Engine Testing Facility.

Testing compatible components is important because your first rockets can have substantial failure risks.

The campaign introduces this step before the first complete launch. Complete the testing objective and check that the appropriate rocket components have reached the facility.

### Step 5: Research and build Rocket Assembly

Continue with the **Rocket Assembly** research required by the campaign.

After the research completes, construct the Rocket Assembly Hall and connect it to the rest of your space industry.

This building combines individual rocket components into a finished vehicle.

You will also need a connection between the Assembly Hall and the Launch Site for transporting complete rockets.

Once the required manufacturing, testing, assembly, and Mission Control facilities are available, the campaign progresses to its first experimental launch.

If you cannot get the correct parts into the Assembly Hall, the [Rocket Assembly and Transport guide](/workers-and-resources-space-program/rocket-assembly) explains the manufacturing queues, compatible vehicles, testing facilities, and rail connections.

## How Do You Complete the First Test Rocket Mission?

**The first Test Rocket mission requires a complete experimental rocket to fly successfully. It does not carry a Sputnik satellite.**

Start by selecting the **R7 Testing Rocket** blueprint in the Rocket Assembly Hall.

Confirm that all the required components have arrived.

### Step 1: Assemble the R7 Testing Rocket

Open the Rocket Assembly Hall and select the testing rocket.

If assembly cannot begin, inspect its missing-component list.

Even when all the components have been manufactured, they may still be waiting at the factory or testing facility.

Deliver the correct parts, provide the required workers, and allow assembly to finish.

### Step 2: Transport the completed rocket

The campaign requires a railway connection between the Assembly Hall and Launch Site.

Use a suitable locomotive and configure a route that collects the complete rocket from the Assembly Hall and delivers it to the launch facility.

Make sure the train can reach both buildings.

Once the rocket arrives, it is positioned at the Launch Site, but it still needs fuel.

### Step 3: Fuel the rocket

The early test rocket requires:

- **Kerosene**
- **Liquid Oxygen**

Baikonur has prepared fuel infrastructure and tanker vehicles.

Open the relevant Distribution Office and confirm that the required sources and launch-site destinations are configured correctly.

The vehicles must load the appropriate fuels and unload them at the launch facility.

Wait until the rocket receives the required quantities.

**Do not leave a fueled rocket waiting unnecessarily.** Liquid Oxygen cannot remain available indefinitely, so finish your other preparations before the final deliveries whenever possible.

### Step 4: Launch from Mission Control

When the rocket is ready:

1. Open the Mission Control Center.
2. Select the Launch Site containing the test rocket.
3. Review the available mission and orbit settings.
4. Check the displayed Expected Failure Rate.
5. Authorize the launch.

Watch the flight and wait for the mission result.

The rocket can fail even when every preparation step is complete.

### What if the first Test Rocket fails?

A failed launch does not prove that your manufacturing or fuel delivery setup was incorrect.

Check the launch result, the remaining condition of the Launch Site, and the rocket's displayed failure risk.

Repair damaged facilities and prepare another test rocket if necessary.

Once the game confirms that the experimental rocket has passed its flight test, the campaign advances to the Sputnik program.

## How Do You Complete the Sputnik Mission?

After the first successful Test Rocket, the campaign introduces the goal of placing an artificial satellite into Earth orbit.

**Sputnik is not simply another empty test flight.** You now need the correct satellite payload and launch vehicle.

### Step 1: Complete the Sputnik research

Open the Space University and research the Sputnik technology required by the mission.

Wait for the research objective to complete before attempting to assemble the new vehicle.

### Step 2: Prepare the Sputnik rocket components

The Sputnik vehicle shares parts of the earlier R7 design, but it is not identical to the R7 Testing Rocket.

Check the new blueprint carefully.

You need the rocket's required core, boosters, fairing, and the Sputnik satellite payload.

Do not reuse the test core or test fairing merely because they have similar names.

In particular, the Assembly Hall may still have compatible RD107 boosters while requiring different core and fairing components for the satellite mission.

Manufacture the missing parts, arrange testing where needed, and deliver everything to the Assembly Hall.

### Step 3: Assemble and transport Sputnik

Select the Sputnik rocket blueprint and confirm the correct payload is included.

Allow assembly to finish, then transport the complete rocket to the Launch Site.

Check that the destination is the standard launch facility intended for the R7 vehicle.

### Step 4: Refuel and authorize launch

Supply Kerosene and Liquid Oxygen.

Wait until the rocket is ready, then open Mission Control and authorize the satellite mission.

The rocket still has an Expected Failure Rate.

A high displayed risk does not guarantee failure, but it means you may need to rebuild the rocket if the launch goes wrong.

### How do you know Sputnik succeeded?

**Wait for confirmation that Sputnik has reached Earth orbit.**

Do not rely only on the rocket clearing the atmosphere.

A successful mission displays the Sputnik completion message, confirming that the satellite has reached Earth orbit. The campaign then advances to **First Human in Space**.

Mission Control also allows you to inspect the spacecraft or satellites currently in orbit.

The important distinction is that Sputnik must achieve its orbital objective, rather than merely completing a short experimental flight.

Once the game confirms Sputnik is in orbit, you unlock **First Human in Space**.

## How Do You Complete First Human in Space With Vostok?

The Vostok mission is the first major change in how you complete a spaceflight.

You must do two things:

1. Send the Vostok spacecraft and its cosmonaut into Earth orbit.
2. Bring the spacecraft and cosmonaut safely back to Earth.

**Reaching orbit only completes the first part.**

### Step 1: Prepare the Vostok spacecraft

Follow the new research objectives and prepare the Vostok rocket and spacecraft.

Check the selected blueprint instead of assuming it uses exactly the same components as Sputnik.

Manufacture the required parts, complete the necessary preparations, and assemble the rocket.

Transport it to the Launch Site and supply its required fuel.

A crewed rocket may require different fuel quantities from the earlier test vehicle, so follow the numbers displayed for the current rocket.

### Step 2: Launch Vostok into orbit

Open Mission Control and select the appropriate launch site.

Choose a compatible orbit and check the Expected Failure Rate.

Once fueling and preparation are complete, authorize launch.

Watch the flight until the game confirms that the first human has reached Earth orbit.

At this point, do not assume the whole mission is finished.

The next objective is to return the cosmonaut safely.

### Step 3: Initiate Vostok reentry

After Vostok reaches orbit, open the spacecraft's own information window.

Look for the command to **initiate reentry**.

The mission instructions tell you to perform this operation when the spacecraft is in a suitable position for its return.

Once issued, the spacecraft begins its return sequence.

**Do not expect the capsule to land instantly when you press Reentry.**

There can be a noticeable delay between the command and the final descent.

### Step 4: Monitor supplies and wait for safe return

Vostok carries limited supplies.

Check the spacecraft's remaining resources while it is in orbit, and avoid leaving the cosmonaut there unnecessarily.

In one completed Vostok mission, the spacecraft remained in orbit long enough for its displayed supplies to run down before the return finished. The campaign nevertheless confirmed that the cosmonaut survived.

That result is not a reason to deliberately exhaust supplies.

Initiate the return when the mission is ready, then monitor the spacecraft until the game confirms that it has safely returned to Earth.

### What completes the Vostok mission?

The stage finishes when the game acknowledges that the first human traveled into orbit and returned alive.

If Vostok is still orbiting Earth, the task is not finished.

If the return command has been issued but the spacecraft has not landed, allow the sequence to continue and monitor its status.

After safe return, the campaign introduces the next generation of crewed spacecraft: **Soyuz**.

## How Do You Complete the First Soyuz Mission?

The Soyuz stage prepares your space program for more complex orbital operations.

Unlike the final Rendezvous mission, this first Soyuz flight is a single-spacecraft mission.

Your goal is to launch a crewed Soyuz spacecraft, reach Earth orbit, and return the crew safely.

### Step 1: Research Soyuz technology

Return to the Space University and complete the newly required spacecraft research.

Follow the mission's current research objective before scheduling parts for the new rocket.

The Soyuz vehicle has its own spacecraft and fairing requirements, so use the correct blueprint.

### Step 2: Manufacture and assemble the Soyuz rocket

Open the Rocket Assembly Hall and inspect the Soyuz design.

Manufacture the required rocket parts and spacecraft, then deliver them to the assembly facility.

If you still have compatible boosters from earlier production, check whether the new blueprint accepts them before manufacturing replacements.

Complete assembly and transport the rocket to the Launch Site.

### Step 3: Launch and complete the orbital mission

Fuel the rocket with its required propellants.

Open Mission Control, select the launch site, and authorize the Soyuz flight when everything is ready.

The mission requires Soyuz to reach Earth orbit.

After orbital insertion, monitor the spacecraft and complete its return to Earth.

Do not treat the launch itself as the final result. The campaign confirms the stage only after the required crewed mission and safe return are achieved.

### What unlocks after Soyuz?

Once the first Soyuz mission succeeds, the final stage becomes available.

The campaign now asks you to demonstrate that **two spacecraft can meet and connect in Earth orbit**.

Before attempting that operation, complete the required **Orbital Rendezvous** research.

## How Do You Complete the Final Orbital Rendezvous Mission?

**The final Baikonur Campaign objective requires two Soyuz spacecraft to connect in Earth orbit.**

Launching both spacecraft is not enough.

The campaign must register the successful orbital connection before it displays its completion message.

### Step 1: Research Orbital Rendezvous

Open the Space University and complete the Rendezvous research required by the final mission.

The research unlocks the procedures needed to bring spacecraft together in orbit.

Wait for this stage to finish before attempting the final connection.

### Step 2: Prepare two complete Soyuz rockets

You need **two Soyuz launch vehicles**, not one rocket carrying two spacecraft.

The required production quantities are therefore greater than the previous Soyuz mission.

<GuideTable
  caption="Rocket components prepared for the final two-Soyuz Rendezvous mission."
  columns={rendezvousPartsColumns}
  rows={rendezvousPartsRows}
/>

The quantities above reflect two standard R7 Soyuz assemblies. Check your current Assembly Hall blueprint before placing the orders, particularly if you already have compatible completed parts in storage.

Make sure both rockets are assembled and ready.

You can prepare the second rocket while the first is being delivered or launched, provided the facilities and production queues allow it.

### Step 3: Launch the first Soyuz spacecraft

Transport the first completed Soyuz rocket to the Launch Site.

Supply Kerosene and Liquid Oxygen, then open Mission Control.

Select an orbit that the Soyuz rocket can actually reach.

This first spacecraft will become the rendezvous target for the second.

A practical precaution is to avoid choosing a demanding orbit merely because the option is available in Mission Control. The second rocket must be able to reach a compatible target afterward.

Launch the first Soyuz and confirm that it has reached orbit.

### Step 4: Prepare the second Soyuz launch

Once the first Soyuz is in orbit, move the second completed rocket to its Launch Site and supply the required fuel. Open Mission Control and select the pad containing that second vehicle.

Use the control for **launching to an existing spacecraft's orbit**, and select the first Soyuz as the rendezvous target.

This is different from manually choosing a Circular, Elliptical, or Geosynchronous orbit without targeting the existing spacecraft.

If the game reports **"This rocket cannot reach this orbit,"** do not launch repeatedly with the same rejected target. Check the first spacecraft's orbit and whether the second rocket can reach it.

### What if the game says "This rocket cannot reach this orbit"?

This warning can appear when attempting to launch the second spacecraft toward the selected target orbit.

It indicates that the current rocket cannot reach that requested orbit.

One failed rendezvous preparation involved a first spacecraft sent toward a Geosynchronous target. The second rocket could not use the requested orbit.

The exact reason was not established by that warning alone, so do not assume all Geosynchronous rendezvous attempts are impossible.

Instead:

1. Check the orbit occupied by the first spacecraft.
2. Compare it with the second rocket's capabilities.
3. If necessary, prepare a compatible target using another launch.
4. Use the existing-spacecraft orbit option again.
5. Confirm that the launch request is accepted before proceeding.

It is better to correct an incompatible target than to repeatedly submit the same failed orbital request.

### Step 5: Bring the second Soyuz into orbit

Once the target is valid, launch the second Soyuz.

Check that it successfully reaches the intended orbit.

At this stage, you should have two spacecraft in orbit, but the campaign may still show the final objective as incomplete.

That is expected until the actual connection operation has been performed.

### Step 6: Connect the two Soyuz spacecraft

**This is the step most likely to be missed.**

Open Mission Control and inspect the spacecraft currently in orbit.

Select the relevant Soyuz spacecraft and open its orbital controls.

Look for the command corresponding to **attaching or connecting to another module**.

Execute that command for the intended spacecraft.

Do not assume that launching both Soyuz vehicles into compatible orbits automatically performs the final connection.

In the completed campaign, both spacecraft were already in orbit, but the success message did not appear until the required attachment operation was executed.

The exact wording of the command may vary with the game's language and version. The important action is to use the spacecraft's connection control, not merely the launch-orbit selector.

### Step 7: Confirm Orbital Rendezvous succeeded

After the connection operation succeeds, the game displays a message confirming that two spacecraft have met and connected in Earth orbit.

The campaign then displays its final completion state.

If you still have two separate spacecraft in orbit and no completion message, return to the spacecraft controls and check whether the connection operation has actually been issued.

## Why Is the Baikonur Campaign Not Progressing?

When a mission does not advance, first identify whether the game is waiting for research, a construction objective, a particular component, a successful flight, or an action performed on a spacecraft already in orbit.

<GuideTable
  caption="Baikonur Campaign progression problems and next actions."
  columns={campaignProblemsColumns}
  rows={campaignProblemsRows}
/>

### Rocket parts are finished, but the campaign has not advanced

Check whether the current objective requires component production, delivery to the Assembly Hall, or completion of the assembled rocket. These are different conditions.

A component may disappear from finished-parts storage when the Assembly Hall consumes it. If the rocket is already being assembled, wait for its completion and inspect the updated mission objective before producing another set of parts.

If assembly has not started, compare the missing-component list with the parts still waiting at manufacturing, testing, or transport.

### The campaign stopped after a failed rocket launch

A failed rocket may require you to rebuild and repeat the current mission.

The game can retain earlier research or completed infrastructure even when the flight itself must be attempted again.

Inspect the newly displayed objective and prepare the required replacement rocket.

</section>

<section id="campaign-completion" className="scroll-mt-24">

## What Happens After You Complete the Baikonur Campaign?

The game displays **Campaign Completed** after the two Soyuz spacecraft successfully connect in Earth orbit. This confirms the final rendezvous objective, but it does not mean that a permanent space station has already been built or supplied.

You can continue developing advanced rockets, orbital infrastructure, and space stations after finishing the campaign. In a regular republic, those projects still depend on research, production capacity, fuel, and reliable launches.

For the next industrial or research goal, return to [Space Program progression](/workers-and-resources-space-program).

</section>
