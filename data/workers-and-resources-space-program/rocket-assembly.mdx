<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
An **R7 rocket** can be assembled only when the Assembly Hall has received every component required by its selected blueprint. Manufacture the matching core, boosters, and fairing, test compatible parts if you want to reduce failure risk, and transport them to the Assembly Hall. Once construction finishes, use a suitable rail transport arrangement to move the complete rocket to the Launch Site.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

A Manufacturing Hall can report finished rocket parts while the Assembly Hall still lists those components as missing. They may be waiting for transport, undergoing testing, or sitting on a vehicle that has not unloaded. Even after delivery, an incorrect part variant or insufficient engineering staff can stop assembly. Identify where the component is being held before scheduling another manufacturing batch.

<section id="rocket-buildings">

## Which Buildings Do You Need to Build a Rocket?

You need two main production facilities, with an optional testing stage between them.

<GuideTable
  caption="Buildings used to manufacture, test, assemble, and deliver rockets."
  columns={rocketBuildingColumns}
  rows={rocketBuildingRows}
/>

Manufacturing and assembly halls come in different sizes. The larger versions require more room and infrastructure, so check the rockets supported by the selected building before committing to construction.

For your first small rocket, there is little reason to build the largest industrial complex immediately if a smaller facility can handle the planned components.

### Plan the transport connections before placing the halls

Leave enough space for the vehicles that will move rocket components between buildings.

A practical layout places the manufacturing and assembly facilities close enough to avoid long journeys while leaving room for:

- Road access for high-capacity flatbed trucks.
- Rail connections if you intend to transport components by train.
- A connection to the engine testing facility.
- A rail route from the Assembly Hall toward the Launch Site.
- Worker transport and the industrial deliveries needed to keep production running.

The important distinction is that a Rocket Assembly Hall is not simply another ordinary factory in a production chain. You must account for the physical movement of large rocket components.

Placing two halls next to one another does not eliminate that requirement.

## How Do You Choose the Right Rocket Blueprint?

**Start with the Assembly Hall, not the manufacturing queue.**

Open your Rocket Assembly Hall and examine the available rocket blueprints. Choose the rocket required by your current goal, then note every component listed in its assembly requirements.

For an early test flight, the **R7 Testing Rocket** is a useful example.

### R7 Testing Rocket parts

<GuideTable
  caption="Required parts for an R7 Testing Rocket."
  columns={r7TestingRocketColumns}
  rows={r7TestingRocketRows}
/>

These are the components for the R7 Testing Rocket example, not a universal recipe for every rocket.

Once you know the required parts, open the Rocket Parts Manufacturing Hall and select the matching components from its production options.

Make sure you select the **test** versions where the blueprint requires them. A component with a similar name is not necessarily interchangeable.

### Why won't the Assembly Hall accept my parts?

The Assembly Hall checks the components required by its selected rocket.

If you manufactured parts for a different variant, those parts may exist in storage but still fail to satisfy the current assembly requirements.

When this happens:

1. Open the Assembly Hall and check its selected blueprint.
2. Compare the missing component names with the completed manufacturing orders.
3. Confirm whether the component is a test version or a regular variant.
4. Produce the exact missing part rather than assuming a similar-looking component will work.

For advanced rockets, return to the Assembly Hall's requirements before manufacturing another batch. Do not reuse the R7 Testing Rocket's parts list for later designs.

<section id="manufacture-parts">

## How Do You Manufacture Rocket Parts?

Select a component in the Rocket Parts Manufacturing Hall and inspect its material requirements.

Rocket production uses several industrial products that may already exist in a developed republic, including:

- Steel and aluminium.
- Plastics and fabric.
- Electronic components and electronics.
- Mechanical components.

The required amounts vary by component. Check the selected part's current production panel instead of treating a single material list as the recipe for every engine, booster, and fairing.

### Step 1: Supply the manufacturing hall

Connect the building to the infrastructure it needs and arrange deliveries for the input resources shown in its interface.

If your republic does not produce one of the required goods locally, you can arrange imports through your existing logistics network where your game settings permit them.

A manufacturing hall does not produce components merely because it has been built. It still needs the required inputs and an available workforce.

Check the production panel after deliveries begin. Missing resources will continue to prevent the selected part from completing.

### Step 2: Add the required components to the queue

Open the available rocket component blueprints and choose the part you want to manufacture.

Use the component counts shown in the Assembly Hall's selected blueprint. Queue each required part, including repeated boosters, before starting another rocket design.

Use the **Next in Production** queue to schedule additional components rather than repeatedly returning to the building after every part finishes.

Before adding more orders, compare the queue with the current Assembly Hall requirements. Manufacturing an extra core will not compensate for a missing booster.

### Step 3: Confirm the parts are finished

Watch the production progress and the finished-parts storage at the manufacturing hall.

Once a component is complete, it becomes available for transport.

If the queue is progressing but the Assembly Hall still shows missing parts, that does not necessarily mean manufacturing has failed. The finished components may simply be waiting to be collected.

<section id="test-rocket-engines">

## How Do You Test Rocket Engines Before Assembly?

**Testing compatible rocket components before assembly is one of the most useful ways to reduce the risk of losing an expensive rocket.**

A rocket can be assembled and launched without every component being tested, but that shortcut can leave you with a much higher failure risk.

For a more reliable build, place the Rocket Engine Testing Facility between manufacturing and assembly in your production workflow.

### Step 1: Build the testing facility

Select the appropriate Rocket Engine Testing Facility and make sure your chosen version can receive the components you intend to test.

Inspect its available transport connections before building it. If your production network relies on trains, choose a compatible testing facility and provide the necessary rail access.

The building must also have the workers and services needed to operate.

### Step 2: Deliver compatible components for testing

Set up a separate transport movement from the Rocket Parts Manufacturing Hall to the testing facility.

For a railway-based setup, this can be a dedicated train that collects newly manufactured engines or boosters and unloads them at the testing building.

Once a compatible component is delivered and the facility can operate, testing begins.

You can inspect the component's testing status and expected failure value in the building interface.

### Step 3: Wait for the testing result

Do not collect a component while it is still being tested if your goal is to use its completed test result.

The component panel shows both **Expected failure** and **Testing status**. Use those fields to distinguish parts that still need testing from parts that have already passed.

Three RD107 booster panels show different testing states:

- **No tested:** An RD107 booster displayed **14.6% expected failure** before testing.
- **In progress:** Another RD107 booster displayed **12.1% expected failure** while testing was underway.
- **Passed:** A tested RD107 booster displayed **4.3% expected failure**.

These are different boosters, not three stages of the same component. The values demonstrate the difference between testing states but do not establish a fixed failure-rate reduction.

Check the status of each component before moving it to the Assembly Hall.

### Step 4: Move tested parts to the Assembly Hall

Testing does not automatically mean the component has reached the assembly building.

Arrange another transport movement from the testing facility to the Assembly Hall.

A useful railway setup can separate the three movements:

1. **Manufacturing Hall → Testing Facility** for untested components.
2. **Testing Facility → Assembly Hall** for components after testing.
3. **Assembly Hall → Launch Site** for the complete rocket.

This arrangement also makes it easier to identify where a component is waiting.

If you want to save time on an early experimental launch, you can skip the optional testing stage. Just understand that the resulting rocket may carry considerably more failure risk.

For the full-rocket Expected Failure Rate and what to do after a launch fails, use the [Rocket Launch and Failures guide](/workers-and-resources-space-program/rocket-launch).

<section id="rocket-parts-transport">

## How Do You Transport Rocket Parts to the Assembly Hall?

Rocket components require suitable transport equipment. They should not be treated like ordinary steel, plastics, or other bulk industrial cargo.

You can use compatible high-capacity road vehicles or a properly configured railway movement, depending on the facilities and vehicles available in your republic.

### Method 1: Use high-capacity flatbed trucks

In the Baikonur Campaign, the rocket transport instructions specifically call for **high-capacity flatbed vehicles** because rocket components are too large and heavy for ordinary trucks.

To arrange road transport:

1. Open the depot or vehicle purchase menu.
2. Select a flatbed vehicle whose capacity and cargo compatibility match the component.
3. Create a route from the Rocket Parts Manufacturing Hall to the required testing or assembly destination.
4. Configure loading at the manufacturing facility and unloading at the destination.
5. Start the vehicle and confirm the component actually leaves storage.

Check the available vehicle selection in your own game. Earlier-start republics may not have access to the same heavy transport options as a later-start campaign.

If a vehicle cannot collect a part, verify its cargo compatibility and capacity before adding more ordinary trucks.

### Method 2: Transport rocket parts by train

A rail connection is another workable approach, particularly when you are building several rocket components or placing the facilities farther apart.

A successful railway setup used a locomotive with compatible **Rocket Flatbed** wagons to move individual parts from manufacturing to assembly.

Follow this sequence:

1. Connect the manufacturing building to the railway network.
2. Connect the Assembly Hall to a reachable rail line.
3. Purchase a locomotive and compatible flatbed wagons.
4. Create a vehicle line with the manufacturing hall as the loading stop.
5. Set the Assembly Hall as the unloading stop.
6. Configure the line to carry the completed rocket components.
7. Start the train and verify that the parts appear in the Assembly Hall.

The number of components that fit on a wagon depends on their size and the vehicle's capacity. Do not assume every part requires its own wagon.

### Why does the Distribution Office say "Building Is Incompatible"?

A normal Distribution Office may not accept rocket component buildings as ordinary cargo sources or destinations.

In one railway setup, attempting to add a rocket building to the Distribution Office produced the message:

**"Building is incompatible."**

The working alternative was a directly configured vehicle line using Rocket Flatbed wagons.

If you encounter the same error, stop trying to add the building to that Distribution Office configuration. Use a suitable vehicle and direct line instead.

This is different from ordinary material deliveries *into* the manufacturing hall, which can still use the republic's regular logistics arrangements where supported.

### Why does the train say "First Stop Is Unreachable"?

Check the railway itself before changing the rocket blueprint or replacing the wagons.

A route can appear connected while one of its rail segments is still under construction. The train cannot collect the part until the track is finished and the route becomes reachable.

If the route remains unavailable afterward, inspect the signals and the train's access to the loading platform.

Check these conditions:

- Is every required rail segment fully constructed?
- Can the train enter and leave each building's rail connection?
- Do one-way tracks and signals allow the intended movement?
- Can the locomotive reach the correct loading platform?
- Does the route provide a way to return to the depot or next stop?

Do not confuse a route-finding error with an unavailable rocket component. A part may be ready for collection while the assigned vehicle cannot reach it.

<section id="assemble-rocket">

## How Do You Assemble a Complete Rocket?

Once all required components have been manufactured and transported, open the Rocket Assembly Hall.

The building should show the selected rocket blueprint and the components currently available for that build.

If the necessary parts are present, the hall can begin assembling the rocket when its other operating requirements are met.

### Check the assembly workforce

Rocket assembly can demand substantial labor, including highly educated specialists.

A large Assembly Hall may appear to have sufficient general worker capacity while making very slow progress because too few engineers have arrived.

In one production setup, the hall had room for many workers but only a handful of engineers. Rocket assembly was progressing at just a few percent despite the components already being available.

To correct this, check:

- Whether workers can reach the Assembly Hall from the nearest stop or residential area.
- Whether enough highly educated workers are reaching the building.
- Whether your bus or rail service actually delivers workers to the intended stop.
- Whether the necessary building services and operating conditions are available.
- Whether the Assembly Hall has all the required rocket parts.

A nearby university or a large city does not automatically mean the correct specialists are working at the hall. Check the people actually present in the building.

### Check the assembly queue

If you have already selected the correct rocket but assembly does not begin, inspect the component list.

Look for a missing core, booster, fairing, or another component specific to the chosen design.

Then check the three possible locations where the missing part may be held:

1. Finished storage at the Manufacturing Hall.
2. Storage or processing at the Testing Facility.
3. A vehicle that has not yet unloaded at the Assembly Hall.

This is usually more useful than ordering another full set of components.

When assembly reaches completion, the finished rocket becomes available for collection.

</section>

## How Do You Move the Finished Rocket to the Launch Site?

The finished rocket is a different transport task from moving individual boosters and cores.

Once the Assembly Hall completes construction, arrange transport from the **Rocket Assembly Hall to the Launch Site**.

In a railway-based setup, a locomotive collects the assembled rocket using the appropriate rocket transport arrangement. The complete rocket is not loaded as ordinary boxed industrial cargo.

One successful transport sequence used an existing train line between the Assembly Hall and Launch Site. The locomotive collected the finished rocket and carried it directly to the launch facility.

1. Confirm that the Assembly Hall shows a completed rocket ready for pickup.
2. Make sure a usable railway connection reaches the Launch Site.
3. Set the transport line to load the completed rocket at the Assembly Hall.
4. Set the destination to the Launch Site.
5. Check the vehicle's route, loading configuration, and available capacity.
6. Watch the rocket arrive at the Launch Site.

If the transport train cannot return to its depot, check the rail layout and signals before sending it again.

### Is the rocket ready to launch as soon as it arrives?

Not necessarily.

Delivering the completed rocket only finishes the manufacturing and assembly stage.

You still need the relevant launch preparations, including fuel, Mission Control, a suitable mission, and the required launch conditions.

Follow the [Rocket Launch guide](/workers-and-resources-space-program/rocket-launch) for the steps after delivery.

<section id="next-rocket">

## What Should You Build After Your First Test Rocket?

Before manufacturing another batch of R7 components, open the Assembly Hall and select the next rocket you intend to build. Sputnik and later spacecraft use their own blueprints, so a test core or fairing left in storage may not satisfy the new requirements.

If the completed rocket is already at the Launch Site, [prepare its fuel and launch settings](/workers-and-resources-space-program/rocket-launch). If you are following Baikonur, [continue from the current mission objective](/workers-and-resources-space-program/baikonur-campaign) rather than producing components for a later spacecraft too early.
