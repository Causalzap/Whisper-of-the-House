In a regular republic, first secure the required research, industrial materials, workers, transport access, and rocket fuel. The Space Program DLC turns rocket development into an industrial project rather than a single building you place and activate.

Research unlocks new spacecraft and facilities, but your republic still has to manufacture individual rocket parts, transport oversized components, test engines, assemble vehicles, deliver specialized fuel, and control each launch.

A rocket can be fully manufactured and still go nowhere because one required booster has not reached the Assembly Hall. A finished rocket can reach its launch pad and remain grounded because the Liquid Oxygen delivery has not arrived. Even when everything is ready, a launch can fail.

The most efficient way to begin is to establish one complete route from research to launch, confirm that it works, and only then invest in more advanced spacecraft.

Already working on your first rocket? Check the [Rocket Assembly instructions](/workers-and-resources-space-program/rocket-assembly) for missing components, engine testing, and oversized cargo transport. If your rocket is at the launch pad, use the [Rocket Launch instructions](/workers-and-resources-space-program/rocket-launch) to prepare fuel, Mission Control, and the target orbit.

For the official story missions, follow the [Baikonur Campaign walkthrough](/workers-and-resources-space-program/baikonur-campaign) from the first Test Rocket and Sputnik through Vostok, Soyuz, and the final Orbital Rendezvous.

## How Do You Start the Space Program DLC?

You can begin in the **Baikonur Campaign** or develop the new systems in a republic of your own.

The two approaches have different starting requirements.

### Start with the Baikonur Campaign

The Baikonur Campaign takes place at an established Soviet space center in the early 1960s.

The starting area already provides a population, existing infrastructure, a Space University, and a designated launch location.

Your first responsibility is to develop the industrial facilities needed to build a complete rocket.

The opening objectives introduce the major systems in sequence, including Rocket Parts Manufacturing, Mission Control, Engine Testing, and Rocket Assembly.

This is the more straightforward choice if you want to learn what each facility does before creating an entire space industry in a separate republic.

### Start in your own republic

In an existing or new republic, you must develop the infrastructure yourself.

Before building the first rocket facility, check whether your republic has:

- Access to the research required for rocket technology.
- A functioning Space University or the ability to establish one.
- Workers and qualified personnel for the new facilities.
- A stable supply of industrial construction and manufacturing materials.
- Space for rail connections and oversized rocket transport.
- A way to provide the required rocket fuel.
- Enough money and production capacity to survive unsuccessful launches.

A republic that already produces steel, electronics, plastics, and mechanical components has a useful foundation for rocket manufacturing.

However, access to materials does not automatically unlock rocket blueprints or provide the specialized transport and fuel infrastructure needed for spaceflight.

**Do not start by constructing every space-related building at once.** First confirm the research and logistics required for one rocket type.

## What Should You Build First in Space Program?

Plan your first space industry around the movement of large rocket components. A Manufacturing Hall may have everything it needs to produce boosters, yet become difficult to use if the finished parts cannot leave the facility.

Before placing the main buildings, make four decisions:

1. **Manufacturing location:** Choose an industrial area with reliable workers, material deliveries, and enough room for rocket-part pickup.
2. **Testing and assembly access:** Reserve suitable road or railway connections between these facilities. The correct transport vehicle matters as much as the distance between buildings.
3. **Launch Site location:** Keep the launch facility away from densely populated districts and important industrial infrastructure. A failed launch can damage nearby buildings.
4. **Staffing and fuel supply:** Confirm that the production buildings can receive workers and that the launch site can eventually obtain the required propellants.

In Baikonur, much of the supporting city infrastructure already exists. In a normal republic, these decisions may require new passenger lines, industrial capacity, or fuel production. Build for the first rocket you can actually support rather than constructing every available space facility at once.

</section>

## How Does the Rocket Production Chain Work?

Rocket manufacturing has two distinct outputs: individual components from the Manufacturing Hall and a completed launch vehicle from the Assembly Hall. Testing and transport take place between those stages, depending on the rocket and the setup you are using.

<GuideTable
  caption="Rocket production and launch stages in Workers & Resources Space Program."
  columns={productionColumns}
  rows={productionRows}
/>

A finished RD107 booster cannot launch on its own. It must reach the correct destination and be included in an Assembly Hall blueprint alongside the other required parts. Similarly, a completed rocket still has to travel to a Launch Site before fueling and launch can begin.

If the Assembly Hall reports missing components, look at the manufacturing storage, testing facility, and transport line before ordering replacements. The [rocket manufacturing and assembly instructions](/workers-and-resources-space-program/rocket-assembly) explain the compatible vehicles, component variants, and production problems.

</section>

<section id="rocket-fuel" className="scroll-mt-24">

## What Fuel Do Rockets Need in Space Program?

Early R7 rockets require **Kerosene and Liquid Oxygen**. Baikonur provides fuel infrastructure and delivery vehicles, but a normal republic may have to establish the supply first. Check the current rocket's propellant requirements rather than assuming every vehicle uses the same combination.

Liquid Oxygen should not be delivered too early. Finish assembly, rocket transport, and Mission Control preparations before relying on the final fuel deliveries. If a tanker arrives without completing the fuel requirement, inspect the remaining quantities and the Distribution Office's unloading orders.

Larger rocket families may require different propellants, including UDMH and N2O4. For the actual [fueling and launch procedure](/workers-and-resources-space-program/rocket-launch), use the requirements of the vehicle standing at your launch pad.

</section>

<section id="first-launch" className="scroll-mt-24">

## How Do You Launch Your First Rocket?

With the rocket positioned at the Launch Site and its required fuel available, open Mission Control and select the occupied pad. Choose an orbit the rocket can reach, review its Expected Failure Rate, and authorize launch when the preparation requirements are satisfied.

The mission result matters more than the launch animation. An R7 test flight must satisfy its experimental objective; a Sputnik mission must place its payload into Earth orbit. If the rocket explodes, misses the required orbit, or fails to deploy the payload, inspect the result before preparing a replacement.

For the exact [Mission Control settings and launch failure checks](/workers-and-resources-space-program/rocket-launch), start with the rocket currently on your pad.

</section>

<section id="progression" className="scroll-mt-24">

## What Should You Do After Your First Rocket?

Your first successful R7 test proves that the basic manufacturing and launch system can work. In Baikonur, the next mission introduces Sputnik, which must enter Earth orbit rather than simply reach space.

Crewed missions add a different requirement. Vostok and the first Soyuz mission must return their cosmonauts safely, while the final Orbital Rendezvous requires two Soyuz spacecraft to meet and connect in orbit. Launching the second spacecraft does not complete that final objective by itself.

If you are following the official scenario, the [Baikonur mission sequence](/workers-and-resources-space-program/baikonur-campaign) continues from the first test through the final connection. In a normal republic, choose your next spacecraft based on the technology, propellants, and launch capacity you can support.

</section>

<section id="first-rocket-problems" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Why Is My Space Program Not Progressing?

Start with the rocket's current location. If it does not exist yet, inspect the manufacturing queue, missing components, testing status, and Assembly Hall workforce. A completed booster waiting at the factory is not a finished rocket. Use the [assembly troubleshooting steps](/workers-and-resources-space-program/rocket-assembly) if parts are not moving or the building cannot assemble the selected design.

If the complete rocket is already at the Launch Site, investigate fuel, Mission Control, orbit compatibility, and launch readiness. A full-looking tanker does not confirm that the pad has received its required propellants. The [launch checks](/workers-and-resources-space-program/rocket-launch) cover these conditions and failures during flight.

If the rocket has flown but the next campaign objective remains locked, inspect the required result rather than repeating the launch immediately. Sputnik needs orbital insertion, Vostok needs a safe return, and the last Soyuz mission requires a completed orbital connection. Follow the [Baikonur mission requirements](/workers-and-resources-space-program/baikonur-campaign) for the remaining action.

</section>

<section id="advanced-space-program" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Can You Build Space Stations in Space Program?

Yes. The DLC extends beyond the first rockets and the Baikonur Campaign.

You can research more advanced spacecraft, build larger launch infrastructure, and eventually send space station components into orbit.

Unlike a single completed rocket, an orbital station requires multiple pieces to reach space and connect.

The Space Program also includes advanced technologies and industries such as specialized propellant production, larger Proton rockets, and additional orbital capabilities.

### When should you begin a space station?

Start thinking about a station after your republic can reliably:

- Manufacture advanced rocket components.
- Transport complete launch vehicles.
- Supply the required propellants.
- Launch suitable payloads into orbit.
- Support the research and infrastructure needed for orbital operations.

Attempting a station before your first launch system works reliably creates a large number of additional problems to solve at once.

You can develop the necessary facilities progressively rather than constructing everything before your first successful satellite.

### Is Orbital Rendezvous the end of the DLC?

No.

It is the final objective of the Baikonur Campaign, but the wider space program supports additional development beyond that achievement.

A permanent orbital station and more advanced infrastructure are separate long-term goals.

</section>
