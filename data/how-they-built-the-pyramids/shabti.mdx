A Shabti can take over much of the repetitive labor in How They Built the Pyramids, but buying one does not automatically solve a production problem. You still need somewhere for the worker to operate, an appropriate task, and equipment or materials within reach.

That difference becomes clear with the first hire. A newly purchased worker can stand among the molds doing nothing while the player continues carrying clay. Once it is assigned to a Work Post and given the Mud job, it can begin handling that part of the process.

The Shabti Market also offers workers with different prices, attributes, and traits. A more expensive worker may be attractive for a particular machine, but the better purchase depends on which job needs attention.

<h2 id="hire-shabti" className="scroll-mt-24">
  How do you hire your first Shabti?
</h2>

Visit the Shabti Market and inspect the workers currently on offer. Look beyond the purchase price: the visible traits can affect how useful a particular worker will be once you assign a job.

In one market, two ordinary Clay Shabti were available for 350 coins each. Hapka had the `Thick-Headed` trait, while Djehuty the Elder had `Forgetful`.

Those are two individual market offers, not a guaranteed price for every Clay Shabti.

For your first worker, it is worth checking the job before buying. If the main delay is carrying clay from the pit to the molds, you need a worker who can perform that task and a Work Post positioned to make it useful.

Budget for the assignment as well as the hire. In an early construction session, the player had enough to purchase a Shabti but still needed to build a Work Post before the worker could begin hauling.

Buying a second worker at that point would not have solved the missing assignment.

<h2 id="shabti-stats" className="scroll-mt-24">
  How do you improve Shabti stats and choose their best jobs?
</h2>

Open a Shabti's profile to compare its four displayed attributes: Strength, Speed, Stamina, and Skill. The profile also lists its traits and the work it performs best.

The training information shown beside these attributes is useful when deciding whether to improve an existing worker or replace it.

- **Strength:** The profile identifies the Weight Bench as its training equipment.
- **Speed:** The Treadmill is listed as the way to train this attribute.
- **Stamina:** The profile indicates that it grows through work.
- **Skill:** This also grows through work.

These attributes should be considered alongside the actual job. A worker assigned to a machine that is already short of materials cannot make the missing materials appear simply by having better statistics.

The `BEST AT` section is especially useful because it connects an individual worker to specific activities.

For example, one Gold Shabti profile shows:

- Cranking — 116%
- Rocking — 116%
- Grinding — 115%

These are that worker's displayed work ratings. They are not universal ratings for all Gold Shabti.

If two candidates have different strengths, choose based on the job you intend to give them. A good Rocking rating has a more obvious use at a Rocker Cradle than on a production task that does not involve rocking.

<h2 id="shabti-traits" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Which Shabti traits are worth looking for?
</h2>

Traits can change the value of a Shabti even when its basic attributes look similar to another candidate's.

Several traits have clear effects on particular jobs.

**Ox Shoulders** increases how much a worker can carry at a Work Post: two additional bricks or 300 g more clay. Its description also provides a 10% improvement to several physical operations, including grinding, cranking, rocking, sifting, and reloading.

That makes Ox Shoulders worth considering for regular material handling and relevant equipment work.

**Eats Like a Sparrow** reduces the worker's ration consumption by half. This is a useful consideration for a worker you plan to keep employed over a long construction session.

**Scarab Kiss** increases gold-chip findings by 20% at the Sifting Tray and Rocker Cradle, according to the trait description encountered during recruitment.

**Tireless** reduces how often a worker needs to nap, making it attractive when interruptions matter.

Not every trait improves production. The market also contains workers with disadvantages such as `Forgetful` and `Thick-Headed`. Inspect those candidates rather than assuming that a lower purchase price is the best long-term bargain.

A trait is most valuable when its effect matches the work you will actually assign. Extra clay-carrying capacity will not repair a Work Post with no selected job, and better work ratings will not solve a machine's missing input.

<h2 id="assign-work-post" className="scroll-mt-24">
  How do you assign a Shabti to a Work Post?
</h2>

A Work Post gives a Shabti a designated working location and a task. Building the post and placing the worker are separate from selecting what the worker should do.

A useful first assignment is moving mud to molds when you are still filling them manually.

Use this sequence:

1. **Build a Work Post** near the area where the worker will be needed.
2. **Pick up the Shabti** you purchased from the market.
3. **Carry it to the Work Post** and use the placement interaction to assign it.
4. **Open the Work Post's job options** and select the task, such as Mud.
5. **Watch the worker perform the job** before leaving it unattended.

The Work Post's in-game instructions describe carrying a worker to it and using the left mouse button to place the worker.

The job selection is an important additional step. In one multiplayer setup, a worker was already at the post, but the player eventually discovered that the intended clay job had never actually been selected.

Once the Mud job was chosen, the worker began filling molds.

It is possible to complete the first part of the assignment and overlook the second.

<h3 id="mud-and-brick-jobs" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Should a Work Post haul mud or carry finished bricks?
</h3>

Choose the task according to the manual work that is taking the most time.

A Mud job is useful when molds or other eligible clay receivers need a steady supply. If you are repeatedly returning to the clay pit and filling molds by hand, assigning that task to a worker can free you to collect and place finished bricks.

Brick hauling addresses a different part of the operation. It moves finished bricks from supported pickup locations toward storage or other valid receiving locations. The destination matters: a worker cannot complete useful deliveries if there is nowhere suitable for the bricks to go.

<h2 id="work-post-range" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Why is my Shabti working at the wrong molds?
</h2>

A correctly assigned worker can still spend time on a target you did not intend.

In an early Work Post setup, a worker had been assigned to move mud. It successfully began working, but later moved toward another eligible mold after its previous target became filled.

The player had to reconsider where the Work Post was placed relative to the molds.

This is an important difference between an idle worker and a worker doing the wrong task.

If the Shabti is walking around and moving material, the basic job may already be functioning. Before removing it, inspect which targets are within the working area and which of those targets can still receive material.

For a Work Post near several mold groups, check the actual deliveries rather than assuming the worker will favor the group closest to you.

If necessary, move the post or rearrange its eligible targets so that the worker's activity matches your intended production area.

A worker who is productive in the wrong location can be more confusing than one that displays `No job yet`, because nothing appears completely broken.

<h2 id="work-post-feeding" className="scroll-mt-24">
  How do you know a Work Post is feeding a machine?
</h2>

Some receiving machines show worker-fed supply information directly in their interaction panel.

A Rocker Cradle, for example, can display `Fed by Work Post x2` when it is being supplied by two Work Posts.

This gives you a useful confirmation beyond simply seeing workers near the machine.

Check the actual machine status when evaluating the assignment. A worker can be present while the material supply still needs attention, and a Work Post can exist without having the right target or job configured.

The screenshot also shows `Crew 2/2` for the Rocker Cradle. That is separate from the `Fed by Work Post x2` status: one describes occupied Crew capacity, while the other identifies worker-post feeding.

Do not treat those two values as interchangeable.

If a receiving machine still has empty inputs after you set up its Work Posts, verify that the selected task and its receiving area match what the machine needs. For problems involving the machine's own input capacity, output, belts, or power, use the <Link href="/how-they-built-the-pyramids/automation">automation and brick-production guide</Link>.

<h2 id="crew-seats" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Should you use a Work Post or put a Shabti into a machine?
</h2>

A Shabti does not always need a Work Post. Some processing machines have Crew seats where workers can operate the equipment directly.

The decision depends on which part of your factory needs labor.

Use a Work Post when the missing task is moving mud, hauling bricks, or another job controlled through the post.

Assign a worker to a machine's Crew seat when the machine itself needs labor and has space for another operator.

Check the machine's Crew display before assigning another Shabti. If every seat is occupied, the worker needs another available assignment or the machine needs more Crew capacity.

Hiring another Shabti does not create an additional seat. For machine-specific capacity upgrades, see the <Link href="/how-they-built-the-pyramids/automation">automation guide</Link>.

Before moving workers between tasks, look at what is actually waiting:

- If molds need clay, assign a worker to the relevant Work Post.
- If a processing machine has an empty Crew seat, consider assigning a worker whose ratings suit that job.
- If every Crew seat is occupied, choose another useful assignment before hiring or moving an additional Shabti.

<h2 id="shabti-training-and-rest" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Do Shabti get better through training and work?
</h2>

Shabti profiles display experience progress for their attributes. Some attributes have dedicated training equipment, while others grow through ordinary work.

That creates a choice between improving a worker you already employ and spending more coins on another market offer.

If an existing worker is already doing a useful job, first check its profile. You may be able to improve the relevant ability rather than replacing the worker.

Training has an opportunity cost, though. A worker spending time at training equipment is not performing its previous production task during that time.

For a factory that is short of clay, taking away its only useful mud worker may slow production even if that worker eventually becomes better trained.

A Shabti may take a nap while its Mud job remains assigned. When it stops moving, check the worker's status before changing the Work Post or hiring a replacement. Tireless is useful for repetitive work because it reduces interruptions.

<h2 id="shabti-not-working" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Why is my Shabti not working?
</h2>

Several different problems can make a worker appear unproductive. The fastest way to distinguish them is to inspect the worker and its assigned building before replacing either.

<GuideTable
  caption="Shabti troubleshooting: distinguish missing assignments, unsuitable targets, and occupied crew positions."
  columns={shabtiProblemColumns}
  rows={shabtiProblemRows}
/>

If the worker has never completed a task, start with the assignment. A displayed `No job yet` message is much more decisive than the worker's price or attributes.

If it already completed work, inspect what changed. A mold may have become full, another group may now be within reach, or the receiving machine may no longer have capacity for additional material.

There is no reason to replace a worker solely because an upstream or downstream machine has stopped accepting its deliveries.

The most useful success check is an actual change in the intended destination: clay arriving in molds, ready bricks moving toward storage, or the worker appearing in a machine's Crew display.

<h2 id="when-to-hire-more" className="scroll-mt-24">
  When should you hire another Shabti?
</h2>

Before purchasing another Shabti, inspect the jobs your existing crew is performing. A second mud hauler has little to do when every nearby mold is already supplied. A separate hauling task or an available Crew seat on a busy machine gives the next worker a clearer purpose.

Compare the market candidates against that position. Look for the traits and work ratings that fit the task, then make sure the workplace is ready before purchasing. If your current worker is idle because its assignment is missing or its targets are full, fix that problem first rather than hiring another worker to wait beside it.

Once your Shabti are working on the right jobs, check whether the remaining delays come from your equipment or the construction site.
