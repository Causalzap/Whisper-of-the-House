How They Built the Pyramids starts with a Clay Bowl, a few molds, and an enormous construction job. The standard pyramid contains 23,426 bricks across 26 layers, reaching a height of 65 metres. You can complete it alone or work with up to seven other players.

Your first few trips between the molds and the pyramid reveal where the work is going. You may leave finished bricks behind because you cannot carry them all, or return to find that the next batch is still wet. Finish the gym tutorial, make another round of bricks, and watch which part of the cycle keeps you waiting. That gives you a reason to choose Strength, Speed, or faster drying before spending your first coins.

<h2 id="first-brick" className="scroll-mt-24">
  How do you make and place your first brick?
</h2>

Start at a clay deposit near the Nile. Equip your Clay Bowl, collect clay, and take it to one of the available Clay Molds.

The opening tutorial teaches the process in order:

1. Scoop clay into the Clay Bowl.
2. Pour the clay into an empty mold.
3. Allow the molded brick to dry.
4. Use the blow interaction to accelerate drying if you are waiting.
5. Pick up the finished brick and place it on the pyramid's current layer.

On the default mouse controls, holding the right mouse button lets you blow on a drying mold. The mold's drying indicator shows when the brick is ready to collect.

If the game tells you `Your clay bowl is full — pour it into a mold`, stop trying to collect more clay. Empty the bowl into an available mold first.

Once the first brick is ready, put the Clay Bowl away, pick up the brick, and take it to the highlighted building area. Place it on the current layer rather than dropping it beside the pyramid.

The opening placement controls also allow you to carry several bricks and drop them individually or as a group. As your carrying capacity improves, collecting multiple finished bricks becomes more useful than repeating the entire journey for a single one.

After the first placement, the next tutorial task introduces gym training. Complete that step before settling into a long cycle of making and carrying bricks.

<h2 id="strength-or-speed" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Should you train Strength or Speed first?
</h2>

Strength and Speed solve different parts of the opening workload.

**Strength** lets you carry more bricks. That reduces the number of trips needed to empty your molds and complete the current pyramid layer.

**Speed** helps you cover the distance between production, storage, and construction more quickly. It becomes increasingly valuable when your working area spreads out or you spend much of each cycle travelling.

The gym provides a Weight Bench and a Treadmill. Weight training uses a timing-based interaction, while the Treadmill involves repeated movement input.

For a fresh solo start, Strength is often the more useful first improvement because the initial carrying limit is small. Increasing the number of bricks per journey immediately changes how many trips you need to make.

But that choice can reverse.

Suppose you can already carry nearly every brick ready in your molds. More Strength will not help much until you produce larger batches. If the molds are far from the pyramid and you spend most of your time travelling, Speed may provide the more noticeable improvement.

Pay attention to the work you are repeating rather than automatically levelling one attribute far ahead of the other.

<h2 id="first-upgrades" className="scroll-mt-24">
  What should you buy first in How They Built the Pyramids?
</h2>

The first coins should improve the operation you already have, not finance a large factory that cannot yet supply or use its machines.

A Standing Fan is an attractive early purchase when drying time keeps you waiting. In an early solo setup, the player positioned a Fan to cover several existing molds and began collecting batches faster.

The important detail was the number of molds receiving the benefit. Putting the Fan beside a group that is barely used would have done much less.

Mold Racks become useful as you expand the number of bricks being made simultaneously. A Brick Crate helps when ready bricks are accumulating and you are spending too much time collecting each one.

These purchases serve different purposes. Buying a second Fan when your finished bricks are already sitting uncollected is not an improvement to the part of production that needs attention.

If several ready bricks remain after each collection trip, improve carrying or collection capacity. If the molds are still wet when you return, faster drying is likely to help more than adding another row of molds.

A useful test is to complete one normal cycle after making a purchase. Did you collect the next batch sooner? Carry more bricks to the pyramid? Spend less time refilling molds?

If nothing changed, identify the actual delay before spending more coins.

<h2 id="earn-coins" className="scroll-mt-24">
  How do you earn more coins without slowing construction?
</h2>

Placing bricks into the pyramid generates money for your operation. That income funds the tools, workers, and transport equipment needed to make later construction easier.

Your first income is modest because the initial bricks and batches are small. Increasing throughput helps you place more bricks, while improvements to brick quality can make the finished bricks more valuable.

Golden bricks can provide a much larger payout, but placing ordinary bricks remains a dependable way to fund your early purchases. Keep construction moving rather than waiting for a rare high-value brick.

If you want a golden exterior, decide whether the immediate coin reward is worth giving up a brick you could reserve for a visible section of the pyramid.

Later processing equipment can improve the value of the bricks your operation produces. The practical question is whether you already have enough clay production and brick delivery to keep that equipment useful.

Spending all your money on refining machinery while basic production remains slow may leave you waiting longer to recover the investment.

There are also rewards tied to construction milestones. As your team places more bricks and finishes layers, check which new tools and upgrades become available rather than assuming the opening build menu contains everything you can eventually use.

<h2 id="first-layer" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Why does the first pyramid layer take so long?
</h2>

The first layer contains roughly 2,600 brick positions. It is the broad foundation of the pyramid, so the amount of work can feel disproportionate to the progress displayed on screen.

Do not judge the remaining construction time by how slowly the first layer appears to move.

The pyramid narrows with each completed layer. Later layers contain fewer positions, even though transporting bricks to the higher construction area creates new difficulties.

This changes which upgrades matter.

During the first layer, your problem may be generating and collecting enough bricks. When the pyramid becomes tall, the same production rate can be sufficient while vertical transport becomes the expensive part of the operation.

Keep building while improving your setup. You do not need to finish the first layer before making every useful purchase, nor should you stop construction to build a large factory that is not ready to operate.

The first layer is also a good opportunity to learn how far your production equipment can be from the build site before travelling becomes inconvenient.

Once the distance starts costing too much time, begin planning a delivery route rather than adding another unrelated mold.

<h2 id="when-to-hire-shabti" className="scroll-mt-24">
  When should you hire your first Shabti?
</h2>

Hire a Shabti when you can identify a task that repeats constantly and does not require your personal attention.

Consider your first hire when a repeated manual task is keeping you away from placing bricks. If trips to the clay pit take up most of your time, a worker may free you to concentrate on construction. If you are still using only a few molds, improving your own carrying capacity or drying speed may be the better investment.

Allow enough coins to give the worker a useful assignment, not just to purchase it. Before hiring, identify the task you want to stop doing and make sure the necessary workplace is available. A Shabti standing without a job will not save you time.

If you cannot yet afford both the worker and a usable assignment, continue building until the purchase can make a practical difference.

For hiring prices, Gold Shabti, Work Posts, Traits, and workers that refuse to start a job, use the <Link href="/how-they-built-the-pyramids/shabti">Shabti guide</Link>.

<h2 id="automation-transition" className="scroll-mt-24">
  When should you switch from manual bricks to automation?
</h2>

If you can carry most of a batch while the next molds dry, your simple setup may still be enough. Automation becomes more useful when collecting or hauling materials repeatedly interrupts pyramid construction. Replace one of those manual jobs first instead of spending your savings on a complete conveyor network.

After the change, make another batch and see whether more bricks reach the pyramid without your help. When the remaining problem involves Clay Hopper reach, belt connections, Sorter rules, or a processing machine that has stopped, use the <Link href="/how-they-built-the-pyramids/automation">automation instructions</Link>.

<h2 id="monuments-and-blessings" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Should you build monuments early or save the coins?
</h2>

Monuments use construction resources to provide Blessings that can benefit the building operation.

The Obelisk of Ra is one useful example. Completing it can grant a team-wide drying bonus. One completed Obelisk displayed `Blessing of Ra: Drying +37% for the whole team, forever`.

That is a substantial benefit when your crew is still producing bricks with many molds. Faster drying can help throughout the remaining construction rather than improving only one machine.

The timing of the purchase matters, however.

If you are playing alone and still lack a useful way to collect and deliver bricks, putting coins and building effort into a monument may delay more immediate improvements.

If you have a group operating many molds and everyone benefits from the same drying improvement, the monument becomes more attractive.

Use the current limitation to make the choice:

- If molds are constantly waiting to dry, a team-wide drying Blessing may provide lasting value.
- If finished bricks are already accumulating, solve collection or transport first.
- If construction money is scarce and your operation is barely producing, establish a steadier supply before committing resources to another building project.

Monuments must be completed, not merely placed. Supply the required bricks until the monument reports completion.

In current versions, bricks can go directly into a monument's empty positions, and you can aim at those positions from inside the monument plaza.

Completing a monument also satisfies the requirement for the Blessed achievement. The other Steam unlock conditions are listed in the <Link href="/how-they-built-the-pyramids/achievements">achievements guide</Link>.

<h2 id="gold-bricks" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Should you save gold bricks for the outside of the pyramid?
</h2>

Golden bricks present a choice between immediate building progress and the appearance of the completed pyramid.

You can place a golden brick as soon as one becomes available. This contributes to construction and can provide a valuable coin reward.

Alternatively, you can reserve golden bricks for visible outer positions if you want a gold exterior or decorative sections.

A co-op crew pursuing a gold-heavy finish deliberately placed golden bricks around the outside rather than spending them indiscriminately on every available position. They continued ordinary production while saving the materials needed for that appearance.

That is a design choice, not a requirement for completing the standard pyramid.

For an early solo game, a golden brick's immediate value may be more useful than preserving an elaborate exterior plan. Coins earned now can fund carrying, production, and transport improvements while most of the pyramid remains unfinished.

Later, when gold production becomes more reliable and the factory has enough ordinary bricks, it becomes easier to reserve gold for selected visible areas.

Decide what you want before the affected layers are filled. A construction plan that relies on specific bricks being visible is easiest to follow while you are still placing them.

You do not need a fully golden pyramid to reach the final crown. The objective remains completing every required brick position.

<h2 id="higher-layers" className="scroll-mt-24">
  How do you keep building when the pyramid gets taller?
</h2>

After the foundation, the distance between your factory and the unfinished portion of the pyramid becomes a more important cost.

Carrying bricks manually can still work, particularly for a small number of bricks, but repeatedly travelling from ground level to a higher layer can take more time than producing the batch.

This is where transport equipment starts earning its place in the construction plan.

Conveyors can move bricks toward suitable collection or delivery points, while trebuchets and Pigeon Lofts provide other ways to move them toward the pyramid.

The useful question is no longer just how many bricks your factory makes. It is how many actually reach the unfinished layer.

A strong production area can still leave the construction site nearly idle if bricks accumulate at the bottom.

As the building rises, review your transport route after each major change in height. Equipment that reached one layer may not be positioned well for the next.

Do not rebuild expensive transport equipment every time a layer is finished. Check whether it can still serve the current area or whether a small adjustment would be enough.

Near the top, the number of bricks required becomes much smaller. At that point, moving a large machine for the last few bricks may take more effort than simply carrying the remaining batch yourself.

This is a judgment about your current construction stage, not a reason to abandon an otherwise useful transport system early.

<h2 id="coop-vs-solo" className="scroll-mt-24">
  What changes when you play solo instead of with friends?
</h2>

How They Built the Pyramids supports solo play and co-op with up to eight players on the standard pyramid.

In co-op, you can divide responsibilities. One player can collect clay or manage molds while another moves bricks, trains, or places them on the pyramid.

The main benefit is being able to work on multiple parts of the construction process at the same time.

But more players do not automatically remove every bottleneck. If everyone produces bricks and nobody collects them, the shared operation can still stall. If the crew keeps purchasing equipment without making sure it is supplied, the factory can become complicated before it becomes productive.

The team wallet also affects purchasing decisions. Expensive upgrades may consume money another player was saving for a worker or a transport machine.

Agree on the current limitation before making larger purchases.

Solo play requires more frequent changes of task. You may spend time making bricks, switch to collection, then move to the pyramid to place a batch.

That makes early improvements to carrying capacity, collection, and worker assignments particularly helpful. Each reliable automation step reduces the number of activities you must handle personally.

A complete solo pyramid is possible. The tradeoff is that you need the production system to handle more work while you are occupied elsewhere.

<h2 id="complete-pyramid" className="scroll-mt-24">
  How do you finish the pyramid and place the capstone?
</h2>

The standard pyramid has 26 layers and requires 23,426 bricks.

As you approach the final layers, concentrate on the remaining empty positions rather than continuing to expand the factory automatically.

The top becomes progressively smaller. In one complete solo build, the final layer required only nine bricks.

Once the last construction requirements are met, the game presents the final crowning interaction.

The confirmation screen lets you name the pyramid and includes a `CROWN IT!` button.

Choose the name you want and confirm the crown.

Crowning the pyramid completes the major construction objective and leads into the final results sequence. The game can display information about the completed build, including how the crew constructed it.

If you still cannot crown the pyramid, check that every required layer and placement position has been completed. Being at the top of the building is not the same as having finished every brick.

After the crown, the game continues into another sequence rather than immediately ending at the results screen.

<h2 id="leave-earth" className="scroll-mt-24">
  What do you do after the pyramid is crowned?
</h2>

A spacecraft arrives above the construction site after the pyramid has been completed.

The next task is to pack up the camp. Equipment begins disappearing as the site is cleared, but some structures may still need your help.

Use the hammer interaction shown by the game. In the solo completion sequence, holding Q with the hammer allowed the player to beam up remaining equipment.

Continue cleaning up the camp until the departure sequence can proceed.

This is separate from crowning the pyramid. A completed crown means the building itself is finished; leaving Earth requires continuing through the cleanup and departure.

When the remaining requirements are satisfied, enter the departure sequence and leave with your builders.

The game's corresponding achievements reflect this distinction: Capstone is awarded for crowning the pyramid, while Now You Know requires leaving Earth.

For the exact achievement conditions and the other optional tasks, use the <Link href="/how-they-built-the-pyramids/achievements">achievements checklist</Link>.

<h2 id="grand-pyramid" className="scroll-mt-24">
  What is the Grand Pyramid, and how do you unlock it?
</h2>

After finishing a normal pyramid, you can begin a larger construction project through Grand Pyramid, the game's New Game+ option.

Choose `GRAND PYRAMID` on the new pyramid card after completing the standard build.

Grand Pyramid expands the construction target to **105,995 bricks** and supports crews of up to 16 players on its larger site.

It is a separate, substantially larger undertaking rather than another small layer added to the standard 23,426-brick pyramid.

The same general preparation decisions remain relevant: make enough bricks, improve transport, recruit useful workers, and ensure the materials are reaching the construction area.

Do not treat the normal pyramid's factory size or transport arrangement as automatically sufficient for the larger project.

A reliable production chain will give you a stronger starting point than a large collection of equipment that frequently stalls.

<h2 id="what-to-do-next" className="scroll-mt-24">
  What should you do if your pyramid progress stalls?
</h2>

Look at what is preventing the next brick from reaching an empty position on the pyramid.

If bricks are waiting somewhere in your production line instead of reaching the construction site, check the <Link href="/how-they-built-the-pyramids/automation">automation guide</Link> for machine and transport problems.

If the work depends on Shabti who are idle or handling the wrong materials, use the <Link href="/how-they-built-the-pyramids/shabti">Shabti guide</Link> to correct their assignments.

If construction is progressing normally but you are missing a particular Steam unlock, compare your remaining objectives with the <Link href="/how-they-built-the-pyramids/achievements">achievements checklist</Link>.

Otherwise, keep finishing the current layer. A new upgrade is worth buying when it solves a problem you are actually experiencing, not simply because it has become available.
