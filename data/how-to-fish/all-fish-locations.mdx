<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
*How to Fish* has **49 normal creature entries** for **Collector** and a separate **49-entry Drip collection** for **Fishipedia**.

I open the encyclopedia with `Tab` before starting cleanup.

For ordinary catches, the **Rod and equipped Lure** determine the available creature pool. I do not need to return to a specific island for every normal fish once I own the correct equipment.

The exceptions are:

- **Clam**, which is found on the ground;
- optional creatures summoned with **Boss Lures**;
- story creatures that require a quest item, NPC hand-in, or boss trigger.

My cleanup order is:

**regular Rod and Lure pools**

→ **Boss Lure creatures**

→ **missing story creatures**

→ **Drip variants for Fishipedia**

I still kill every missing creature after catching it. Catching or releasing the correct species without completing the kill does not satisfy the achievement requirement.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

<h2 id="collector-vs-fishipedia">
  How the 49-creature Collector checklist works
</h2>

Both collection achievements use the same encyclopedia, but they check different sides of it.

**Collector** requires all 49 normal creature types.

**Fishipedia** requires the separate Drip variant of every creature.

A normal catch does not fill its Drip slot, and finding a Drip variant does not replace the normal entry. I use `Tab` to identify the exact blank rather than relying only on the total or trying to remember which creatures I have seen.

I do not hold up the story waiting for rare Drip variants. If later Lures, Boss Lures, or quest encounters are still locked, I finish the <Link href="/how-to-fish">main progression route</Link> first.

The <Link href="/how-to-fish/achievements#collector-fishipedia">Achievements guide</Link> places Collector and Fishipedia within the wider 100% route.

<h2 id="all-49-creatures">
  All 49 creatures by Rod, Lure, and special trigger
</h2>

The encyclopedia numbers remain useful when only one or two entries are blank, but the practical question is not always which island I am missing.

For regular creatures, I first identify the correct **Rod and Lure pool**. The island usually tells me where that equipment becomes available, not necessarily the only place where its creatures can be caught.

Boss Lures and story creatures work differently, so I handle them after the regular pools.

<h2 id="missing-creatures">
  The entries I check first near 49/49
</h2>

When only a few entries remain, I stop changing Lures and repeating encounters at random.

The first entries I recheck are:

- **Clam**, because it feels like an opening pickup rather than a collection entry;
- **Brown Crab** and **Blue Crab**, because the early crab species are easy to combine in memory;
- **Sunfish**, because The Old Pike can make the Beginner Boss Lure pool look complete;
- **Flounder**, because it is easy to overlook among ten Professional Lure creatures;
- **Goblin Shark**, because the Scientific Boss Lure is optional;
- whichever story creature still has a blank encyclopedia slot.

I trust the exact blank in `Tab`. Its number tells me which Rod, Lure, Boss Lure, or story trigger I should check next.

<h2 id="cleanup-route">
  Best Collector and Fishipedia cleanup order
</h2>

Once every Rod and Lure tier is available, I clear the inexpensive regular pools first:

**Crab Fishing Rod**

→ **Free and Beginner Lures**

→ **Standard Lure**

→ **Professional Lure**

→ **Scientific Lure**

I inspect `Tab` between pools. If all regular entries are complete, I move to the more expensive or time-consuming checks:

**Beginner Boss Lure**

→ **Standard Boss Lure**

→ **Professional Boss Lure**

→ **Scientific Boss Lure**

→ **remaining story creatures**

There is no reason to repeat an expensive Boss Lure if the blank entry belongs to a regular pool. There is also no reason to continue ordinary fishing if the blank belongs to a quest boss.

After Collector reaches 49/49, I stop repeating normal entries that are already complete and move to the missing Drip variants.

<h2 id="separate-entries">
  Similar creature names can use separate encyclopedia slots
</h2>

Several names are similar enough that one catch can feel as though it should cover both entries.

It does not.

- **Pike** and **The Old Pike** are separate.
- **Piranha** and **Giant Piranha** are separate.
- **Halibut** and **Flounder** are separate.
- Brown Crab, Blue Crab, and Rock Crab each have their own slot.
- **Bowhead Whale** and **Mutated Bowhead Whale** are separate.

When the total looks wrong, I trust the exact encyclopedia name instead of remembering that I have already caught “a Pike,” “a crab,” or “a Whale.”

<h2 id="drip-creatures">
  Fishipedia and faster Drip cleanup
</h2>

A Drip creature comes from the corresponding creature pool but registers on the separate Fishipedia side.

For a regular creature, I equip the same Rod and Lure used for its normal version. For a Boss Lure or story creature, I repeat the corresponding special trigger instead of fishing an unrelated regular pool.

Drip variants are visually distinctive and continue to respawn. Spending one at the Reel of Fortune does not erase its encyclopedia record.

### Cycle ordinary results when hunting one Drip variant

When the only missing result is a Drip variant from a regular Lure pool, I cast as far as possible and watch the creature notification while reeling.

If the result is not Drip, I immediately use the cancel or release input and begin reeling again. With clean timing, the same cast can cycle through several candidates before I need another full cast.

I slow down as soon as a Drip name appears. Repeating the input automatically can release the exact variant I need.

Aggressive creatures require extra caution. A released Tigerfish can leave the water and attack while I am concentrating on the next result. If repeated releases begin filling the area with hostile creatures, I clear them before continuing.

This method only speeds up rarity checks. It does not replace choosing the correct Rod, Lure, Boss Lure, or story trigger.
