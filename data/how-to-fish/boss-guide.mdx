<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
The main *How to Fish* story boss route is:

**Spider Crab → Giant Piranha → Pufferfish → Terrorizing Bird → Mutated Bowhead Whale**

I do not prepare for all five fights the same way.

The **Spider Crab** is mostly a timing lesson: I wait for the opening it creates instead of trying to force a stun.

The **Giant Piranha** is where I start caring much more about food and reliable ranged damage.

The **Pufferfish** taught me that terrain can matter more than another weapon purchase.

For the **Terrorizing Bird**, I catch the Tuna first, then fight from a position where I can use cover.

For the **Mutated Bowhead Whale**, I stay inside the Volcano encounter area instead of trying to escape in the boat.

My biggest rule is simple:

**if the boss has not spawned, I check the quest trigger before I spend more money on combat gear.**
</GuideQuickAnswer>

What repeatedly matters is the hand-in after the fight.

The game often expects:

**finish the local request**

→ **prepare the boss trigger**

→ **kill the target**

→ **keep the important drop**

→ **return to the NPC**

→ **receive the next route**

If I kill something important and the Radar does not update, I check the quest item before assuming I need another boss.

<h2 id="before-spending">
  I check whether the problem is progression or combat before buying anything
</h2>

Some of my worst early spending came from trying to fix the wrong problem.

If the boss is not on screen yet, I do not need more damage.

I am usually missing a quest interaction, bait, required creature, or previous hand-in.

Once the boss has spawned, then equipment starts to matter.

I look at the failed attempt itself.

If recoil or movement is making me miss most of the magazine, I improve control before raw damage.

If I am already landing consistent shots but the health bar barely moves, I start investing in damage.

If I repeatedly die while reloading, I look at magazine capacity and where I am standing when the reload begins.

If I enter the fight hungry or already damaged, I fix that before spending another dollar.

That approach kept me from buying every new item just because a shop had opened.

<h2 id="first-three-bosses">
  The first three bosses punish three different mistakes
</h2>

<h3 id="spider-crab">
  Spider Crab: the opening comes from the boss itself
</h3>

The Spider Crab initially felt much harder than it actually was because I kept asking how I was supposed to stun it.

I tried attacking more aggressively and looked for a separate parry or stun mechanic.

The useful opening happens after the Spider Crab commits to its own attack and ends up vulnerable.

Once I stopped forcing the issue, the fight became much cleaner.

I give it enough room to commit, wait for the vulnerable state, move in for a short burst of damage, and leave before it recovers.

I do not stay beside it trying to squeeze out one more hit.

That was where a lot of my early deaths came from.

The complete Beer, Empty Beer Can, Spider Crab Shell, and Boat Keys sequence is in the <Link href="/how-to-fish/spider-crab">Spider Crab guide</Link>.

<h3 id="giant-piranha">
  Giant Piranha: this is where I stop treating ranged damage as optional
</h3>

The Giant Piranha is a very different fight.

Before it appears, the real problem is the Island 2 Leech quest.

If I am still searching the ground for Leeches or have not received the converted bait, buying a gun does not move the quest forward. I finish the <Link href="/how-to-fish/leech-bait">Leech Bait route</Link> first.

Once the fight is active, I want food and a ranged weapon I can actually keep on target.

The arena gets cluttered quickly.

I found the fight worse when I turned away from the Giant Piranha every time a smaller creature became annoying. That stretched the encounter long enough for hunger, reloads, missed shots, and accumulated damage to become bigger problems.

I deal with immediate threats when necessary, but I keep most of my damage focused on the boss.

The full fight, progression drop, and Island 3 hand-in are in the <Link href="/how-to-fish/giant-piranha">Giant Piranha guide</Link>.

<h3 id="pufferfish">
  Pufferfish: another gun did less for me than better positioning
</h3>

Pufferfish was the point where I stopped assuming that a more expensive weapon automatically meant an easier boss.

There is also no reason to prepare for the fight if I do not have the **Carrot** yet.

Without the Carrot, I am still solving the Tourist&apos;s requirement. I finish the <Link href="/how-to-fish/endangered-fish">Endangered Species route</Link> first.

Once I can trigger the Pufferfish, I use the trees.

Standing in open ground gave the boss too clean a path toward me.

With a tree between us, I can force it to reposition, move around the obstacle, and get a cleaner ranged window.

I do not chase it just because its health is getting low.

A nearly dead Pufferfish can still ruin the attempt if I abandon the positioning that kept me alive.

The complete Carrot setup, fight route, Pufferfish Tail, and Island 4 progression are in the <Link href="/how-to-fish/pufferfish">Pufferfish guide</Link>.

<h2 id="dynamite">
  Dynamite is a cheap burst option when I already know the boss route
</h2>

Dynamite costs **25**, which makes it much easier to experiment with than replacing my main weapon.

I do not use it as my source of sustained damage.

Its value is putting a lot of damage into a short, predictable window.

The placement matters more than the throw.

If I know a boss is going to cross a particular piece of ground, I can prepare that area and continue shooting while the explosive does its job.

I avoid steep slopes and edges where the charge can roll or sit too far from the target.

I also do not bet the whole fight on one explosion.

For normal progression, Dynamite supplements the weapon I already trust.

It becomes more interesting when I return for **Easy**, because killing a boss within 10 seconds rewards front-loaded damage. I keep that achievement-specific setup in the <Link href="/how-to-fish/achievements#easy-fast-boss">Achievements guide</Link>.

<h2 id="terrorizing-bird">
  Terrorizing Bird starts with catching Tuna
</h2>

Island 4 gave me one of the clearest examples of why I read the NPC request before firing at whatever looks threatening.

The local NPC explains that an enormous bird has been terrorizing the island and that it comes back when someone brings **Tuna**.

The Tuna is tied to the **Professional Boss Lure**.

So I treat Tuna as the actual progression item.

My route is:

**get the Professional Boss Lure**

→ **catch Tuna**

→ **use the Tuna to bring back the Terrorizing Bird**

I do not waste ammunition killing ordinary birds and expecting that to trigger the boss.

<h3 id="terrorizing-bird-fight">
  Chasing the bird made the fight harder
</h3>

My worse attempts happened when I kept following the Terrorizing Bird around the island.

Every time I chased it, I gave up the cover and firing lane I had already established.

The fight felt much more controlled once I stopped moving with the bird and made it return to me.

I pick an area where a building, wall, or another solid object is close enough to break the incoming attack.

When the bird approaches, I move out of the line of pressure rather than trying to out-damage it in the open.

As soon as the dangerous pass is over, I come back out and continue shooting.

The point of cover is to create another firing window.

It is not a place to hide until the fight somehow ends.

By Island 4, I am also willing to spend more on the ranged weapon I already know how to use.

This late in the route, I get more value from concentrating money into one reliable gun than from buying several weapons and leaving all of them half-developed.

If my shots are already staying on the bird but the fight is taking too long, that is when I consider more damage.

If I am missing entire bursts as it moves, control still comes first.

<h3 id="birds-head">
  I keep the Bird&apos;s Head before I leave
</h3>

The Terrorizing Bird dying is not the end of the Island 4 progression step.

The NPC wants the **Bird&apos;s Head**.

I make sure I have it before I wander off, sell things, or sail away.

Once I return the Head, I get the coordinates needed to continue.

If the boss is dead but the next route has not appeared, this hand-in is the first thing I check.

<h2 id="mutated-bowhead-whale">
  The final boss route starts with the scientist and a normal Bowhead Whale
</h2>

The military / Volcano section does not immediately throw the final boss at me.

The scientist first asks for help catching a large Whale for an experiment.

That means the normal **Bowhead Whale** is part of the setup.

The progression chain is:

**speak to the scientist**

→ **prepare the required Whale bait**

→ **catch and defeat the Bowhead Whale**

→ **take it to the Volcano**

→ **throw it into the Volcano**

→ **fight the Mutated Bowhead Whale**

I separate those stages because buying more combat gear does nothing if I am still missing the scientist&apos;s experiment step.

<h3 id="volcano-boundary">
  Trying to escape the Volcano reset the fight
</h3>

The Mutated Bowhead Whale initially made the Volcano arena feel too small.

My instinct was to use the boat and create as much distance as possible.

That turned out to be a bad solution.

When I pulled the Whale too far away from the Volcano, the boss **despawned**.

After that happened, I stopped treating the boat as an escape mechanic.

I still create enough distance to read the attack, but I do it inside the intended encounter area.

There is a big difference between:

**making space**

and:

**leaving the fight**

I want the first one without accidentally doing the second.

<h3 id="mutated-whale-fight">
  Surviving the landing is not always the end of the danger
</h3>

By the final boss, I do not want to be testing an unfamiliar weapon.

I bring the ranged setup I have already invested in and know I can control.

One mistake I had to unlearn was rushing back toward the boss the moment the obvious landing attack was over.

The Whale can still move aggressively after coming down.

If I survive the landing and immediately run straight back into point-blank range, I can lose the attempt to the follow-up instead.

I wait long enough to see where the boss is actually moving, then resume ranged damage.

That gives the fight a more deliberate rhythm:

**read the attack**

→ **move through the landing**

→ **watch the follow-up**

→ **shoot once the space is safe again**

I do not need maximum distance.

I need enough distance to understand what is happening.

<h3 id="mutated-whale-loadout">
  The final fight is where I want one mature ranged build
</h3>

The final sequence is not where I spread money across multiple guns.

I want one weapon that already has the control and damage I need.

If recoil is still throwing shots away, I fix that.

If the weapon is stable but the boss health barely moves, I spend on damage.

If reloads are repeatedly getting me killed, I look at magazine capacity and make sure I am not starting reloads in the middle of the Whale&apos;s pressure.

I also handle food before the fight.

If I am constantly stopping to eat, the encounter becomes much longer and gives the boss more chances to punish me.

The goal is not to carry every possible upgrade.

It is to remove the problems that are actually ending the attempt.

<h3 id="handyman">
  Handyman changes the finishing hit, not the whole fight
</h3>

When I come back for **Handyman**, I do not punch the Mutated Bowhead Whale through its entire health bar.

I fight normally first.

Near the end, I become much more careful with damage.

I stop shooting before the boss is actually dead, unequip the weapon, and use bare hands for the **final hit**.

The main danger is accidentally firing one bullet too many.

I would rather leave slightly more health than I think I need than repeat the entire fight because the gun got the kill.

The full achievement route stays in the <Link href="/how-to-fish/achievements#handyman">Handyman section</Link>.

<h2 id="boss-wont-spawn">
  If a boss will not spawn, I check the trigger before changing my build
</h2>

When nothing is appearing, I use a short progression check.

**Spider Crab** — I make sure the Lighthouse Keeper has given me the Empty Beer Can.

**Giant Piranha** — I finish the Leech requirement and get the converted boss bait.

**Pufferfish** — I give the Tourist a valid Endangered Species creature and get the Carrot.

**Terrorizing Bird** — I catch the Tuna with the Professional Boss Lure.

**Mutated Bowhead Whale** — I finish the scientist&apos;s Bowhead Whale and Volcano experiment.

Once the boss is actually attacking me, I stop retracing those steps.

Then I look at the failed attempt itself.

Missing shots tells me to work on control or positioning.

Consistent hits with weak damage tell me to improve damage.

Deaths during reloads tell me to rethink the reload window or magazine setup.

Entering hungry tells me I was not ready to trigger the fight yet.

That is the distinction I use throughout the boss route: I solve the progression requirement first, then spend money only when the fight shows me what I actually need.
