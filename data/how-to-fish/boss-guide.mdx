<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
I treat the main *How to Fish* boss route as five progression fights:

**Spider Crab**

→ **Giant Piranha**

→ **Pufferfish**

→ **Terrorizing Bird**

→ **Mutated Bowhead Whale**

I do not prepare for all five the same way.

For the **Spider Crab**, I stay cheap and punish the opening it creates after attacking.

For the **Giant Piranha**, I make sure the boss can actually be triggered and arrive with food and a usable ranged weapon.

For the **Pufferfish**, I stop treating weapon price as the answer and use the island terrain to control the fight.

For the **Terrorizing Bird**, I prepare the Tuna first and fight around cover.

For the **Mutated Bowhead Whale**, I stay in the volcano fight area and bring my strongest reliable ranged setup.

My biggest rule across every boss is:

**if the boss has not spawned yet, I check the quest trigger before I spend more money on weapons.**

The pattern matters more than memorizing every item here.

For most progression bosses I am doing some version of:

**finish the local NPC requirement**

→ **prepare the boss trigger**

→ **fight**

→ **secure the important boss trophy**

→ **return to the NPC**

→ **take the new route**

That is why killing a boss does not always mean I am finished.

If the Radar or next objective has not changed, I check the hand-in before I start farming or buying upgrades again.

<h2 id="before-bosses">
  I solve the trigger first and the combat problem second
</h2>

The most expensive mistake I made was buying combat upgrades before confirming what was actually blocking me.

Before a story boss, I ask two different questions.

### Has the boss appeared?

If the answer is **no**, I stop shopping.

I am probably missing:

- a quest interaction;
- the correct bait;
- a required creature;
- or a previous hand-in.

A stronger gun cannot make a missing quest trigger work.

### Is the boss already attacking me?

Now I have a combat problem.

My preparation order becomes:

**food**

→ **reliable weapon**

→ **weapon control**

→ **damage**

I do not automatically buy every new attachment.

If recoil is making me miss, control matters more than another damage upgrade.

If I can already stay on target but the boss health barely moves, then damage becomes the better purchase.

If I am dying because I started hungry, I stop spending money and fix the preparation instead.

That distinction saves more money than any single shop recommendation.

<h2 id="spider-crab">
  Spider Crab gets much easier once I stop trying to stun it myself
</h2>

The Spider Crab was my first real combat misunderstanding.

I kept asking:

**How do I stun it?**

I tried attacking more aggressively.

I looked for a separate stun mechanic.

I tried forcing the opening myself.

That was the wrong approach.

The useful window appears after the Spider Crab commits to its own attack and enters a vulnerable stunned state.

My loop is:

**give it room**

→ **let it commit**

→ **wait for the vulnerable state**

→ **deal damage**

→ **back away**

I do not spend heavily before this fight.

Understanding the opening did more for me than trying to brute-force it with early upgrades.

The other place I can get stuck is after the boss dies.

I inspect the drops before selling everything and finish the Lighthouse Keeper hand-in.

That is what turns the boss kill into actual progression.

If the Spider Crab is dead but I still cannot leave the opening area, I check the quest completion rather than looking for another boss.

<h2 id="giant-piranha">
  Giant Piranha is where I stop treating ranged damage as optional
</h2>

The Giant Piranha is my first major Island 2 combat check.

But I separate two problems here.

If I cannot get the boss to appear, that is not a Giant Piranha combat problem.

I finish the <Link href="/how-to-fish/leech-bait">Leech Bait route</Link> first.

Once the bait is already ready, I focus on the fight.

My priorities are simple:

**do not trigger it hungry**

→ **bring a ranged weapon**

→ **keep damage on the boss instead of chasing every distraction**

→ **secure the boss trophy after the kill**

I found the fight much worse when I kept turning away from the main target to clean up every smaller creature around me.

That stretches the fight and gives hunger, damage taken, and missed shots more time to become problems.

If I am dying after the correct boss trigger is already working, I improve the combat setup.

If the boss itself, its trophy, or the Island 3 unlock is blocking me, I switch to the <Link href="/how-to-fish/giant-piranha">Giant Piranha guide</Link>.

<h2 id="pufferfish">
  Pufferfish is the first boss where terrain matters more than another random upgrade
</h2>

The Pufferfish was my biggest early lesson in not equating:

**more expensive gun**

with:

**solved boss**

If I do not have the required boss bait yet, I am still in the Tourist progression chain.

I finish the <Link href="/how-to-fish/endangered-fish">Endangered Fish requirement</Link> instead of buying equipment for a fight I cannot start.

Once the fight is available, my most important change is positional.

I stop standing in open ground.

I use the trees to interrupt the Pufferfish's direct approach and create safe shooting windows.

The short version is:

**keep terrain between us**

→ **make the boss reposition**

→ **shoot during that opening**

→ **move again before it closes cleanly**

That principle matters more here than repeating every weapon and attachment price.

If I am still struggling after the fight is available, I use the <Link href="/how-to-fish/pufferfish">Pufferfish guide</Link> for the Carrot setup, tree-circling route, later phase, Pufferfish Tail, and Island 4 hand-in.

<h2 id="dynamite">
  Dynamite is my cross-boss burst option
</h2>

Dynamite is useful because it solves a different problem from a normal gun.

I am not buying it for sustained damage.

I am buying it to put a large amount of damage into a short opening.

It becomes available early enough to matter and costs **25**, so experimenting with it does not require the same investment as replacing my main weapon.

I can set it up and trigger the explosion rather than waiting until the boss is already on top of me.

The biggest mistake is assuming one explosive solves every encounter.

If I want to build around Dynamite, I prefer several useful blast positions rather than putting everything into one badly placed charge.

I also avoid slopes and edges where the explosive can end up away from the boss.

The placement question I ask is:

**Where is the boss actually going to move?**

not:

**Where can I throw this most easily?**

Dynamite is also one of the first tools I consider when I later come back for a fast boss kill.

When I come back specifically for **Easy — kill a boss within 10 seconds**, I follow the setup in the <Link href="/how-to-fish/achievements">Achievements guide</Link>.

<h2 id="terrorizing-bird">
  Terrorizing Bird starts with Tuna, then I fight around cover
</h2>

The Terrorizing Bird has one of the clearest boss triggers in the game.

The island NPC tells me that the bird comes back when someone brings:

**Tuna**

and points me toward the **Professional Boss Lure**.

So I do not stand around shooting normal birds.

I catch the Tuna first and use it to start the real encounter.

Once the boss appears, I do not chase it across the island.

I establish a position near cover and make the bird come back into my firing lane.

The ranged attack is much easier to handle when I am already close to something solid.

My loop becomes:

**stay close to cover**

→ **watch and listen for the incoming attack**

→ **break line of attack**

→ **come back out**

→ **keep dealing ranged damage**

I still need enough damage to finish the fight.

Cover keeps me alive, but hiding forever does not progress the boss.

By this stage I am more willing to spend aggressively on my main weapon because the remaining story fights are getting much more punishing.

After the Terrorizing Bird dies, I do not leave the body behind.

The NPC specifically wants the:

**bird's head**

I keep it and complete the hand-in before expecting the next coordinates.

If the boss is dead but the next route has not appeared, the bird's head is the first progression item I check.

<h2 id="mutated-bowhead-whale">
  Mutated Bowhead Whale is where I stop trying to escape the arena
</h2>

The final major boss comes out of the military / scientist progression chain.

The route leading into it is:

**work with the scientist**

→ **prepare the Whale bait**

→ **defeat the Whale**

→ **take it to the volcano**

→ **throw it in**

→ **fight the Mutated Bowhead Whale**

The important distinction is that the normal Whale is part of the setup.

**Mutated Bowhead Whale** is the real final combat wall.

My first instinct was to create as much distance as possible.

That did not work well.

Trying to drag the boss away can break the encounter, and the boat does not become a reliable safe zone just because I am off the beach.

The Mutated Bowhead Whale can still close distance and land close enough to turn a defensive retreat into another death.

So I stop treating the boat as part of the combat plan.

I fight inside the intended area.

The other mistake is relying on an early-game weapon simply because it worked against earlier bosses.

By the final sequence I want:

**reliable sustained ranged damage**

→ **enough control to stay on target**

→ **food / healing already prepared**

→ **room to move after a landing**

The dangerous moment is not always the first impact.

I give the boss space after it lands because an immediate follow-up movement can punish me before I recover.

I do not rush back into point-blank range just because I survived the landing.

My cycle is:

**keep enough distance to read the attack**

→ **move through the landing**

→ **wait for the follow-up movement**

→ **resume ranged damage**

If I spend most of the fight trying to heal, I take that as a sign that either my damage is too low or I am standing somewhere too dangerous.

If I am going for **Handyman**, I still fight the Mutated Bowhead Whale normally until its health is low.

When I am ready to attempt the bare-hands finish, I follow the <Link href="/how-to-fish/achievements#handyman">Handyman strategy</Link>.

<h2 id="which-boss-needs-more-money">
  I spend more only when the fight tells me what I am missing
</h2>

My spending curve across the five bosses is not:

**new island = buy everything**

It is closer to:

**Spider Crab**

→ learn the opening before spending heavily

**Giant Piranha**

→ make sure I have functional ranged damage

**Pufferfish**

→ fix control and positioning before blindly adding damage

**Terrorizing Bird**

→ invest more confidently in the weapon I already trust

**Mutated Bowhead Whale**

→ arrive with a mature ranged build instead of improvising during the final fight

I also stop spreading money across multiple weapons late in the game.

One gun that I can control and have properly upgraded is more useful to me than several unfinished weapons competing for the same cash.

<h2 id="when-the-boss-is-not-the-problem">
  If the boss will not appear, I stop buying weapons
</h2>

This is the rule I use whenever progression feels stuck.

If the boss is **not on the screen**, I check the trigger.

For the Giant Piranha, I check whether the Leech-bait chain is actually complete.

For the Pufferfish, I check whether I have finished the Tourist requirement and obtained the boss bait.

For the Terrorizing Bird, I check the Tuna setup.

For the final sequence, I go back through the scientist and volcano steps.

If the boss is **already fighting me**, then I diagnose combat:

**am I missing shots?**

→ control

**am I landing shots but doing too little damage?**

→ damage

**am I repeatedly dying while reloading?**

→ magazine / positioning

**am I entering the fight hungry or already damaged?**

→ preparation

That distinction keeps me from spending hundreds on a weapon upgrade when the real problem is one missing quest interaction.
