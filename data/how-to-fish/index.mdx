<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
The main *How to Fish* progression route is:

**Lighthouse**

→ **Spider Crab and Boat Keys**

→ **Island 2: Leeches and Giant Piranha**

→ **Island 3: Endangered Species and Pufferfish**

→ **Island 4: Tuna and Terrorizing Bird**

→ **military / Volcano area**

→ **scientist&apos;s Bowhead Whale experiment**

→ **Mutated Bowhead Whale**

→ **final route back toward the mainland**

The story is driven much more by NPC requests and coordinates than by free exploration.

If the next boss has not appeared, I check the current quest, bait, required creature, or hand-in before spending more money on combat gear.

If the boss is already attacking me, then I know the progression requirement worked and I can focus on the fight.

<h2 id="how-progression-works">
  How progression works in How to Fish
</h2>

*How to Fish* looks open-ended when I first get off the dock.

I can catch creatures, sell them, buy weapons, gamble, cook, upgrade the boat, and eventually sail between islands.

The story itself is much more structured.

Most of my progress comes from following an NPC chain:

**find the NPC who is blocking progression**

→ **complete the creature or bait requirement**

→ **trigger the important fight**

→ **keep whatever the NPC needs afterward**

→ **finish the hand-in**

→ **receive the next coordinates**

That last step caused more confusion for me than the basic island order.

A boss being dead does not always mean the island is finished.

If the Radar still has not given me somewhere new to go, I check the boss drop and the NPC before I start farming money or sailing around randomly.

<h2 id="how-many-islands">
  How many islands are there?
</h2>

I treat the main story as **five major island stops** before the final route toward the mainland:

1. **Lighthouse / starting island**
2. **Island 2 / Forest**
3. **Island 3 / Desert**
4. **Island 4 / Rocks**
5. **Island 5 / military and Volcano area**

I do not unlock the full route simply by exploring farther into the ocean.

The game gradually gives me the coordinates I need as I finish each progression chain.

That means seeing another island in the distance is less important than making sure the current NPC has actually finished with me.

<h2 id="lighthouse">
  Lighthouse: get the boat and learn how quest hand-ins work
</h2>

The starting island teaches the basic economy.

I catch creatures, kill them, inspect their value, sell what I do not need, and use the money to get started.

I do not stay here trying to build a perfect weapon.

The important progression character is the **Lighthouse Keeper**.

His Beer problem leads into the **Spider Crab**, and completing that sequence is what eventually gives me access to the boat.

The part that initially confused me is that the Spider Crab itself is only part of the chain.

The route includes the Beer setup, the boss, its quest drop, and the return to the Lighthouse Keeper.

If any of those steps are still unclear, I use the <Link href="/how-to-fish/spider-crab">Spider Crab and Boat Keys guide</Link> for the complete opening-island route.

Once I have the boat, I start treating the **Radar and coordinates** as my main progression tools rather than trying to navigate by guesswork.

<h2 id="radar-coordinates">
  Radar and coordinates tell me whether an island is actually finished
</h2>

The Radar gives me a useful way to diagnose stalled progression.

If I think I have finished an island but nothing new appears, I retrace the latest objective.

The usual causes are simple:

- the important boss drop is still on the ground;
- I picked it up but never returned to the NPC;
- an NPC still needs a creature;
- I have the correct bait but have not used it to trigger the target;
- or I have not yet collected the coordinates from the finished quest.

This becomes increasingly important later because the main route is not always one boss followed immediately by another island.

Some sections contain an NPC requirement, a special catch, a boss, and another hand-in before the Radar finally changes.

<h2 id="island-2">
  Island 2: Leeches come before the Giant Piranha
</h2>

Island 2 is the first place where I found it easy to work on the wrong problem.

The local progression starts with a **three-Leech requirement**.

If that counter is still unfinished, I am not at the Giant Piranha fight yet.

The Leeches are ground creatures rather than something I solve by repeatedly casting into the water, so I finish that requirement first.

The <Link href="/how-to-fish/leech-bait">Leech Bait guide</Link> covers where I look for them, how I complete the three-Leech objective, and how that quest becomes the bait needed for the next stage.

Once the bait is ready, I stop treating Leeches as my blocker.

Now the problem is the **Giant Piranha**.

The <Link href="/how-to-fish/giant-piranha">Giant Piranha guide</Link> covers the fight, the important progression drop, and the hand-in that leads to Island 3.

Before leaving Island 2, I want the new coordinates to exist.

If the boss is dead but the Radar still points nowhere new, I check the hand-in instead of continuing to farm the island.

<h2 id="island-3">
  Island 3: the Tourist comes before the Pufferfish
</h2>

Island 3 also contains two different progression problems.

The first is not the Pufferfish.

It is the **Tourist**.

He wants an **Endangered Species** creature before I can get the item needed for the scary fish.

If I do not have the **Carrot**, I am still solving that requirement.

The <Link href="/how-to-fish/endangered-fish">Endangered Fish guide</Link> covers the valid catch, the Tourist hand-in, and getting the Carrot.

Once the Carrot is already in my inventory, I stop searching for another endangered creature.

The game state has changed.

Now the blocker is the **Pufferfish**.

The <Link href="/how-to-fish/pufferfish">Pufferfish guide</Link> covers the Carrot trigger, the fight itself, the Pufferfish Tail, and the progression that opens Island 4.

This is one of the habits that saves me the most time in *How to Fish*:

**when the quest state changes, I stop solving the previous problem.**

<h2 id="island-4">
  Island 4: Tuna is the Terrorizing Bird trigger
</h2>

Island 4 changes the boss setup again.

The local NPC tells me that an enormous bird is terrorizing the island and that it comes back when someone brings **Tuna**.

That means I do not progress by shooting the ordinary birds flying around the area.

I need the Tuna.

The Tuna is tied to the **Professional Boss Lure**.

Once I catch one, it becomes part of the boss progression rather than something I immediately sell or eat.

The full Terrorizing Bird fight is in the <Link href="/how-to-fish/boss-guide#terrorizing-bird">Boss Guide</Link>.

For progression, the important part after the fight is the **Bird&apos;s Head**.

The NPC wants it, and returning it gives me the coordinates that move the story forward.

If the Terrorizing Bird is dead but the next route has not appeared, the Bird&apos;s Head hand-in is the first thing I check.

<h2 id="military-volcano">
  Military and Volcano: follow the scientist&apos;s experiment in order
</h2>

The military / Volcano section is the part of the story I found least useful to brute-force.

I can see the Volcano before I fully understand what the game wants me to do with it.

Throwing random creatures into the lava is not the progression route.

I follow the military objective until it brings me to the **scientist**.

Her experiment has several steps.

I first bring the **smaller creatures she asks for**.

That moves the experiment forward and eventually gives me the setup I need for the Whale.

Then I get the appropriate **Whale bait**, catch the **Bowhead Whale**, and defeat it.

The Bowhead Whale is not just another valuable late-game creature.

I need it for the experiment.

So I do not sell it.

I take the defeated Whale to the **Volcano** and throw it in.

That is what advances the experiment into the final major boss sequence.

The important order is:

**scientist**

→ **smaller-creature requests**

→ **Whale bait**

→ **Bowhead Whale**

→ **take the defeated Whale to the Volcano**

→ **throw it in**

→ **Mutated Bowhead Whale**

This section behaves differently from the earlier trophy hand-ins.

With the Spider Crab, Giant Piranha, and Terrorizing Bird, I care about returning an important drop to an NPC.

Here, the **Whale itself** is what needs to reach the Volcano.

<h2 id="mutated-bowhead-whale">
  Mutated Bowhead Whale is the final major combat check
</h2>

Once the Volcano experiment advances, I reach the **Mutated Bowhead Whale**.

At that point I stop troubleshooting the scientist&apos;s request and start treating the problem as combat.

This fight also has one unusual limitation: trying to pull the Whale too far away from the Volcano can reset the encounter.

The <Link href="/how-to-fish/boss-guide#mutated-bowhead-whale">Mutated Bowhead Whale section</Link> covers the fight area, the landing and follow-up timing, weapon decisions, and why I do not use the boat to drag the boss away.

After the final combat sequence and remaining military progression are complete, the game opens the route that takes me back toward the mainland.

<h2 id="upgrade-priority">
  I buy upgrades when I can name the problem they solve
</h2>

I do not use a fixed rule like:

**new island = buy the newest weapon**

My spending changes with the situation.

On the opening island, understanding the Spider Crab opening can matter more than adding another small amount of damage.

By the Giant Piranha, reliable ranged damage becomes much more important.

Against the Pufferfish, positioning can improve an attempt more than another random weapon purchase.

Later in the game, once I know which ranged weapon I actually like, I concentrate my money there instead of spreading upgrades across several unfinished guns.

I use the failed attempt to decide what I need.

If recoil or movement is making me miss most of the magazine, I care about **control**.

If my shots are landing but the boss health is moving too slowly, I care about **damage**.

If I repeatedly die while reloading, I look at **magazine capacity and reload positioning**.

If I keep starting fights hungry, the problem is not the shop. I bring food.

Boat upgrades make more sense once repeated travel between unlocked islands is actually becoming a time cost.

I want every purchase to answer:

**What problem does this fix right now?**

If I cannot answer that, I usually keep the money.

<h2 id="all-fish">
  I do not force the full creature collection during the story
</h2>

Completing the story does not automatically complete the encyclopedia.

There are **49 normal creature entries**, and some are much easier to miss than the normal fish I catch while progressing.

Several come from different lure pools, optional catches, or creature types that do not feel like obvious fishing targets.

Once all five main areas are accessible, cleanup becomes much easier because I can work through the collection systematically instead of interrupting every story objective.

The <Link href="/how-to-fish/all-fish-locations">All 49 Creatures guide</Link> covers the normal collection by island, lure, and special trigger.

I also keep that separate from **Drip creatures**.

The game tracks normal creatures and Drip variants independently, so completing the normal collection does not automatically complete Fishipedia.

<h2 id="after-story">
  Story completion and 100% completion are different goals
</h2>

When the main route is finished, I stop treating every remaining objective as progression.

*How to Fish* has **28 achievements**, and several of them ask me to play in ways that have little to do with reaching the next island.

The remaining goals include things such as:

- Killscore conditions;
- cooking;
- casino and money challenges;
- weapon and boat upgrades;
- normal creature collection;
- Drip creature collection;
- killing a boss quickly;
- finishing the game within one hour;
- and the final-boss bare-hands requirement.

I use the <Link href="/how-to-fish/achievements">How to Fish Achievements guide</Link> for that cleanup.

Keeping those goals separate lets me finish the first run without constantly stopping for roulette, Drip farming, trick shots, or challenge kills.

<h2 id="where-to-go-when-stuck">
  Where to go when progression stops
</h2>

When nothing seems to be moving, I use the current game state rather than replaying every previous step.

If I am still on the **Lighthouse** without a usable boat, I check the <Link href="/how-to-fish/spider-crab">Lighthouse Keeper and Spider Crab route</Link>.

If I am on **Island 2** and the three-Leech requirement is unfinished, I use the <Link href="/how-to-fish/leech-bait">Leech Bait guide</Link>.

If the correct bait is already ready and the Giant Piranha is now the blocker, I switch to the <Link href="/how-to-fish/giant-piranha">Giant Piranha guide</Link>.

If I reach **Island 3** without a Carrot, I am still solving the <Link href="/how-to-fish/endangered-fish">Tourist&apos;s Endangered Species request</Link>.

If I already have the Carrot, I stop fishing for that prerequisite and move to the <Link href="/how-to-fish/pufferfish">Pufferfish</Link>.

On **Island 4**, I make sure I have the Tuna before expecting the Terrorizing Bird.

In the **military / Volcano area**, I return to the scientist if I have not yet reached the Mutated Bowhead Whale.

If one of the major bosses is already active and combat is the actual blocker, I use the <Link href="/how-to-fish/boss-guide">Boss Guide</Link>.

The question I keep coming back to is:

**Is the game waiting for a progression requirement, or am I actually losing a fight?**

If the target has not appeared, I retrace the quest.

If it is already attacking me, I stop searching for another quest step and work on food, positioning, weapon control, reload timing, or damage.
