<h2 id="how-many-islands">
  How many islands are there in How to Fish?
</h2>

For the main story, I treat *How to Fish* as **five major island stops**:

1. **Lighthouse / starting island** — Spider Crab, Boat Keys, and Radar.
2. **Island 2 / Forest** — three Leeches, Giant Piranha, and the route to Island 3.
3. **Island 3 / Desert** — Tourist, Endangered Species, Carrot, and Pufferfish.
4. **Island 4 / Rocks** — Tuna and the Terrorizing Bird.
5. **Island 5 / military and Volcano area** — scientist's experiment, Bowhead
   Whale, Volcano, and Mutated Bowhead Whale.

After that sequence, the story opens the final route back toward the mainland.

I do not unlock the full chain simply by exploring farther from shore. Each
major stop gives me something that makes the next one usable: Boat Keys,
coordinates, bait, a quest item, or another NPC objective.

So when I can see another island but cannot make meaningful progress there, I
go back to the current quest rather than assuming I need a better boat.

<h2 id="how-progression-works">
  How island progression works
</h2>

*How to Fish* looks much more open-ended than its story actually is.

I can spend a long time fishing, cooking, gambling, upgrading weapons, improving
the boat, and filling the creature collection, but none of those automatically
move me to the next island.

Most of the main route follows the same underlying pattern:

**meet the progression NPC → complete the creature or bait requirement →
trigger the important encounter → keep the quest item afterward → return to the
NPC → receive the next coordinates.**

The hand-in is the part I watch most carefully.

Several times, killing the boss felt like the obvious end of an island, but the
Radar did not change until I collected the correct drop and finished the NPC
conversation.

That gives me one useful rule for the whole game:

**boss dead does not always mean island finished.**

<h2 id="lighthouse">
  Island 1: Spider Crab, Boat Keys and Radar
</h2>

The Lighthouse is the starting island and teaches me how the rest of the
progression works.

I can catch creatures, inspect their value, sell what I do not need, and buy
some early equipment, but I do not stay here trying to build a perfect combat
setup.

The important NPC is the **Lighthouse Keeper**.

His Beer problem leads to the **Spider Crab**, and that chain eventually gives
me the **Boat Keys** I need to leave.

The boss itself is only part of the sequence. I still have to handle the Beer
and Empty Beer Can setup, defeat the Spider Crab, keep its Shell, and finish the
Lighthouse Keeper hand-in.

The
<Link href="/how-to-fish/spider-crab">
  {" "}Spider Crab boss guide
</Link>
covers the Empty Beer Can, the Crab's self-stun opening, the Shell, and the Boat
Keys.

Before I leave, I also want the **Radar**.

The Boat Keys let me sail. The Radar turns that freedom into an actual route.

<h2 id="radar-coordinates">
  Radar and coordinates tell me when an island is finished
</h2>

Once I have the boat, I start using the Radar as a progression check rather than
trying to navigate the entire game by sight.

If I think I have completed an island but nothing new appears, I look at the
most recent quest state.

The usual problems are:

- an important boss drop is still on the ground;
- I picked it up but never returned it to the NPC;
- the NPC still needs a creature;
- I obtained the correct bait but never used it to trigger the target;
- or the quest finished but I have not collected the new coordinates.

Later islands make this even more important because the route can contain an NPC
request, a special catch, a boss, and another hand-in before the Radar finally
changes.

I do not repeat a completed boss just because the next destination has not
appeared.

I check the hand-in first.

<h2 id="island-2">
  Island 2: Leeches, Giant Piranha and how to unlock Island 3
</h2>

Island 2 is where I first found it easy to work on the wrong problem.

The local progression begins with a **three-Leech requirement**.

Those Leeches are ground creatures, so repeatedly casting into the water does
not move the objective forward.

If the counter is still incomplete, I am not at the Giant Piranha stage yet.

The
<Link href="/how-to-fish/leech-bait">
  {" "}Leech Bait guide
</Link>
covers the three-Leech requirement and how completing it produces the bait
needed for the next part of the island.

Once that bait is ready, I stop searching for Leeches.

The blocker has changed to the **Giant Piranha**.

The
<Link href="/how-to-fish/giant-piranha">
  {" "}Giant Piranha guide
</Link>
covers the fight and the important progression item afterward.

Before I leave Island 2, I make sure the hand-in is finished and the new
coordinates exist.

If the Giant Piranha is already dead but the Radar still has no new destination,
I do not keep farming the island. I check what happened after the kill.

<h2 id="island-3">
  Island 3: Endangered Species, Pufferfish and how to unlock Island 4
</h2>

Island 3 has two separate progression gates, and the order matters.

The first blocker is the **Tourist**, not the Pufferfish.

He wants an **Endangered Species** creature. Completing that request gives me
the **Carrot** needed for the boss.

If I do not have the Carrot yet, the
<Link href="/how-to-fish/endangered-fish">
  {" "}Endangered Fish guide
</Link>
covers the catch, the Tourist hand-in, and the item I am waiting for.

Once the Carrot is in my inventory, I stop trying to solve the endangered
creature requirement.

Now the progression problem is the **Pufferfish**.

I use the
<Link href="/how-to-fish/pufferfish">
  {" "}Pufferfish boss guide
</Link>
for the actual fight: triggering it with the Carrot, using the trees to break
its charges, surviving the later phase, and collecting the Pufferfish Tail.

That Tail matters because killing the boss alone does not unlock the next
island.

I bring it back to the Tourist and wait for the **Flash Drive with coordinates
to Island 4**.

Once I have those coordinates, Island 3 progression is finished.

<h2 id="island-4">
  Island 4: Tuna, Terrorizing Bird and the next coordinates
</h2>

Island 4 changes the trigger again.

The local NPC tells me that an enormous bird is terrorizing the island and that
it returns when someone brings **Tuna**.

So shooting ordinary birds around the area is not the progression solution.

I need the Tuna.

It is tied to the **Professional Boss Lure**, and once I catch one for this
objective I treat it as boss progression rather than ordinary food or something
to sell.

The actual fight belongs in the
<Link href="/how-to-fish/boss-guide#terrorizing-bird">
  {" "}Terrorizing Bird strategy
</Link>

For the island route, the important item comes afterward:

**Bird's Head.**

I return that to the NPC to receive the coordinates that move the story
forward.

If the Terrorizing Bird is already dead but nothing new appears on the Radar,
the Bird's Head hand-in is the first thing I check.

<h2 id="military-volcano">
  Island 5: Military, Volcano and the scientist's Bowhead Whale experiment
</h2>

The military and Volcano section is less intuitive than the earlier islands
because I can see parts of the area before I understand what they are for.

The Volcano in particular looks like something I should experiment with.

I do not progress by throwing random creatures into it.

I follow the military route until it brings me to the **scientist**.

Her requests lead through several stages.

I first bring the **smaller creatures she asks for**.

That advances the experiment until I can get the appropriate **Whale bait**.

Then I catch and defeat the **Bowhead Whale**.

This time, I do not treat the defeated creature like an ordinary boss drop.

The Whale itself is part of the experiment.

I take it to the **Volcano** and throw it in.

That advances the route into the final major boss sequence:

**scientist → smaller-creature requests → Whale bait → Bowhead Whale → take
Whale to Volcano → throw it in → Mutated Bowhead Whale.**

This is different from the earlier islands.

With the Spider Crab, Giant Piranha, Pufferfish, and Terrorizing Bird, I am
usually thinking about a bait requirement or an important trophy hand-in.

Here, the **Bowhead Whale itself** has to reach the Volcano.

<h2 id="mutated-bowhead-whale">
  Mutated Bowhead Whale is the final major combat check
</h2>

Once the experiment reaches the **Mutated Bowhead Whale**, I know the
progression setup has worked.

At that point I stop searching for another scientist objective and treat the
problem as combat.

One unusual part of this fight is the arena itself. Pulling the Whale too far
away from the Volcano can reset the encounter, so I do not try to solve it by
dragging the boss across the map with the boat.

The
<Link href="/how-to-fish/boss-guide#mutated-bowhead-whale">
  {" "}Mutated Bowhead Whale strategy
</Link>
covers the fight area, attack timing, weapon decisions, and the reset problem.

After the remaining military progression is complete, the game opens the route
back toward the mainland.

<h2 id="upgrade-priority">
  When I spend money on weapons and upgrades
</h2>

I do not use a rule like:

**new island = buy the newest weapon.**

The useful upgrade depends on why the current attempt failed.

Early on, understanding the Spider Crab's opening can matter more than adding a
small amount of damage.

By the Giant Piranha, reliable ranged damage matters much more.

Against the Pufferfish, positioning around the trees can improve the fight more
than another random attachment.

Later, once I know which ranged weapon I actually like, I would rather
concentrate money there than spread upgrades across several guns I am not going
to use.

If recoil or movement makes me miss most of a magazine, I improve **control**.

If the shots are landing but the health bar barely moves, I look at **damage**.

If reloads repeatedly leave me exposed, I care more about **magazine capacity
and reload positioning**.

And if I keep entering fights hungry, I buy food instead of pretending the gun
is the problem.

Boat upgrades become more attractive when repeated travel between already
unlocked islands starts costing enough time to matter.

<h2 id="all-fish">
  I leave the full creature collection until the islands are open
</h2>

Finishing the story does not automatically finish the creature collection.

There are **49 normal creature entries**, including catches that are much easier
to miss than the ordinary fish I encounter while progressing.

Some come from different lure pools, optional catches, or creature types that do
not initially feel like normal fishing targets.

Once all five major areas are accessible, cleanup becomes much easier because I
can work through the collection deliberately rather than interrupting every
story objective.

The
<Link href="/how-to-fish/all-fish-locations">
  {" "}All 49 Creatures guide
</Link>
organizes that collection by island, lure, and special trigger.

I also treat normal creatures and **Drip creatures** separately because the game
tracks those variants independently.

<h2 id="after-story">
  Story completion and 100% completion are different
</h2>

When the main route is finished, I stop treating every remaining objective as
something required for progression.

*How to Fish* has **28 achievements**, and several ask me to play very
differently from the normal island route.

They include challenges involving Killscore, cooking, money, casino activity,
weapon and boat upgrades, creature collection, Drip creatures, fast boss kills,
a sub-one-hour completion, and the final-boss bare-hands requirement.

I handle those separately with the
<Link href="/how-to-fish/achievements">
  {" "}How to Fish Achievements guide
</Link>

That keeps the first story run focused instead of turning every island into a
checklist for unrelated challenge conditions.

<h2 id="where-to-go-when-stuck">
  Where to go next when progression stops
</h2>

When I get stuck, I look at the current game state rather than replaying the
entire story in my head.

On the **Lighthouse**, no usable boat usually sends me back to the Lighthouse
Keeper and Spider Crab chain.

On **Island 2**, an unfinished three-Leech counter means I am still on the
Leech requirement. If the bait is already ready, the Giant Piranha is the next
problem.

On **Island 3**, no Carrot means I am still working for the Tourist. With the
Carrot already in my inventory, I move on to the Pufferfish.

On **Island 4**, I make sure the Tuna requirement is finished before expecting
the Terrorizing Bird.

In the **military / Volcano area**, I return to the scientist if the Bowhead
Whale experiment has not yet reached its final stage.

The distinction that saves me the most wasted time is:

**Has the target appeared yet?**

If not, I am probably missing a quest, bait, creature, drop, hand-in, or
coordinate.

If the boss is already attacking me, I stop searching for another progression
step and work on the fight.
