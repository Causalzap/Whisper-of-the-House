<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
**How to Fish has five main island stops:** Lighthouse, Forest, Desert, Rocks, and the Military/Volcano area.

Follow this story boss order:

**Spider Crab → Giant Piranha → Pufferfish → Terrorizing Bird → Mutated Bowhead Whale.**

To reach the next island, complete the local NPC request, defeat the required boss, collect its quest drop, and return that item to the NPC. Then use the Radar to find your next destination.

**If your Radar is not showing the next island, check the unfinished quest hand-in before fighting the boss again.**
</GuideQuickAnswer>

<h2 id="how-many-islands">
  How many islands are in How to Fish?
</h2>

There are **five main island stops** in the story, followed by the final trip toward the mainland.

They open in this order:

1. **Island 1 — Lighthouse:** Get the Empty Beer Can, defeat the Spider Crab, and unlock the boat.
2. **Island 2 — Forest:** Collect three Leeches, defeat the Giant Piranha, and receive the Island 3 coordinates.
3. **Island 3 — Desert:** Help the Tourist, obtain the Carrot, and defeat the Pufferfish.
4. **Island 4 — Rocks:** Catch Tuna, summon the Terrorizing Bird, and unlock the final island.
5. **Island 5 — Military & Volcano:** Complete the Scientist's experiment, defeat the Mutated Bowhead Whale, and obtain the route home.

The fifth stage includes the military settlement, the Scientist's quest, and the Volcano. It is one connected final progression stage rather than three additional numbered islands.

You can return to previously unlocked areas for fishing, equipment, and optional activities. You do not need to complete every collection or shop purchase before moving forward.

<h2 id="how-progression-works">
  How island progression works
</h2>

The most important rule in How to Fish is that **defeating a boss does not always complete the island's quest**.

Each stage has a specific requirement that opens the next destination. Sometimes that means obtaining special bait before the boss will appear. Other times the fight is already finished, but the NPC is still waiting for the trophy.

The route I follow is straightforward: speak to the local quest giver, finish the requested collection or bait step, defeat the story target, and bring back the required item. Only after receiving the next coordinates do I return to the boat.

For example, killing the Giant Piranha on Island 2 is not the same as unlocking Island 3. The Giant Piranha Skeleton must go back to the woman who requested it. The reward is the Flash Drive containing the next coordinates.

This also changes how I decide what to buy. If the quest is waiting for Leeches or an endangered creature, a stronger gun will not solve the problem. If the boss has spawned but I cannot survive the fight, equipment and positioning become the priority.

The distinction saves a lot of unnecessary fishing and spending throughout the game.

<h2 id="lighthouse">
  Island 1: Lighthouse — Get the Boat Keys
</h2>

The opening island teaches fishing, creature handling, selling, and combat, but the actual progression objective is to help the Lighthouse Keeper with the creature stealing his Beer.

I start by gathering Clams and using the available fishing equipment to earn enough money for the opening purchases. A Crab Fishing Rod is useful for catching and selling crustaceans, while an inexpensive melee weapon makes the first boss much more manageable.

The first important purchase is **Beer**. I bring it to the Lighthouse Keeper rather than drinking it. He returns an **Empty Beer Can**, which I equip as bait to summon the Spider Crab.

The Spider Crab is a fight where timing matters more than buying every available upgrade. It can briefly daze itself after committing to an attack. I wait for that opening, deal damage, and move away before it recovers.

Once the boss dies, I collect the **Spider Crab Shell** and return it to the Lighthouse Keeper.

He gives me the **Boat Keys**, which allow me to use the motorboat. Before leaving, I buy the **Radar for $10**.

At this point the essential checklist is complete: Boat Keys obtained, Radar purchased, and the next island available to navigate toward.

If the Spider Crab is not appearing, the <Link href="/how-to-fish/spider-crab">Spider Crab and Empty Beer Can guide</Link> explains the bait requirement, boss opening, and Boat Keys hand-in.

<h2 id="radar-coordinates">
  How to use Radar and unlock the next island
</h2>

The Radar is the navigation tool for the island route. I buy it from the Lighthouse Keeper on the opening island and use its markers to find destinations while sailing.

The crucial distinction is that **the Radar displays unlocked destinations; it does not unlock them by itself**.

When I finish an island's required hand-in, I receive the next coordinates or progression reward. I then check the Radar and steer toward the new destination.

If I cannot find a new marker, I check three things before sailing around the ocean:

- **Do I own the Radar?** The opening island has a separate $10 purchase, even after the Boat Keys are unlocked.
- **Did I return the required boss item?** A Shell, Skeleton, Tail, or Bird's Head may still be waiting for its NPC.
- **Did I collect the next coordinates?** On the middle islands, the Flash Drive is the important confirmation that progression has advanced.

I do not assume that killing a boss, entering the boat, or buying a faster engine automatically reveals the next island.

<GuideCallout title="Radar still showing the old island?">
After the Giant Piranha, confirm that the Skeleton was returned and the Island 3 Flash Drive was received. After the Pufferfish, check the Tail hand-in and Island 4 Flash Drive. After the Terrorizing Bird, return its Head to the local NPC.

If the hand-in is already complete, check the Radar again before repeating any boss fight.
</GuideCallout>

The normal story route is the reliable way forward. Older shortcuts that allowed players to reach locked islands were removed in Patch 1.0.5, so trying to sail directly to the Volcano is not a substitute for completing the preceding quests.

<h2 id="island-2">
  Island 2: Forest — Three Leeches and Giant Piranha
</h2>

The Forest is where I stop treating every objective as a fishing problem.

The woman near the settlement wants **three Leeches** so she can prepare bait for a much larger creature. The quest starts with a 0/3 counter.

The Leeches are ground pickups. I search the grass, paths, and nearby terrain instead of repeatedly casting into the water or buying different lures.

Once the counter reaches **3/3**, I return to the woman. She converts the collected Leeches into the special bait needed for the Giant Piranha and warns me to prepare for a dangerous encounter.

The <Link href="/how-to-fish/leech-bait">three-Leech collection route</Link> covers how to recognize the pickups and finish the NPC interaction if the counter is not advancing.

Before triggering the Giant Piranha, I make sure I have food and a reliable ranged weapon. The Forest shop provides an important spending choice: the **150 Shotgun** is more useful than the **230 Big Motor** if I cannot defeat the boss yet.

During the fight, the smaller Piranhas can become distractions. I keep my attention on the Giant Piranha and only clear smaller enemies when they directly interfere with movement or shooting.

After the boss dies, I secure the **Giant Piranha Skeleton**.

That is the progression item I need to bring back to the woman. Completing the hand-in gives me the **Flash Drive containing the Island 3 coordinates**.

I do not leave after merely killing the boss. I leave after the Skeleton has been accepted and the new route is available.

If the Giant Piranha is escaping, overwhelming me with smaller enemies, or surviving repeated attempts, the <Link href="/how-to-fish/giant-piranha">Giant Piranha guide</Link> covers the full combat approach.

<h2 id="island-3">
  Island 3: Desert — Tourist, Endangered Fish, and Pufferfish
</h2>

Island 3 begins with a different kind of requirement.

The Tourist is afraid to enter the water because of a dangerous creature. He has the bait needed to deal with it, but first asks for an **endangered creature**.

I speak to the Tourist before spending money on new Boss Lures. Then I catch and inspect creatures around the island until I find one displaying the **Endangered Species** status.

Needlefish can satisfy this request, but it is not the only possible answer. The important condition is the creature's status, not one fixed species name.

Once I have a valid catch, I keep it intact and return it to the Tourist. In exchange, he gives me the **Carrot**, which is the bait required to trigger the Pufferfish encounter.

The <Link href="/how-to-fish/endangered-fish">Endangered Fish and Tourist guide</Link> covers the inspection requirement and the Carrot exchange.

With the Carrot available, I switch to preparing for the Pufferfish rather than continuing to search for endangered creatures.

This boss becomes more dangerous as the fight progresses, particularly when the purple hazard spreads across the area. I use the trees to interrupt its approach, maintain space, and continue attacking from range.

I do not rush into melee merely because the health bar is almost empty.

Once the Pufferfish dies, I collect its **Tail** and return to the Tourist. The reward is the **Flash Drive with the coordinates to Island 4**.

That hand-in is the completion checkpoint. Without it, killing another Pufferfish or searching for another endangered creature is unlikely to help.

For the complete fight, including the later phase and Tail hand-in, use the <Link href="/how-to-fish/pufferfish">Pufferfish strategy</Link>.

The Desert also has optional activities, including the Grillmaster's Blue Shark request. They are useful for cooking and achievements, but they do not need to delay the main Pufferfish progression.

<h2 id="island-4">
  Island 4: Rocks — Tuna and Terrorizing Bird
</h2>

Island 4 introduces the Terrorizing Bird, but the immediate objective is not to chase the bird around the island.

The local NPC explains that the creature has been terrorizing the area and can be attracted with **Tuna**.

I buy the **Professional Boss Lure**, catch the Tuna, and keep it for the boss trigger instead of selling or eating it.

The Tuna matters because it is what brings the Terrorizing Bird back. Ordinary birds around the island are not substitutes for the story encounter.

Before starting the fight, I choose a position with solid cover nearby. The bird's movement makes it tempting to follow it across the island, but doing so gives up a useful firing position and creates unnecessary exposure.

I stay near cover, attack when the bird presents a safe opening, and avoid spending the entire encounter running after it.

This is also a good point to improve the ranged weapon I have already been using successfully. A stable weapon with better damage and magazine capacity is more valuable than several partly upgraded alternatives.

After defeating the Terrorizing Bird, I collect the **Bird's Head**.

The Head is the important quest drop, not just another trophy to sell. I return it to the local NPC to receive the coordinates for the military and Volcano stage.

If the Bird is dead but the next island does not appear, this is the hand-in I check first.

The <Link href="/how-to-fish/boss-guide">Boss Guide</Link> covers the full Terrorizing Bird fight, including the Tuna trigger, cover, and damage windows.

<h2 id="military-volcano">
  Island 5: Military & Volcano — The Scientist's Experiment
</h2>

The final island starts with the military settlement and a Scientist who needs help conducting an experiment involving a large Whale.

I do not go straight to the Volcano looking for the Mutated Bowhead Whale. There is an earlier task that must be completed.

The Scientist asks for **five smaller creatures** to prepare the bait for the Whale. I gather the required catches and complete the 0/5 hand-in before attempting the next stage.

Once the request is complete, I receive the **Fish Bucket**, which is used to attract the normal Bowhead Whale.

The route then changes from collecting fish to transporting the experiment target.

I catch and defeat the Bowhead Whale, keep its body for the experiment, and take it toward the Volcano rather than treating it as a normal creature to sell.

The Scientist's instructions are the useful checkpoint here: the Whale must be thrown into the Volcano after it has been defeated.

This is where I separate the two Whales. The normal **Bowhead Whale** is part of the experiment. The **Mutated Bowhead Whale** is the final boss created by the Volcano sequence.

If the final boss has not appeared, I check whether I completed the Scientist's five-creature request, obtained the Fish Bucket, caught the normal Whale, and delivered it to the Volcano.

Repeatedly casting ordinary Scientific Lures will not replace those story steps.

<h2 id="mutated-bowhead-whale">
  Mutated Bowhead Whale — Final Boss and Mainland Route
</h2>

Throwing the normal Bowhead Whale into the Volcano starts the final encounter with the Mutated Bowhead Whale.

By this point I want a ranged weapon that already has enough damage and control for a long fight, with food prepared before entering the encounter area.

The most important positioning mistake is trying to escape too far from the Volcano.

The boss can despawn when pulled outside its intended area. Creating some space to survive its attacks is useful, but sailing away or retreating excessively can reset the encounter rather than make it easier.

I stay in the Volcano area, avoid the Whale's landing attack, and wait for the follow-up movement before committing to another damage window.

The full <Link href="/how-to-fish/boss-guide#mutated-bowhead-whale">Mutated Bowhead Whale strategy</Link> covers the fight itself.

After the final boss dies, I still need to finish the Scientist's request. I collect the required proof from the defeated Whale and return to him.

Completing that hand-in unlocks access to the military's return boat.

I use the newly available boat and follow the route toward the **mainland** to finish the story. Defeating the Whale is the final major combat objective, but it is not the last progression interaction.

<h2 id="upgrade-priority">
  When to upgrade weapons, inventory, and the boat
</h2>

I do not try to purchase every upgrade as soon as a new shop becomes available. The better decision depends on what is actually preventing progress.

At the Lighthouse, I spend only enough to make fishing and the Spider Crab manageable. A useful melee weapon, the required Beer, and the $10 Radar matter more than accumulating expensive equipment.

On Island 2, the Giant Piranha makes ranged combat much more important. If I cannot defeat it, I prioritize the Shotgun over the Big Motor. A faster boat will not help while the boss is still blocking access to the next island.

By Island 3, inventory space, useful healing food, and an affordable automatic weapon become more attractive. The Pufferfish rewards positioning, so I do not assume that buying several different guns is better than learning the encounter.

On Island 4 and the final island, I concentrate upgrades into one reliable ranged weapon. Better recoil control, damage, and magazine capacity are valuable because the remaining bosses require sustained hits while moving.

The engine becomes worth upgrading when repeated travel is consuming time and the next boss is already manageable. For normal progression, I would rather arrive a little later with a working combat setup than arrive quickly without enough damage or healing.

<h2 id="all-fish">
  All 49 creatures and where to find them
</h2>

How to Fish has **49 creature entries** to complete in the encyclopedia.

The important distinction is that normal fishing catches, Boss Lures, and story-triggered creatures do not all use the same requirements.

An ordinary Lure can produce several different creatures, while the Spider Crab, Giant Piranha, Pufferfish, Terrorizing Bird, and Whales belong to specific progression chains.

I do not try to finish the entire collection on the opening islands. Later Lure tiers and special encounters become available as the story advances, so extensive early collection grinding can create unnecessary backtracking.

Once the relevant equipment is unlocked, I check the missing encyclopedia entries rather than guessing which island needs another visit.

The <Link href="/how-to-fish/all-fish-locations">All 49 Creatures guide</Link> lists the individual entries, their Rod or Lure requirements, Boss Lures, and story triggers.

<h2 id="after-story">
  What to do after finishing the story
</h2>

Finishing the mainland route does not mean every optional activity is complete.

After the ending, I can revisit the unlocked islands with better equipment and access to the full range of available creatures and activities.

The main remaining goals are collecting normal and Drip creature entries, completing optional boss encounters, finishing cooking and casino challenges, and earning the achievements that require unusual combat conditions.

There are **28 achievements** in total, including a one-hour completion challenge and a final-boss challenge requiring a bare-hands killing blow.

Those conditions do not need to complicate the first normal playthrough. I finish the story and unlock the major systems first, then approach the remaining requirements with the equipment and locations already available.

The <Link href="/how-to-fish/achievements">All 28 Achievements Checklist</Link> lets me track completed requirements and identify which optional tasks are still missing.

<h2 id="where-to-go-when-stuck">
  Where to go when stuck in How to Fish
</h2>

When I cannot advance, I first identify whether I am missing a quest trigger, struggling with a boss, or waiting for the next coordinates.

**If the boss has not appeared**, I check the bait requirement. The Spider Crab needs the Empty Beer Can; the Giant Piranha needs the completed Leech quest; the Pufferfish needs the Carrot; and the Terrorizing Bird needs Tuna. The final Whale encounter requires completing the Scientist's experiment.

**If the boss is alive but keeps defeating me**, I stop repeating the same preparation. I check whether my weapon can deal reliable damage, whether I have enough food, and whether the arena offers useful cover or movement space. The <Link href="/how-to-fish/boss-guide">Boss Guide</Link> covers the major encounters.

**If the boss is dead but the Radar has not updated**, I check the quest drop and NPC hand-in. The Skeleton, Tail, and Bird's Head are particularly important because defeating their bosses alone does not provide the next route.

**If I have the next Flash Drive but cannot see the destination**, I return to the Radar and check the unlocked marker before trying another fight. More fishing and another engine purchase will not complete an unfinished quest.

Once the next coordinates are available, I move on. Optional fish, upgrades, and achievement challenges can wait until I have finished the main route.
