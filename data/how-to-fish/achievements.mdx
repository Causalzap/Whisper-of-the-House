<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
**How to Fish has 28 achievements.**

I do not try to force all 28 during my first story run.

My cleaner 100% route is:

**finish the normal island progression**

→ pick up easy **Killscore, cooking, boat, gear, and casino achievements** as those systems unlock

→ finish the story

→ clean up **Easy, Rich! Millionaire, Everyone's dream, and I'm the bird now**

→ finish **Collector and Fishipedia**

→ do **Bean** on a dedicated sub-1-hour run

→ use the **final hit with bare hands** for **Handyman**

The rarest achievements in the current Steam snapshot are **Bean, Fishipedia, and Handyman at 0.1%**, so I treat those as dedicated cleanup goals instead of bending my first playthrough around them.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

<h2 id="best-100-percent-route">
  I split 100% completion into three passes
</h2>

Trying to combine the story, Fishipedia, gambling, trick shots, speedrunning, and boss challenges into one run creates more problems than it solves.

I use three passes instead.

### Pass 1 — finish the story

I follow the normal island progression and let the progression achievements unlock while I solve the actual quest blockers.

That naturally covers most of:

**Getting started**

**Who stole my beer**

**Getting an upgrade**

**Dinnertime**

**Vacation**

**Terrorizing bird**

**Deadliest catch**

and finally:

**We are so back**

I do not delay a story boss because I still need a roulette achievement or another Drip creature.

Story progression gives me access to better bait, stronger weapons, better boat upgrades, more valuable creatures, and more places to finish the cleanup later.

### Pass 2 — clear systems I have already unlocked

Once the relevant mechanics are available, I work through:

**Noob**

**Impressive**

**360 no scope**

**Grillmaster**

**Yummy in my tummy**

**Competitive eating**

**Fully equipped**

**I am speed**

**GOLD GOLD GOLD**

**All in**

**Let me go**

and:

**Everyone's dream**

Most of these only need one correct setup.

They do not deserve an entire second story run.

### Pass 3 — leave the expensive and route-changing achievements for last

I save:

**Easy**

**Rich! Millionaire**

**I'm the bird now**

**Collector**

**Fishipedia**

**Bean**

and:

**Handyman**

until I understand the progression route and combat well enough that I am not learning basic mechanics at the same time.

That matters most for **Bean**.

A sub-one-hour clear is a completely different run from a first playthrough where I am exploring shops, collecting Drip creatures, gambling, testing weapons, and stopping for every side activity.

<h2 id="story-achievements">
  I let the main progression achievements unlock naturally
</h2>

I let the story achievements unlock during normal progression.

If one is missing, I check the progression stage tied to it.

**Who stole my beer** belongs to the first-island boss sequence.

**Dinnertime** belongs to the Giant Piranha progression on Island 2.

If I am stuck there, I use the <Link href="/how-to-fish/giant-piranha">Giant Piranha guide</Link> rather than farming unrelated achievements.

**Vacation** belongs to the Tourist and Pufferfish route on Island 3.

If I do not have the Carrot yet, I check the <Link href="/how-to-fish/endangered-fish">Endangered Fish guide</Link>.

If the Carrot is already ready but the boss is stopping me, I move to the <Link href="/how-to-fish/pufferfish">Pufferfish guide</Link>.

**Terrorizing bird** and **Deadliest catch** come from later progression fights.

If either fight is blocking me, I use the <Link href="/how-to-fish/boss-guide">Boss Guide</Link>.

Finally, **We are so back** unlocks for finishing the game.

If one of these is still missing, I first assume I have missed a quest hand-in, boss trophy, or next progression step rather than assuming I need to repeat random fishing.

<h2 id="killscore-achievements">
  I combine Noob, Impressive, and 360 no scope cleanup
</h2>

The three achievements tied directly to Killscore are:

**Noob** — get a kill with no Killscore multiplier

**Impressive** — get a **5x Killscore multiplier**

**360 no scope** — kill a creature with a 360 no scope

I start with **Noob** because it wants the opposite of the other two.

I use an ordinary creature and finish it without deliberately adding a trick-shot condition.

Once Noob is done, I start styling the kills.

The game can recognize conditions such as:

**head shot**

**one shot kill**

**point blank**

**no scope**

**360**

**quick scope**

**last bullet kill**

**kill steal**

and:

**overkill**

I do not treat that as a complete mathematical formula.

What matters is that the conditions can raise the final Killscore multiplier, and the multiplier also affects how much money I get from the kill.

That means learning Killscore is useful even after the three achievements are finished.

### Impressive — I use a fight where I can stack score safely

For **Impressive**, the final number I care about is:

**5x**

I do not force this on a story boss.

A crowded fight with a **Shotgun** worked much better: I was able to get repeated **5x multipliers** while controlling several creatures at once.

That gives me a much safer place to experiment than a boss where one failed trick shot wastes a quest lure.

My rule is:

**easy targets first**

→ **stack several score conditions**

→ **watch the final multiplier**

→ stop as soon as **5x** registers

I do not keep farming style kills after the achievement unless I am deliberately trying to raise sale value.

### 360 no scope — I use a predictable normal creature

For **360 no scope**, I want a target that is slow enough that I can control the finishing shot.

I line up the creature first.

Then I rotate, fire without aiming down a scope, and make sure that shot actually gets the kill.

<h2 id="cooking-achievements">
  I unlock the grill first, then do all three cooking achievements together
</h2>

The grill is not immediately available just because I can see it.

The Grillmaster NPC first asks me for a **Shark**.

I bring him the Shark, and that opens the cooking system for my own catches.

The NPC also explains why cooking matters outside the achievements:

**cooked creatures sell for more**

and:

**cooked creatures are better food**

So I do not treat cooking as a one-off gimmick.

### Grillmaster

The achievement requires:

**Start the grill.**

Once the Shark request is finished and I can use the grill myself, I start it and take this achievement immediately.

I do not leave it for endgame cleanup.

### Yummy in my tummy

This requires:

**Eat a burnt creature.**

Cooking normally increases the value of a catch, so I do not burn something valuable unless I am intentionally doing the achievement.

I take a cheap normal creature and keep cooking it past the useful state until it is clearly burnt.

Then I eat it instead of selling it.

My decision is:

**money run**

→ stop cooking while the value is good

**Yummy in my tummy**

→ deliberately keep going until the creature is burnt

I never use a quest trophy or boss progression item for this.

### Competitive eating

This requires:

**Eat a mini-boss.**

I use a repeatable mini-boss rather than sacrificing something tied to the story.

Once it is dead:

**do not sell it**

→ **do not gamble it**

→ **eat it**

This is easiest after I already have enough money that losing one mini-boss sale does not matter.

**GOLD GOLD GOLD**

**All in**

**Rich! Millionaire**

I do not chase them during early progression when every useful purchase matters.

### GOLD GOLD GOLD

The requirement is:

**Unlock a legendary skin from the slot machine.**

This is RNG, so I do not expect one fixed number of attempts.

The visual confirmation I use is the important part:

**gold skins are Legendary.**

I have seen the Legendary result appear on more than one weapon type, so I do not target one particular gun.

I keep using the skin machine until I get a gold result.

### Rich! Millionaire

This requires:

**Sell something worth 100,000 or more.**

The wording matters.

I do **not** need:

**100,000 cash in my wallet**

I need:

**one item with a sale value of at least 100,000**

Killscore, cooking, valuable late-game creatures, and gambling all connect back to this goal because they can increase the value of what I eventually sell.

I leave Rich! Millionaire until late game because my starting item matters enormously.

My route is:

**start with a valuable creature I can afford to risk**

→ **increase its value**

→ **inspect it before selling**

→ only sell when the displayed value is **100,000+**

I do not use a quest item.

I also do not gamble something irreplaceable just because it has a higher starting value.

<h2 id="gear-and-boat">
  I concentrate upgrades instead of spreading money across everything
</h2>

Three achievements fit naturally into normal equipment progression.

### Getting an upgrade

Buy an engine upgrade for the boat.

I usually get this naturally once traveling between islands becomes part of the main route.

### Fully equipped

Apply all attachments to a single weapon.

The mistake I avoid is buying one attachment for every gun.

That gives me several half-built weapons and delays the achievement.

Instead I choose the gun I actually use most and concentrate the attachment spending there.

If recoil is my problem, I prioritize control.

If accuracy is already good enough but boss health is barely moving, I prioritize damage.

The achievement and my actual combat build should be working toward the same goal.

### I am speed

Buy the best engine for the boat.

I leave this later than the first engine upgrade.

Early in the game, spending money on a weapon can solve a boss that is completely blocking progression.

Maximum boat speed does not solve that problem.

Once I am traveling repeatedly between later islands and combat is already stable, I finish the engine chain.

<h2 id="one-off-achievements">
  I save the weird one-off setups for controlled cleanup
</h2>

These achievements are much easier once I stop expecting them to happen naturally.

### Let me go

The requirement is:

**Get picked up by a seagull.**

When I want this achievement, I stop shooting every seagull immediately.

I stay exposed long enough for one to complete the pickup.

Once it registers, I go back to killing or avoiding them normally.

### Everyone's dream

This requires:

**Kill a seagull with Dynamite.**

I use the bird's scavenging behavior instead of trying to hit it in midair.

Seagulls already move toward creatures and useful items lying around.

I create the opportunity on the ground, let the bird commit, then use Dynamite for the kill.

I do not use my last useful food item or anything tied to a quest as bait.

### I'm the bird now

The requirement is:

**Make the boat fly.**

I no longer wait for this to happen during normal sailing.

The boat's physics can be forced.

One setup that worked was:

**drive the boat against uneven shoreline terrain**

→ **get it tilted or wedged**

→ **hit the boat to keep forcing movement**

→ **let the physics launch it airborne**

In multiplayer, another useful trick was having someone stand at the front and **punch the boat while another player drove**.

That noticeably increased the boat's movement, and uneven terrain could send it into the air.

Another successful setup came from getting the boat stuck at an angle and **shooting it** until it launched.

The important part is not distance.

I only need the boat to become clearly airborne for the achievement to register.

I still leave this until cleanup because a bad launch can send the boat far from shore.

<h2 id="easy-fast-boss">
  I do Easy after I already know the fight
</h2>

**Easy** requires:

**Kill a boss within 10 seconds.**

Only **3.3%** of players had it in the current achievement snapshot.

I do not attempt this while learning a boss.

My first clear tells me:

- where the boss spawns;
- whether there is a forced phase;
- how much damage my weapon actually does;
- where I can safely stand;
- and whether the arena lets me start shooting immediately.

Then I prepare specifically for burst damage.

My checklist is:

**boss trigger ready**

→ **strong weapon already equipped**

→ **full magazine**

→ **food handled before the fight**

→ **burst damage ready**

→ **start dealing damage immediately**

Dynamite can help with burst damage, but I do not assume one explosive solves every fight.

I choose whichever early boss I can reproduce and damage consistently.

If I need the exact strategy for a specific boss, I use the <Link href="/how-to-fish/boss-guide">Boss Guide</Link>.

<h2 id="collector-fishipedia">
  Collector and Fishipedia are separate from finishing the story
</h2>

These two are true completion achievements.

### Collector

Requirement:

**Find and kill all the creatures.**

One full story route still ended at:

**44 / 49**

That is the warning I care about.

Finishing the story does not automatically finish the creature collection.

I check the collection itself instead of assuming the ending means I am done.

### Fishipedia

Requirement:

**Find and kill all Drip creatures.**

A normal creature and its Drip version are not interchangeable for this achievement.

So my cleanup becomes two separate checks:

**missing base creatures**

and:

**missing Drip variants**

I do not keep recatching a species if the version I actually need is already registered.

The current completion rates also show why I leave this late:

**Collector — 1.7%**

**Fishipedia — 0.1%**

These are not normal first-run achievements.

<h2 id="bean-speedrun">
  Bean gets a dedicated run
</h2>

**Bean** requires:

**Finish the game within 1 hour.**

I do not combine that with 100% exploration.

My first full playthrough is what teaches me the route.

Then the Bean run becomes:

**first-island boss**

→ **Island 2 Leech quest and Giant Piranha**

→ **Island 3 Tourist requirement and Pufferfish**

→ **Island 4 Tuna and Terrorizing Bird**

→ **military / Whale progression**

→ **final route**

Anything that does not move that chain forward becomes optional.

I skip:

- Fishipedia detours;
- Drip hunting;
- casino cleanup;
- unnecessary cooking;
- repeat mini-boss farming unless I need the money;
- cosmetic gambling;
- attachments that cost more time than they save.

I also change how I look at purchases.

On a normal run, I will buy comfort.

On Bean, every purchase has to answer:

**Will this save more time than buying and equipping it costs?**

If not, I keep moving.

That is why I do Bean after the normal clear instead of trying to turn my first playthrough into a speedrun.

<h2 id="handyman">
  I save the bare-hands condition for the final hit
</h2>

**Handyman** says:

**Defeat the final boss with your bare hands.**

I do not spend the entire fight punching the boss.

The useful interpretation is that the **finishing hit** is what I need to deliver bare-handed.

So I fight normally first.

My sequence is:

**damage the final boss with my normal weapon**

→ **watch the health carefully near the end**

→ **stop shooting before the kill**

→ **switch to bare hands**

→ **land the finishing hit**

The difficult part is not the punching.

It is avoiding the accidental weapon kill.

Once the boss is almost dead, I stop firing earlier than feels necessary.

One extra bullet can turn the entire attempt into another full repeat.

That is why I leave Handyman until I already understand the final fight.

<h2 id="final-cleanup">
  I diagnose the missing achievement by category
</h2>

When I am down to the last few achievements, I do not replay random parts of the game.

I check what category is missing.

If it is **Story**, I check the corresponding quest, boss trophy, or progression hand-in.

If it is **Killscore**, I work on the finishing shot rather than changing islands.

If it is **Cooking**, I return to the grill.

If it is **Casino / Money**, I prepare replaceable items and enough value to absorb failed rolls.

If it is **Gear / Boat**, I check one fully attached weapon or the remaining engine tier.

If it is **Collector / Fishipedia**, I compare the actual collection gaps.

If it is **Easy**, I prepare one known boss specifically for burst damage.

If it is **I'm the bird now**, I use shoreline terrain and the boat physics instead of waiting for a natural jump.

If it is **Bean**, I start a dedicated speedrun.

If it is **Handyman**, I repeat the final boss with the bare-hands finishing hit in mind.

That is much faster than fishing, gambling, and replaying bosses randomly while hoping the last achievement appears.
