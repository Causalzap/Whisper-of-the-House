**How to Fish has 28 achievements.**

I do not try to force all 28 into my first story run.

My cleaner 100% route is:

**finish the normal island progression**

→ pick up easy combat, cooking, gear, boat, and casino achievements as those systems unlock

→ clean up the unusual one-off achievements

→ finish **Collector** and **Fishipedia** after every island and lure tier is available

→ do **Bean** on a dedicated sub-one-hour run

→ finish **Handyman** by making the final hit on the last boss with bare hands

The biggest time saver is separating achievements that happen naturally from achievements that need their own setup.

<h2 id="best-100-percent-route">
  A practical three-pass route to 100%
</h2>

Trying to speedrun, complete Fishipedia, gamble, hunt Drip creatures, learn bosses, and finish every trick-shot achievement in one playthrough made the route much less efficient.

I separate the work.

### Pass 1 — finish the story and take free achievements

My first goal is simply to unlock every island, lure tier, shop, boss, and travel option.

The story-related achievements come from the same progression chain I already need to complete.

If **Who stole my beer** is missing, I return to the <Link href="/how-to-fish/spider-crab">Spider Crab route</Link> rather than farming random creatures on the Lighthouse island.

If **Dinnertime** is missing, the relevant progression is the <Link href="/how-to-fish/giant-piranha">Giant Piranha</Link> chain.

For **Vacation**, I separate the two Island 3 blockers: the <Link href="/how-to-fish/endangered-fish">Endangered Species requirement</Link> comes first, then the <Link href="/how-to-fish/pufferfish">Pufferfish</Link>.

After Island 3, the next major fights are the Terrorizing Bird and Mutated Bowhead Whale. If either fight is blocking the run, I use the <Link href="/how-to-fish/boss-guide">Boss Guide</Link> before spending time on optional achievements.

My rule on the first run is simple:

**if an achievement does not help me reach the next progression gate, I do not delay the story for it.**

### Pass 2 — clean up unlocked systems

Once the full game is open, I return to systems that only need a controlled setup:

**Killscore**

→ **cooking**

→ **casino**

→ **gear and boat upgrades**

→ **seagull and boat-physics achievements**

At this point I have better weapons, more money, better lures, and access to more valuable creatures.

That makes experimentation much cheaper.

### Pass 3 — collection and challenge runs

I leave the objectives that can consume an entire session until last:

**Collector**

→ **Fishipedia**

→ **Easy**

→ **Bean**

→ **Handyman**

These are much easier once I already know the islands, boss triggers, and endgame route.

<h2 id="killscore-achievements">
  Combine the Killscore achievements
</h2>

The three Killscore-related achievements ask for different finishing conditions, but I practice them in the same cleanup session.

For **Noob**, I want the opposite of a stylish kill.

I use a disposable normal creature and finish it without deliberately stacking trick-shot bonuses.

After that, I start working toward **Impressive** and **360 no scope**.

The Killscore system can recognize conditions such as:

- head shots;
- one-shot kills;
- point blank kills;
- no scope;
- 360;
- quick scope;
- last-bullet kills;
- kill steals;
- overkill.

I do not treat that list as a mathematical recipe for one guaranteed multiplier.

I use it as a menu of conditions I can combine on easy targets.

### Impressive — use repeatable creatures, not a story boss

For **Impressive**, I need the final Killscore multiplier to reach **5x**.

I do not practice this on a progression boss where a failed attempt can waste special bait.

A normal or repeatable fight gives me much more control.

Once I start landing several qualifying conditions on the finishing sequence, I watch the displayed multiplier and stop when the achievement registers.

There is no reason to keep forcing 5x kills afterward unless I also want the increased sale value.

### 360 no scope — the spinning shot has to be the kill

This became much easier when I stopped using fast or dangerous targets.

I weaken a predictable creature first.

Then I create enough distance to:

**line up the target**

→ **rotate**

→ **fire without aiming down a scope**

→ **make that shot the finishing hit**

<h2 id="cooking-achievements">
  Unlock the grill before farming cooking achievements
</h2>

I do not start deliberately burning creatures before I have solved the Island 3 grill setup.

The Grillmaster asks me for a **Shark**.

Once I complete that request, I can use the grill myself.

That is when I clear the cooking achievements.

### Grillmaster

I start the unlocked grill as soon as I have access to it.

There is no advantage to saving this achievement for the endgame.

### Yummy in my tummy

For this one I intentionally burn a **cheap normal creature**.

Normally I stop cooking while the creature still has useful sale or food value.

For the achievement I do the opposite:

**use a cheap catch**

→ **keep cooking past the useful state**

→ **wait until it is burnt**

→ **eat it**

I never experiment with a quest item or progression trophy.

### Competitive eating

I use a repeatable mini-boss.

The decision is easy once I have enough money:

**kill the mini-boss**

→ **do not sell it**

→ **do not gamble it**

→ **eat it**

I leave this until losing one mini-boss sale no longer affects my progression budget.

<h2 id="casino-money-achievements">
  Casino achievements: chase the requirement, not profit
</h2>

The casino can consume a surprising amount of time because it is easy to turn one achievement attempt into a long gambling session.

I avoid doing that.

### GOLD GOLD GOLD

I use the skin machine until I unlock a **Legendary** result.

The visual confirmation I care about is the gold skin.

### All in

For **All in**, I only need the correct roulette result:

**bet on green and win.**

That means my achievement strategy is not the same as my money strategy.

I bring replaceable items I can afford to lose.

Then I repeat one simple loop:

**bet on green**

→ lose? use another disposable item

→ win? stop

### Rich! Millionaire

The requirement is about the value of **one item**:

**sell something worth at least 100,000.**

It is not asking me to hold 100,000 cash.

That distinction changes how I approach it.

I leave this until late game, when I have access to more valuable creatures and more ways to raise sale value.

Killscore and cooking can improve value, and the casino can change item value too, but I do not treat gambling as mandatory.

My route is:

**start with something valuable but replaceable**

→ **raise its value using systems I already understand**

→ **inspect the item**

→ **sell only once the displayed value reaches 100,000+**

I never use a progression trophy just because its starting value looks attractive.

<h2 id="gear-and-boat">
  Make equipment achievements support the build
</h2>

I avoid spending money across several half-finished weapons.

### Getting an upgrade

I normally get the first boat-engine upgrade during progression.

Once sailing between islands starts taking meaningful time, that purchase solves a real problem as well as the achievement.

### Fully equipped

For this achievement I put all required attachments onto **one weapon**.

I do not buy a different attachment for every gun.

I choose the weapon I already rely on and finish that build.

That way the achievement spending also improves my boss setup.

### I am speed

I save the best boat engine until combat is stable.

Early money is more valuable when it solves an actual progression blocker.

Once I am repeatedly traveling between unlocked islands, the final engine becomes worth buying.

<h2 id="one-off-achievements">
  Leave the strange one-off achievements for cleanup
</h2>

These are the achievements I no longer expect to happen naturally.

### Let me go

I need to be **picked up by a seagull**.

When I am deliberately doing this achievement, I stop killing every bird that approaches me.

I stay in an exposed position and let the pickup finish.

Once it registers, I go back to treating seagulls normally.

### Everyone&apos;s dream

This requires killing a seagull with **Dynamite**.

I do not try to throw Dynamite at a bird that is already flying away.

I use the fact that seagulls come down toward creatures and items.

My setup is:

**create a reason for the bird to come down**

→ **wait for it to commit**

→ **use Dynamite while it is close to the ground**

I use expendable bait, not my last food item or anything needed for a quest.

### I&apos;m the bird now

This achievement wants me to:

**make the boat fly.**

I leave it for cleanup because the successful setup can also leave the boat in a terrible position.

The most reproducible result I found came from abusing uneven shoreline terrain.

I drive the boat into a slope until it becomes tilted or wedged.

Then I keep forcing it while applying extra impact to the boat.

<h2 id="easy-fast-boss">
  Easy: use a boss you already understand
</h2>

**Easy** requires me to kill a boss within **10 seconds**.

I do not choose the boss while I am still learning its trigger or attack pattern.

My first successful clear gives me the information I actually need:

- where the boss appears;
- how quickly I can start dealing damage;
- whether it has a forced opening;
- how much damage my current weapon does;
- and whether I can safely stay on target.

Then I prepare a separate burst-damage attempt.

Before triggering the boss, I want:

**food already handled**

→ **strong weapon equipped**

→ **full magazine**

→ **damage setup ready**

→ **no shopping or inventory sorting after the trigger**

Dynamite can contribute burst damage, but I do not assume every boss is solved by one explosive.

I use whichever repeatable boss I can damage most consistently.

If the fight itself is still the problem, I go back to the <Link href="/how-to-fish/boss-guide">Boss Guide</Link> before turning it into a timed attempt.

<h2 id="collector-fishipedia">
  Collector and Fishipedia belong late in the run
</h2>

These are the two achievements I deliberately leave until every island and lure tier is available.

<GuideCallout title="Collector and Fishipedia are not the same checklist">
**Collector** tracks the normal creature collection.

**Fishipedia** tracks the separate Drip variants.

Finishing one does not mean the other is complete.
</GuideCallout>

A normal story clear can finish while the creature encyclopedia still has gaps.

Once progression is done, I stop guessing from memory.

I use the <Link href="/how-to-fish/all-fish-locations">All 49 Creatures and Collector guide</Link> to match each missing normal slot to the correct island, lure pool, Boss Lure, or story trigger.

For **Fishipedia**, I use the same creature map but check the Drip side separately.

If the normal creature is already registered but its Drip variant is not, catching another normal copy does not solve the achievement.

This is deliberate endgame cleanup, not something I force every time I first visit an island.

<h2 id="bean-speedrun">
  Bean deserves a dedicated run
</h2>

**Bean** requires finishing the game within **1 hour**.

I do not combine this with Collector, Fishipedia, casino cleanup, or cosmetic hunting.

The first playthrough teaches me the route.

Before the timed attempt, I use the <Link href="/how-to-fish">main progression walkthrough</Link> to refresh the NPC hand-offs and island order without mixing in collection cleanup.

My Bean progression chain is:

**Spider Crab**

→ **Boat Keys and Radar**

→ **Island 2 Leeches and Giant Piranha**

→ **Island 3 Endangered Species requirement and Pufferfish**

→ **Island 4 Tuna and Terrorizing Bird**

→ **military / Volcano progression**

→ **Whale sequence**

→ **final route**

Anything that does not move that chain forward has to justify its time.

I normally skip:

- Drip farming;
- collection cleanup;
- optional casino sessions;
- cosmetic gambling;
- unnecessary cooking;
- repeat mini-boss farming unless I need the money;
- attachments that cost more time to obtain than they save.

I also become much stricter about purchases.

On a normal run I will spend money for comfort.

On Bean I ask:

**Will this purchase save more time than buying and equipping it costs?**

If not, I keep moving.

Before I start the timed run, I make sure the progression route is familiar and revisit any boss I still fail inconsistently. A minute spent learning a fight before the attempt is cheaper than losing a nearly complete Bean run.

<h2 id="handyman">
  Handyman only needs the final hit with bare hands
</h2>

This was the most important requirement clarification for my final-boss cleanup.

**Handyman** says:

**Defeat the final boss with your bare hands.**

I initially read that as:

> fight the entire boss bare-handed.

That makes the challenge look much worse than it needs to be.

The useful requirement is the **last hit**.

So I fight normally first.

My sequence is:

**use my normal ranged setup**

→ **lower the final boss carefully**

→ **stop shooting before the health reaches zero**

→ **unequip the weapon**

→ **move in with bare hands**

→ **land the final hit**

The actual danger is over-damaging the boss.

I stop shooting earlier than feels necessary because one extra bullet can kill it and turn the entire attempt into another repeat.

I do not try Handyman while I am still learning the final fight.

If the encounter itself is still inconsistent, I use the <Link href="/how-to-fish/boss-guide#mutated-bowhead-whale">Mutated Bowhead Whale strategy</Link> first.

Once I can reach the final damage window reliably, changing only the finishing hit is much more manageable.

<h2 id="final-cleanup">
  Diagnose the last achievement before repeating anything
</h2>

When I am down to the last few achievements, I do not replay random islands hoping something unlocks.

I identify the missing system.

If the missing achievement is tied to a **story boss**, I check its quest trigger and hand-in.

If it is **Killscore**, I work on the finishing-shot condition.

If it is **cooking**, I return to the unlocked grill.

If it is **casino or money**, I prepare replaceable items and enough cash to absorb failed attempts.

If it is **gear or boat progression**, I finish one weapon build or the remaining engine tier.

If it is **Collector or Fishipedia**, I compare the actual encyclopedia gaps against the <Link href="/how-to-fish/all-fish-locations">49-creature checklist</Link>.

If it is **Easy**, I prepare one familiar boss specifically for burst damage.

If it is **I&apos;m the bird now**, I use shoreline terrain instead of waiting for normal sailing to launch the boat.

If it is **Bean**, I restart with the one-hour route as the only objective.

If it is **Handyman**, I return to the final boss and protect the bare-hands finishing hit.

That category check is much faster than fishing, gambling, upgrading, and replaying bosses randomly while waiting for the last achievement to appear.
