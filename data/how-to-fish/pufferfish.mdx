<h2 id="get-carrot-first">
  I only start this fight once the Carrot is already in my inventory
</h2>

The Pufferfish is not something I find by sailing around Island 3 and waiting for it to appear.

The **Carrot** is the boss trigger.

If I do not have one yet, I stop here and finish the Tourist requirement in the <Link href="/how-to-fish/endangered-fish">Endangered Fish guide</Link>.

Once the Carrot is in my inventory, that earlier problem is finished.

From this point on, I am preparing for the Pufferfish.

I do not repeat the Endangered Species route here because the important decision has changed:

**when should I spend the Carrot on an actual boss attempt?**

<h2 id="prepare-before-carrot">
  I prepare before using the Carrot because a failed attempt costs more than a respawn
</h2>

My first mistake was using the Carrot as soon as I received it.

The Pufferfish appeared immediately, and I discovered that I was not prepared for how much punishment it could take.

Before I trigger the fight, I check:

**health**

→ **food**

→ **ranged weapon**

→ **whether I can actually control that weapon**

I do not use the Carrot while already hungry.

I also do not trigger the boss just because I finally bought a new gun.

If I already know that the weapon is difficult for me to keep on target, I fix that problem first.

There is another useful emergency option here.

**Beer restores health**, but in one run it also reduced the food meter.

So I treat Beer as emergency boss healing, not as a replacement for starting the fight properly fed.

That means:

**eat before the fight**

and then:

**use Beer only if I need the health recovery**

rather than entering the Pufferfish fight hungry and trying to repair both bars in combat.

<h2 id="carrot-is-consumed">
  If I fail, I may have to earn another Carrot
</h2>

The Carrot is not an infinite boss lure.

After I used one and failed to kill the Pufferfish, I could not simply walk back and start another free attempt.

I needed another Carrot.

That means another failed attempt can send me back into the previous quest step before I can fight the boss again.

So if the first attempt clearly shows that my setup is bad, I do not immediately spend another Carrot.

I fix the problem first.

If I died because I had no food, I bring food.

If I could not keep my gun on target, I improve control.

If my damage was consistently landing but the health bar barely moved, I improve damage.

This is one of the fights where:

**another immediate attempt**

is not always:

**the fastest way forward**

A few minutes of preparation can save an entire repeat of the Carrot requirement.

<h2 id="smg-and-upgrades">
  The 650 SMG did not help until I dealt with its recoil
</h2>

I saved **650** for the SMG because the Pufferfish looked like exactly the kind of tanky boss where high fire rate should help.

The problem was not firing enough bullets.

It was landing them.

  </figcaption>
</figure>

Once the Pufferfish started moving, I spent too much time dragging my aim back onto it.

That changed how I evaluated upgrades.

I stopped asking:

**What is the most expensive upgrade I can afford?**

and started asking:

**Why did the previous attempt fail?**

If recoil makes me miss:

→ **buy control**

If I can stay on target but damage is too low:

→ **buy damage**

If I keep getting caught during reloads:

→ **consider magazine capacity**

If the problem is my route around the boss:

→ **stop buying attachments and change my positioning**

The **Compensator costs 600**, and it became a much more sensible purchase once recoil was clearly the thing wasting my SMG damage.

I do not buy a Red Dot just because it is available.

In another run, the Red Dot did not meaningfully solve the weapon-control problem.

I spend money on the failure I can actually identify.

<h2 id="tree-dodge-route">
  I circle one tree and make the Pufferfish lose its direct path to me
</h2>

The biggest improvement in this fight was positional.

The trees are not just background objects.

They can physically interfere with the Pufferfish's direct approach.

Instead of running straight away from the boss across open ground, I stay close to a large tree and move around it.

  <Image

    width={1600}

    className="rounded-xl"
  />
  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">
    I circle the tree rather than running straight away. The trunk interrupts the boss's direct route, and I shoot when the Pufferfish has to reposition around it.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

My movement loop is:

**keep the tree between us**

→ **circle when the Pufferfish commits toward me**

→ **let the trunk block or redirect the approach**

→ **shoot while the boss is repositioning**

→ **move around the tree again before the next charge**

This is different from simply hiding behind the tree.

I am constantly changing which side of the trunk I am on.

The goal is to make the Pufferfish keep solving the obstacle while I already know where I am going next.

If I run straight out into the open, I give the boss a clean path.

If I stay tight to the tree, its movement becomes much easier to read.

This ended up being more valuable than another random attachment.

The same approach was later the simplest answer I gave when someone asked how to beat the Pufferfish:

**use the trees to your advantage.**

<h2 id="when-to-shoot">
  I shoot after the charge is disrupted instead of trying to trade damage
</h2>

The tree route only helps if I use the opening it creates.

I do not spend the entire fight sprinting in circles.

Once the Pufferfish commits toward one side of the tree and has to redirect, I turn and put ranged damage into it.

Then I start moving again before it reaches me cleanly.

That gives me a rhythm:

**bait the approach**

→ **break line of attack with the tree**

→ **shoot**

→ **move**

The mistake is staying in the shooting window too long.

One extra burst is not worth losing the spacing I just created.

If the Pufferfish starts closing the distance again, I move first and reload or shoot on the next safe angle.

<h2 id="stay-ranged">
  I do not chase a low-health Pufferfish into melee range
</h2>

The knife is not my main answer here.

Even when the boss looks nearly dead, I do not suddenly abandon the tree and rush it for a melee finish.

That is exactly when I am most tempted to throw away a good attempt.

A low-health Pufferfish can still hit hard.

And because another failed attempt may require another Carrot, being "almost done" does not make the loss cheaper.

I keep using the same ranged pattern until the boss is actually dead.

The safe finish is worth more than shaving a few seconds off the last part of the fight.

<h2 id="later-phase">
  When the Pufferfish grows, I give it more room instead of getting greedy
</h2>

The later part of the fight becomes visually much more aggressive.

The Pufferfish grows larger, moves differently, and can fill the area around it with a **purple hazard effect**.

I do not stand inside the effect trying to work out exactly how much damage I can tolerate.

I move.

The tree route still helps, but I give myself a little more room before committing to another shooting window.

By this point the fight rewards patience more than greed.

If I already have the boss under control, I do not change the strategy simply because the health bar is getting low.

<h2 id="pufferfish-tail">
  I pick up the Pufferfish Tail before I leave the beach
</h2>

Killing the Pufferfish is **not** the final progression step.

This is the part that is easy to miss.

After the boss died, the Tourist did not immediately finish the route.

At first I looked at the ordinary chunks and wondered whether one of those was what he wanted.

It was not.

The important boss trophy is the:

**Pufferfish Tail**

I pick it up before I start cooking, selling, or chasing other loot.

I also keep an eye on the seagulls.

Loose creatures and drops around these islands can get carried away, and in one fight the missing Pufferfish trophy was only noticed after checking what a nearby bird had picked up.

So after the boss dies, my priority is:

**find the Tail**

not:

**sort the money drops**

and definitely not:

**leave for the boat**

If the Pufferfish is dead but the Tourist still is not advancing, this is the first item I check.

<h2 id="unlock-island-4">
  I bring the Pufferfish Tail to the Tourist and wait for the Island 4 coordinates
</h2>

Once I have the **Pufferfish Tail**, I return to the Tourist.

That hand-in finishes the boss request.

The progression reward I care about is:

**Flash Drive with coordinates to Island 4**

This gives me a very clear troubleshooting rule.

If:

**Pufferfish is still alive**

→ I have a combat problem.

If:

**Pufferfish is dead but the Tourist will not progress**

→ I check for the **Pufferfish Tail**.

If:

**the Tail has been handed in and I have the Island 4 coordinates**

→ the Island 3 boss sequence is finished.

I do not kill another Pufferfish once I have reached that state.

<h2 id="leave-for-island-4">
  Once the coordinates are registered, I follow the Radar and leave
</h2>

After the quest hand-in, I return to the boat and check the Radar.

I do not keep hunting another Endangered Species creature.

I do not spend another Carrot.

I do not remain on Island 3 buying upgrades just because the shop still has things I could afford.

The blocker is finished.

My complete route is:

**Carrot ready**

→ **eat and prepare**

→ **trigger the Pufferfish**

→ **keep a tree between me and the boss**

→ **circle the tree to break its direct charge**

→ **shoot during the repositioning window**

→ **stay ranged through the later phase**

→ **kill the Pufferfish**

→ **pick up the Pufferfish Tail**

→ **give the Tail to the Tourist**

→ **take the Island 4 Flash Drive**

→ **follow the Radar**

→ **leave for Island 4**

Once I have the coordinates, I stop treating Island 3 as an unfinished progression problem.
