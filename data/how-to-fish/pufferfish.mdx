<h2 id="how-to-beat-pufferfish">
  How to beat the Pufferfish boss in How to Fish
</h2>

The most important thing I learned from this fight is that the beach itself is
part of the strategy.

At first I tried to create distance by running away from the Pufferfish in open
ground. That gave the boss exactly what it wanted: a direct path toward me.

The fight became much more manageable when I used one of the large trees as the
center of the encounter.

My basic rhythm is:

**keep a tree between us → circle when the Pufferfish charges → let the trunk
interrupt its approach → shoot while it repositions → move again before the
next charge.**

I stay ranged for the entire fight.

I do not chase the boss with the knife when its health gets low, and I do not
stand still trying to squeeze one more burst out of my gun once the Pufferfish
has recovered its approach.

The tree gives me the opening.

My job is to use that opening without getting greedy.

<h2 id="get-carrot-first">
  Get the Carrot before trying to fight the Pufferfish
</h2>

The Pufferfish does not appear just because I reach Island 3.

The **Carrot is the boss trigger**.

If I do not have one yet, I finish the Tourist's earlier request first. The
<Link href="/how-to-fish/endangered-fish">
  {" "}Endangered Fish guide
</Link>
covers the Endangered Species requirement and how I get the Carrot.

Once the Carrot is in my inventory, that part of the quest is finished.

From there, the important question is no longer how to unlock the boss.

It is whether I am ready to spend a Carrot on an actual attempt.

<h2 id="prepare-before-carrot">
  Prepare before using the Carrot
</h2>

My first bad attempt started because I used the Carrot as soon as I received it.

The Pufferfish appeared immediately, and I found out during the fight that I was
not prepared for how long its health bar would last.

I want my health and food situation settled before the boss appears.

I also want a ranged weapon I can actually control. Owning a more expensive gun
does not help much if half the magazine misses once the Pufferfish starts
moving.

**Beer can restore health**, but in one run it also reduced my food meter.

That makes it emergency healing for me rather than preparation.

I eat before the fight and save Beer for the moment when recovering health is
worth the tradeoff.

<h2 id="tree-dodge-route">
  The safest strategy is to circle one large tree
</h2>

The trees are not just scenery.

They can physically interfere with the Pufferfish's direct approach.

Instead of backing away across the beach, I stay close enough to one large tree
that I can keep changing which side of the trunk I am on.

  <Image

  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">
    I circle the trunk instead of retreating in a straight line. The Pufferfish

  </figcaption>
</figure>

I am not simply hiding behind the tree.

I keep moving around it.

When the Pufferfish commits toward one side, I move around the other side and
make it solve the obstacle again.

That makes its movement much easier for me to read.

The difference is especially noticeable compared with fighting in the open. On
open ground, the boss can keep a clean path toward me. Around a tree, I can
repeatedly break that path without needing to outrun it.

<h2 id="when-to-shoot">
  Shoot when the Pufferfish has to reposition
</h2>

The tree only matters if I turn the interruption into damage.

When the boss commits to one side and has to redirect around the trunk, I stop
long enough to fire.

Then I start moving again before it reaches me cleanly.

The rhythm is:

**bait the approach → break the path with the tree → shoot → move.**

I do not stay in the shooting window until the boss is already on top of me.

One extra burst is rarely worth giving up the spacing I just created.

If I need to reload, I would rather move first and take the next safe angle than
reload in front of a direct charge.

<h2 id="smg-and-upgrades">
  The 650 SMG works better once recoil is under control
</h2>

I saved **650** for the SMG because the Pufferfish looked like a boss where high
fire rate should be useful.

The problem was not how many bullets the weapon could fire.

It was how many I could actually land.

After that attempt, I stopped buying upgrades simply because they were more
expensive.

I looked at what was actually costing me the fight.

When recoil kept dragging my shots off target, weapon control became more
valuable than another generic damage purchase.

The **Compensator costs 600**, and it made much more sense once recoil was the
specific problem I needed to solve.

A Red Dot did not fix that issue for me.

If I can already stay on target but the boss health barely moves, then I look at
damage. If reloads are creating dangerous gaps, magazine capacity becomes more
interesting.

But if my positioning is bad, I stop shopping.

No attachment fixes fighting the Pufferfish in the open.

<h2 id="carrot-is-consumed">
  A failed attempt can cost another Carrot
</h2>

This is one reason I care so much about preparation.

The Carrot is not an unlimited boss lure.

After I spent one and failed, I could not simply walk back and trigger another
free Pufferfish fight.

I needed another Carrot.

That means a failed attempt can send me backward into the previous quest step
before I am allowed to fight the boss again.

So I do not automatically spend another Carrot the moment I respawn.

I first ask why the attempt failed.

If I was hungry, I fix food.

If recoil was wasting most of the magazine, I fix control.

If I was landing clean shots and still lacked damage, I improve damage.

If I kept getting hit despite having a reasonable weapon, I change how I am
using the terrain.

A short preparation detour can be faster than repeating the entire Carrot
requirement after another identical death.

<h2 id="later-phase">
  Give the Pufferfish more room when it grows larger
</h2>

The later part of the fight becomes much more visually aggressive.

The Pufferfish grows larger and can fill the area around itself with a
**purple hazard effect**.

I do not stand inside the effect trying to calculate how much damage I can
survive.

I move.

The tree strategy still works, but I give myself more room before stopping to
shoot again.

This is where greed starts ruining otherwise good attempts.

If the same patient pattern got me through the first part of the fight, I do not
abandon it just because the boss health is getting low.

<h2 id="stay-ranged">
  Do not rush a low-health Pufferfish with the knife
</h2>

The knife is not my finishing strategy.

A nearly dead Pufferfish can still punish a bad approach, and because another
death may mean another Carrot, "almost dead" does not make the mistake cheaper.

I keep using ranged damage until the boss is actually finished.

That may take slightly longer than trying to rush the final sliver of health,
but I would rather spend a few more seconds circling the tree than repeat the
whole setup.

Killing the Pufferfish is not the final progression step.

I also keep an eye on the nearby seagulls.

Loose creatures and drops can be moved around these islands, and in one fight a
missing Pufferfish trophy only became obvious after checking what a nearby bird
had picked up.

So once the boss is dead, my priority is the Tail.

If the Tourist refuses to advance afterward, this is the first thing I verify.

<h2 id="unlock-island-4">
  Return the Pufferfish Tail to the Tourist to unlock Island 4
</h2>

With the Tail in my inventory, I return to the Tourist on Island 3.

Handing it over finishes the boss request.

The progression reward I am waiting for is the:

**Flash Drive with coordinates to Island 4.**

This gives me an easy way to tell where the sequence has stalled.

If the Pufferfish is still alive, I have a boss fight problem.

If the Pufferfish is dead but the Tourist will not advance, I check the
**Pufferfish Tail**.

If the Tail has been handed in and I have the **Island 4 coordinates**, the
Island 3 Pufferfish sequence is complete.

I do not need to kill another Pufferfish after reaching that state.

<h2 id="leave-for-island-4">
  Leave Island 3 once the Island 4 coordinates are registered
</h2>

After the hand-in, I return to the boat and check the Radar.

I do not spend another Carrot or continue searching for another endangered
creature just because Island 3 still has things I could do.

The progression blocker is finished.

If the Radar has not changed, I verify that I picked up the Tail, handed it to
the Tourist, and actually received the Island 4 Flash Drive.

Once those coordinates are registered, I follow the Radar and leave.
