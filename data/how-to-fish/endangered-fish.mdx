<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
For the Tourist on Island 3, I do **not** hunt one specific fish called an "Endangered Fish."

I do this:

**talk to the Tourist**

→ **fish around Island 3**

→ **inspect each useful catch**

→ look for the **Endangered Species** status

→ **keep that creature intact**

→ **give it to the Tourist**

→ take the **Carrot**

A **Needlefish can be a valid answer**, but it is not the only species I look for.

The important check is the endangered status.

Once the Tourist gives me the Carrot, this quest is finished and I move on to the Pufferfish.

<h2 id="where-this-starts">
  I start with the Tourist after reaching Island 3
</h2>

After finishing the Giant Piranha progression and following the new route to Island 3, I find the Tourist who is too afraid to go swimming.

If I have not reached Island 3 yet, I finish the <Link href="/how-to-fish/giant-piranha">Giant Piranha route</Link> first.

The Tourist tells me there is a scary creature in the water.

He has bait that will help with that problem, but he wants something in return first:

**an endangered creature**

This wording is where I originally overcomplicated the quest.

I assumed there must be one exact rare fish hidden somewhere around the island.

That is not the rule I use.

The important part is the creature's **status**, not one fixed species name.

<h2 id="what-counts">
  I inspect the catch for the Endangered Species status
</h2>

When I catch something unfamiliar, I inspect it before I cook or sell it.

The confirmation I want is:

**Endangered Species**

That is the distinction that matters.

I do **not** judge a catch by:

**how rare it looks**

**how hard it was to catch**

or:

**whether it is a Drip variant**

I judge it by the endangered label.

More than one creature can satisfy that category, which is why I do not keep fishing for one exact name after I already have a valid tagged catch.

<h2 id="needlefish">
  Needlefish can work, but I do not treat it as the only solution
</h2>

Needlefish is where the advice around this quest can get confusing.

**Needlefish** comes up as a possible endangered creature around this quest.

So if I catch a Needlefish and its inspection confirms the endangered status, I can use it.

But I do not turn the quest into:

**find a Needlefish or fail**

The broader requirement is still:

**bring an endangered creature**

That means my decision process is:

**catch Needlefish?**

→ inspect it

**catch another unfamiliar creature?**

→ inspect that too

**see the endangered status?**

→ stop fishing and return to the Tourist

This also explains why two players can complete the same Tourist quest with different catches.

The species can differ.

The status is what I use as the reliable check.

<h2 id="bait">
  I do not search the shops for an Endangered Bait
</h2>

My other wrong assumption was that an endangered creature must require its own special lure.

I started thinking:

**rare quest creature = special bait**

But I do not need an item literally called:

**Endangered Bait**

The Tourist has asked me for a type of creature, not for one specific lure setup.

So I do not spend money cycling through boss lures or weapon upgrades while this quest is still incomplete.

I keep the process simple:

**fish**

→ **inspect**

→ **check status**

If the catch does not have the endangered label, I keep fishing.

If it does, the search is finished.

That prevents me from turning a straightforward prerequisite into an unnecessary shopping problem.

<h2 id="keep-the-creature">
  As soon as I find a valid catch, I stop treating it as money or food
</h2>

Island 3 encourages me to do something with almost everything I catch.

I can sell creatures.

I can cook them.

I can eat them.

That habit becomes risky during this quest.

Once I see the endangered status, I mentally turn that creature into a quest item.

I do not:

**cook it**

**sell it**

or:

**eat it because I am hungry**

I take it back to the Tourist first.

The money I could make from one catch is not worth repeating the search because I accidentally used the creature I needed for progression.

If my hunger is already low, I eat something else before I start carrying the endangered catch around.

<h2 id="give-it-to-tourist">
  I give the endangered creature to the Tourist and take the Carrot
</h2>

I return to the same Tourist and hand over the valid creature.

The reward that confirms I have finished this step is:

**Carrot**

That is the completion signal I care about.

I do not need to keep bringing him endangered creatures.

I do not need to find a more specific species afterward.

I already have the item that moves Island 3 forward.

<h2 id="what-the-carrot-is-for">
  Once I have the Carrot, the blocker changes
</h2>

The Carrot is the handoff point between two different problems.

Before the Carrot, my problem is:

**find a valid endangered creature**

After the Carrot, my problem becomes:

**prepare for the Pufferfish**

I do not sell the Carrot or use it as ordinary food.

I keep it for the next progression fight.

From this point onward, I stop worrying about the Tourist's endangered-creature request.

If I already have the Carrot and need to know when to use it, how to survive the fight, how to use the trees, or what to pick up after the boss dies, I switch to the <Link href="/how-to-fish/pufferfish">Pufferfish guide</Link>.

<h2 id="tourist-not-accepting">
  If the Tourist will not accept my catch, I check the status before doing anything else
</h2>

If I bring something back and the Tourist does not progress, I do not immediately assume the quest is bugged.

I inspect the creature again.

The check is:

**does this catch actually have the endangered status?**

A strange-looking creature is not automatically valid.

A difficult catch is not automatically valid.

A Drip creature is not automatically valid.

And a species name that someone else used does not matter if my own catch does not meet the requirement.

So my troubleshooting route is:

**Tourist not progressing**

→ **inspect the creature**

→ **no endangered status**

→ go back to fishing

or:

**endangered status confirmed**

→ **keep the creature intact**

→ return to the Tourist

My complete route is:

**reach Island 3**

→ **talk to the Tourist**

→ **fish and inspect catches**

→ **find an Endangered Species creature**

→ **keep it**

→ **give it to the Tourist**

→ **receive the Carrot**

Once the Carrot is in my inventory, I am done with the Tourist&apos;s endangered-creature request. From there, I use the <Link href="/how-to-fish/pufferfish">Pufferfish route</Link> for the next Island 3 progression step.
