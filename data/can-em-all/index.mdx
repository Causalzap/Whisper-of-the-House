Buy **more cans per creature** and **Premium Cans ($12)** first, then unlock the **Industrial Vacuum ($40)** to move bodies into the Grinder faster.

<h2 id="first-upgrades">
  What Should You Upgrade First in Can 'Em All?
</h2>

Your first factory earns very little because a single creature produces few cans, each can has a low selling value, and nearly every part of production requires manual work.

Early money is better spent improving the value of each completed delivery than buying every combat upgrade as soon as it appears.

Follow this order while your factory is still small:

1. **Increase cans per creature.** The opening upgrade tree allows you to produce two cans from one creature. This improves the output of each kill without requiring more creatures to arrive.
2. **Increase can value.** Premium Cans raises the selling value of your production. More cans and higher prices reinforce each other.
3. **Bring in more creatures.** Add arrivals once you can kill and process the existing batch during the workday.
4. **Improve your attacks when necessary.** Extra damage, a faster attack, or an early weapon matters when creatures are surviving long enough to slow production.
5. **Start connecting your machines.** Conveyor sections become increasingly useful once carrying cans by hand is taking too much of the shift.

The opening progression offers several attractive alternatives, including stronger punches, additional creatures, Shiny-related bonuses, and weapons.

Which one should come next depends on how the last workday ended.

A factory that processes every creature and sells every can has room to increase incoming production. A factory that leaves bodies or cans unfinished needs to improve its existing production chain first.

More arrivals are only profitable when you can turn them into completed deliveries.

### Should You Buy the Baseball Bat Immediately?

The Baseball Bat is an early combat unlock, but it does not automatically deserve your first available money.

During the opening workdays, punching is sufficient for small groups of weak creatures. The Bat becomes more valuable when you are spending too much time defeating creatures instead of collecting and processing them.

Buying it before a can-value upgrade can delay your next increase in income. Conversely, repeatedly struggling with creatures while saving for an expensive production upgrade is also inefficient.

Check whether your attack speed is actually holding up the next delivery. If the factory's limiting factor is already its Grinder, stronger melee attacks will simply create more bodies waiting to be processed.

The Knife offers another early combat improvement. Once firearms become available, their damage and ammunition will require their own investment, so there is little reason to purchase every melee upgrade automatically.

<h2 id="basket-and-vacuum">
  How Do You Move Bodies and Cans Faster?
</h2>

During the first few shifts, much of your working time disappears into carrying bodies to the Grinder and moving finished cans to the seller. Even a small pile of completed cans can require several trips, leaving less time to process the next batch of creatures.

### Use the Basket Before You Unlock the Vacuum

There is a **Basket in the corner of the factory** that can carry multiple cans at once.

Use it to consolidate finished cans instead of making a separate trip for every item. It is particularly useful while you are still carrying most of your output manually.

The Basket does not increase the number of cans produced or their value. It reduces the time required to move the production you already have.

That distinction matters when choosing upgrades. A useful carrying tool cannot compensate for insufficient Grinder speed, but it can prevent finished goods from sitting on the floor until the shift ends.

### Unlock the Vacuum When Manual Handling Becomes the Bottleneck

The **Industrial Vacuum costs $40** to unlock. It lets you suck up creatures' bodies, cans, and other objects, then launch them toward the next part of the production line instead of carrying everything by hand.

With the Vacuum equipped, you can collect bodies and launch them toward the Grinder instead of walking each one into position.

This is especially helpful when creatures are spread across the factory floor or when machines are positioned away from where most kills occur.

Its ordinary upgrades improve different parts of the task:

- **Storage capacity:** Hold more material before unloading.
- **Suction range:** Collect objects from farther away.
- **Firing speed:** Launch collected objects faster.
- **Firing range:** Send bodies farther toward a machine or collection point.

Storage is helpful when collecting several bodies together. Firing speed becomes more valuable once the Vacuum is already full and you spend too much of the shift unloading it.

Range upgrades are particularly useful when you can aim directly at a Grinder from farther away. A later factory setup can send bodies into processing machines without walking back to each intake.

Don't automatically buy every Vacuum upgrade. Look at whether you are spending more time collecting, moving into range, or unloading, and improve the part that is actually slow.

<h2 id="factory-bottlenecks">
  Which Factory Upgrade Should You Buy When Progress Slows Down?
</h2>

There is a major change in the way your factory grows.

Early on, killing another creature produces more money. Later, killing additional creatures can accomplish almost nothing because the Grinders are already full and unfinished bodies are accumulating around them.

A factory can become worse at completing deliveries even while its weapons become dramatically stronger.

The useful question at the end of each shift is: **What stopped the next can from being sold?**

A quick walk around the factory after the last delivery is often enough to identify the limiting step.

Check the bodies waiting near the machines, the cans left around the conveyor system, and the creatures still alive when the shift ends.

You do not need to purchase an upgrade from every branch of the tree each day. Concentrating on the current obstruction usually changes your next day's income more than spreading purchases across unrelated bonuses.

<h2 id="grinder-upgrades">
  When Should You Buy Another Grinder?
</h2>

A Grinder processes creatures one at a time. As your kill rate improves, the number of bodies waiting for processing can grow much faster than the machine can handle.

The early upgrade tree offers reduced machine processing time. This is the first option worth considering when your Grinder is consistently occupied.

Additional machines become useful once you are producing enough bodies to keep more than one Grinder working.

The difference is easy to recognize:

- **One Grinder finishes early:** You probably need more incoming creatures or better can value, not another machine.
- **One Grinder runs continuously but bodies remain:** Improve its processing speed or unlock another machine.
- **Several Grinders are busy and bodies still accumulate:** Additional processing capacity may be more valuable than another combat upgrade.
- **Grinders finish production but cans remain unsold:** The transport system has become the next problem.

One useful safeguard is to inspect your current machine immediately before buying another. Spending heavily on additional processing capacity while your existing machines sit idle will not increase output.

### Should You Increase Cans per Creature or Grinder Speed?

Increasing cans per creature is generally attractive because each processed body produces more saleable output.

The regular upgrades can raise production from two cans per creature through higher amounts such as three, four, and five. A later factory can produce considerably more from each creature.

However, an increase in can output also increases the amount of finished material your transport system needs to handle.

When bodies are already waiting for processing, reducing processing time helps you finish more creatures during the same shift. When machines have spare capacity, increasing cans per creature may provide the more immediate benefit.

These are complementary upgrades, but buying them in the wrong order can leave money trapped in unfinished production.

<h2 id="conveyor-belts">
  How Do You Automate the Factory With Conveyor Belts?
</h2>

Conveyor upgrades are purchased in sections. Unlocking the first section does not necessarily give you a complete automated route from the processing machine to the selling area.

During early factory expansion, a partial belt can move cans closer to their destination while still leaving a manual pickup or delivery step.

The important milestone is connecting enough sections that processed cans can travel along the system without requiring you to carry each one.

Work through the available conveyor sections as your budget permits, then watch what happens to the next batch of cans.

A functional line should reduce the amount of time you spend collecting finished products. If cans still stop at a gap or accumulate at the end of a section, your delivery process is not yet fully automated.

### When Is Conveyor Speed Worth Upgrading?

A connected line is not automatically fast enough.

After you increase the number of cans produced or add more Grinders, the belts may begin carrying a large backlog of cans.

Conveyor Speed matters when finished output is waiting to move through an otherwise connected route.

Buying more speed while your machines rarely produce cans provides little immediate value. Improve the Grinders and incoming production first, then increase belt speed as output rises.

For a factory with several processing machines, inspect each machine's connection independently. One well-connected production line does not mean the rest of your facility has the same level of automation.

### When Should You Buy Corpse Roombas?

The **Corpse Roomba** is an automated helper that moves bodies around the factory.

Its most useful role appears after creature arrivals and weapon damage increase enough that bodies are scattered faster than you can collect them.

A Roomba can reduce the amount of time spent using the Vacuum, especially when several machines need a steady supply of material.

But the Roomba is not an immediate substitute for your entire handling setup. Early versions may still require assistance, while further upgrades improve how effectively the robot performs its job.

Prioritize it when:

- Bodies are spread across the factory floor.
- You regularly stop shooting to recover corpses.
- Your Grinders have enough capacity to process more material.
- You want to spend less of the shift handling individual bodies.

A Roomba cannot solve a processing bottleneck by itself. Moving more corpses into a machine that is already overloaded does not increase the machine's throughput.

<h2 id="making-more-money">
  How Do You Make More Money From Every Can?
</h2>

Once bodies are moving through the factory reliably, the next major income gains come from making each creature and each finished can worth more.

Several ordinary upgrades multiply different parts of the production chain.

### How Do More Cans and Premium Cans Work Together?

**Premium Cans costs $12** for its first upgrade and increases can value from 0% to **+200%**.

The value bonus becomes more useful as your factory produces additional cans from each creature. Raising output from three cans to four adds one more saleable can per processed creature, and that extra can benefits from your existing price upgrades.

Once your factory can process deliveries consistently, continue improving can quantity and selling value together instead of buying only one type of upgrade.

### Shiny Cans and Duplication Are Different Upgrades

The regular tree also includes Shiny-related bonuses and duplication effects.

They should not be treated as interchangeable.

- **Shiny creature chance:** Improves the chance that valuable Shiny creatures arrive.
- **Shiny can chance:** Provides opportunities to produce more valuable Shiny cans.
- **Shiny value upgrades:** Improve what Shiny output is worth.
- **Can duplication:** Gives produced cans a chance to duplicate.
- **Money duplication:** Gives money earned from cans a chance to duplicate.

One midgame upgrade raised the chance of duplicating each produced can to **10%**. This illustrates why the production tree becomes especially profitable once multiple cans are being generated from each creature.

Shiny bonuses can also provide strong returns, but their early value depends on how often you actually obtain Shiny output.

Avoid purchasing only probability bonuses while ignoring basic can production. More expensive output is helpful only when enough finished product makes it to the selling system.

### Is Hole in One Better Than Another Conveyor Belt?

The first **Hole in One upgrade costs $20,000** and increases the chance of earning bonus money from shooting cans directly into the seller from **0% to 10%**.

Manual shots are manageable when only a small number of cans need delivering. Once several Grinders are producing continuously, connected conveyor belts let you spend more of the workday handling creatures and keeping the machines supplied.

### Are Smoked Cans Worth Producing?

The late-game **Smoked Meat upgrade costs $100,000,000**. Smoked creatures produce Smoked Cans worth **twice as much** as normal cans.

This provides another way to increase the value of a creature before its remains enter the normal processing system.

Still, the production chain must keep up. Higher-priced cans are helpful, but bodies left unprocessed do not deliver that value.

<h2 id="weapons-and-turrets">
  Which Weapons and Turrets Are Worth Upgrading?
</h2>

The weapon tree grows from melee attacks into firearms, explosive weapons, and automatic defenses.

Your early combat upgrades are mainly about clearing the day's creatures quickly enough to begin processing them. Later, weapon selection becomes more important as creature numbers rise and tougher targets take additional hits.

The game includes weapons such as the **Baseball Bat, Knife, Pistol, SMG, AK-style rifle, Shotgun, Chainsaw, RPG, and Flamethrower**.

They do not all solve the same problem.

### Melee Weapons and the Pistol

Melee weapons are useful early because they provide attacks without depending on the firearm ammunition supply.

The Pistol becomes a practical option when melee attacks are taking too long or creatures are difficult to reach.

However, ammunition is limited. During a long shift, a gun can run dry before all available creatures have been killed.

That is when damage and ammunition upgrades become more important than simply unlocking another weapon.

The regular tree includes improvements to the ammunition available at the start of a day and chances to recover a bullet after killing a creature.

Use these to support sustained combat rather than relying on a powerful weapon that cannot fire for the rest of the shift.

### Shotgun, RPG, and Chainsaw

The Shotgun becomes useful when you want a powerful short-range attack. Upgrades can improve its pellet count and the delay between shots.

The RPG introduces explosive attacks, with upgrades affecting explosion radius and damage.

The Chainsaw offers another way to attack at close range, but it has a fuel limitation. Running out of fuel during a workday makes it unreliable as the only weapon you depend on.

These weapons become attractive when the existing combat setup is struggling with the creatures arriving at the factory.

Do not assume buying the most expensive weapon available will always improve overall income. Once your Grinders are overloaded, additional firepower can make the backlog larger without increasing the number of cans sold.

### When Should You Buy an Automatic Turret?

The **Automatic Turret** reduces the amount of direct shooting required during a shift.

After unlocking it, the turret can begin attacking arriving creatures without waiting for you to aim at every target.

Later upgrades improve turret firepower, firing behavior, and ammunition capacity. Additional turrets also become available as the factory grows.

Turrets are especially useful when:

- Large groups of creatures arrive together.
- You need to spend more time operating the Vacuum or feeding Grinders.
- Firearm ammunition is becoming a concern.
- You want creature clearing to continue while you manage production.

Prioritize turret damage and ammunition when creatures regularly survive until the shift ends. Once your turrets already clear incoming batches reliably, spend the next upgrade budget on whichever part of the factory is delaying completed can sales.

<h2 id="workday-management">
  Should You Extend the Workday or Buy Another Upgrade?
</h2>

The factory's original working day is short. In one early progression, purchasing workday extensions increased the available time from **60 seconds to 1 minute 45 seconds**.

More time can allow you to kill additional creatures, process more bodies, and complete more deliveries.

But the benefit depends on whether there is productive work available throughout those extra seconds.

A longer shift is attractive when you regularly reach the timer with machines still operating, bodies waiting to be processed, or cans about to be delivered.

It is less urgent when the factory has already processed everything and you are waiting for the next batch of creatures.

There is another useful detail about creature arrivals: some factory setups do not immediately receive a fresh batch while the previous creatures remain on the floor.

If more creatures are not arriving, check for survivors before assuming that the spawn upgrades have stopped working.

### What Should You Do Before the Shift Ends?

During the final part of the day, decide whether you can still complete another sale.

A body that cannot reach processing and delivery before the end of the shift may contribute nothing to the current day's earnings.

Before purchasing the permanent <Link href="/can-em-all/prestige-ending">Cold Storage upgrade</Link>, prioritize finishing deliveries before the shift ends because creatures and cans are normally cleared overnight.

Also remember that buying another ordinary upgrade at the end of the day can be more valuable than purchasing something that will not contribute until several shifts later.

<h2 id="slot-machine">
  Is the Slot Machine Worth Using?
</h2>

The factory eventually provides access to a **Slot Machine** in your living quarters.

It offers a chance to win additional money, but a failed bet can remove cash that would otherwise fund your next guaranteed upgrade.

During extended factory progression, repeated gambling sometimes produced useful wins and sometimes consumed substantial amounts of money.

There is no demonstrated betting pattern that guarantees a positive return.

Treat gambling as an optional activity, not a required way to fund the factory.

A practical rule is to secure your next important production purchase before risking money on the Slot Machine. Losing a bet is much less damaging when it does not postpone the Grinder, conveyor connection, or weapon upgrade your next shift actually needs.

If you are trying to finish the normal upgrade tree quickly, dependable factory production is the more controllable way to progress.

<h2 id="late-game-progression">
  What Should You Focus On After the Factory Is Automated?
</h2>

Once multiple Grinders, connected conveyors, automated body handling, and turrets are operating together, your money increases much faster than it did during the opening workdays.

The upgrade tree continues expanding, however.

Later choices include more valuable creature types, additional machines, stronger weapons, larger production quantities, and improved automation.

One late-stage creature unlock was priced at **$50 million**, showing how far the cost of expansion can grow beyond the opening factory.

Before committing $50 million to another creature unlock, run a full shift with your current setup. Bodies still waiting beside occupied Grinders or finished cans stranded before the seller are reasons to improve the existing production line before increasing arrivals.

After expanding the ordinary factory tree, you can either <Link href="/can-em-all/prestige-ending">sell the factory for permanent upgrades or work toward Freedom</Link>. Both options become important once the remaining purchases grow expensive.

Before leaving, check your <Link href="/can-em-all/achievements">12 Steam achievements</Link>. Several require specific interactions with factory equipment and will not unlock through ordinary production alone.
