<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
To Prestige, choose **Sell Your Factory**, check the points offered, and confirm the reset. To finish the game instead, complete every regular factory upgrade, purchase **Freedom at Last** for **$666,666,666**, and leave through the upstairs exit.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

<h2 id="prestige-or-freedom">
  Should You Prestige or Pay Off Your Debt First?
</h2>

The choice becomes important once Happy Meat Food Corp offers to buy your factory. You can keep expanding your existing operation toward Freedom, or sell it and rebuild with permanent upgrades that make later runs faster.

Those choices do different things:

- **Sell Your Factory:** Ends your current factory run, awards permanent upgrade points based on the factory's value, and sends you back to a fresh facility.
- **Freedom:** Makes the final debt repayment, provides your exit key, and allows you to leave the factory.

Selling the factory is not the same as paying off your debt. You may return to the opening equipment and machines after Prestige, even though your permanent bonuses remain active.

The final requirement shown for Freedom is to complete the regular factory upgrade tree. The unlock does not display a required number of factory sales, so check your remaining ordinary upgrades before deciding whether another reset is worthwhile.

If you are already close to finishing the regular upgrade tree and only want to see the ending, stay with your current factory. Prestige is more attractive when you intend to continue playing and can use the permanent points to accelerate the next run.

<h2 id="how-to-prestige">
  How Do You Sell Your Factory and Prestige?
</h2>

Prestige becomes available after you receive an offer from Happy Meat Food Corp. The message congratulates you on building a valuable factory and proposes buying it in exchange for permanent upgrade points.

Accepting the offer unlocks the option to sell whenever you are ready. It does not require you to give up the factory immediately.

Once the option appears:

1. Read and accept the **factory purchase offer** from Happy Meat Food Corp.
2. Return to the factory sale option when you are ready to reset.
3. Check the number of **Permanent Upgrade Points** displayed for your current factory.
4. Confirm **Sell Your Factory** to start a new facility.
5. Spend your points in the permanent upgrade tree before rebuilding your ordinary production setup.

One factory sale confirmation showed **$2,519,132 generated** in exchange for **50 Permanent Upgrade Points**. That amount reflects the factory being sold; it is not a guaranteed reward for reaching a particular workday.

Check the current point offer before pressing Sell. Continuing to develop the factory can increase the reward, but the next permanent upgrade's cost should determine whether the reset is worth taking.

The practical reason to check the confirmation before selling is simple: you want enough points to purchase something that will materially improve the next run, rather than resetting for a reward you cannot use.

<h2 id="what-resets">
  What Do You Keep and Lose After Prestige?
</h2>

Your normal factory progress does not survive the sale. After the first reset, the player returned to weak starting attacks without the Vacuum that had been available before selling. Ordinary equipment and factory upgrades needed to be purchased again.

The permanent upgrade tree is the exception.

After the reset, your next purchases depend on the bonuses already active. Starting Cash can shorten the wait for equipment, while Brute Force can make the opening creatures easier to kill before you regain your old weapons.

Rebuild the production line according to what slows the new facility down. The <Link href="/can-em-all">Vacuum, Grinder, and conveyor upgrade choices</Link> become relevant again as soon as your early can sales begin.

<h2 id="best-permanent-upgrades">
  Which Permanent Upgrades Should You Buy First?
</h2>

The first Prestige awarded 50 Permanent Upgrade Points. The player spent **25 points on Shiny Shipment**, which makes every creature in the first truck of each day Shiny.

This gives the next factory a predictable supply of valuable Shiny creatures instead of relying entirely on random spawns. Ordinary upgrades that increase can value can then build on that early advantage.

These are not equally valuable at every point in your progression.

**Starting Cash** reduces the time spent recovering early equipment and infrastructure. Higher starting-cash tiers become more appealing when you have enough points to reach them without giving up other useful bonuses.

**Profit Boost** provides a broad earnings increase. Unlike a bonus tied to a particular creature type, it can support income across different stages of the factory.

**Grinder Speed** addresses processing capacity. Killing more creatures does not automatically mean selling more cans when bodies are waiting to be processed, so a permanent speed increase can help the next facility convert production into actual sales.

**Brute Force** makes the reset's early combat less restrictive, particularly before the normal weapon tree has been rebuilt.

Shiny Shipment improves the value of your first delivery each day, while Profit Boost supports earnings more broadly. Choose according to whether your next run needs stronger early deliveries or additional income across the whole shift.

### Which Later Permanent Upgrades Change the Factory Most?

The larger Prestige reset opens access to more specialized benefits, including:

- **Death Touch:** Improves your chance of instantly killing a creature.
- **First Blood (permanent upgrade):** The first 10 creatures of a run die in one hit.
- **Damaged Delivery:** Causes 25% of arriving creatures to start at half health.

### Why Is Cold Storage Worth Buying?

**Cold Storage costs 100 Permanent Upgrade Points** and prevents creatures and cans from being cleared overnight.

This changes how you handle unfinished production. Bodies and cans can remain in the factory after the shift ends, allowing loaded Grinders to resume processing when the next day begins.

That is a better reason to consider another short Prestige cycle than simply watching your current cash balance rise. At that stage, the missing reward was specific and affordable after another reset.

<h2 id="when-to-prestige-again">
  When Is It Worth Prestiging Again?
</h2>

You do not need to sell every time the option appears. The displayed point reward can increase substantially as you develop the factory further.

The observed progression illustrates the difference:

  columns={prestigeOfferColumns}
  rows={prestigeOfferRows}
/>

The 16-point offer was rejected because it was not attractive enough for the upgrades the player wanted. Later, the 515-point sale funded a large portion of the remaining permanent tree.

This does not mean everyone should wait for exactly 515 points. Use the available reward and the next purchase as your decision:

1. Open the factory sale option and check the permanent points available.
2. Identify the next permanent upgrade you actually want.
3. Continue the current factory if selling now cannot fund that purchase.
4. Sell once the reward justifies rebuilding, or remain in the run if you are approaching Freedom.

A separate distinction matters near the end: having the money for more ordinary upgrades does not automatically make another Prestige worthwhile. A factory that is nearly finished may be closer to the final exit than to the benefits of restarting.

<h2 id="unlock-freedom">
  How Do You Unlock Freedom and Pay Off Your Debt?
</h2>

The upstairs exit displays a contractual restriction while you still owe money. You cannot simply walk through it after making your first large profits.

The actual ending requirement is tied to **completing the regular factory upgrade tree**.

Once every available factory upgrade has been purchased, Happy Meat Food Corp sends a message confirming that you qualify for the final debt repayment.

The final upgrade is called **Freedom at Last** and costs **$666,666,666**. It appears at the end of the regular factory upgrade tree. Purchase it to clear your remaining debt and receive the exit key. Unlocking the node alone does not open the upstairs exit.

Follow this order:

1. Purchase every regular factory upgrade to unlock **Freedom at Last**.
2. Read the completion message confirming that you qualify for the final debt repayment.
3. Return to the final upgrade terminal and locate **Freedom at Last**.
4. Earn **$666,666,666** if you cannot afford the final purchase yet.
5. Purchase **Freedom at Last** to clear your debt and receive the exit key.

An earlier message from another employee may mention receiving an exit key, but it does not unlock your own exit. Your debt is cleared only after you make the final purchase and receive the confirmation.


<h2 id="exit-key-ending">
  Where Do You Use the Exit Key to Finish the Game?
</h2>

After purchasing Freedom, another employee message confirms that you are officially permitted to leave the factory.

The exit is **upstairs**, at the door that previously refused to let you leave while your debt remained unpaid. It is not a new room hidden among the Grinders or conveyor belts.

Once you have paid for Freedom:

1. Finish your current factory activity and return toward the elevator.
2. Go back upstairs to the previously locked exit.
3. Approach the door after receiving the confirmation that you may leave.
4. Use the exit to trigger the ending.

If the exit still says you cannot leave until your debt is paid, return to the upgrade terminal and confirm that **Freedom was purchased**, not merely unlocked. Buying more ordinary upgrades after the Freedom option appears is not a substitute for the final repayment.

Leaving through the upstairs exit triggers the retirement ending and closing credits. Purchasing the final upgrade gives you permission to leave; walking out completes the escape.
