<GuideQuickAnswer>
Clear Rock Bottom in three steps: restore the mine reactor in **Power Problems**, destroy the Acidium plumbing in **Running on Fumes**, then finish **Hive Mind** by sealing the Hive and continuing until you recover and decrypt Dr. Pierce's intel.

Refill before activating a terminal, breaker, valve, or console whenever supplies are nearby. Most of those interactions commit the fireteam to another swarm.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

<h2 id="rock-bottom-walkthrough-for-all-three-missions">
  Rock Bottom walkthrough for all three missions
</h2>

Rock Bottom is where I stop treating every objective marker as a simple destination. Most of the important markers here are switches that commit the fireteam to another defense: three door terminals and two breakers in Power Problems, three self-destruct consoles in Running on Fumes, then two Hive terminals, a destruction command, and two late intel holds in Hive Mind.

The opening mission is heavily Xenomorph-focused, so I bring thermal damage when it already fits the loadout and keep at least one crowd-control option for the longer fixed defenses. I do not spend both strong consumables in the first tunnel; the third door terminal and reactor room are much better places to use them.

Before launching, I make sure the primary and signature weapons have their available attachments and that the loadout meets the mission's Combat Rating requirement. Buying one attachment I expect to keep using is more valuable than gambling most of my early Requisition on a Challenge Card.

The mines provide regular ammunition and one Aid Kit per human player near many resupply points. I do not buy consumables merely to survive the travel sections. I save them for the rooms where an interaction limits movement or starts a long hold.

For broader damage-type, ammo, Perk Grid, and weapon-purchase decisions, use the <Link href="/aliens-fireteam-elite-2/beginner-guide">beginner guide</Link>.

If the mines are not available yet, finish the <Link href="/aliens-fireteam-elite-2/boarding-party-walkthrough">Boarding Party walkthrough</Link> and complete the required Endeavor conversations first.

<h2 id="mission-4-1-power-problems">
  Mission 4-1: Power Problems walkthrough
</h2>

<h3 id="open-the-mine-and-clear-the-three-door-terminals">
  Open the mine and clear the three door terminals
</h3>

Push through the exterior approach until the first dark-code interaction opens the way into the tunnels.

The first easy mistake is running through the newly opened door with half a magazine because another terminal is already marked ahead. The next room is not a quiet travel section. I clear the immediate enemies, refill, and only then continue toward the entrance terminal.

There are **three door-terminal defenses** in total.

At the first terminal, I activate the control, hold the route I just used, and wait until the door fully opens before moving on.

At the second, I stay close enough to the nearby ammunition that someone can refill without abandoning the firing lane.

The third terminal is the longest hold. I use the nearby resupply before touching it rather than assuming I can reorganize after the door opens, because the toxic green section beyond it is a poor place to stop.

If the objective appears stuck after a wave, I do not immediately backtrack through the mine. I first confirm that the terminal interaction completed and check the room, walls, and ceiling for a surviving Xenomorph. The door advances only after the hold is resolved.

<h3 id="flip-both-breakers-in-the-battery-room">
  Flip both breakers in the battery room
</h3>

After the third door opens, I pass through the contaminated section and keep moving until I reach clean air and the battery room.

There are **two breakers** here. They are close enough to activate during the same encounter, but each interaction leaves me exposed.

I clear the immediate pack, flip the first breaker, then turn back toward the room instead of sprinting blindly toward the second marker. Once the enemies that entered during the interaction are under control, I cross to the second breaker.

In co-op, one player can operate the breaker while someone else holds the firing lane. Solo, I wait for a safe opening rather than trading a large amount of health just to finish the interaction a few seconds sooner.

Once both breakers are active, the reactor becomes the next objective.

I do not use an Aid Kit for light damage here if I can safely continue. A full kit is much more useful during the reactor defense, and there is another resupply point on the approach.

<h3 id="reset-the-reactor-and-vent-the-pressure">
  Reset the reactor, then vent the pressure
</h3>

The reactor room contains two different interactions separated by a long defense.

I begin by resetting the reactor. That starts the hold and causes the system pressure to rise.

I fight near the ammunition supply rather than following individual Xenomorphs around the machinery. The unlimited-ammo sidearm is useful if the main weapons run low, but I treat it as an emergency option rather than the weapon I want in hand when an elite reaches the platform.

The important change happens in the middle of the fight.

When the objective updates to the **pressure valve**, someone has to leave the firing position and turn it. Waiting for the swarm to stop is the trap—the pressure does not stabilize on its own.

After the valve reports that pressure is stabilized, I return to the same defensible position and continue fighting until the reactor reset completes.

There is no second hidden switch after the valve. The mission finishes once the remaining defense and reactor sequence resolve.

<h3 id="before-running-on-fumes">
  Before Running on Fumes
</h3>

Back on the Endeavor, I complete the conversations that advance the campaign. They establish that Acidium is being pumped through the colony plumbing and unlock Running on Fumes.

This is the point where I upgrade only if the reactor room exposed a real weakness. If the loadout handled the mission comfortably, I keep my Requisition rather than replacing a weapon simply because new stock has appeared.

Running on Fumes still presents a predictable Xenomorph threat, so positioning and having a dependable weapon matter more than making a speculative purchase between missions.

<h2 id="mission-4-2-running-on-fumes">
  Mission 4-2: Running on Fumes walkthrough
</h2>

<h3 id="close-the-gas-valve-and-enter-the-plumbing-tunnels">
  Close the gas valve and enter the plumbing tunnels
</h3>

The first room shows the mission's hazard immediately. Fire is venting from the pipes, and fighting beside it indefinitely will not open the route.

I clear the first rush, move to the marked gas valve between spawns, close it, and continue only after the burning vent reports shut.

From there, I follow the safe corridor into the former office and search the marked point before leaving. That intel interaction is part of the route, so I send it before continuing into the plumbing tunnels.

Steam vents farther ahead release Acidium. I cross during the clear gaps instead of standing in the green cloud to finish an enemy.

If I reach this section already low on health, I use the Aid Kit I am carrying and take the replacement near the next ammo crate. Carrying a kit past a free replacement while injured wastes a safe heal.

Continue through the tunnels to the warehouse, locate the Acidium stock, and use the marked terminal for the next intel upload.

That upload draws a sustained wave. I hold beside the ammunition rather than directly on top of the terminal and wait until the objective confirms that the transmission finished before moving on.

<h3 id="trigger-all-three-self-destruct-consoles">
  Trigger all three self-destruct consoles
</h3>

The main plumbing room contains **three consoles**.

Activating the first begins the self-destruct sequence, but the other two still have to be triggered individually while Xenomorphs continue entering the room.

I work around the room in a loop: activate one console, return to a defensible position, clear the immediate pressure, refill when the route is safe, then move to the next marker.

Trying to press all three consoles in one sprint only works when the team already controls the room. If an elite enters while I am moving between them, I kill or disable it before committing to another interaction.

There is no separate extraction marker hidden in another plumbing tunnel. Survive the blast sequence and the mission returns the fireteam to the Endeavor.

<h3 id="before-hive-mind">
  Before Hive Mind
</h3>

I use the debrief to finish any weapon or perk purchase I had already planned, but I do not spend heavily on a narrow anti-Hive setup.

Hive Mind begins with familiar Xenomorph pressure but ends with a new armored threat and another prolonged hold. A balanced primary, a close-range answer for the Hive, and at least one crowd-control option are safer than committing the entire loadout to a single damage type.

<h2 id="mission-4-3-hive-mind">
  Mission 4-3: Hive Mind walkthrough
</h2>

<h3 id="clear-the-two-terminal-holds-and-enter-the-hive">
  Clear the two terminal holds and enter the Hive
</h3>

Enter the mines, clear the opening area, and follow the tunnels until Piper calls out the terminal objectives.

There are **two terminal holds** this time.

I activate Terminal One, finish its wave, and use the supply point between objectives before continuing to Terminal Two.

Terminal Two opens the route into the Hive.

This section is deliberately harder to read than the industrial rooms. Resin narrows the passages, enemies move along the walls and ceiling, and Facehuggers punish anyone who runs too far ahead.

I follow the open passage instead of treating every side pocket as another possible route. When the objective changes to exiting the Hive, I keep winding forward until the industrial environment appears again.

<h3 id="enter-the-destruction-command-and-keep-moving">
  Enter the destruction command and keep moving
</h3>

At the Hive exit, I reach the destruction terminal.

If supplies are available nearby, I refill before interacting. The escape that follows is long enough that spending a few extra seconds here is safer than reaching the mining facility on reserve ammunition.

Enter the destruction command to seal the Hive.

The important decision during the escape is knowing when not to stop and fight.

I clear enemies blocking the narrow route, but I do not turn every new spawn into another full hold. I keep moving until the dialogue confirms that the fireteam has returned to the mining facility.

If a teammate is grabbed or downed, I stabilize the immediate corridor before attempting the rescue. Starting a revive while the pursuing wave still controls the lane simply gives the swarm another stationary target.

<h3 id="search-the-cryopods-and-survive-the-red-alarm">
  Search the cryopods and survive the red alarm
</h3>

Returning to the industrial section does **not** finish Hive Mind.

I continue until I find Dr. Pierce's cryopods, search the marked terminal, and send the recovered intel.

That discovery triggers a red alarm and introduces armored hybrids. Their exterior absorbs far more punishment than an ordinary runner, so I concentrate the team's damage on one target instead of spreading fire across several enemies.

I stay close to the room's ammunition supply during the first alarm hold.

Once that defense ends, Piper reports that the recovered intel is encrypted. I return to the terminal and begin the decryption instead of following the extraction direction immediately.

That interaction starts the **final large wave**.

This is where I spend anything I deliberately saved. There is no later Rock Bottom defense that benefits from carrying a turret, enhanced ammunition, grenade, or stim back to the Endeavor.

I place deployables where they cover an approach without blocking the team's retreat, refill before the armored target arrives, and keep crowd control on the smaller enemies while the squad concentrates damage on the hybrid.

When the decryption and final wave are complete, Raptor confirms the pickup.

<h2 id="what-to-do-after-rock-bottom">
  What to do after Rock Bottom
</h2>

Completing Hive Mind finishes Rock Bottom and unlocks **Canary**.

Before moving on, I check four things:

- Power Problems, Running on Fumes, and Hive Mind should all show complete. If I returned after only sealing the Hive, I still need the cryopod intel and decryption sequence.
- I equip attachment rewards only on weapons I expect to carry into the quarry rather than buying around a temporary weakness from the previous mission.
- I keep a general-purpose loadout available. The next campaign opens with Synthetics and gun turrets, so a setup built entirely around the Rock Bottom Xenomorph encounters becomes much less flexible.
- If Canary did not unlock, I check which Rock Bottom mission lacks completion before replaying anything. Intel and Endeavor conversations advance the story, but the achievement requires the three-mission campaign clear.
