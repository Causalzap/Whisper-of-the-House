<h2 id="best-order-for-100-percent">
  Best order for unlocking all 30 achievements
</h2>

I do not approach the list as 30 separate objectives.

Seven achievements come directly from campaign progression, while weapon XP, Kit rank, attachments, cosmetics, and kill totals can advance in the background at the same time.

My first run is therefore the main campaign on **Casual or higher**. I keep one useful Kit active long enough to work toward Rank 10 and leave one dependable weapon in the loadout until it reaches four stars.

Between missions, I complete the cheap Armory requirements whenever the necessary cosmetic or attachment is already available. I also attempt one reasonable Challenge Card run once cards have unlocked.

After the story, I switch to short, familiar missions for the three higher-difficulty clears and the no-friendly-fire attempt. Only after those are finished do I deliberately spend time on Specialist, Horde Mode, ten two-star weapons, 50 attachments, five Augments, ten hats, and whatever kill counters remain.

Changing Kits and weapons constantly during the story makes almost every later progression achievement take longer. I only abandon the long-term setup when Combat Rating blocks the next mission or the weapon is genuinely poorly suited to the enemy list.

<h2 id="campaign-achievements">
  Campaign achievements and Legionnaire's Service Medal
</h2>

The campaign itself accounts for seven achievements:

- **Welcoming Committee** — Prologue.
- **It's a rescue mission, you'll love it!** — Rescue and Recovery.
- **Worst Party Ever** — Boarding Party.
- **Canary** — Rock Bottom.
- **The Story of the Quarry** — Ancient Anomaly.
- **Reckoning** — Final Endeavor.
- **Legionnaire's Service Medal** — every campaign completed on Casual or higher.

I start the Prologue on Casual or above and keep at least that difficulty for the entire story run. That way the individual campaign achievements and Legionnaire's Service Medal progress together.

The campaign order is:

**Prologue → Rescue and Recovery → Boarding Party → Rock Bottom → Ancient Anomaly → Final Endeavor**

<h2 id="difficulty-achievements">
  Intense, Extreme, and Insane achievements
</h2>

The three main difficulty achievements need only **one completed mission** on each named setting:

- **Hardened Trooper** — Intense.
- **Noble Soldier** — Extreme.
- **Fervent Warrior** — Insane.

Insane becomes available after completing the campaign on another difficulty, so I leave these clears until after the story.

I use a mission the squad already knows well rather than choosing the Queen fight or another complicated late-game route. Knowing exactly where the ammunition, Aid Kits, objective interactions, and holdout lanes are is more valuable than choosing a mission solely because its displayed Combat Rating looks lower.

<h3 id="pure-professionals">
  Do Pure Professionals as a separate Intense run
</h3>

**Pure Professionals** requires completing a mission on **Intense or higher without anyone taking friendly-fire damage**.

This is primarily a positioning achievement.

I prefer Intense rather than adding Extreme or Insane pressure to the same attempt. I choose a familiar mission, keep firing lanes separated, and avoid weapons that encourage several players to shoot through the same narrow corridor.

During a holdout, each player gets an angle. If someone needs to rotate across another marine's line, I stop firing before they cross.

The same rule applies when an elite pushes the team. I do not keep pouring heavy fire through a teammate just because the target is almost dead.

If friendly fire happens near the start of the run, I still finish when the group needs the Intense clear or weapon XP. If Pure Professionals is the only reason for being there, restarting early saves more time than spending consumables on an attempt that can no longer satisfy the condition.

<h2 id="armory-achievements">
  Clear the easy Armory and cosmetic achievements early
</h2>

Several achievements take almost no additional mission time once the right items have appeared:

**Next Top Marine** needs cosmetics equipped on the head, torso, and legs.

**Not Just For Decoration** needs at least one attachment equipped on a sidearm.

**Yellow Makes It Shoot Better** needs one colorway and three decals on the same gun.

**How Do You See Out Of These Things?** requires collecting ten hats.

I do the first three as soon as the required options are available rather than leaving them for final cleanup.

For the sidearm requirement, I open the sidearm I already use and equip a compatible attachment. There is no need to buy several new sidearms just for the achievement.

The ten-hat achievement is different because it checks the collection rather than one cosmetic setup. I equip a hat while completing Next Top Marine, then let campaign rewards and later purchases build the collection naturally before buying around whatever remains.

<h2 id="weapon-attachment-achievements">
  Combine the weapon and attachment achievements
</h2>

Five achievements fit naturally into the same progression plan:

**Hell and Back**, **Fully Loaded**, **Well Stocked Arsenal**, **Overly Attached**, and **Voided Warranty**.

I start by taking one dependable weapon all the way to **four stars**.

That completes Hell and Back and opens the weapon's Augment slot, making the same gun the natural candidate for Fully Loaded.

For **Fully Loaded**, every compatible attachment slot on that weapon needs to be filled, including the Augment slot.

Once the first gun reaches four stars, I stop feeding every mission into it if 100% completion is now the goal. **Well Stocked Arsenal** needs ten weapons at two stars, so I rotate nine additional weapons through reliable missions until each reaches that breakpoint.

I still favor stable mission clears over a theoretically faster farm that repeatedly fails. Weapon XP only helps if the run reaches extraction.

**Overly Attached** requires 50 owned weapon attachments, while **Voided Warranty** requires five Augments. Those items can arrive through the Armory and mission rewards, so I let normal progression contribute before spending large amounts of Requisition on random attachment categories.

I buy directly when one compatible attachment completes Fully Loaded or when the collection is nearly finished and I know exactly what is missing.

<h2 id="kit-perk-achievements">
  Rank one Kit and fill the Perk Board
</h2>

Three achievements use Kit progression:

**Seasoned Veteran** requires one Kit at **Rank 10**.

**Maxed Out** requires every slot in a Perk Board to be filled.

**Custom Made Marine** requires five completed missions with Specialist.

I keep one useful Kit active through most of the first campaign instead of changing after every extraction. That lets Rank 10 progress naturally while the story is already providing mission XP.

For Maxed Out, the important check is whether every available board slot is occupied. This is not the moment to judge whether the temporary layout is an ideal combat build.

<h2 id="horde-mode-achievement">
  Survive five Horde waves for Wave Goodbye
</h2>

**Wave Goodbye** requires surviving **five waves in Horde Mode**.

It is a survival threshold, not a demand to stay until every possible wave has been completed.

I enter with a loadout already proven in the campaign, decide the firing lanes before the first wave, and keep the group near its resupply route instead of chasing the last runner across the arena.

This is not the time to combine three experiments at once. I avoid entering with an unfamiliar weapon, an unfinished Perk Board, and a Kit I have never used simply because all three still need XP.

Once the fifth wave registers, I can leave the achievement itself behind. Continuing is useful only when the team still wants weapon XP, kill progress, or Horde rewards.

<h2 id="kill-achievements">
  Leave the large kill counters until the rest is finished
</h2>

Five achievements are cumulative combat totals:

- **Head Hunter** — 1,000 headshot kills.
- **Another Bug Hunt** — 10,000 Xenomorph kills.
- **Decommission Mission** — 1,000 Combat Automatons.
- **Purge the Unclean** — 1,000 Pathogen.
- **Spilled Milk** — 1,000 Synthetics.

I do not deliberately farm these during the first campaign.

The full story, Intense/Extreme/Insane clears, five Specialist missions, Horde Mode, weapon ranking, and Challenge runs already contribute thousands of kills. Farming first creates work that later mandatory runs would have completed anyway.

Only after those objectives are done do I check which counter remains.

For enemy-specific totals, I choose missions by the **enemy list shown before launch**. Xenomorphs, Pathogen, Synthetics, and Combat Automatons have separate counters, so I do not assume an enemy advances multiple categories because it looks mechanically or biologically similar.

Headshots are easier for me to preserve during Synthetic-heavy missions because upright ranged enemies provide more predictable weak points than Xenomorphs moving across walls and ceilings.

If **Another Bug Hunt** is the last achievement remaining, I replay a Xenomorph-heavy mission or continue Horde Mode. At that point, buying more gear does not solve the bottleneck—the remaining requirement is simply enemy count.

<h2 id="what-to-check-if-an-achievement-is-missing">
  What to check if one achievement is still missing
</h2>

Before starting another long replay, I look at the specific type of requirement.

If **Legionnaire's Service Medal** is missing, I check the six individual campaign achievements first.

If **Pure Professionals** is missing, I treat it as a dedicated Intense-or-higher run and make zero friendly-fire damage the priority from the first room.

If **Fully Loaded** is missing, I inspect the chosen weapon for an unfilled attachment slot or missing Augment.

If **Maxed Out** is missing, I check the entire Perk Board for one remaining empty square rather than rebuilding the whole Kit.

If **Worthiness Tested** is still incomplete and no cards appear in mission setup, I progress further or check Wrench aboard the Endeavor.

For a kill achievement, I stop changing equipment and choose missions that actually contain the enemy family still required.

That diagnosis prevents the longest kind of achievement cleanup: replaying a campaign, difficulty, or enemy type that cannot advance the requirement I am missing.
