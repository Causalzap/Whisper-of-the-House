<h2 id="where-to-start">
  Where to start in Aliens Fireteam Elite 2
</h2>

Aliens: Fireteam Elite 2 is a linear four-marine co-op shooter built around **14 campaign missions across six stages**. There is no campaign branch to choose on a first playthrough, so the first question is usually not *which region should I do?* but *what kind of problem is stopping me?*

If I am unsure about the next story mission, I use the <Link href="/aliens-fireteam-elite-2/walkthrough">complete campaign walkthrough and mission order</Link>.

If the mission is already unlocked but my Kit, weapons, Combat Rating, damage type, or Requisition spending feels wrong, I use the <Link href="/aliens-fireteam-elite-2/beginner-guide">beginner guide</Link> before buying more equipment.

Once I am inside a campaign, I use its dedicated walkthrough for the actual terminals, holdouts, route changes, and boss mechanics rather than trying to solve every later objective from the Hub.

<h2 id="campaign-order">
  Campaign order and walkthroughs
</h2>

The campaign runs in one fixed order:

1. **Prologue — Banshee Down**
2. **Rescue and Recovery**
3. **Boarding Party**
4. **Rock Bottom**
5. **Ancient Anomaly**
6. **Final Endeavor — Reckoning**

The <Link href="/aliens-fireteam-elite-2/walkthrough">full 14-mission walkthrough</Link> shows how those six stages break down into individual missions and what to do when the next campaign does not unlock.

For a specific blocker, I go directly to the relevant campaign:

- <Link href="/aliens-fireteam-elite-2/rescue-and-recovery-walkthrough">Rescue and Recovery</Link> for Piper, the Data Storage jammer, high-rise intel, Esther, the security terminals, turrets, and landing-pad extraction.
- <Link href="/aliens-fireteam-elite-2/boarding-party-walkthrough">Boarding Party</Link> for the Demetrius warhead terminals, Cargo Bay upload, cryopods, Engine Bay, and final holdout.
- <Link href="/aliens-fireteam-elite-2/rock-bottom-walkthrough">Rock Bottom</Link> for the mine terminals, breakers, reactor pressure valve, Acidium plumbing, Hive, and late cryopod intel.
- <Link href="/aliens-fireteam-elite-2/ancient-anomaly-walkthrough">Ancient Anomaly</Link> for the quarry turrets, Engineer ship, chained intel objectives, consoles, and hybrid defenses.
- <Link href="/aliens-fireteam-elite-2/final-endeavor-walkthrough">Final Endeavor</Link> for Dr. Pierce, the first Queen escape, and the actual Xenomorph Queen boss fight.

<h2 id="prologue-banshee-down">
  What to do during the Prologue
</h2>

**Banshee Down** is the first campaign mission and begins with a displayed **Combat Rating requirement of 500**.

I use the Prologue to learn the pattern that repeats throughout the game: move through a combat route, interact with the marked objective, survive the response, refill, and only then leave the room.

The most useful habit to establish immediately is the **last refill before moving on**. Ammo crates often remain available while a defense is active, but a newly opened door or airlock can close behind the squad once the next phase begins.

After Banshee Down, I return to the Endeavor and complete the marked conversations rather than assuming the next campaign should unlock directly from the results screen.

Wrench introduces the Armory during this early Endeavor sequence, but I do not treat newly available stock as a requirement to spend immediately.

If the next mission rejects the loadout because Combat Rating is too low, I first check the Perk Grid, equipped gear, and weapon progression. If the rating is already legal but the squad keeps failing because everyone leaves ammunition behind or uses the wrong firing lanes, another gun will not fix the actual problem.

<h2 id="which-kit-to-start-with">
  Which Kit should you start with?
</h2>

I choose the first Kit by the job I want during a holdout rather than looking for one universally correct answer.

**Duelist** is the simplest gun-first choice. **Machinist** helps control lanes with deployables. **Marauder** brings heavy weapons and crowd control. **Hunter** rewards accurate ranged fire. **Medic** works best when I stay close enough to support the rest of the fireteam.

**Specialist** makes more sense later, after other Kits have unlocked abilities, perks, and weapons worth combining.

I do not keep switching Kits after every mission simply because another one looks interesting. Early progression becomes easier when one Kit and one dependable weapon are allowed to gain enough XP to open useful upgrades.

For Perk Grid connections, Combat Rating, weapon slots, four-star Augments, thermal versus electric damage, and early buying decisions, use the <Link href="/aliens-fireteam-elite-2/beginner-guide">complete beginner guide</Link>.

<h2 id="next-mission-locked">
  Why is the next mission still locked?
</h2>

A locked mission is not always a combat-progress problem.

After completing a campaign, the game frequently returns me to the Endeavor with a required crew conversation waiting. The next mission card may already be visible while still refusing to launch.

When that happens, I check progression in this order:

1. Confirm the previous mission actually shows as completed.
2. Leave the mission board.
3. Look for marked crew members aboard the Endeavor.
4. Finish the required debriefs.
5. Return to the campaign terminal.

I only replay the previous mission after checking those steps.

Combat Rating is a separate problem. If the mission itself is available but the loadout cannot launch it, I adjust the Perk Grid, progressed weapons, or compatible equipment until the requirement is met.

That distinction saves a lot of unnecessary replaying: **crew conversations unlock story progression; Combat Rating determines whether the current loadout can enter an already available mission.**

<h2 id="solo-co-op-and-crossplay">
  Can you play solo, co-op, and cross-platform?
</h2>

Fireteams contain up to **four marines**.

When human slots are empty, Alpha, Beta, and Gamma can fill the squad with their own loadouts, so I do not need a full four-player group to continue the campaign.

Quickplay also supports matching with players across platforms, which makes public co-op useful when I want human teammates without maintaining a fixed squad.

Solo and co-op reward slightly different habits. With bots, I avoid rotating so far from the main firing position that their support breaks down. With human players, spacing matters more because everyone can create conflicting firing lanes, especially once friendly fire becomes active on higher difficulty.

<h2 id="difficulty">
  Which difficulty should you start on?
</h2>

The five difficulty settings run from **Casual through Insane**.

For a first clear, I choose the setting that lets me learn objective locations, holdout lanes, and supply points without repeatedly exhausting consumables.

I move up when the squad can finish familiar missions while keeping ammunition, Aid Kits, and positioning under control.

Friendly fire becomes an important concern at **Intense and above**, so moving to a higher difficulty is not just a Combat Rating check. A stronger loadout does not help much if four players continue crossing the same firing lane.

**Insane** becomes available after the campaign has been completed on another difficulty. I therefore do not build the first playthrough around reaching it immediately.

The higher-difficulty clears eventually become part of the <Link href="/aliens-fireteam-elite-2/achievements">100% achievement route</Link>, where one familiar mission can be used for each required difficulty instead of replaying the entire campaign at every setting.

<h2 id="first-queen-encounter">
  Do you fight the Xenomorph Queen the first time you see it?
</h2>

No.

During **Final Endeavor**, the first Queen appearance is an escape sequence. When General Shipp tells the fireteam not to engage, I follow the exit objective and spend ammunition only on enemies that block the route or make the escape unsafe.

The real Queen fight begins later, after the objective changes and the Queen becomes the target that must actually be defeated.

This distinction matters because Final Endeavor contains a long boss phase. Spending strong consumables and large amounts of ammunition during the first encounter leaves less available when damage finally counts.

The <Link href="/aliens-fireteam-elite-2/final-endeavor-walkthrough">Final Endeavor walkthrough and Queen guide</Link> covers both encounters and the full boss strategy.

<h2 id="after-final-endeavor">
  What opens up after Final Endeavor?
</h2>

Defeating the Queen and completing the final incident report ends the campaign, but it does not exhaust progression.

This is where I stop thinking in terms of *what is the next story mission?* and start looking at whichever system I want to finish:

- higher-difficulty mission clears;
- Specialist missions;
- weapon ranks and Augments;
- attachment collection;
- Challenge Cards;
- Horde Mode;
- Kit and Perk Board progression;
- enemy and headshot counters.

I do not begin farming **10,000 Xenomorphs** immediately after the credits. Difficulty clears, Specialist missions, Horde waves, Challenge runs, and weapon leveling all add kills while completing other objectives.

The <Link href="/aliens-fireteam-elite-2/achievements">all 30 achievements guide</Link> includes the interactive checklist and orders those tasks so the required replays overlap as much as possible.
