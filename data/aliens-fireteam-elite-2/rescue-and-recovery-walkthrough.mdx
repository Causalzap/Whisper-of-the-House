<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
Complete **Piping Hot**, **Breaking and Entering**, and **Synth Headache** in that order.

In **Piping Hot**, follow the Storr’s Boon sign to the crashed dropship, disable the signal jammer in Data Storage, then clear the Ventilation Room to rescue Piper.

In **Breaking and Entering**, search the Weyland-Yutani high-rise for the marked intel terminals and hold the final room until Raptor can extract the team.

In **Synth Headache**, reset all three security terminals, follow Esther’s signal, disable both live turrets, and initiate the landing protocol before surviving the final pickup.

I keep my early Requisition instead of rebuilding the entire loadout. The starting weapons are enough for Standard, but I switch toward accurate or Electric damage before the Synthetic-heavy final mission.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

<h2 id="rescue-and-recovery-walkthrough-for-all-three-missions">
  Rescue and Recovery walkthrough for all three missions
</h2>

**Rescue and Recovery** is the first full three-mission campaign after the Prologue. It begins as a search for Piper, turns into an investigation of Storr’s Boon, and ends with the recovery of Esther from a facility controlled by hostile Synthetics.

The route itself is linear, but several objective changes can send me in the wrong direction if I treat every marker as another search. The main trouble spots are Data Storage in Piping Hot, the damaged intel sequence in Breaking and Entering, and the transition from finding Esther to preparing the landing pad in Synth Headache.

The enemy mix also changes across the campaign. Piping Hot is primarily a close-range Xenomorph mission, while Synth Headache combines armed Synthetics, automated turrets, and a final Xenomorph attack. I do not use exactly the same weapon priorities for all three missions.

<h3 id="before-starting-rescue-and-recovery">
  Before starting Rescue and Recovery
</h3>

After completing the Prologue, I finish the available crew conversations aboard the Endeavor before opening the mission screen. Captain Co provides the search objective, while Wrench makes the early Armory options available.

Wrench recommends a flamethrower for the rescue, but I do not buy one solely for Piping Hot. Thermal damage is useful against Xenomorphs, and a flamethrower works well in the colony’s narrow passages, but the starting weapons already have enough damage on Standard.

My early priority is simpler:

- make sure every available Perk Grid slot is filled;
- equip any weapon trait or attachment I have already unlocked;
- keep one weapon suited to close-range swarms;
- avoid spending most of my Requisition on a weapon I have not tested.

Combat Rating can increase from equipped perks and weapon progression, not just from buying a new gun. If the mission screen shows me below its expected rating, I check the empty sections of my loadout before visiting the Armory.

<h2 id="piping-hot">
  Piping Hot walkthrough: disable the jammer and rescue Piper
</h2>

Piping Hot begins on the surface of Storr’s Boon. Captain Co believes Piper survived the crash, but a signal jammer is blocking radio contact.

<h3 id="follow-the-storrs-boon-sign-to-the-crash-site">
  Follow the Storr’s Boon sign to the crash site
</h3>

From the landing point, I stay on the main route and move toward the large **Storr’s Boon** sign. The crashed dropship is straight ahead beyond the first Xenomorph groups.

I do not search the side edges of the opening area for Piper. The first objective is the wreckage itself. At the crash site, I interact with Piper’s recorded message, which confirms that she moved inside and followed emergency shelter protocol.

The nearby entrance is jammed. I use the marked interaction to cut through it, then continue through the corridors as the Xenomorph attacks become denser.

This is where I start conserving primary ammunition. Small enemies in open corridors are manageable with short bursts or the sidearm, while the close-range weapon stays loaded for enemies that rush or grapple.

<h3 id="disable-the-signal-jammer-in-data-storage">
  Restock before disabling the jammer in Data Storage
</h3>

The route eventually passes an ammo crate and first aid supplies before the objective points deeper into the facility. I refill here even if my magazines are only partly empty.

The next section contains a heavier swarm followed by hostile Synthetics in **Data Storage**. Moving past this supply point without refilling can leave me relying on the sidearm during the jammer fight.

When Captain Co asks again for Piper’s location, the correct next destination is Data Storage. Piper does not become a visible rescue target until the jammer is disabled.

Inside Data Storage, I clear the armed Synthetics before using the marked jammer controls. Standing on the terminal while ranged enemies are still firing makes the interaction unnecessarily risky.

<h3 id="clear-the-ventilation-room-and-rescue-piper">
  Clear the Ventilation Room before rescuing Piper
</h3>

Once the jammer is down, Piper identifies the **Ventilation Room** as her location. I follow the new marker instead of returning toward the dropship.

The final room is a holdout rather than a search puzzle. Piper is already secured behind a locked shelter, but opening it is not the first priority. A large Xenomorph wave reaches the room before the rescue can finish.

Before the attack fully develops, I locate the ammo and health supplies and choose a defensible side of the room. I avoid standing directly beside Piper’s door because enemies can approach from more than one direction, leaving little space to dodge a grapple.

I clear the complete wave before using the rescue interaction. The mission ends after Piper is recovered, with no separate run back to the dropship, so there is no reason to carry every remaining consumable into an extraction sequence that never occurs.

<h2 id="breaking-and-entering">
  Breaking and Entering walkthrough: recover the colony intel
</h2>

Back aboard the Endeavor, Piper reveals that hundreds of colonists are missing. Breaking and Entering sends the fireteam into a Weyland-Yutani high-rise to find records explaining what happened to them.

<h3 id="enter-the-high-rise-and-search-the-marked-terminals">
  Enter the high-rise and search the marked terminals
</h3>

From the starting point, I move toward the high-rise directly ahead. Raptor opens the entrance remotely, after which the route continues through offices, laboratories, and the Trauma Ward.

The objective is not fulfilled by finding a single collectible. I have to reach multiple marked terminals and transmit their records as I move through the building.

The first records establish that Dr. Pierce was running Xenomorph research behind the mining operation. Later terminals reveal the proposed human trials and the attempt to disguise deaths as mining accidents.

One terminal in the Trauma Ward returns corrupted data. This does not mean the mission is stuck or that I missed a required item in an earlier room. I clear the enemies around the terminal and follow the updated marker to another access point.

If the interaction remains unsafe, I finish the current wave before trying again. Repeatedly pressing the terminal while Xenomorphs are attacking is more likely to get me interrupted than to advance the upload.

<h3 id="prepare-before-the-final-high-rise-holdout">
  Prepare before transmitting the final records
</h3>

Near the end of the high-rise route, the mission places ammo and first aid supplies close to the final terminals. If I am already injured, I use the Aid Kit I am carrying and then take the replacement before starting the last sequence.

After the final records are transmitted, Raptor announces that he is coming for pickup. This begins the main holdout.

The final display can make the objective feel like a timer that has stopped. I do not need another exit switch. I continue killing the required enemies until Raptor confirms the extraction.

I stay near the supply point rather than chasing individual Xenomorphs into the previous corridor. The wider room provides more space, but pushing too far away from the ammo crate makes the last part of the wave harder than necessary.

When the final group is dead, the mission completes automatically and the fireteam returns to the Endeavor.

<h2 id="synth-headache">
  Synth Headache walkthrough: find Esther and prepare extraction
</h2>

Synth Headache is the final Rescue and Recovery mission. It replaces most of the early Xenomorph pressure with combat Synthetics, security terminals, and automated turrets before bringing Xenomorphs back for the extraction.

<h3 id="change-weapons-before-synth-headache">
  Change weapons before launching Synth Headache
</h3>

I check the enemy preview before starting and adjust away from a purely close-range Xenomorph loadout.

Synthetics fire from cover and remain accurate at longer distances. I prefer:

- an accurate rifle or DMR for headshots;
- Electric damage when it is already available;
- Armor Piercing Rounds or another ranged option;
- a close-range backup for the Xenomorph section at the end.

I do not buy several new weapons immediately before launching. Equipping an existing weapon trait, available attachment, or unused Perk Grid piece can close the Combat Rating gap without wasting early Requisition.

<h3 id="reset-all-three-security-terminals">
  Reset all three security terminals
</h3>

The facility begins in lockdown. I move through each combat room, clear the Synthetics and turrets that have a line of sight on the terminal, and then perform the reset.

The first two terminals are on the lower route. After the second reset, the objective specifically directs me **upstairs** for the third.

This is the easiest navigation mistake in the mission. Remaining on the lower floor leads back into cleared rooms without advancing anything. I use the stairs, reset the third terminal, and wait for Captain Co to confirm that Piper has located Esther’s signal.

Against armed Synthetics, I use cover long enough to remove the ranged threats instead of charging directly into the room. Headshots and Electric damage shorten these encounters; spraying at their bodies burns through ammunition before the extraction section.

<h3 id="follow-esthers-signal">
  Follow Esther’s signal through the residential building
</h3>

After the third terminal, the route moves into the residential section. The objective marker now leads directly toward Esther.

The rooms become busier, but there is no separate key or access code to find. I continue through the marked doors until the fireteam reaches Esther and confirms that she has been hacked.

<h3 id="prepare-the-landing-protocol">
  Restock before preparing the landing protocol
</h3>

The next supply point is the most important one in Synth Headache. I heal, replace the first aid kit, refill both main weapons, and reload before interacting with the landing-code terminal.

Entering the codes triggers an alarm and brings Xenomorphs into the facility. The enemy change can punish a loadout built only for ranged Synthetic combat, which is why I keep a close-range weapon or crowd-control ability available.

After surviving the alarm wave, Captain Co warns that **two live turrets** are covering the route ahead. I do not sprint through their combined fire. I use cover, focus on one turret at a time, and move forward only after the first is disabled.

Once both turrets are down, I interact with the landing controls and initiate the protocol.

<h3 id="hold-the-landing-pad-until-raptor-arrives">
  Hold the landing pad until Raptor arrives
</h3>

Starting the landing protocol does not end the mission. The final Xenomorph wave continues while Raptor lowers the dropship.

I remain near the landing area and keep the fireteam together. Running into the previous corridor can split the squad and make revives much more difficult.

If my primary ammunition runs dry, the sidearm provides unlimited emergency ammunition. I use it to finish smaller enemies while saving the remaining primary rounds for elites.

The mission ends when Raptor calls the fireteam aboard. Captain Co, Piper, and Esther are already loaded into the dropship, so there is no additional escort interaction at the end.

<h2 id="what-to-do-after-rescue-and-recovery">
  What to do after Rescue and Recovery
</h2>

Completing Synth Headache finishes the campaign and unlocks **“It’s a rescue mission, you’ll love it!”**

Back on the Endeavor, I speak to Esther, Captain Co, and the other available crew members before opening the next mission. These conversations establish that Esther discovered the Weyland-Yutani warship **Demetrius**, which becomes the next destination.

I also check my newly unlocked items and weapon progress, but I still avoid buying every attachment that has appeared. The next campaign contains more armed Synthetics, so I keep enough Requisition to improve the accurate or Electric weapon that performed best in Synth Headache.
