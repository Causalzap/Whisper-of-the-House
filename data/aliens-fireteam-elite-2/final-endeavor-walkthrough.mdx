<h2 id="final-endeavor-walkthrough">
  Final Endeavor walkthrough and Xenomorph Queen fight
</h2>

Final Endeavor is one long mission rather than another three-mission campaign. It begins as a pursuit through the mines, pauses for one last laboratory search, turns into an escape when the Queen appears, and only then becomes the final boss fight.

The most expensive mistake is treating the first Queen encounter as the boss arena. The objective tells the fireteam to escape, and spending ammunition or consumables there only makes the real fight harder later.

<h3 id="before-starting-final-endeavor">
  Before starting Final Endeavor
</h3>

I prepare for an all-Xenomorph mission. Thermal damage remains useful, but reliability matters more than replacing a ranked weapon immediately before the finale.

If I have Requisition to spend, I improve the primary or signature weapon that already carried the previous campaign. I do not buy a restrictive Challenge Card for the first clear.

I also bring two different kinds of help for the final fight:

- one crowd-control option for smaller Xenomorphs;
- one strong damage option to use against the Queen.

Finish all three missions in the <Link href="/aliens-fireteam-elite-2/ancient-anomaly-walkthrough">Ancient Anomaly walkthrough</Link> and complete the required Endeavor debriefs first.

Final Endeavor does not unlock just because the Engineer ship has been reached. Mission 5-3 must continue through all four Engineer consoles and the final command-room hybrid defense before the story can advance.

<h2 id="follow-dr-pierce-through-the-mines">
  Follow Dr. Pierce through the mines
</h2>

The mission begins with **Find Dr. Pierce and end this**.

From the landing point, I follow the mine route and clear each Xenomorph group before moving through the next narrow doorway. There is no alternate quarry entrance, key, or hidden terminal to find during the opening stretch.

This section rewards steady movement rather than speed. I use the sidearm on isolated runners and keep both main weapons ready for rooms where several wall and ceiling approaches converge.

If an ammunition supply is behind the team, I refill before leaving it. The route eventually closes into a lockdown section, so carrying a half-empty reserve forward provides no advantage.

<h3 id="override-the-mine-lockdown">
  Override the lockdown before searching the lab
</h3>

After the larger mine-room defense, the objective changes to overriding the lockdown.

I clear the enemies around the interaction point before using it. Trying to hold the control while a runner or grappling enemy is still active usually costs far more health than the few seconds it saves.

When General Shipp says Dr. Pierce is throwing everything at the fireteam, I keep moving forward. That dialogue confirms the pursuit is still progressing; it is not a cue to backtrack for another switch.

<h2 id="search-dr-pierces-final-lab">
  Search Dr. Pierce's final lab
</h2>

The next distinct area is another laboratory. I search the marked intel before trying to leave.

The record explains that Dr. Pierce learned to control Xenomorphs by imitating a Queen's ability to command an entire Hive. Collecting it advances the confrontation that follows.

I reload both main weapons and heal if using the carried Aid Kit lets me take a replacement nearby. I keep the strongest consumables unused.

The Queen is about to appear, but this first encounter is still **not** the damage phase.

<h2 id="escape-the-first-xenomorph-queen-encounter">
  Escape the first Xenomorph Queen encounter
</h2>

Dr. Pierce reveals the Queen immediately after the laboratory sequence.

General Shipp explicitly orders the fireteam **not to engage** and to find a way out. I follow the escape objective rather than turning around to fight.

I keep moving along the objective path and kill only the Xenomorphs that block a doorway, grab a teammate, or make the next section unsafe.

Emptying magazines into the Queen does not advance this encounter. It only gives the rest of the Hive more time to catch the squad.

I keep the fireteam close enough to respond to a grapple without stacking everyone into one narrow firing lane. If one player stops in a corridor, the players behind them can end up absorbing the same rush.

If someone goes down, I clear the immediate doorway before starting the revive. Otherwise the revival animation simply creates another stationary target.

The chase changes when Dr. Pierce orders the Queen to kill the fireteam and then loses control of it.

Once the objective updates to dealing with the Queen, the real boss fight has begun.

<h2 id="how-to-beat-the-xenomorph-queen">
  How to beat the Xenomorph Queen
</h2>

The Queen fight is much longer than the first encounter and combines sustained boss damage with continuous pressure from smaller Xenomorphs.

My first clear on Standard spent roughly **12 minutes** in the Queen phase, so I treat ammunition, positioning, and the timing of consumables as part of the fight rather than expecting one short damage burst.

<h3 id="refill-before-the-queen-damage-phase">
  Refill before committing to the damage phase
</h3>

As soon as the objective changes to killing the Queen, I identify the nearby ammunition supply and the open route around the arena.

I do not place every deployable directly where the Queen first enters. A turret or mine positioned slightly inside the team's fallback route can continue contributing after everyone has to move.

I refill during safe gaps instead of waiting until both main weapons are empty.

The unlimited-ammo sidearm is useful for removing an isolated runner, but relying on it for Queen damage makes an already long fight take much longer.

<h3 id="rotate-around-the-arena">
  Keep the arena moving in one direction
</h3>

The safest movement pattern is a controlled rotation rather than four players dodging independently.

I stay at mid-range and move around the arena when the Queen closes the distance. I avoid cutting directly across another player's firing lane.

On difficulties with friendly fire, that matters even more. A teammate crossing sustained fire can turn a controlled attempt into a failed one before the Queen itself becomes the problem.

I do not stand directly in front of the Queen and trade health for damage.

When the Queen commits to one player, that player creates distance while teammates with clear angles keep firing from the sides. The targeted player should prioritize staying alive rather than trying to match the damage output of the other three.

<h3 id="control-the-smaller-xenomorphs">
  Clear adds when they break the firing line
</h3>

Smaller Xenomorphs continue entering the fight.

Ignoring every add eventually leads to grapples, blocked dodges, and failed revives. Chasing every runner around the arena creates the opposite problem and gives the Queen too much time without pressure.

I clear the smaller enemies when they reach the team's firing position, then immediately return damage to the Queen.

This is where the saved crowd-control option becomes most useful. I use it when the Queen and several adds reach the team together, not on one isolated runner.

Damage consumables go onto the Queen during a stable firing window. If the team is already rotating, reviving, or dealing with a broken formation, I wait instead of spending enhanced ammunition while nobody has a clean shot.

<h3 id="why-xenomorph-queen-attempts-fail">
  Why Xenomorph Queen attempts fail
</h3>

Most failed attempts come from resource and positioning mistakes rather than a missing interaction:

- Consumables were spent during the first Queen encounter even though the objective was to escape.
- Players rotate in different directions and create crossed firing lanes or isolated revives.
- Everyone keeps shooting the Queen after runners and grappling enemies have already reached the firing position.
- Both main weapons are allowed to run dry before anyone returns to the ammunition supply.
- An Aid Kit is used for a small amount of damage earlier in the mission, leaving less recovery for the long final phase.

If the fight feels endless, I correct those problems before replacing the entire loadout.

Better firing uptime, coordinated targeting, and earlier refills usually provide more value than bringing an unfamiliar unranked weapon into the finale.

<h2 id="what-happens-after-defeating-the-queen">
  What happens after defeating the Queen
</h2>

The mission ends when the Queen is dead.

There is no additional extraction terminal, second Dr. Pierce encounter, or ending choice. Raptor is called for pickup, and the final incident report confirms that Dr. Lily Pierce and the Xenomorph Queen are dead while Fireteam Banshee and the Endeavor crew survive.
