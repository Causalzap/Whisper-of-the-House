<h2 id="complete-campaign-order">
  Complete campaign walkthrough and all 14 missions in order
</h2>

Aliens: Fireteam Elite 2 has **14 campaign missions across six stages**.

The Prologue and Final Endeavor contain one mission each. Rescue and Recovery, Boarding Party, Rock Bottom, and Ancient Anomaly each contain three.

The first-playthrough route is linear:

**Prologue → Rescue and Recovery → Boarding Party → Rock Bottom → Ancient Anomaly → Final Endeavor**

There is no alternate campaign branch or region-order choice to make. The part that can look less linear is the transition between campaigns.

Finishing an extraction does not always unlock the next operation immediately. The game can return me to the Endeavor with one or more required conversations waiting. I finish the marked debriefs, return to the campaign terminal, and only replay the previous mission if it genuinely does not show as complete.

<h2 id="prologue-banshee-down">
  1. Prologue — Banshee Down
</h2>

**Banshee Down** is the opening mission and begins at a listed **Combat Rating of 500**.

The operation introduces the basic campaign rhythm: move through the colony, restore communications, find Captain Ko, complete the marked combat objectives, and reach extraction.

The important progression step happens after the mission.

When Banshee Down ends, I return to the Endeavor rather than selecting the Prologue again because the next cards are still unavailable. Captain Ko and the other marked crew members provide the debriefs that open the next stage and introduce the Armory.

This is also the best point to correct an early settings or loadout problem. The <Link href="/aliens-fireteam-elite-2/beginner-guide">beginner guide</Link> covers Kits, visibility settings, the Perk Grid, Combat Rating, weapon slots, damage types, and early Requisition decisions.

If the Prologue felt comfortable, I keep the dependable weapon that already worked instead of replacing it simply because the Armory has opened.

<h2 id="rescue-and-recovery">
  2. Rescue and Recovery — Missions 2.1 to 2.3
</h2>

Rescue and Recovery is the first three-mission campaign:

1. **Mission 2.1 — Piping Hot**
2. **Mission 2.2 — Breaking and Entering**
3. **Mission 2.3 — Synth Headache**

Piping Hot begins the search for Piper. Breaking and Entering moves into the Weyland-Yutani high-rise and its colony records. Synth Headache follows the trail to Esther and finishes with the landing-pad extraction.

One of the easiest progression mistakes happens before Piping Hot. The campaign cards can already be visible while a required Endeavor conversation is still preventing launch.

A visible card is not necessarily an unlocked card.

Once the campaign starts, the enemy mix also changes enough that I check the mission preview before assuming the same elemental setup should remain equipped throughout all three missions. Piping Hot is strongly Xenomorph-focused, while Synth Headache puts much more emphasis on armed Synthetics before Xenomorphs return near the end.

The <Link href="/aliens-fireteam-elite-2/rescue-and-recovery-walkthrough">Rescue and Recovery walkthrough</Link> covers the jammer, Piper rescue, high-rise terminals, Esther route, security resets, turrets, and final landing-pad holdout.

After Synth Headache, I remain on the Endeavor long enough to finish the conversations about Esther's recovered information and the **Demetrius**. Boarding Party does not open directly from the extraction screen.

1. **Mission 3.1 — Bomb Squad**
2. **Mission 3.2 — Dumpster Diving**
3. **Mission 3.3 — Sleep Study**

Bomb Squad begins with a launch card showing **Combat Rating 800**. Dumpster Diving raises the observed requirement to **850**.

This is where I become more careful about reacting to every Combat Rating increase by buying another weapon.

If the loadout that cleared the previous mission still handles the required ranges and enemy types, I first look for unused Perk Grid space, a progressed attachment, or another permanent improvement before replacing a weapon that already has XP invested in it.

After Mission 3.3, the next destination is not another part of the Demetrius. I return to the Endeavor and finish the debriefs until General Shipp redirects the fireteam into the mines.

That opens Rock Bottom.

The progression trap in Rock Bottom is assuming that a dramatic objective must be the end of the mission.

Restoring the reactor concludes the central problem in Power Problems, but the mission still has to complete normally.

Running on Fumes does not finish the instant the self-destruct sequence begins.

Most importantly, **Hive Mind does not end when the Hive is sealed**. The fireteam still has to return to the industrial section, investigate the cryopods, recover Dr. Pierce's intel, decrypt it, survive the final defense, and reach pickup.

If Rock Bottom appears complete in my head but Ancient Anomaly does not open, Hive Mind is the first mission I check.

After Hive Mind actually reaches extraction, I finish the Endeavor conversations that move Dr. Pierce's trail toward the quarry.

<h2 id="ancient-anomaly">
  5. Ancient Anomaly — Missions 5.1 to 5.3
</h2>

Ancient Anomaly is the last three-mission campaign before the finale:

1. **Mission 5.1** — cross the quarry, disable the turret route, and locate Dr. Pierce's hybrid laboratory.
2. **Mission 5.2** — enter the Engineer ship and follow the chained intel requests through its systems.
3. **Mission 5.3** — return to the ship, find the hybrid cryopods, restore the required systems, and eliminate the remaining experiments.

The enemy mix changes sharply here.

The opening combines Combat Synths and fixed turrets, while the later missions introduce Pathogen and Engineer hybrids. A loadout that was built almost entirely around Rock Bottom's Xenomorph fights can suddenly feel much less flexible.

I bring one weapon that remains comfortable against armored or distant targets and another option that can handle enemies closing quickly. I also avoid restrictive Challenge Cards on the first run through unfamiliar Engineer encounters.

The <Link href="/aliens-fireteam-elite-2/ancient-anomaly-walkthrough">Ancient Anomaly walkthrough</Link> covers the quarry turrets, corrupted upload, Engineer ship terminals, four-console sequence, hybrid encounters, and final command-room defense.

Mission 5.3 must reach that final completion point. Simply reaching the Engineer ship does not unlock Final Endeavor.

After 5.3, I return to the Endeavor and finish the marked conversations with Piper, General Shipp, and anyone else required by the story.

Once Dr. Pierce's location is confirmed, Final Endeavor becomes available.

<h2 id="final-endeavor">
  6. Final Endeavor — Reckoning
</h2>

Final Endeavor contains one extended mission: **Reckoning**.

It is the **14th and final campaign mission**, and its launch screen recommends **Combat Rating 1,050**.

The route sends the fireteam back through the mines, into Dr. Pierce's final laboratory, through the first Xenomorph Queen encounter, and eventually into the actual Queen boss fight.

This is the point where I am willing to spend strong consumables that I had been saving for a later story fight—there is no later campaign mission that benefits from carrying them unused.

I still do not rebuild a reliable loadout solely to raise the displayed rating. I want weapons I already know how to keep supplied, something that can control the smaller Xenomorphs, and enough sustained damage for a long boss phase.

The most important objective change occurs when the Queen first appears.

**The first Queen encounter is an escape sequence.**

I follow the exit objective instead of spending ammunition trying to kill it. The actual fight begins later, after the objective changes and the Queen becomes the enemy that must be defeated.

<h2 id="next-mission-not-unlocking">
  What to do if the next campaign is not unlocking
</h2>

When a campaign card stays locked, I check progression in this order:

1. Confirm the last mission actually shows as completed.
2. Return to the Endeavor instead of immediately replaying it.
3. Look for marked crew conversations.
4. Finish every required debrief.
5. Return to the campaign terminal and check the next card again.

This matters most between the major campaign stages.

A mission can end successfully while the next operation still waits for a story conversation. Likewise, reaching an apparent climax such as sealing the Hive or entering the Engineer ship does not count if the current mission still has objectives remaining afterward.

I only replay a combat mission after checking those conditions.

<h2 id="what-to-do-after-finishing-the-campaign">
  What to do after finishing the campaign
</h2>

Defeating the Queen in Final Endeavor completes the story route, but it does not finish progression.

I let the final report complete, return to the Endeavor, and check what registered before starting another replay.

The most useful next step depends on what remains.

If I still need **Intense, Extreme, or Insane**, I use a familiar mission rather than immediately repeating the finale.

If weapon progression remains, I keep the necessary weapons equipped through successful extractions instead of spreading XP randomly across the Armory.

If the missing requirement involves Specialist, Horde Mode, Challenge Cards, attachments, Augments, cosmetics, or enemy kill totals, I switch from campaign progression to deliberate cleanup.
