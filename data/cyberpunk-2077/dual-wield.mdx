<GuideQuickAnswer title="How Do You Dual Wield in Cyberpunk 2077?">
Raise **Cool to 9**, unlock the first rank of **Double Trouble**, and equip two Power Pistols or Power Revolvers in the Primary and Secondary weapon slots. Spend a second Perk Point on Double Trouble to unlock **Heywood Hurricane**.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

</section>

Dual wielding in Cyberpunk 2077 is no longer limited to NPCs or mods. Update 2.5 adds **Double Trouble**, a Cool perk that lets V fire two compatible handguns simultaneously. It works with existing saves and does not require Phantom Liberty.

The trade-off appears immediately: **each equipped weapon loses 37.5% of its damage while dual wielding**. Having two guns does not automatically make every pistol setup stronger. Weapon type, accuracy, Stamina consumption, and the timing of Heywood Hurricane all affect whether the extra firepower pays off.

You can start experimenting with just one Perk Point once Cool reaches 9. The full six-point branch adds a more aggressive combat loop built around defeating enemies, accumulating Triple Tequila stacks, and spending those stacks on a stronger Hurricane attack.

<section id="unlock" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Unlock Double Trouble at Cool 9

Open the **Character** menu and select the **Cool** attribute tree. Double Trouble is in the Level 9 section, so you need at least 9 Cool before purchasing it.

You do not need character Level 9 specifically. The restriction applies to the Cool attribute, not V's overall level or Street Cred.

Double Trouble has two ranks:

- **Rank 1 — 1 Perk Point:** Unlocks dual wielding for Power Pistols and Power Revolvers. Each weapon deals 37.5% less damage. Aiming reduces recoil and bullet spread by 15%, and both guns fire simultaneously.
- **Rank 2 — 1 additional Perk Point:** Unlocks Heywood Hurricane, a special attack that automatically fires both weapons for one second with 50% greater bullet spread. Ammunition spent on successful hits is automatically refilled.

Rank 2 also replaces the normal **Quick Melee Attack** with Heywood Hurricane while using the dual-wield setup.

The minimum investment is therefore **Cool 9 and one Perk Point**. Taking both ranks and all four supporting perks requires **six Perk Points total**.

A player who only wants to try two handguns can stop at Rank 1. There is no reason to spend all six points before checking whether the shooting style feels comfortable.

<section id="compatible-weapons" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Which Pistols and Revolvers Can You Dual Wield?

Double Trouble checks the weapon's **Power** classification, not just whether it looks like a handgun.

Compatible categories are:

- **Power Pistols**
- **Power Revolvers**

You can pair a Power Pistol with another Power Pistol, use two Power Revolvers, or combine one of each.

Tech Pistols and Smart Pistols do not qualify. Neither do SMGs, shotguns, or other weapon types, even if they normally use the same combat attributes.

Several weapons shown with the new system provide useful starting points:

| Weapon | Type | Attack Speed | Damage |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| DR5 Nova, Tier 5+ | Power Revolver | 3.3 | 95 |
| Plan B, Tier 5 Iconic | Power Pistol | 5.0 | 53 |
| Nue, Tier 5 | Power Pistol | 2.9 | 97 |

These are values from the displayed weapon configurations, not universal base stats. Upgrades, installed mods, and the character's build can change the numbers.

The differences still illustrate an important choice. Plan B has a higher displayed Attack Speed but lower Damage than the DR5 Nova in this loadout. Nue sits closer to the revolver's displayed damage while remaining a Power Pistol.

### Plan B Has a Hidden Resource Cost Worth Considering

Plan B is particularly easy to overlook because it qualifies for dual wielding and offers an Iconic effect. Its item description also warns that **every shot costs eddies**.

That matters more when you begin firing two weapons simultaneously or repeatedly using Heywood Hurricane.

Plan B can be an interesting choice when you specifically want its Bleeding effect, but it is not automatically a good default for prolonged fights. You are trading money as well as ammunition to use the weapon.

For a less expensive starting setup, use conventional Power Pistols or a Power Pistol alongside a DR5 Nova. You can test the new system without building your entire loadout around Plan B's spending mechanic.

## Equip a Primary and Secondary Weapon

Unlocking Double Trouble does not automatically place a second gun in V's hand.

Open your inventory and equip a compatible handgun in the **Primary Weapon** slot. Then select the **Secondary Weapon** slot and assign another qualifying Power Pistol or Power Revolver.

The inventory displays both equipped weapons, including their individual attachments and installed mods.

Once both slots are correctly assigned, draw your weapons to use the dual-wield setup.

On a controller using the demonstrated default layout:

- **Left Trigger / L2:** Aim.
- **Right Trigger / R2:** Fire.
- **LB / L1:** Perform Heywood Hurricane after buying Double Trouble Rank 2.

On PC, the demonstrated configuration uses **Q** for Heywood Hurricane. Check your current control bindings if you have changed them.

### Why Can't I Equip My Second Pistol?

Start with the item details.

A weapon labeled **Tech Pistol** or **Smart Pistol** is not eligible, even if it occupies a normal handgun weapon slot. Double Trouble must also be purchased; merely having 9 Cool does not enable the second weapon.

Check these three things before changing your build:

1. Double Trouble Rank 1 is unlocked.
2. The Primary Weapon is a Power Pistol or Power Revolver.
3. The Secondary Weapon is also a compatible Power handgun.

If the secondary selection still will not appear, try equipping two ordinary Power Pistols before testing Iconic combinations. That helps separate a basic setup issue from a problem involving a particular weapon.

You do not need to purchase a second copy of the same gun. Different eligible weapons can be paired together.

<section id="damage" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Is Dual Wielding Actually More Powerful Than One Pistol?

The 37.5% penalty applies to **each weapon** while dual wielding.

For a simplified example, suppose a pistol normally deals 100 damage per shot. Under the Double Trouble penalty, that becomes 62.5 damage before considering other bonuses and defenses.

If two identical pistols fire together and both shots hit, their combined damage would be 125 in that simplified situation.

That is greater than the original single 100-damage shot, but it does **not** establish a guaranteed 25% DPS improvement. Actual performance depends on both weapons' Attack Speed, hit consistency, ammunition behavior, and the bonuses active during combat.

More shots also create more opportunities to miss, especially when trying to hit small targets at range.

The new playstyle has several practical implications:

- **Close and medium range:** The additional gun and simultaneous fire are easier to take advantage of when targets occupy more of your aiming area.
- **Long-range precision:** Losing the normal single-pistol sight picture in the demonstrated dual-wield setup makes exact shots less comfortable.
- **Extended encounters:** Stamina recovery and handling bonuses become increasingly useful as you continue firing.
- **Iconic weapons:** The weapon's special properties still matter, but an activated special move can conflict with the new dual-wield controls.

For players who already rely on carefully aimed single-pistol shots, Double Trouble is not necessarily a straight upgrade. Its strongest advantages become clearer when you invest in the supporting perks and fight aggressively enough to trigger them.

## How Triple Tequila and the Four Supporting Perks Work

Double Trouble has four supporting perks in the Cool tree. Each costs one Perk Point and applies to the new Power Pistol and Power Revolver dual-wield system.

The strongest interaction revolves around **Triple Tequila**.

### Triple Tequila: Build Stacks Before Heywood Hurricane

Neutralizing an enemy while dual wielding grants **+10% Mitigation Chance for 15 seconds**.

The effect stacks up to three times, giving a maximum of **30% additional Mitigation Chance** while those stacks are active.

Using Heywood Hurricane consumes the stacks and grants **+15% damage per consumed stack**.

At three stacks, that means a **+45% Hurricane damage bonus**.

There is an important limitation: **you cannot earn new Triple Tequila stacks during Heywood Hurricane**.

This creates a choice between retaining the stacks for Mitigation Chance and spending them for a stronger special attack.

Against ordinary enemies, building stacks through regular dual-wield fire gives you the option to save Hurricane for a tougher group. Activating it immediately after every neutralization prevents you from taking full advantage of the three-stack bonus.

### Splash of Love: Extend Hurricane at Three Stacks

Splash of Love increases Heywood Hurricane's fire rate by **50%**.

It also adds **one second** to Hurricane's duration when you activate the ability at maximum Triple Tequila stacks.

The normal Hurricane lasts one second. With the full-stack bonus, its duration becomes two seconds.

This is a substantial change in how the special attack functions: you get additional firing time as well as the damage benefit from consuming three Triple Tequila stacks.

The extended duration is only available when the stack requirement is met. Purchasing Splash of Love alone does not make every Hurricane last two seconds.

### Keep 'Em Comin': Recover Stamina Through Neutralizations

Keep 'Em Comin' restores **40 Stamina** after neutralizing an enemy while dual wielding.

It adds another **20 Stamina per active Triple Tequila stack**.

This rewards continuing to fight with your regular dual-wield attacks rather than using Hurricane immediately whenever it becomes available.

The amount of recovery depends on the stacks active when the effect is applied. You should not treat the maximum possible recovery as a fixed bonus after every kill.

### Two Right Hands: Improve Hip-Fire and Aiming

Two Right Hands provides separate bonuses depending on how you shoot:

- **Hip-firing:** 25% lower Stamina cost.
- **Aiming:** 20% lower recoil and 20% lower bullet spread.

These bonuses should not be confused with the Hurricane damage multiplier. Two Right Hands improves normal gun handling and Stamina efficiency, while Triple Tequila and Splash of Love determine how much value you can get from the special attack.

The perk descriptions refer to aiming bonuses, but they do not mean dual wielding provides the same precise iron-sight view as using a single pistol. In the tested setup, players could aim their paired guns but could not use conventional single-pistol ADS in the normal way.

<section id="combat-loop" className="scroll-mt-24">

## When to Use Heywood Hurricane Instead of Normal Fire

With all six points invested, the useful sequence is to build Triple Tequila stacks using normal dual-wield attacks, then spend those stacks when Hurricane has a good opportunity to land its shots.

A typical encounter looks like this:

1. **Open with normal dual-wield fire.** Neutralize enemies to build Triple Tequila stacks and recover Stamina through Keep 'Em Comin'.
2. **Build toward three stacks.** The active stacks improve Mitigation Chance while you continue fighting.
3. **Use Heywood Hurricane on a target you can reliably hit.** Three stacks grant the full damage bonus and activate Splash of Love's additional duration.
4. **Return to normal fire.** Hurricane consumes the Triple Tequila stacks, and you cannot rebuild them during the special attack itself.

Hurricane increases bullet spread by 50%, so distance matters. Using it on a distant enemy with only a small part of their body exposed can waste the opportunity created by your accumulated stacks.

Its ammo-refill mechanic also depends on successful hits. It should not be interpreted as unlimited ammunition for every shot fired during the animation.

This makes Hurricane especially useful when you can maintain a clear firing angle against a nearby target or a tightly grouped encounter.

There is also a reason to delay it. The Mitigation Chance from Triple Tequila exists while the stacks are active. Spending those stacks immediately sacrifices that defensive benefit in exchange for the special attack's damage.

The decision is therefore more interesting than simply pressing Hurricane whenever the control becomes available.

## Johnny's Pistol and Other Iconic Weapons: What Changes?

Power-class Iconic Pistols can still qualify for Double Trouble, but you should distinguish between an Iconic weapon's passive effects and special actions that require a particular button.

A notable example is **Johnny Silverhand's Malorian Arms 3516**.

When used as a single weapon, it has a special fire-based melee action. In a tested dual-wield configuration, the same **Q** input triggered Heywood Hurricane instead of the pistol's special attack.

Switching back to single-wield use restored access to the original action.

This happens because Double Trouble Rank 2 replaces Quick Melee with Hurricane. It does not prove that every Iconic Power Pistol loses its unique properties when paired with another weapon.

Before choosing an Iconic combination, check whether its defining advantage is:

- A passive weapon effect that continues working during normal shooting.
- An activated action that shares the Quick Melee control.
- A property that is particularly useful when hitting the same target repeatedly.

Plan B provides another meaningful trade-off. Its Bleeding effect remains part of the weapon's identity, but shooting it costs eddies. Combining it with aggressive dual-wield fire increases how often you may incur that expense.

An ordinary Nue or DR5 Nova avoids building your entire setup around that particular cost. There is no need to commit to an expensive Iconic pairing before deciding whether the new combat style is worth keeping.

## Can You Unlock Dual Wielding on an Existing Level 60 Character?

Yes, provided your character can meet the **Cool 9** requirement.

The distinction is between Attribute Points and Perk Points.

Perk Points can be refunded and reassigned outside combat. If you already have Cool 9 or higher, you can usually free a Perk Point from another Cool perk and purchase Double Trouble without restarting your game.

Attribute Points are more restrictive. Cyberpunk 2077's normal attribute reset is a **one-time reset per character**.

For an existing high-level character, check the following:

- **Cool is already 9 or higher:** Refund any unnecessary perks and purchase Double Trouble.
- **Cool is below 9, with unspent Attribute Points:** Raise Cool to 9, then unlock the perk.
- **Cool is below 9, with the attribute reset still available:** Consider using the reset, but review the other attributes your build depends on first.
- **Cool is below 9, with no available Attribute Points or reset:** Refunding perks alone cannot increase Cool. You may need a different character or playthrough to meet the requirement.

The important warning is not to waste a one-time attribute reset just to test a combat style you might dislike. Reassigning individual Perk Points is much easier than undoing your attribute distribution.

Also, do not confuse the cost of unlocking dual wielding with the cost of its complete supporting branch. **One Perk Point** enables the basic playstyle; **six Perk Points** purchase both Double Trouble ranks and all four auxiliary perks.

<section id="worth-it" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Is Double Trouble Worth Six Perk Points?

Start with Double Trouble Rank 1 and test a compatible pair of handguns against enemies you normally fight. Pay attention to whether both weapons land their shots consistently and whether the combined fire compensates for the individual damage penalty.

The remaining five Perk Points are most useful when you regularly neutralize multiple enemies, build Triple Tequila stacks, and have opportunities to land Heywood Hurricane at close or medium range.

A character who relies on distant precision shots or Johnny's Quick Melee attack may get more value from keeping a single pistol equipped. You can refund the perk investment without using your one-time Attribute Point reset.
