**"Gasoline supply depleted at Gas Station"** means the station
cannot maintain its fuel stock. The shortage may originate at
the Pump Jacks, the Oil Refinery, or the transportation network
connecting them.

A Gas Station placed beside a busy road is not automatically
supplied with fuel. Crude Oil must reach a refinery, be
processed into Gasoline, and then travel to the station in
a separate delivery vehicle.

Fuel facilities become available during **Car Dependency
Level 3** progression. Establish the complete supply chain
before expanding to several Gas Stations, because each
additional station needs deliveries from the same production
network.


<h2 id="gasoline-production-chain" className="scroll-mt-24">
  How to Produce Gasoline in Car Park Capital
</h2>

Gasoline production involves five facilities arranged in
a specific order:

1. **Pump Jack** — extracts Crude Oil.
2. **Crude Oil Transportation** — collects oil from Pump Jacks
   and delivers it to an Oil Refinery.
3. **Oil Refinery** — converts Crude Oil into Gasoline.
4. **Gasoline Transportation** — collects Gasoline from the
   refinery and delivers it to Gas Stations.
5. **Gas Station** — sells the delivered fuel to drivers.

The two transportation buildings perform different jobs.
Crude Oil Transportation brings raw material into the
refinery; Gasoline Transportation takes the finished
product out.

A refinery supplied with Crude Oil still needs Gasoline
Transportation before its output can reach customers.
Likewise, building several Pump Jacks does not supply a
Gas Station directly.

For the initial setup, choose a compact industrial area
with a connection to the city road network. Keep the
collection facilities close enough to serve their
respective production buildings, then establish a
working route to the first Gas Station.

Expand only after checking which stage is failing to
keep up. A larger oil field cannot compensate for
Gasoline waiting at the refinery without a delivery
vehicle.


<h2 id="pump-jacks" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Place Pump Jacks Within Crude Oil Collection Range
</h2>

Open **Oil and Fuel** in the construction menu and
select **Pump Jack**.

The October 2026 Early Access construction menu
lists a Pump Jack at **$500**. Its output is
**Crude Oil**.

Use land outside your strongest commercial parking
areas where possible. The Pump Jack's effect on
nearby parking demand makes it unsuitable for many
valuable office-district plots.

The machines also have placement restrictions.
Pump Jacks cannot be built arbitrarily close to
one another, so leave enough space between
extractors before buying land for additional units.

Once the first Pump Jack is operating, build
**Crude Oil Transportation**.

Its trucks must be able to collect the oil being
produced. Check the facility's collection area
and make sure the Pump Jack falls within it.

Road access matters as much as proximity.
An extractor inside the collection area cannot
supply the refinery if the transport vehicle
has no usable route.

Before purchasing another Pump Jack, check whether
the existing oil is being collected. Uncollected
Crude Oil indicates a transport or access problem,
not a need for more extraction.


<h2 id="refinery-and-crude-delivery" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Deliver Crude Oil to the Oil Refinery
</h2>

Build the **Oil Refinery** on a road-connected
industrial plot accessible to your crude oil trucks.

An Oil Refinery costs **$3,000** to build.
Keep enough money for the connecting roads
and transportation facilities so Crude Oil
can reach the refinery.

The refinery processes Crude Oil into Gasoline.
Its incoming stock is the first useful check
after the crude oil trucks begin operating.

When the refinery remains empty, inspect the
collection route rather than expanding production.

Start with the Pump Jacks and follow the oil
toward the refinery:

- Confirm the Pump Jacks are producing Crude Oil.
- Check that Crude Oil Transportation covers
  the extraction area.
- Locate a crude oil truck and confirm that it
  can travel between the collection point and
  the refinery.
- Inspect the refinery after a delivery to
  confirm that Crude Oil has arrived.

Once the refinery is receiving oil, allow it
to process the input before checking for
Gasoline output.

A refinery with raw material arriving but no
usable Gasoline output requires attention to
the refining stage. Additional gasoline
delivery trucks will not create more fuel.

Gasoline accumulating at the refinery, on the
other hand, establishes that extraction and
refining are producing output. The next
operation to inspect is outbound transportation.


<h2 id="gasoline-transport" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Set Up Gasoline Transportation From Refinery to Station
</h2>

**Gasoline Transportation** moves the refined
fuel from Oil Refineries to Gas Stations.

Its construction menu lists a cost of **$500**
in the October 2026 Early Access build.

Place the facility where its collection area
covers the refinery and where its vehicles
can join the roads leading to the station.

Do not confuse this facility with Crude Oil
Transportation. An oil collection truck does not
replace the separate service needed to deliver
Gasoline to customers.

After placing Gasoline Transportation, wait for
refined fuel to become available. Locate the
delivery vehicle and follow it toward a Gas Station.

The useful confirmation is a completed trip:
the truck collects Gasoline, reaches a station,
and restores its fuel stock.

A truck may be away on an earlier delivery while
the refinery continues producing. Fuel waiting
for collection does not mean production has stopped.

The important question is how regularly the
existing fleet can move that output. A long
route, traffic congestion, or several stations
sharing the same trucks can leave enough fuel
at the refinery but too little at the pumps.

The conditions that justify another Gasoline
Transportation vehicle are covered below with
the depleted-supply warning.


<h2 id="gas-station-setup" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Build a Working Gas Station Drive-Thru
</h2>

A **Gas Station** is constructed as a drive-thru.
Placing the facility beside a street is not
sufficient; vehicles need a route through the
queue and past the Gas Pump.

Choose a plot with enough room for the entrance,
queue, pump, and exit before beginning construction.

The building interface guides the layout in stages:

1. **Place Entrance** — connect the facility to
   a road that customers can use.
2. **Build Queue** — extend the vehicle route
   toward the service area.
3. **Build Object** — add the required Gas Pump.
4. Complete the remaining driving route so
   customers can leave the station.

Complete the queue and install the required object
before treating the station as operational.

The construction menu also labels
**Gasoline** as an **optional input**.

That label does not establish that a finished
Gas Station has a continuous fuel supply.
The station can be constructed separately from
the industrial network, but actual fuel deliveries
still depend on the refinery and Gasoline
Transportation.

After opening, watch whether customers can enter
the drive-thru, reach the pump, and leave without
blocking the road.

Long vehicle queues can arise even when the station
has Gasoline available. That is a service-capacity
issue rather than evidence that the refinery needs
more Pump Jacks.


<h2 id="gasoline-supply-depleted" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Fix Gasoline Supply Depleted and Missing Fuel Deliveries
</h2>

When a Gas Station displays **"Gasoline supply
depleted at Gas Station,"** inspect the station's
fuel stock and trace the missing delivery backward
through the network.

A completed Gas Station may remain without fuel
until the next transport vehicle arrives.
The warning alone cannot distinguish a temporary
delivery delay from an ongoing supply shortage.

### Refinery Has Gasoline but the Station Is Empty

Start with **Gasoline Transportation**.

Check the refinery's finished stock and locate
the available delivery trucks.

A refinery holding **4 units of Gasoline** while
no truck is present to collect them has fuel
waiting at the transportation stage.

That stock does not justify building another
Pump Jack or Oil Refinery.

Check where the gasoline trucks are and whether
they can reach the refinery and station.

If a truck is already returning, allow it to
collect and deliver the waiting fuel before
making another purchase.

**Add more Gasoline Transportation capacity when
all of these conditions persist:**

- The refinery repeatedly has Gasoline waiting
  for collection.
- Existing delivery trucks are busy elsewhere
  or returning from earlier trips.
- The Gas Station runs out before the next
  shipment arrives.
- The refinery-to-station road route is usable.

A shortage that disappears after the next
delivery may only reflect transport timing.
Repeated depleted warnings with finished fuel
accumulating at the refinery indicate that
the outgoing delivery rate is insufficient.

### Refinery Has No Gasoline Available

Follow the supply chain upstream.

Inspect whether the refinery has received
Crude Oil. When its input is empty, locate
the crude oil truck and check the extraction
area's collection coverage.

A refinery without incoming Crude Oil cannot
maintain gasoline production.

When Crude Oil is arriving but Gasoline output
remains insufficient, inspect the refinery's
processing activity before buying additional
Gasoline Transportation.

More trucks cannot collect fuel that has not
been produced.

### Pump Jacks Have Crude Oil Waiting

Check **Crude Oil Transportation** and the
road connecting it to the refinery.

The extraction and collection facilities must
be connected by a functioning transport route.
A production area with stranded Crude Oil
does not benefit from additional Pump Jacks.

Correct the collection coverage or road
access, then confirm that a crude oil truck
delivers its cargo to the refinery.

### Gasoline Truck Cannot Reach the Station

Inspect the road network and station entrance.

A missing road segment, disconnected industrial
area, or unusable destination route can prevent
fuel delivery even when both production stages
are working.

Adding another Gasoline Transportation vehicle
will not fix the same broken connection.

Follow a truck from the refinery to the station.
The supply problem is resolved when it can reach
the destination and unload successfully.


<h2 id="gas-station-prices-and-capacity" className="scroll-mt-24">
  When to Raise Gasoline Prices or Add Another Gas Station
</h2>

A functioning fuel network can still underperform
when customers cannot reach the pump quickly or
the station's daily costs exceed its earnings.

The Gas Pump interface allows the gasoline price
to be adjusted. A developed station can display
**$12.47 per gallon** and **$500 per day in upkeep**,
as shown in the station's operating panel.

Raise or lower the price according to what
customers are willing to pay. A higher price
does not automatically improve profit when
drivers are unwilling to purchase fuel.

The <Link href="/car-park-capital">Car Park Capital pricing and Car Braininess instructions</Link>
explain how customer satisfaction affects
willingness to pay.

When a station has Gasoline available but
vehicles are forming long queues, check
the drive-thru layout and the number of
customers the existing pump can serve.

Another Gas Station may help when drivers
are spread across distant neighborhoods
or the current service location is
consistently congested.

That expansion also increases the number
of destinations requiring fuel deliveries.
Before building the second station, confirm
that the refinery and its transport fleet
can supply the additional demand.

Other industrial facilities can also
consume Crude Oil. The **Plastic Tree Factory**
uses oil to produce items associated with
reducing Civil Unrest; its construction
and use are explained in the
<Link href="/car-park-capital/unrest-protests">Unrest and Protests instructions</Link>.

When those facilities share the same
industrial network, check whether enough
Crude Oil continues reaching the refinery
before expanding gasoline sales.
