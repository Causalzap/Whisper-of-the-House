</div>

A **Car Shop Carousel** displaying **"No Vehicles for Sale"**
cannot sell more cars, even when customers are waiting outside.
The shop needs vehicles delivered to it, either through direct
stock purchases or through the manufacturing and transport
network.

There are three places where sales can stall: the **Car Factory**
may not be producing enough, the **Car Carrier** may not be
delivering the finished vehicles, or the **Carousel** may be
stocked but unable to attract buyers at its current location
and price.

Check those stages separately before purchasing another factory.
An expensive new production building will not solve an
inaccessible shop or a shortage of delivery trucks.


<h2 id="build-car-sales" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Start Selling Cars With a Car Shop Carousel
</h2>

The first car sales do not require a large factory complex.
Place a **Car Shop Carousel** in a populated district where
customers can reach it.

In Bald Town City, the office towers are a suitable starting
location. They attract people throughout the day, so a
carousel near an office building has access to existing
pedestrian activity.

Buy enough land for the shop and connect it to the road.
Do not purchase an isolated industrial plot for your first
sales location merely because the land is cheaper.

A completed carousel can still display
**"No Vehicles for Sale"** when no stock has been supplied.

At the beginning, stock the carousel directly through its
vehicle-purchase options. This allows residents to start
buying cars before you have invested in automated production.

For an early vehicle purchase, the shop can offer cars at
**$200 each**. The amount depends on the vehicle and
available purchasing options, so inspect the current price
before buying several at once.

Direct stocking is useful while sales are small or when
you need only a few additional car owners for the next
dependency upgrade.

It becomes less convenient when several car shops are
selling out repeatedly. At that point, buying a Car Factory
and arranging regular deliveries can reduce the need to
restock each shop manually.

Establish vehicle sales early enough that car ownership
can grow alongside your parking capacity. A large parking
network does not increase ownership when residents have
nowhere to buy cars.


<h2 id="factory-and-carrier" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Build a Car Factory and Connect It to a Car Carrier
</h2>

Automated vehicle sales depend on three buildings working
together:

1. **Car Factory:** Manufactures vehicles.
2. **Car Carrier:** Collects finished vehicles and transports
   them to car shops.
3. **Car Shop Carousel:** Receives vehicles and sells them
   to customers.

The construction menu in the October 2026 Early Access
build shows:

- **Car Factory:** $2,000 to construct.
- **Car Carrier:** $1,000 to construct.

These prices exclude the cost of purchasing land, laying
roads, and running the buildings. The Car Carrier vehicle
interface also shows **$200 per day in upkeep** for an
individual carrier.

Place manufacturing facilities away from your strongest
commercial parking locations. A factory can reduce nearby
parking demand, so the office district is better reserved
for parking and customer-facing businesses when suitable
industrial land is available elsewhere.

The factory location still needs practical access to the
main road network. A cheap plot separated from the city
by inaccessible terrain can become an expensive choice
once additional connecting land is required.

After purchasing the plot:

- Place the Car Factory and select an available vehicle
  for production.
- Build a Car Carrier facility that can collect the
  factory's output.
- Connect the industrial area to the road network used
  by the car shops.
- Allow manufacturing to begin, then inspect whether
  a carrier collects the finished vehicles.

**Cabriolets** are available during early manufacturing.
When the factory has produced a batch, the carrier should
be able to collect its output and transport it toward
the sales location.

Do not expand to several factories before the first
shipment has reached a shop successfully. A single
working production-and-delivery route establishes whether
the buildings and roads are connected correctly.


<h2 id="first-delivery" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Check Whether the Car Carrier Is Delivering Vehicles
</h2>

Select a Car Carrier to inspect its vehicle status.
The interface displays the type and quantity of cargo
being transported, along with the carrier's available
controls.

A loaded carrier can show **Carrying: Vehicle 2**, meaning
it currently has two vehicles on board.

The number in the cargo interface is the current load,
not the number of cars available for sale across the city.

A developing factory can also have **four Cabriolets ready**
before the next collection. The relevant check is whether
those finished vehicles leave the factory and eventually
appear at a Car Shop Carousel.

Use the carrier's location controls to follow its movement
through the road network. Watch for a completed delivery
rather than assuming that a loaded truck has already
restocked the intended shop.

Manual vehicle control is available, but an ordinary delivery
does not require the truck to be driven by hand. Switching
into manual driving can interrupt its automated route;
return the carrier to normal control when you want the
delivery system to operate automatically.

When a carrier reaches the sales location, inspect that
carousel again. The out-of-stock warning should clear
once the shop has vehicles available for purchase.

A truck moving around the city is not sufficient
confirmation. The saleable inventory at the destination
is what matters.

  Why Does the Shop Say No Vehicles for Sale When the Counter Shows 14?
</h2>

The city's vehicle indicator can show **14** while a
Car Shop Carousel simultaneously displays
**"No Vehicles for Sale."**

    A citywide vehicle count of 14 does not establish
    that the affected Car Shop Carousel has stock
    available to sell.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

The top counter does not identify the inventory of every
individual factory or carousel. Check the actual buildings
before deciding which part of the operation needs attention.

Follow the vehicles through the network:

1. **Inspect the empty Carousel.** Confirm that its
   vehicle stock is unavailable rather than assuming
   low sales are caused by a lack of customers.
2. **Select the producing Factory.** Check whether
   completed vehicles are waiting for collection.
3. **Locate the Car Carrier.** Inspect its cargo,
   current position, and road access.
4. **Watch the next delivery.** Confirm that the truck
   reaches the intended shop and that saleable stock
   becomes available.

A factory holding finished vehicles while its carrier
is occupied elsewhere suggests a transport bottleneck.
Check the existing truck's route and availability before
adding another carrier.

A broken road connection requires a route correction,
not another delivery vehicle.

When the factory has no finished cars ready, additional
transport alone cannot increase the number of vehicles
reaching the shop. Production must catch up first.

If finished vehicles are consistently available and
existing carriers cannot complete deliveries fast enough,
another Car Carrier may improve the supply rate.

The decision should follow the building-level stock
and vehicle status, not the top counter alone.


<h2 id="factory-not-producing" className="scroll-mt-24">
  What to Do When the Factory Cannot Keep Up With Sales
</h2>

Car shops that sell through every shipment can eventually
outpace a small manufacturing operation.

Check whether the factory is producing cars regularly
and whether carriers are collecting them soon after
production finishes.

When finished vehicles are collected promptly and
the factory repeatedly has little stock waiting,
additional production capacity may be useful.

An extra factory is less effective when the original
factory already holds a large quantity of undelivered
cars. That indicates the next investment should be
considered at the transportation stage.

Different factories can be configured to manufacture
different available vehicle models. Adding another
factory therefore offers both extra output and the
option to supply more than one type of car.

However, the industrial plot and its connection costs
still matter. Do not turn a profitable high-demand
parking area into a manufacturing district merely
because vehicle sales are growing.

Expand production where the new buildings can share
a practical road connection with the existing
delivery network.


<h2 id="car-shops-not-selling" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Cars Have Arrived, but Customers Are Not Buying Them
</h2>

A stocked Carousel can have slow sales for reasons
unrelated to the factory.

Start with its location. A shop needs access to
customers, so a populated area near offices or
other active destinations is generally more useful
than an isolated plot.

Also check whether pedestrians and vehicles can
reach the shop. An inaccessible sales location
will not become effective simply because carriers
continue delivering stock.

Price is another factor. Residents' willingness
to pay changes with their Car Braininess and
satisfaction.

A vehicle purchased for **$200** and offered at
approximately **$285.59** demonstrates that a
shop can sell above its purchase cost. It does
not establish that the same selling price is
appropriate for every city, car model, or level.

When vehicles remain available but sales are
weak, inspect the shop's asking price against
what residents are currently willing to pay.
Reducing an excessive price can be more useful
than adding another factory.

If customers are purchasing every delivery
quickly, however, lowering prices is not the
first issue to solve. Check whether the shops
need more regular stock.

explain how satisfaction affects what residents
are willing to pay across the city's businesses.


<h2 id="choose-vehicles" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Which Vehicles Should Your Car Factories Produce?
</h2>

Factories can manufacture different vehicles as
additional models become available during
Car Dependency progression.

**Cabriolets** are an early production option.
Later selections include **Banana Cars**, vans,
and smaller three-wheeled vehicles.

A newly available model does not automatically
make the current production line obsolete.

Inspect whether the cars you already manufacture
are reaching shops and being purchased. A steady
supply of affordable vehicles can continue
contributing to car ownership without requiring
every factory to change models.

More variety becomes useful when customers can
afford the available vehicles and the transport
network has enough capacity to supply the shops.

Avoid changing several factories at once while
you are diagnosing a stock shortage. Keep an
established production line running until the
new model is being manufactured and delivered.

Gulf of Freedom also introduces the
**Amphibious Car Factory** at Car Dependency
Level 2. Those vehicles can travel between
roads and water where the routes connect.
Their shoreline requirements are covered in
the <Link href="/car-park-capital/gulf-of-freedom">Gulf of Freedom scenario instructions</Link>.


<h2 id="when-to-expand" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Should You Buy Another Factory, Carrier, or Carousel?
</h2>

The next investment depends on where the
vehicles stop moving.

- **Buy another Factory** when shops have demand,
  carriers can keep up with collections, and
  manufacturing cannot supply enough vehicles.
- **Buy another Carrier** when completed vehicles
  are waiting at factories while shops run out
  of stock, provided the delivery routes are usable.
- **Buy another Carousel** when manufacturing and
  delivery are functioning but more residents
  need access to a sales location.

Each building addresses a different constraint.
Extra factories increase potential production;
extra carriers provide more delivery capacity;
extra car shops create more places to purchase
the available vehicles.

The distinction is especially important while
trying to increase car ownership for **Bald Town
City's Level 7 objective**. A Level 6-to-7
screen can show enough qualifying parking
spaces while the ownership counter is still
short of its requirement.

At that point, keep vehicles flowing into
accessible shops rather than purchasing
additional parking land for a counter that
has already been completed.

provides the applicable victory conditions
and final dependency requirements.
