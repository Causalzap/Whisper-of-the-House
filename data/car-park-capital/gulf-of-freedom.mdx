  Car Park Capital — Early Access, October 2026.
</div>

Gulf of Freedom has a different economy from Bald Town City.
Its hotel, beaches, and harbor generate parking demand in
separate pockets rather than around a dense concentration
of office buildings. Buying large stretches of empty
coastline can exhaust the starting budget without bringing
many customers to a parking lot.

The map also introduces **Amphibious Cars**. These vehicles
can travel between roads and water, allowing residents to
reach destinations across the Gulf. However, water-capable
cars do not automatically solve the transport requirements
of factories, delivery trucks, or ordinary residential
neighborhoods.

The safest opening is to establish income on accessible
mainland land, unlock the amphibious vehicle options,
and expand toward the islands only after the required
connections are working.

- **Car Dependency Level 7**

- **30 people living in suburban homes**

Unlike Bald Town City, this objective panel does not
include separate victory conditions for demolishing
seven subway stations or having 400 cars parked
simultaneously.

Car Dependency still requires parking capacity and
vehicle ownership at individual upgrade stages. Those
requirements appear in the dependency panel rather
than as additional Gulf of Freedom victory conditions.

Keep the two panels separate when deciding what to build.
A completed car-ownership requirement does not mean
the next dependency level is available if qualifying
parking spaces are still missing.

The suburban population target also depends on actual
residents. Constructing houses is insufficient until
the objective counter records people living in them.


<h2 id="where-to-build-first" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Where to Build Your First Parking Lots in Gulf of Freedom
</h2>

The **hotel area** is a suitable starting location
because visitors are already traveling there.
The nearby beach and harbor offer additional demand,
but the surrounding coastline is not equally valuable.

Open the **Parking Demand Heatmap** and compare the
hotel, beach, and harbor before purchasing land.

  <Image
    src="/images/car-park-capital/car-park-capital-gulf-parking-demand-map.webp"
    alt="Car Park Capital Gulf of Freedom parking demand heatmap showing demand concentrated around the hotel, beach, and harbor"
    width={1600}

  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">
    Parking demand is concentrated around existing
    destinations. Large sections of undeveloped
    coastline have much weaker demand.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

Start with a small parking operation near the hotel.
Purchase the necessary plot, remove obstacles, and
connect the parking entrance, exit, and bays to a
usable road.

Allow customers to begin parking before expanding
into another section of the coast.

### Avoid Expensive Land That Adds Little Parking Demand

A coastal plot costing approximately **$13,000**
can consume a substantial amount of the early budget.
That purchase is difficult to justify when the site
does not overlap enough parking demand to attract
customers.

Treat the land price and its expected parking activity
as separate decisions.

A smaller plot near an existing destination may
serve more customers than an expensive, undeveloped
beachfront area.

Retain enough money to finish the entrance, exit,
road connections, and the next required facility.
An unfinished parking area produces no useful
income simply because the land has been purchased.

### Establish Vehicle Sales While the First Lot Operates

Residents need to own cars for dependency progression.

Place an accessible **Car Shop Carousel** near existing
pedestrian activity and begin selling vehicles before
committing to island construction.

The first vehicles can be stocked directly. A factory
is not necessary before the initial car sales begin.

For vehicle stocking, factory setup, and delivery
shortages, follow the
<Link href="/car-park-capital/car-production">Car Shop Carousel and Car Production instructions</Link>.

Once the hotel-side lot is operating, use the beach
as the next potential parking location. A small lot
serving an established beach destination is preferable
to buying a large coastal plot without confirming
its demand.


<h2 id="amphibious-car-unlock" className="scroll-mt-24">
  How to Unlock Amphibious Cars at Level 2
</h2>

**The Amphibious Car Factory unlocks at Car Dependency
Level 2 in Gulf of Freedom.**

It appears among the newly available construction
options after the Level 2 upgrade.

    className="rounded-xl"

  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">
    Car Dependency Level 2 unlocks the Amphibious
    Car Factory, which produces water-capable vehicles.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

The factory description explains that Amphibious Cars
can enter and leave lakes, rivers, and seas wherever
a road connects with water.

They can also travel through areas built on water and
visit other islands.

There are two requirements beyond unlocking the
factory: vehicles must reach customers, and the
road network must provide a suitable route to
the water.

A factory producing Amphibious Cars does not
guarantee that existing residents immediately
own them. Cars still need to be delivered and sold.

Level 2 also introduces a Food need. Check the
population's current satisfaction as new services
become available; the
<Link href="/car-park-capital">Car Dependency and Car Braininess instructions</Link>
explain how those requirements affect progression.

You can continue building mainland parking facilities
while amphibious production begins. There is no need
to transfer the entire business to an island
immediately after the unlock.


<h2 id="build-amphibious-factory" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Should You Build the Amphibious Car Factory on an Island?
</h2>

An industrial island can keep manufacturing away
from the hotel's valuable parking land, but it
creates another construction expense: the factory
still needs a usable connection for vehicle
deliveries.

Before buying an island plot, establish how the
finished cars will reach the Car Shop Carousel.

An industrial island connected by a raised road
or bridge can work, provided the factory and its
transport vehicles can reach the mainland road
network.

The additional road land may cost more than
expected.

A Gulf of Freedom industrial expansion required
another **$1,500 land purchase** to close the
remaining gap in its road connection. Until that
plot was purchased, the completed factories
could not begin the intended deliveries.

### Build the Connection Before Expanding Production

A mainland factory location with existing road
access is the simpler opening choice when cash
is limited.

For an industrial island, purchase the required
connecting land and establish a road that the
delivery vehicles can use before adding several
production buildings.

Check for these construction problems:

- **Unowned land:** A short missing plot can prevent
  the final road section from being completed.
- **Incorrect elevation:** A raised bridge or ramp
  may not meet the adjoining road.
- **Disconnected industrial roads:** Factories can
  be constructed without a usable delivery route.
- **Insufficient remaining money:** An operational
  factory still needs transport access and vehicle
  deliveries before it contributes to car sales.

After connecting the factory, inspect a Car Carrier
and confirm that finished Amphibious Cars can
reach the intended shop.

Avoid confusing the amphibious abilities of
residents' cars with the movement of ordinary
industrial trucks. The latter still need routes
their own vehicles can complete.


<h2 id="connect-road-to-water" className="scroll-mt-24">
  How to Connect a Road to Water for Amphibious Cars
</h2>

A road reaching the shoreline is the essential
connection for amphibious travel.

You do not need a bridge crossing the entire
Gulf merely to provide a water entry point.

Inspect existing coastal roads first. Gulf of
Freedom has shoreline approaches that can
be extended toward the sea without creating
an entirely new island connection.

To build an entry point:

1. Find a road near the shoreline and identify
   the land required to reach the water.
2. Purchase any missing plots.
3. Extend the road to the edge of the sea.
4. Adjust the terrain or ramp height where
   the new section does not meet the shoreline.
5. Confirm that the road actually reaches
   the water rather than stopping inland.

A road ending too high above the surface may
not provide the intended transition. Lower the
affected terrain or correct the road section
before attempting another long bridge.

### Confirm That Amphibious Cars Are Using the Water

Once Amphibious Cars have been sold, observe
whether one reaches the shoreline and enters
the sea.

Water travel is useful for destinations separated
by the sea, including island attractions and
services that amphibious vehicles can reach.

The road-to-water connection is only part of
the route. There must also be an accessible
destination and a way for the vehicle to
continue its journey.

A waterfront drive-thru may be reachable by
Amphibious Cars without being conveniently
accessible to ordinary supply trucks.

For a Gas Station, check its actual road access
and fuel delivery before committing to a
water-only location. The
<Link href="/car-park-capital/oil-gasoline">Gasoline Transportation and supply checks</Link>
cover the delivery requirements.


<h2 id="island-houses-not-working" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Fix Can't Navigate Home on Gulf of Freedom Islands
</h2>

**"Can't navigate home"** can appear after
building suburban homes on islands or
altering the surrounding road network.

A finished home does not guarantee a usable
route for the resident's vehicle.

Start by tracing the actual journey from
the home to the nearest road connection
or shoreline entry.

Pay particular attention to barriers.
A blocked road connection can make
an otherwise connected neighborhood
unreachable.

Removing an obstructing barrier can restore
access when that barrier is the cause of
the navigation failure.

However, not every island navigation
problem is caused by the same obstruction.

Ordinary cars cannot use the sea in the
same way as Amphibious Cars. Residents
living on isolated islands may still own
vehicles that cannot complete the required
water crossing.

When the warning persists, check:

- **Road connectivity:** Follow the route
  away from the house and look for
  disconnected segments.
- **Barriers:** Remove or reposition objects
  obstructing the intended street.
- **Road elevation:** Make sure raised and
  lowered sections connect correctly.
- **Vehicle type:** Confirm that an island
  resident has a usable way to cross the
  water instead of assuming every car
  can travel there.
- **Parking access:** Inspect nearby entrances
  and exits if vehicles become trapped
  inside a parking facility.

Avoid demolishing entire neighborhoods
before locating the failed connection.
The problem may be a single inaccessible
road section rather than the houses
themselves.

### Where to Build the 30 Suburban Residents

Gulf of Freedom requires **30 people living
in suburban homes**.

For that objective, a road-connected
mainland neighborhood avoids the additional
difficulty of island transport.

Build the residential area where people
can move in and travel normally, then
check the actual suburban population
shown in the objective panel.

Island housing can be developed later
when its travel routes are functioning.
It is not necessary to complete the
30-resident target on an isolated island.

The general housing controls and
population requirements are covered in
the <Link href="/car-park-capital">Car Park Capital suburban housing instructions</Link>.


<h2 id="beach-parking-expansion" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Increase Beach Parking Demand and Expand Existing Lots
</h2>

Higher Car Dependency levels can require
more parking spaces in areas with sufficient
demand.

This becomes difficult in Gulf of Freedom
because the strongest demand is concentrated
around specific destinations rather than
distributed evenly along the coast.

Return to the demand heatmap when a
dependency upgrade is waiting for
qualifying parking spaces.

The hotel, existing beaches, and harbor
are useful places to check before
purchasing more distant land.

### Make Beach Areas More Attractive

**Beach Umbrellas**, **Lifeguard Towers**,
and suitable coastal attractions can help
develop parking demand around beach locations.

Use them where there is room for both
visitors and accessible parking facilities.

A large lot on an undeveloped stretch of
coastline may contribute little to a
high-demand parking requirement.

Once the local demand increases, add
parking bays within the area that
qualifies for the upgrade.

### Extend a Beach Parking Lot or Build Another Entrance?

When an existing beach lot is already
serving customers, extending that facility
may be more economical than opening a
separate operation.

Additional land can be connected to the
original parking route, allowing new bays
to use the existing entrance and exit.

That arrangement is particularly useful
when the available beachfront is narrow
or divided into small purchased plots.

A raised extension may require ramps
or height adjustments. Inspect those
connections before adding another floor.

For an extension to work:

- New bays must connect to the
  original drivable area.
- Cars must be able to reach the
  added spaces.
- The existing entrance and exit
  must remain usable.
- The extension must overlap
  sufficient parking demand to
  advance the relevant counter.

A second entrance becomes worth
considering when the existing access
arrangement cannot serve the new
parking area effectively.

Do not construct an entirely separate
business simply because the parking
surface occupies another purchased plot.


### Level 3 → 4: Parking and Car Ownership

To reach Car Dependency Level 4,
Gulf of Freedom requires:

- **250 parking spaces in high-demand areas**
- **340 car owners**

Check these requirements separately
from the scenario's Level 7,
82% Car Braininess, and
30 suburban resident objectives.

Use the thresholds currently shown
in your own dependency panel.

When the ownership requirement is
already satisfied, adding another
Car Factory will not produce the
missing qualifying parking spaces.

Conversely, further coastal parking
construction will not directly resolve
a shortage of car owners.

Spend on whichever counter is
preventing the next upgrade.


<h2 id="gulf-next-steps" className="scroll-mt-24">
  What to Do When Gulf of Freedom Stops Progressing
</h2>

Reaching **Car Dependency Level 5** still
leaves two further dependency levels
before Gulf of Freedom's Level 7
victory requirement is satisfied.

At this point, beach parking, car sales,
residential access, and customer services
may all need attention.

Open the dependency panel and
the scenario objective panel.

**Parking capacity is behind:** Add
accessible spaces around established
hotel and beach demand. Expand a
working lot before purchasing a
distant island solely for parking.

**Car ownership is behind:** Keep
Car Shop Carousels stocked. Check
vehicle manufacturing and delivery
if shops repeatedly run out.

**Car Braininess is below 82%:**
Inspect the population's unsatisfied
needs. Food, fuel, drinks, and
restroom services become relevant
during progression, and their
availability matters even when
parking demand is strong.

**Suburban population is below 30:**
Complete accessible mainland
housing and confirm that residents
are moving in before developing
more isolated island homes.

**Amphibious Cars are not
reaching the water:** Confirm
that the vehicles have been
sold and that a usable road
actually meets the sea.

**Factories are built but
deliveries are not occurring:**
Check the industrial road
connection, especially any
unowned land separating the
factory from the mainland
transport network.

For the final scenario
objectives, the game must
show **Level 7**, at least
**82% Car Braininess**, and
**30 suburban residents**.

Continue advancing the
incomplete requirements shown
in those panels rather than
adding attractions that do
not address the current
shortage.
