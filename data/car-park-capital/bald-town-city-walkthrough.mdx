<div className="my-4 text-sm text-slate-400">
  Car Park Capital — Early Access, October 2026.
</div>

Bald Town City gives you **40 in-game days** to turn an office-heavy
city into a car-dependent one. The office towers already create
strong parking demand, but the scenario cannot be completed through
parking construction alone.

You need residents buying cars, enough occupied parking spaces,
subway stations removed, and people living in suburban homes.
Those activities progress at different speeds, so the order in
which you spend your money matters.

A fast city can complete the objectives as early as **Day 10**.
That is possible rather than required; there is no separate
Day 10 victory condition. Keep the five objective counters
moving instead of attempting to reproduce an exact completion day.


<h2 id="bald-town-objectives" className="scroll-mt-24">
  All Five Bald Town City Victory Conditions
</h2>

Open the **Scenario Objective** panel to track these requirements:

- **Car Dependency Level 7**
- **75% Car Braininess**
- **400 cars parked simultaneously**
- **Buy and demolish 7 subway stations**
- **75 residents living in suburban homes**

The deadline is **Day 40**. Every requirement must be satisfied
for the scenario to finish.

Two parking-related counters deserve special attention.

**Car Dependency Level 7** requires enough qualifying parking
spaces and car owners to complete the level upgrades.

**400 cars parked simultaneously** measures vehicles currently
occupying parking spaces. Building 400 bays does not automatically
complete that objective.

The remaining conditions have their own counters. More cars
will not replace a missing subway demolition, and more houses
will not count until the required residents live in them.


<h2 id="first-parking-lot" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Build Your First Parking Lot Near the Office Towers
</h2>

The tall office buildings are the strongest starting landmarks
in Bald Town City. They generate concentrated parking demand,
making nearby land more useful than empty plots farther from
the city center.

Before purchasing anything, open the **Parking Demand Heatmap**.
Look for red and orange areas around the office towers, then
compare them with the land you already own.

  <Image
    src="/images/car-park-capital/car-park-capital-bald-town-parking-demand.webp"
    alt="Car Park Capital Bald Town City parking demand heatmap highlighting very high demand around office skyscrapers"
    width={1600}

    className="rounded-xl"
  />
  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">
    The office skyscrapers create very high parking demand.
    Prioritize accessible plots within these areas.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

Start with a ground-level parking lot that connects to the
existing road. Clear the necessary obstacles, add a usable
entrance and exit, and place parking bays where arriving
vehicles can reach them.

There is no advantage to spending your starting budget on
a large multistory structure before the first lot is operating.

Let customers begin using the parking spaces and check
occupancy before buying adjacent land.

For Bald Town City, the opening decision is straightforward:

- **The lot fills regularly:** Look for nearby land within the
  same high-demand district.
- **Most bays remain empty:** Inspect demand and road access
  before adding capacity.
- **Cars cannot enter:** Correct the entrance, exit, or bay
  orientation before expanding the plot.

Reserve part of your starting money for vehicle sales. Car
ownership is required for dependency upgrades, so parking
construction and vehicle sales must progress together.

Set up an accessible car sales location while the first
parking business is operating. If shops begin displaying
**"No Vehicles for Sale,"** use the
<Link href="/car-park-capital/car-production">Car Factory, Carrier, and sales checks</Link>
to restore stock rather than building more empty parking bays.


<h2 id="suburban-residents" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Reach 75 Suburban Residents Without Wasting City-Center Land
</h2>

Bald Town City's housing objective requires **75 people
living in suburban homes**. The counter measures residents,
not the number of houses constructed.

Use the outskirts for suburban housing so that valuable
office-area plots remain available for parking.

The housing objective can progress while your car ownership
and parking network are still growing. There is no reason
to wait until Level 7 before beginning the residential work.

At **55/75**, the remaining task is to accommodate another
20 residents. Purchasing additional office-area land will
not directly solve that shortage.

Build accessible suburban homes and confirm that their
residents are being counted. An unfinished house or one
with an unusable connection should not be treated as
completed population progress.

Evicting tenants from urban buildings can also help clear
land for additional parking, but only when the suburban
housing network has enough room to accommodate them.

If the game displays **"Not enough space in suburban homes,"**
provide more usable residential capacity before attempting
the eviction again. The general housing and relocation
controls are explained in the
<Link href="/car-park-capital">Car Park Capital beginner and progression instructions</Link>.

Avoid clearing occupied buildings simply to speed up this
objective. Repeated evictions can contribute to unrest,
and centrally located buildings should only be removed
when the land is needed for a useful expansion.

Once the objective panel reaches **75/75**, stop spending
money on suburban housing solely for this victory condition.
The remaining budget can support whichever objective is
still incomplete.


<h2 id="subway-stations" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Buy and Demolish 7 Subway Stations
</h2>

Removing public transport is a separate requirement.
Bald Town City specifically asks you to **buy and demolish
seven subway stations**.

The purchase must come before demolition. Select a subway
station, inspect the plot containing it, and purchase the
necessary land. Remove the station after ownership transfers,
then confirm that the objective counter increases.

Demolishing a bus stop does not substitute for removing
a subway station.

### When Should You Buy the Stations?

Central plots can be expensive. A subway station plot may
cost approximately **$40,000**, depending on the land
being purchased.

Spending that amount at the beginning can delay your first
profitable parking operation or leave insufficient money
for vehicle sales.

A practical purchase order is to prioritize stations
whose land also supports a planned parking expansion.

Stations that are expensive or surrounded by weak parking
demand can wait until the city has a stronger income.

You do not need to build a new garage over every demolished
station. Once the station is removed and the counter advances,
its contribution to the demolition objective is complete.

Check the counter after each purchase-and-demolition action.
When it reaches **7/7**, spend nothing further on subway
removal unless that land is independently useful to your
parking business.


<h2 id="reach-level-seven" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Reach Car Dependency Level 7: Parking Spaces and Car Owners
</h2>

Level 7 is likely to require much more expansion than
the starting parking lot can support.

The important distinction is that the upgrade counts
**parking spaces in high-demand areas** and **car ownership**
separately.

The Level 6-to-7 panel in a Bald Town City Early Access
build shows these requirements:

- **1,000 parking spaces in high-demand areas**
- **1,390 car owners**

The displayed city has already reached **1,112/1,000**
qualifying parking spaces, but car ownership remains
at **1,376/1,390**.

With those exact counters, constructing another garage
does not address the remaining upgrade requirement.
The city needs **14 additional car owners**.

Keep the sales operation supplied and allow more residents
to buy vehicles. If the shops have no stock, correct the
production or delivery bottleneck first.

The numbers in your own dependency panel should take
priority when playing a different build or scenario.

### When Parking Spaces Are the Missing Requirement

The counter only recognizes parking capacity in areas
with sufficient demand.

A city may have more than enough total bays while still
falling short of the qualifying-space requirement.

For example, a Level 6-to-7 panel showing **925/1,000**
qualifying spaces is short by **75 spaces**.

Adding parking signs alone does not replace the missing
capacity. Find another high-demand plot or expand an
existing parking area where demand is already strong.

The office towers and their surrounding commercial
blocks are worth checking again at this stage.

A higher-capacity parking structure may help when
valuable land is limited, provided its new spaces are
accessible and located inside the qualifying demand area.

Avoid purchasing distant, low-demand land merely because
it is large enough for another garage. Extra bays outside
the relevant demand area may leave the dependency counter
unchanged.

### When Car Ownership Is the Missing Requirement

Stop treating parking expansion as the immediate priority
once qualifying spaces have met the displayed threshold.

Check whether the existing car shops can continue making
sales. A city with enough parking but an interrupted
vehicle supply can remain stuck below the ownership target.

Keep the current sales network operating until the
Car Dependency panel shows that the required number of
residents own cars.

Level 7 unlocks once both counters meet their applicable
requirements.


<h2 id="four-hundred-parked" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Get 400 Cars Parked Simultaneously
</h2>

The scenario's **400-car objective** is not the same as
the Level 7 requirement for high-demand parking spaces.

The level upgrade checks qualifying capacity. The scenario
checks actual vehicles occupying parking spaces at the
same time.

A parking network with 1,000 qualifying bays can still
fail the 400-car objective if fewer than 400 vehicles
are currently parked.

Check the number at different times of day before buying
another plot. Office districts attract commuting traffic,
so occupancy may rise when people arrive for work.

When the total remains below 400, inspect the parking
network rather than assuming it always needs more bays.

- **Parking lots are full:** Expand usable capacity near
  the strongest existing demand.
- **Many spaces are empty:** Check the demand heatmap,
  current parking fees, and the number of residents
  who own cars.
- **Vehicles cannot reach a parking area:** Inspect
  road access, entrances, exits, and blocked routes.
- **The counter increases during working hours:** Allow
  the active parking businesses to reach a busy period
  before making another major land purchase.

Pricing can also affect how many drivers use a lot.
If the spaces are accessible and demand is strong but
customers are not arriving, inspect the entrance fee
against what residents are willing to pay.

The key number is the live parked-car counter.
Once it reaches **400 or more**, this scenario
condition is satisfied.


<h2 id="keep-braininess" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Keep Car Braininess at 75% While Finishing the Scenario
</h2>

Bald Town City's objective panel must show at least
**75% Car Braininess** when the remaining conditions
are completed.

An earlier increase does not guarantee that the percentage
will stay above the target.

New Car Dependency levels introduce additional customer
needs, which can reduce satisfaction until the required
services are operating.

Check the needs indicator after each upgrade. The
<Link href="/car-park-capital">Car Dependency and Car Braininess progression instructions</Link>
explain how new needs affect the population and
which services become relevant as levels increase.

For Bald Town City, this check is particularly important
while you are expanding parking capacity and clearing
land around the office district.

A shortage at a Gas Station can leave a customer need
unsatisfied even though the building has already been
constructed. Check the
<Link href="/car-park-capital/oil-gasoline">Gasoline production and delivery chain</Link>
when a station repeatedly runs out of fuel.

Protests can interrupt otherwise functioning businesses
and damage parked vehicles. When protesters are already
blocking services, restore those operations before
committing money to further expansion; the
<Link href="/car-park-capital/unrest-protests">riot control and recovery steps</Link>
cover the immediate response.

Return to the objective panel after the affected services
are operating again. The percentage, rather than the
number of newly constructed buildings, determines whether
the 75% condition is complete.


<h2 id="final-objective" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Finish the Remaining Objective Before Day 40
</h2>

Bald Town City's five objectives can reach completion
in different orders.

Use the current objective panel to decide where the
remaining budget belongs.

When **7/7 subway stations** and **75/75 suburban
residents** are already complete, additional demolitions
or residential construction will not advance those goals.

If **Level 7** is complete but the parked-car total is
below 400, concentrate on occupancy and access to
existing parking facilities.

When **400 cars are parked** but dependency remains
at Level 6, return to the separate qualifying-space
and ownership counters. Meeting the scenario's live
parking target does not automatically unlock Level 7.

A drop below **75% Car Braininess** requires attention
to the population's current needs, even when the
other four objectives are already finished.

Once all five conditions are satisfied before Day 40,
the game displays its scenario completion message.
