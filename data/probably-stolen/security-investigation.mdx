Open your **Security Record** and read the amount recorded under each suspected crime, plus **Investigation Progress**. Enter those figures in the calculator below, stop adding new offenses, and compare natural decay with **Wild's Fixers** before spending Credits needed for rent. A Security inspection can empty your counter without ending the run. A criminal investigation is a different danger: transactions and other offenses build a case over several days, and an officer can eventually arrive with a fine, arrest, or execution. Treating those two systems as the same thing is how a shopkeeper can feel safe after an inspection and still lose the store later.

The decision that matters is what you can change **before the next day starts**. A large case with a slowly filling evidence meter may leave time to reduce recorded offenses. A smaller case with nearly complete investigation progress can be more urgent, especially when a Security officer has already been assigned.

## Where Do You Find Your Security Record?

The Mentor gives you a **Security Record** document, described as a copy of your file held by the Security Department. Keep it in your documents and open it whenever you need to assess the shop's criminal exposure. The record shows suspected criminal activities, an **Estimated Total**, assigned-officer details, an active operation, investigation progress, and the expected sentence.

![The Mentor hands the shopkeeper the Security Record document, which is updated with information held by the Security Department.](/images/probably-stolen/security-record-mentor-document.webp)

For example, one early record lists **Possession (Stolen Goods): 5 Credits** and **Estimated Total: 5 Credits**. It also shows **Investigation Progress: Not Applicable**, **Expected Sentence: Not Applicable**, and no assigned case officer. There is a recorded offense, but the amount is not high enough to start ordinary daily investigation growth.

![A Security Record showing 5 Credits of suspected possession of stolen goods and Not Applicable for investigation progress and expected sentence.](/images/probably-stolen/security-record-low-crime-example.webp)

Do not enter **Estimated Total Wealth**, lifetime sales, or the value of illegal items still sitting on your shelf in place of the Security Record figures. Those numbers describe different things. When you see *Not Applicable* after an earlier investigation, do not assume previously accumulated progress has been erased: the display can suppress the meter when the recorded crime total falls below 100.

## What Adds Crime to Your Security Record?

The game tracks more than simply owning an illegal-looking item. A contraband sale records its negotiated value under the relevant trafficking category, while selling stolen merchandise can add **Possession (Stolen Goods)** based on the item's selling price and stolen heat. Something that is both stolen and contraband can contribute to two separate entries. Selling it quickly does not make the transaction disappear from Security's case file.

Other entries arise from different mistakes. **Fraudulent Misrepresentation** covers problems such as falsely labeled water or certain defective products sold as legitimate goods. **Consumer Endangerment** can grow from unsafe merchandise, including contaminated water represented as safe. Bribery, operating without a required permit, and killing an ordinary customer are separately tracked offenses. If you are dealing with [water purity and labeling](/probably-stolen/water-purification) or [chemical production](/probably-stolen/chemist), the item's actual condition and the way you sell it both matter.

### Why can two identical sales produce different evidence?

The customer and your Starting Perks can change the value recorded against you. Certain Black Market and Revolution transactions produce reduced evidence; **Lower Levels +80 Tight Lips** also reduces evidence from relevant sales. **Intimidating** reduces recorded crime, whereas **Blabbermouth** can increase it. These adjustments apply when the offense is entered, not as a daily percentage reduction to an old case.

Check the amounts that actually appear in your Security Record after a risky transaction instead of multiplying a buyer's price by a guessed universal modifier. Special visitors and story transactions do not always follow ordinary customer rules. Your current recorded values are a much better starting point for deciding whether to pay Fixers.

Some offenses do not enter your Security Record immediately. Earlier sales of mislabeled water, unsafe chemicals, or defective ammunition can leave tracked amounts that convert into criminal evidence when the shop opens on a later day.

The timing matters: ordinary crime decay and investigation growth are calculated before those delayed tracking amounts are converted into new crime entries. Your Security Record can therefore increase at opening even though the newly added offenses did not contribute to that morning's investigation growth.

If the numbers change despite making no new illegal sales, review your earlier transactions. Enter the crime amounts currently visible on the Security Record; the calculator does not predict evidence that has not yet been recorded.

## How Fast Does Investigation Progress Increase Each Day?

The Security Record has two distinct quantities: **recorded crime in Credits** and **Investigation Progress out of 100**. Crime determines the ordinary rate of growth; the progress meter determines when the case can advance toward a Security officer. Reducing the first number does not automatically subtract the same amount from the second.

When the investigation meter is **below 80 at the start of the calculation**, each recorded crime category naturally loses **20 Credits**, stopping at zero. The remaining crime total then determines that day's base increase:

<GuideTable columns={investigationGrowthColumns} rows={investigationGrowthRows} />

The sentence column describes what the current crime amount could lead to **if an officer rules on that amount**. A large recorded crime total alone does not mean the officer arrives immediately, and 100 investigation progress does not by itself lock in the final sentence.

Consider a shop with **340 Credits of weapons trafficking**, **180 Credits of stolen-goods possession**, and investigation progress **30/100**. With no applicable reputation modifier or Fixers service, the two categories fall to 320 and 160 after one natural decay. Their new total is **480 Credits**, giving **+6** investigation progress. The next reading is therefore **36/100** if no new offenses or other events intervene.

At **80/100 or higher**, that ordinary 20-Credit reduction stops. Waiting without taking action is much less effective at that point, especially if the crime total remains in a high-growth bracket. At 100/100, look for an assigned officer or active operation instead of projecting another ordinary week as if no case action were pending.

### How does reputation change investigation speed?

Security reputation **+60 Trusted** halves daily investigation growth, discarding the fractional part. **-20 Watchlist** raises it by about 20%; **-60 Most Wanted** raises it by 50% instead of stacking with Watchlist. **Upper Levels -60 Petition to Security** adds another 50% multiplier, applied after the Security adjustments. The calculation uses integer rounding between the relevant steps, so simply multiplying the original base rate by every percentage can be wrong.

These thresholds concern the investigation itself. The wider consequences of raising or losing standing with Security, Upper Levels, and other groups are covered in [Probably Stolen Factions](/probably-stolen/factions).

## How Do Wild's Fixers I, II, and III Reduce Suspected Crime?

With the normal introduction enabled, **Joe Wild gives you his business card on Day 7**. With **Skip Intro** enabled, Joe Wild's Assistant is available from Day 1. You can dial **8376** without waiting for the normal Day 7 visit. Use the shop telephone to dial **8376** for Joe Wild's Assistant, then open **Wild's Network** and select **Wild's Fixers** under Informant.

Fixers I is the first available tier. Fixers II requires I, and Fixers III requires II. Once activated, the service reduces suspected crime records each night while its nightly fee is paid. Unlocking a tier is not the same as paying for a single immediate evidence reset.

<GuideTable columns={fixerColumns} rows={fixerRows} />

These are the base network purchase prices for **Price Gouge and Mark Up**. Network unlock prices ordinarily use **1.5× on Thin Margins** and **2× on Red Ink**, before other price-changing effects. Wild's Favor is spent toward an unlock first, with any shortfall covered by Credits; the **nightly fee is a Credits expense**. Use your own Wild's Network screen if its price differs from the table.

A common mistake is to remove a percentage from the combined case amount and then deduct the fixed value once. Fixers apply both reductions **to every crime category separately**, with the percentage portion truncated to an integer. For Fixers I, 400 Credits of trafficking becomes **290** after removing 15% and another 50; 200 Credits of possession becomes **120**. The Fixers-only result is **410 Credits**, rather than treating the original 600 as a single crime. The planner's final daily total may be lower because ordinary natural decay is calculated separately when Investigation Progress is below 80.

This matters when deciding between I and III. A case spread across several modest entries may fall rapidly because each receives the fixed reduction. One enormous entry behaves differently. Higher tiers are faster, but they are not automatically worth buying when ordinary decay would clear the remaining records before the next rent payment.

### Should you upgrade to Fixers III immediately?

Compare the number of nights the service is likely to run and the amount each tier removes, rather than choosing the strongest option by default. Starting without any Fixers, the base cumulative unlock cost through III is **1,350** before available Favor and difficulty adjustments; running III then costs **70 Credits per night** at the listed base rate. If you already own I or II, the earlier purchase is sunk and should not be charged again in your next decision.

For a shop with little available cash, paying a large network unlock while rent is due can create a new failure risk. Enter what you have **now** in the planner, not the projected resale value of unsold contraband. If a fee displayed in your save differs from the listed amount, use the planner's custom-price inputs rather than changing the difficulty multiplier for every type of cost.

## What Is the Difference Between Evidence Removal and Fixers?

**Fixers work on suspected crime amounts over successive nights.** **Evidence Removal** is a separate Wild's Network option aimed at evidence Security has already collected and an ongoing investigation. Its listed base purchase price is **500 Credits**, increasing by **100 with subsequent uses**, and it has a **14-day cooldown**. Do not treat its effect as though it were another Fixers level or a subscription that remains active automatically.

![Wild's Network displaying Evidence Removal, its description of interfering with collected evidence and ongoing investigations, and its 14-day cooldown.](/images/probably-stolen/security-joe-wild-evidence-removal.webp)

If the problem is a growing crime total but the investigation meter is still low, a nightly Fixers service may buy time while the records shrink. If the meter is already close to 100, slowly clearing the case total may not remove the urgency of collected evidence. Evidence Removal is the option to inspect when you need to interfere with that accumulated progress. Its cooldown means you cannot assume it will be available again next week.

Once Security has assigned an officer, check the **Active Operation**, **Assigned Case Officer**, and the next Security encounter after any intervention. A payment does not guarantee that an already scheduled visit has disappeared, and new illegal transactions can start rebuilding the case. The planner intentionally leaves one-time Evidence Removal out of its nightly forecasts because it changes the case state rather than behaving like a percentage reduction to ordinary crime records.

## When Does Security Fine, Arrest, or Execute You?

A growing investigation does not immediately end the run. Once progress reaches **100/100**, a case can be passed to a Security officer. The critical distinction is that Security evaluates the **crime amount still on the record when the officer's encounter begins**, not necessarily the amount from several days earlier.

Under the documented ordinary case rules, a positive recorded total **below 300 Credits** leads to a **fine equal to that amount**. Paying it clears the case but costs Security reputation; being unable or unwilling to pay can lead to arrest. A total of **300–1,499 Credits** leads to **Arrest**, while **1,500 Credits or more** enters the **Execution** branch. If the evidence meter has fallen below **95** or the case has no remaining crime when the officer evaluates it, that investigation path can be withdrawn instead, with a Security reputation penalty.

This is why the same 100/100 investigation meter does not always produce the same ending. Lowering the criminal record before the officer arrives may change the consequence, but the window is narrow and an officer already assigned should never be treated as harmless. An encounter caused by separate reputation penalties, such as **Security -100**, follows a different path and is not covered by the ordinary case brackets.

![The Arrested ending explains that the shopkeeper is imprisoned and the shop is leased to someone else while the sentence is served.](/images/probably-stolen/security-investigation-arrested-ending.webp)

One Fence run reached an **Arrested** ending after **84 days**. The closing screen establishes the consequence clearly: the shop is lost while the character is in the brig. It does not show the complete prior Security Record, so it cannot tell you exactly how fast that particular investigation advanced.

## Why Can Security Confiscate Items Without Completing an Investigation?

**Routine inspections** concern what officers discover in the shop during a visit. **Investigations** concern the offenses accumulated on your Security Record across transactions and days. Passing one inspection does not clear past criminal records, and a confiscation notice does not by itself reveal how many points the long-term investigation meter gained.

In a Gunsmith inspection, an officer confiscated **two Smoke Grenades listed at 60 Credits each** and a **counterfeit Immunivax Injector listed at 40 Credits**. The Inspection Report explicitly stated that Security reputation decreased. This is a concrete example of the cost of carrying prohibited or counterfeit merchandise when an inspector searches the store; it is not an isolated measurement of nightly investigation growth.

![A Security Inspection Report lists two confiscated Smoke Grenades and a counterfeit Immunivax Injector, followed by a Security reputation loss.](/images/probably-stolen/security-inspection-confiscated-smoke-grenades.webp)

Inspections can vary by officer and event. An ordinary inspection, a surprise search, a case officer arriving with a sentence, and an alarm response are not interchangeable encounters. Before making another risky sale, check both the goods currently exposed to inspection and the older entries already listed in the Security Record.

### When Does Security Inspect Your Shop, and How Should You Prepare?

Routine inspections can begin once your combined Trafficking and Possession records reach **50 Credits**. Higher recorded amounts can shorten the time between inspections, while Security reputation, active events, and inspector behavior affect what happens when officers arrive.

Before opening the shop, remove Contraband from the Display Case and exposed counters. Locked or concealed storage can keep merchandise out of an ordinary search, but moving the goods does not clear offenses already recorded in your Security Record.

The consequences also depend on what Security finds. Low-Level Contraband may receive a warning, Mid-Level goods can be confiscated, High-Level goods can bring a fine as well as confiscation, and Critical-Level Contraband can cause an arrest. Check the item's actual restriction before relying on its selling price.

### Can You Bribe a Security Inspector?

Only the **Shady Security Inspector** offers a bribe. Under the documented rules, the quoted payment is calculated from the current game day and Security reputation:

`100 + (2 × current day) − (2 × Security reputation)`

The amount is limited to **40–400 Credits**. A successful bribe ends that inspection, but it also contributes to your Bribery of Security Personnel record. Paying to escape an immediate search can therefore make a later criminal investigation more dangerous.

A regular Security Inspector does not accept that offer. The Lazy Security Inspector instead has a less thorough search pattern, including skipping the trash bin.


### What If You Cannot Avoid the Next Inspection?

**Inspection Early Warning Service** costs **300 Credits** to unlock, followed by a **10-Credit nightly fee**. It warns you about upcoming routine inspections, giving you time to move exposed merchandise into appropriate storage. It does not warn you about surprise inspections, and it does not reduce criminal evidence already recorded against the shop.

**Clemency** is a separate one-use protection with a base price of **250 Credits**. Each subsequent purchase costs another **100 Credits** before applicable price modifiers. If an inspection would normally lead to imprisonment, Clemency can turn that outcome into a warning instead. The protection is consumed when triggered, so it should not be treated as permanent immunity or a way to cancel an existing criminal investigation.

Use Early Warning when repeated inspections threaten inventory you still need to store or sell. Consider Clemency when a single high-risk inspection could end the run. Neither replaces Wild's Fixers or Evidence Removal when Investigation Progress is already increasing.

## Why Can Security Still Investigate You at 100 VIP Reputation?

At **Security +100**, the **VIP** benefit protects the shop from ordinary Security inspections and thief attacks. It does **not** say that previously recorded crimes are erased, that Investigation Progress drops to zero, or that an officer carrying out an existing criminal case must disregard it.

A shop can therefore have excellent Security standing and an investigation still progressing in the background. The **Trusted** benefit at +60 reduces the rate at which the investigation grows; VIP does not turn that rate into zero. If your Security Record shows a case, read the crime amounts and investigation meter even if routine inspectors have stopped visiting.

The reverse distinction also matters. **Security -100** can trigger an immediate **Framed** outcome independently of the normal crime-total calculation. Fixers do not repair negative faction standing. To see the thresholds and their other effects, check [Security reputation and faction penalties](/probably-stolen/factions).

## What Should You Do Before the Next Morning?

**If the record is small and Investigation says Not Applicable:** Stop generating new offenses and check again after the next opening. A 5-Credit possession entry is not the same threat as a 500-Credit trafficking case. There is little reason to buy a costly Fixers upgrade solely because one minor entry appears on the file.

**If progress is below 80 but crime is rising:** Stop the transactions that are adding new offenses. Compare how quickly the existing records would shrink without assistance against the upfront and nightly costs of Fixers. If ordinary decay can clear the case before investigation progress reaches 100, preserve your Credits for rent rather than paying for an upgrade you may not need.

**If progress is at 80–99:** Normal 20-Credit decay has stopped. Continued illegal sales add fresh exposure, while high recorded totals can push the meter to 100 quickly. Compare Fixers against Evidence Removal and confirm you have enough Credits left for the next rent. Do not spend the entire emergency fund on a service that does not directly cancel collected investigation progress.

**If progress is already 100 or a case officer is listed:** Stop relying on the next seven ordinary mornings. Inspect the active case and the Joe Wild options actually available in your run. A change in the record before the officer's dialogue can matter, but it is not safe to assume that ignoring or dismissing the officer will cancel the proceeding.

The useful number is not how much you've earned from contraband so far. It is how much crime is still recorded, how close Security is to acting, and how much cash you can spare to change that outcome without failing rent. For broader shop cash decisions, use the [Money-Making Planner](/probably-stolen/money-making) alongside the Security calculation.
