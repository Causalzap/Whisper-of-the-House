<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
To open a locked Supply Crate in Probably Stolen, use a **Disposable Keycard matching the crate's department**. A Security Keycard opens both the **Security Department Supply Crate** and the **Security Department Evidence Locker**. A **Command Keycard** opens any of the five standard department crates.

Pick up the card and use it on the locked crate. The card is consumed when the crate unlocks. If you do not have the correct card, use a powered **Cryptographic Desequencer** with the matching department chip and a Blank or existing Access Keycard. Security cards take **125 Work**, normally four nights with an unmodified machine.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

A locked crate can be valuable, but buying one is only the first step. You still need the correct access permissions, and opening it consumes a card that could have been sold, rewritten, or used on another container.

Before spending money on another card, identify the exact crate, check whether you already own a suitable key, and decide whether you actually want to open the contents. Some requests require the crate to remain sealed.

<section id="keycard-matches">

## Which Keycard Opens Each Supply Crate in Probably Stolen?

There are five standard locked department crates. Four use their department's own Access Keycard, while Security has two different containers that share the same access permission.

<GuideTable columns={keycardMatchColumns} rows={keycardMatchRows} />

A **Disposable Keycard (Command)** works on all five. It is not tied to a separate Command Supply Crate.

![The Disposable Keycard (Command) item description showing its single-use access permissions and ability to open most Station locks.](/images/probably-stolen/keycards-command-card.webp)

### Does a Supply Keycard Open Every Supply Crate?

No. **Supply Crate** is an item category, not a universal access permission.

A **Disposable Keycard (Supply)** exists, but it does not unlock the five standard departmental crates. These require Security, Engineering, Medical, or Service access according to the crate's department. A Command Keycard works on all five.

A Blank Keycard cannot unlock a crate until it has been programmed in the Cryptographic Desequencer.

Read the full name of the locked item before choosing a card.

For example, a **Security Department Supply Crate** specifically requires Security access, even though its item category is Supply Crate.

![A locked Security Department Supply Crate displaying Access card required and describing its Station Security Armory contents.](/images/probably-stolen/keycards-security-crate-locked.webp)

### How do you use a Keycard to open a locked crate?

1. Place the locked crate somewhere in your shop where you can select it.
2. Find a **Disposable Keycard** with the matching department permission, or use a Command Keycard.
3. Pick up the card and apply it to the locked crate.
4. After a successful unlock, the card disappears and the crate changes to its open state.
5. Double-click the opened crate to inspect its inventory, then transfer the contents into your available storage.

You do not need to wait overnight after applying a valid card. The unlocking step is immediate.

A wrong department card does not unlock the crate. Check both item names instead of repeatedly trying cards with unrelated permissions.

**Do not open a crate that you have already agreed to deliver sealed.** Once unlocked, it is no longer the same item category required by some buyers and requests.

<section id="get-keycards">

## How Do You Get Keycards and Supply Crates?

There are three practical ways to build a supply of Access Keycards: purchase them from traders, find them inside other crates, or make them with a Cryptographic Desequencer.

### Buy crates and cards from Thieves

Thieves can bring sealed Supply Crates and various Access Keycards to your shop.

Their stock varies between visits. Do not assume that a Thief selling an Engineering crate will also have an Engineering Keycard available.

When a seller offers a crate, check:

- Its full department name.
- Whether you already have the correct Keycard.
- The asking price and your remaining Credits.
- Whether you want the contents or need to retain a sealed crate for another purpose.

One Thief offered a **Security Department Evidence Locker for 200 Credits** while the player had only **205 Credits** available.

![A Thief offering a Security Department Evidence Locker for 200 Credits, with only 205 Credits available before the purchase.](/images/probably-stolen/keycards-thief-evidence-locker.webp)

That is a poor time to buy without a plan. You may still need a suitable Keycard, Energy, rent money, or funds for your normal business.

The 200 Credits shown here is one actual offer, not a promise that every Thief will sell the same container at the same price.

As you develop your Black Market connections, more reliable Thief access becomes useful for acquiring locked goods. See the [Factions Guide](/probably-stolen/factions) for reputation and supplier relationships.

### Buy Desequencer equipment from Dr. Jackson

Dr. Jackson can supply the **Cryptographic Desequencer**, Blank Keycards, and compatible chips, depending on his current stock.

The important distinction is that buying a Blank Keycard alone does not give you access to any department. You must program it first.

Likewise, a Security Desequencer Chip is not itself a usable Security Keycard. It must be installed in the machine to write Security access.

If Jackson has only one part of the setup, keep your Credits available for the remaining components instead of assuming production can begin immediately.

### Find more Keycards inside opened crates

Some crates can contain additional Access Keycards.

For example, a Security Department Supply Crate may contain a Security Keycard, while an Evidence Locker has a broader set of possible card rewards.

This creates an opportunity to replace a card you just consumed, but it is not guaranteed. Do not plan your next opening around receiving a specific card from the previous box.

You can also exchange certain cards for other cards or crates through the Underground Exchange, explained below.

<section id="cryptographic-desequencer">

## How Do You Make Keycards With the Cryptographic Desequencer?

The **Cryptographic Desequencer** converts one Access Keycard into another type by writing the permissions defined by an installed Desequencer Chip.

To make a Security Keycard, you need:

- A Cryptographic Desequencer.
- A **Cryptographic Desequencer Chip (Security)**.
- One Blank Keycard or another existing Access Keycard.
- A charged Energy Cell or sufficient compatible power supply.
- Enough overnight production cycles to complete the required Work.

The machine's base requirement is **10 Energy per day**, and its unmodified processing speed is **33 Work per day**.

![The Cryptographic Desequencer item description showing its 10 Energy per day requirement, 33 Work per day progress speed, and need for an Access Keycard and Desequencer Chip.](/images/probably-stolen/keycards-desequencer-machine.webp)

### Step 1: Install the department chip

Open the Desequencer and place the required chip in **CHIP SLOT**.

For a Security Keycard, select the Security chip. For an Engineering or Medical Keycard, use the corresponding chip instead.

The chip controls which permission is written. It is reusable and is not consumed when a card finishes.

Machine Performance Modules are separate from the department chip. A machine with several Performance Modules may finish cards faster, but those modules do not determine the resulting department.

### Step 2: Insert a Blank Card or Rewrite an Existing Keycard

Place one **Disposable Keycard (Blank)** in the **INPUT** slot.

A Blank Keycard has no usable department permission until processing finishes. The starting card has a base value of **15 Credits**.

![A Cryptographic Desequencer with a Security chip installed and a Disposable Keycard (Blank) in the input slot.](/images/probably-stolen/keycards-desequencer-blank-input.webp)

You can also use an **already programmed Access Keycard** as the input.

This allows you to rewrite a card you no longer need. For example, an unwanted Service Keycard can be turned into a Security Keycard if the machine has the Security chip installed.

The original card is consumed by the conversion. You receive one newly programmed card rather than keeping both.

This is especially useful when you own cards for departments whose crates you do not currently have.

### Step 3: Supply Energy and let the machine process overnight

Check the Energy Cell and leave the machine able to operate when you end the day.

Each successful production cycle adds Work toward the selected card.

<GuideTable columns={keycardPrintingColumns} rows={keycardPrintingRows} />

These are base processing times without Performance upgrades.

A Security chip requires **125 Work**. At 33 Work per night, the machine needs four successful cycles.

A Command chip needs **150 Work**, so it normally takes five successful cycles.

Do not mistake the machine's **Overnight** description for a promise that every Keycard finishes after a single night.

Higher Performance increases the work completed per cycle. Efficiency affects power use, not the access permission written onto the card.

### Step 4: Collect the completed Keycard

When processing finishes, inspect **OUTPUT** and take the finished Keycard.

The result should have the permissions associated with the installed chip. In this example, the machine has produced a **Disposable Keycard (Security)**.

![A Cryptographic Desequencer with a completed Disposable Keycard (Security) in the OUTPUT slot, confirming the finished card's department permission.](/images/probably-stolen/keycards-security-card-output.webp)

You can now use this Security Keycard to unlock either a Security Department Supply Crate or a Security Department Evidence Locker.

The card can open only one locked container before being consumed.

### Why is my Desequencer not producing a Keycard?

Check the following in order:

1. **No department chip:** The machine cannot determine which permission to write.
2. **No input card:** A chip alone does not create a Keycard.
3. **Insufficient Energy:** The machine needs power to advance overnight.
4. **Not enough Work completed:** Security and Command cards require several cycles at base speed.
5. **Output handling:** Check the OUTPUT slot and nearby shop inventory if the expected card is missing.

Since update **1.0.0.12**, completed Desequencer products can spill into available shop space when the output inventory is full. Check where the finished item was placed before assuming production failed.

If you are repeatedly making the same card, keep the appropriate chip installed and replenish the input as each cycle completes.

<section id="evidence-locker">

## How Do You Open the Security Department Evidence Locker?

A **Security Department Evidence Locker** is a locked, restricted container. It uses the same access permissions as the Security Department Supply Crate.

You need either:

- **Disposable Keycard (Security)**, or
- **Disposable Keycard (Command)**.

The Evidence Locker itself is **not** a Keycard. Buying one does not grant Security access.

![A Security Department Evidence Locker in the player's inventory, showing its Locked state, Mid-Level Contraband classification, and Access card required message.](/images/probably-stolen/keycards-evidence-locker-locked.webp)

Once you own the locker, pick up the appropriate Keycard and use it on the locked container. Then open the unlocked locker to inspect the contents.

### What can an Evidence Locker contain?

Its possible rewards include restricted chemical supplies, medicines, contraband, weapons, uncommon equipment, and various Access Keycards.

The pool is broader than that of an ordinary Security Department Supply Crate. However, broader does not mean every locker contains a rare or valuable item.

A low-cost or unwanted result is possible even when you spend a Command Keycard.

If you need the sealed locker for a customer request, do not unlock it just to inspect the contents. Decide which outcome matters before consuming the card.

### Should you buy an Evidence Locker without a Keycard?

Only when you can afford to keep it until a matching card becomes available.

If you already have a Security chip, a Desequencer, and spare Energy, you have a predictable way to make an opening card.

If you have none of those, purchasing a locked Evidence Locker ties up Credits and inventory space while you search for access.

A Command Keycard works, but using a Security Keycard is generally the more targeted choice when you have both available.

The locker and some of its contents are Contraband. Opening it does not make restricted items safe to display or sell. If Security has recorded an offense or started investigating your shop, check the [Security Investigation Guide](/probably-stolen/security-investigation) before making further risky transactions.

<section id="crate-rewards">

## Which Supply Crate Is Worth Opening?

Different departments have different reward pools. Choose a crate based on what your business needs, not only on the sealed container's displayed price.

<GuideTable columns={keycardCrateColumns} rows={keycardCrateRows} />

These are **possible contents**, not guaranteed rewards or fixed quantities.

### Engineering vs Security: Which is more useful?

An Engineering crate is a better fit when you are building production machinery.

One Engineering opening produced multiple useful workshop items, including batteries, a Module Bay Expansion Kit, a Current Converter, tools, and processing supplies.

A Security crate is more useful when your inventory needs ammunition, defensive tools, or weapons.

In another opening, the Security crate contained items such as a Combat Knife, ammunition-related equipment, and other security supplies.

The decision depends on whether your next bottleneck is **production equipment** or **combat stock**.

### Medical vs Service: Which should you choose?

Medical crates are a reasonable option when you need medicines, bandages, injectors, or Chemical Supplies.

In one opening, the Medical crate provided medical items, bandages, a battery, and chemical ingredients.

Service crates focus more on household goods and practical utilities. They can contain Water Filters, cleaning materials, seeds, and other useful equipment.

If you are working on Pure Water production, a Service crate may offer relevant supplies, but it is not a reliable substitute for buying the specific missing purification equipment.

For actual purification requirements, use the [Pure Water Guide](/probably-stolen/water-purification).

### Should You Sell a Sealed Crate or Open It?

Opening a crate does not guarantee that its contents will sell for more than the sealed container.

Before using a Keycard, compare the price you paid for the crate, the card you will consume, and whether the possible rewards are useful to your shop.

- **Keep it sealed** if a customer or faction request specifically requires an unopened Supply Crate. Opening it first can make it unsuitable for that request.
- **Open it** if you need the possible contents immediately and already have a suitable Keycard.
- **Consider selling it sealed** if you primarily need Credits and cannot afford to risk receiving unwanted items.

An Engineering crate may be worth opening when you need machine modules or a Current Converter. A Security crate is more useful when you need weapons, ammunition, or other security supplies.

**Check your Luck before unlocking.** If the crate has not already had its contents scanned, and your current Luck is higher than when its contents were generated, the game can reroll them at the higher value.

Use Liquid Luck or another available boost before applying the Keycard. Higher Luck can improve eligible rewards but does not guarantee a particular rare item.

After opening, inspect the individual items. Restricted or stolen goods may still need careful handling even though the crate is no longer sealed.

For a broader comparison of purchase costs, expected sales, and rent safety, use the [Money-Making Planner](/probably-stolen/money-making).

<section id="underground-exchange">

## Should You Use Keycards in the Underground Exchange?

The **Underground Exchange** provides another way to turn Access Keycards into useful stock.

Sawyer normally introduces the Underground Exchange on Day 25. The Fence starting scenario and Sawyer's Crew perk can provide earlier access. Read the **Underground Exchange Directive** to see the currently available trades.

If your Black Market reputation falls to **-40**, the Exchange becomes unavailable. Do not leave valuable Keycards in the trash can expecting a reward while you are Blacklisted. Check the [Factions Guide](/probably-stolen/factions) for the reputation restrictions.

![The Underground Exchange Directive showing permanent and promotional trades, including Access Keycards and department Supply Crates.](/images/probably-stolen/keycards-underground-exchange.webp)

One Keycard exchange is permanently available, while the crate exchanges depend on the current weekly promotions.

**Permanent Exchange**

- **1 Command Keycard → 2 random department Keycards**, drawn independently from Security, Engineering, and Medical. Both results can be the same type.

**Promotional Exchanges — Only When Listed**

- **3 Security Keycards → 1 Security Department Supply Crate**.
- **4 Medical Keycards → 1 Medical Bay Supply Crate**.
- **4 Engineering Keycards → 1 Engineering Foundry Supply Crate**.

Only three promotional offers are available at a time, and the selection resets on Monday. Check your current Underground Exchange Directive before placing Keycards in the trash can.

### How do you complete an Underground Exchange?

1. Read the current directive and select the offer you want.
2. Gather the exact required items.
3. Place those items in the trash can used for the exchange.
4. Enable and pay for the **Janitorial Service** that night.
5. Check the returned goods the following morning.

Do not place unrelated valuable items in the trash can or assume that every combination will be recognized as an exchange.

Make sure there is room for the expected delivery before starting.

### Is trading Keycards for a crate always worthwhile?

No. Consider how many cards the exchange consumes and whether you will have another appropriate card left to open the returned crate.

For example, exchanging your only three Security Keycards for a Security Supply Crate leaves you with **zero Security Keycards** to unlock that new crate.

You would then need another Security card or a Command card.

If you have a powered Desequencer that can regularly produce Security cards, this may be manageable.

If you urgently need a sealed crate for a delivery request, the exchange may be more valuable than opening an existing box for random loot.

The Command exchange serves a different purpose. Converting one flexible Command card into two random department cards gives you more individual cards but sacrifices the ability to choose any lock with that one card.

Use it when extra cards are more useful than preserving a Command Keycard.

<section id="keycard-problems">

## Why Can't You Open a Crate With a Keycard?

Before buying a new card or machine, identify which part of the process is failing.

### The crate still says Access Card Required

This message means the item remains locked.

Check whether you have applied a matching Disposable Keycard. Merely owning the card elsewhere in your shop does not automatically unlock the container.

A Security Evidence Locker accepts Security or Command access. An Engineering Foundry crate accepts Engineering or Command access.

If the card's department does not match, you need another card or must rewrite the one you have.

### I have a Command Keycard, but it does not work

Command access works on the five standard department crates listed above. It does not mean every sealed or suspicious-looking object can be opened.

For example, **Care Package for Eleanor** is a story-related package, not a normal department Supply Crate.

A Security or Command Keycard cannot unlock it as if it were an Evidence Locker. A Case Cracker does not turn it into a standard openable crate either.

Check the item's exact name and category before spending money on additional access equipment.

### Can you reuse a Keycard after opening a crate?

No. Disposable Keycards are consumed by successful unlocks.

If you need to open several Security crates, prepare multiple Security cards or other valid alternatives.

Do not assume a lucky crate drop will replace the card you just spent.

### Should you use a Command Keycard on every crate?

A Command Keycard is flexible, but that flexibility has value.

When you have the matching department card, use it first unless there is a specific reason to preserve it.

Save Command cards for crates whose department cards are unavailable, for the Underground Exchange, or for situations where you need access without preparing several different chips.

### What Should You Do After Opening Your First Crate?

Inspect the items before selling them. Keep equipment that solves an immediate production shortage, and set aside any matching Keycard for your next locked crate.

If the contents include restricted goods, check how you plan to store or sell them before placing them on display.

For your next opening, choose which department card to produce based on the crates you already own. Avoid spending several nights printing cards for containers you do not have.

</section>
