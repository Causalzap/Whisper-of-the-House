A rat with **1.48 Growth** can be born from a breeding program that began with animals closer to 1.10–1.18. Reaching that point takes more than keeping a cage full of rats. You need to choose which animals reproduce, make enough room for their offspring, and avoid losing promising breeders to hunger, disease, or old age.

There are two ways to approach rat farming. You can raise a mixed colony for meat, collecting animals as they mature, or selectively breed a smaller group to improve the next generation. Both use the same cages and food supplies, but the second approach becomes harder when every newborn is allowed to breed freely.

The most useful starting point is a manageable colony where you can tell your breeding animals apart from the rats you intend to harvest.

## Starting a Rat Farm With the Rat Wrangler Scenario

Rat Wrangler begins with an Animal Cage, rats, and some food, allowing you to start raising animals immediately. You do not have to wait until you've built an entire production system to see the first animals grow and reproduce.

In one Hard Mode Rat Wrangler opening, the shop began with **600 credits** after its selected starting conditions. That amount belonged to that particular character setup rather than being a fixed balance for every Rat Wrangler.

The first task is getting the rats into a controlled space.

1. Place or open the Animal Cage where you can access its contents.
2. Move your starting rats into the cage.
3. Keep food available for the animals as their needs increase.
4. Inspect the cage after each night for growth, births, crowding, and damaged equipment.

An Animal Cage can also be expanded using Metal Ingots. The cage shown here has **five expansions remaining** and is fully repaired at **100/100 durability**.

![Animal Cage showing 100/100 durability and the option to expand with Metal Ingots, with five upgrades remaining.](/images/probably-stolen/rat-cage-metal-ingot-expansion.webp)

The cage matters for more than organization. Rats outside controlled enclosures can move around the shop and consume food you intended to keep for sale or for later feeding.

You may have enough food for the first few animals without needing another purchase immediately. That changes as babies arrive and the population grows. The first expansion decision should follow the number of rats you can actually house and feed, not simply how quickly you can acquire more.

### Another cage or a feed dispenser?

A second Animal Cage gives you space to separate groups, which becomes useful when you want specific parents to reproduce. It can also keep animals of different ages and conditions from becoming mixed together.

A Livestock Feed Dispenser addresses a different need: managing food for the animals you already have.

Expanding the existing cage adds capacity without creating another group to manage. Buy a second cage when you need to separate selected parents from growing or aging animals. A Livestock Feed Dispenser addresses feeding instead of housing, so choose it when available food is the immediate concern.

During one early Rat Wrangler run, the original cage became overcrowded within the opening days, and rats began moving around the shop. The player considered adding another cage to control which animals were fed and where they stayed.

## Which Rat Stats Matter for Breeding?

**Growth** is the main attribute to watch when you're trying to produce larger rats. A high-Growth animal is a valuable breeding candidate, but it should not be evaluated without considering the condition and other attributes of the potential parents.
Several displayed attributes help determine which rats are worth keeping:

- **Growth Rate:** The main value to compare when selecting rats for faster weight gain.
- **Hunger:** The rat's current feeding meter, displayed separately from its Hunger Rate.
- **Hunger Rate:** An attribute to consider when maintaining a larger colony.
- **Longevity:** Particularly useful for breeding animals you want to keep across multiple pregnancies.
- **Immunity and Max Health:** Help you assess whether a promising rat is likely to need additional care.
- **Fertility:** Check the current percentage alongside the animal's age and pregnancy status.
- **Expected Litter Size:** An estimate of offspring per litter, not a guaranteed number of newborns.

Growth is especially easy to focus on because you can compare the numbers directly. However, breeding solely for the highest displayed Growth can leave you with animals whose other characteristics are inconvenient for a working farm.

For example, an animal with excellent Growth but poor health may not survive long enough to contribute much to the next generation. A female with useful Growth and longevity can be worth retaining even when another rat has a slightly higher number.

### How to select the parents

Begin by inspecting adult male and female rats. Choose a pair with favorable Growth and acceptable health, then keep them together in a cage with room and food.

Once a female becomes pregnant, watch her pregnancy status and keep her alive through the remaining days.

A recorded pregnancy displayed **2/3 days**, so the status can be used to track progress toward birth. Treat that displayed progress as the immediate information for the animal you're checking, rather than assuming every rat will reproduce on a perfectly fixed schedule.

Make sure the cage has room for additional rats before the birth. A colony that looks comfortable with two adults can become crowded when several infants appear at once.

## Breeding Example: 1.46 and 1.15 Growth Parents

A selective-breeding run provides a useful example of how the colony can improve over several generations.

The initial breeding group contained animals with Growth values around **1.07–1.18**. Rather than keeping every rat as a potential parent, the player began retaining stronger candidates and checking new litters for better attributes.

One major improvement came from introducing a particularly expensive female rat with **1.46 Growth**. She was paired with a male whose Growth was approximately **1.15**.

![Aurora with 1.46 Growth and pregnancy status, beside Fuzzy with 1.15 Growth at age 11 days.](/images/probably-stolen/rat-breeding-parent-growth.webp)

The breeding pair was chosen for more than the high number on the female. The male offered a useful combination of Growth, longevity, and hunger-related characteristics, making him a reasonable partner for the stronger newcomer.

### What Growth did their babies have?

The female produced **four offspring**. The observed Growth values included:

- **1.30**
- **1.37**
- **1.30**
- **1.30**

![Two newborn rats with Growth Rates of 1.30 and 1.37, including an Immunocompromised male.](/images/probably-stolen/rat-breeding-offspring-growth.webp)

This was a substantial improvement over the earlier colony, where a Growth value around 1.18 had been one of the strongest available.

The useful breeding decision came after the birth. The offspring were not all identical, and the 1.37 rat was an especially valuable candidate for continued breeding.

You should inspect each newborn individually rather than keeping or discarding an entire litter based on its parents.

These results do not mean the game always calculates offspring Growth as the exact average of both parents. The observed litter varied, and the later generations produced additional differences. Use the parents' displayed attributes to make informed selections, then judge the actual offspring when they arrive.

### The later 1.48 Growth result

Continued breeding eventually produced a rat with **1.48 Growth at birth**.

![Newborn female rat, age 0 days and weighing 5 g, with a natural Growth Rate of 1.48.](/images/probably-stolen/rat-natural-growth-148.webp)

The important detail is that this value appeared on a naturally born animal, not merely as the result of applying a growth-enhancing item to an older rat.

Once your colony produces offspring with higher natural Growth, they can replace weaker members of the breeding group. Keep suitable males and females rather than replacing every breeding animal with whichever individual has the single highest Growth value.

A colony needs enough healthy breeding partners to continue producing the next generation. Selecting only one exceptional animal while ignoring sex, fertility, health, and available space can slow the program instead of improving it.

## Natural Growth vs Growth Serum: What Changes?

A boosted rat can display two Growth values at once. Fuzzy shows **1.56 (1.30)**, separating its current boosted Growth from the underlying value.

![Fuzzy displaying 1.56 boosted Growth over a base value of 1.30, with active Livestock Steroid Serum.](/images/probably-stolen/rat-growth-serum-boost.webp)

The tooltip also shows **15 UL of Livestock Steroid Serum remaining**, with **5 UL consumed per day**. Fuzzy's Hunger Rate reads **84.11 (56.07)**, higher than its underlying value. Keep food available and check the remaining serum before the next overnight cycle.

The distinction matters when choosing parents. A naturally strong rat and a rat temporarily showing a larger number after treatment are not automatically equivalent breeding candidates.

The observed increases also do not establish how enhanced attributes are inherited by offspring. To judge the next generation, inspect the children's actual Growth values after birth.

### When should you use a serum?

A practical approach is to reserve growth-enhancing items for animals you intend to keep long enough to benefit from the treatment.

Before using one, check:

- Whether the animal is part of your breeding group or is being raised for harvest.
- Its current Growth, age, health, and expected remaining lifespan.
- Whether you have sufficient food to support further growth.
- Whether another animal has better natural attributes for the next breeding pair.

A serum is less useful when an animal is already near the end of its life, or when your farm cannot support the additional feeding and space required for continued growth.

Serums can also compete with other purchases. Keeping enough food and maintaining the cages come before spending your last available credits improving one animal.

## How to Stop Rats From Escaping, Starving, or Overcrowding

Rapid breeding produces a second challenge: keeping the growing population under control.

A cage can become overcrowded after births even when it had enough room the previous night. One early colony progressed from a handful of contained rats to animals roaming around the shop because the enclosure could no longer hold everyone.

![A juvenile rat showing Overcrowding 2 while other rats are outside the Animal Cage.](/images/probably-stolen/rat-cage-overcrowding.webp)

When this happens, check the number and condition of the animals rather than assuming they can remain together indefinitely.

Separate valuable breeding animals from the wider colony where possible. A dedicated breeding enclosure also makes it easier to identify newborns and compare their attributes without sorting through every adult you own.

### Don't leave food where escaped rats can reach it

Loose rats can interfere with food storage. In an uncontrolled early colony, animals consumed ordinary food items that the shop could otherwise have sold.

Protect valuable merchandise by keeping the rats contained and moving food intended for customers away from roaming animals.

Feeding the colony should be a deliberate part of the daily routine. Check which rats need food before the next overnight period, especially pregnant females and the animals you intend to keep as breeders.

Another run experienced a food-price increase of **30%** after the player had already allowed the colony to grow. With more rats to support, food became a significant operating pressure.

That is a useful reminder not to expand the population merely because the animals are reproducing successfully. Each additional rat creates another feeding obligation.

### Watch for cage damage

Animal Cages require maintenance. In one early run, repairing a cage left its displayed condition at **77/100**.

That was an improvement, but it was not a full repair.

Check the remaining cage condition and prepare additional maintenance when needed. A damaged enclosure can become another problem on top of the population and food issues.

For a large breeding program, inspect cage condition as part of the daily animal checks rather than waiting until rats are already moving around the shop.

## What Should You Do With Diseased or Aging Rats?

A valuable breeding rat can still become Diseased or Immunocompromised. Removing it immediately may mean losing several generations of progress, particularly when its natural Growth is higher than the rest of the colony.

During one breeding run, an immunity serum was used to keep a promising diseased animal rather than immediately removing it. Check the rat's condition, age, and breeding value before committing that treatment. An ordinary low-Growth rat being raised for meat may not justify the same resources.

For an ordinary meat-producing colony, the decision may differ. A low-Growth animal with poor health does not necessarily justify the same resources as a strong breeding female.

### Age affects breeding decisions

Rats can advance through Infant, Juvenile, Adolescent, Adult, and Elder stages.

In the extended breeding run, some animals died at approximately **14–16 days old**. These were observed lifespans, not a fixed death age for every rat.

Longevity becomes valuable because you need a breeding animal to survive long enough to reproduce and, potentially, produce more than one litter.

Pay particular attention to pregnant females approaching old age. A valuable pregnancy offers little benefit if the mother does not survive to deliver the offspring.

Inspect the current health, age, and pregnancy status before deciding whether an older animal should remain in the breeding group.

### Keep a smaller, healthier breeding group

Sorting rats by purpose makes health management more manageable.

You can maintain a group of selected breeders, separate animals being raised for meat, and move older rats out of the main breeding enclosure when you need room for the next generation.

The exact number of cages depends on how many animals you can feed and maintain. There is no benefit to creating several specialized groups if you cannot support their basic needs.

Prioritize animals whose continued survival has a clear purpose: producing better offspring, supplying meat, or replacing aging breeders.

## How to Raise Bigger Rats and Get More Meat

Growth selection ultimately needs to produce a useful result: heavier animals that can be harvested.

One rat-farming run kept adults around **500 g** for slaughter, obtaining a Steak from a 500 g rat. Another showed an adult weighing **600 g**.

One adult male reached **521 g at six days old**, with **1.31 Growth** and Neutered status. Its weight shows the size achieved at that age, but the Neutered status rules it out as a breeding parent. This makes it a candidate to evaluate for meat production rather than continued selective breeding.

![A six-day-old adult male rat weighing 521 g, with 1.31 Growth and Neutered status.](/images/probably-stolen/rat-adult-521g.webp)

These are useful observed weights, but they do not establish a universal minimum weight for every meat item.

There was also a more unusual observation: **two rats both weighing 600 g did not necessarily display the same Giant appearance**. One was visibly Giant while the other appeared normal.

That means weight and Giant appearance should not automatically be treated as interchangeable measurements. Inspect the animal itself rather than using its weight alone to declare it a Giant Rat.

### Which rats should you keep?

For a meat-producing colony, a sensible harvest order starts with animals that are no longer useful to your breeding plan.

- Keep healthy animals with valuable natural Growth and suitable breeding characteristics.
- Reconsider older breeders when stronger replacements are ready.
- Raise the animals intended for meat until their current weight and harvest output justify using them.
- Check disease and available space before leaving more adults in the colony.

The recorded farming runs used a Meat Cleaver to harvest rats, then stored the resulting meat for customers who wanted food.

A Private Chef was one of the buyers encountered for rat meat. However, buyers still had limited budgets, and large pieces could be difficult to sell when a customer lacked sufficient money.

You may therefore need to keep some meat in storage rather than harvesting every adult at once.

To see whether harvested rat meat is worth selling now, [compare its feed costs, saleable quantity, customer price, and time until sale](/probably-stolen/money-making) against your other businesses.
