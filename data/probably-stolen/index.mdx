<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
Choose **Apprentice** if this is your first run. Check the next rent payment, put merchandise in the **Display Case before opening**, and inspect incoming goods before spending your credits.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

The first expensive mistake in Probably Stolen usually happens before the shop has earned anything. A tempting machine, a discounted item, or a customer offering a large batch of goods can absorb the cash you still need for rent. The shelves look more impressive, but the landlord wants credits, not inventory.

Your starting scenario determines the tools and merchandise available at the beginning. After that, the shop runs on a repeating cycle: prepare stock, attract customers, negotiate trades, manage what you bought, and make it through another rent payment. Production machines, faction relationships, and riskier trading opportunities add more decisions as the business grows.

<section id="starting-scenarios">

## Which Starting Scenario Should You Choose?

**Apprentice is the best first choice** if you want to learn the trading counter, customer requests, inspection tools, and inventory management without committing to a specialized production business immediately.

Other starts provide equipment for a particular trade or deliberately make survival harder. Pick one based on what you want to manage every day, not just which merchandise sounds most valuable.

The Demo includes Apprentice, Water Merchant, Rat Wrangler, and Rock Bottom. The full 1.0 release is scheduled to expand the selection to nine scenarios.

### Water Merchant or Rat Wrangler?
**Water Merchant** suits a shopkeeper who prefers buying, testing, and processing goods. Its equipment gives you a path toward regular water production, but rent still has to be paid while the business develops. [Start with the water trading route](/probably-stolen/water-merchant-guide) if that is the business you want to build.

**Rat Wrangler** trades machine management for a growing animal colony. Food, cage space, disease, and breeding choices all need attention as the population increases. [Check how to raise and breed rats](/probably-stolen/rat-breeding) before letting the colony expand.

For a first run, Apprentice offers a less specialized starting point. Rock Bottom is more demanding when you're still learning how to complete trades and manage limited stock.

## What Should You Do on Your First Day?

Before opening the shutter, take a moment to inspect the room. The calendar, negotiation area, Display Case, storage spaces, and tools you begin with all affect the day's trades.

Use this opening routine:

1. **Read the calendar and rent information.** Note the amount due and how many days remain.
2. **Clear enough counter space to trade.** Keep tools accessible, but move unrelated merchandise out of the way.
3. **Separate your merchandise.** Keep goods ready for sale apart from items that need inspection and materials you intend to use later.
4. **Fill the Display Case with goods you want customers to buy.** Do this before opening the shop.
5. **Open the shutter and handle the first customer.** Read their request, inspect the goods, and confirm the credits involved before accepting.
6. **After closing, check your inventory and overnight needs.** A machine or animal that requires attention cannot be ignored just because trading has ended.

The first day does not need to be profitable enough to buy a new machine. It needs to leave you with usable stock, a clearer understanding of customer behavior, and enough cash to continue operating.

### How do you buy and sell items?

Place the goods involved in a transaction on the appropriate trading counter to open the negotiation interface. A customer may be selling merchandise, trying to purchase something from you, or offering a trade that includes several items.

Check what they are actually asking for before choosing goods from storage. A customer looking for Food does not necessarily want the expensive tool or machine you have available, even if its estimated value is higher.

Customer budgets matter just as much as item values. Someone who cannot afford a large piece of merchandise may still purchase a smaller item of the correct category.

When you're buying, inspect the offered item's condition, contents, and relevant details first. A low purchase price is useful only when the item is something you can store, use, or reasonably sell again.

<section id="display-case">

## How Does the Display Case Attract Customers?

The Display Case is positioned to the left of the shutter door. The type of merchandise inside influences which additional customer comes to your shop.

For example, displaying Food can attract someone interested in purchasing Food. Including merchandise from another category can also affect the kind of buyer you attract.

The initial Display Case can attract **one additional customer per day**. Upgrading it increases that capability.

There are two details worth checking before you open:

- **Item category:** This helps determine what the attracted customer wants to buy.
- **Displayed merchandise value:** This influences the social class of the attracted customer, which can matter when you're trying to sell expensive stock.

A shop full of valuable products can still struggle with sales when its display attracts the wrong customers.

### You're attracting buyers, but still not selling

Look at the merchandise in the window and the stock you actually want to sell.

A Food-focused display isn't especially useful when the business needs customers for Water or Medical goods. Adjust the display to match the available merchandise rather than leaving the same products there indefinitely.

Also check whether you need **buyers or sellers**. Purchasing more stock while your storage is already full can make the next day's trading more difficult. Signs and other customer-attraction options should support what the business needs at its current stage.

Display changes won't guarantee that every customer can afford an expensive product. Keep some smaller or lower-priced goods available when appropriate.

**Never put contraband in the Display Case.** Doing so can trigger arrest, even when you only intended to attract a particular customer.

<section id="rent">

## How Do You Survive Rent in Probably Stolen?

Rent is collected regularly, regardless of whether your shop has made enough successful trades.

The calendar provides the next payment date. The amount can change with difficulty, selected perks, and the state of the run, so rely on the payment displayed in your own game.

The most dangerous situation is having enough merchandise to appear wealthy but not enough available credits when the landlord arrives.

### What to do when rent is approaching

Look at the amount due before making another large purchase.

- **Several days remain:** Keep trading, but avoid committing so much cash that ordinary business expenses become difficult to cover.
- **One or two days remain:** Concentrate on completing sales with buyers who can afford your available stock. Delay optional machinery and speculative purchases.
- **Rent is due today:** Preserve the required credits. Merchandise that might sell tomorrow cannot cover today's payment.

Suppose a customer offers an expensive machine that would improve production next week. The purchase could still be harmful if it leaves your available cash below the landlord's next payment.

The machine's future value does not make those credits immediately available again.

Before purchasing another production line, [compare its upfront cost, expected sale date, and rent-day cash balance](/probably-stolen/money-making) using the prices in your current shop.

### Does Hard Mode change rent?

Yes. In the Demo, Hard Mode removes the normal retail markup, removes the starting rent grace period, increases certain service costs, and makes rent grow more aggressively.

This changes the consequences of early spending. Trades that provide a comfortable margin under easier settings may leave much less room for upgrades on Hard Mode.

Rent-related negative perks can increase that pressure further. For example, **Bad Lease** adds 40 credits to the initial rent in the recorded Demo setup.

Read the difficulty and perk descriptions before starting a challenging run. The difficulty affects how much financial room you have for mistakes, not merely the number of customers you must serve.

<section id="perks">

## Which Starting Perks Are Worth Taking?

Starting Perks can change the shop's finances, reputation, customer availability, or the problems your character must manage.

Positive perks generally consume perk points, while negative perks provide points that can be used for other choices. An attractive bonus can therefore come with a separate disadvantage.

**Starting Perks are unavailable on a first run in the Demo.** If that selection is locked, begin playing rather than assuming a shop upgrade or missing item will enable it immediately.

A few choices deserve particular attention:

**Bad Lease:** Raises the starting rent. This is especially punishing in a business that needs expensive equipment before generating regular income.

**Known Snitch:** Prevents customers appearing on the Wanted poster from visiting and starts the character with reduced standing toward the Revolution and Black Market factions. That can eliminate encounters you might otherwise want.

**Convict and other reputation-affecting choices:** Consider how the starting relationship changes the services or opportunities you expect to use. A cheaper or more convenient early setup is not necessarily the best fit for every trading strategy.

**Addiction-related drawbacks:** These create recurring pressures rather than a harmless one-time penalty. Account for the consequences across several business days.

For your first specialized run, a straightforward perk selection is easier to manage than stacking several penalties. A water business already has equipment and energy needs; a rat farm already needs food and space. Additional disadvantages can make it harder to tell which part of the operation is causing the trouble.

<section id="appraisal">

## How Do You Tell Whether a Customer Is Selling Counterfeit Goods?

Some merchandise requires more than checking its listed price. A customer can bring items with counterfeit markings, misleading information, or contents that don't match their label.

For inspectable goods, use the **Magnifying Glass** to examine the item before agreeing to buy it.

Look for a specific visible fault rather than treating a suspiciously low price as proof of a counterfeit. Cigarette packaging, for example, can require checking details such as seals and other identifying markings.

When the inspection interface identifies a fault you can select, use that information before choosing **Confront**.

A correct confrontation can force the seller to reconsider their offer. An incorrect accusation can cost reputation and cause the customer to leave, so a doubtful item is not always worth challenging.

### Should you buy an item that might be counterfeit?

Decide based on what you have actually identified.

A genuine item offered cheaply may be useful stock. A counterfeit has different resale implications, especially when a buyer expects the genuine version.

Do not assume that every suspicious item must be purchased and fixed later. When you cannot identify the item confidently, declining the trade preserves both money and space.

A water container's label may not match its actual purity. [Scan the contents and correct the label](/probably-stolen/water-purification) before selling it as a higher-grade product. A label alone doesn't prove that the water inside is genuine High-Quality or Pure Water.

<section id="security">

## How Do You Avoid Security Trouble While Trading?

Security risks become more important as the shop begins handling valuable goods, suspicious merchandise, and customers connected to other factions.

A legal trading business can generally avoid some of these problems by separating ordinary merchandise from stolen or restricted goods.

Before putting an item up for sale, check whether it carries a stolen or contraband status. The distinction matters because Security can respond to what the shop is visibly displaying or handling.

A few precautions are especially useful:

- Keep contraband out of the Display Case.
- Check suspicious merchandise before agreeing to a trade.
- Avoid leaving questionable goods exposed when a Security inspection is approaching.
- Read faction and reputation changes after significant encounters instead of assuming every successful transaction is harmless.

The Wanted poster and characters affected by Augmentation introduce another set of encounters. These are not ordinary item-quality checks; they can affect the shopkeeper's standing and the outcome of the run.

On a new character, learn how normal customer trading works before deliberately taking on additional security risks. A profitable shop can still fail for reasons unrelated to cash.

</section>

<section id="after-closing">

## What Should You Do After Closing the Shop?

Closing the shutter ends the trading session, but it doesn't make every business problem wait until the next customer arrives.

Use the time before the next day to prepare the things that cannot be fixed during a busy sale.

**Organize merchandise.** Move newly purchased goods out of the counter area, separate finished products from inputs, and make sure valuable stock is easy to find.

**Check powered equipment.** Machines that work overnight need enough energy for their next cycle. A container placed in a machine does not guarantee a completed product if the equipment cannot operate.

**Check living stock.** Rats need appropriate food, usable enclosures, and attention to health and crowding. A colony that was manageable before closing can look quite different after another round of births.

**Prepare tomorrow's customers.** Reconsider what the Display Case is advertising. A day spent buying supplies may need a different customer mix from a day intended to sell finished merchandise.

**Consider scavenging when supplies are scarce.** The Dumping Grounds can provide useful materials, but carrying capacity and injury risk matter. Bring enough room for what you intend to collect, and don't ignore wounds that could interfere with later trips.

There is little benefit to scavenging or buying more items when storage is already so full that the shop cannot handle its existing stock.

</section>

<section id="shop-buyout">

## Can You Buy the Shop in Probably Stolen?

In the Demo, **you cannot purchase the shop from the landlord**. The landlord continues collecting rent even when your inventory and property value become substantial.

Reaching a high property value or surviving for many days does not unlock a landlord buyout in the Demo. Rent remains payable regardless of the shop's accumulated inventory.

The full **1.0 release, scheduled for October 28, 2026**, adds an ending in which the shop can be purchased and owned. It also adds more starting scenarios, expanded difficulty settings, and Vending Machines capable of making sales overnight.

The exact requirements for completing that purchase should be checked against the available options in the full game rather than assuming that the Demo's property value alone is sufficient.

Demo saves are intended to be compatible with the full release, although starting a fresh game is recommended to avoid possible compatibility problems.

Until you can actually purchase the property, continuing to cover rent remains a central survival requirement.

</section>
