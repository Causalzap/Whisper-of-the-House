<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
To make **Master Vintage Wine** in Probably Stolen, unlock the **Retired Winemaker**, inspect your BloomBerries with a **Magnifying Glass**, and prepare a Wine Bottle with approximately **750 mL of water**.

Two reliable recipes reach the required **6 Wine Score**:

- **2 Small BloomBerries + Basewater + Yeast**
- **1 Small BloomBerry + 1 Normal BloomBerry + High-Quality Water + Yeast**

Press **Start Fermenting** and wait two nights with ordinary Yeast, or one night with Super Yeast. Wine quality is determined when fermentation starts. Aging increases its value but cannot turn a lower-grade bottle into Master Vintage.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

A bottle of wine can finish fermenting successfully and still be worth much less than expected.

The difference is usually decided before the first night: one underripe BloomBerry, the wrong water grade, or missing Yeast can push a batch below the Master Vintage threshold.

There is also a second decision once you understand the recipes. Combining your best berries into one expensive bottle is not always the most profitable use of your inventory. When Wine Bottles, water, Yeast, customer budgets, and rent are involved, producing several cheaper bottles can sometimes leave you with more Credits.

## How Do You Unlock Wine Making in Probably Stolen?

You do not need a dedicated starting scenario to begin making wine.

Open **Joe Wild's Network** and purchase the **Retired Winemaker** unlock for **200 Credits**.

The starter supplies include:

- Three Wine Bottles.
- Three ordinary Yeast portions.
- Two BloomBerries.
- A Water Ration.
- A Magnifying Glass.
- The BloomBerry Wine Fermenting Guide.

The Retired Winemaker also becomes available as a recurring supplier of Wine Bottles, Yeast, water, and BloomBerries.

You can begin with the supplied equipment. No powered fermentation machine is required for ordinary Wine Bottles.

### What should you inspect before making your first batch?

Open the BloomBerry Wine Fermenting Guide and read through its instructions.

Pay attention to three requirements:

1. A Wine Bottle needs approximately **750 mL of liquid**.
2. A regular batch needs at least one BloomBerry.
3. Yeast is optional, but it improves the score and reduces fermentation time.

The important distinction is between making any drinkable wine and making high-quality wine.

If your starter berries are not suitable for Master Vintage, you can still make a first batch to learn the process. Alternatively, keep the better ingredients until you have the combination needed for a stronger bottle.

Do not spend all your starting Credits purchasing additional equipment before checking your next rent payment.

## How Do You Make Your First Bottle of Wine?

Wine fermentation happens inside the **Wine Bottle** itself.

You do not need to install the bottle in an AgeWell or supply Energy for the ordinary fermentation process.

![The in-game BloomBerry Wine Fermenting Guide showing 750 mL of water, one or two BloomBerries, and optional Yeast.](/images/probably-stolen/wine-making-fermenting-guide.webp)

Follow this order:

1. Place an empty Wine Bottle somewhere accessible.
2. Inspect the BloomBerries you intend to use.
3. Add one or two BloomBerries to the bottle's ingredient slots.
4. Add ordinary Yeast if available.
5. Fill the bottle with approximately **750 mL of water**.
6. Open the Wine Bottle and select **Start Fermenting**.
7. Allow the required nights to pass.
8. Inspect the completed wine before deciding whether to sell or age it.

A standard Water Ration can provide the required **750 mL**. Two Small Water Rations of 375 mL each can also fill a bottle.

The start check accepts a nearly full bottle, but filling it completely is the simplest way to avoid the insufficient-water message.

![A Wine Bottle showing fermentation in progress with two days remaining until completion.](/images/probably-stolen/wine-making-fermentation-progress.webp)

### Should you add one or two BloomBerries?

One BloomBerry can start an ordinary batch.

However, using at least two provides an additional **+1 Wine Score**, separate from the points contributed by the berries themselves.

That bonus is important when aiming for Reserve or Master Vintage.

For a beginner, two inspected berries and one ordinary Yeast portion make a predictable recipe.

A bottle can hold up to three berry portions without Yeast, but using two berries and Yeast is usually the simpler way to reach a high score while keeping fermentation short.

### Can you change the ingredients after fermentation starts?

No. Treat **Start Fermenting** as the point where the recipe becomes final.

Once fermentation begins, the game determines the wine's quality from the selected ingredients and water. Waiting longer will not correct the recipe.

If you have valuable berries, inspect and arrange them before pressing Start rather than hoping a low-grade batch will improve later.

<section id="bloomberry-quality">

## How Do You Identify the Best BloomBerries for Wine?

Use the **Magnifying Glass** to inspect each BloomBerry before putting it into a Wine Bottle.

![A BloomBerry enlarged with a Magnifying Glass so its visible condition can be checked before making wine.](/images/probably-stolen/wine-making-bloomberry-quality.webp)

Berries that look similar in your inventory can have different conditions and contribute different amounts to the wine score.

<GuideTable columns={wineBerryColumns} rows={wineBerryRows} />

### Small vs Normal BloomBerries: which is better?

**Small BloomBerries are the best individual ingredient for high-quality wine.**

A healthy Small BloomBerry contributes **+2**, compared with **+1** from a healthy Normal BloomBerry.

Compare the overall size and fullness of the berry cluster rather than relying on the stem alone. Both Small and Normal BloomBerries can have visible stems.

Small BloomBerries have a more compact appearance, while Normal BloomBerries form a fuller bunch. Use the Magnifying Glass to inspect uncertain berries before assigning them to a recipe.

If you cannot confidently tell the two apart, inspect them side by side rather than using them immediately.

### Are underripe and overripe berries useless?

Not necessarily.

An Underripe or Overripe BloomBerry contributes **0 points**, so either is weaker than a healthy Normal or Small berry.

However, two such berries still qualify for the extra **+1 bonus** from using multiple berry portions.

With good water and Yeast, they can produce lower-grade wine that still has some sale value.

This can be useful when you have spare bottles and are trying to convert imperfect ingredients into merchandise.

Do not sacrifice a Small BloomBerry merely to improve an otherwise poor bottle unless the resulting quality increase is worth the extra ingredient.

### What should you do with Moldy BloomBerries?

A Moldy BloomBerry contributes **-1 point**.

It can reduce a promising recipe to a much lower grade, especially when combined with weak water or no Yeast.

Inspect suspicious gray, fuzzy fruit carefully.

If you have a better ingredient available, save the bottle for that ingredient rather than filling an expensive recipe with moldy fruit.

<section id="wine-quality-score">

## How Does Wine Quality Scoring Work?

Probably Stolen calculates a hidden Wine Score from the ingredients in the bottle.

For ordinary BloomBerry Wine, the calculation is:

**Wine Score = Berry Points + Multiple-Berry Bonus + Yeast Bonus + Water Score**

Each part has a different role.

**Berry Points:** Add the value of every Berry portion. Small contributes +2, Normal +1, Underripe or Overripe 0, and Moldy -1.

**Multiple-Berry Bonus:** Add +1 when the recipe contains at least two Berry portions.

**Yeast Bonus:** Add +1 when ordinary compatible Yeast is included. Stronger Yeast does not provide a larger quality bonus.

**Water Score:** Add or subtract points according to the water's grade.

### Which water should you use for wine?

<GuideTable columns={wineWaterColumns} rows={wineWaterRows} />

**High-Quality Water and Pure Water give the same +1 scoring bonus.**

This means Pure Water does not produce a higher wine tier simply because its purity is greater.

Basewater gives no bonus, but it does not reduce the score either.

That makes Basewater perfectly usable when your berries and Yeast already provide enough points for Master Vintage.

Ghostwater, Rustwater, and Gutterflow reduce the score. Avoid them for expensive ordinary wine batches unless you have calculated the final result.

If you need to produce High-Quality Water reliably, use the [Water Purification Guide](/probably-stolen/water-purification).

### What score produces each wine grade?

<GuideTable columns={wineGradeColumns} rows={wineGradeRows} />

These are the initial item values before aging, customer offers, negotiation, and other trading modifiers.

A score of **6 or higher** produces Master Vintage.

A score of **5** produces Reserve.

Scores of **3 or 4** produce Production Wine.

A higher score within the same grade does not create a new, more expensive tier.

For example, a score of 7 does not unlock a grade above Master Vintage.

### Does wine quality improve while fermenting?

No.

The game determines the grade when fermentation starts.

The fermentation time controls when the finished product becomes available; it does not provide extra quality points.

Once the bottle finishes, later aging increases its value, not its original quality tier.

If you want Master Vintage, the important moment is **before you press Start Fermenting**, not after the wine is ready.

</section>

<section id="master-vintage-recipes">

## What Are the Best Master Vintage Wine Recipes?

There are several ingredient combinations that reach the required six points.

The best recipe depends on whether your limited resource is high-quality water, Small BloomBerries, ordinary Yeast, or empty Wine Bottles.

![A completed Master Vintage BloomBerry Wine bottle showing an estimated value of 186 Credits after two days of aging.](/images/probably-stolen/wine-making-master-vintage.webp)

### Recipe 1: Two Small BloomBerries with Basewater

Use:

- **2 Small BloomBerries**
- **750 mL Basewater**
- **1 ordinary Yeast**

The score is:

| Ingredient | Points |
|---|---:|
| First Small BloomBerry | +2 |
| Second Small BloomBerry | +2 |
| Two-Berry Bonus | +1 |
| Basewater | 0 |
| Yeast | +1 |
| **Total** | **6** |

**Result: Master Vintage.**

This is a particularly useful recipe when you have excellent fruit but do not want to spend additional time or equipment processing water to a higher grade.

High-Quality Water would add another point, but the finished product would still be Master Vintage.

Save your cleaner water for another recipe if it would be more valuable there.

### Recipe 2: Small + Normal BloomBerry with High-Quality Water

Use:

- **1 Small BloomBerry**
- **1 Normal BloomBerry**
- **750 mL High-Quality Water**
- **1 ordinary Yeast**

The score is:

| Ingredient | Points |
|---|---:|
| Small BloomBerry | +2 |
| Normal BloomBerry | +1 |
| Two-Berry Bonus | +1 |
| High-Quality Water | +1 |
| Yeast | +1 |
| **Total** | **6** |

**Result: Master Vintage.**

This recipe is useful when Small BloomBerries are scarce but your water production already reaches High-Quality.

It uses only one Small Berry, leaving another for a future high-grade batch.

Using Pure Water instead produces the same grade.

### Recipe 3: Two Normal BloomBerries with High-Quality Water

Use:

- **2 Normal BloomBerries**
- **750 mL High-Quality Water**
- **1 ordinary Yeast**

The score is:

**1 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 1 = 5**

**Result: Reserve.**

This is still valuable wine, but it does not reach the Master Vintage threshold.

If you have one Small BloomBerry available, replacing one Normal Berry raises the score from five to six.

That single substitution is more useful for improving the wine grade than changing High-Quality Water to Pure Water.

### Recipe 4: Two Normal BloomBerries with Basewater

Use:

- **2 Normal BloomBerries**
- **750 mL Basewater**
- **1 ordinary Yeast**

The score is:

**1 + 1 + 1 + 0 + 1 = 4**

**Result: Production Wine.**

This is a reasonable fallback when you need saleable wine and cannot obtain better berries or water.

If you want Reserve, improve the water by one point.

If you want Master Vintage, improve the berry combination or make another change that raises the score to at least six.

### Why did your Master Vintage recipe produce Reserve instead?

Check whether the water or one Berry contributed fewer points than expected.

For example:

**Small + Normal + Basewater + Yeast = 5 points.**

That is Reserve, not Master Vintage.

The same berries with High-Quality Water reach six.

Likewise, two Small Berries without Yeast and with Basewater reach only five points:

**2 + 2 + 1 = 5.**

The missing Yeast bonus is enough to prevent Master Vintage.

When a batch finishes one grade below your goal, recalculate the individual components rather than replacing all your equipment.

<section id="yeast-and-fermentation">

## Which Yeast Should You Use, and How Long Does Wine Take?

Yeast affects the wine's quality score and fermentation speed.

<GuideTable columns={wineYeastColumns} rows={wineYeastRows} />

For normal wine production, ordinary Yeast is often enough.

It provides the full **+1 score bonus** and reduces the fermentation time from four nights to two.

### Is Super Yeast better for Master Vintage?

Super Yeast is faster, not stronger in Wine Score.

It can finish fermentation in one night, but it provides the same +1 quality point as ordinary Yeast.

Use it when:

- Rent is close and you need saleable wine sooner.
- You want to free up storage space.
- Your next customer opportunity arrives before a normal two-night batch would finish.

If you have plenty of time before your next sale, ordinary Yeast can produce the same Master Vintage grade.

### Is the Yeast Bioreactor worth buying?

The **Yeast Bioreactor** is useful for repeated wine production because it can be reused.

It contributes the ordinary Yeast bonus and gives the normal two-night fermentation time.

Its advantage is that it returns for another batch instead of being permanently consumed.

In one established wine business, buying the Bioreactor required enough future bottles to justify the upfront cost. It was not a useful first purchase for a shop still struggling to afford water and empty Wine Bottles.

A good purchase order is:

1. Obtain enough bottles and ingredients for reliable production.
2. Confirm you can sell the finished wine.
3. Check how much ordinary Yeast you consume each week.
4. Buy the Bioreactor when repeated Yeast purchases become a meaningful expense.

The Bioreactor is returned when fermentation starts, not when the wine finishes.

![Yeast Bioreactor tooltip explaining that it returns to inventory when fermentation starts instead of being consumed.](/images/probably-stolen/wine-making-yeast-bioreactor.webp)

Keep inventory space available so the returned item has somewhere to go.

### What does Synthetic Yeast make?

**Synthetic Yeast is not a stronger version of ordinary Yeast.**

It produces **GloomBerry Wine**, a separate counterfeit product.

GloomBerry Wine:

- Can be produced without ordinary BloomBerries.
- Is treated as contraband.
- Has a fixed low-value product outcome rather than ordinary Master Vintage scoring.
- Cannot gain value through normal wine aging.

Do not use Synthetic Yeast when trying to improve genuine BloomBerry Wine.

It creates a different business and legal-risk decision. If you are selling contraband, check the consequences in the [Security Investigations Guide](/probably-stolen/security-investigation).

</section>

## Why Is Your Wine Not Fermenting or Coming Out Low Quality?

First decide whether the problem is preventing fermentation or lowering the quality of a completed batch.

<GuideTable columns={wineProblemColumns} rows={wineProblemRows} />

### The Wine Bottle says Not Enough Water

A standard batch needs approximately **750 mL**.

Check the actual liquid volume inside the Wine Bottle rather than assuming the container you poured from was full.

A partially used Water Ration may not supply enough liquid.

If you are filling several bottles, reserve 750 mL for each planned batch before using the rest of your water for sales.

In one shop, several bottles had already been loaded with berries and Yeast, but fermentation could not begin because the player had sold most of the available water.

The problem was not a missing machine or a defective recipe. It was a shortage of liquid.

### The Wine Bottle says Not Enough Ingredients

Check that the bottle contains a valid Berry ingredient for ordinary wine.

Water alone does not start a normal BloomBerry batch.

Ordinary Yeast improves an eligible Berry recipe, but it does not replace the Berries.

Synthetic Yeast uses a different GloomBerry process.

### Your wine is only Production Wine

Production Wine is not necessarily a failure.

If you used two Normal Berries with Basewater and ordinary Yeast, the expected score is four.

That recipe cannot produce Master Vintage without improving the ingredients.

Check the actual berry conditions before assuming the fermenting process caused the result.

If you are close to the next grade, improving one scoring component is often enough.

### Your wine is Reserve, not Master Vintage

A five-point bottle is only one point away.

Common fixes for the next batch include:

- Replace one Normal BloomBerry with a Small one.
- Use High-Quality Water instead of Basewater.
- Add ordinary Yeast if the previous bottle had none.

You do not need to make all three changes.

Choose whichever fixes the missing point at the lowest cost.

### The wine has finished but will not become Master Vintage

Aging cannot change a finished bottle's grade.

If the bottle is Reserve, it remains Reserve even after many nights.

Selling it, aging it for additional value, or saving its contents for another use are separate decisions.

For Master Vintage, prepare a new six-point recipe.

### Your bottles are fermenting too slowly

Check which Yeast type was installed when the batch started.

Without Yeast, a normal bottle takes four nights.

With ordinary Yeast, it takes two.

With Super Yeast, it can finish in one.

The Wine Bottle handles fermentation without a separate power source. Adding Energy modules to unrelated equipment will not speed up an ordinary bottle.

### You have plenty of berries but cannot start more wine

Count your empty Wine Bottles.

A reliable BloomBerry supply does not automatically create more fermentation capacity.

If you are waiting for the Retired Winemaker, check whether other customers can provide bottles or whether you have suitable finished bottles whose contents you no longer need.

Do not buy more berries than you can use before the next supply opportunity.

## How Do You Age Wine, and Is AgeWell Worth It?

Once ordinary BloomBerry Wine finishes fermenting, it begins gaining value as it ages.

The quality grade stays fixed, but the bottle becomes more valuable over time.

This creates a choice between selling immediately and holding inventory for a higher future value.

### How much value does ordinary wine aging add?

A finished wine bottle gains **one age step each night**.

Each age step adds **3 Credits to its internal base value**, before the wine's quality modifier is applied.

The initial base value is 100 Credits.

For example, before other trading effects:

- A new Master Vintage bottle starts at 175 Credits.
- After 10 ordinary age steps, its internal base value rises from 100 to 130.
- Applying the Master Vintage quality modifier gives a value of approximately 228 Credits.

Actual customer offers may differ because trading conditions and buyer budgets affect what you can receive.

The important point is that aging increases the underlying value, while the Master Vintage grade remains unchanged.

### What does AgeWell do?

The **AgeWell** is a powered machine that accelerates the aging of finished wine.

It can hold up to **six bottles** and requires **24 Energy per day** at the displayed base configuration.

Each completed powered cycle adds one extra day of aging to eligible bottles, on top of their normal overnight aging.

That effectively doubles the aging speed without changing the wine's original quality grade.

![An AgeWell holding six wine bottles, requiring 24 Energy per day, and adding an extra day of aging during each powered cycle.](/images/probably-stolen/wine-making-agewell.webp)

To use it:

1. Obtain an AgeWell from Dr. Jackson when available.
2. Provide a compatible power source.
3. Insert completed, genuine wine bottles.
4. Leave enough Energy for the next overnight cycle.
5. Inspect the bottles after processing to check their increased age and value.

An AgeWell cannot turn unfinished wine into finished wine.

It also cannot turn Production Wine into Master Vintage.

### Should you buy AgeWell before improving your recipes?

Usually not.

Improving a recipe from Production Wine to Master Vintage changes its initial value substantially.

AgeWell increases the value of bottles you already have, but it requires an equipment investment, Energy, and storage space.

For a small operation, prioritize:

1. Reliable Master Vintage or Reserve recipes.
2. Enough Wine Bottles to use your available berries.
3. Enough water and Yeast to avoid missed fermentation cycles.
4. A dependable way to sell finished stock.
5. AgeWell when you have valuable bottles worth holding.

The more high-grade bottles you can keep inside AgeWell, the better use you make of each powered night.

### When should you sell instead of aging?

Check your next rent payment before holding a bottle for another night.

If you need the Credits today, a buyer's current offer may be more useful than the wine's possible future value.

If rent is already covered and you have spare storage, holding Master Vintage can be attractive.

However, inventory appreciation is not cash income until someone buys the product.

Do not buy another expensive AgeWell while your existing finished bottles are waiting for suitable customers.

</section>

<section id="bloomberry-supply">

## Where Do You Get More BloomBerries, Yeast, and Wine Bottles?

A working wine business needs three renewable supplies:

- BloomBerries of acceptable quality.
- Enough water and Yeast to ferment them.
- Empty Wine Bottles to hold new batches.

The Retired Winemaker is a convenient recurring source of berries and equipment.

Other customers can also offer useful supplies, but you cannot assume they will bring the same items every day.

### Should you grow your own BloomBerries?

Growing BloomBerries gives you a more predictable ingredient supply than waiting for merchants.

BloomBerry plants are **Perennial**, so a healthy plant can produce repeated harvests without replacing the seed after every cycle. However, not every harvested berry will be Small or suitable for Master Vintage.

If you want to maintain your own supply, check the [Hydroponics Guide](/probably-stolen/hydroponics) for the required Minerals, growing water, Growth Score, and repeated harvest cycle.

Inspect the harvested berries with a Magnifying Glass before assigning them to Wine Bottles.

### Is one Hydroponic System enough for wine production?

Start with one productive BloomBerry plant before purchasing several machines.

Watch which resource becomes scarce first.

If your storage fills with berries while you are waiting for Wine Bottles, another Hydroponic System will not increase the number of bottles you can ferment.

If you consistently have empty bottles and Yeast available but run out of berries, growing capacity may be the bottleneck.

If you have both berries and bottles but keep postponing fermentation because your water is sold out, protect a dedicated water reserve.

<section id="wine-profit">

## Should You Combine or Split BloomBerries for Wine?

A Master Vintage bottle has a higher individual value than Production Wine, but combining your best berries is not always the best use of limited ingredients.

Before starting fermentation, compare how many bottles you can make and which wine grades those ingredients will produce.

### Example: One Master Vintage or Two Production Wines?

Suppose you have:

- One Small BloomBerry.
- One Normal BloomBerry.
- High-Quality Water.
- Ordinary Yeast.
- Enough empty Wine Bottles for either plan.

**Option A: Make one Master Vintage.**

Combine the Small and Normal berries in one bottle with High-Quality Water and Yeast.

The score is six.

The result has an initial value of **175 Credits** before other trading modifiers.

**Option B: Make two Production Wines.**

Use the Small Berry in one bottle and the Normal Berry in another.

Each bottle receives High-Quality Water and Yeast.

The Small Berry bottle scores four.

The Normal Berry bottle scores three.

Both produce Production Wine, with an initial value of **125 Credits each**.

Together, the two bottles have an initial value of **250 Credits** before additional costs and trading modifiers.

That is **75 Credits more in total initial product value** than the single Master Vintage bottle.

But it also requires:

- One additional Wine Bottle.
- Another Yeast portion unless you have a reusable Bioreactor.
- Another 750 mL of suitable water.
- Enough time and inventory space for two bottles.
- Buyers willing and able to purchase both.

This is why a single high-grade bottle is not automatically the best financial decision.

### When should you make Master Vintage?

Prioritize Master Vintage when:

- Wine Bottles are scarce.
- You have enough good berries but limited storage.
- You want to attract buyers for higher-value Luxury Items.
- You intend to age valuable bottles.
- Your current customer opportunities favor expensive merchandise.

Combining berries makes more sense when it preserves scarce bottles or produces stock that is easier to sell to your target customers.

### When should you split the berries into more bottles?

Consider multiple lower-grade bottles when:

- You have spare empty Wine Bottles.
- Your water supply is reliable.
- Ordinary Yeast is inexpensive or reusable.
- You can sell several bottles before the next rent payment.
- The total additional selling value exceeds the extra materials and waiting costs.

The two-bottle option produces more total initial item value, but also consumes another bottle, another 750 mL of water, and additional Yeast.

To determine whether that extra output is actually profitable after ingredient costs and rent, compare both plans in the [Money-Making Planner](/probably-stolen/money-making).

### How Do You Find Buyers for Expensive Wine?

High-grade wine is more valuable when your shop attracts customers who can afford Luxury Items.

Displaying Master Vintage can help attract buyers with larger budgets. If you also have Red Imp Beer, selling both during the same trading period can provide more opportunities to move alcohol inventory.

Choose your display stock according to what you actually need to sell. For the full customer-attraction mechanics, see [How the Display Case Attracts Buyers](/probably-stolen#display-case).

## What Should You Do After Making Your First Master Vintage?

Once you can produce Master Vintage reliably, identify what is preventing you from making or selling the next batch.

If you have plenty of berries but few empty bottles, prioritize Wine Bottle supply rather than expanding your farm. If bottles are available but good berries are scarce, grow more BloomBerries or save Small berries for recipes that need them.

If finished wine is accumulating, decide which bottles should be sold immediately and which are worth aging in an AgeWell.

Before expanding further, compare the additional production costs and expected sale dates in the [Money-Making Planner](/probably-stolen/money-making). A larger wine inventory is only useful when your shop can turn it into Credits.
