Open the customer's **Custom Gun Order**, identify every **Required** condition, and enter your firearm's current attributes into the checker below. Raise any missing required stat before pursuing Bonus rewards, then offer the completed gun with the correct Weapon Permit active.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

A Class I customer wants an accurate gun. Your Nestfire Compact shows **17 Firepower, 25 Accuracy, and 25 Reliability**. Three bonus conditions are already satisfied, yet the customer still cannot accept it.

The reason is one point of Firepower. The order requires **Firepower greater than 17**, not 17 or higher.

That distinction changes how you should approach Gunsmith. A firearm with impressive Accuracy can fail a simple contract, while a cheaper gun with the right attachments can qualify and earn several additional rewards. The valuable work happens before the sale: choosing a compatible firearm, meeting its mandatory properties, and deciding whether another modification earns more than it costs.

## Will Your Gun Complete the Custom Order?

<GunsmithOrderChecker />

Choose the customer's permit class and the request that matches the trade screen. Enter the **current displayed attributes** from the gun's inventory tooltip, not the base attributes shown in parentheses.

For a normal gun sale, the checker separates three outcomes:

- **Required conditions:** Every one must pass before the firearm qualifies.
- **Bonus conditions:** Each completed threshold adds its stated reward. Multiple thresholds for the same attribute can all pay.
- **Estimated payment:** The listed base range and qualifying bonuses are combined. Your actual quote may change with difficulty and weapon-market events.

Select **Improve the customer's gun** when the visitor brings an existing firearm instead. That request checks the improvement from its starting attributes, not whether the finished weapon reaches a fixed absolute Firepower or Accuracy value.

<section id="first-custom-order">

## How Do You Complete Your First Gunsmith Order?

The Gunsmith starting scenario provides a Class I Weapon Permit, firearm inventory, modification stock, a Gunsmith Storage Bay, and basic 3D-printing equipment. It also schedules a Class I gun buyer on **Day 1 and Day 4**, giving you an early opportunity to learn the order interface.

A customer may arrive asking for something accurate, more powerful, better handling, or a particular caliber. These are actual weapon contracts rather than ordinary purchases from the Display Case.

![A Class I Custom Gun Order requiring Firepower above 17, with additional Accuracy and Reliability bonuses worth 46 Credits each.](/images/probably-stolen/gunsmith-class-i-custom-order.webp)

When the order opens, work through this sequence:

1. **Read the entire request before selecting a firearm.** Note the buyer's permit class, every Required stat, any caliber restriction, and the Bonus conditions. A large bonus is irrelevant if the mandatory requirements cannot be met.
2. **Choose an eligible gun from your back inventory.** Check its current condition, caliber, and equipped attachments. A similar-looking weapon is not necessarily compatible with the customer's exact request.
3. **Fit modifications in the relevant slots.** Check the firearm's updated attributes after each change. Parts can improve one requirement while reducing another.
4. **Recheck every Required condition.** Numerical targets must be beaten strictly. A value equal to the listed requirement is still insufficient.
5. **Offer the finished gun and inspect the quoted reward.** Customers with custom gun contracts do not use ordinary haggling. Keep the completed firearm available until the transaction is confirmed.

The first expensive mistake is modifying the wrong firearm. Class II and Class III buyers can require attributes that basic Class I equipment cannot realistically reach. Check the contract before committing a valuable suppressor, specialist magazine, or metal-printed component.

There is also no reason to maximize every attribute. A gun that already satisfies the request can be profitable without receiving all the optional bonuses. Whether further modifications are worthwhile depends on their cost and the next reward threshold.

</section>

<section id="required-bonus-stats">

## Why Does Your Custom Gun Order Fail Even When the Stats Look High Enough?

**Required** and **Bonus** are separate conditions. Missing a single Required value prevents the order from qualifying; missing a Bonus only removes the corresponding additional payment.

The Class I Accurate Gun request is a useful example:

- **Required:** Firepower greater than 17.
- **Bonus:** Accuracy greater than 20, 24, and 30, each worth 46 Credits.
- **Bonus:** Reliability greater than 24, worth another 46 Credits.
- **Base reward:** 86–96 Credits before applicable modifiers.

With **17 Firepower, 25 Accuracy, and 25 Reliability**, the Nestfire Compact already satisfies three bonus conditions worth **138 Credits**. The sale is still blocked because Firepower must be greater than 17.

Increasing Firepower to **18** makes the gun eligible for a reference payment of **224–234 Credits**, provided its other attributes remain unchanged. The next Accuracy bonus requires 31, so an upgrade from 25 to 26 adds nothing to this order. Reaching 31 would earn another 46 Credits.

A different Class I customer may instead ask for serious firepower. In this request, the same **17 Firepower** clears the mandatory **Firepower > 11** requirement and earns the **> 14** bonus, but falls short of the additional bonuses at **> 17**, **> 20**, and **> 22**.

![A Class I gun order showing Firepower 17 meeting the base requirement but missing higher bonus thresholds while fitting a .22 Magazine to the Nestfire Compact.](/images/probably-stolen/gunsmith-firepower-bonus-magazine.webp)

This gun can already complete the second order. Installing a .22 Magazine is worthwhile only if the resulting Firepower crosses another bonus threshold and the added payment justifies the part's cost.

### Why do numbers in parentheses not count?

A firearm tooltip can display an adjusted attribute beside a parenthesized base value. The order checks the firearm's actual current properties, which depend on its condition and equipped modifications.

If the current Firepower reads 17 and the parenthesized value is 20, the gun still fails **Firepower > 17**. Enter 17 into the checker.

This is particularly important for damaged guns. Buying a weapon with promising base attributes does not guarantee it can satisfy a higher-class order in its current condition.

### Do multiple Bonus thresholds stack?

Yes. When a gun has Accuracy 31, it satisfies **Accuracy > 20**, **> 24**, and **> 30**. All three rewards apply.

The same principle works for Style, Firepower, Reliability, and other attributes when an order has several bonus levels. The checker adds each satisfied entry instead of awarding only the highest tier.

A proper **Suppressor** can also satisfy both the general suppressor condition and the separate proper-suppressor bonus on orders that list both.

That creates potentially valuable upgrades, but only after the mandatory conditions are secure.

<section id="weapon-permits">

## How Do You Unlock Class I, II, and III Weapon Permits?

Weapon Permits determine which firearm classes you can legally trade and which custom gun buyers you can attract.

The Gunsmith start includes Class I. Other starts can obtain the Gunsmith-related network projects through **Joe Wild's Network**. The **Retired Gunsmith** project costs a base **650 Credits** and provides a starter kit plus access to the visiting specialist, who subsequently returns weekly.

Higher Weapon Permits are separate network purchases with Security reputation requirements. Buying the network project and receiving the actual permit are not identical steps.

<GuideTable columns={weaponPermitColumns} rows={weaponPermitRows} />

The prices above are the base network amounts before applicable difficulty and other purchasing modifiers. Class II requires the Class I project, and Class III requires Class II.

After purchasing the required project, reach the stated Security reputation. The permit is issued at a morning opening when its conditions are satisfied. **Place the issued Weapon Permit in the Display Case** to attract the corresponding gun buyer; purchasing it does not automatically fill the store with that buyer type.

### Why are gun buyers not visiting?

A Class I permit begins with a **50% daily arrival chance**, increasing to 75% and then 100% on successive days without a buyer. Class II begins at **40%**, progressing to 60%, 80%, and 100%. Class III begins at **35%**, then approximately 52%, 70%, 87%, and 100%.

These are the normal chances while the matching permit is displayed. Weapon-market events can alter them, and a gun buyer occupies one of the day's customer opportunities.

There are three checks worth making before spending money on additional equipment:

1. The corresponding **permit is physically in the Display Case**.
2. Your current **Security reputation** still supports the permit.
3. A gun buyer has not already appeared recently, resetting the increasing arrival chance.

The Firearm Neon Sign is useful for attracting customers who **sell guns to your shop**. It is not a substitute for a Weapon Permit when you want customers bringing custom gun orders.

### What happens if Security revokes a Weapon Permit?

The revocation thresholds are different from the thresholds required for issuance.

Class I is at risk below **0 Security reputation**, Class II below **+30**, and Class III below **+60**. Revocation can remove firearm authorization and return matching goods to Contraband status.

Recovering Security reputation can allow an already purchased permit project to issue a replacement at a later morning opening. The issuance threshold still matters: recovering to +35 may prevent further Class II revocation, but it is not enough to issue a missing Class II permit, which requires +50.

This distinction is important before spending on Class III stock. A weapon that was legal under an active permit can become an investigation risk after authorization is lost. For the consequences of an existing crime record, use the [Security Investigation Calculator](/probably-stolen/security-investigation); firearm modifications do not erase offenses already recorded.

<section id="weapon-modifications">

## Which Gun Mods Should You Install for Custom Orders?

A typical moddable firearm has slots for **Stock, Rail, Magazine, Underbarrel, Barrel**, and three **Special** modifications. Slots and compatibility still depend on the weapon. A Lancewell Revolver, for example, does not accept the ordinary modification setup.

The fastest way to complete an order is to identify the failing attribute and select a compatible part that improves it without dragging another mandatory stat below its target.

Gun condition also changes the result. The condition adjustment applied to base attributes is:

- New: **+30%**
- Excellent: **+15%**
- Good: **0%**
- Fair: **-15%**
- Poor: **-30%**

A modification can contribute an additional percentage of the gun's base stat and a fixed amount. The combined result is rounded down, so the actual displayed gain may differ from simply adding a percentage to the current tooltip number.

For example, a New Liberator 3D Printed Pistol with base Firepower 15 reaches **19 Firepower** from condition alone. Equipping a .22 Magazine with **+15% base Firepower and +5 Firepower** raises the calculated total to **26**.

Use the resulting **26**, not the original 15, when testing a custom order.

### Is a Suppressor worth using?

A real Suppressor can earn two rewards on orders that separately pay for a makeshift-or-proper suppressor and a proper Suppressor. For the Class I suppressed Accurate Gun order, those rewards are **+22 and +53 Credits**, or **75 Credits combined**.

The Makeshift Suppressor qualifies for the lower reward but can seriously damage other attributes. Its modifiers include **Style -50% -15**, **Reliability -35% -10**, and **Ergonomics -30% -9**, alongside a Recoil Control improvement.

That makes it a poor automatic choice when the same order requires Reliability, Style, or handling. A modification that earns 22 extra Credits is not helpful if it makes the firearm ineligible for the entire sale.

For the accurate-gun example, secure **Firepower > 17** first. Only then compare the suppressor reward against its acquisition cost and the effect on the gun's remaining attributes.

### Should you repair a gun before modifying it?

Gun Parts can attempt to improve a firearm in Poor, Fair, or Good condition by one condition tier, up to Excellent. Each attempt consumes the Gun Parts, even when it fails. The chance of success increases with repeated unsuccessful attempts.

Repairing a gun may increase several attributes at once, making it preferable to installing multiple expensive modifications on a badly damaged weapon. But the repair is not guaranteed, and a gun bought cheaply in Poor condition can consume more resources than its eventual contract reward justifies.

A Screwdriver can also dismantle a gun you own into recoverable materials. This may be more useful than keeping an incompatible firearm indefinitely when you need Gun Parts for another order.

</section>

<section id="upgrade-service">

## How Do You Complete an Upgrade Service Order When the Customer Brings Their Own Gun?

Not every Gunsmith customer wants to buy one of your firearms. Some arrive with a gun and request improvements to specific attributes.

This is an **Upgrade Service Order**. You receive the customer's firearm, install or use appropriate parts, and return it after achieving the required improvements. The important figures are the **change in each attribute**, not its absolute final value.

![A Lower Level Citizen requesting upgrades to their Liberator 3D Printed Pistol and offering to pay for the installed parts.](/images/probably-stolen/gunsmith-upgrade-service-order.webp)

For example, if a Class I customer requests **Firepower +8**, a firearm starting at Firepower 17 needs to reach at least **25**. A result of 24 does not qualify, even though 24 may satisfy a separate custom sale asking for Firepower greater than 17.

The three service classes work differently:

- **Class I:** Improve one required attribute by at least **8**. Raising a required attribute by 16 earns a 15-Credit bonus. Two other specified attributes can each earn 12 Credits for an improvement of 8.
- **Class II:** Improve two required attributes by at least **10 each**. Raising one required attribute by 20 earns 25 extra Credits. Two designated bonus attributes can each add 20 Credits for an increase of 10.
- **Class III:** Improve two required attributes by at least **12 each**. Raising one required attribute by 24 earns 40 extra Credits. Three designated bonus attributes can each add 32 Credits for an increase of 12.

Base service fees are **30–40 Credits for Class I**, **60–70 for Class II**, and **100–110 for Class III**. The customer also pays for the value of modifications and Gun Parts used on the firearm.

A Class I request to improve one attribute by 8 is therefore not interchangeable with a Class I gun sale requiring an attribute above a fixed number. Select the **Improve the customer's gun** option in the checker, choose the actual required and bonus attributes, then enter their before-and-after values.

### Why is the upgraded gun still rejected?

The most common failure is improving the wrong stat. A customer may request Recoil Control and Ergonomics while an attachment mainly increases Firepower. Even a large overall improvement cannot substitute for the named requirements.

Another failure is using a part that improves one requested stat but reduces another. Compare the entire before-and-after attribute list rather than judging the modification by its most prominent positive number.

Service orders are also a way to earn Credits without first buying an expensive firearm. The customer supplies the gun, and the parts you install are paid for at their value. However, reimbursement is not automatically profit: the parts still have an acquisition or production cost.

If a customer leaves before completing the service, your installed modifications and Gun Parts are returned to you. That reduces the risk of permanently losing those materials, but it does not compensate for the customer opportunity and preparation time you have spent.

</section>

<section id="3d-printing">

## How Do You Use the 3D Printer for Guns, Gun Parts, and Attachments?

The Gunsmith start includes basic printing equipment. A different starting scenario can obtain the printer through specialist stock or the Retired Gunsmith network upgrade.

A 3D Printer needs a compatible **blueprint chip**, the exact recipe materials, sufficient power, and any specialist modules required by the chosen blueprint. The chip is reusable: it is not consumed every time you complete a print.

The critical distinction is between ordinary attachments and metal-frame production. Many useful modifications need no special printing module, while a gun frame or Gun Parts job requires both **Metal Printing** and **CNC**.

<GuideTable columns={gunsmithPrintingColumns} rows={gunsmithPrintingRows} />

The table lists base recipe quantities and **work points**, not guaranteed calendar days.

At its normal starting speed, a printer completes **24 work per powered night** and consumes approximately **8 Energy per night**. The first effective cycle consumes the recipe inputs and begins progressing immediately. A 50-work pistol therefore takes **three effective powered cycles** at a constant speed of 24.

Printer condition, installed modules, and performance effects can change that schedule. Do not promise an order customer a finished gun tomorrow simply because the recipe is already loaded.

### Why does the printer say that Metal Printing is required?

Some chips cannot operate in a basic printer. A 10mm Magazine needs the Metal Printing Module, while Nestfire Compact Frames, Ironcraft 10 Frames, and Gun Parts additionally need the **Computer Numerical Control Module**.

![Computer Numerical Control Module showing -25% Performance and Efficiency, and the requirement to pair it with Metal Printing for gun frames and Gun Parts.](/images/probably-stolen/gunsmith-metal-cnc-printer-requirements.webp)

A Metal Printing Module by itself does not satisfy a frame chip's requirements. Install both modules before committing resources to frame production.

These modules also affect performance. The Metal Printing Module carries a **-50 Efficiency** modifier; the CNC Module applies **-25 Performance and -25 Efficiency**. Your printer may therefore take longer or consume more resources than an unmodified base-speed estimate suggests.

For a missing order attachment, check whether a normal chip already produces what you need. A **Red Dot Sight ×2** print costs **3 Scrap Metal and 1 Common Electronic Part**, with only 24 base work. That is a much smaller commitment than installing multiple expensive specialist modules solely to produce a new firearm frame.

### How do you turn a printed frame into a gun?

A printed frame is not a complete firearm. Combine a compatible **Nestfire Compact Frame** or **Ironcraft 10 Frame** with **one Gun Part** to assemble the corresponding weapon.

The printed frame creates a New-condition gun. Its resulting attributes depend on the frame's quality, which is influenced by the printer and the Metal Ingot used to produce it.

This is why collecting a high-purity ingot can matter more than simply obtaining the first available scrap metal. Gun Parts production provides different output counts by ingot grade:

- Very Low: **2 Gun Parts**
- Low or Fair: **3 Gun Parts**
- High or Very High: **4 Gun Parts**
- Perfect: **5 Gun Parts**

A higher-quality ingot can provide more Gun Parts from the same one-ingot recipe. For frames, it also affects the generated quality and the gun's final attributes.

The tradeoff is timing. Waiting several days for better metal can make an otherwise straightforward Class I order unprofitable when a completed firearm and suitable attachment are already available for purchase.

### Why is printing not progressing overnight?

Inspect the printer in this order:

1. **Chip and ingredients:** Every required input must be loaded in the correct amount. Having Scrap Metal elsewhere in the shop does not complete the machine's recipe.
2. **Specialist modules:** Frame and Gun Parts chips require Metal Printing and CNC together. Standard pistol and many attachment chips do not.
3. **Energy:** An outage or insufficient Energy pauses the work instead of automatically completing it the next morning.
4. **Work remaining:** A 60- or 70-work frame cannot finish in one ordinary 24-work cycle.
5. **Output space:** Leave enough room for the completed items. Do not begin a large batch assuming the printer can safely overflow into another container.

The printer consumes its recipe inputs when an effective production cycle begins. Losing power later delays the remaining work, so check the Energy Cell, required modules, and available work capacity before starting a batch with your last Metal Ingot or Filament.

<section id="gunsmith-profit">

## Are Custom Gun Orders More Profitable Than Selling Ordinary Guns?

The reward displayed on a Custom Gun Order is revenue, not profit. Before accepting the job, compare the expected payment with what you paid for the gun, the value of parts you must install, and any Gun Parts consumed during refurbishment.

A cheap damaged pistol can become an expensive order once repeated repair attempts and multiple modifications are included. A gun already close to its Required thresholds may earn less in bonuses but leave more Credits after the transaction.

Check the next required improvement before buying another attachment. If the gun already qualifies, the remaining decision is whether another bonus will pay enough to justify its cost. Money spent on printer upgrades or specialized equipment also needs to be recovered across later orders, not assumed to pay for itself immediately.

Difficulty and weapon-market events can also change the quoted price. On standard Mark Up difficulty, the applicable weapon-sale price modifier is **+15%**, while Price Gouge provides **+20%**. Thin Margins and Red Ink do not provide that positive difficulty modifier.

The checker shows a reference payment rather than pretending to predict every possible market quote. The trade screen's actual offer determines what you receive.

### Should you chase every Bonus reward?

A contract that already qualifies should be treated as a potential sale, not as a firearm that must be perfected at any cost.

For the Accurate Gun example, taking Accuracy from 25 to 31 adds another **46 Credits**. If reaching 31 requires a modification worth more than the extra payment, or damages another attribute enough to fail the order, it is not worthwhile solely for that bonus.

Conversely, a compatible part costing considerably less than 46 Credits may make sense if it produces that additional reward without damaging mandatory attributes.

An important exception involves reusable equipment. Buying a 3D Printer or specialist module to produce one attachment has a different cost from using a printer you already own. If future orders are uncertain, do not justify an expensive machine purchase by assuming it will immediately pay for itself.

For a wider comparison of available Credits, stock costs, and the next rent obligation, use the [Money-Making Planner](/probably-stolen/money-making). A profitable gun order is still a bad investment when its required equipment prevents you from paying rent.

### What does a "Best Gun" order pay?

The Best Gun request works differently from the fixed-attribute contracts.

A Class I buyer may request your best **.22 SR** firearm, while Class II and Class III buyers can request a **10mm** weapon. These orders pay **150% of the gun's market value**, rather than a fixed contract fee plus several stat bonuses.

That makes condition and market value especially relevant. Enter the firearm's actual market value in the checker instead of choosing an arbitrary price based on its Firepower.

The caliber must match. A powerful 10mm gun does not satisfy a request for a .22 SR weapon, regardless of how expensive it is.

<section id="gunsmith-extra-sales">

## Can You Sell Ammo, Melee Weapons, or Explosives After a Gun Order?

A successful firearm sale can lead to additional customer requests. The buyer may ask for ammunition matching the gun's caliber, a backup melee weapon, or munitions and explosives.

The normal chances are:

- **Matching ammunition:** 50%.
- **Melee weapon:** 40%.
- **Munitions or explosives:** 30%, for Class II and III buyers.

Completed gun-order bonuses increase these chances by **5 percentage points per qualifying bonus**. That makes a well-matched firearm useful beyond its initial payment.

Customer budgets still limit the value of the extra sale. Ammunition spending is approximately **one-third of the gun's value**. The melee-weapon budgets are **60 Credits for Class I**, **120 for Class II**, and **250 for Class III**. Munitions budgets are **150 Credits for Class II** and **350 for Class III**.

Stocking matching ammunition can be useful because the request is directly connected to the firearm you sold. Bringing an unrelated caliber does not satisfy that need.

Melee weapons provide a second opportunity, including some encounters where a buyer who did not purchase a firearm still asks whether you have a melee weapon available.

But extra sales are not guaranteed. Do not buy an expensive explosive or fill the counter with ammunition based on the assumption that every gun buyer will ask for more after the main order.

</section>

<section id="gunsmith-stuck">

## Why Are You Getting Upgrade Service Orders Instead of Gun Buyers?

Owning the correct Weapon Permit does not guarantee that the next customer will ask to buy a firearm. Each permit class has several possible requests, including orders to upgrade a weapon the customer already owns.

When you have **no firearms belonging to that permit class**, an Upgrade Service Order receives an additional **50% chance before the normal order selection**. This is why a shop displaying a Class II or III permit can repeatedly receive customers asking for modifications instead of buying stock.

That changes what you should prepare. A service customer supplies the weapon, so compatible attachments and Gun Parts can be more useful than holding an expensive firearm that no buyer has requested. Keep those materials available before the customer arrives, then compare the requested attribute improvements with what your parts can actually deliver.

There is also a restriction on Class III shotgun service orders: a **Rampart Dual-Action Shotgun** request does not select Firepower as an attribute to improve. Prepare modifications for the attributes actually requested rather than assuming every high-tier service job rewards additional Firepower.

</section>
