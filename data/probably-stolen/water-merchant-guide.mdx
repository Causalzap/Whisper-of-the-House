A full water jug is valuable only when someone can afford to buy it. In the early Water Merchant days, a shop may have enough water to trade but too few small bottles to complete affordable sales. Later, the same business can accumulate more High Quality Water than its storage and customer traffic can handle.

The starting Bottle Printer helps solve the packaging problem, while the Display Case influences who visits the counter. Moisture Farms and Water Purifiers eventually make production more dependable, but they add power requirements and equipment costs before the extra water has earned anything.

The most useful purchases follow the current bottleneck: containers when buyers cannot afford large jugs, storage when finished water has nowhere to go, or production equipment when the shop cannot replenish what customers are buying.

## What Do You Start With as a Water Merchant?

Water Merchant begins with water-testing and purification supplies, additional water storage, and a **Bottle Printer**.

In one **Demo Hard Mode** opening, the shop started with **400 Credits**, a large water jug, Ghost Water, and some High Quality Water. The Bottle Printer was already available, allowing the player to prepare smaller containers without purchasing another machine.

Starting Credits, rent, and available resources can vary with the selected difficulty and Starting Perks. Check your actual opening inventory before committing to equipment purchases in 1.0.

![Day 1 Water Merchant start with 400 Credits, six days until rent, and a Bottle Printer requiring 4 Energy to print a small bottle.](/images/probably-stolen/water-merchant-starting-stock.webp)

The starting Bottle Printer uses 4 Energy to print a Small Water Bottle. Its upgrade requires three Common Electronics, so check the printer's energy and materials before counting on extra bottles for the next trading day.

### Sort the water before your first customer

Put the water containers somewhere you can access them easily, then identify which ones are worth selling and which need processing.

- **High-Quality Water:** Keep it separate from poorer stock so you can offer it to buyers without accidentally lowering its purity.
- **Base or Ghost Water:** Store it for later filtering rather than presenting it as a higher-grade product.
- **Empty bottles and jugs:** Keep enough usable capacity for buying, processing, and selling water.
- **Bottle Printer:** Leave room around it for printed containers and the materials you'll eventually need for upgrades.

A large jug is especially useful because you can move a substantial amount of water without managing many individual bottles. Small bottles still have a purpose: they let you complete sales to customers whose budgets are too low for a large container.

You do not need to empty every starting container into a single jug. Keeping grades separate makes the next several transactions easier and avoids creating extra purification work.

### How Much Can You Spend Before the First Rent?

In that Hard Mode opening, there was no rent grace period or ordinary retail markup. The selected **Bad Lease** perk added **40 Credits** to rent, bringing the first payment to **240 Credits**.

With **400 Credits** available at the start, only **160 Credits** remained after reserving the rent payment.

That makes an early Moisture Farm purchase difficult to justify unless trading has already generated enough additional cash. Use the water and Bottle Printer you own, complete affordable sales, and leave the next rent payment untouched before committing to optional machinery.

## Get Your First Water Customers Without Filling the Shop With Unsold Stock
Put a water container in the Display Case before opening. Keep the larger jugs accessible in storage, but prepare smaller bottles for customers who cannot afford an entire jug.

![A water jug placed in the shop display case on Day 43, with bottles and trading supplies organized in storage.](/images/probably-stolen/water-merchant-display-water.webp)

A buyer with limited Credits may still be able to purchase a smaller quantity of water. Check the amount they can afford before offering your largest container. The starting Bottle Printer becomes useful here because it gives you more flexibility when packaging stock for different customers.

Keep at least one usable large container for later purchases and purification. Selling your only convenient storage vessel to complete a small transaction can make the next batch harder to handle.

High-Quality Water can remain unsold even when production is no longer the problem. In one Red Ink Water Merchant run, an Upper Levels water buyer appeared on **Day 14** with approximately **500 Credits**, allowing the shop to sell water that had previously been difficult to move. A Water Supply Crisis was not required for that transaction.

This was one buyer's arrival, not a guaranteed Day 14 unlock. Even a wealthier customer may be unable to afford an entire large jug. When rent is approaching, offering an affordable quantity can be more useful than waiting to sell everything at once. Make sure the container's label matches its tested purity; selling lower-grade water as a higher grade can damage reputation.

### Buying water is not the same as making money

A customer offering water can be useful even when the water is not High Quality. Lower-grade water can be improved later, but that requires working filters, containers, and possibly powered machines.

Check three things before accepting the trade: the actual water grade, the current buying price, and whether you have enough space to process it.

An early Water Merchant session bought lower-grade water, improved it to Base Water, and then used additional filtering to reach High Quality. That worked, but it consumed filtering durability along the way.

Check whether the current purchase price leaves enough room for the [filtering and purification work](/probably-stolen/water-purification) the batch still needs. Heavily contaminated water can consume more filtering durability than a cleaner batch bought for a similar price.

Avoid buying large amounts of questionable water merely because the seller calls it High Quality. Storage filled with low-grade stock can become a bigger problem than an empty display case.

### Don't keep asking for water you no longer need

Seeking additional water stock makes sense while your containers are nearly empty. Once several jugs are full, continuing to attract water sellers can consume customer opportunities and leave you spending Credits on inventory you cannot easily store or sell.

Review the buying signs and Display Case before opening. When your storage is already full, attracting more water sellers can create additional purchases without improving sales.

If the displayed merchandise matches your intended customers but traffic remains low, check your **Lower Levels reputation**. Falling to -20 removes one random customer per day; [check the reputation thresholds and recovery options](/probably-stolen/factions) before spending more Credits on equipment intended to support greater sales volume.

## What Should You Buy First: More Storage, a Moisture Farm, or Better Equipment?

The first significant equipment decision usually arrives before your cash balance feels comfortable.

One early Hard Mode run encountered desirable filtering equipment and a Moisture Farm while rent was approaching. Rather than buying the Moisture Farm immediately, the shop continued trading with the equipment and water already available.

That restraint matters because new machinery solves only one part of the business. A Moisture Farm produces water, but production capacity does not automatically create storage space, energy, or a buyer.

### Upgrade only the part of the business that is holding you back

A few situations justify different purchases.

**Not enough water to sell:** Improving your water supply becomes useful once the shop can afford its equipment and running costs. A Moisture Farm is attractive when you want a repeatable supply instead of relying on customers.

**Enough water, but nowhere to store it:** Storage capacity is the immediate limitation. More production equipment would make the crowding worse.

**Large jugs, but too few smaller containers:** Use the Bottle Printer. Later upgrades help expand your options for packaging and selling water.

**Machines sitting idle:** Check their power requirements and available batteries before purchasing another machine. An unused Water Purifier does not improve your sales merely by occupying a Machine Bay.

**Rent is due soon:** Preserve enough cash to pay it. Postpone optional equipment if the purchase would leave you dependent on another customer arriving.

The Bottle Printer is particularly easy to underestimate because you already begin with one. In the middle of a longer Water Merchant run, upgrading it with collected materials became useful as the shop started handling larger volumes and different bottle sizes.

That upgrade has more immediate value when bottles are limiting sales. It is less urgent when your problem is simply not having water worth putting into them.

### The storage expansion decision

Around the middle of a run, an expansion opportunity presented a choice between more machine capacity and more storage. The shop already had substantial water inventory but relatively few additional machines to place, so **Storage** was chosen instead of a Machine Bay.

The useful question is how you would fill the new space.

A Storage Bay gives you room to hold existing goods and containers. A Machine Bay only solves a problem if you already have, or can realistically afford, additional machinery that needs somewhere to operate.

Buying the wrong expansion can leave the original bottleneck untouched while reducing the cash available for rent.

<section id="georgio">

## What Can Georgio Offer During a Water Merchant Run?

Georgio can offer an investment opportunity while your shop is still developing. He asks for help funding a spaceship and provides an IOU after receiving the Credits.

![Georgio returning on Day 35 and confirming that the investment has been repaid with interest.](/images/probably-stolen/water-merchant-georgio-return.webp)

In one run, an investment of approximately **1,100 Credits** returned roughly **4,500 Credits** around Day 35. The return was substantial, but the money remained unavailable until Georgio came back.

The investment made sense because the shop could still pay rent while those Credits were tied up. A Water Merchant with insufficient cash for the next lease payment would face a different decision: losing access to essential equipment or stock is not worth the risk of waiting for a later return.

After receiving the repayment, prioritize whichever limitation is preventing sales. Additional storage is useful when full jugs are crowding the shop; production equipment is more valuable when water supply cannot keep up with buyers. Neither purchase needs to consume the entire repayment.

### Georgio Can Also Help With Scavenging

Georgio can offer scavenging assistance separately from his investment opportunity. In one Red Ink run, he agreed to help for **three nights**. While he accompanied the shopkeeper, scavenging did not cause injuries, and the arrangement provided **two additional scavenging attempts**.

That can be valuable early in a Water Merchant run, when usable filters, bottles, modules, and materials may be more useful than purchasing another machine. The extra trips provide more opportunities to collect supplies without tying up Credits in a speculative investment.

The benefit also depends on your Starting Perks. If you already have protection against scavenging injuries, that part of Georgio's assistance overlaps with your existing advantage. The extra scavenging opportunities remain useful, provided you have enough carrying capacity and storage for what you collect.

<section id="water-shortages">

## Prepare Water Before a Supply Crisis, Then Make Sure You Can Actually Sell It

A Water Supply Crisis is one of the most valuable opportunities for an established Water Merchant.

The useful work happens before the event begins: collecting water, improving its quality, finding enough containers, and keeping stock ready to display.

A later crisis occurred when the shop was already operating with multiple water jugs, High-Quality Water, Pure Water, and other merchandise. The first day of that event opened with **13 customers in the queue**.

In one extended Water Merchant run, a **Water Supply Crisis** appeared on **Day 77**, the same day that **618 Credits** in rent were due. The Calendar showed a **+100% price increase for Water and Water Rations**.

The opportunity was valuable because the shop already had prepared water available for sale. Starting production only after the price increase would have delayed the first sales, while buying more water at inflated prices could have reduced the benefit.

Check your own Calendar before holding inventory for a shortage. The Day 77 timing and rent amount describe that particular run, not a guaranteed event schedule.

![Day 77 calendar showing a Water Supply Crisis with +100% Water prices while 618 Credits in rent are due.](/images/probably-stolen/water-merchant-supply-crisis.webp)

### Prepare the inventory before opening

A useful shortage-day routine is:

1. Move finished High-Quality and Pure Water into accessible storage.
2. Check the actual grades and labels on the containers you intend to sell.
3. Put water in the display case to attract suitable buyers.
4. Keep smaller bottles available for customers who cannot afford a large jug.
5. Leave enough empty capacity to handle any useful stock you purchase.

The exact number of customers and their budgets can vary. A long queue does not guarantee that all your water will sell, and a Water Crisis does not turn every visitor into a high-budget buyer.

For that reason, starting the event with only expensive containers can leave usable demand unfilled. Packaging matters alongside water quality.

During the shortage, prioritize sales that can actually close. A customer with insufficient Credits cannot buy an expensive water jug simply because market prices have doubled. Offer smaller containers when appropriate, and secure the rent payment before holding remaining stock for a more profitable buyer.

<section id="production-expansion">

## Moving From Trading Water to Producing It Regularly

Buying low-grade water, filtering it, and reselling it can keep the business moving early on. Over time, production equipment reduces how heavily you depend on water sellers appearing with suitable stock.

A developed Water Merchant shop can operate Moisture Farms and Water Purifiers alongside storage containers and the Bottle Printer. This creates a repeatable supply, but it adds another daily responsibility: checking that the machines have enough energy to run.

Machine configurations can change their energy consumption substantially. A powerful setup is only useful while its batteries and supporting power equipment can sustain it.

### Is a Second Moisture Farm Worth Buying?

Running multiple Moisture Farms reduces the production rate of each machine. With **two farms operating simultaneously, each produces 80% of its normal output**.

In one setup, a farm displayed **680 mL per day** while two machines were active. The combined production of two identically configured farms would therefore be **1,360 mL per day**, compared with 1,700 mL at their full individual rates.

The second farm still increases total water production, but it does not double it. Consider the additional energy, storage, and available buyers before investing in another machine. If your existing water containers are already full, expanding production may do little to improve daily sales.

### Keep Overnight Production Running

Once Moisture Farms and Water Purifiers become part of the daily business, check their available energy before closing the shop. A machine that cannot complete its overnight cycle will not produce the water you're expecting to sell the next day.

When output stops changing, inspect the machine's power supply and displayed energy requirement before purchasing another unit. Rechargers and power supplies should not be treated as interchangeable without checking their actual equipment interactions.

### Make Pure Water a repeatable product

High-Quality Water remains useful even before you can reliably produce Pure Water. It is not necessary to discard or indefinitely hold every batch that falls short of the maximum grade.

After the overnight cycle, inspect how much finished water is available before preparing the next day's sales. When the grade is still below Pure Water, [check the filters and purifier configuration](/probably-stolen/water-purification) before committing another batch.

At the business level, successful production means something more practical than achieving a single Pure Water result: you have enough charged equipment, usable containers, and finished inventory to repeat the process without rebuilding your setup every night.

<section id="late-game">

## What Changes After Your Water Business Becomes Profitable?

A developed Water Merchant can reach a point where the shop has more goods than it can easily sell in one day.

During the later stages of the long run, water production continued alongside bottle printing, wine, medical stock, modules, and other trade goods. The shop increasingly depended on managing storage and attracting the appropriate customers rather than finding the next basic item to buy.

There are several signs that your water business has reached this stage:

- Multiple containers of High-Quality or Pure Water are ready without requiring a full day of manual work.
- The Bottle Printer can provide containers when a suitable buyer appears.
- Your machines have enough power to complete their overnight work.
- Rent can be covered without selling every valuable item in storage.
- You can decline poor purchase offers instead of accepting every trade to keep cash moving.

At this point, adding more water production is not automatically the best expansion. More output has little value while full containers are already occupying storage and suitable buyers are scarce.

### When the shop becomes too crowded

One late-game business had enough water to fill multiple jugs while also holding medicine, modules, weapons, wine, and packaging materials. The resulting problem was physical space: valuable items were scattered among bays, and finding the right stock during a customer transaction took extra work.

Keep the layout simple enough that you can distinguish finished water from unfinished water, empty containers, and merchandise intended for other buyers.

A practical arrangement is to give finished water a consistent storage area, keep bottles near the Bottle Printer, and leave room around important powered machines for changing batteries and modules. The precise bay arrangement is less important than being able to retrieve and replace goods without accidentally mixing batches or losing access to inventory.

### Keep Side Trades From Disrupting the Water Business

Late in the run, contraband and other side inventory created security problems even though the water operation was already profitable. Check questionable merchandise before opening the shop rather than putting a working water business at risk for an unrelated trade.

<section id="shop-buyout">

## When Should a Water Merchant Stop Upgrading and Save for the Store Deed?

The full 1.0 release allows you to purchase the store and receive its **Deed**. For a developed Water Merchant, that changes the purpose of late-game expansion: once water production and regular sales are dependable, saving for ownership can be more valuable than buying another machine.

Check the property's valuation on the **Calendar** and compare it with your available Credits. Estimated store wealth is not the same as spendable cash. A shop can own valuable water, containers, and machinery while still being unable to afford the purchase.

The Water Merchant's advantage is repeatable inventory. Keep enough finished water ready for customers, maintain the equipment needed to replace what sells, and direct the remaining income toward the property's purchase price.

Once the next rent payment and essential production supplies are covered, compare further upgrades with the amount needed for the Deed. An additional Moisture Farm is less valuable when full water containers are already waiting for buyers.

For **Prodigious**, you must obtain the Deed before Day 50. [Check the buyout achievement conditions](/probably-stolen/achievements) before committing your remaining cash to another expansion.

</section>
