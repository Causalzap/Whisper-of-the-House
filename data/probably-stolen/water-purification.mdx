Use a **Moisture Farm with +100% Quality** to produce High Quality Water. Pass it through **Kotton Fabric, a Water Filter, and a UV Lamp**, then run it through a **Water Purifier with +100% Quality** overnight or with a Turbo Booster.

Getting water into the High Quality category is fairly straightforward once you have decent filtering equipment. Getting that last improvement to **Pure Water** is where a lot of water setups stall. You can have a machine full of clean-looking water, several upgraded modules, and a working production line without actually producing the highest water grade.

Start with relatively clean water from a Moisture Farm, remove the remaining contaminants with manual filters, and finish the batch in a Water Purifier. Check the Water Scanner after each stage. A working machine does not guarantee that the water has reached the next grade, especially when a filter has lost durability or the incoming batch was heavily contaminated.

<section id="starting-water">

## Start with Moisture Farm Water Instead of the Tap

The tap is available early, but its water contains enough contamination to make purification inefficient. Starting with heavily contaminated water means asking your filters to do much more work before the batch is even suitable for the final purifier.

A **Moisture Farm** provides a better starting point. An unmodified farm initially produces **Ghost Water**, but adding Quality bonuses improves what it produces:

- **+50% Quality:** Base Water.
- **+100% Quality:** High Quality Water.

These are equipment Quality bonuses, not the water's displayed Purity percentage. A machine showing +100% Quality does not mean its water has 100% Purity.

The distinction becomes important when comparing an upgraded farm with a Water Purifier. Each machine has its own Quality configuration, and upgrading one does not automatically improve the other.

### Check the Water Scanner before upgrading anything

Scan the water you're about to process. The Water Scanner shows its contaminants, their quantities, and the overall Purity percentage used to determine its grade.

![Water Scanner showing the water's contaminants, measured purity, and quality.](/images/probably-stolen/water-scanner-contaminants.webp)

Look at the contaminant readings rather than judging the batch by the name on its container. Two batches described as High Quality Water can still have different measured purity, leaving different amounts of work before they reach Pure Water.

This also matters when purchasing water from a customer. A container labeled Base Water or High Quality Water is not a substitute for checking what's actually inside it. Buying several containers without testing them can leave you with lower-quality water than you planned to process.

### Set up the Moisture Farm's Quality modules

Add Quality-producing modules to the Moisture Farm and inspect the resulting Quality value. For the multi-stage Pure Water setup, **+100% Quality on the farm** is a useful target because it produces High Quality Water before you begin manual filtering.

![Moisture Farm showing a production rate of 1000 mL Ghostwater per day and an energy requirement of 4 per day.](/images/probably-stolen/moisture-farm-output.webp)

After adjusting the modules, inspect the water inside the Moisture Farm rather than relying on the machine configuration alone. This 6000 mL jug shows 99.60% purity and is tested as High-Quality Water.

![Moisture Farm with installed modules and a full 6000 mL jug tested as 99.60% High-Quality Water.](/images/probably-stolen/moisture-farm-high-quality.webp)

Check the water's tested grade after each production cycle. The modules determine the machine's configuration, while the container shows the actual water available for further filtering.

Purchased or collected water can also be filtered, but the amount of contamination determines how much work remains. Scan any existing batch before processing it; water that is already close to High Quality may need much less filtering than heavily contaminated tap water.

<section id="manual-filtering">

## Filter the High Quality Water Before Using the Purifier

Prepare a set of pitchers or containers for manual filtration. The demonstrated setup uses three filtering tools:

- **Kotton Fabric**
- **Water Filter**
- **UV Lamp**

Put the tools into the filtering setup and transfer the water through the available stages. Water moves between containers as you process it, so make sure you're pouring the batch into the container equipped with the next working filter rather than into ordinary storage.

![Kotton Fabric showing 980 out of 1000 durability and its use for filtering large particles from water.](/images/probably-stolen/kotton-fabric-filter.webp)

After processing, scan the water again. Its purity should improve as contamination is removed, although that improvement does not necessarily change the displayed grade immediately.

A batch can remain High Quality Water after a successful filter pass because it has not yet reached the next quality threshold. Conversely, a small improvement may be enough to change a batch that was only just below High Quality.

That is why checking the actual purity is more useful than repeatedly transferring water and hoping its name changes.

### Don't exhaust your filters on poor starting water

Heavily contaminated tap water can consume substantial filter durability before reaching a useful grade. Processing several large containers this way may exhaust your filters before the cleaner batches are ready.

This is especially costly when you're trying to prepare a large amount of water at once. Before buying another low-quality batch, check how much usable durability remains in your current setup.

Once a filter runs out of durability, another pass will not provide the expected improvement. Check which filter you are using before replacing it: an Advanced Water Filter can recover durability at the start of the next day, while an ordinary Water Filter may also stop being effective when the water is already above its useful purification range.

</section>

<section id="water-purifier">

## Finish the Water in a Water Purifier

When the manually filtered water is ready, transfer it into a **Water Purifier**.

Configure the purifier with **+100% Quality**. This is a separate target from the +100% Quality used on the Moisture Farm. The farm improves the water you're starting with; the purifier handles the final processing stage.

![Water Purifier showing +109% Quality, 11 energy per day, and 9.2 mL of contaminants removed during the previous night.](/images/probably-stolen/water-purifier-module-stats.webp)

One upgraded Water Purifier shows +109% Quality and 11 Energy/Day, compared with a base requirement of 5 Energy/Day. It also reports removing 9.2 mL of contaminants during the previous night. These numbers describe that particular module configuration, not a fixed cost or output for every purifier.

Leave the Water Purifier running **overnight**, or use a **Turbo Booster** if you have one available. Once the processing finishes, inspect the output with the Water Scanner before moving it into your sale containers.

The sequence is:

1. Produce High Quality Water with the upgraded Moisture Farm.
2. Pass it through the Kotton Fabric, Water Filter, and UV Lamp setup.
3. Transfer the filtered water into the +100% Quality Water Purifier.
4. Let the purifier complete its run, then check the finished water grade.

The result you're looking for is **Pure Water** in the water's actual quality display.

A high machine Quality number alone does not establish that the batch has finished correctly. The incoming water, any contamination left after manual filtering, and whether the purifier completed its work all matter.

### Can you make Pure Water without a Moisture Farm?

Yes. You can start with purchased or collected water instead of producing it yourself. Scan the batch first, remove its remaining contaminants with working filters, and use the Water Purifier for the final stage.

An upgraded Moisture Farm makes it easier to obtain consistently cleaner starting water, but it is not required merely to process a suitable batch you already own.

<section id="quality-troubleshooting">

## Why Is My Water Still Not Pure?

The most useful troubleshooting method is to identify the first stage where the water stops improving. Repeatedly processing the finished batch without checking the earlier steps makes it difficult to tell whether the issue is the source water, an exhausted filter, or the final machine.

### The filter has no durability left

A water-processing session can appear to be working even though the filtering tools have already run out of durability.

One particularly revealing failure occurs when water remains Base Water after a filtering attempt. Checking the equipment shows exhausted tools; once working filtering equipment is used, the water improves to High Quality.

![Advanced Water Filter showing 0/400 durability and recovery of 50 durability at the start of each day.](/images/probably-stolen/water-filter-durability.webp)

Check the tools individually before spending money on new Quality modules. More machine Quality cannot compensate for a manual filtering stage that is no longer removing contamination.

A standard Water Filter can also stop improving water once its purification limit is reached, even when durability remains. Check the item's description before assuming that another pass will help.

An Advanced Water Filter behaves differently when depleted. It has a maximum of 400 durability and recovers 50 durability at the start of each day. A filter showing 0/400 is therefore temporarily unusable, but it does not necessarily need to be replaced.

### The Moisture Farm is upgraded, but the Water Purifier isn't

Check which machine actually received your Quality modules.

The **+100% Moisture Farm** target produces High Quality Water in this setup. The **+100% Water Purifier** target is used for the final step toward Pure Water.

Reaching the farm's target and then transferring its output into an unupgraded purifier is not the same configuration.

Inspect each machine separately, make sure it has the modules you intended, and check the grade after the purifier completes a run.

### Mixing water lowered the quality of the batch

Water retains its actual quality when stored, but combining batches can change the resulting grade.

Pouring Base Water into a container of High Quality Water can lower the combined water's purity. A batch that previously qualified as High Quality might drop below that category after mixing with poorer water.

The opposite can also occur: adding cleaner water can improve a mixture enough to reach a higher grade. It is useful when deliberately blending supplies, but it is risky when you are protecting a batch intended to become Pure Water.

Keep Pure Water separate from lower-grade stock unless you have a specific reason to mix it. Scan the contents after every intentional blend instead of assuming the destination container preserves the previous quality.

### The purifier hasn't finished processing

A batch placed into the Water Purifier is not finished just because the machine is configured correctly.

Check that the machine can run, that its energy supply is sufficient, and that the processing cycle has completed. Running the purifier overnight or using a Turbo Booster are two ways to finish the demonstrated process.

A machine that stops operating partway through is a different issue from one whose Quality bonuses are too low. Inspect its running state before changing modules again.

### The scanner says Pure Water, but the label still says High-Quality

A finished container can hold Pure Water while still carrying its previous label. The scanner below confirms **99.97% Pure Water**, but the container is labeled **High-Quality Water**.

![Water Scanner showing 99.97% tested Pure Water, an outdated High-Quality Water label, and the Match Label button.](/images/probably-stolen/pure-water-scanner-result.webp)

Click **Match Label** to update the container's description to its tested contents. You do not need to purify the water again just because the label is outdated.

Check this before selling expensive water, especially when you have several batches with different purity levels in storage.

</section>

<section id="finished-water">

## Store the Finished Pure Water Separately

Once the scanner confirms Pure Water and the label matches its contents, transfer the finished batch into storage without mixing it with lower-grade water.

Keep unfinished High-Quality Water in a different container. Adding lower-purity water to a completed batch can change its tested grade, forcing you to process it again.

Scan the water again before use or sale if you have transferred or combined containers since the last test.

</section>
