<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
To start Aquaculture in Probably Stolen, choose **Aquafarmer** or unlock **Water Trading → Aquaculture** through Joe Wild. Place a **Recirculating Aquaculture System (RAS)**, fill its tank with enough water, supply Energy, and add fish and suitable food.

For breeding, keep healthy adult male and female Tilapia of the same species together. For Caviar, unlock **Aquaculture II**, buy Sturgeon from the Retired Aquafarmer, feed them meat, and harvest their eggs with a **Surgery Toolset**. A three-tank setup can use Gutter Tilapia for meat, Graft Tilapia for medical products, and Sturgeon for Caviar.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

Fish farming becomes much easier when you separate three jobs: keeping fish alive, maintaining breeding stock, and harvesting products.

A tank full of fish is not automatically a productive farm. Growing fish need more water as they mature, hungry adults can eat smaller fish or eggs, and an incorrectly configured Auto Butcher can remove the animals you intended to keep.

Start with one working system. Once you can keep its fish fed, powered, and healthy overnight, expand into specialized tanks rather than adding expensive fish to an unstable setup.

<section id="start-aquaculture">

## How Do You Start Aquaculture and Set Up an RAS Tank?

The fastest way to learn fish farming is the **Aquafarmer** starting scenario. It begins with Aquaculture equipment, an adult Tilapia breeding pair, fertilized Tilapia Roe, and fish feed.

You can also add fish farming to an existing shop through Joe Wild's Network.

The base unlock order is:

1. **Water Trading — 300 Credits:** Unlocks the water-trading services required for Aquaculture.
2. **Aquaculture — 400 Credits:** Provides an Aquaculture Starter Kit and access to the Retired Aquafarmer.
3. **Aquaculture II — 1,200 Credits:** Expands the available fish to Eel, Electric Eel, and Sturgeon.

These are base network costs. Check the price displayed in your current run before spending Credits.

The Retired Aquafarmer normally visits weekly and sells fish, fertilized Roe, feed, filters, and equipment. Her ordinary scheduled visit is Tuesday.

### Set up your first Recirculating Aquaculture System

The **Recirculating Aquaculture System (RAS)** is the tank that keeps fish alive overnight. The basic fish inventory uses an 8 × 4 grid and a separate water reservoir holding up to **12,000 mL**.

To start:

1. Place the RAS where you can access its fish inventory and water tank.
2. Install or connect a suitable Energy source.
3. Fill the RAS reservoir with water.
4. Put your starting Tilapia and fertilized Roe inside the fish inventory.
5. Add compatible fish food.
6. Inspect the RAS status before ending the day.

![The starting Recirculating Aquaculture System showing its Water, Filters, Dispenser, Tank, and Butcher sections, with a prompt to add water using a jug.](/images/probably-stolen/aquaculture-first-ras.webp)

The main mistake is treating the fish inventory as ordinary storage.

A fish left outside a working RAS at the end of the day will die. Roe and Fry left outside a valid tank can also be lost during overnight processing.

**Finish moving the fish before ending the day.** A temporary transfer is fine while reorganizing, but do not leave live animals on the counter overnight.

### Should you buy a second RAS immediately?

Not usually.

A second tank costs equipment, water, Energy, and inventory space. Your first system should already support its current fish without repeated health damage.

Buy another RAS when you need to separate species, protect Fry, or run a different production cycle. For example, keeping a breeding population of Gutter Tilapia separate from meat-fed Sturgeon makes feeding much easier.

Expanding a fish inventory also does not increase the RAS reservoir beyond its 12,000 mL capacity. More inventory squares will not solve a water shortage.

<section id="ras-water-power">

## How Much Water and Energy Does an RAS Need?

A working RAS needs enough liquid for every fish, Roe group, and Fry group inside it. The total requirement increases as fish grow.

The base system uses **5 kJ per active overnight cycle**, before relevant equipment changes.

Water is often the more serious bottleneck because a tank that safely supported small fish can become overcrowded as they reach adulthood.

<GuideTable columns={fishWaterColumns} rows={fishWaterRows} />

These are adult-stage requirements, not fixed requirements throughout a fish's life.

For example, four adult Gutter Tilapia need approximately:

**4 × 750 mL = 3,000 mL**

Three adult Sturgeon need approximately:

**3 × 3,000 mL = 9,000 mL**

That leaves much less capacity for additional fish, and the requirements can change again when fish grow into their next stage.

Always use the RAS's displayed **Water / Needs** figures for your current tank.

### Why does a tank show Suffocating or Low Water?

The warning can appear when the fish need more water than the RAS currently contains.

Check the combined requirement instead of looking only at whether the reservoir contains some water.

A tank with 2,000 mL remaining is not safe merely because its reservoir is visibly filled. If its fish collectively need 4,000 mL, the supply is only half of what they require.

Under the normal fish-care rules:

- Below the full requirement, fish can suffer health damage.
- Below half the total requirement, the shortage can be fatal.
- Growth can turn yesterday's safe tank into today's dangerous one.

When a warning appears, add water first or remove enough fish to bring the demand below the available amount.

If a large Sturgeon is consuming too much capacity, move it into another prepared RAS rather than risking the entire breeding population.

### Does the RAS need clean water?

The RAS can hold different water grades, but it must control the **Organic Waste** produced by fish.

Living fish add waste as they grow and remain in the tank. Water Filters remove contamination while the system has the power to operate.

Ordinary fish become unhealthy when Organic Waste grows too high. For fish with normal waste sensitivity, damage begins above roughly **3% waste concentration**, and around **8%** can be fatal.

Graft Tilapia are more sensitive than standard Tilapia. Gutter Tilapia tolerate organic-waste damage, but that does not make the RAS safe for every other species sharing the same tank.

A tank with adequate water can still kill its fish if filtration falls behind.

### What happens when the RAS runs out of Energy?

An unpowered RAS cannot operate its normal filtration and recovery processes. Fish can also suffer direct health damage.

This can become a problem even when both the water reservoir and fish feed look adequate.

![An RAS displaying INSUFFICIENT ENERGY despite having 9,695 mL of water against a 3,850 mL requirement.](/images/probably-stolen/aquaculture-ras-insufficient-energy.webp)

In an early Aquafarmer run, the player initially focused on water before realizing the tank's immediate problem was missing Energy.

When fish look unhealthy despite adequate water and food, check the power source before buying more feed.

A reliable pre-night check is:

**Water → Energy → Filters → Food → Available Space**

Do not advance several days with a warning visible simply because the fish survived the previous night.

<section id="fish-feeding-growth">

## What Should You Feed Fish, and How Do You Make Them Grow Faster?

Not every fish accepts the same food.

The three Tilapia varieties are **omnivores**. Eel, Electric Eel, and Sturgeon are **carnivores** that require meat.

This difference is the foundation of a productive fish farm.

### Gutterflow Pellets vs Industrial Fish Feed

The two common prepared feeds serve different purposes.

**Gutterflow Pellets**

- A cheap way to supply calories.
- Especially useful for Gutter Tilapia.
- Can cause Trash Grade meat when more than 25% of a non-exempt fish's absorbed diet comes from them.
- Gutter Tilapia are exempt from that meat-quality penalty.

**Industrial Fish Feed**

- Suitable for Tilapia varieties.
- Does not carry the same Gutterflow Pellet meat-quality penalty.
- An all-industrial diet reduces fish growth speed to approximately **75%** of its normal rate.

For Gutter Tilapia, cheap Gutterflow Pellets are often the practical choice.

For Graft Tilapia, Industrial Fish Feed is useful when preserving the value of their medical products and meat matters more than maximum growth speed.

For Eel and Sturgeon, neither feed replaces meat.

### How do you use the Livestock Feed Dispenser?

The **Livestock Feed Dispenser** holds and processes food for animals in its connected enclosure.

For a fish farm:

1. Install the dispenser with the RAS you want to feed.
2. Add suitable food to its ingredient inventory.
3. Use its blending controls to store calories.
4. Check the feeding target and available calories.
5. Confirm that the fish can eat the stored food type.

![Livestock Feed Dispenser tooltip showing stored calories, automatic blending, and feeding up to 85% Hunger.](/images/probably-stolen/aquaculture-feeding-dispenser-tooltip.webp)

The dispenser can feed hungry fish automatically, reducing the need to manage every food item by hand.

However, stored calories alone do not guarantee successful feeding.

A Sturgeon cannot use an unsuitable plant-based feed merely because it is inside the dispenser. Keep a meat supply available for carnivores.

If you have fish fillets and other suitable meat products, the dispenser can help turn them into a repeatable feeding supply.

### Why are the fish not growing?

Check the current Hunger level.

Fish need sufficient nutrition to advance through their growth cycle. Their progress can slow when the available food is unsuitable or their Hunger remains too low.

A fish surviving the night is not the same as a fish receiving enough food for normal growth.

If you are using Industrial Fish Feed, remember that slower growth is an expected tradeoff, not necessarily a broken RAS.

</section>

<section id="breeding-and-roe">

## How Do You Breed Tilapia and Hatch Fish Eggs?

Tilapia, Gutter Tilapia, and Graft Tilapia can reproduce inside the RAS.

They use the same broad breeding process, but the resulting fish and their products serve different purposes.

### Step 1: Keep adult fish of the same species together

To produce fertilized Roe, keep at least one healthy adult female and one healthy adult male of the **same Tilapia species**.

Both must have enough food.

A female can lay Roe without a male, but it will remain unfertilized unless a suitable male is present when fertilization occurs.

A male ordinary Tilapia does not replace a male Gutter Tilapia for producing Gutter Tilapia offspring.

### Step 2: Wait for Roe

A fed adult female Tilapia has a **15% chance per night** to produce a clutch of Roe under the documented normal breeding rules.

That is a chance, not a guaranteed daily output.

A healthy female may go several nights without laying eggs. Do not buy additional machines simply because no Roe appeared on the first night.

A typical Roe clutch contains **six eggs**.

### Step 3: Protect fertilized Roe and Fry

Once fertilized Roe begins developing, it progresses into Fry and then Juvenile fish.

The early stages take relatively little space, but a group of six developing fish can create a much larger demand after it separates into individual Juveniles.

Before that transition, check:

- Whether there is enough room for the new fish.
- Whether the RAS has enough water for their next stage.
- Whether their food supply can support the larger population.
- Whether hungry adults could eat the young.

A full inventory can prevent new fish from being placed successfully.

Do not wait until the tank is completely packed before preparing another enclosure or removing excess adults.

### Should you keep fertilized or unfertilized Roe?

That depends on the purpose of the tank.

Keep **fertilized Roe** when you want the next generation of fish.

Collect **unfertilized Roe** when you are producing items for sale or other uses rather than expanding the population.

This is especially useful for Graft Tilapia, whose unfertilized Roe is a medical product.

If you only want Graft Tilapia Roe, a tank of healthy adult females can produce it without maintaining a male breeding population.

<section id="fish-dying">

## Why Are Your Fish Dying or Not Breeding?

Fish often fail because several small problems compound over multiple nights.

A tank may have enough water but no Energy. Another may have food but too much Organic Waste. A third may contain healthy adults that are eating their own Roe.

Use the current fish status and RAS readings to identify the problem before buying replacements.

<GuideTable columns={fishProblemsColumns} rows={fishProblemsRows} />

### The fish died even though there was food

Food does not protect fish from a water shortage, missing power, or excessive contamination.

In one Aqua Farmer run, fish died even though food was still present in their tank. The immediate cause was not established from that observation alone.

A better response is to inspect the full system:

1. Compare available water with the total requirement.
2. Confirm the RAS has usable Energy.
3. Inspect Organic Waste and filter condition.
4. Check each surviving fish's Hunger and Health.
5. Look for crowding or an unsafe mixture of fish sizes.

Do not assume another stack of food will resolve a problem caused by low water or accumulated waste.

### The fish are alive but keep losing Health

A fish can remain alive for several nights while taking repeated damage.

If the tank has low water, fix the supply before adding more animals.

If the water volume is adequate but Organic Waste is increasing, restore filtration.

If both readings look safe, inspect Energy and Hunger.

Healthy, fed fish in a powered tank can recover Health over time, but that recovery should not be used as an excuse to keep exposing them to damage.

### Why Is There No New Roe?

A healthy, fed female may still go several nights without producing Roe. Breeding is not guaranteed every day.

If you want fertilized Roe, check that an adult male of the same species is present. If you only have females, they can still produce unfertilized Roe.

Before buying more fish, check the adult Hunger levels and the available tank space. The full breeding process is explained in [How Do You Breed Tilapia and Hatch Fish Eggs?](#breeding-and-roe).

### Why did all the Fry disappear?

The most important possibilities are predation and insufficient placement space.

An adult fish can eat Roe or Fry when it cannot satisfy its Hunger normally.

Roe or young fish moved outside a valid RAS can also be lost overnight.

Keep the adults fed and avoid filling every inventory slot just before the Fry develop.

A separate nursery tank is useful if you are repeatedly losing young fish in a crowded breeding system.

</section>

<section id="fish-species">

## Which Fish Should You Raise in Probably Stolen?

There are six main fish species.

<GuideTable columns={fishSpeciesColumns} rows={fishSpeciesRows} />

Choosing the right species depends on what you need from the farm.

### Tilapia: the easiest starting point

Ordinary Tilapia teach the basic breeding cycle.

They accept different food types, reproduce in captivity, and produce fish meat when harvested.

They are useful for learning how much water and food a growing population requires.

However, once Gutter and Graft Tilapia become available, ordinary Tilapia offer fewer specialized products.

### Gutter Tilapia: cheap feed and useful meat

Gutter Tilapia have two important advantages.

They can eat **Gutterflow Pellets** without the normal Trash Grade meat penalty, and they tolerate organic-waste damage that would harm other Tilapia varieties.

Gutter Tilapia can also remove Heavy Metals from suitable liquid mixtures, making them useful beyond ordinary fish farming.

![Fertilized Gutter Tilapia Roe tooltip showing six eggs, one-day hatching, and the species' tolerance to dirty water and ability to remove contaminants.](/images/probably-stolen/aquaculture-gutter-tilapia-roe.webp)

For an ordinary fish farm, their most useful role is often producing affordable meat for more valuable carnivorous fish.

Instead of selling every fillet, keep enough meat to feed your Sturgeon or Eel tank.

To clean chemical reagents with Gutter Tilapia, including reagent preparation and recovery, follow the [Chemist Guide](/probably-stolen/chemist). A fish tank's liquid should not be assumed to be safe, sellable Pure Water.

### Graft Tilapia: Roe and medical products

Graft Tilapia provide products that can appeal to medical buyers.

Their **Unfertilized Roe** has medical uses, while harvested Graft Tilapia can produce **Tilapia Skin Grafts** as well as fish meat.

This makes female Graft Tilapia useful even when you do not want them reproducing.

They are also more sensitive to Organic Waste than ordinary Tilapia.

Keep their tank well filtered instead of relying on the waste tolerance of a separate Gutter Tilapia population.

### Eel: valuable meat without breeding

Eel are carnivores.

They need meat rather than ordinary prepared fish feed, and they **do not reproduce in captivity**.

Their main product is Eel Fillets.

You receive two young Eel when unlocking Aquaculture II, and the Retired Aquafarmer can provide additional stock.

Do not build an Eel farm around waiting for new fertilized Roe. Expansion requires obtaining more fish from the supplier.

### Electric Eel: fish that help supply Energy

Electric Eel are also carnivores that do not breed in captivity.

Their special advantage is that living, adequately fed Electric Eel can generate Energy for the RAS and compatible nearby batteries or machines.

The amount increases as they mature. Under the ordinary stage rules, the output can reach approximately **3 kJ per night per adult Electric Eel**.

![An Electric Eel tooltip showing its carnivorous diet, 2,000 mL water requirement, and 3 kJ of nightly power generation.](/images/probably-stolen/aquaculture-electric-eel-power.webp)

Several Electric Eel can contribute to the same system.

This is useful when battery maintenance is a bottleneck, but Energy generation does not eliminate their food and water requirements.

Electric Eel are not free generators. They need meat, tank space, and enough water to remain alive.

### Sturgeon: the Caviar producer

Sturgeon are large carnivorous fish.

They do not breed in captivity, and the Sturgeon available for this system are female.

Their value comes from producing eggs that can be harvested as **Caviar**.

An adult Sturgeon requires approximately **3,000 mL** of water, much more than an adult Tilapia.

Do not move several Sturgeon into a small mixed tank without checking how much water and meat the system can support.

<section id="sturgeon-caviar">

## How Do You Unlock Sturgeon and Farm Caviar?

To obtain Sturgeon, purchase **Aquaculture II** through Joe Wild's Network after unlocking the first Aquaculture upgrade.

Aquaculture II has a base cost of **1,200 Credits** and expands the Retired Aquafarmer's stock to include Eel, Electric Eel, and Sturgeon.

The upgrade does not automatically give you a Sturgeon. It provides access to the supplier's advanced fish selection.

### Step 1: Buy a Sturgeon and prepare its tank

Before purchasing, prepare:

- A working RAS with sufficient water.
- A dependable Energy source.
- A meat supply for feeding.
- Enough free inventory space for the Sturgeon as it grows.

Sturgeon are much larger than Tilapia and need more water in adulthood.

If your existing tank already contains breeding fish, consider keeping Sturgeon in a separate system.

This also prevents their meat-only diet from complicating the feeding of an omnivorous Tilapia population.

### Step 2: Grow the Sturgeon and watch its eggs

As Sturgeon mature, they begin accumulating eggs.

Egg production requires adequate Health. Under the normal rules, a Sturgeon must have at least **90% of its maximum Health** for egg growth to continue.

When the fish reaches its egg capacity, its status can show **Gravid**.

![An adult Sturgeon displaying the Gravid status and accumulated eggs before Caviar harvesting.](/images/probably-stolen/aquaculture-sturgeon-gravid.webp)

An adult Sturgeon can hold approximately **525 g of eggs** before reaching its adult-stage limit. Elder Sturgeon have a larger potential egg capacity.

Do not confuse egg accumulation with natural reproduction. You cannot create a new Sturgeon breeding population by keeping two Sturgeon together.

### Step 3: Harvest Caviar with a Surgery Toolset

A **Surgery Toolset** lets you take eggs from a living Sturgeon without immediately killing it.

To use it:

1. Wait until the fish has accumulated enough eggs for a harvest.
2. Confirm its Health is high enough to survive the procedure.
3. Use the Surgery Toolset on the living Sturgeon.
4. Collect the resulting Caviar or Sturgeon Roe.
5. Allow the fish to recover before attempting another harvest.

![A Sturgeon beside harvested Caviar, with the finished Caviar item showing a base value of 240 Credits.](/images/probably-stolen/aquaculture-sturgeon-surgery-harvest.webp)

The procedure damages the Sturgeon.

Its eggs will not resume growing until its Health returns to at least 90%.

A full **Gravid** status is useful when you want a larger harvest, but an operation can be possible earlier once enough eggs are available.

The exact product from repeated surgeries also changes over the fish's lifetime. Early full jars yield Caviar, while later surgical harvests can produce Sturgeon Roe instead.

Do not assume one Sturgeon will provide the same number of Caviar items forever.

### Surgery Toolset vs Kitchen Knife: which is better?

You have two different harvesting choices.

**Surgery Toolset**

- Keeps the Sturgeon alive if the procedure succeeds.
- Allows it to recover and grow more eggs.
- Causes Health damage.
- Repeated operations can change the mix of Caviar and Sturgeon Roe produced.

**Kitchen Knife**

- Kills the Sturgeon.
- Allows its meat to be harvested.
- Converts the full egg jars available at slaughter into Caviar.
- Ends all future production from that fish.

For ongoing Caviar production, surgery is usually the more sustainable approach while the Sturgeon is healthy and has enough remaining life to produce more eggs.

Slaughter becomes more attractive when the fish is nearing the end of its useful life or you need its meat and current eggs immediately.

### Where can you find a Surgery Toolset?

Surgery Toolsets can appear among medical goods sold by certain Thieves or Scavengers.

They can also appear in a **Medical Bay Supply Crate**.

If you obtain a locked Medical crate but cannot open it, see the [Keycards Guide](/probably-stolen/keycards) for the required access.

You only need a usable Toolset to perform the operation; you do not need to kill a Sturgeon each time you want Caviar.

<section id="auto-butcher">

## How Do You Set Up Auto Butcher Without Killing Your Breeding Fish?

The **Auto Butcher** can harvest fish according to the settings you choose.

Its most useful feature for Aquaculture is the ability to keep selected breeding fish alive while processing surplus animals.

A fully automatic farm still needs a deliberate breeding population.

### Protect your breeding fish first

Use a **Marking Pen** on fish you want to retain.

The Auto Butcher does not select marked animals for processing.

This is especially important for:

- Adult males needed for fertilized Roe.
- Healthy adult females needed for egg production.
- Valuable Sturgeon you intend to harvest surgically.
- Electric Eel you want to keep for Energy generation.

Marking prevents the Auto Butcher from selecting those animals. It does **not** protect them from starvation, water shortages, old age, or predation by other hungry fish.

### Set the butcher's harvesting rules

Open the Auto Butcher settings attached to the fish tank.

Its available options include age or weight thresholds, breeding-stock retention, carcass processing, and Roe collection.

![Auto Butcher settings showing fish retention, minimum age, and Roe collection controls in an established Aquaculture farm.](/images/probably-stolen/aquaculture-auto-butcher-settings.webp)

For a breeding-focused Gutter Tilapia tank:

1. Keep at least one suitable adult male.
2. Keep several healthy adult females.
3. Mark valuable breeding fish if necessary.
4. Set the butcher to harvest surplus fish after they reach your chosen age or weight.
5. Check the output inventory before the next overnight cycle.

An established three-tank operation retained **one male and three females** in its Gutter Tilapia breeding population while harvesting surplus older fish.

Those numbers are a practical example, not a compulsory game requirement.

### How do you collect Graft Tilapia Roe automatically?

For a Graft Tilapia production tank, you can prioritize **Unfertilized Roe** rather than breeding more fish.

A practical setup uses adult females without a male breeding population and enables the Auto Butcher's Roe collection option.

Collect Roe as it appears while retaining enough healthy females for future production.

The Auto Butcher's Roe collection control is separate from fish harvesting. You do not need to slaughter the egg-laying fish merely to collect their unfertilized Roe.

### Why is my Auto Butcher destroying useful fish?

Check its keeper rules, harvesting threshold, and which fish you marked.

A common mistake is assuming the machine knows which fish you intend to breed.

If you selected aggressive harvesting settings and did not mark the best animals, the butcher may remove valuable fish once they meet its conditions.

Also inspect the butcher's output tray. Items that cannot fit in the available output space can be lost.

Do not configure a fully automatic meat farm until you have protected the animals that maintain its production.

<section id="three-tank-farm">

## What Is a Good Three-Tank Aquaculture Setup?

A practical late-game setup separates inexpensive fish, medical fish, and high-value carnivores.

This avoids competing food requirements and makes it easier to control breeding and harvesting.

### Tank 1: Gutter Tilapia for cheap meat

Use this tank to maintain a renewable source of fish meat.

A working example uses:

- Gutter Tilapia as the main species.
- Gutterflow Pellets for inexpensive feeding.
- One male and several females retained as breeders.
- An Auto Butcher for older surplus fish.
- Enough filters, water, and Energy to support the population.

Gutter Tilapia tolerate the special feed without the ordinary Trash Grade penalty.

Instead of selling every fish fillet, reserve part of the harvest for Tank 3.

If you need another generation, retain fertilized Roe until it develops into replacement fish.

### Tank 2: Graft Tilapia for medical products

Use this tank for Graft Tilapia Roe and Tilapia Skin Grafts.

A demonstrated high-output setup retained **nine female Graft Tilapia**, with no males.

![Graft Tilapia Auto Butcher setup retaining nine females and zero males, with Unfertilized Roe available for collection.](/images/probably-stolen/aquaculture-graft-tilapia-setup.webp)

Its purpose was to produce unfertilized Roe while periodically harvesting excess older fish.

Feed them with suitable food, maintain filtration, and collect the medical products.

Nine females are not automatically the right number for every shop. The tank must still have enough water and its Organic Waste must remain under control.

If water or Energy is scarce, start with fewer fish.

### Tank 3: Sturgeon and Eel for high-value output

The third tank receives meat from the cheaper fish farms.

A working production loop is:

1. Harvest surplus Gutter and Graft Tilapia.
2. Keep the resulting fish meat instead of selling all of it.
3. Put suitable meat into the Tank 3 Feeding Dispenser.
4. Maintain your Sturgeon and any Eel or Electric Eel.
5. Harvest Caviar with a Surgery Toolset when the Sturgeon is ready.
6. Allow the Sturgeon to recover before its next egg-producing cycle.

Sturgeon do not naturally replenish themselves through breeding, so you must account for replacement purchases from the Retired Aquafarmer.

Electric Eel may help with Energy, but they consume meat and water that could otherwise support additional Sturgeon.

If your main objective is Caviar, you can eventually dedicate this tank entirely to Sturgeon rather than keeping Eel alongside them.

### Should You Sell Fish Meat or Feed It to Sturgeon?

If your goal is Caviar production, do not automatically sell every Gutter Tilapia fillet.

Keep enough fish meat to feed your Sturgeon through the next overnight cycles. A hungry Sturgeon can lose Health, delaying egg production and making the next Surgery Toolset harvest less useful.

Sell surplus fillets when the carnivore tank already has enough stored calories. If your Sturgeon are repeatedly running short of food, increase Tilapia production or reduce the number of carnivores before purchasing more expensive fish.

Graft Tilapia offer a separate choice: collect their Unfertilized Roe for medical buyers while keeping enough healthy females for continued production.

## What Should You Do After Your First Successful Fish Farm?

Once your first Tilapia survive and reproduce, decide what is limiting your next expansion.

If the tank is repeatedly showing **Low Water**, reduce its population or prepare another RAS before buying more fish.

If the fish are growing slowly or not reproducing, fix Hunger and protect the adult breeding pair before expanding the farm.

If you want a useful low-cost feeding supply, move toward Gutter Tilapia. If you want medical products, add Graft Tilapia. If you have stable water, Energy, and a meat supply, unlock Aquaculture II and begin raising Sturgeon for Caviar.

For an established farm, your goal is not simply to own more fish. It is to keep the breeding fish alive, harvest the surplus, and produce enough suitable food for the animals that generate your most valuable products.
