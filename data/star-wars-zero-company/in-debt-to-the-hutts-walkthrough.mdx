<h2 id="how-to-pay-80000-credit-debt">
  You do not need to earn 80,000 Credits yourself
</h2>

The **80,000-Credit** number is intimidating because almost everything Zero
Company earns at this point is tiny beside it.

That is what initially sent me in the wrong direction.

My instinct was to stop spending, look for the highest-paying optional work,
and somehow turn nine Cycles into enough money to satisfy Gorga.

The payouts quickly showed how unrealistic that plan was.

On my route:

* an early Gorga Operation paid **500 Credits**;
* the Lothal Tactical Mission paid **1,000 Credits**;
* my Kaller job later paid **1,200 Credits**.

That is **2,700 Credits** from three useful steps — only a small fraction of an
80,000-Credit debt.

So I stopped treating In Debt to the Hutts as an economy challenge.

The money displayed on the Holo Table is real.

The solution is story progression.

The route I follow is:

**Bruckner → Lothal → Pick Your Poison → selected Tactical Mission → Republic
Intelligence contract → Payback.**

The nine-Cycle timer changes what I prioritize, but it does not make every
Credit sacred.

I still buy something when it meaningfully improves the next deployment. What
I avoid is wasting money on a marginal purchase while the squad has a more
important gap to fill.

More importantly, I do not spend Cycles farming unrelated jobs because I think
the counter itself needs to reach 80,000.

What I need is the next Critical step.

<h2 id="gorga-critical-chain">
  During the debt chapter, I protect Intel before I protect Credits
</h2>

The first resource that can actually slow me down is **Intel**.

Gorga's early work around Bruckner can ask for **50 Intel**.

At one point I had **100 Intel**, which meant a single unnecessary 50-Intel
Operation could consume half of what I had available for the progression I
actually wanted.

That changes how I read the Holo Table.

If a Critical Gorga Operation is waiting, I give it first claim on the Intel
pool.

I can come back to a low-priority optional Operation later if the schedule
allows it.

I cannot make a Critical window less urgent by earning another 500 Credits.

There is also an important timing distinction here.

Resolving an **Operation** does not use a Cycle in the same way launching a
Tactical Mission does.

So when the next Gorga step is already sitting on the Holo Table and I have the
Intel for it, I resolve it rather than leaving it untouched while I go looking
for something else to do.

If Operations, Tactical Missions, and Cycle timing are still getting mixed
together early in the campaign, I keep the broader system explanation in my

<Link href="/star-wars-zero-company/beginner-guide">
  {" "}Zero Company beginner guide
</Link>
.

For this chapter, I only need one habit:

**check the Critical Operations before I spend Intel elsewhere.**

### Bruckner is not a hidden fail-state for the debt route

The Bruckner situation eventually gives me a choice between letting him
disappear and handing him over to Gorga.

I chose to let him disappear on my first route.

The immediate reward was useful while Zero Company was still short on money,
but that decision is not what pays the 80,000 Credits.

More importantly, I can continue toward Lothal without needing one specific
Bruckner outcome.

That keeps me from wasting time reloading because I am afraid I picked the
"wrong" debt route.

The actual reward and Influence tradeoff belongs in my

<Link href="/star-wars-zero-company/choices-consequences">
  {" "}choices and consequences guide
</Link>
.

Here, the only thing I care about is progression:

**resolve Bruckner → move on to Lothal.**

<h2 id="lothal-next-step">
  Lothal is the first Tactical Mission I do not postpone for extra farming
</h2>

After Bruckner, Gorga sends Zero Company to an abandoned settlement on
**Lothal** to deal with pirates raiding his smuggling warehouses.

This is where I stop thinking about squeezing another optional payout into the
schedule.

I launch Lothal once the squad is ready enough to complete it.

My completion rewards were:

* **1,000 Credits**
* **100 Intel**
* **2 Focus Points for every Zero Company Operator**
* **Crew Quarters**

The 1,000 Credits are useful.

The **100 Intel** is more important to how I route the next stage because it
gives me room to keep interacting with Operations.

The Focus Points strengthen the roster, while Crew Quarters pushes the company
forward as well.

That is why I do not look at Lothal and think:

> only 1,000 Credits toward 80,000.

The Mission gives me progression, resources, and access to what comes next.

That is the real value.

<h2 id="pick-your-poison-debt">
  Kaller or Dantooine does not change how I solve the debt
</h2>

After Lothal, Gorga presents **Pick Your Poison**.

The choice sends me toward either:

**Kaller**

or

**Dantooine**.

This is another point where I can accidentally turn a straightforward
progression step into a much bigger decision than it needs to be.

For the debt route, I do not need a special "money" answer.

Both routes continue Gorga's sequence.

I chose **Kaller**.

My Kaller completion paid **1,200 Credits**, along with other rewards, but even
that payout reinforces the same lesson: I am not supposed to stack ordinary
Mission rewards until the debt counter reaches 80,000.

I finish the selected Tactical Mission because I need to move the story
forward.

The exact Kaller-versus-Dantooine reward tradeoff stays in my

<Link href="/star-wars-zero-company/choices-consequences">
  {" "}choices and consequences guide
</Link>
.

For In Debt to the Hutts, the useful answer is simpler:

**pick the route you want, complete it, and return to the Den.**

That return is what finally changes the financial problem.

<h2 id="republic-intelligence-contract">
  Republic Intelligence is the point where the 80,000-Credit problem disappears
</h2>

After the Gorga work moves far enough, **Jae** returns to the Den and the
mysterious employer behind the earlier Serelonus job is finally brought into
the open.

She introduces **Lieutenant Howerin of Republic Intelligence**.

The Republic is investigating the **Infinite Coil** and wants Zero Company on
the job.

That new contract is the part of the debt chapter I had been waiting for
without knowing it.

The payment combines money still owed for the earlier work with an advance on
the Republic Intelligence contract.

It is enough to repay Gorga **and still leave Zero Company with money left
over**.

This is also why I do not freeze Zero Company's entire economy during those
nine Cycles.

I still avoid waste.

But if spending Credits fixes a real problem — an Operator needs treatment, a
facility matters now, or an upgrade materially improves the squad I am about to
deploy — I do not reject it solely because Gorga's 80,000 is still displayed.

Hoarding every Credit does not unlock the Republic contract faster.

Advancing the right work does.

<h2 id="payback">
  When Payback appears, I do it before another Tactical Mission
</h2>

Receiving the money is not quite the end.

I still need to repay Gorga.

Back at the Holo Table, **Payback** appears as an Operation.

On my route it showed:

**0 Intel**

and

**1 Cycle remaining**.

This is one of the few points in the chapter where I do not overthink the Holo
Table.

Payback is already available.

It costs me no Intel on this route.

Resolving the Operation itself does not consume another Cycle.

So I do it.

I do **not** launch a Tactical Mission first just because another reward looks
interesting. A Tactical Mission can move the campaign forward, while Payback
is sitting there with a visible one-Cycle window.

Once the Operation resolves, Zero Company repays Gorga in full.

The financial problem that dominated the opening Cycles is over.

From this point, I stop building my schedule around Hutt debt.

The new center of the campaign is **Republic Intelligence, the Infinite Coil,
and the next Critical Missions**.
