<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
If an Operator has **one Injury**, I check the next Holo Table deadline before
using them again. At **two Injuries**, I normally send them to the **MedBay**;
with Permadeath enabled, a **third Injury kills them permanently**.

A standard MedBay bed takes **one Cycle**. The Bacta Tank heals instantly when
it is available, but **Expert disables it**.

And if I am trying to save **Aurelio** in the tutorial, I stop reloading:
his death is scripted and cannot be prevented.

<h2 id="can-operators-die">
  Operators can die, and two Injuries is where I stop gambling
</h2>

An Operator reaching zero Health is not automatically dead.

They first become **Downed**.

If another Operator can reach them, **Rally** gets them back into the current
fight. That is the part that initially made the system look more forgiving
than it really is.

The problem is what follows the knockdown.

A normal Downed Operator receives an **Injury**, and Injuries can stack.

With Permadeath enabled:

**1 Injury** still leaves me room to make a decision.

**2 Injuries** means I am one bad knockdown away from losing that Operator.

**3 Injuries** means **Killed in Action**.

That distinction matters even after Rally restores the Operator to a usable
state. Getting them off the floor does not erase the Injury they just received.

This changed how I finish fights.

If somebody takes their first Injury and I have the encounter under control, I
do not panic. I Rally them, move them back toward useful cover, and finish the
objective without treating them like the squad's disposable front line.

At two Injuries, I play differently.

That Operator is no longer the person I send across open ground for a marginal
flank, leave alone on an exposed objective, or push forward just to squeeze out
one more attack.

If extraction is available, getting them home is worth more to me than clearing
the last unnecessary enemy.

Injuries also hurt combat performance before they become lethal. An injured
Operator can suffer a **Chance-to-Hit penalty**, so restored Health does not
mean the character is back to normal.

There are ways to soften that problem. Effects such as Morale can let an
Operator ignore the Injury hit-chance penalty while active.

I still prefer removing the Injury when the campaign gives me room.

Ignoring the penalty helps me survive a deployment.

Treating the Injury removes the step toward permanent death.

<h2 id="can-you-save-aurelio">
  No, Aurelio cannot be saved in the tutorial
</h2>

Aurelio is the first place where the Injury system looks like a puzzle I might
be able to solve differently.

I tried treating him like an Operator I needed to protect.

During the opening mission he goes Downed, and Hawks can Rally him. At that
point the tutorial explicitly shows Aurelio sitting at **two Injuries** and
warns that another one will kill an Operator when Permadeath is active.

My response was exactly what I would use later in the campaign:

keep him in cover, stop taking unnecessary melee risks, use Overwatch instead
of forcing bad movement, and get the squad toward extraction.

It does not save him.

Aurelio's death is part of the tutorial sequence. I cannot route the fight
differently, heal him before the trigger, or reload until I find a version
where he becomes a permanent member of Zero Company.

He dies after the opening job regardless of the normal Permadeath setting.

The game then uses that loss to introduce the systems I will actually control
for everyone who comes afterward.

When I reach the Den, Aurelio already has a place in the **Memorial**. His entry
records that he was lost in **Cycle 1**, along with information about his death
and what he accomplished during his short time with the company.

So if I am replaying the opening because I think the MedBay, Rally timing, or a
different combat route can keep Aurelio:

**I continue the campaign instead.**

His death is not evidence that I failed the tutorial.

<h2 id="when-to-heal-injuries">
  I decide whether to heal before I spend the next Cycle
</h2>

For everybody after Aurelio, the Injury system becomes a real roster decision.

I do not automatically send every Operator with one Injury to bed.

I return to the **Den**, check the injured character, then look at the Holo
Table before I spend Credits or remove somebody from the available roster.

With one Injury, I ask what is expiring.

If the next important Mission can wait and another Operator can cover the same
job, I heal immediately.

If a Critical Mission has little time left and the injured Operator fills a
role I cannot replace, I may take one Injury into another deployment.

That is usually the most risk I accept deliberately.

At two Injuries, I need an exceptional reason not to stop.

Early in the campaign I had already seen how quickly a bad turn could move one
Operator from recoverable to dangerous. Aurelio goes from being Rallied at two
Injuries to permanently gone before the tutorial is finished.

I do not need that lesson twice.

There is another trap here: leaving an injured Operator out of the combat squad
does not necessarily make them safe.

Some **Operations** ask me to assign Operators and carry their own risk of
Injury.

Before I send a hurt character out because "they are not doing a Tactical
Mission anyway," I read the Operation first.

If the Operator already has two Injuries, I normally use somebody healthier.

Later, **MedBay Networking** makes this easier to manage by reducing the
**chance of Injuries on Dangerous Operations by 20%**.

That is an upgrade I value more once my roster is large enough that Operators
are regularly being sent to different jobs at the same time.

It reduces risk.

It does not make a two-Injury Operator somebody I casually volunteer for every
Dangerous Operation.

<h2 id="heal-injuries-medbay">
  How I use the MedBay without losing track of the next deadline
</h2>

To remove a normal Injury, I return to the **Den** and use the **MedBay**.

A standard bed requires **one Cycle**.

In my early campaign, putting an injured Operator into a bed cost **800
Credits**.

The Credit cost is rarely what makes me hesitate.

The **Cycle** is.

Putting an Operator into the bed starts treatment; it does not immediately
finish it.

The first time I used the MedBay, it was easy to look at the "one Cycle"
requirement, resolve something else, and wonder why the Operator was still
injured.

I now plan around the campaign clock instead of treating the bed like a combat
Medpack.

My usual order is:

**check the Holo Table → identify what expires first → put the injured Operator
into treatment → build the next deployment without them → advance the Cycle →
check the MedBay again.**

That order matters when several things are competing for the same stretch of
campaign time.

If an optional job has three Cycles left but something important expires in
one, I do not burn the short window just because I want my preferred four-person
squad back together.

I rotate.

This is also why I stopped building Zero Company around four irreplaceable
Operators.

If losing one person to a bed for a Cycle destroys my entire tactical plan, the
roster is too narrow.

<h2 id="bacta-tank">
  I save instant Bacta recovery for when one Cycle actually matters
</h2>

The **Bacta Tank** removes the waiting problem.

When it is available, it provides **instant Injury recovery** instead of tying
the Operator up for a full Cycle.

I do not use it simply because I dislike seeing an Injury icon.

I care about it when the campaign gives me a reason to need that specific
Operator immediately.

A Critical Mission close to expiry is the obvious example.

If my strongest support character is injured, I have no equivalent replacement,
and waiting one Cycle would cost me the mission window, instant treatment can
be worth far more than preserving the resources.

The opposite situation is common too.

If the Holo Table is quiet and I have a healthy substitute, I am perfectly
happy using a normal bed and letting the Operator sit out.

There is one major exception:

**Expert disables the Bacta Tank.**

That turns Injuries into a much bigger roster-management problem because I
cannot keep recycling the same core squad through instant recovery.

On Expert, I assume bed rest and rotation are part of the campaign rather than
something I can avoid with enough Credits.

The rest of Expert's changes — including reduced effective weapon range, lower
starting Bonds, and how Beskar fits into the run — stay in my
<Link href="/star-wars-zero-company/difficulty-settings">
  {" "}difficulty and Beskar guide
</Link>
.

For this page, the important consequence is simple: **an Injury can cost me a
Cycle even when I can afford treatment.**

<h2 id="trick-first-down">
  Trick is the Operator I am willing to expose once
</h2>

Trick breaks the normal Downed pattern in a way that actually changes how I use
him.

His **For My Brothers** talent gives him a built-in recovery when he goes down.

The base version can revive him automatically rather than making me spend
another Operator's turn reaching him.

With investment, I can make that safety net much stronger.

The version I use can bring Trick back at **full Health**.

Another upgrade makes the first Down even better:

if Trick is Downed on his turn, he can **revive immediately**, recover **1
Action Point**, and **does not receive an Injury the first time he is Downed in
that Mission**.

This is one reason I like putting Trick in a role where survivability matters.

It also stops me from applying the same Injury rule mechanically to every
member of the squad.

With most Operators, a Down means I am already thinking about what happened to
their Injury count.

With Trick, I first ask whether **For My Brothers has already triggered this
Mission**.

If it has not, I have a safety net.

If it has, I stop pretending he is special and go back to protecting him like
everyone else.

The protection is valuable precisely because it is limited.

I do not turn Trick into bait for the rest of the battle just because the first
mistake was free.

<h2 id="permadeath-vs-beskar">
  Permadeath decides who can be lost; Beskar decides how easily I can undo it
</h2>

I keep **Permadeath** and **Beskar Mode** separate because they solve different
questions.

Permadeath determines whether ordinary Operators can be permanently killed by
the Injury system.

It is enabled by default, and the choice is made when I begin the campaign.

With it on, accumulating three Injuries can permanently remove an Operator.

With it off, I am not playing under that normal three-Injury death rule.

Aurelio is the exception. His tutorial death still happens because it belongs
to the opening story rather than the normal roster-loss system.

Beskar is different.

Beskar controls the save structure and makes campaign consequences much harder
to undo. The full one-save rules belong in the
<Link href="/star-wars-zero-company/difficulty-settings">
  {" "}difficulty and Beskar guide
</Link>
rather than here.

For Injury management, the distinction I care about is this:

with normal saves, I still have a fallback after a disastrous result.

With Beskar, losing somebody is much more likely to become part of the campaign
I have to continue playing.

That makes the two-Injury threshold even more important.

<h2 id="permanent-operator-loss">
  Losing an Operator costs more than their specialization
</h2>

The **Memorial** is what made permanent death feel different from simply
recruiting another unit.

It keeps a record of fallen members of Zero Company, including their service,
accomplishments, and where they died.

The gameplay cost is larger than losing a class slot.

By the time I care about an Operator, I may already have put Focus Points into
their abilities, equipped good gear, built Bonds around them, chosen Cross
Training bonuses, and relied on their personal talent for a specific squad
role.

Recruitment can give me another body.

It does not instantly rebuild all of that.

There is also **A Natural Part of Life**, the achievement for losing a member
of Zero Company.

I do not sacrifice an important story Operator early just to force it.

If somebody dies naturally, the requirement takes care of itself. If my roster
survives, I leave deliberate achievement cleanup until I know what I am giving
up.

The broader completion route stays in my
<Link href="/star-wars-zero-company/achievements-trophies">
  {" "}achievements and trophies guide
</Link>
.

For a normal campaign, I use a much simpler rule:

**one Injury makes me check the schedule; two send me toward treatment; the
third is not a roll I take with an Operator I want to keep.**
