<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
When one of my Operators comes back injured, I return to the **Den**, go to the
**MedBay**, and assign them to treatment before the injuries stack too high.

A standard bed takes **one Cycle**. If an Operator already has **two injuries**,
I stop deploying them unless I am deliberately accepting the risk of losing
them. The **third injury kills an Operator when Permadeath is enabled**.

Rally only gets a downed Operator back into the current fight. It does not erase
the injury they already suffered.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

<h2 id="how-injuries-work">
  What actually happens when an Operator goes down
</h2>

The first thing that confused me was the difference between being **Downed** and
being dead.

When an Operator loses all Health, they become Downed and cannot keep taking
normal actions.

Going Downed is still recoverable at this point, provided I can get another
Operator close enough to Rally them.

I can use **Rally** to bring them back into the current fight.

The dangerous part is what comes with the knockdown:

**the Operator suffers an injury.**

That is why I no longer judge a bad turn only by how much Health I lost.

An Operator who gets back up may still carry the consequence into every battle
afterward.

The game allows injuries to stack.

With Permadeath enabled, the important limit is:

**first injury → second injury → third injury = killed in action.**

So when somebody is sitting on two injuries, I do not look at their restored
Health bar and tell myself they are safe again.

They are one more bad knockdown away from being gone.

The danger starts before the third Injury.

An injured Operator also carries a **Chance-to-Hit penalty**, so bringing the
same character straight back into another Tactical Mission can make their
shots less reliable even if their Health has been restored.

I notice this most when an Operator who would normally have a comfortable shot
starts showing worse percentages from the same kind of position.

That gives me two reasons to treat injuries:

**survival and combat reliability.**

Some talents and effects can suppress the Injury accuracy penalty. **Fortitude**
can eventually let an injured Operator ignore it, and Medic support can create
similar breathing room.

I still prefer clearing the Injury when the schedule allows. Ignoring the
penalty is useful during an emergency; removing the Injury also removes the
progress toward permanent death.

<h2 id="rally-vs-healing">
  Rally saves the turn, not the injury
</h2>

Rally is extremely useful in combat, but I do not confuse it with treatment.

If an Operator drops during a fight, Rally can get them acting again instead
of leaving them helpless on the ground.

Rally gets that Operator moving again, which may be exactly what I need to
finish the objective or escape a bad position.

Back at the Den, however, the Injury is still waiting for me. I always check
the roster after a Mission where somebody went down, even if the Rally made
the rest of the fight look completely normal.

That distinction changes how aggressively I play after the first knockdown.

If somebody with no previous injuries gets Rallied, I can still finish the
Mission normally if the situation is under control.

If somebody entered the fight with an existing injury and gets knocked down
again, I become much more conservative.

And if they are now sitting at **two injuries**, I stop treating them as the
person who should cross open ground, hold an exposed objective, or absorb
another risky attack.

Finishing the current Mission matters.

Keeping the Operator alive long enough to reach the MedBay matters more.

<h2 id="operation-injuries">
  I check Operation risk before sending an injured Operator
</h2>

Combat is not the only place where I can come back with another injury.

Some **Operations** can put the assigned Operator at risk even though I never
enter a Tactical Mission.

That changed how I use characters who are already hurt.

If somebody is carrying an Injury, I check the Operation's risk and possible
outcomes before assigning them simply because they are sitting out my next
combat squad.

An Operator on the bench is not automatically a safe Operator.

When the Operation looks risky and that character is already close to the
injury limit, I use somebody healthier or leave the job until I have a safer
option.

After resolving an Operation, I check the returning Operator before launching
another job. If another Injury appeared, the MedBay becomes part of my next
decision immediately.

<h2 id="heal-injuries-medbay">
  How I heal injuries at the MedBay
</h2>

After the Mission, I return to the **Den** and go to the **MedBay**.

A standard MedBay bed removes injuries through treatment, but it is not
instant.

The standard bed requires **one Cycle**.

Early in my campaign, assigning an Operator to one of these beds cost me
**800 Credits**.

I do not automatically pay for every minor injury the second I see one.

My decision depends on the next job.

With **one injury**, I check the Holotable first.

If the next important objective is safe to postpone and I have another Operator
who can cover the role, I prefer treating the injury now.

If an important objective is about to expire and the injured Operator is
genuinely difficult to replace, I may carry one injury into another Mission.

That is a risk decision, not a free decision.

At **two injuries**, my threshold changes completely.

Unless the campaign leaves me no reasonable alternative, I put that Operator
into treatment.

An 800-Credit bill hurts early.

Losing a story Operator, Bond partner, or highly developed squad member hurts
much more.

my

</Link>
, but the injury decision itself is simple:

**I check the next deadline before I commit the Operator to another fight.**

<h2 id="medbay-one-cycle">
  Why my Operator is still injured after I put them in the MedBay
</h2>

This is the part that made the MedBay feel broken the first time I used it.

I assigned an Operator to a standard bed.

The interface said treatment needed **one Cycle**.

Then the Operator was still injured.

The important distinction is that putting someone in bed does not instantly
complete treatment.

I have started the recovery.

I still need the campaign to advance that Cycle.

I also do not expect resolving another Holotable **Operation** to act like a
day-skip for the MedBay.

Operations and Tactical Missions serve different purposes in the campaign. For
treatment planning, I care about when the actual Cycle advances.

That means my normal sequence is:

**return to the Den → assign the injured Operator to the MedBay → check the
Holotable timers → build the next squad without them → advance the campaign →
check the MedBay again.**

This is why roster depth matters much earlier than I expected.

If I only have one healer, one close-range Operator, or one reliable damage
dealer, putting that person into bed can suddenly make the next deployment much
harder.

I would rather recruit enough role coverage to survive one absence than keep
sending the same injured four Operators into every Tactical Mission.

<h2 id="bacta-tank">
  When I use the Bacta Tank instead of waiting a Cycle
</h2>

The MedBay also has a much faster answer.

The **Bacta Tank provides instant recovery** under normal rules.

That is the option I value when the Operator is important enough that losing
them for the next Cycle creates a real problem.

For example, if a Critical Mission is close to expiring and the squad member I
need is carrying dangerous injuries, instant recovery can be much more valuable
than preserving resources simply because treatment looks expensive.

I do not use it just because I dislike seeing an injury icon.

I use it when the alternative is:

**delay important progression**

or

**deploy an Operator who is already too close to permanent death.**

There is one major limitation:

**the Bacta Tank is disabled on Expert.**

That makes standard bed treatment and a deeper roster much more important on
Expert, because I cannot plan around instant recovery saving the same core squad
every time.

On Expert, losing that instant recovery option changes how aggressively I can
reuse the same core squad.

If I want the full set of changes — reduced effective weapon range, tougher
enemies, lower starting Bonds, Beskar Mode, and how the difficulty choices fit
together — I use my
<Link href="/star-wars-zero-company/difficulty-settings">
  {" "}Zero Company difficulty settings guide
</Link>
.

For injury management, the consequence I plan around is straightforward:
**Expert leaves me relying much more heavily on bed rest and roster rotation.**

<h2 id="trick-injury-exception">
  Trick can survive a first knockdown differently
</h2>

Trick is the first Operator who made me stop treating every Downed state as
identical.

His unique **For My Brothers** talent can automatically bring him back after he
is Downed for the first time in a Mission.

With further investment, that recovery becomes even stronger.

One version I used brought him back at full Health, and another upgrade prevents
him from receiving an injury from that first Down.

I still do not treat him as immortal.

The protection is tied to that first Downed event in the Mission.

Once I have already used the safety net, I go back to treating another
knockdown as a real injury risk.

<h2 id="permadeath">
  Permanent death removes more than one combat unit
</h2>

The first permanent loss hit harder than I expected because Zero Company
actually records it.

The **Memorial** keeps Operators who died with details from their service,
including their accomplishments and where they were lost.

The mechanical cost can be larger than replacing one body in the squad.

That Operator may already have:

- Focus investment;
- useful equipment;
- a role I do not have duplicated;
- developed Bonds;
- Cross Training progress;
- personal story progress;
- an achievement-related relationship.

That is why I do not judge permanent death only by whether Recruitment can give
me another character with the same specialization.

A replacement can fill a tactical role.

They do not automatically replace everything I had already built around the
person who died.

<h2 id="a-natural-part-of-life">
  I do not sacrifice a useful Operator early for A Natural Part of Life
</h2>

There is an achievement that complicates all of this.

**A Natural Part of Life** requires losing a member of Zero Company.

I do not deliberately force that during the first half of a story run.

If a death happens naturally, the requirement is taken care of.

If everybody survives, I leave it for a point where I understand what that
particular loss will remove from the campaign.

Killing off somebody who still matters to a Bond achievement, personal story,
or core squad plan just to tick one box can create several new cleanup
problems.

For the rest of the 53 requirements and which ones I let grow naturally, I keep
the full planning route in my
<Link href="/star-wars-zero-company/achievements-trophies">
  {" "}Zero Company achievements and trophies guide
</Link>

For normal campaign play, the rule I use is much simpler:

**one injury makes me check the schedule; two injuries send me toward the
MedBay; a third injury is not a risk I take casually.**
