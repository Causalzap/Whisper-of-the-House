<h2 id="where-to-start">
  The first-run rhythm that keeps my campaign from getting messy
</h2>

*STAR WARS Zero Company* became much easier to read once I stopped treating the
Holo Table like a checklist.

There is usually more available than I can comfortably do at once.

Critical work competes with Tactical Missions, Operations, Dilemmas, personal
stories, Zone rewards, recruitment, Injuries, equipment, and opportunities that
expire after only a few Cycles.

I do not try to make all of those equally important.

On a fresh campaign, I keep one simple rhythm:

**finish a meaningful Mission → return to the Den → inspect the roster and new
conversations → check what opened → read the Holo Table timers → decide whether
the story can wait.**

That return to the Den matters.

A Mission can leave an Operator hurt, open a facility, create a new
conversation, unlock recruitment, advance a Bond, or add another Critical step.

Launching the next Tactical Mission immediately can mean spending a Cycle
before I have even looked at what changed.

When nothing urgent has appeared, I use the spare room for useful optional
work.

That is where I build Credits, Intel, Influence, equipment, Bonds, roster
depth, and long-term achievement progress.

When a Critical timer is getting uncomfortable, I stop decorating the campaign
and move the story.

For the underlying rules around Cycles, Operations, Tactical Missions,
Influence, Recruitment, Bonds, and early company systems, I use my
<Link href="/star-wars-zero-company/beginner-guide">
  {" "}Zero Company beginner guide
</Link>

<h2 id="what-kind-of-run">
  I decide what kind of campaign I am playing before I optimize everything
</h2>

I get better results when I decide what I actually want from the run.

If I am mainly here for the campaign, characters, and tactical combat, I do not
need to route every Bond or Optional Mission around 100% completion from Cycle
one.

If I already know I want all achievements, I start several slow requirements
much earlier because some of them need an entire campaign to mature.

Difficulty is a separate decision.

For somebody still learning Zero Company's specific combat and management
systems, **Normal** gives enough room to understand mistakes.

If I already have a lot of turn-based tactical experience, **Hard** is the
first-run setting I prefer.

I save **Expert**, and especially **Expert + Beskar**, for a campaign where I
already understand which Missions create reinforcement pressure, when Injuries
become expensive, and which losses would actually hurt the roster.

The detailed difficulty differences stay in my
<Link href="/star-wars-zero-company/difficulty-settings">
  {" "}difficulty and Beskar guide
</Link>

I take the same approach with Hawks and the squad.

I do not need to discover a permanent endgame build during character creation.
Specializations can be reconsidered later, while named Operators gradually
develop through their talents, equipment, Bonds, and Cross Training.

When I need the actual class decisions rather than the broader campaign plan, I
use my
<Link href="/star-wars-zero-company/classes-specializations">
  {" "}classes and specializations guide
</Link>

<h2 id="campaign-shape">
  The story changes shape several times before Fathom is finally the only target left
</h2>

The campaign has **14 chapters**, but I do not experience them as fourteen
isolated boxes.

The opening is about a struggling mercenary company.

Gorga and the company's debt dominate the early schedule.

Then Republic Intelligence turns the **Infinite Coil** and **Kundri Fathom**
into the central threat.

Cortilius pushes the investigation deeper, Tel-Rea enters the story, and the
company eventually goes from following Coil leads to attacking Fathom's
stronghold on **Luunata**.

<h2 id="when-holotable-is-crowded">
  When the Holo Table is crowded, I spend the next Cycle on what is hardest to replace
</h2>

The difficult Holo Table decisions are usually not about identifying the
highest number.

They are about identifying what disappears if I wait.

A short Critical timer is hard to replace.

A personal story that can still sit for several Cycles is easier to postpone.

A useful Optional Mission may be worth doing if it also grows a Bond, earns
Influence, gives equipment, and advances a long achievement counter.

A low-value Operation is much less attractive if spending the Intel prevents me
from launching something important five minutes later.

I also look at the roster before I commit.

A Mission that looks easy can be expensive if the only Operator who makes my
current squad function is already injured or assigned elsewhere.

That is why I do not use a universal priority list such as:

**Critical > personal > optional > everything else.**

Critical progression usually gets first attention, but the real decision is
whether delaying something creates a problem I cannot cheaply undo.

The campaign is much more comfortable once I start thinking in those terms.

<h2 id="roadblocks">
  A few intimidating moments are much simpler once I know what they are asking from me
</h2>

Several parts of the campaign look like they need a hidden solution when they
first appear.

Most of them do not.

### The 80,000-Credit debt is not an early-game farming test

When Gorga puts **80,000 Credits** in front of me with a limited number of
Cycles remaining, I do not stop buying everything useful and try to earn the
entire amount from ordinary jobs.

I keep following his Critical progression until the campaign produces the
financial solution.

The full Bruckner → Lothal → Pick Your Poison → Republic Intelligence →
Payback route stays in my
<Link href="/star-wars-zero-company/in-debt-to-the-hutts-walkthrough">
  {" "}In Debt to the Hutts walkthrough
</Link>

### Meeting Tel-Rea is not the same thing as recruiting her

**Dark Waters** introduces Tel-Rea.

She does not become a normal roster member at that first meeting.

I continue the Cortilius progression rather than searching the Den for a missed
recruitment prompt.

Her route develops later through **Smuggler's Den**.

If Takali Canal, the underground path, Fathom, or the end of Dark Waters is the
actual obstacle, I use my
<Link href="/star-wars-zero-company/dark-waters-walkthrough">
  {" "}Dark Waters walkthrough
</Link>

If the problem is getting through the warehouse progression and actually
bringing Tel-Rea into Zero Company, I switch to my
<Link href="/star-wars-zero-company/smugglers-den-walkthrough">
  {" "}Smuggler's Den and Tel-Rea guide
</Link>

### Order 66 does not mean I permanently ruined Trick

Later, Trick becomes unavailable because of a major story event.

I do not search for a MedBay cure, a special item, or an earlier dialogue
choice that supposedly prevents it.

I keep following the Critical campaign.

Captain Rex eventually provides the thread that moves Trick's story forward.

<h2 id="roster-decisions">
  I build enough depth that one Injury or story absence does not define the whole run
</h2>

I like having a preferred squad.

I do not like needing that exact squad for every Mission.

Operators can be injured.

Some work temporarily commits them elsewhere.

The story itself can change availability.

And certain maps reward a different balance between movement, healing, ranged
pressure, melee, control, or objective play.

That makes redundancy useful.

I want another Operator who can keep the squad alive if my normal support is
unavailable.

I want somebody who can still pressure targets from range if a close-range
favorite is sitting in the MedBay.

I want enough roster depth that resting one valuable Operator for a Cycle does
not make me afraid to launch the next Mission.

When Injuries start stacking, I stop judging the character only by their
current Health bar.

The full Downed, Rally, MedBay, Bacta Tank, three-Injury death rule, Trick
exception, Aurelio, and permanent-loss route belongs in my
<Link href="/star-wars-zero-company/permadeath-injuries">
  {" "}injuries and Permadeath guide
</Link>

At the campaign level, the judgment is easier:

**if losing one Operator would break everything I can field, I need more roster
depth before I need another tiny damage upgrade.**

<h2 id="choices">
  I judge choices by what they change now before I worry about a perfect answer
</h2>

Zero Company gives me choices that can affect Credits, Intel, Influence, Bonds,
character reactions, and which immediate job I take.

I do not approach every dialogue box as though there is one secret canon answer
that produces a perfect campaign.

The useful question is usually more practical:

**what does my company need right now?**

Early on, 500 Credits can be meaningful.

Later, Influence, a Bond reaction, an Operator commitment, or preventing an
enemy upgrade can matter more.

That is also why I avoid reloading every choice simply because one Operator
looks unhappy with it.

Different characters do not always want the same thing.

The decisions where I actually want the reward and consequence comparison —
including **Kaller or Dantooine, Gorga's Revenge, Bruckner, resource Dilemmas,
and Crisis decisions** — stay in my
<Link href="/star-wars-zero-company/choices-consequences">
  {" "}choices and consequences guide
</Link>

For a first run, I let the broader campaign survive imperfect choices.

I save reload-heavy optimization for the decisions where I already know the
specific outcome matters to what I am trying to build.

<h2 id="after-story">
  Finishing the story and finishing the game are two different projects
</h2>

Reaching Fathom is not the same thing as completing everything Zero Company can
ask from a campaign.

The launch achievement list contains **53 achievements**.

Several are natural story or system unlocks.

Others need much more deliberate preparation across a long run: Optional
Missions, kills, roster size, Zone Rewards, Very High Bonds, Operator stories,
Astromechs, unusual combat setups, and the hardest difficulty completion.

That is why I decide early whether I am simply playing the story or trying to
turn the same campaign into a serious completion run.

If I want 100%, I let those slow counters grow while the campaign still has
useful Missions to give me.

I do not wait until Fathom is dead and then discover that I still need dozens
of Optional Missions or several relationships that barely started.

The complete route stays in my
<Link href="/star-wars-zero-company/achievements-trophies">
  {" "}53-achievement and trophy roadmap
</Link>

There is also **no New Game Plus at launch**, so I do not build the first
campaign around the assumption that every relationship, build decision, or
piece of progression will simply roll forward into NG+.

Once I reach the epilogue, I make a cleaner choice:

continue with this campaign for the unfinished goals that still make sense, or
start another run because I specifically want a different difficulty,
Permadeath/Beskar setup, roster plan, or story experience.

That is a much better reason for another campaign than saving every useful
decision because I expected an NG+ system that is not there.
