<h2 id="where-to-start">
  Where I would start in STAR WARS Zero Company
</h2>

The first few Cycles become much easier once I stop expecting to finish every
icon that appears on the Holotable.

There is always more competing for my attention than a normal linear quest log
would suggest: Critical work, Tactical Missions, Operations, injured Operators,
new conversations at the Den, Influence rewards, equipment, and optional
opportunities with their own deadlines.

On a fresh campaign, I give the **Critical story thread** a clear place at the
center of the schedule.

Around it, I fit optional work when the current timers give me room.

My usual post-Mission rhythm is:

**return to the Den → check the roster → talk to anyone with new dialogue →
look at available progression → open the Holotable → choose the next job.**

That short reset saves me from rushing directly into another deployment with a
damaged squad or missing something that just opened back at base.

For the systems behind Cycles, Operations, Tactical Missions, Influence,
Recruitment, Bonds, and early company management, I use my

  {" "}STAR WARS Zero Company beginner guide

<h2 id="choose-difficulty">
  I choose the campaign difficulty before worrying about a perfect build
</h2>

My first-run choice depends more on my experience with tactical games than on
whether I normally select the hardest option in an RPG.

**Normal** gives me room to learn the maps and systems.

**Hard** is where I would start if I already understand cover, positioning,
action economy, and the cost of leaving an Operator exposed.

Expert changes enough of the campaign that I treat it as a deliberate step up,
while Beskar adds a separate commitment to living with major mistakes.

The detailed differences between Story, Normal, Hard, Expert, Permadeath, and
Beskar belong in my
<Link href="/star-wars-zero-company/difficulty-settings">
  {" "}Zero Company difficulty settings guide
</Link>
.

Once that choice is made, I build for the campaign in front of me rather than
trying to predict an endgame squad during character creation.

<h2 id="campaign-structure">
  The Critical route carries me through a 14-chapter campaign
</h2>

The story spans **14 chapters**, but that does not mean one playthrough is a
fixed checklist of every Mission and Operation available.

The campaign begins with Zero Company taking private work and dealing with
Gorga, grows into the Republic Intelligence investigation of the Infinite Coil,
then pushes through Cortilius and Lunata before the political situation changes
the company completely.

<h2 id="build-company">
  I want a company that can survive one missing Operator
</h2>

My opening Hawks specialization matters, but it is not the decision I build the
entire campaign around.

Specializations can change.

Weapon choice is flexible.

Bonds and Cross Training gradually reshape Operators.

Named characters also bring their own talents, which can matter more than the
class label I gave them several Missions earlier.

That makes the roster itself more important than finding one supposedly perfect
four-person lineup.

If my healer is unavailable, I need another way to keep the next squad alive.

If a close-range Operator is hurt, somebody else has to work around the
objective.

Story progression can also change availability later, so having one usable
replacement is valuable even during a run where my preferred four are already
performing well.

My starting recommendations, all eight specializations, weapon fit, Focus
Trees, and Cross Training are in the
<Link href="/star-wars-zero-company/classes-specializations">

<h2 id="injuries-permadeath">
  I deal with injuries before they turn into a roster crisis
</h2>

An Operator surviving a Mission does not necessarily mean the problem is over.

Once injuries begin stacking, I check the MedBay and the next Holotable
deadline before deciding whether that same character should deploy again.

The full Downed, Rally, Medical Bed, Bacta Tank, Trick, Memorial, and permanent
loss rules are in my
<Link href="/star-wars-zero-company/permadeath-injuries">
  {" "}Permadeath and injuries guide
</Link>
.

My practical rule is simple:

**an injured favorite is still replaceable for one Mission; a permanently lost
favorite is not.**

That is why I recruit for coverage rather than assuming the same four Operators
will always be ready.

<h2 id="choices-consequences">
  I read choices for the immediate cost, not for a hidden ending meter
</h2>

Dialogue and Operation choices can change more than flavor text.

I have seen decisions alter **Credits, Intel, Influence, Bonds, and character
reactions**, and those short-term consequences can matter immediately when the
company is already short on a resource or trying to strengthen a relationship.

Brookner is an early example of the pattern.

Different responses can change the immediate reward without sending the entire
campaign onto a completely different story branch.

That is how I approach later choices as well.

Before confirming one, I check what the company needs at that moment and which
people are likely to care about the decision.

I have not found the campaign useful to play as though every conversation is
secretly setting an invisible ending flag.

The larger story continues to converge around the same central conflict with
Fathom, while the route there can carry different resources, relationships, and
character moments.

So far, the choices that matter most to me are the ones with a visible cost:
resources, Influence, Bonds, or an immediate character reaction.

I make those decisions around the company I have at that moment rather than
assuming every dialogue option is secretly selecting a different ending.

His inhibitor chip activates, and the clone who had been fighting beside the

Nothing I selected during an early build or ordinary dialogue exchange caused

Rex's knowledge of the clone inhibitor chips provides the way forward.

That sends the campaign into Imperial-occupied territory and eventually gives
Trick a path back to himself.

So when Trick becomes unavailable after Order 66, I keep following the Critical

<h2 id="ending-postgame">
  Fathom can be defeated without turning the epilogue into a hard stop
</h2>

<div className="my-6 rounded-xl border border-amber-500/30 bg-amber-500/10 p-5">
  <strong>Ending spoiler:</strong> this section discusses the result of the
  final confrontation with Fathom.

The final campaign eventually brings Zero Company back into direct conflict
with Fathom.

Her defeat weakens the Shadow Plague's hold on the surviving infected and
leaves the Infinite Coil without the force that had been holding it together.

That closes the central Fathom conflict.

Zero Company itself still has a reason to exist.

The Empire remains, the surviving team is still operating, and the epilogue
leaves the company in an Imperial-era galaxy rather than shutting the door on
everything that follows.

At launch, there is also **no New Game Plus** for me to build the first run
around.

I therefore finish the campaign with the roster and relationships I want for
that playthrough instead of hoarding every decision for an assumed NG+ carryover.

<h2 id="completion">
  A completion run needs different habits from a story run
</h2>

I decide fairly early whether I care about full completion.

A story-focused campaign can let many requirements happen in the background.

A 100% attempt benefits from earlier planning because Bonds, Optional Missions,
roster growth, Astromechs, Zone progression, and several unusual combat
requirements accumulate over a long run.

The difficulty achievements add another decision: the hardest completion route
eventually asks for a campaign built around **Expert and Beskar** rather than a
casual late-game toggle.

I keep the complete checklist and long-term planning in my
<Link href="/star-wars-zero-company/achievements-trophies">

I use that checklist whenever completion starts affecting which Operators,
Optional Missions, Bonds, or difficulty run I should prioritize next.
