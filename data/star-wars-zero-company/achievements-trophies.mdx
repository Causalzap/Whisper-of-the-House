I start the long requirements immediately: **50 Optional Missions, 1,138

<h2 id="achievement-roadmap">
  How I would start a 53-achievement run
</h2>

My biggest mistake would be treating every unchecked achievement as something
that deserves attention immediately.

Some requirements grow almost for free while I play.

Others become painful if I discover them after the campaign has already done
most of its work.

So from the first few Cycles, I separate the list mentally into three jobs.

The first is **background progress**: Optional Missions, kills, Zone Influence,
recruitment, Bonds, and Operator stories. These need time more than they need a
special setup.

The second is **opportunistic cleanup**. If a map already gives me high ground,
a ledge, a terrible low-percentage shot, or two enemies lined up for a Force
interaction, I use the opportunity. I do not distort a Critical Mission trying
to manufacture it.

The third is the **difficulty run**. Expert and especially Expert + Beskar ask
for campaign knowledge, so I would rather arrive there knowing which Missions
spawn reinforcements, which Operators I cannot afford to lose, and when an
objective is more important than clearing the map.

The complete tracker stays here while I work through those layers:

<h2 id="start-early">
  The achievements I start before they turn into cleanup
</h2>

The requirements I worry about most are not necessarily the hardest ones.

They are the ones that need dozens of Missions or many Cycles to mature.

The five counters I keep in mind from early in the campaign are:

- **Active Duty — complete 50 Optional Missions**
- **If You Know You Know — defeat 1,138 enemies in Tactical Missions**
- **Full Roster — have 20 Operators in one campaign**
- **Across the Stars — unlock all Zone Rewards**
- **There Will Be No Bargain — win Hutt Roulette Prize 3 times**

I do not stop Critical progression just to inflate those numbers.

Instead, when the Holo Table gives me breathing room, I prefer work that moves
several goals at once.

A useful Optional Mission can add to the **50-Mission counter**, give me more
kills toward **1,138**, build Bond XP, earn Influence toward a Zone Reward, and
put another Operator into meaningful combat time.

That is much better than finishing the campaign with several hundred kills
still missing and no other reason to keep taking fights.

I use the same thinking for **The High Ground**, which needs **10 kills from
higher elevation**.

Ten is small enough that I never farm it deliberately at the start. Whenever a
normal Mission already gives me a safe elevated firing position, I let that
kill count grow in the background.

### I rotate Bond pairs instead of using the same four people forever

There are **10 Very High Bond requirements** that matter for achievements.

The pairs are:

- Hawks + Luco
- Hawks + Trick
- Hawks + Jae
- Hawks + Tel
- Hawks + Kabb
- Hawks + Cly
- Trick + Luco
- Kabb + Jae
- two Astromechs
- two Custom Operators

The trap here is obvious only after the roster gets large.

It is easy to find four Operators I like, deploy them constantly, and arrive
late in the campaign with excellent Bonds on people who were already finished
while another required pair has barely spent time together.

I do not rotate just for the sake of rotation.

If Hawks and Kabb already fit the Mission and still need relationship progress,
I keep using them.

What I watch for is the pair that is being ignored.

If Trick and Luco still need Very High Bond and I have barely deployed them
together, I start fixing that while normal Missions still matter for the
50-Mission and 1,138-kill counters.

The two unusual pairings need even more lead time.

**Thick as Thieves** needs Very High Bond between **two Custom Operators**.

**Beep Boop** needs Very High Bond between **two Astromechs**.

Neither is something I want to discover after every other relationship is
already finished.

The broader Bond and Cross Training systems belong in my
<Link href="/star-wars-zero-company/beginner-guide">
  {" "}Zero Company beginner guide
</Link>
. For achievement planning, I only care that the required pair exists early
enough to spend real campaign time together.

### I recruit Astromechs as soon as the roster can support the plan

Astromechs create a second problem beyond Beep Boop.

**\*Angry Beeps\*** requires completing a Mission with **4 Astromechs**.

That means my eventual target is not one useful droid.

I need four available at the same time, while two of them also need enough
shared experience to reach Very High Bond.

The progression gate I watch for is the **Droid Repair Bay**.

Once the facility is operational, Astromechs can begin appearing through
Recruitment.

### I keep the six Operator stories moving when the Holo Table is quiet

Six achievements are tied to completing personal stories for:

**Jae, Cly, Tel, Kabb, Trick, and Luco.**

I do not drop a Critical Mission that is about to expire just because a personal
objective has appeared.

But once the current campaign pressure settles, I would rather advance one of
these stories than fill the Cycle with something that only gives me Credits I
do not urgently need.

That keeps character progress, Bond time, and achievement progress moving
together.

Tel is a useful reminder not to assume that meeting someone means their entire
achievement path is already open.

She first enters the story during Dark Waters, while her later roster
progression continues through
<Link href="/star-wars-zero-company/smugglers-den-walkthrough">
  {" "}Smuggler's Den
</Link>
.

I start worrying about personal-story completion once the Operator is actually
part of the campaign rhythm, not the first moment their name appears.

<h2 id="natural-achievements">
  I let the opening and story achievements come to me
</h2>

A large part of the 53 does not deserve a special route.

The early campaign naturally teaches me to:

apply Focus Points, customize an Operator, customize equipment, recruit new
people, and complete the opening Mission.

I do those when the systems appear rather than stopping to turn each one into a
separate achievement objective.

The same applies to the Critical story achievements.

The campaign awards achievements around Missions including:

**Eavesdropping**

**Dark Waters**

**Stillwatch**

**Smugglers' Den**

**Unfinished Business**

**Casualties of War**

**Behind Enemy Lines**

**Enemy Intelligence**

**The Infinite Coil**

**Deep Cover**

I follow the actual Mission order through my
<Link href="/star-wars-zero-company/walkthrough">
  {" "}Zero Company walkthrough
</Link>
instead of duplicating those routes here.

Two accuracy achievements can also happen without planning much.

**Do or Do Not** asks me to **miss a shot at 90% or higher**.

Mine happened during ordinary combat.

I wait until a low-chance attempt costs me very little.

If the objective is already secure, another Operator can finish the enemy, or
the miss will not expose anyone, I am happy to take the gamble.

I do not turn a fragile Critical Mission into a casino just because the
achievement is still unchecked.

<h2 id="combat-cleanup">
  I save awkward combat setups for a map that gives them to me
</h2>

Several achievements care about one very specific battlefield moment rather
than long campaign progress.

The conditions I watch for include:

- knocking an enemy out of the combat space;
- hitting a shot at **10% or lower**;
- missing at **90% or higher**;
- getting **10 kills from higher elevation**;
- using the Force to Push or Pull one enemy into another;
- defeating a **Seer in a single blow**.

The important part is not memorizing six tricks before Mission one.

It is recognizing the setup when the map finally gives it to me.

If a level has an obvious ledge and I already have a push effect available,
that is when I think about the knock-out requirement.

If I have high ground that also gives me a genuinely useful firing lane, I
collect one of the ten elevation kills without compromising the Mission.

**Forceful** becomes much more reasonable once I can field a Force user
normally. I wait until two enemies are positioned closely enough that a Push or
Pull can create the collision instead of trying to drag an entire fight into
the correct geometry.

**See Your Own Demise** is the one I approach more deliberately.

The Seer has to die in **a single blow**.

That means I do not chip the target first and then wonder why the achievement
did not unlock.

I wait until I have a damage setup I trust, keep incidental attacks away from
the Seer, and commit only when one hit has a realistic chance to finish it.

This is why I leave most of these for later.

A stronger roster gives me more movement tools, more Force options, more burst
damage, and more control over how the fight unfolds.

<h2 id="roster-cleanup">
  A few achievements can damage the run if I chase them at the wrong time
</h2>

**A Natural Part of Life** is the clearest example.

It requires losing a member of Zero Company.

I do not kill an important Operator early just because I want one more box
checked.

A developed Operator may still be needed for a personal story, a specific Very
High Bond pair, Cross Training, or the squad I use for difficult Critical
Missions.

If somebody dies naturally, I take the achievement and continue.

If everyone survives, I leave the deliberate loss until I know which Operator
can disappear without creating three new cleanup jobs.

The actual Downed → Injury → MedBay → permanent-loss system stays in my
<Link href="/star-wars-zero-company/permadeath-injuries">
  {" "}Permadeath and injuries guide
</Link>
.

For this route, my rule is narrower:

**I do not trade away unfinished achievement progress to earn the achievement
for losing somebody.**

Another small requirement I prepare rather than force is **Fully Geared**.

It needs **4 Weapon Mods on one Weapon**.

I still equip useful mods normally throughout the campaign, but once I am
collecting enough compatible pieces, I stop scattering every good mod across a
different weapon.

I keep one eventual four-mod weapon in mind.

That avoids the annoying cleanup state where I own plenty of mods but no single
weapon is actually configured for the achievement.

The same planning mindset applies to **Full Roster**.

Because I need **20 Operators in one campaign**, I do not treat Recruitment
only as a way to replace people I lose. When Credits and roster space permit, I
keep expanding toward the requirement while I still have Missions left for
those Operators to contribute to Bonds and other goals.

<h2 id="difficulty-achievements">
  I start Expert + Beskar when the checklist is no longer teaching me the game
</h2>

Five achievements are tied to campaign difficulty:

**Hired Gun — Story or higher**

**Veteran — Normal or higher**

**Operator — Hard or higher**

**Captain — Expert**

**Legend — Expert + Beskar Mode**

I do not choose Expert + Beskar on a blind first run simply because Legend is
sitting at the bottom of the checklist.

Expert changes more than enemy Health, while Beskar changes how much of a bad
result I can undo. That combination is much easier to manage once I already
know the Mission structure.

The full Expert and Beskar rules stay in my
<Link href="/star-wars-zero-company/difficulty-settings">
  {" "}difficulty and Beskar guide
</Link>
.

For the achievement route, I care about preparation.

Before I make Legend the main goal of a campaign, I want to understand:

- which objectives trigger reinforcement pressure;
- which Operators I trust when weapon range becomes less forgiving;
- how I rotate the roster when Injuries remove somebody for a Cycle;
- which Critical Missions punish a slow approach;
- and when extraction matters more than another kill.

That lets the difficulty be the challenge.

I do not want the challenge to be discovering halfway through a one-save run
that I misunderstood a campaign system.

<h2 id="before-finishing-campaign">
  What I check before I let the campaign run out of useful work
</h2>

Near the end of a normal campaign, I stop looking at the checklist by category.

I look for anything that still needs **time**.

If Optional Missions are well below **50**, I take useful optional work while
it still advances the kill counter.

If I am far from **1,138 Tactical Mission kills**, I stop skipping fights that
also offer Influence, Bond progress, or equipment I still need.

If a required Very High Bond pair is behind, I start deploying those two
together instead of continuing to over-level a relationship that is already
finished.

If I still need **20 Operators**, four Astromechs, or two Custom Operators with
a relationship requirement, Recruitment stays active.

If a named Operator story is unfinished, I check whether its next step is
available before spending the Cycle elsewhere.

If **Across the Stars** is incomplete, I look at the Zones that still need
their Rewards instead of blindly taking Missions in an area I already finished.

If Hutt Roulette still needs wins, I do not leave all three Prize results for a
final cleanup session.

Only after those slow requirements are healthy do I care about forcing the
remaining strange combat conditions.

That is the order that keeps the 53-achievement route from turning into dozens
of disconnected errands.

Most of the list can grow while I am already doing useful Zero Company work.

The handful that cannot are much easier to clean up once the squad, roster, and
campaign knowledge are already there.
