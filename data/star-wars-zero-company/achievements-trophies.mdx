<h2 id="achievement-roadmap">
  How I plan a 53-achievement run
</h2>

Trying to turn all 53 achievements into separate objectives from the opening
Mission makes the campaign harder to route for very little benefit.

I split them into four groups:

**natural progression**

**long campaign requirements**

**specific combat cleanup**

**difficulty runs**

Natural progression mostly takes care of itself.

The long requirements deserve attention much earlier because I cannot
manufacture dozens of Optional Missions, several Very High Bonds, a 20-Operator
roster, and more than a thousand kills during the final hour.

Combat cleanup works in the opposite direction. Many of those achievements
become easier once the roster has more abilities and I already understand which
maps create the right setup.

The dedicated difficulty run comes last for me. By then I know the Mission
structure, reinforcement pressure, and which squad losses would be hardest to
recover from.

<h2 id="natural-achievements">
  I let the basic and story achievements happen during normal progression
</h2>

Several early achievements are essentially checks that the campaign systems
have opened.

Completing the first Mission, applying a Focus Point, customizing an Operator,
customizing a weapon, and recruiting somebody all happen while Zero Company is
still establishing itself.

The campaign also has achievements attached directly to named story Missions:

**Eavesdropping**

**Dark Waters**

**Stillwatch**

**Smugglers' Den**

**Unfinished Business**

**Casualties of War**

**Behind Enemy Lines**

**Enemy Intelligence**

**The Infinite Coil**

**Deep Cover**

Following the Critical route handles those without another achievement-specific
path. I keep the actual sequence in my
<Link href="/star-wars-zero-company/walkthrough">
  {" "}Zero Company walkthrough
</Link>
.

Two accuracy achievements are different because they depend on a particular
roll rather than story progression.

**Do or Do Not** requires a missed shot at **90% or greater**.

I had that happen naturally. A normal run would make me wish the roll had gone
the other way; an achievement run gives me a reason to keep it.

**Never Tell Me the Odds** asks me to land a shot at **10% or lower**.

I wait for a low-percentage attempt where failure will not compromise the
objective rather than firing terrible shots every turn until one finally
connects.

<h2 id="long-term-achievements">
  I start the long counters while the campaign is still giving me useful work
</h2>

The requirements most likely to create an ugly cleanup grind are the ones that
grow slowly across an entire campaign.

The biggest examples are:

**Active Duty — 50 Optional Missions**

**If You Know You Know — 1,138 Tactical Mission kills**

**Full Roster — 20 Operators in one campaign**

**Across the Stars — all Zone Rewards**

**There Will Be No Bargain — Hutt Roulette Prize 3 times**

Critical progression still takes priority when something important is about to
expire.

When the schedule gives me room for optional work, however, I prefer a Tactical
Mission that advances several requirements together.

One useful Mission can contribute toward:

- the 50 Optional Mission count;
- the 1,138 kill count;
- Bond progress;
- high-ground kills;
- equipment and Influence;
- Operator story or roster development.

That is far better than finishing the story and discovering that several
hundred kills now have to come from Missions I no longer need for anything
else.

**The High Ground** fits the same approach.

It requires **10 kills from higher elevation**.

Whenever a normal Mission gives me a safe upper firing position, I take
advantage of it. Ten kills collected gradually are much easier than searching
for one late map to farm all ten at once.

<h2 id="bond-achievements">
  I rotate the required Bond pairs before the final chapters
</h2>

There are **10 achievements** tied to reaching Very High Bond between specific
pairs.

The required combinations are:

- Hawks + Luco
- Hawks + Trick
- Hawks + Jae
- Hawks + Tel
- Hawks + Kabb
- Hawks + Cly
- Trick + Luco
- Kabb + Jae
- two Astromechs
- two Custom Operators

This is where using the same four Operators for an entire campaign can create a
large amount of avoidable cleanup.

I keep effective pairs together when they already fit the current Mission, but
I also watch for required relationships that have barely moved.

If Hawks and Kabb are naturally gaining Bond together, I leave that alone.

If Trick and Luco still have almost no shared time while I know they eventually
need Very High Bond, I start giving them Missions together before the campaign
is nearly over.

The two non-named pairings need more preparation.

**Thick as Thieves** requires Very High Bond between **two Custom Operators**,
so that pair needs to exist early enough to accumulate real campaign time.

**Beep Boop** requires the same Bond level between **two Astromechs**, which
first means getting Astromechs into the roster.

The underlying Bond and Cross Training systems are covered in my
<Link href="/star-wars-zero-company/beginner-guide">
  {" "}beginner guide
</Link>
.

For this page, the achievement decision is simply to give required pairs enough
shared Missions before cleanup.

<h2 id="character-stories">
  I advance the six Operator stories as they become practical
</h2>

Six achievements are tied to completing a named Operator's personal story:

**Jae**

**Cly**

**Tel**

**Kabb**

**Trick**

**Luco**

These are another reason I check the Den after major progression.

When new personal business opens, I look at the current Holotable timers before
putting it off automatically.

A Critical Mission about to expire still wins.

When the schedule is clear, progressing an Operator story removes one more
long-term requirement while also giving that character more meaningful campaign
time.

Tel-Rea is a useful example because meeting her does not immediately make her a
normal roster member. Her actual recruitment progresses later through
<Link href="/star-wars-zero-company/smugglers-den-walkthrough">
  {" "}Smuggler's Den
</Link>
.

Once a character is properly available, I start treating personal-story and
Bond progress as part of the campaign rather than something to discover during
final cleanup.

<h2 id="astromechs">
  Astromech achievements require a small roster of their own
</h2>

Astromechs have two separate achievement requirements.

**Beep Boop** needs Very High Bond between two Astromechs.

**\*Angry Beeps\*** needs a completed Mission using **4 Astromechs**.

The progression gate I watch for is the **Droid Repair Bay**.

Once it becomes operational, Astromechs can begin appearing as Operators at
Recruitment.

Eventually I need **four available at the same time** for \*Angry Beeps\*, while
two of them also need enough shared play to reach Very High Bond.

That makes early recruitment much more useful than waiting until the rest of
the checklist is already finished.

<h2 id="combat-cleanup">
  I save awkward combat achievements for maps that naturally support them
</h2>

Several achievements ask for one specific battlefield event:

- knock an enemy out of the combat space;
- land a shot at **10% or lower**;
- miss a shot at **90% or higher**;
- get **10 kills from higher elevation**;
- Push or Pull one enemy into another with the Force;
- defeat a **Seer in a single blow**.

A Critical Mission is rarely the place where I want to force one of these at
the expense of the real objective.

Knock-outs and elevation become easier once I know which maps contain useful
ledges and firing positions.

The accuracy achievements can simply appear during ordinary combat.

**Forceful** becomes practical once I can field a Force user normally. I watch
for two enemies close enough together that a Push or Pull can create the
collision.

Tel-Rea first appears during Dark Waters and becomes a normal roster option
later through
<Link href="/star-wars-zero-company/smugglers-den-walkthrough">
  {" "}Smuggler's Den
</Link>
.

**See Your Own Demise** needs more care.

The Seer has to die in a **single blow**.

Any chip damage ruins that attempt, so I wait until I have a burst setup I
trust before committing to the one-hit plan.

<h2 id="permadeath-achievement">
  I leave A Natural Part of Life for a loss I can afford
</h2>

**A Natural Part of Life** requires losing a member of Zero Company.

I keep that requirement visible without sacrificing an important Operator early
just to make the achievement disappear from the checklist.

If somebody dies naturally, the requirement is handled.

If everyone survives, I leave the deliberate loss until I know which Operator
I can afford to remove from the campaign.

The actual Downed, Rally, Injury, Operation-risk, MedBay, treatment, and
permanent-death mechanics stay in my
<Link href="/star-wars-zero-company/permadeath-injuries">
  {" "}Permadeath and injuries guide
</Link>
.

<h2 id="weapon-mods">
  I keep one weapon ready for Fully Geared
</h2>

**Fully Geared** requires **4 Weapon Mods on one Weapon**.

The important part is **one Weapon**.

I still equip useful mods throughout the campaign, but I keep one eventual
four-slot target in mind instead of spreading every compatible mod across
different weapons and reaching cleanup with nothing close to complete.

Once I have enough compatible mods, I finish that one weapon and move on.

<h2 id="difficulty-achievements">
  The difficulty achievements determine which run I finish on
</h2>

There are five completion achievements tied directly to difficulty:

**Hired Gun — Story or higher**

**Veteran — Normal or higher**

**Operator — Hard or higher**

**Captain — Expert**

**Legend — Expert + Beskar Mode**

The requirements belong here because they are part of the achievement
checklist.

The underlying difficulty systems do not need to be repeated.

I plan **Captain** and especially **Legend** as dedicated campaign goals, then
use my
<Link href="/star-wars-zero-company/difficulty-settings">
  {" "}Zero Company difficulty settings guide
</Link>
for the actual Expert changes, Beskar rules, Permadeath interaction, and whether
I want to combine them on the same run.

By the time I reach that campaign, I want the long checklist items largely under
control:

**required Bonds progressing, Optional Missions accumulating, roster expansion
underway, Astromechs recruited, and one four-mod weapon already planned.**

That leaves the final run focused on surviving the difficulty instead of also
trying to repair dozens of neglected achievement counters.
