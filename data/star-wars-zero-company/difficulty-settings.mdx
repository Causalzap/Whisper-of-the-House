<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
For a first *STAR WARS Zero Company* campaign, I would use **Normal** if I am
still learning the game's combat and roster systems, or **Hard** if I already
play turn-based tactical games and want mistakes to carry more weight.

I would not start blind on **Expert** just because I usually pick the hardest
difficulty. Expert changes weapon range, enemy durability and damage, starting
Bonds, and injury recovery by disabling the Bacta Tank.

**Beskar Mode is separate from combat difficulty.** I turn it on only when I
want the campaign tied to one persistent save and am prepared to keep the
consequences of bad missions and decisions.

Mission labels such as **Cautious, Risky, Dangerous, and Extreme** are also
separate. They describe the danger of an individual mission, not the
difficulty setting of the whole campaign.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

<h2 id="which-difficulty">
  Which difficulty I would actually choose
</h2>

The four campaign difficulties are:

**Story, Normal, Hard, and Expert.**

For a first campaign, my choice comes down to how much of my attention I want
going into learning *Zero Company* itself.

I use **Story** when I mainly want the campaign, characters, and decisions
without turning every positioning error into a tactical problem.

I use **Normal** when I am still learning how this game handles cover,
Advantage, assists, injuries, Bonds, mission timers, and reinforcement waves.

For somebody already comfortable with games built around Action Points,
flanking, Overwatch, and protecting a limited squad, **Hard is my preferred
first-run setting**.

It gives mistakes enough weight that the tactical systems matter, but I can
still learn what a mission is about before committing to Expert's harsher
campaign rules.

Expert is different.

I would not describe it as Normal or Hard with larger enemy numbers. It
changes enough surrounding systems that I start planning the roster
differently before the fight even begins.

<h2 id="campaign-vs-mission-difficulty">
  Cautious, Risky, Dangerous, and Extreme are mission difficulty
</h2>

There is another difficulty label that appears after the campaign has already
started.

Individual Tactical Missions can be marked:

**Cautious**

**Risky**

**Dangerous**

and later

**Extreme**.

These labels do not replace Story, Normal, Hard, or Expert.

If my campaign is on Hard and the Holo Table shows a Dangerous mission, I have
not somehow switched the campaign to a fifth difficulty. The mission is
warning me about that particular operation.

That distinction matters because I do not choose between **Risky and
Dangerous** the same way I chose between Normal and Hard at the start.

For a mission, I look at what I am risking and what I am being paid to risk it
for.

Early on, I had a **Dangerous** mission offering **1,200 Credits** and a basic
weapon mod. It also had a limited number of Cycles before it expired.

Later, an **Extreme** Crisis Mission offered an additional Focus Point and
**2,000 Credits**.

Those rewards can make the harder mission tempting, but the label alone is not
enough for me to decide.

I also check:

- how many Cycles remain;
- whether Operators are already committed elsewhere;
- whether the objective is timed;
- whether the mission can strengthen the enemy if ignored;
- what the reward actually solves for my squad;
- and whether I can afford an Injury afterward.

A Dangerous mission with a reward I urgently need can be worth taking.

A slightly easier mission with an expiring Contact or important progression
reward can be more valuable than chasing difficulty for its own sake.

For Crisis Missions, the calculation becomes even less about the word
**Extreme**. Ignoring one can allow the opposing force to gain another upgrade,
so sometimes I am choosing which future problem I would rather prevent.

That is why I read mission difficulty as **risk information**, not as a score I
am supposed to keep pushing upward.

<h2 id="expert-difficulty">
  Expert changes the campaign in four important ways
</h2>

Expert lists four changes that matter well beyond enemy damage:

- enemies are **tougher and deal greatly enhanced damage**;
- effective **weapon range is greatly reduced**;
- Operators begin with **much lower Bond levels**;
- the **Bacta Tank is disabled**.

The reduced effective range is the rule I notice most often from turn to turn.

It changes what counts as safe positioning.

Good cover at the back of the battlefield is not automatically useful cover
if every shot I take from there is poor.

At the same time, moving closer just to improve the percentage can create a
worse problem than the shot I was trying to fix.

So on Expert I spend much more time thinking about the position at the **end**
of the turn.

I am not asking only whether an Operator can reach an enemy.

I want to know whether that Operator can reach a useful tile, still accomplish
something, and survive whatever happens after I press End Turn.

<h2 id="expert-combat">
  Expert made me stop wasting AP on bad firing positions
</h2>

An early fight on Lothal made this obvious.

The enemies were far enough away that staying near the starting position gave
me unattractive attacks. Waiting in the rear did not make the squad safer if I
was giving the enemy good positions while spending turns on low-value shots.

I started moving forward toward cover that actually put the squad inside a
useful engagement range.

The important correction was not simply “play more aggressively.”

Moving too far forward can spend every AP, remove my ability to Overwatch, and
leave an Operator exposed when the enemy moves.

What worked better was usually:

**advance to real cover → take the useful shot if I have it → otherwise protect
the next enemy turn.**

That became even more important once objectives started spawning
reinforcements.

In one crate mission, the fight looked controlled until the objective phase
turned into repeated waves. I eventually stopped treating every enemy as
something that had to die before extraction.

Once the objective was secured, leaving safely mattered more than squeezing
another kill out of the map.

That is the kind of mistake Expert punishes.

A normal tactical error costs Health.

A bad sequence of AP decisions can leave somebody close enough for one melee
enemy or one reinforcement wave to turn the whole mission.

Enemy durability adds to the pressure. Even attacks I expected to finish a
target could leave it standing, which makes plans built around one guaranteed
kill much less comfortable.

I keep more contingency in the turn now.

If the entire plan collapses because one enemy survives, it was too brittle for
Expert.

<h2 id="expert-recovery">
  No Bacta Tank changes what an Injury costs me
</h2>

The Expert rule I feel most after combat is the disabled **Bacta Tank**.

On easier settings, the Bacta Tank gives me an instant way to get an injured
Operator back.

Expert removes that shortcut.

When I need the MedBay instead, recovery can take a Cycle, cost Credits, and
temporarily remove that Operator from the group I wanted to deploy.

That turns an Injury into a roster problem.

If I keep using the same small group for every mission, one ugly deployment can
leave several of my preferred Operators unavailable at the same time.

This is why I value roster depth more on Expert than I did at the start.

I also become less willing to take an unnecessary late-fight risk after the
objective is already won.

The extra enemy is rarely worth more than keeping an important Operator healthy
for the next Cycle.

The full rules for Downed Operators, Rally, Injuries, MedBay recovery, and
permanent death are in my

  {" "}Permadeath and injuries guide
</Link>
.

Here, the important Expert difference is simply that **I cannot use Bacta to
erase recovery time**.

<h2 id="expert-bonds">
  Lower starting Bonds make early squad pairings matter more
</h2>

Expert also begins the roster with **much lower Bond levels**.

That sounds less dramatic than reduced range or losing the Bacta Tank, but it
changes early progression.

Bonds eventually feed into Focus Points, shared bonuses, and Cross Training.
Starting lower means I cannot assume those benefits will appear as quickly as
they did in a more forgiving run.

I therefore pay more attention to who repeatedly deploys together.

If I want two Operators developing toward the same long-term pairing, I would
rather use them together deliberately than keep rotating the entire roster
without a reason.

Call for Backup and other interactions between Operators also become more
interesting when I am already trying to strengthen a pairing I expect to keep.

This is one reason I would learn the roster on a normal save before attempting
a Legend run.

Expert asks for better long-term planning at the same time that its battles
give me less room to repair poor short-term decisions.

<h2 id="beskar-mode">
  Beskar Mode changes the save, not the enemy difficulty
</h2>

Beskar is easy to mistake for a difficulty above Expert.

It is not.

I can choose my campaign difficulty and then decide separately whether I want
**Beskar Mode**.

Beskar uses a **single persistent save** that continues updating as I play.
Manual saving is removed.

That changes how I approach more than combat.

Without a stack of earlier saves, I cannot make a decision, inspect the result,
and casually return to the version I liked better.

The same applies to a bad tactical result.

If an Operator is lost, I do not have the normal habit of going back several
saves and trying to manufacture a cleaner outcome.

Beskar also has critical failures capable of ending the campaign. Losing Hawks,
losing another critical squad member, or failing a crucial mission can end the
run.

That is why I think of Beskar as a **commitment setting**.

The enemies do not need another damage multiplier for Beskar to make the game
much more stressful.

The fact that I have to keep the result is enough.

<h2 id="permadeath-vs-beskar">
  Permadeath is not the same thing as Beskar
</h2>

Permadeath is another separate campaign option, and it is **enabled by
default**.

With Permadeath active, Operators can eventually be permanently lost.

Beskar determines how much freedom I have to undo the campaign state that led
to that loss.

Those are different decisions.

On my first campaign, I am comfortable leaving Permadeath active while keeping
Beskar off.

That still gives injuries and Operator survival real weight, but I am not
forcing myself to learn every mission trigger, reinforcement pattern, and major
choice through one irreversible save.

I like that middle ground for a blind run.

I still care when somebody goes down.

I still care about Injuries.

I still have to use the MedBay and think about the next Cycle.

But if I misunderstand a major campaign mechanic rather than simply make a bad
tactical decision, the entire run does not have to become the price of learning
it.

<h2 id="expert-beskar">
  Expert + Beskar is a very different first-run commitment
</h2>

Expert and Beskar remove different safety nets.

Expert gives me:

**tougher enemies + more damage + shorter effective weapon range + lower
starting Bonds + no Bacta Tank.**

Beskar then adds:

**one persistent save + consequences I cannot casually reload away.**

I would not stack those settings blind just because I already know turn-based
strategy games.

The combination punishes information I have not learned yet.

For example, a crate objective can turn into a long reinforcement sequence
after activation.

A mission that looks manageable can suddenly become an extraction problem.

An Operator who takes an Injury may be unavailable when another important
mission appears next Cycle.

A choice at the Holo Table can commit Operators or Intel I later wish I had
saved.

Those are not simply questions of tactical skill.

They are campaign knowledge.

In the Expert + Beskar run I watched develop, even ordinary fights produced
situations where an enemy surviving unexpectedly, running short on AP, or
getting caught by another reinforcement wave made survival the priority.

That is exactly why I would rather learn the campaign first and then start
Expert + Beskar with a plan.

By the time I attempt it, I want to know:

- which Operators I am comfortable rotating;
- how much healing support I want in the squad;
- when an objective tends to trigger reinforcements;
- when extraction is smarter than clearing the map;
- which mission rewards are worth taking a Dangerous or Extreme deployment;
- and which risks only make sense when I have an older save to fall back on.

On Beskar, that last category of risk should disappear.

<h2 id="difficulty-achievements">
  Captain needs Expert, while Legend needs Expert + Beskar
</h2>

Difficulty also matters for several completion achievements.

The progression is:

**Hired Gun** — finish the campaign on Story or higher.

**Veteran** — finish on Normal or higher.

**Operator** — finish on Hard or higher.

**Captain** — finish on Expert.

**Legend** — finish on **Expert with Beskar Mode**.

I would not use those achievements as the reason to force Expert + Beskar onto
my first playthrough.

A normal first run teaches me the things the achievement description cannot:
which objectives produce the most pressure, which Operators I rely on, when
Injuries start disrupting the roster, and which missions become much more
dangerous after the visible objective is complete.

Then I can start a Legend campaign knowing what I am committing to from Cycle
one.

<h2 id="my-difficulty-pick">
  The settings I would use if I started again
</h2>

For somebody completely new to tactical games, I would choose **Normal** and
learn the systems without trying to prove anything with the difficulty menu.

For somebody already comfortable with XCOM-style positioning and Action Point
management, I would choose **Hard** for the first campaign.

I would leave **Permadeath on** because surviving and managing Injuries are
important parts of the squad.

I would leave **Beskar off** until I know enough of the campaign that one
persistent save feels like an intentional challenge rather than a way to make
unknown mechanics expensive.

Then, if I wanted the hardest completion run, I would start **Expert +
Beskar** from the beginning and build around it.

The biggest difference is that I would enter that campaign expecting some
missions to end with:

**objective complete → stop chasing kills → get everyone out.**

On Expert, that is already good tactical discipline.

On Beskar, it can be the decision that keeps the campaign alive.
