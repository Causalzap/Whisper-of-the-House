<h2 id="which-difficulty">
  Which difficulty I would choose for a first campaign
</h2>

*STAR WARS Zero Company* gives me four main difficulty levels:

**Story, Normal, Hard, and Expert.**

I choose the four combat difficulties first, then decide whether I also want
Beskar's one-save rules.

Story through Expert changes the pressure I face while playing the campaign.
Beskar changes how permanent the result of a bad decision can become.

That separation makes the setup screen much easier to read.

That distinction is why I would rather choose the combat difficulty first.

If I am mainly here for the characters and campaign and do not already spend a
lot of time with turn-based tactical games, I start with **Story or Normal**.

If I already understand cover, action economy, flanking, Overwatch, and the
cost of exposing one Operator just to take a mediocre shot, **Hard** is the
first-run choice I prefer.

That gives me room to learn Zero Company's specific systems without immediately
stacking every punishment onto the campaign.

I do not pick Expert just because I normally select the hardest difficulty in
an RPG.

Expert changes several systems that affect how I build and move the squad, not
just how much damage enemies deal.

<h2 id="expert-difficulty">
  What Expert difficulty actually changes
</h2>

Expert is the first setting where I change the way I plan the entire campaign.

The game identifies four important differences:

- enemies are **tougher and deal greatly enhanced damage**;
- effective **weapon range is greatly reduced**;
- Operators begin with **much lower Bond levels**;
- the **Bacta Tank is disabled**.

The reduced range is the change I feel most often during actual combat.

On lower pressure settings, it is easy to look at good cover in the rear of the
map and assume staying there is automatically the safe play.

On Expert, that can leave me taking too many poor shots while the enemy gets
the useful positions first.

So I do not build my plan around:

**find strong cover → stay there → trade shots until everything dies.**

I expect to move.

<h2 id="expert-weapon-range">
  On Expert, I close distance before wasting turns on bad shots
</h2>

An early Lothal fight made the range change obvious to me.

The battlefield stretches out in front of the starting squad, with pirates
using cover far enough away that sitting at the back produces unattractive
shots.

The solution was not to keep firing from the starting area and hope the
percentages eventually worked.

I moved Hawks and the rest of the squad forward toward useful cover first.

That creates a different risk.

Moving forward can improve my shot, but moving too far can expose the Operator
to a flank or leave no AP to respond afterward.

So the question I ask before advancing is not merely:

**Can I get closer?**

It is:

**Where will this Operator be standing when the enemy gets a turn?**

I prefer one deliberate move into cover followed by a useful attack or
Overwatch over spending every AP sprinting into a position I cannot defend.

Expert rewards aggression.

It does not reward careless aggression.

That difference becomes especially important when reinforcements can appear
after an objective changes.

I do not empty the squad's movement and defensive options just because the
first visible group is almost dead.

<h2 id="expert-bacta-bonds">
  Expert also makes recovery and early Bonds more expensive
</h2>

The two Expert changes outside combat are easy to underestimate.

First, **the Bacta Tank is disabled**.

On easier settings, the Bacta Tank gives me an instant recovery option for an
injured Operator.

On Expert, I cannot rely on that shortcut.

When I checked the MedBay during an Expert campaign, the normal answer was bed
rest: treatment costs Credits, occupies the Operator, and takes a Cycle.

That makes roster depth more important.

If I keep taking the same four Operators into every fight and two of them come
home badly hurt, Expert gives me fewer ways to erase the problem immediately.

The complete Downed → Rally → Injury → MedBay → permanent-death chain belongs

For difficulty planning, the part I care about is simply:

**Expert removes the Bacta safety valve.**

The other difference is Bonds.

Expert begins with much lower initial Bond levels.

That does not make Bonds useless.

It means I should not expect early relationship progression and Cross Training
to arrive as quickly as they did in a more forgiving campaign.

I make more deliberate use of squad combinations and Call for Backup when I
want two Operators to grow together instead of assuming the early campaign will
hand me strong Bonds automatically.

<h2 id="beskar-mode">
  Beskar Mode is not just another difficulty above Expert
</h2>

Beskar is the setting I am most careful not to misunderstand.

It sits alongside the campaign difficulty rather than simply replacing Expert
with a fifth combat level.

Beskar uses a **single persistent save file**.

That save is continually updated, and manual saving is removed.

The practical result is that I cannot make a risky choice, see how it turns
out, and casually restore an older manual save.

Operator losses also become much more serious.

If an Operator dies, I cannot recover them by returning to an older save.

And Beskar has campaign-ending failure conditions.

If **Hawks** dies, another critical squad member dies, or a crucial Mission is
failed, the run can end.

That changes how I value a marginal reward.

On a normal save structure, I may be willing to test a risky approach because I
know I have a fallback.

On Beskar, I need a much better reason to expose a critical Operator for one
extra kill or force a bad objective push just to save a turn.

I am not trying to make every battle slow and defensive.

I am removing unnecessary risks that only made sense when failure was
reversible.

<h2 id="permadeath-vs-beskar">
  Permadeath and Beskar are separate decisions
</h2>

The campaign setup also has a separate **Permadeath** setting.

Permadeath is enabled by default.

With it active, Operators can be permanently killed rather than every Downed
state being recoverable forever.

I keep that separate in my head from Beskar.

I can leave Permadeath enabled without committing the campaign to Beskar.

With Permadeath active, repeated Injuries can still cost me an Operator. A
normal save structure gives me more room to recover from a disastrous Mission
or campaign decision.

Turning on Beskar removes much of that fallback. The run stays tied to its
persistent save, so losses and failures carry forward instead of becoming
something I casually test and reload.

For my first campaign, I am comfortable keeping Operator loss meaningful while
leaving Beskar off. Once I know the dangerous Missions and the roster demands,
I am much more willing to combine the two.

That is why I can enjoy the intended risk of losing Operators without
necessarily turning my first campaign into a one-save run.

For a first playthrough, that is the combination I prefer:

**keep meaningful consequences → keep manual recovery from a catastrophic
campaign mistake.**

Once I know which Missions have reinforcements, where objective pressure
appears, which Operators I cannot afford to lose, and how much roster depth I
need, Beskar becomes much more attractive.

<h2 id="expert-beskar">
  I do not combine Expert and Beskar just to save a second playthrough
</h2>

Expert and Beskar attack different safety nets.

Expert makes the tactical and roster-management side harsher.

Beskar makes the outcome harder to undo.

Stacking them means:

**harder enemies + shorter effective range + lower starting Bonds + no Bacta
Tank + one persistent save + permanent campaign consequences.**

That is why I would not choose **Expert + Beskar** blindly just because I
eventually want 100%.

The dangerous part is not merely losing one firefight.

A mistake earlier in the campaign can leave me carrying injuries, missing an
Operator, losing relationship progress, or reaching a later Critical Mission
with a weaker roster.

Learning those consequences on a normal save costs me much less than learning
them after dozens of hours on a persistent one-save run.

<h2 id="difficulty-achievements">
  Which difficulty I need for Captain and Legend
</h2>

The achievement list gives difficulty a clear progression.

**Hired Gun** requires finishing on Story or higher.

**Veteran** requires Normal or higher.

**Operator** requires Hard or higher.

**Captain** requires Expert.

And **Legend** requires **Expert + Beskar Mode**.

I do not let that achievement list choose my first difficulty for me.

If I am doing a normal first campaign, I use it to learn the Mission structure,
reinforcement patterns, Operator roles, injury pressure, and the places where a
bad story decision or failed objective matters.

Then I build the Legend run around the rules from the beginning.

That is cleaner than reaching the middle of a blind campaign and wishing I had
either chosen a safer first run or prepared the roster for Expert from Cycle
one.
