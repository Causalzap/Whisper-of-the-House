<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
If I am at **Pick Your Poison**, I choose **Kaller** on a first run. It gives
**+3 Influence in Zone Jenth** and opens a Tactical Mission with **1,200
Credits** and a Basic Weapon Mod, which is more useful to me early than
Dantooine's Utility Item route.

For **Gorga's Revenge**, I let **Bruckner disappear** when Credits are tight.
That gives **500 Credits + 3 Influence**, while handing him to Gorga trades the
Credits for **5 Influence in Zone Jenth**.

For later Dilemmas, I stop looking for one universal "good" answer. I check
Credits, Intel, Bonds, Operator availability, and any enemy upgrade I am about
to leave unresolved before I confirm the choice.

<h2 id="kaller-or-dantooine">
  Kaller or Dantooine in Pick Your Poison?
</h2>

**I pick Kaller if I am undecided.**

Pick Your Poison appears early enough that I still care about every few hundred
Credits and every useful piece of equipment.

The awkward part is that the choice screen does not show me enough information
to make that decision cleanly.

When it appeared on my Holo Table, the Operation:

* expired in **1 turn**;
* cost **50 Intel**;
* and asked which of Gorga's jobs I wanted to tackle first.

I had **100 Intel**, so paying the cost was not the problem.

The problem was that I could see **Kaller** and **Dantooine**, but not the full
reward package waiting behind each Tactical Mission.

### Why I choose Kaller

Choosing **Kaller** gives me **+3 Influence in Zone Jenth** and unlocks the
Kaller Tactical Mission.

Once that mission appeared on the Holo Table, I could finally see the useful
part of the decision: it was marked **Dangerous**, had **three turns**
remaining, and offered a **Basic Weapon Mod + 1,200 Credits**.

That was enough to make me comfortable with the choice.

The mission itself was not free money.

I had to retrieve Gorga's equipment, then get the squad back out while
reinforcements arrived behind us. I finished in **9 Tactical Rounds** after
defeating **15 enemies**.

The final payout on that run included:

* a **Coil Condenser** that adds **+2 Critical Hit Damage** when firing the
  equipped blaster;
* **100 Intel**;
* **1,200 Credits**;
* and **1 Focus Point for every Zero Company Operator**.

That is a strong early return from a choice that initially looks almost
arbitrary.

I also like the Weapon Mod because it solves an immediate problem. Early
Utility Items are useful, but I already have access to basic tools such as a
Medpack and grenade. A weapon improvement gives me something I can immediately
build around on a damage Operator.

### When I would choose Dantooine instead

Dantooine gives the same **+3 Influence in Zone Jenth**, but sends me into its
own Tactical Mission instead.

The immediate reward leans toward a **Basic Utility Item** and **1,000
Credits** rather than Kaller's Weapon Mod and 1,200 Credits.

That is not a bad route.

I would take Dantooine if my current squad needs another Utility option more
than another weapon attachment, or if I simply want that mission rather than
Kaller.

The Dantooine route also taught me a useful lesson about objective triggers.

When I reached the crate objective, I did not want to open it the moment the
button became available. Reinforcements could keep arriving, and movement
became tight enough that getting everyone to extraction took priority over
cleaning up every enemy.

The better sequence was to weaken the last visible threat, keep AP available,
then start the objective when the squad was already positioned to handle what
came next.

That is a mission-level difference, not a reason to fear the choice.

### Does Kaller or Dantooine change the main story?

I do not treat Pick Your Poison as an ending choice.

The immediate branch determines which Tactical Mission and early reward
package I receive, but either route keeps the Critical campaign moving toward
the Republic Intelligence contract.

So if I am stuck on this screen wondering whether one button secretly ruins the
campaign, I stop worrying about that.

I choose around the resource I want **now**.

For my first run, that is Kaller.

<h2 id="gorgas-revenge-bruckner">
  I let Bruckner disappear when Zero Company is still broke
</h2>

Gorga's Revenge looks more dangerous than the reward difference actually is.

Bruckner has been feeding information on Gorga to the Republic, and I get two
choices:

**let him disappear**

or

**hand him over to Gorga**.

I chose **let him disappear**.

Bruckner took the first transport he could find out of Hutt space, and I came
away with:

**+3 Influence in Zone Cresh**

and

**500 Credits**.

Handing him over to Gorga instead gives **+5 Influence in Zone Jenth** and
still opens the Lothal Tactical Mission.

That makes my decision pretty mundane despite the threatening setup.

If the company is short on cash, I take the **500 Credits**.

If I am already comfortable financially and I specifically want more Jenth
Influence, I hand Bruckner over.

I do not reload this decision trying to find a secret perfect outcome.

More importantly, I do not mistake those 500 Credits for the solution to
Gorga's enormous debt. The full route through the **80,000-Credit problem,
Lothal, the next Gorga job, Republic Intelligence, and Payback** stays in my

**do I need Credits right now, or do I value the extra Zone Influence more?**

<h2 id="dilemmas-and-bonds">
  Dilemmas can punish the answer that feels morally obvious
</h2>

The Trade Federation defector was where I stopped assuming that a choice I
liked narratively would also be clean mechanically.

A former Trade Federation associate director asks for Republic asylum in
exchange for information.

I was offered four ways to handle it:

* threaten the defector for Intel;
* help them contact the Republic;
* threaten them for Credits;
* sell them out to the Trade Federation.

I helped the defector contact the Republic.

Trick clearly approved of it. Jay was neutral. Other members of the company
were less enthusiastic.

Then the result screen hit me with **all Bonds decreased**.

That was more useful than another tutorial explanation of choices could have
been.

Now I treat the little approval reactions as information, not a complete
forecast.

If I am deliberately pushing two Operators toward a Bond milestone or Cross
Training, I pay more attention before taking a low-value Dilemma answer that
several of them dislike.

If the Bond difference is not important to my current squad plan, I am much
more willing to take the resource or story outcome I actually want.

This also stops me from trying to role-play every choice around one universal
approval meter.

Zero Company is not one character.

A decision one Operator loves can irritate somebody else standing two meters
away.

<h2 id="credit-choices">
  I keep some Credits unspent before checking new Dilemmas
</h2>

The **Relief Ship** Dilemma changed one small habit in the Den.

A humanitarian freighter carrying supplies for civilians had been forced down
at Caffrine. Without enough money for repairs, the crew risked having to sell
the cargo they were supposed to deliver.

The choices were:

**do nothing**

**help pay for the repairs**

or

**charge the crew for our services**.

This was one of the few times I knew exactly what I wanted to choose and simply
could not afford it.

Helping the crew costs **1,000 Credits**.

Charging them gives **1,500 Credits**.

Doing nothing has no direct resource reward.

I had already spent enough money that the help option was out of reach.

My immediate thought was to leave, sell a couple of things at the Black Market,
and come back with enough Credits.

I could not rely on doing that once I was already resolving the Dilemma.

So I charged them.

The result was less ugly than the wording made it sound. Zero Company arranged
the repairs for a fee, the crew got its ship fixed, and the relief supplies
continued on to the civilians.

I still would have paid the 1,000 if I had entered the choice with enough
money.

The lesson I kept was not "always charge people."

It was **stop draining the company to zero before I check what is waiting at
the Holo Table**.

If I have already bought the upgrade that matters this Cycle, I would rather
keep a small reserve than spend the last Credits on a marginal Black Market
purchase and discover five minutes later that a Dilemma wants cash.

<h2 id="crisis-decisions">
  Crisis choices are about deciding which future problem I can tolerate
</h2>

Crisis decisions are the point where I stop thinking mainly about Credits.

The game warns that Crisis Missions require a squad deployment to prevent the
enemy from becoming stronger.

Sometimes I can only respond to **one** of the available problems.

One Crisis forced me to compare upgrades such as:

an enemy **Striker that could no longer be interrupted by Overwatch**

against

a Striker whose **melee attack could generate Resonance**.

One of the available responses was also marked **Extreme** and offered an
additional **Focus Point + 2,000 Credits**.

That reward is tempting.

It is not the first number I look at anymore.

I ask which enemy upgrade is more disruptive to the squad I am actually using.

If my defensive turns rely heavily on Overwatch, removing an enemy's ability
to ignore Overwatch can matter more than another pile of Credits.

If I can comfortably prevent melee contact but struggle with another modifier,
I may choose the opposite Crisis.

There is no reason to solve this with a universal ranking because my squad may
not have the same weakness from one campaign to another.

The **Dangerous and Extreme** labels themselves belong to my

<Link href="/star-wars-zero-company/difficulty-settings">
  {" "}difficulty and Beskar guide
</Link>
. Here, I care about what happens if I leave the other Crisis alone:

**the enemy gets stronger.**

That is the consequence I make the decision around.

<h2 id="before-confirming-a-choice">
  What I check before I press the final option
</h2>

By the middle of the campaign, I stopped reading every choice as a morality
test.

I look at the company first.

If the Operation costs **50 Intel** and I only have 50 left, I check whether a
Critical job is about to need that pool.

If the choice wants **1,000 Credits**, I decide whether that money is more
important than the facility or equipment purchase I was about to make.

If it commits Operators, I make sure I am not sending away somebody I expect
to need for the next Tactical Mission.

If the Holo Table says an opportunity expires in **one Cycle**, I deal with
that before an optional job sitting on three or four.

If several Operators strongly dislike a Dilemma and I am close to a Bond
upgrade I actually want, I treat that relationship cost as real.

And with a Crisis, I look past the reward entirely long enough to read the
enemy upgrade I am choosing not to stop.

That has been much more reliable than looking for a single "best choices"
route.

Early in *Zero Company*, **500 Credits** can genuinely matter.

Later, one Operator, one Bond threshold, one expiring Critical Operation, or
one permanent enemy upgrade can be worth far more.

The right answer changes with the company I bring to the Holo Table.
