<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
Follow the **Critical** route when you want to move the story. I do not try to
clear every Operation and Tactical Mission on the Holo Table.

My main route is:

**Serelonus → the Den → Gorga → Republic Intelligence → Cortilius → Fathom and
Tel-Rea → Luunata → Viquist → Order 66 → Captain Rex and Umbara → rebuild Zero
Company → Cineurore → the Imperial warship → Fathom.**

After a major Mission, I return to the Den before committing another Cycle. I
heal anyone carrying dangerous Injuries, check new conversations and Critical
work, then use optional Missions only when the current story deadline gives me
room.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

<h2 id="campaign-order">
  The 14-chapter story route I follow
</h2>

The Holo Table can make *Zero Company* look less linear than the main campaign
really is.

Critical Missions, optional Tactical Missions, Operations, Dilemmas, personal
stories, Zone work, and Crisis Missions can all sit beside one another.

When I only want to know **what advances the story next**, I separate that
noise from the chapter spine.

The campaign has **14 chapters**:

1. **Prologue**
2. **Business As Usual**
3. **In Debt to the Hutts**
4. **Republic Intelligence**
5. **Smugglers and Slugrats**
6. **Enter Fathom**
7. **Jedi Undercover**
8. **The Umbaran Connection**
9. **The Fathom Menace**
10. **Liberate Luunata**
11. **Tighten the Noose**
12. **Back to Business**
13. **Fathom's Grand Designs**
14. **Epilogue**

I do **not** need to finish everything offered during those chapters.

Optional work is useful because it can give me Credits, Intel, Influence,
equipment, Bond progress, more Operators, and achievement progress.

It is not automatically the next story step.

My habit at the Holo Table is to check three things in this order:

**What is marked Critical?**

**How soon does it expire?**

**Can I fit useful optional work before that timer becomes uncomfortable?**

If the first two answers already tell me the story wants to move, I move it.

That keeps me from spending several Cycles improving a roster that was already
good enough while a much more important Critical thread sits untouched.

The broader rules for Cycles, Operations, facilities, and early roster
management stay in my
<Link href="/star-wars-zero-company/beginner-guide">
  {" "}Zero Company beginner guide
</Link>
.

Here I am solving a narrower problem:

**where do I go next if I want to finish the campaign?**

<h2 id="serelonus-and-the-den">
  Serelonus teaches the fight; the Den teaches me how the campaign actually moves
</h2>

The **Prologue** starts on Serelonus with Hawks, Trick, Kabb, and the opening
Zero Company squad.

I follow the tutorial objectives rather than trying to preserve a perfect
combat record.

The important destination is the extraction.

Once I am back at **the Den**, the game stops behaving like a sequence of
isolated tactical maps.

The Den becomes my campaign reset point.

This is where I eventually handle:

- Personnel;
- Focus Points;
- specializations;
- Bonds;
- injuries;
- Recruitment;
- facilities;
- equipment;
- conversations;
- and the Holo Table.

I do not try to solve all of those systems the first time they appear.

Several of them are still opening gradually, and I gain very little by staring
at a locked facility trying to plan ten Cycles ahead.

What matters first is building the habit I use for the rest of the game:

**Mission → return to Den → check what changed → then choose the next
deployment.**

That return trip becomes especially important later when a Mission opens a new
conversation, puts somebody in the MedBay, changes the roster, or quietly adds
a Critical Operation that I could miss by immediately launching something
else.

<h2 id="gorga-and-the-debt">
  I follow Gorga instead of trying to solve the opening economy
</h2>

**Business As Usual** establishes how fragile Zero Company's finances are.

Then Gorga turns that problem into the first real campaign deadline.

This is where the Holo Table can mislead me if I take the numbers too
literally.

When the **80,000-Credit debt** appears, the correct reaction is not:

**stop buying everything and farm 80,000 Credits.**

I keep following Gorga's Critical chain.

The progression I care about is roughly:

**Bruckner → Lothal → Pick Your Poison → the selected Gorga Tactical Mission →
Republic Intelligence.**

The debt has a visible **nine-Cycle** window, so I stop wasting Cycles on work
that gives me very little.

I also keep enough Intel available for the Gorga Operations that actually move
the chain.

What I do **not** do is turn the walkthrough into a money-farming route.

Normal early payouts are nowhere near 80,000 Credits, and the story eventually
solves the financial problem through a much larger contract.

The exact debt progression, when I spend Intel, and why **Payback** needs to be
resolved promptly are all in my
<Link href="/star-wars-zero-company/in-debt-to-the-hutts-walkthrough">
  {" "}In Debt to the Hutts walkthrough
</Link>
.

For the full campaign route, the point is simpler:

**stay on Gorga's Critical work until the client behind the earlier job finally
steps into the open.**

<h2 id="republic-intelligence">
  Republic Intelligence gives the campaign its real target
</h2>

The story becomes much clearer once **Jae** returns to the Den with
**Lieutenant Howerin of Republic Intelligence**.

The mysterious work from earlier in the campaign was connected to something
much larger than Gorga's business.

Republic Intelligence is investigating the **Infinite Coil**.

Its leader is **Kundri Fathom**.

Jae has her own reason to pursue her: Fathom has taken **Luunata**, Jae's home.

From this point, I mentally retire Gorga as the main campaign objective.

If new Gorga work appears beside a Critical Republic Intelligence lead, I no
longer assume the Hutt job must come first just because the debt dominated the
previous chapter.

The investigation now points toward **Cortilius**.

That is the thread I follow.

This stage also gives me more reason to expand beyond the tiny opening squad.

More Operators and more Den systems start becoming available, and the campaign
begins asking for enough flexibility that I no longer want one fixed group to
solve every tactical problem.

<h2 id="cortilius-investigation">
  On Cortilius, I follow the shipping trail instead of clearing the map for its own sake
</h2>

The early Cortilius work is investigative.

I am trying to understand how the Infinite Coil is moving people, supplies,
and influence through the area.

**Eavesdropping** is an important story marker.

After it, the trail pushes deeper into the local smuggling network.

That is how I know I am still moving in the right direction.

I am not looking for a random optional contract with the biggest Credit
reward.

I am following the leads that keep mentioning the Coil, its movements, or the
people feeding it resources.

Eventually the briefing points me toward **Takali Canal**.

That begins **Dark Waters**.

<h2 id="dark-waters-and-tel-rea">
  Dark Waters is where Fathom stops being a name in a briefing
</h2>

Dark Waters is the first point where the Infinite Coil investigation becomes
personal.

The progression is:

**Takali Canal → underground route → Coil gathering → Fathom → Tel-Rea.**

This is where I encounter Fathom directly and learn more about the **Shadow
Plague**.

It is also where I first meet Jedi Padawan **Tel-Rea**.

One thing I do not waste time on here is trying to recruit Tel immediately.

She does **not** become a normal Zero Company Operator at the end of this first
meeting.

That is expected.

Likewise, if Fathom cannot be treated like an ordinary target at a particular
moment, I do not assume I missed a hidden damage requirement or brought the
wrong build.

Dark Waters has its own progression gates.

If I need the exact Takali Canal path, the underground route, Fathom sequence,
or the final fight, I use my
<Link href="/star-wars-zero-company/dark-waters-walkthrough">
  {" "}Dark Waters walkthrough
</Link>
.

Once the Mission is complete, I return to the Den and keep following the
Cortilius smuggling trail.

<h2 id="smugglers-den-and-recruitment">
  Smuggler's Den is the point where Tel-Rea becomes part of my real roster
</h2>

The next important lead takes Zero Company into the **Three Tower District**.

The Slugrats' distribution network leads into **Smuggler's Den**.

This is not just another warehouse raid.

The investigation confirms how deeply the Coil has been using the local
smuggling network, while Tel-Rea's relationship with Zero Company finally
moves forward.

After the Mission and the follow-up back at the Den, she becomes part of the
team.

That changes how I treat her from this point on.

Before Smuggler's Den, Tel is a story character I am following.

Afterward, she is someone I can begin thinking about in terms of actual squad
composition, Bonds, abilities, and later Force-related combat opportunities.

The service entrance, warehouse route, droid encounter, and exact recruitment
sequence stay in my
<Link href="/star-wars-zero-company/smugglers-den-walkthrough">
  {" "}Smuggler's Den walkthrough
</Link>
.

For the main campaign, I leave the district once the Critical thread is done.

I do not keep searching the warehouse because I think another room must be
hiding the next chapter.

The next progress comes from returning to the Den.

<h2 id="midgame-operator-stories">
  The middle chapters are where I stop ignoring everyone else's story
</h2>

By **Jedi Undercover** and **The Umbaran Connection**, Zero Company has become
much larger than the opening Hawks–Trick–Kabb group.

This is the part of the campaign where personal stories start competing for
attention with the main Fathom hunt.

Jae, Cly, Tel, Kabb, Trick, and Luco all have their own progression.

I do not automatically complete every personal Mission the moment it appears.

The Holo Table still controls the schedule.

If a Critical Mission is close to expiring, I do that first.

If the main story is sitting comfortably several Cycles away, this is when I
start letting personal work into the route rather than leaving every character
thread until the epilogue.

That choice also helps long-term achievement progress without turning the main
walkthrough into an achievement checklist.

One of the major story events in this middle stretch is **Casualties of War**.

### Casualties of War keeps moving after the first major fight

The Mission brings **Anakin Skywalker** directly into Zero Company's story.

What begins around prisoners and political tension becomes much more urgent
when the Coil threatens the station itself.

The route keeps pushing downward toward the **reactor**.

The mistake I avoid is finishing a large fight on the upper level and mentally
treating the Mission as complete.

When the objective updates, I follow it.

I do not spend several extra turns making the first area perfectly clean if the
campaign is now telling me to move lower.

There are still civilians and a more important objective ahead.

This is one of several places in Zero Company where I learned that:

**the current objective matters more than the battlefield state I wanted to
leave behind.**

Completing this part of the campaign also unlocks **Sky High**.

More importantly for Tel-Rea, her later conversation with Anakin reinforces
that her place with Zero Company is no longer temporary.

<h2 id="luunata">
  Before Luunata, I fix serious injuries instead of hoping the Mission is short
</h2>

The campaign eventually stops investigating Fathom from a distance.

**Liberate Luunata** is the direct assault.

The Republic cannot simply throw a fleet at Fathom's Citadel.

Its orbital and surface defenses make that too costly.

Zero Company therefore approaches through a less conventional route, moving
beneath the normal defensive coverage and into the volcanic terrain around the
stronghold.

Before launching this Mission, I do one of my more serious Den checks.

If an Operator I care about is already sitting on **two Injuries**, this is not
where I gamble on them surviving one more Downed state.

I use the MedBay or rotate the roster.

Luunata is long enough and important enough that I would rather bring my
second-choice healthy Operator than my favorite character one knockdown away
from permanent loss.

Once I am inside the Citadel, I keep progressing toward the **central defense
controls**.

I do not treat every room as a mandatory extermination objective.

If the route opens forward and the current objective is clearly tied to the
defensive network, I move.

Disabling those systems is what allows the larger Republic assault to begin.

The broader Luunata sequence leads through major campaign milestones including
**Behind Enemy Lines** and **Luunata Reclaimed**.

But reclaiming the planet does not finish the Fathom hunt.

It changes it.

<h2 id="tighten-the-noose">
  After Luunata, I start treating extraction as a win condition
</h2>

Once Fathom loses Luunata, she has to move.

**Tighten the Noose** shifts the campaign away from retaking territory and
toward finding where the surviving Coil leadership has gone.

This stretch contains more intelligence gathering, command disruption, and
missions where getting the information matters more than leaving no enemy
alive.

This is where I become much more disciplined about objective changes.

If the game tells me to:

**steal the intelligence**

and then changes to:

**extract**

I extract.

I do not keep four Operators scattered across the map because there are still
two enemies alive behind me.

Likewise, when fresh reinforcements begin arriving after the objective is
already complete, I stop reading them as extra XP or kills I am supposed to
collect.

They are often the game's way of telling me:

**you have what you came for — leave.**

This lesson matters even more on higher difficulty, but I use it on every run.

A successful Mission with everyone extracted is better than turning a finished
objective into two Injuries because I wanted one more kill.

The intelligence gathered through this chapter eventually points Zero Company
toward **Viquist**.

<h2 id="viquist-and-order-66">
  I stop preparing for Viquist the moment the Republic collapses
</h2>

Viquist looks like it is about to become the final major assault against
Fathom.

She has regrouped around an asteroid base with senior Infinite Coil
leadership.

Republic Intelligence prepares the strike.

The approach is ready.

Zero Company appears to be heading directly toward Fathom.

Then the campaign changes completely.

Palpatine announces that the Jedi have betrayed the Republic.

The **Galactic Empire** replaces it.

The Fathom strike is called off.

When this happens, I stop trying to find a way to relaunch the Viquist job.

I did not miss another Holo Table requirement.

The old objective has been overtaken by the story.

The immediate crisis is now inside Zero Company itself.

### Trick turning hostile is not a MedBay problem

Order 66 activates Trick's clone conditioning.

His behavior changes and he turns against people he was fighting beside only a
short time earlier.

For a while he is no longer part of my normal available squad.

I do not keep checking the MedBay expecting an inhibitor-chip cure to appear.

I do not look for a consumable.

And I do not assume the character is permanently removed.

I continue the Critical story.

This temporary loss is also one reason I avoid designing the entire campaign
around exactly four irreplaceable Operators.

If one character disappears for story reasons, I want a roster that can absorb
it.

<h2 id="rex-and-umbara">
  Captain Rex tells me how to get Trick back
</h2>

After the fall of the Republic, Zero Company eventually crosses paths with
**Captain Rex**.

Rex already understands the problem.

The cause is Trick's **inhibitor chip**.

Rex has access to people who understand how to remove it.

That gives me a real route forward.

The story leads toward **Imperial-occupied Umbara**, where Rex is working with
local resistance.

Luco's connection to Umbara also pulls another Operator thread into the same
part of the campaign.

I follow the Rex/Umbara work because it accomplishes two things at once:

**Zero Company adapts to the Imperial era**

and

**Trick moves toward recovery.**

The first Rex conversation is not enough by itself.

I continue the relevant Critical work, return to the Den after those Missions,
and check what has changed.

Eventually Trick comes back with his own mind restored and becomes a usable
Operator again.

At that point I rebuild around him if he still fits what my squad needs.

I do not assume I must permanently replace him because Order 66 temporarily
removed him.

<h2 id="back-to-business">
  Back to Business is where I stop carrying unfinished roster problems into the endgame
</h2>

By **Back to Business**, Zero Company no longer has the same relationship with
the Republic that defined the first half of the campaign.

The team has survived the transition into the Empire.

Fathom is still alive.

The Shadow Plague is still unresolved.

And by now the direction is clear:

**Zero Company is going back after her.**

This is my last major preparation window.

I am not trying to create a theoretical perfect build.

I am fixing obvious weaknesses that I already know will hurt me.

Before I push into the final pursuit, I check:

- serious Injuries;
- unspent Focus Points;
- weapons and useful mods;
- whether I have dependable healing;
- whether the squad can move toward objectives without wasting every AP;
- whether one Operator is carrying too much of the damage plan;
- and whether I still have personal work I genuinely care about finishing.

If somebody has two Injuries, I deal with it.

If a key Operator has several useful points unspent, I stop saving them for a
future that is almost here.

If my squad only works when one specific character is standing, healthy, and
in perfect position, I change the composition.

What I do **not** do is suddenly clear every optional icon on the Holo Table
because the story feels close to the end.

Optional work only stays in my route if it is solving something I still need.

Once the campaign starts talking about locating Fathom for good, I become much
more conservative about spending Cycles casually.

<h2 id="cineurore">
  Cineurore stops being a clearing mission as soon as Fathom starts escaping
</h2>

The final pursuit identifies **Cineurore**, an orbital shipbreaking station
used by Imperial contractors.

The Coil has taken control of the station.

Zero Company uses stolen codes to get through the outer defenses.

Once on the station, I move toward the **security systems**.

That is the important progression target.

The investigation reveals that Fathom is already moving toward a captured
light cruiser.

At that moment, the nature of the Mission changes.

I stop thinking:

**clear Cineurore**

and start thinking:

**do not let Fathom disappear.**

She still gets ahead of Zero Company, and the pursuit moves toward Imperial
warships.

The stakes are bad either way.

If Fathom escapes with the plague, the Coil survives.

If the Empire captures her and the plague intact, the Empire gains access to
the same threat.

So Hawks keeps pursuing her.

This is not where I turn back to the Den because I forgot one optional
objective.

The campaign has entered its final sequence.

<h2 id="final-fathom-fight">
  Fathom falling once does not mean the fight is over
</h2>

The last battle takes place aboard the Imperial warship.

I enter it expecting to need recovery, assists, and enough AP discipline to
keep the squad together.

The main progression trap is simple:

**the first takedown is not the victory screen.**

Fathom falls to the level below.

Hawks immediately understands that the job is unfinished.

I follow.

In the second phase, I stop trying to create four independent firing angles
across the entire arena.

I keep Operators close enough that somebody can still help if another character
goes Downed.

A beautiful flank is not useful if the Operator taking it is now two turns away
from every Rally.

My priority is:

**remove the dangerous target → use assists when they improve the real attack →
heal before a greedy extra shot → preserve enough positioning to recover from a
bad enemy turn.**

I also avoid letting one unexpected survivor destroy the entire plan.

If my turn only works when every attack lands exactly as expected, I have left
myself no recovery path.

Once Fathom finally goes down in the later phase, the campaign moves into its
aftermath.

<h2 id="epilogue">
  The story ends with Fathom, not with the end of Zero Company
</h2>

Fathom's defeat changes the Shadow Plague almost immediately.

Hawks can feel her influence weakening.

Other infected survivors can no longer hear her.

Back at the Den, the broader consequences become clearer.

Coil positions are being abandoned.

Without Fathom holding the organization together, the movement begins to break
apart.

But the galaxy has not reset to the state it was in when the campaign began.

The Republic is gone.

The Empire is expanding.

Zero Company still exists.

The final conversations point toward more work in a galaxy where people now
need help surviving and moving through Imperial territory.

So I do not read the epilogue as:

**everything is over.**

I read it as:

**the Fathom campaign is over.**

If I am staying with the save for completion rather than simply reaching the
ending, I stop following story order and switch to the long-term requirements
in my
<Link href="/star-wars-zero-company/achievements-trophies">
  {" "}53-achievement and trophy roadmap
</Link>
.

Bonds, Optional Missions, roster targets, Astromechs, combat cleanup, and
Expert + Beskar need a different route from the one I used to reach Fathom.
