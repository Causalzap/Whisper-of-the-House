<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
Follow the **Critical Mission** chain instead of trying to clear every
Holotable opportunity.

My story route is:

**Serelonus → the Den → Gorga → Republic Intelligence → Cortilius → Fathom and
Tel-Rea → Luunata → the interrupted Viquist strike → Order 66 → Captain Rex and
Umbara → rebuild Zero Company → Cineurore → the Imperial warship → Fathom.**

Between major story steps, I return to the Den for injuries and new crew
conversations, then only spend extra Cycles when the current Critical route
gives me room.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

<h2 id="campaign-order">
  STAR WARS Zero Company chapter order
</h2>

The easiest way for me to understand the campaign is to separate the **chapter
spine** from everything else appearing on the Holotable.

There are **14 chapters**:

1. **Prologue**
2. **Business As Usual**
3. **In Debt to the Hutts**
4. **Republic Intelligence**
5. **Smugglers and Slugrats**
6. **Enter Fathom**
7. **Jedi Undercover**
8. **The Umbaran Connection**
9. **The Fathom Menace**
10. **Liberate Luunata**
11. **Tighten the Noose**
12. **Back to Business**
13. **Fathom's Grand Designs**
14. **Epilogue**

I do not need to finish every Tactical Mission or Operation offered inside
those chapters.

When I only want to move the story, I look for the **Critical** thread.

Optional work is where I build Influence, equipment, Bonds, Credits, roster
depth, and achievement progress when the current timers leave space for it.

If I am unsure whether another Optional Mission is safe, I check expiry before
committing. I keep the full Cycle, Operation, injury, and roster-management
rules in my
<Link href="/star-wars-zero-company/beginner-guide">
  {" "}Zero Company beginner guide
</Link>
.

The route below answers the other question:

**what story milestone should I be heading toward next?**

<h2 id="prologue">
  Prologue: finish Serelonus and return to the Den
</h2>

The opening Serelonus operation teaches the basic tactical loop and establishes
Hawks, Trick, Kabb, and Zero Company.

I follow the tutorial objectives, finish the extraction, and return to **the
Den**.

That is the real beginning of the campaign.

The Den gradually becomes the place where I manage:

- Operators;
- injuries;
- Personnel;
- Focus Points;
- Bonds;
- Recruitment;
- upgrades;
- the Holotable.

I do not try to optimize every system immediately because several facilities
are still locked.

My next goal is simply to start taking work from the Holotable and let the early
Gorga storyline open.

<h2 id="business-as-usual">
  Business As Usual leads into Gorga's debt
</h2>

The first stretch establishes how financially vulnerable Zero Company is.

Gorga then turns that problem into a story deadline.

I keep taking the Critical work attached to his chain rather than wandering
through every available side opportunity.

The important progression is:

**Gorga's early work → Brookner → Lothal → Republic Intelligence.**

When the **80,000 Credit** debt appears, I do not stop the campaign and try to
farm the entire number through ordinary payouts.

I keep progressing until the Republic Intelligence contract changes the
financial situation.

The Brookner choice, nine-Cycle pressure, early payouts, Republic payment, and
Payback resolution are all covered in my
<Link href="/star-wars-zero-company/in-debt-to-the-hutts-walkthrough">
  {" "}In Debt to the Hutts walkthrough
</Link>
.

For story progression, the point is simple:

**keep doing the Critical Gorga chain until Jae arrives with Republic
Intelligence.**

<h2 id="republic-intelligence">
  Republic Intelligence turns Fathom into the main objective
</h2>

Jae Mortant eventually reveals herself as the client behind the Serelonus job
and introduces **Bennic Howerin of Republic Intelligence**.

That meeting changes the scale of the campaign.

Zero Company is no longer just taking mercenary contracts.

The new target is the **Infinite Coil**, led by **Kundri Fathom**.

Jae also has a direct connection to the conflict because Fathom seized
**Luunata**, her home world.

The first major lead sends me toward **Cortilius**.

Once I accept that contract, I stop treating Gorga as the main story problem
and start following the Republic Intelligence chain whenever it appears as
Critical work.

Recruitment and more Den systems also begin opening around this stage, which is
where having more than one usable squad composition starts paying off.

<h2 id="cortilius">
  Cortilius: follow the shipping trail until it leads underground
</h2>

The first Cortilius missions are mostly about understanding how the Coil is
moving people and supplies.

**Eavesdropping** is one of the important milestones.

After it, the clues point increasingly toward local smugglers and a larger Coil
distribution network.

That is the signal I use to know I am still on the right Critical route.

I keep following Bennic's leads until the briefing sends Zero Company to
**Takali Canal**.

That starts **Dark Waters**.

<h2 id="dark-waters">
  Dark Waters is the first major Fathom encounter
</h2>

Dark Waters is where the investigation finally reaches Fathom herself.

For the story route, I only need to know:

**Takali Canal → underground caverns → Fathom → Tel-Rea.**

I meet Fathom during a Coil initiation, learn much more about the Shadow
Plague, and encounter Jedi Padawan **Tel-Rea** for the first time.

Tel-Rea does **not** join Zero Company here.

That is normal progression, not a missed recruitment prompt.

If I am stuck on the canal entrance, the underground route, why Hawks cannot
shoot Fathom, or the final fight, I use my
<Link href="/star-wars-zero-company/dark-waters-walkthrough">
  {" "}Dark Waters walkthrough
</Link>
.

After completing it, I continue following the Cortilius smuggling network.

<h2 id="smugglers-den">
  Smuggler's Den finishes the Cortilius smuggling investigation
</h2>

The next major lead takes me to the Slugrats' distribution operation in the
**Three Tower District**.

That becomes **Smuggler's Den**.

The warehouse raid confirms that the Coil has been using the local network for
far more than ordinary contraband.

More importantly for my squad, this is where Tel-Rea's relationship with Zero
Company finally changes.

After the mission and the conversation back at the Den, she joins the team.

The service entrance, warehouse fights, battle-droid reveal, and exact Tel-Rea
recruitment sequence are all in my
<Link href="/star-wars-zero-company/smugglers-den-walkthrough">
  {" "}Smuggler's Den walkthrough
</Link>
.

From this point forward, I treat Tel-Rea as part of the active roster.

<h2 id="jedi-undercover">
  Jedi Undercover is where personal stories start mattering more
</h2>

By the time Tel-Rea joins, Zero Company has grown beyond the small opening
group.

The middle chapters start mixing the Fathom hunt with more Operator-specific
work.

This is where I make a habit of returning to the Den after major Missions
instead of immediately launching the next Holotable job.

Jae, Cly, Tel, Kabb, Trick, and Luco all have their own story progression, and
some of those threads intersect the main campaign.

I still give the Critical route priority when something is close to expiring.

But when the story gives me breathing room, this is the phase where personal
Missions become increasingly worth doing rather than leaving every character
thread until the end.

One of the biggest shared story missions in this stretch is **Casualties of
War**.

<h2 id="casualties-of-war">
  Casualties of War: keep pushing toward the reactor
</h2>

Casualties of War brings **Anakin Skywalker** directly into Zero Company's
campaign.

What begins as a political and prisoner crisis escalates once the Coil threatens
the station itself.

The mission keeps moving downward toward the reactor.

The mistake I avoid is treating the first major fight as the end of the job.

When the objective shifts, I keep advancing toward the lower section rather
than spending extra turns trying to make the upper level perfectly clean.

There are still civilians and another critical objective ahead.

Completing the mission also unlocks **Sky High**.

Afterward, Anakin's conversation with Tel-Rea reinforces that she is now
committed to the path she chose with Zero Company rather than simply passing
through the story as a temporary Jedi ally.

<h2 id="luunata">
  Liberate Luunata: infiltrate Fathom's Citadel
</h2>

Luunata is the point where Republic Intelligence finally moves from investigation
to direct attack.

The contract is to infiltrate **Fathom's Citadel**, disable its defenses, and
open the planet to a Republic assault.

The Citadel's orbital and surface defenses make a straightforward fleet attack
too dangerous.

Zero Company instead approaches beneath the normal defensive coverage and uses
a volcanic route into the stronghold.

Once inside, my progression target is the Citadel's **central defense
controls**.

I do not treat every room as something that must be completely cleared before I
move.

I keep pushing toward the systems that let the Republic fleet begin its assault.

This is one of the points where I make sure serious injuries are already dealt
with before launching the Mission.

Luunata is too important and too long a progression step for me to bring an
Operator who is one bad down away from permanent loss.

The broader Luunata progression also leads into **Behind Enemy Lines** and the
**Luunata Reclaimed** achievement.

<h2 id="tighten-the-noose">
  Tighten the Noose changes the objective from retaking territory to finding Fathom
</h2>

Retaking Luunata does not end the Infinite Coil.

It forces Fathom to move.

The next chapter is more about dismantling the remaining command structure,
stealing intelligence, and identifying where Fathom and her senior people are
regrouping.

This is also where I pay much more attention to **extraction objectives**.

Several missions stop being about killing everything once the important
information has been obtained.

If reinforcements arrive, air support appears, or the objective changes to
evacuation, I stop treating leftover enemies as mandatory targets.

I move toward extraction.

The tactical lesson that matters most here is recognizing when the mission has
changed from:

**win the fight**

to:

**leave with what I came for.**

Eventually that intelligence points to **Viquist**.

<h2 id="viquist">
  Viquist looks like the final Fathom assault
</h2>

Fathom regroups at **Viquist**, an asteroid base with senior Coil leadership.

Republic Intelligence prepares what looks like the campaign's final major
strike.

An orbital route through the asteroid field has been secured, troops are ready,
and Zero Company appears to be heading directly toward Fathom.

Then the operation is interrupted.

Palpatine announces that the Jedi have betrayed the Republic and reorganizes it
into the **Galactic Empire**.

Bennic calls off the Fathom strike.

Zero Company is ordered back.

At that point, I stop preparing for Viquist.

The old objective is gone.

The immediate story problem becomes the Empire itself.

<h2 id="order-66">
  Order 66 changes Trick and breaks the old Zero Company
</h2>

The confrontation with the newly Imperial forces escalates quickly.

Zero Company refuses to simply hand over its members.

Then Trick's clone conditioning activates.

He starts treating members of the squad as traitors and turns against the
people he has been fighting beside.

The clearest sign is his behavior and the familiar idea that:

**good soldiers follow orders.**

I do not assume this means Trick is permanently lost.

There is no secret MedBay interaction I need to discover immediately.

I continue the story.

For a while, Trick is no longer part of my normal squad availability.

That temporary loss is one of the strongest reasons I do not build the entire
campaign around a single fixed four-person team.

<h2 id="captain-rex">
  Captain Rex gives me the route to getting Trick back
</h2>

After the Republic falls, Zero Company eventually crosses paths with
**Captain Rex**.

Rex is already working with an underground clone network.

He understands exactly what happened to Trick.

The cause is an **inhibitor chip**, and Rex knows people who can remove it.

So I follow Rex's route instead of trying to solve Trick through an upgrade,
healing item, or repeated Den interaction.

That route leads toward **Imperial-occupied Umbara**.

Luco also has a personal connection to the planet, which brings another
Operator thread into the same progression.

<h2 id="umbara">
  Umbara begins Zero Company's Imperial-era campaign
</h2>

Umbara is where the new political reality becomes normal rather than a one-time
story shock.

Rex is working alongside Umbaran resistance fighters against the Empire.

The old Republic-versus-Separatist divide is no longer what defines Zero
Company's next job.

The Empire is the immediate threat.

I keep following the Rex/Umbara progression because it does two things at once:

**moves Zero Company into the post-Republic story**

and

**moves Trick toward recovery.**

Trick does not instantly return after the first conversation with Rex.

I continue the relevant missions, then check the Den again afterward.

Eventually he returns with his own mind restored and becomes usable normally
again.

Once that happens, I can rebuild the squad around what I actually need rather
than permanently replacing him.

<h2 id="back-to-business">
  Back to Business is my preparation window before the final hunt
</h2>

After Order 66, Zero Company no longer has the same Republic Intelligence safety
net it had during the first half of the story.

The team has to decide what it is going to become in the Imperial era.

Fathom is still alive, and the Shadow Plague has become much more personal for
Hawks.

By this point, I read the story's direction clearly:

Zero Company is going back after her.

This is where I do my last serious roster check.

Before committing to the final hunt, I clear important injuries, spend useful
Focus Points, check equipment, and make sure the squad I intend to use can cover
healing, damage, and objective movement without relying on one fragile
Operator.

I also finish personal work that I genuinely care about if the timers leave
room.

I do not try to complete every Optional Mission simply because I suspect the
story is nearing the end.

I only stop spending Cycles casually once the campaign begins talking about
finding Fathom for good.

<h2 id="cineurore">
  Fathom's Grand Designs starts with Cineurore
</h2>

The final hunt eventually identifies **Cineurore**, an orbital shipbreaking
station used by Imperial contractors.

The Coil has taken over the station.

Zero Company uses stolen codes to get through the outer defenses and land.

Once I am on the station, the important route is toward the **security
systems**.

That is where Zero Company discovers that Fathom is already trying to leave in
a captured light cruiser.

The mission priority changes immediately.

I stop treating Cineurore like a map I need to clear completely and start
chasing the escape.

Fathom still gets ahead of Zero Company, and the pursuit moves toward Imperial
warships.

At that point, letting either side win is dangerous.

If Fathom takes the ships, she escapes with the plague.

If the Empire captures her, the Empire gains access to the same threat.

So Hawks follows her onto the final ship.

<h2 id="final-fathom-fight">
  The final Fathom fight continues after the first takedown
</h2>

The last battle takes place aboard the Imperial warship.

I make sure the squad reaches it without avoidable injuries and with enough
healing to recover from a bad turn.

The main trap is assuming the first time I overpower Fathom ends the campaign.

It does not.

She falls to the level below.

Hawks immediately recognizes that the job is not finished, so I follow her
rather than treating the encounter as complete.

The second phase is where I want the squad staying close enough to support each
other.

I do not spread everyone across the arena just to create four independent
angles.

At this point, one downed Operator can cost enough actions that the whole fight
starts slipping away.

My priority is:

**finish dangerous targets → use assists → heal before greed → keep the squad
close enough to Rally each other.**

Once Fathom finally goes down in the second phase, the campaign moves into its
aftermath.

<h2 id="epilogue">
  Epilogue: Fathom is gone, but Zero Company keeps operating
</h2>

Fathom's defeat has an immediate effect on the Shadow Plague.

Hawks feels its influence weakening.

Other infected survivors confirm that they can no longer hear Fathom.

Back at the Den, reports make the larger result clearer.

Coil strongholds are being abandoned.

Without Fathom holding the organization together, the movement begins to break
apart.

That does **not** mean the galaxy returns to normal.

The Republic is gone.

The Empire is still expanding.

Zero Company is still active.

The final conversations even point toward more work involving people trying to
move through Imperial territory.

So I read the ending as:

**Fathom's campaign is finished, but Zero Company is not.**

If I am staying in the campaign for completion rather than story progression,
I switch over to my
