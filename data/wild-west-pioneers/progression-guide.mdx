Progression in Wild West Pioneers is easier to understand when you separate
three different gates that often appear at the same time:

**1. Objective gate** — what the current Journey task actually wants.

**2. Unlock gate** — which Idea, tier, or Town Hall level makes that building
or system available.

**3. Settlement gate** — whether you have the logs, planks, workers, food, and
infrastructure to use the thing you just unlocked.

Most early confusion comes from solving one gate and assuming the other two
have disappeared. A building can be unlocked while the town is too weak to
construct it, a tier can be ready while smaller tasks remain unfinished, and
you can have enough Journey Points while spending them on something that does
nothing for the current objective. The progression screen works best when you
use it to identify **which of those three gates is stopping you now**.


<h2
  id="town-hall-first-official"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Your First Permanent Choice Happens at the Town Hall
</h2>

The Town Hall immediately introduces a decision that is different from normal
building progression: the first official is not a temporary tutorial choice.
Once selected, that official stays. Later Town Hall upgrades add more official
slots, up to **three officials in total**, rather than letting you freely swap
the original pick.

  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">
    The first official is a lasting choice; Town Hall upgrades add new slots instead of replacing it.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

The opening options include bonuses such as **10% more money from settlement
sources**, **10% faster building construction**, and extra starting resources
for newly established settlements. Their value arrives at different stages:
construction speed is useful almost immediately, extra resources for new
settlements become relevant much later, and a money bonus can help a town that
regularly runs short on cash.

For a first run, I value a bonus that is already helping the opening town. The
important part is to read the whole card as a long-term choice rather than pick
the first positive percentage and assume it can be changed later.


<h2
  id="first-journey-gate"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  The First Journey Gate Is Housing, Not a Full Build Order
</h2>

After the Town Hall sequence, the opening objective turns toward resident
needs. **Tents** are the first important Idea because housing is the requirement
standing directly in front of progression. That is the pattern worth learning:
Journey does not give you a universal list saying every Idea should be bought
as soon as possible; it gives you a current requirement, and the useful unlock
is the one that removes that requirement.

In the opening, Tents do exactly that. Once they are available, the remaining
question is no longer progression because the houses still need practical food,
water, and road access. When the correct building is unlocked but residents
still cannot satisfy the objective,
<Link href="/wild-west-pioneers/best-settlement-layout">the blockage has moved from progression into settlement layout</Link>.


<h2
  id="journey-points"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Journey Points Come From Several Kinds of Progress
</h2>

Journey Points are not tied to one repeatable activity. They can come from:

- completing main, side, and Journey objectives;
- constructing buildings for the first time;
- completing locations on the World Map;
- <Link href="/wild-west-pioneers/settler-needs">fulfilling resident needs</Link>.

  <Image
    src="/images/wild-west-pioneers/wild-west-pioneers-journey-points-sources.webp"
    alt="Wild West Pioneers Journey Points tooltip listing quests, first-time buildings, world locations, and resident needs"
    width={1600}
    height={900}
    className="rounded-xl"
  />

  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">
    Journey Points come from settlement progress, objectives, first-time construction, resident needs, and exploration.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

A point shortage therefore does not automatically mean you need to grind one
specific activity. I look first for work that solves two things at once: a
first-time building may generate progress while improving the economy, a
resident requirement can advance the main objective while adding Journey
progress, and a World Map location can push exploration while feeding the same
system.

The number of Journey Points in the bank is only one part of the decision. An
Idea bought too early can still leave you waiting on the resources and workforce
needed to use it, so gaining access to a building and being ready to operate it
are two different stages.


<h2
  id="progression-tiers"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Five Tiers Do Not Mean Five Checklists You Must Fully Clear
</h2>

The progression screen extends through **five tiers**. Early on, Tier 2 is
locked while Tier 1 shows a main objective alongside smaller tasks such as
gathering mushrooms, producing soup, building roads, improving Warehouse range,
or <Link href="/wild-west-pioneers/settler-needs">fulfilling resident wishes</Link>.

<figure className="my-8">
  <Image
    src="/images/wild-west-pioneers/wild-west-pioneers-progression-tier-one.webp"
    alt="Wild West Pioneers progression screen showing the first tier, main task, and additional Journey objectives"
    width={1600}
    height={900}
    className="rounded-xl"
  />

  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">
    The tier screen mixes the main progression requirement with smaller settlement objectives.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

Those tasks are not all prerequisites for the next tier. The interface can
explicitly mark the current tier as **ready to be unlocked** while smaller
objectives remain visible, and once that state appears, those side tasks do not
need to be cleared before progression continues.

This is where the three-gate model becomes useful: a locked tier points back to
the **objective gate**, an unavailable building points to the **unlock gate**,
and a building that is already available but cannot be constructed or operated
points to the **settlement gate**. Reading the state correctly prevents a lot
of unnecessary Journey Point spending.


<h2
  id="settlement-gate"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  An Unlock Is Not Complete Until the Town Can Support It
</h2>

The settlement gate becomes more visible as construction starts asking for
processed materials. You may already own the correct Idea and still be unable
to move the objective because logs have collapsed, planks are missing, or every
available worker is tied to essential production. At that point, another unlock
does not help because the progression system has already done its part; the
economy has become the blocker.

<Link href="/wild-west-pioneers/production-chains">Trace the production chain when the required building is available but its materials or workers are not</Link>.

This also explains why rushing through Ideas can make progression feel slower.
A larger build menu gives you more choices, but every additional production
building competes for the same population and resources. Unlocking more systems
only helps when the settlement can absorb the cost of actually running them.


<h2
  id="resident-needs"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Resident Needs Can Be Progression Requirements Without Being Progression Problems
</h2>

Shelter, water, food, and later resident objectives appear inside Journey
progression because satisfying the population is part of growing the
settlement, but the fix depends on what is actually missing. An unavailable
requirement may still be an unlock issue, an available service that cannot
reach a home is a coverage issue, and food that never gets produced is a
resource-chain issue.

When a resident objective refuses to complete, first identify whether the game
is withholding a building or whether the town simply cannot use what it already
has. That distinction keeps Journey from becoming the catch-all explanation
for every stalled resident task.


<h2
  id="scout-cabin"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Scout Cabin Marks the Shift From Settlement Progress to World Progress
</h2>

The **Scout Cabin** is one of the clearest progression pivots in the first
town. In the route shown here, it became the current Journey target after the
opening food and wood problems had been stabilized and plank production had
begun.

Its role here is the unlock itself: once Scout Cabin becomes the required
building, progression is beginning to move onto the World Map. The actual
party size, travel supplies, destination selection, location interactions, and
return process come after that unlock and are part of exploration rather than
Journey progression.

Once the building is finished and the first town can spare people,
<Link href="/wild-west-pioneers/scouts-expeditions">set up the scouting expedition and continue on the World Map</Link>.


<h2
  id="town-hall-upgrades"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Town Hall Levels Compound Earlier Decisions
</h2>

The Town Hall does not stop mattering after the first official. At the next
level, another official slot becomes available while the original choice
remains active.

  <Image

    width={1600}
    height={900}
    className="rounded-xl"
  />

  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">
    Town Hall Level 2 adds another official slot instead of replacing the first official.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

The opening official therefore becomes part of a growing combination rather
than a disposable early bonus. Town Hall upgrades also arrive around the stage
where the settlement begins using more processed resources and wider systems,
so progression gradually shifts away from placing one more basic building and
toward whether the economy can support increasingly expensive unlocks.


<h2
  id="settler-base"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Settler Base Is a Progression Milestone; Expansion Starts After It
</h2>

The **Settler Base** appears much later than the opening resident buildings and
marks another major change in what the game expects from you. Its progression
meaning is simple:

**scouting has found somewhere worth settling, and permanent expansion is now
available.**

That is all you need to resolve on the Journey side. The actual settler group,
population commitment, travel supplies, transported resources, destination
choice, and construction of the second settlement happen after this unlock.

<Link href="/wild-west-pioneers/scouts-expeditions">Continue with the Settler Base and second-settlement process when the first town is ready to support expansion</Link>.


<h2
  id="progression-stuck"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  When Progression Stops, Identify Which Gate Is Closed
</h2>

Instead of clearing random Journey tasks, read the state that is blocking you:

**The current objective requires something you have not reached yet**  
→ identify the main Journey requirement.

**The required building does not appear in construction**  
→ look for its Idea, tier, or Town Hall requirement.

**The building is unlocked but cannot be constructed**  
→ the blockage is now resources rather than progression.

**The building exists but the objective still does not complete**  
→ inspect service coverage, resident access, or the system the building feeds.

**The tier says “ready to be unlocked”**  
→ move to the next tier even if smaller tasks remain.

**Scout Cabin is the current objective and already built**  
→ progression has handed the next step to exploration.

**Settler Base has become available**  
→ the next decision is whether the first settlement can afford permanent
expansion.

The same rhythm works throughout the progression tree:

> **read the objective → find the unlock → confirm the settlement can support
> it**

Once those three pieces line up, the next stage usually opens without chasing
every available task on the screen.
