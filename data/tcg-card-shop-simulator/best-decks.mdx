<h2 id="best-deck-choice">
  Which Tetramon deck should I build?
</h2>

There is not one useful answer for every collection because a strong Tetramon deck still depends on which Basic cards, evolution stages, and support effects I actually own.

The three builds I would prioritize are different enough that the choice mostly comes down to how I want the match to develop:

The three builds solve different match problems, so I would choose by how I want the deck to win rather than by rarity alone.

**Wolan Evolution Burst** is the easiest starting point for straightforward duels and tournaments. I build stable Elemental Areas first, preserve strong Fire values such as Dragons, then use Wolan to convert that board into a large finishing boost. Its weakest games are the ones where the opening hand gives me evolution pieces in the wrong order.

**Discard / Recycle** takes longer to build but has a stronger combo feel. The deck fills the discard pile, recovers useful cards with effects such as Angis, then turns that discard count into large boosts through Mentos and other payoffs. It feels weak early when the discard pile is still small, so I need enough cycling and setup cards to reach the payoff turn.

**Shield / Fire** is the one I move toward when opponents are ending games before my damage engine is ready. Shield effects buy extra turns while Fire buffs develop behind them. The balance matters: too little protection leaves the deck exposed, while too many support cards can produce a hand that survives without actually closing the match.

These are not three piles of expensive cards. Each one is built around a repeatable interaction.

If I only have random high-rarity cards but the evolution lines do not connect, I would fix the list first with the <Link href="/tcg-card-shop-simulator/deck-builder">Tetramon deck builder</Link>. A legal deck still needs **50 cards**, no more than **4 copies of the same card name**, and enough playable earlier stages to avoid dead hands.

<h2 id="wolan-evolution">
  Wolan Evolution Burst is the most proven tournament build
</h2>

The Wolan build is the one I would copy first if the goal is to get a functional deck into real matches quickly.

Its strongest pattern is not complicated:

**establish the board → progress the useful evolution lines → preserve the high-value Elemental cards → play Wolan when those cards can produce a large boost.**

The deck does not need to rush Wolan onto an empty board. In fact, holding it until the Elemental Areas are worth copying or converting is what gives the card its finishing role.

One match showed this very clearly. With a Dragon already contributing Fire power, Wolan could select two Dragons and turn the play into a **+28 Fire boost**. The opponent was already under pressure, and that setup pushed the game into a winning next turn.

The same deck kept working through multiple games instead of only producing one good draw. It handled going second, going first, weaker opening hands, mulligans, and a full tournament run.

That matters more to me than seeing one enormous damage number.

A deck that only works when the opening five cards are perfect is not the first deck I want to take into a tournament.

### What I keep in the Wolan core

The exact 50-card list can change with the collection, but I would preserve these jobs:

- **Wolan** as the main conversion / finishing card;
- **Dragon cards** with strong Fire value;
- **Hydron** for additional board value;
- complete evolution lines that can establish stronger cards without clogging the hand;
- enough Basic Tetramon that the opening turns still function;
- a small amount of draw or recovery so a weak first hand does not end the match immediately.

The deck also benefits from patience.

If Wolan is in hand but the best target is still weak, I hold it. If a Dragon can wait one turn and produce a much larger combined swing, I wait. This build wins by making the finishing turn matter, not by spending every effect as soon as it appears.

<h2 id="discard-recycle">
  Discard / Recycle turns used cards into the next damage spike
</h2>

The Discard build feels very different because losing cards from the hand or board is often part of the setup rather than a failure.

The core loop is:

**discard useful fuel → build the discard pile → recover the cards that matter → cash the discard count in for Elemental boosts.**

Mentos is one of the clearest payoffs.

With a large discard pile already built, Mentos produced a **+18 boost** in one winning sequence. The important part was that this was not an isolated card effect. The rest of the deck was already feeding that condition.

That creates an important timing rule:

**Mentos is not an opening card I force onto the board.**

When the discard pile is still small, its payoff is small. I would rather use the early turns to cycle cards, establish Pygmy, set up Bird Pig, and create the discard state that makes Mentos worth drawing later.

### The cards doing different jobs

**Mentos** is the late payoff.

**Angis** helps recover useful cards from the discard pile, so throwing cards away does not mean permanently losing every important piece.

**Pygmy → Bird Pig** gives the deck another source of repeated boosting while the discard engine develops.

**Flamy** can convert discards into immediate pressure instead of waiting entirely for Mentos.

**Lupip** is useful when a discard action also needs to become direct damage.

The strength of this build is that several cards are pushing the same resource in the same direction.

Discarding is not just a cost. Recovery is not just defense. The discard pile itself becomes something the deck wants to grow.

### The failure state is using the payoff too early

I had a turn where the hand contained Mentos, but the discard pile was not developed enough for it to matter.

That is the point where this deck can feel weaker than the Wolan build.

Wolan mainly asks for a useful board.

Mentos asks for a useful discard state first.

If the deck is drawing payoff cards before it has generated enough fuel, I would not add more Mentos. I would add more cards that cycle, discard, recover, or keep the board alive while the engine develops.

<h2 id="shield-fire">
  Shield / Fire is the better answer when matches end too quickly
</h2>

The Shield / Fire build starts from a different problem:

**the opponent is doing enough early damage that the deck never reaches its stronger turns.**

That makes protection part of the win condition.

The useful version is not a deck full of defensive cards. It uses Shield to survive the opening while Fire buffs and damage cards develop behind it.

One tested setup opened with Shield support, used Flame Chick as part of the buff engine, held Hydron for the turn where its effect mattered, then kept adding boosts until the damage caught up.

This is especially useful when going first.

The starting player cannot attack on the first turn, so I do not want to spend that turn pretending I am already racing damage. A Shield effect or setup card can turn the forced non-attacking turn into preparation instead.

The tested match then moved from a protected opening into enough damage to finish the opponent.

That is the balance I would keep:

**enough defense to reach the damage turn, but not so much defense that the deck stops threatening anything.**

### I would not turn it into a pure Shield pile

The danger is easy to see.

If too many slots become Shield cards, the deck can survive without creating a reason for the opponent to lose.

I still want:

- reliable Basic cards;
- Fire-oriented damage;
- one or more effects that reward discarding or cycling;
- Hydron or another meaningful mid-turn boost;
- enough Shield to survive the opening pressure.

If the deck already survives comfortably, the next card I add should probably increase damage or consistency rather than adding another defensive effect.

<h2 id="freeze-counter">
  Freeze Counter is worth keeping as a fourth build, but I would not start here
</h2>

Freeze also worked in a real match.

The sequence was slower to assemble: the opening turns did very little until the key Freeze pieces appeared, then the deck established the lock and finally turned that control into damage.

That is why I treat it differently from the three builds above.

It can work, but it asks for more specific pieces before the deck starts doing what I built it to do.

For a player with a deeper collection, that can be a fun counter-oriented setup. For a first competitive deck, I would rather start with Wolan Evolution Burst or one of the more flexible Fire shells.

The important lesson from the Freeze match is not that control is weak.

It is that **a control deck needs enough search, draw, and usable early cards to reach the control piece before the opponent has already built a winning board.**

<h2 id="missing-cards">
  What should I do if I am missing the exact cards?
</h2>

I replace the **job**, not just the name.

If I am missing a Wolan support card, I look for another card that gives the same Elemental Area a strong value before Wolan arrives.

If I am missing part of the Discard core, I ask whether the missing slot was supposed to:

- discard,
- recover,
- boost from the discard pile,
- draw,
- or finish.

Then I replace that role.

If the Shield list is dying before the damage turn, I add protection. If it is surviving but not closing games, I cut protection for damage.

That is also why I would not copy a 50-card list blindly from a collection I do not own.

The <Link href="/tcg-card-shop-simulator/deck-builder">deck builder</Link> is more useful for finishing the exact 50 cards because it can expose missing earlier evolution stages and copy-limit problems. The <Link href="/tcg-card-shop-simulator/tier-list">Tetramon card tier list</Link> helps when several owned cards could fill the same role.

<h2 id="tournament-deck">
  Which build would I take into a tournament first?
</h2>

I would start with **Wolan Evolution Burst**.

Not because every other archetype loses to it, but because it has the strongest repeated match sample here.

The build continued winning through ordinary customer duels and then through the tournament even when the opening hand was weaker, the player had to mulligan, and going first changed the usual sequencing.

That is exactly what I want from a tournament deck:

**a clear win condition that still functions when the first five cards are not perfect.**

The Discard / Recycle build is the next one I would use once the collection has enough of the engine pieces. Its ceiling is high, but it is more dependent on reaching the right discard state before Mentos and the other payoff cards become efficient.

Shield / Fire is the build I would move toward when my losses are happening too early rather than because the late game lacks damage.
