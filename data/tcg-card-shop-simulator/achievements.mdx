<h2
  id="progression-milestones"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Let Shop Growth Clear the Level, License, Staff, and Expansion Milestones
</h2>

A large part of the achievement list follows things the shop already wants me to
do. Reaching levels 5, 20, 50, and eventually 100 opens more of the business;
pack licenses appear along that route; useful employees arrive as manual work
starts piling up; and a larger floor eventually needs more expansion. I gain
very little by distorting the opening just to make one of those counters move a
few hours earlier.

The expansion achievements are a good example. **Minor Shop Expansion** asks
for four expansions, **Major shop Expansion** for ten, and **Full Shop
Expansion** for twenty. Those numbers are long-term ownership targets rather
than instructions to buy empty rooms as soon as the next expansion becomes
available. I let the floor grow as merchandise, play tables, staff movement, and
new systems actually need the space.

The same logic applies to **Helping Hand**, **Tight-Knit Team**, and **Top
Recruiter**, which require 1, 4, and 8 workers. An eighth employee is useful in
a much larger operation than the shop that only just needed its first hire, so
there is no benefit in forcing the headcount ahead of the workload.

For the unlock order itself, the <Link href="/tcg-card-shop-simulator/progression-guide">progression guide</Link> follows the points where expansion, staff, the Workbench, and later pack licenses begin changing what the shop can do.

<h2
  id="manual-checkouts"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Keep 1,000 Manual Checkouts From Becoming an Endgame Chore
</h2>

**I LOVE Working In Retail** is unusual because making the shop more automated
can slow achievement progress. It needs **1,000 checkouts completed manually by
the player**, so a register that spends most of the save under employee control
can leave this counter far behind the rest of the business.

I keep a meaningful share of checkout work for myself while the store is still
small enough that running the register is not getting in the way of everything
else. Restocking is often easier to hand off first: that frees time away from
the shelves without surrendering every customer transaction.

There is no special transaction size to chase here. A normal customer counts
toward the same 1,000-checkout requirement, which makes steady progress far less
painful than discovering at shop level 100 that several hundred manual sales are
still missing.

<h2

>
  Petty Cash, I'm Rich!, and Capitalism HO! Check Profit, Not Revenue
</h2>

The three daily economy milestones are **$1,000**, **$10,000**, and **$30,000
profit in one day**. The important word is *profit*.

A real Daily Report shows why sales alone can be misleading. That day recorded
**$1,668.00 in item earnings** and **$401.35 in game earnings**, for
**$2,069.35 of revenue**. Supply purchases cost **$1,963.52**, another
**$2,000.00** went into upgrades, and the final Profit line landed at
**-$1,894.17**.

When one of these achievements is the target, I use a day when the shop enters
the morning well stocked and does not need a major expansion or license
purchase. The threshold is checked against the result left after the day's
costs, so moving a large upgrade to another day can matter more than squeezing
a little extra revenue from one shelf.

Pricing, inventory turnover, and the cash required to keep the next trading day
healthy are separate business decisions covered in the <Link href="/tcg-card-shop-simulator/money-guide">money guide</Link>. For these three achievements, the Daily Report's **Profit** number is the result that matters.

<h2
  id="seller-achievements"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Seller Achievements Need One Valuable Card, Not Cumulative Card Revenue
</h2>

**Novice Seller**, **Pro Seller**, and **Ultimate Seller** require individual
card sales worth **$200**, **$5,000**, and **$10,000**.

The thresholds do not add together across several transactions. Selling ten
$500 cards may be excellent business, but it still does not produce the single
$5,000 sale needed for Pro Seller.

That distinction becomes useful very early because a moderately valuable pull
can finish Novice Seller while the other two remain far away. Once the $200
milestone is gone, I stop treating every decent single as progress toward the
next threshold and let higher-value cards emerge as the collection and pack
access improve.

<h2
  id="customer-counters"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Customer Play and Smelly-Customer Achievements Can Run in the Background
</h2>

The passive customer counters have generous time to grow. **Cozy Card Shop**,
**Popular Card Shop**, and **THE Card Shop** require **100**, **1,500**, and
**5,000 customers** to play in the store. As long as useful play-table capacity
remains available, ordinary shop operation keeps feeding those totals.

The deodorant chain works the same way at a smaller scale: **Who you gonna
call?** needs 100 smelly customers cleaned and **BO Buster!** raises that to
500. I deal with them when they appear rather than trying to create a separate
grind around the count.

These are exactly the kind of achievements I want progressing alongside other
work. They consume time when ignored completely, but they do not justify
warping the shop around them.

<h2
  id="pack-collection"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Make the 25,000-Pack Grind Advance the 4,000-Card Collection at the Same Time
</h2>

Pack opening scales from **100 → 1,000 → 10,000 → 25,000**, while collection
milestones rise through **100 → 500 → 1,500 → 2,500 → 4,000 cards**. Those two
chains overlap so naturally that I treat them as one long completion project.

The first milestones arrive without much planning. By 10,000 packs, and
especially on the way to **Certified Card Goblin** at 25,000, the counter is too
large to dismiss as something that will certainly finish by accident. That is
where I check the collection at the same time: if unique cards are also still
missing, every pack can contribute to two unfinished goals.

This is not a good reason to starve an early shop of sellable inventory. The
large pack totals are much easier to absorb after pack access and ordinary
income are already established, when opening another batch does not decide
whether tomorrow's shelves can be stocked.

**Lucky Bastard** requires a **full art foil Ghost card**, while **Limitless
Pull** specifically asks for a **borderless full art foil Ascension card**.
Neither requirement benefits from opening a small batch, missing the pull, and
then treating that batch as evidence that another pack is better.

I keep both conditions active in the background while working through the large
pack totals. The 10,000- and 25,000-pack achievements already create thousands
of chances for rare cards to appear, so combining the RNG goals with that grind
avoids turning each rare variant into its own isolated session.

<h2
  id="grading-achievements"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Start the Gem Mint Chain Before Everything Else Is Finished
</h2>

Grading is the achievement branch most likely to punish a very late start.
**Grading Apprentice**, **Grading Prodigy**, and **Grading Master** require
**10**, **100**, and eventually **500 Gem Mint cards**. At the same time,
**Obsessed With Grades** wants a complete set from **grade 1 through grade 10**,
while **Graded Gold** asks for a graded card worth more than **$100,000** to be
sold.

Those goals pull in different directions. A low grade may be disappointing for
value, yet it can be exactly the grade still missing from the 1–10 set. I check
that set before disposing of apparently bad results.

comfortably pay for submissions, but well before final cleanup. Cards can be
away for grading while shop levels, customer counts, pack totals, and the
collection continue moving.

<h2
  id="tetramon-wins"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  TCG Arcade Needs 50 Wins Against Customers
</h2>

The 1.0 achievement **TCG Arcade** is not another play-table counter. It
requires **50 Tetramon TCG duel wins against customers**.

That makes it fundamentally different from Cozy Card Shop, Popular Card Shop,
and THE Card Shop. Those achievements can progress while customers play on
their own; TCG Arcade requires the player to enter the card game and win.

I wait until I have a legal deck that can produce useful opening hands, then
start collecting those wins as part of normal deck testing. The <Link href="/tcg-card-shop-simulator/tetramon-duel-master">Tetramon Duel Master guide</Link> covers the level-7 Workbench, 50-card deck rules, Elemental Areas, evolution, Guardians, customer challenges, and tournaments.

There is little benefit in grinding all 50 wins with a deck whose Basic and
evolution ratios are still fighting each other. Fixing those problems first
turns the achievement into repeated useful matches instead of repeated failed
setup.

<h2
  id="late-game"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  The New 1.0 Goals Sit on Top of Existing Long-Term Grinds
</h2>

Five achievements arrived with the latest layer of progression: **Ascension
Card Pack**, **Card Master**, **Certified Card Goblin**, **Limitless Pull**, and
**TCG Arcade**. In the current launch-period snapshot, all five show **0.1%**
unlock rates.

That number needs context. These goals have had far less time to accumulate
unlocks than achievements that have existed since the Early Access launch, so I
do not treat 0.1% by itself as proof that each is harder than every older
achievement.

Their requirements are still substantial. Card Master raises the collection to
4,000, Certified Card Goblin extends pack opening to 25,000, Limitless Pull
adds a highly specific Ascension rarity, and TCG Arcade introduces a separate
50-win duel grind.

Ascension also sits deep in shop progression rather than in the opening
economy. I let the <Link href="/tcg-card-shop-simulator/progression-guide">normal level and license route</Link> bring that product line into reach instead of spending the first half of the save as though an endgame pack were the next objective.

<h2
  id="completion-route"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  The 100% Route I Use
</h2>

I organize the run around achievements that can become inefficient if they are
noticed too late, not around the Steam unlock percentages.

1. **Run the early shop normally.** Let the first levels, licenses, customer-play
   totals, first worker, basic expansion, 100 cards, and 100 packs arrive through
   ordinary progression.
2. **Keep some register work manual.** Continue adding to the 1,000-checkout
   requirement before employees take over most of the front counter.
3. **Begin grading once submissions are affordable.** The 500-Gem-Mint chain
   benefits from having many in-game days to run beside everything else.
4. **Pair packs with collection progress.** Carry 1,000 into 10,000 and finally
   25,000 packs while the 1,500, 2,500, and 4,000-card targets are still useful.
   Leave Sick Pull, Lucky Bastard, and Limitless Pull active during the same work.
5. **Use mature-shop days for deliberate profit attempts.** Enter those days
   stocked, avoid unnecessary major purchases, and read the Daily Report Profit
   rather than gross Revenue.
6. **Move into Tetramon wins with a functioning deck.** Customer play counts do
   not substitute for the 50 victories needed by TCG Arcade.
7. **Finish the remaining outliers together.** Level 100, 500 Gem Mint cards,
   missing grades, the $100,000 graded sale, Ascension rarity, and any unfinished
   long counters are much easier to see once the tracker contains only a small
   number of unchecked rows.

At that point I use the checklist as the cleanup screen rather than repeatedly
scanning the entire achievement list. Filtering unfinished goals makes it
obvious whether the remaining work belongs to packs, grading, shop progression,
profit, or Tetramon.
