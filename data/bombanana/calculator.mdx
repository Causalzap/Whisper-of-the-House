<h2
  id="two-answers"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  The raw result and final key do different jobs
</h2>

The arithmetic answer does not finish the Calculator.

It advances the module.

I keep the two values separate:

**raw result**  
The answer to the equation. Enter this first.

**final key**  
The single digit chosen after the new LED appears.

That distinction matters because both numbers can be correct at different moments.

If the equation gives **57**, entering 57 may be exactly right even though 57 is not the number that eventually finishes the module.

Once the raw result is accepted, I stop treating the Calculator as an unfinished math problem and read the new state.


<h2
  id="no-light"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Why there is no colored light at the start
</h2>

The first Calculator state can show an equation with no useful LED at all.

That is normal.

The correct order is:

1. Read the equation.
2. Calculate the raw result.
3. Enter that result.
4. Wait for the module to change.
5. Read the new LED.
6. Classify the raw result as odd or even.
7. Use the table for the final key.

  src="/images/bombanana/bombanana-calculator-red-led-keypad.webp"

  width={1600}

So if I am staring at an equation and asking:

**“Where is the light?”**

I am usually one stage early.

If the arithmetic result has already been accepted and a colored LED is now visible, I am in stage two and do not need to solve the equation again.


<h2

>
  The final key comes from LED color and parity
</h2>

Once the raw result has been accepted, I need two pieces of information:

**the new LED color**

and:

**whether the raw result is odd or even**

Those two values select the final key.

The complete mapping is:

- **Red:** odd **3**, even **5**
- **Green:** odd **2**, even **9**
- **Blue:** odd **0**, even **1**
- **Yellow:** odd **7**, even **0**

The digit **0 is a valid final answer**.

A Blue LED with an odd raw result gives **0**.

A Yellow LED with an even raw result also gives **0**.

I do not treat zero as “no answer.”


<h2
  id="worked-solves"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Two complete Calculator solves
</h2>

### 55 + 2, then a Red light

Start with:

**55 + 2 = 57**

Enter:

**57**

The Calculator advances and shows a Red LED.

Now classify the raw result:

**57 is odd**

The table gives:

**Red + odd → 3**

So the complete solve is:

**55 + 2 → 57 → Red → odd → 3**

The important transition is between **57** and **Red**.

57 belongs to stage one.

3 belongs to stage two.


### 55 - 27, then a Green light

Calculate:

**55 - 27 = 28**

Enter:

**28**

After the Calculator advances:

**28 is even**

With a Green LED:

**Green + even → 9**

The complete solve is:

**55 - 27 → 28 → Green → even → 9**

This second example confirms the same process with subtraction and an even result rather than adding another special rule.


<h2
  id="callout"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Label the two numbers so they cannot be confused
</h2>

Calculator becomes much safer when the arithmetic result and the final key have different names.

I use:

**raw**

for the equation result,

and:

**final**

for the digit chosen from the LED table.

A compact exchange can sound like:

**“Calculator. Raw 57.”**

then, after the panel changes:

**“Red. Odd. Final 3.”**

The important part is not the exact wording.

It is that nobody hears an unlabeled number and has to guess whether it belongs to stage one or stage two.

If the team understands Calculator but numbers still get lost or attached to another module, I move to the <Link href="/bombanana/roles">BOMBANANA Roles Guide</Link> instead of adding more Calculator rules here.


<h2
  id="diagnose"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Diagnose the stage that failed
</h2>

When Calculator goes wrong, I check where the process stopped instead of restarting every rule from memory.

### Equation visible, but no LED

That is still stage one.

Solve the equation and enter the raw result.

### Raw result accepted, but no final answer yet

Read the new LED.

Then classify the **raw result** as odd or even.

Do not classify the final key.

### Final key looks impossible

Check the process in this order:

1. arithmetic;
2. parity;
3. LED color;
4. table lookup.

An arithmetic mistake changes parity and therefore contaminates every step after it.

### The correct digit was called, but the press failed

Look at the actual Calculator keypad.

Press the **digit printed on the button**, not the physical position where that digit normally appears on a phone, keyboard, or standard numpad.

The keypad layout can be shuffled.

### Nobody knows whether the first number was already entered

Stop using remembered state.

Read the Calculator as it appears now before pressing anything else.


<h2
  id="when-to-stop"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Re-read the panel when the current stage becomes uncertain
</h2>

I stop entering numbers when:

- two players heard different equations;
- nobody knows whether the raw result was already submitted;
- the LED was called before the raw result was accepted;
- odd/even is still being argued about;
- a mistake has already occurred;
- somebody remembers a number but not which stage it belonged to.

At that point, another guess is less useful than rebuilding the current state.

If the panel still shows the equation, I am in stage one.

If the raw result has been accepted and the LED is visible, I am in stage two.

I continue from what the bomb shows now rather than from what somebody remembers seeing before the mistake.


<h2
  id="solver-or-manual"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  When I use the solver
</h2>

During a live bomb, the solver is useful for both parts of Calculator:

**equation → raw result**

and then:

**LED + parity → final key**

The tool removes the need to scan the table while the timer is running.

The manual is still useful when learning the module because it makes the two-stage structure visible.

What I actually want to remember is not eight isolated final digits.

It is:

**do the math → enter it → read the new state → finish the lookup**

Once that sequence is automatic, the table itself becomes the easy part.

>
  Calculator is ready when nobody waits for the wrong stage
</h2>

I do not need the whole LED table memorized before moving deeper into the Campaign.

I do want two mistakes to disappear:

**waiting for an LED before solving the equation**

and:

**treating the raw arithmetic result as the final key**

If those are gone, the team understands the module's real structure.

The solver can handle forgotten table values.
