<h2 id="which-module" className="scroll-mt-24">
  I decide whether the panel belongs in the solver before entering a single value
</h2>

The solver works best on modules that can be reduced to a small set of stable inputs.

Right now, that means three cases.

**Cable / Wire** needs the number of cables, the active light, and the cable colors in their physical left-to-right order.

**Direction** needs the current light and the Braille number Blind is reading.

**Calculator** starts with the equation. The colored light only matters after the raw arithmetic result has been accepted.

Anything else is a bad fit for this tool.

A Symbol shape, a Piano sequence, a Soundboard position, a Morse pattern, or a moving Chaos hazard cannot be made reliable by forcing it into one of these three forms.

The stage indicator gets my attention before the numbers do.

If the panel has already advanced, the previous values are history. I would rather spend five seconds reading the new state than spend a strike proving that the old answer used to be correct.


<h2 id="cable" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Cable starts with physical order, not the color I happen to notice first
</h2>

Cable looks simple enough that it invites incomplete callouts.

Someone sees a blue cable.

Someone else notices the light.

A third player starts counting.

That is exactly how a valid Cable state gets assembled from pieces that may not belong together.

I collect the panel in one pass:

- total cable count;
- active light;
- every cable color from left to right.

The count comes first because a 3-cable panel and a 4-cable panel are not interchangeable.

The left-to-right order stays intact because the final answer has to become a physical cut on Blind's side. Knowing the target color is much less useful if the team then has to reconstruct where that color sits.

If the result points somewhere that does not make sense on the panel, I do not rescue the solve with a guess.

I check the count again.

Then the light.

Then the colors in order.

A missing cable, a duplicated-looking color, or one swapped position can make a perfectly functioning lookup appear wrong.

When the difficult part is the Cable rule itself rather than entering the state, the <Link href="/bombanana/wire">BOMBANANA Cable / Wire Guide</Link> has the full Cable workflow.


<h2 id="direction" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Direction is fast only when the light and Braille number are from the same stage
</h2>

Direction gives the solver very little information:

**one active light**

and:

**one Braille number**

That compact state is the reason it can be solved quickly. It is also the reason one stale value causes so much trouble.

A real call can be as small as:

**Green light + 5**

which returns:

**Right**

The physical input is immediate once both fields are confirmed.

<Image
  src="/images/bombanana/bombanana-direction-module-manual.webp"
  alt="BOMBANANA Direction Module manual showing the light and Braille number lookup"
  width={1600}
  height={900}
  className="my-8 rounded-xl"
/>

The dangerous part comes after the press.

Direction can advance instead of disappearing. If that happens, the new stage gets a fresh read.

I do not carry the old green light forward.

I do not carry the old five forward.

I do not assume the next direction is related to the previous one.

The current Direction lookup uses Braille numbers **1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, and 9**. If the reading appears to be an 8, that is a reason to inspect the Braille again before committing an input.

One more failure sits outside the solver entirely: **Left** is useless if two players mean two different lefts.

When the lookup is correct but the physical direction is still ambiguous, I use the <Link href="/bombanana/roles">BOMBANANA Roles & Communication Guide</Link> and settle the perspective before another press.


<h2 id="calculator" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Calculator changes the question halfway through the solve
</h2>

Calculator is the module where I am most careful about asking:

**What stage are we actually on?**

At the beginning, there is an equation.

That is all I need.

The colored light is not part of the first arithmetic step.

I calculate the raw result and enter it.

Only after the game accepts that result does the useful information change.

Now the Calculator is asking for:

- the new light;
- whether the raw result was odd or even.

Those two facts produce the final key.

<Image
  src="/images/bombanana/bombanana-calculator-module-manual.webp"
  alt="BOMBANANA Calculator Module manual showing the odd-even and active-light final key table"
  width={1600}
  height={900}
  className="my-8 rounded-xl"
/>

A clean real run makes the separation obvious.

The equation was:

**60 + 22**

The raw result was:

**82**

That result is even.

After 82 was accepted, the Calculator showed a **blue** light. The next answer was **1**, and pressing 1 completed the step.

The important part is not that 60 + 22 is easy arithmetic.

The important part is that **82** and **1** are two different answers used at two different moments.

If somebody hears “82” after the panel has already moved to the colored-light stage, the old number can sound completely legitimate while being completely useless.

For the detailed Calculator logic, examples, keypad handling, and final-key lookup, use the <Link href="/bombanana/calculator">BOMBANANA Calculator Guide & Solver</Link>.


<h2 id="chaos" className="scroll-mt-24">
  A rising Chaos hazard can make a good solver answer unsafe to use
</h2>

The solver does not calculate Chaos.

Chaos is doing something different: it can demand attention while another module is still waiting for an answer.

Suppose Direction has already been entered into the solver and the result is sitting on screen.

Before Blind presses, a warning hazard becomes urgent.

The team deals with the hazard.

Several seconds pass.

Now I do not automatically use the waiting Direction answer.

I look back at Direction first.

If the same light, Braille value, and stage are still there, the answer is still usable.

If anything changed, I solve what is on the bomb now.

The same rule applies to Cable and Calculator.

Chaos can interrupt the timing of the answer even when the lookup itself was perfect.

For Slider, Pressure, Alarm, interruption priority, and returning safely to a paused module, use the <Link href="/bombanana/chaos">BOMBANANA Chaos Guide</Link>.


<h2 id="stale-answer" className="scroll-mt-24">
  The solver result expires the moment the panel stops matching its inputs
</h2>

A stale answer is more dangerous than an obviously wrong one.

An obviously wrong result makes everybody hesitate.

A stale result often sounds familiar enough to get pressed again.

The usual failure is straightforward.

The solver returns a valid answer.

Blind uses it.

The panel advances.

Someone repeats the answer they still remember.

Blind uses it on the new state.

The first lookup was not bad. It was simply finished.

Calculator makes this especially easy to see. The equation state produces a raw result, but once that result is accepted, the active light belongs to a new decision.

Direction creates the same risk whenever a correct press advances the stage.

After any successful input, my attention goes back to the physical panel before the solver.

If the module cleared, there is nothing left to reuse.

If it advanced, I enter the new state.

If nothing happened, I verify whether the intended physical action was actually performed before changing the lookup.


<h2 id="bad-input" className="scroll-mt-24">
  An impossible-looking result usually sends me back to the panel, not deeper into the tool
</h2>

The solver only knows the state it receives.

If the output does not fit what is physically in front of Blind, I audit the observation before questioning the rule.

For **Cable / Wire**, the first checks are:

- 3 cables or 4;
- active light;
- every cable color;
- left-to-right order.

For **Direction**:

- current light;
- current Braille number;
- whether a previous press already advanced the module.

For **Calculator**:

- first number;
- second number;
- operator;
- raw result;
- whether the panel is still on the equation stage or has moved to the light stage.

The operator deserves special attention.

A misread `+` or `-` does not create a slightly wrong answer. It sends the entire Calculator solve down a different path while everything else can still look normal.

I also become suspicious when two modules were being called out at the same time.

“Blue, five, red, two” can contain four accurate observations and still be unusable if nobody remembers which values belonged together.

Once the state is mixed, adding one more number rarely repairs it.

The fastest recovery is usually a fresh read of the current panel.


<h2 id="communication" className="scroll-mt-24">
  A repeated correct lookup means the problem may have moved away from the solver
</h2>

There is a point where pressing Solve again stops adding information.

The tool says the same thing.

The table agrees.

The bomb still gets a strike.

Now I look at what happened between the returned answer and Blind's hand.

Direction may be solved as **Right**, but Blind may be waiting to learn whose right that means.

Cable may identify the correct target, but the final call may still describe a color instead of the physical cable position.

Calculator may have two correct numbers in circulation—the raw result and the final key—with neither one labeled by stage.

Those are not lookup failures.

The <Link href="/bombanana/roles">BOMBANANA Roles & Communication Guide</Link> is where I fix unclear gestures, unlabeled numbers, physical-position language, perspective, and information that changes meaning on the way back to Blind.


<h2 id="manual" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Some modules cannot be compressed into a few solver fields
</h2>

Cable, Direction, and Calculator fit this tool because a small amount of current state can produce one deterministic next action.

Many BOMBANANA modules ask for something else entirely.

**Symbol** depends on identifying shapes consistently.

**Piano** can involve an ordered note sequence.

**Soundboard** needs the team to identify a sound and keep track of positions.

**Morse** depends on reading and preserving a signal sequence.

**Maze** is spatial.

**Monkey-Says** can depend on what already happened in earlier stages.

**Color Slider** combines several ordered values.

**Chaos** is managed over time rather than cleared by one solver answer.

Those panels need their own method.

I do not choose Cable because a module happens to contain colors.

I do not choose Direction because a panel contains a number.

I do not choose Calculator because there is a light.

The solver only helps when the module itself and the required fields match.

If the question is where an unfamiliar module appears in Campaign, the <Link href="/bombanana/levels">BOMBANANA Levels & Walkthrough</Link> is the faster place to check.


<h2 id="during-run" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Under the timer, my eyes move from bomb to solver and straight back to bomb
</h2>

I use the tool in short bursts.

For Cable, I read the whole physical row before typing anything.

For Direction, I wait until the light and Braille number have both been confirmed.

For Calculator, I refuse to mix the equation stage with the later light stage.

Then the solver gives me one job.

I use that answer once.

My next glance is not at the previous result.

It is at the bomb.

That single habit prevents a surprising number of mistakes because BOMBANANA modules do not promise to stay in the same state after a correct action.

When the panel still matches, the answer remains useful.

When it changes, the next solve starts from the new state.

When the state cannot be read confidently, I wait instead of manufacturing a complete input from partial information.

The tool is fastest at one very specific moment:

**the module is known, the current state is complete, and the team needs the next physical action now.**
