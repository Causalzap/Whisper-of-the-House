<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
I do not spend every Abyss Artifact as soon as I get it.

I use them on the stat or skill that is **actually limiting what I am doing
right now**.

Early on, Stamina was the obvious problem. Later, my Stamina reached **Lv
10/14**, but another upgrade was blocked by Research knowledge and Red Seaweed,
so having more Abyss Artifacts would not have solved the problem.

At another point, **Spirit was only Lv 6/12** and was my lowest base stat, so I
spent there instead.

I also pushed Health through several levels until my Artifacts ran out, while
much later I was holding **21 Abyss Artifacts** and chose not to spend them
because my current setup was already doing what I needed.

My rule is:

**identify the actual bottleneck → check the full upgrade requirement → spend
only if the upgrade solves something → save the rest**
</GuideQuickAnswer>

<h2 id="what-i-spend-them-on">
  I spend Abyss Artifacts on the problem I can actually feel
</h2>

I stopped treating Abyss Artifacts like points that needed to disappear from my
inventory.

When I first understood the system, my instinct was simple:

**I have Artifacts, so I should upgrade something.**

That works early on because almost every improvement feels useful.

It becomes a worse habit later.

Health, Stamina, Spirit, and individual skills do not solve the same problems,
and higher upgrades can also ask for other materials or Research progress.

So before I spend anything, I ask what is currently getting in my way.

If I am constantly draining Stamina while moving, dodging, or climbing, I look
at Stamina.

If I already survive comfortably but keep running short of Spirit during the
skills I actually use, I look at Spirit.

If incoming damage is the problem, Health has a much clearer purpose.

If none of those problems is bothering me, I am comfortable keeping the
Artifacts.

</h2>

One inventory description changed how freely I spend them.

The Abyss Artifact itself says it can be used in the **Skills menu to enhance
skills**, or taken to a **blacksmith to refine equipment**.

If my current character already feels comfortable and I know I may want the

I do not need to predict every future upgrade.

I just avoid draining the stack for an improvement I cannot explain.

<h2 id="stamina-first">
  Stamina was useful early, but I stopped forcing it once Research became the blocker
</h2>

Stamina was one of the first stats I actively wanted more of.

That made sense for the way I was playing.

Stamina is being consumed while I am performing actions such as moving and
dodging, so I notice a weak Stamina pool constantly rather than only during one
specific fight.

Later, my Stamina had reached:

**Lv 10 / 14**

with:

**Max Stamina +200**

The next level would take it to:

**Max Stamina +220**

The upgrade screen showed that I had enough Abyss Artifacts for the Artifact
part:

**Abyss Artifact ×5 (15)**

But that was not enough.

**Max Spirit +60**

**Max Spirit +70**

If I am dying too easily, I keep pushing.

If survivability already feels comfortable, I stop before Health consumes the
entire reserve merely because another level is available.

<h2 id="skills-vs-stats">
  I do not automatically choose a skill over Health, Stamina, or Spirit
</h2>

Individual skills can look cheaper than base-stat upgrades.

For example, I had **21 Abyss Artifacts** while looking at **Stab Lv 1/3**.

The next enhancement only required:

**Abyss Artifact ×1**

The skill itself cost:

**40 Stamina**

to use.

This is where I stopped comparing upgrades only by Artifact cost.

A skill that I use constantly can absolutely be worth one Artifact.

A skill I rarely press is still a weak purchase even when it is cheap.

The question I use is:

**Will I actually notice this upgrade in the way I play?**

If yes, I spend.

If I am upgrading a skill only because the price says `×1`, I leave it alone.

<h2 id="when-i-save">
  Having 21 Abyss Artifacts did not make me spend 21 Abyss Artifacts
</h2>

The clearest change in my spending happened much later.

I had:

**21 Abyss Artifacts**

available.

Earlier in the game I would have looked at that stack and immediately tried to
max something.

This time I did not.

My current difficulty and build were already giving me enough.

I was not getting stopped by survivability.

I was not desperate for more Stamina.

I was not looking at Spirit and thinking it was ruining my combat.

So I left the stack alone.

I would have reconsidered if I raised the difficulty or started hitting a new
combat wall.

That gives me a very simple late-game rule:

**If nothing is currently failing, an unused Artifact is not a problem.**

I would rather keep 21 available for the next real need than turn them into a
series of upgrades I barely notice.

<h2 id="sealed-abyss-artifact">
  I do not confuse an Abyss Artifact with a Sealed Abyss Artifact objective
</h2>

The names are close enough that I initially treated them as the same thing.

They are not the same item in the decisions I am making here.

The **Abyss Artifact** in my inventory is the spendable resource I see in skill
and stat enhancement requirements.

A **Sealed Abyss Artifact** can appear as part of a challenge objective.

For example, I have seen challenges that ask me to:

**Use the sealed Abyss artifact**

and then award an **Abyss Artifact** after the challenge requirements are
completed.

So when a challenge tells me to use a Sealed Abyss Artifact, I do not expect
that wording to mean:

**spend one of the Artifacts currently sitting in my skill-upgrade stack.**

That distinction matters because otherwise it is very easy to mix up a
challenge requirement with the currency I am trying to budget.

<h2 id="when-i-spend">
  The order I use before I spend another Abyss Artifact
</h2>

I start with the problem, not the number in my inventory.

If I keep running dry while moving or dodging, I check Stamina.

If Spirit is limiting the skills and actions I actually use, I check Spirit.

If one mistake is taking too much of my life bar, I check Health.

If a favorite skill is part of nearly every fight, I look at that skill.

Then I read the full requirement.

If Research is blocking the node, I do Research.

If Red Seaweed or another material is missing, I get the material.

If the stack looks empty, I check storage before I leave camp.

And if my current setup already handles the content comfortably, I save the
Artifacts.

My spending loop is:

**find the real weakness**

→ **open the exact node**

→ **read every requirement**

→ **remove Research or material blockers first**

→ **spend only when the upgrade changes something I care about**

→ **keep the rest**

That is why I do not have one permanent answer to:

**Health, Stamina, or Spirit first?**

The answer changes with the point I am at.

What does not change is the rule:

**I spend Abyss Artifacts to remove a bottleneck, not just because I have them.**
