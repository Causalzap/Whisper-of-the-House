<h2 id="where-i-start">
  I start with the blocker, not the biggest icon on the map
</h2>

The part of Crimson Desert that slowed me down most was not the amount of
content.

It was deciding which piece of content mattered **right now**.

I can have a main quest ready while Research is running, crops are growing,
livestock need attention, hunting challenges are unfinished, new requests have
appeared, and several ways to make money are available.

If I react to every icon, I spend the whole session changing direction.

So I start with one question:

**What is preventing the next thing I actually want to do?**

If the answer is Silver, I trade.

If the answer is a camp resource, I work the farm or ranch.

If the answer is a wild animal or hunting challenge, I hunt.

If the answer is weak Health, Stamina, Spirit, or a skill I rely on, I check my
Abyss Artifacts.

If the answer is a quest state, Research interruption, or locked character, I
open the Journal.

Once that blocker is gone, I go back to the original goal instead of finishing
the entire system just because I started it.

<h2 id="trading">
  When I need Silver, I find the buyer before I load the wagon
</h2>

Trading is useful to me when I already know why I want the money.

The mistake I avoid now is buying a packaged good simply because the current
price looks cheap.

I find the destination first.

Then I decide whether the cargo deserves space on the wagon.

<h2 id="farming-ranching">
  I return to camp when production is the thing holding me back
</h2>

I do not stop at the farm every time I pass camp.

I return when I actually need something from it.

Sometimes that is a crop.

Sometimes a slower plant is lagging behind the rest.

Sometimes livestock need feed.

Sometimes the ranch is full of animals I no longer need.

<h2 id="hunting">
  I leave a normal hunting spot when the spawn stops being productive
</h2>

Hunting taught me not to confuse a known location with a guaranteed animal.

A place can be worth checking repeatedly without giving me the exact target
every visit.

So I no longer reach a familiar wildlife area, see the wrong spawn, and spend
the next twenty minutes circling it.

I check the area properly.

If the target is there, I use the opportunity.

If it is not, I leave and progress something else before coming back.

<h2 id="abyss-artifacts">
  I spend Abyss Artifacts when the upgrade fixes a real weakness
</h2>

Abyss Artifacts became much more useful once I stopped thinking of them as points
I was supposed to spend immediately.

The first thing I check is the problem I am trying to solve.

If movement and dodging keep draining me, Stamina gets my attention.

If I am comfortable surviving but keep running out of Spirit during the actions
I actually use, I look at Spirit.

If incoming damage is the problem, Health makes more sense.

If one skill is central to the way I fight, I consider that skill.

<h2 id="side-quests">
  When something looks missing or stuck, I check the quest state before the map
</h2>

Some of my longest detours started because I assumed a missing quest or unusable
system had to be a location problem.

Often it was not.

A request can be waiting on an earlier prerequisite.

Research can stop because a related quest needs attention.

A character can be unavailable because another mission is using them.

A Research Institute can be in the correct place but still not be ready for me
to use.

So the Journal is now my first stop.

<h2 id="money-or-resources">
  Before I grind anything, I decide what kind of shortage I actually have
</h2>

I wasted a surprising amount of time solving the wrong shortage.

A lack of Silver and a lack of materials can feel similar because both stop the
next action.

But they need completely different responses.

If the thing I need is bought with Silver, trading makes sense.

If I need something grown or produced at camp, I go back to farming and
ranching.

If I need something from wild animals, I hunt.

If the upgrade is already showing enough Abyss Artifacts but another requirement
is red, I solve that requirement.

If a request is locked behind progression, I stop farming resources entirely
and fix the quest state.

Before I start another grind, I ask:

**What exact requirement is still missing?**

That usually tells me where to go next.

<h2 id="fishing-cooking">
  I use fishing and cooking when they solve the next problem
</h2>

Fishing and cooking are useful parts of my progression, but I do not make either
one a permanent checklist.

With fishing, I care about what I need the catch for before turning a session
into a long collection run.

Once other systems let me keep or use fish more efficiently, I take advantage
of that and move on.

Cooking works the same way for me.

If a request wants a specific dish, or I actually need the food, I make it.

If nothing is waiting on that recipe, I do not interrupt a useful trading,
camp, hunting, or quest session just to complete another cooking entry.

For now, I treat both as supporting systems rather than the thing that decides
my whole route through the game.

<h2 id="when-i-go-back">
  I leave the detour once the blocker is gone
</h2>

I do not try to finish an entire system every time it interrupts the main thing
I wanted to do.

If I needed Silver, I stop the money run when I have enough for the next
purchase or upgrade.

If camp production was holding me back, I fix the crop, feed, or ranch problem
and leave once it is running again.

If a normal hunting target refuses to appear, I stop wasting the session on one
spawn.

If Research halted, I clear the interruption and let the timer run while I do
something else.

If an Abyss Artifact upgrade solves the weakness I was feeling, I take the
upgrade and move on instead of trying to empty the whole stack.

The question I use is:

**Did this detour remove the problem that sent me here?**

If yes, I go back.

<h2 id="my-progression-loop">
  The progression loop I use now
</h2>

I start each session with the main thing I want to accomplish.

When that goal stops moving, I identify the system responsible.

My normal loop is:

**main goal**

→ **identify the blocker**

→ **Trading if the blocker is Silver**

→ **Farm/Ranch if the blocker is camp production**

→ **Hunting if the blocker is wildlife or a hunting objective**

→ **Abyss Artifacts if the blocker is a stat or skill weakness**

→ **Journal if a quest, character, or Research state looks wrong**

→ **return to the main goal**

That has been much more useful than trying to clear every icon the moment I see
it.

Crimson Desert gives me plenty of reasons to take a detour.

I just want each detour to bring me back with something I actually needed.
