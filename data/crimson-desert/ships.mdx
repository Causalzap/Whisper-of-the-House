<h2 id="unlock-ships">
  How to Unlock Ships in Crimson Desert
</h2>

Buying **Charting the Unknown** does not immediately place a sailing ship in your inventory. Ships become available through the Greymane Camp progression, and the first ocean-related quest introduces the construction system.

The important requirement is **Expanding the Camp**, part of **Gathered Will** in the **Grounds of the Sunrise** Greymane faction storyline.

Finish **Howling Hill**, establish the Greymane Camp, and continue its expansion objectives until the new ocean quest becomes available.

You do **not** have to finish **Journey's End** to unlock sailing. The post-game character stories and Garden of Trials have different requirements.

### Where to Find A Wider World

Open **Journal → Faction Quests** and look for **A Wider World**.

The opening objective, **A Call from Beyond the Horizon**, asks you to speak with your comrades. During the conversation, the Greymanes discuss expanding their activities beyond the land and exploring an uncharted island across the southern sea.

Complete the conversation to unlock the Shipbuilding system.

A player returning to a completed save can easily miss this objective because the new character stories appear elsewhere in the Journal. Look in Faction Quests rather than continuing to search Kliff's post-game Main Quests.

If the quest has not appeared, check the following before visiting another port:

- **Howling Hill** is complete.
- Your Greymane Camp has been established and sufficiently expanded.
- The relevant **Gathered Will / Expanding the Camp** objectives are complete.
- You are playing as **Kliff** when advancing Greymane faction objectives.

The first shipbuilding objective starts with your comrades, not by purchasing an expensive vessel or traveling to a distant island.

<h2 id="build-first-ship">
  How to Build Your First Ship
</h2>

Once the opening conversation has finished, the game introduces a dispatch-based construction system.

Your comrades build ships at ports. The construction order is not placed through the normal camp building menu, which explains why searching the camp for a shipyard can lead nowhere.

Use the world map instead.

### Find Shipbuilding in the Port Menu

1. Open the **world map**.
2. Locate an available **port**.
3. Select the port and open its **Details**.
4. Switch to **Missions**.
5. Select **Shipbuilding**.
6. Choose the ship construction mission and review its requirements.

On PC, the shipbuilding tutorial directs you to select the port with a double left-click before entering its details.

The mission panel lists the currency, materials, and eligible comrades required for construction.

Check the requirements **before assigning workers**. Having enough funds does not guarantee that the dispatch can begin.

### Why Does Shipbuilding Say "Skill Requirements Not Met"?

This is one of the first genuine obstacles in the construction process.

A comrade may be available but still lack the ability needed for Shipbuilding. Simply filling the dispatch with any idle characters does not satisfy the job requirement.

Small Shipbuilding requires **five comrades** and the appropriate **Engineering** capability. The skill list includes Engineering I, Skilled Engineer II, and Expert Engineer III, all of which allow engineering missions to be carried out.

If the dispatch displays **Skill requirements not met**, check the Engineering icons before assigning more workers. The selected crew must satisfy the skill requirement as well as the five-person requirement.

Qualified comrades may already be marked **Dispatched** on other missions. Find an available engineer or free one from a lower-priority assignment before attempting construction again.

The fastest fix is to free an existing qualified comrade rather than hiring several unsuitable workers.

During one early construction attempt, workers were already committed to other assignments, including a tomato-harvesting dispatch. Canceling that assignment freed personnel who could be reassigned to Shipbuilding.

Use this order to fix the failure:

1. Inspect the skill icons required by the Shipbuilding mission.
2. Check whether your qualified comrades are already working elsewhere.
3. Cancel or finish a lower-priority dispatch.
4. Assign the newly available qualified comrades.
5. Confirm that the construction requirements are satisfied before dispatching.

Contribution Workers can fill empty personnel slots, but they cannot substitute for the required Engineering skill.

However, hiring additional workers does not automatically resolve a missing Engineering skill. Check the skill requirement first, then use Contribution Workers when you also need more personnel.

### How Long Does It Take to Build a Small Ship?

The **Small Shipbuilding** mission shown at the port requires **five comrades** and takes **12 in-game hours**. Its Materials panel lists four resource requirements: **600, 600, 1,200, and 10,000** of the respective displayed resources.

Check the resource icons in the mission panel before dispatching. Having all five comrades available does not remove the material or Engineering requirements.

Once the proper comrades are assigned and the required resources are available, confirm the dispatch. The game begins counting down the construction period.

You do not have to stand at the port while the workers build.

Sleeping advances the in-game clock, so the 12-hour construction can be completed much faster than waiting through normal play.

The exact construction requirements depend on the ship selected. Read the resource quantities displayed in your current mission panel rather than assuming that the small-ship requirements also apply to a medium or large vessel.

Construction consumes camp resources as well as the required funds. Those resource requirements are an important reason to consider buying a ship instead when your camp stockpiles are low.

When construction finishes, the mission updates and your new ship becomes available through the port.

<h2 id="buy-or-build">
  Should You Buy a Ship or Build One?
</h2>

You have two ways to obtain a ship after unlocking the system.

**Shipbuilding dispatches** use comrades, camp resources, and construction time. Purchasing from a **Fleetmaster** skips the construction process but requires enough Camp Funds to pay the listed price.

The tradeoff becomes significant with larger ships.

### Which Ship Should You Get First?

The **Small Hernandian Ship** is the practical starting point for most players who want to complete the early ocean activities.

It provides access to ship crew management and ordinary sailing without committing the much larger amount required for a medium or large vessel.

The **Humble Rowboat** is cheaper, but its limited capability makes it a poor substitute for a proper sailing ship when you intend to carry comrades, explore islands, or prepare for naval combat.

For a newly unlocked ocean route, I would prioritize getting a working small ship over spending 1.2 million Camp Funds on a medium vessel immediately.

The medium ship becomes more attractive when your available funds and maritime activities can justify the upgrade.

A large ship costs **6 million Camp Funds**. Purchasing it as your first serious vessel ties up a substantial amount of resources before you have learned which parts of the ocean you will actually use.

### When Is Building Better Than Buying?

Build the ship when your camp already has the needed materials and you can spare qualified comrades.

The construction timer is less important when you can sleep through most of it. A 12-hour dispatch is a manageable delay compared with gathering a large amount of additional Camp Funds.

Buying makes more sense when:

- Your camp has enough funds for the vessel.
- Engineering-capable comrades are unavailable.
- Construction would require a lengthy resource-gathering trip.
- You need the ship immediately rather than waiting for dispatch completion.

Shipbuilding is generally the more resource-efficient option in terms of Camp Funds, but it draws on materials that your camp may need for other work.

Do not start gathering an arbitrary quantity of timber or ore before checking the selected Shipbuilding mission. The required materials are displayed in the construction interface, and different ship sizes can have different requirements.

<h2 id="fleetmaster">
  Where to Buy and Manage Ships
</h2>

**Fleetmasters** operate at ports across Pywel.

They allow you to purchase vessels and access ship management. Fleet offices can be found in locations including **Vellua, Demeniss, and Delesyia**.

At Vellua, **Hopkins** operates as a ship seller in the Grace Estate area.

The Fleetmaster's available options depend on the port and your current progression. Use the ship list to compare the vessel you can afford with the one you already own.

<h2 id="assign-crew">
  How to Assign Crew Before Setting Sail
</h2>

Completing construction does not automatically make your ship ready to depart.

The Ship Manager requires you to assign comrades to the ship's crew positions.

**The Manage Crew screen shows three mandatory positions for departure:**

- **First Mate — 1 required**
- **Rigger — 1 required**
- **Lookout — 1 required**

Each missing position appears in red under **Departure Requirements**. Assign all three before trying to leave the harbor.

The ship also has other positions, including Carpenter, Gunner, and Deckhand, but the displayed departure warning specifically identifies First Mate, Rigger, and Lookout.

At the harbor, speak with the Ship Manager or Fleetmaster, open the ship management interface, select your owned vessel, and enter the crew assignment screen.

The available positions include:

- **First Mate:** Helps operate the ship when you are away from the helm.
- **Lookout:** Supports navigation and spotting objects or activity at sea.
- **Carpenter:** Helps maintain and repair the vessel.
- **Gunner:** Operates the ship's cannons during naval combat.
- **Rigger:** Supports sail handling and sailing performance.
- **Deckhands:** Perform general shipboard work and support activities such as island dispatches.

### Which Crew Roles Matter Most?

Your priorities should reflect the next activity.

For the first exploratory voyage, meet the required crew assignments and make sure you can operate the vessel normally.

A **Gunner** becomes important when you expect to fight pirates or other hostile ships. A ship equipped with cannons is not automatically ready to fire them without the crew needed to operate them.

A **Carpenter** matters more when combat or rough travel may damage your hull.

For newly discovered islands, keep suitable **Deckhands** available for activities requiring comrades to be dispatched.

You can assign a known comrade such as Matthias to a crew position if he is available in your roster, but a familiar character is not necessarily the most efficient choice for every role.

Match the job to the abilities and personnel you have rather than assigning your strongest combat companion to every available position.

Crew changes are handled in port. Check the assignments before leaving because discovering an empty combat position after sailing into hostile waters may force an unnecessary return trip.

<h2 id="first-voyage">
  How to Set Sail for the First Time
</h2>

After your first ship is ready, continue the ocean quest until it sends you to the harbor.

The next stage includes **Where the Wind Blows**.

Before departure, speak with your comrades near the harbor, inspect the requested supplies, and visit the Ship Manager.

The harbor also contains a **Bounty Notice Board** displaying information about pirate ships. You do not need to hunt pirates before completing your first voyage.

The crew introduces a **Harpoon Gun** during these preparations. Unlike ordinary ranged weapons, the Harpoon Gun can be used underwater.

Keep it available when exploring the ocean, but do not confuse it with the ship's cannons. They serve different purposes.

Once the crew is assigned, board your vessel and interact with the helm.

### Sailing Controls on PC

The ship's introductory controls include:

- **W / A / S / D:** Steer and control the ship.
- **Left Shift:** Unfurl the sails to increase sailing speed.
- **Alt:** Furl the sails to reduce speed.
- **Q:** Adjust the camera for naval combat.
- **Right Mouse Button:** Fire the ship's cannons during the relevant combat interaction.

Wind direction changes how effectively your sails work. When the wind blows from behind, the square sails unfurl and provide a sailing advantage.

When sailing into a headwind, the square sails do not unfurl, and the ship receives no wind assistance. This does not necessarily mean the wind applies an additional speed penalty.

Adjust your heading and sail settings instead of assuming that unfurling the sails always produces the same speed.

### Why Does the Ship Feel Difficult to Steer?

Camera orientation can make navigation confusing, especially when looking backward during combat.

The ship may respond differently from what you expect when your viewpoint changes. This is particularly noticeable while tracking a pursuing enemy and attempting to turn at the same time.

For ordinary travel, keep the camera oriented in the direction you want to sail. During a fight, check which way the ship actually turns before making a large steering correction.

I would practice changing direction and adjusting sails in clear water before approaching a pirate vessel. Learning the controls while taking cannon fire is a much more expensive mistake.

<h2 id="ship-upgrades">
  How to Upgrade and Customize Ships
</h2>

There are two distinct ways to improve your sailing capability.

The first is to obtain a **larger ship**, increasing your available space and capability.

The second is to **customize your existing vessel** with ship parts.

Fleetmasters in **Demeniss and Delesyia** sell customization parts, including:

- Figureheads
- Stern components
- Sails

These are purchased separately from the vessel itself.

Buying a large ship does not mean every customization option is automatically included, and changing one part does not convert your existing ship into a larger vessel.

### When Should You Upgrade to a Medium Ship?

The medium vessel becomes worthwhile once your small ship is limiting an activity you intend to perform regularly.

The clearest reasons are increased cargo demand, more extensive naval operations, or the need for additional crew capacity.

For occasional island exploration, spending **1,200,000 Camp Funds** immediately may provide less practical value than keeping those funds available for camp activities and your first maritime trades.

Use the small ship until you understand its limitations.

When trading or combat becomes a recurring part of your routine, the medium vessel is a more sensible investment.

### Is the Large Ship Worth 6 Million?

The large vessel is the expensive option for players planning sustained maritime activities.

Its value depends on whether you can actually use the additional capacity.

A player who spends most of their time exploring on foot or diving near the coast has little reason to spend 6 million immediately.

The large ship makes more sense when your trade routes, crew management, and frequent voyages have already made the medium ship feel restrictive.

<h2 id="ship-combat">
  What to Do Before Fighting Pirate Ships
</h2>

The Great Ocean contains hostile vessels, including pirates that travel established routes.

Check the **Bounty Notice Board** at a harbor to learn where dangerous pirate activity has been reported.

Some pirates operate in groups or fleets, so an encounter may involve more than a single enemy ship.

Your first small ship can carry cannons, but preparation matters more than simply owning a cannon-equipped vessel.

Make sure a Gunner is assigned before leaving port and avoid committing to a fight when you are unfamiliar with the steering controls.

### Avoiding an Early Naval Defeat

The most dangerous situation for a new sailor is fighting while struggling to maintain the correct heading.

You can lose control of the engagement when:

- The camera is aimed backward while the ship is moving forward.
- Wind direction reduces your ability to reposition.
- Your cannon aim is obstructed by the combat interface.
- You remain close to another hostile ship while already taking damage.

The safer first encounter is one you can disengage from.

Keep open water available, adjust the sails when you need to maneuver, and avoid allowing multiple ships to surround your vessel.

A larger ship will eventually provide more room for combat preparation, but purchasing one is not the only answer to difficult naval controls.

<h2 id="repair-ship">
  How to Repair or Recover a Damaged Ship
</h2>

A ship can take serious damage from naval combat or dangerous ocean conditions.

One of the worst places to learn this is near a whirlpool. Strong currents can make steering difficult, and entering the center without a reliable route out can result in the vessel being destroyed.

When your ship is lost, return to a Fleetmaster or Ship Manager to access the available recovery and repair options.

A destroyed ship does not necessarily mean you must rebuild it from the beginning.

### How Much Does Ship Repair and Retrieval Cost?

When no ship is nearby, the Ship Manager can offer to **repair and retrieve a ship**.

At Vellua, Hopkins displayed a total cost of **19,880** for one repair-and-retrieval operation.

Check the amount in your own confirmation window before accepting. The displayed 19,880 is the total for that specific recovery, not a fixed repair price for every ship or damage state.

<h2 id="first-island">
  What to Do After Your Ship Reaches an Uncharted Island
</h2>

Once you can sail, the ocean opens into new islands and exploration opportunities.

The first voyage introduces an **Uncharted Island** and the **Pioneering** system.

Discovering an island does not automatically establish a permanent Greymane presence there.

A pioneering mission creates an **Anchorage**, which provides a way to retrieve your ship and supports further activity on that island.

To begin:

1. Approach the island and open the world map.
2. Switch to the facility view and locate the Anchorage.
3. Select it to inspect the Pioneering mission.
4. Check its currency and comrade requirements.
5. Dispatch eligible personnel once those requirements are met.

After an island has been pioneered, additional resource-gathering missions become available.

This is another reason to pay attention to your available comrades. Shipbuilding, crew assignments, and island operations can compete for the same personnel.

The efficient progression is to secure a functioning ship, learn to sail, then commit personnel to an island once you know which resources or exploration opportunities you want from it.

For underwater exploration, carry the **Harpoon Gun** and appropriate diving equipment before making a long trip away from the ship. The Harpoon Gun can fire underwater, while ordinary ranged weapons cannot.

The first island also introduces additional exploration opportunities, including treasure and new materials, but those activities do not require buying a medium or large ship before you begin.
