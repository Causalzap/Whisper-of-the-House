<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
CONTROL Resonant's Manhattan map is split into **seven main zones**:
**Downtown, Central, Evacuation Zone, West Incursion Zone, The Park,
Underpass, and Unknown**.

Central becomes the main early-game point of orientation, with the
Evacuation Zone to the east and West Incursion Zone to the west.

To reveal a normal zone map, activate its **Map Kit Pylon**, turn on all
**three Sensors**, then return to the Pylon and interact with it again.
A revealed map does not mean every route is already open — story gates,
traversal abilities, Mold, Patterning, and mapless interiors can still block
the path.

<h2 id="all-zones">
  Full CONTROL Resonant map overview
</h2>

The full Manhattan map is built around **seven main zones**, but they do not
all behave like equal open-world districts.

Some act as broad street-level regions, while later areas become increasingly
vertical, distorted, or partially mapless. That difference matters because the
map is good at showing your general position, but it does not always tell you
whether Dylan can actually reach the route in front of him.

<h2 id="zone-connections">
  How the CONTROL Resonant zones connect
</h2>

The game opens Manhattan gradually rather than presenting all seven zones as a
single level-select screen.

**Downtown** is the opening district. The story then pushes Dylan toward
**Central**, which becomes the easiest place to use as a geographic reference
for much of the game.

From Central, two of the earliest major directions are easy to remember:
the **Evacuation Zone lies east**, while the **West Incursion Zone lies west**.

The Park becomes relevant as wider Manhattan exploration opens, but it works
better as a branch of the larger world than as a mandatory step between every
main-story region.

Later progression through the Sinkhole and deeper FBC operations leads toward
the **Underpass**. **Unknown** is the final major Manhattan zone to open and
changes the navigation rules more dramatically than the earlier street-level
areas.

This is why I do not treat the map as a strict zone-by-zone checklist. Once
Central opens up, several objectives can run in parallel and the useful
question becomes **where the current objective is and whether Dylan already
has the ability to reach it**.

For the actual story order and what to do next between those branches, use the
<Link href="/control-resonant/walkthrough"> main progression walkthrough</Link>.

<h2 id="how-to-unlock-map">
  How to reveal each zone map
</h2>

Entering a new part of Manhattan does not automatically uncover its map.

Mapped zones use an FBC **Map Kit** built around one central Pylon and
**three nearby Sensors**.

The full sequence is:

1. Find the main Map Kit Pylon.
2. Interact with the Pylon.
3. Locate all three Sensors tied to that kit.
4. Activate each Sensor.
5. Return to the original Pylon.
6. Interact with it again to bring the local map data online.

The last return trip is the part that is easiest to miss.

If all three Sensors are active but the zone is still blank, do not keep
searching for a fourth Sensor. Go back to the central Pylon and use it again.

There is no purchase or upgrade cost attached to this process. Map Kits are
exploration infrastructure rather than a progression upgrade.

committing to a long objective. CONTROL Resonant frequently uses vertical

having the general zone layout visible saves much more time than the short
detour needed to reveal it.

<h2 id="zone-guide">
  All seven CONTROL Resonant map zones
</h2>

<h3 id="downtown">
  Downtown
</h3>

**Downtown** is the starting Manhattan zone and contains the Oldest House.

The opening route is comparatively directed, which makes Downtown more useful
as an introduction to the navigation systems than as a district you should try
to fully clear immediately.

Get the local mapping system working, restore the route toward Central, and
continue with the story rather than treating every visible branch as something
that must already be reachable.

Downtown becomes more useful to revisit later once additional traversal options
open routes that were visible much earlier.

<h3 id="central">
  Central
</h3>

**Central** becomes the practical navigation hub for a large part of the game.

Its most useful reference points include the **Field Office**, the Watchtower,
subway routes, and connections used by several major objectives.

Because so many missions begin, end, or reconnect around FBC operations here,
Central is the first place I mentally reset my position when the story sends
Dylan back across Manhattan.

The area is not completely static. Patterning and later objectives can reuse
familiar locations in altered states, so the map is more useful for identifying
the general district and landmark than for assuming every street will always
work the same way.

For the larger mission structure, use the
<Link href="/control-resonant/quests"> quest and mission guide</Link>.

<h3 id="evacuation-zone">
  Evacuation Zone
</h3>

The **Evacuation Zone** lies east of Central.

Its major early landmarks include the evacuation site and the **Sinkhole**.
The surface streets are only part of the region's importance because later
progression sends Dylan much farther below Manhattan.

Once an objective has moved deep into the Sinkhole, do not expect the surface
zone map to describe every corridor and interior route underneath it.

The distinction becomes increasingly important as the story pushes beyond the
normal street map and toward deeper FBC-controlled spaces.

<h3 id="west-incursion">
  West Incursion Zone
</h3>

The **West Incursion Zone** lies west of Central and is one of the earliest
areas where severe Patterning makes the city stop behaving like an ordinary
street grid.

Buildings, roads, and usable surfaces can fold into unnatural orientations.

That means the map works best here as a **general position and objective
reference**, not as a promise that every visible road will behave like a normal
Manhattan street.

When a route appears close on the map but impossible in front of Dylan, look at
the environment and available traversal abilities before assuming you have
missed a normal doorway.

<h3 id="the-park">
  The Park
</h3>

**The Park** becomes available as wider Manhattan exploration opens and is
defined by heavy Mold contamination.

Its routes extend across the surface and into sewer and underground spaces,
which makes direct map distance less useful than it first appears.

Mold can block what looks like the obvious route, so objectives here often make
more sense when you think in terms of **which entrance is currently usable**
rather than which marker is geographically closest.

The Park also works more like a branch of the larger progression structure than
a required link between every main-story zone.

For the actual Mold route, locations, and boss progression, use the
<Link href="/control-resonant/the-park"> The Park guide</Link>.

<h3 id="underpass">
  Underpass
</h3>

The **Underpass** is a later subterranean zone reached after progression through
the Sinkhole and deeper FBC operations has moved forward.

It is not simply another floor of the Evacuation Zone.

The area introduces much larger vertical drops, research sites, overlapping
iterations, and sections where a normal surface-style map becomes less useful.

That is why the map can tell you roughly where the objective sits while still
leaving the actual route difficult to read.

Around the Research Site in particular, the environment and people inside it
can appear in different iterations.

For the route through those sections, use the
<Link href="/control-resonant/underpass"> Underpass guide</Link>.

<h3 id="unknown">
  Unknown
</h3>

**Unknown** is the final major Manhattan zone to open.

Instead of relying mainly on streets and buildings, navigation shifts toward
floating Patterned structures, altered gravity, and late-game traversal.

By this point, the map is better used to understand the broad arrangement of
the zone than to trace a normal road from one objective to another.

Do not assume every structure visible in the distance can be reached directly
from the platform Dylan is currently standing on. Later progression uses
Pattern geometry, relay equipment, and other traversal requirements to bridge
gaps that initially look impossible.

<h2 id="blocked-route">
  Why a revealed map can still show an unreachable route
</h2>

Revealing a zone and being able to fully explore that zone are two different
things.

Dylan can have the map online and still run into:

- gaps that require a later traversal ability;
- Patterned barriers tied to story progress;
- locked FBC access;
- Mold-blocked routes;
- interiors whose geometry has changed;
- dungeon-like spaces that do not provide a normal local map.

When this happens, do not assume the Map Kit is incomplete.

First check the current mission and Dylan's available traversal abilities.
Several locations are deliberately visible before the game gives you the power
or story state needed to reach them.

This is especially common once the game starts mixing vertical routes and
Patterned geometry with otherwise familiar Manhattan landmarks.

<h2 id="map-not-revealing">
  Why the map is still blank after finding a Map Kit
</h2>

There are three common situations that look like a broken Map Kit.

**You only activated the main Pylon.**  
The Pylon is the starting point, not the entire process. Find and activate all
three Sensors tied to it.

**All three Sensors are active, but nothing changed.**  
Return to the original Pylon and interact with it again. That final interaction
is what brings the collected map data online.

**You are inside a mapless interior.**  
Some Patterned or dungeon-like spaces do not provide their own usable local
map. Revealing the surrounding Manhattan zone does not generate a floor plan
for every enclosed area.

Once the Pylon updates successfully, use the map for **zone-scale orientation**.
For enclosed spaces, rely more heavily on objective markers, environmental
landmarks, and the geometry Dylan can actually traverse.

<h2 id="where-to-go-next">
  Where to go after revealing a new zone
</h2>

Do not try to fully clear one zone just because its map is now visible.

After Central opens, CONTROL Resonant frequently runs objectives in parallel.
One mission can send Dylan east, another Resonant can pull him west, while a
side objective or traversal unlock creates a reason to revisit somewhere you
passed much earlier.

Use the map to answer **where the objective is** and **which landmark gets you
closest**.

routes reconnect, continue with the
