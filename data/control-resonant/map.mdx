<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
To reveal a zone map, find its **Map Kit Pylon**, activate the **three nearby Sensors**, then return to the Pylon and use it again. CONTROL Resonant has **seven main zones**, but several later areas only become reachable as the story and Dylan's traversal abilities progress.

<h2 id="how-to-unlock-map">
  How to reveal the map in CONTROL Resonant
</h2>

The map does not uncover itself just because Dylan enters a new part of Manhattan.

Each mapped zone uses an FBC **Map Kit** built around one main Pylon and **three Sensors**. The opening setup teaches the system before Dylan reaches the Central Checkpoint, and the same basic rule applies when you find later kits.

The sequence is:

1. find the main Map Kit Pylon;
2. locate its three nearby Sensors;
3. activate all three Sensors;
4. return to the Pylon;
5. use the Pylon again to bring the local map data online.

The FBC dropped these kits into Manhattan before the airspace became too unstable. Each kit's three Sensors scan for Hiss, Mold, Patterning, and resonance around the area.

When I reach a new zone, I usually handle its Map Kit before doing anything that sends me deep into the area. CONTROL Resonant regularly bends streets, removes familiar sight lines, and connects objectives through vertical routes, so having the zone visible saves much more time than it costs.

<h2 id="map-kit-sensors">
  You need all three Sensors before the Pylon fully reveals the zone
</h2>

A Map Kit is not complete when you first interact with the central structure.

There are **three Sensors** tied to it.

The Sensors are separate pieces of equipment around the nearby area. Look for the signal-style map icons and the FBC hardware rather than searching for another large Pylon.

Activate each Sensor at close range, then return to the central Pylon.

That return trip is easy to miss. If you have touched all three Sensors but the map is still blank, go back to the main Pylon and interact with it again.

There is no resource cost and nothing to purchase. Map Kits are exploration infrastructure, not upgrades.

<h2 id="all-zones">
  All seven CONTROL Resonant map zones
</h2>

Manhattan is divided into **seven main zones**:

- **Downtown** — the opening streets around the Oldest House.
- **Central** — the Field Office, Watchtower, subway connections, and several major story routes.
- **Evacuation Zone** — the Evac Building and the entrance to the Sinkhole.
- **West Incursion Zone** — severe Patterning, fractured gravity, and the Theater route.
- **The Park** — heavy Mold contamination with surface, sewer, and underground connections.
- **Underpass** — later underground routes, research sites, large drops, and overlapping time states.
- **Unknown** — floating Patterned structures and heavily distorted gravity.

The **Gap** and the **Oldest House** are important locations, but they are not counted as part of those seven Manhattan zones.

Some interior spaces also behave more like enclosed mission areas than normal map regions. They may not provide a usable local map even when the surrounding zone has already been revealed.

<h2 id="early-map">
  Central becomes your main point of orientation
</h2>

After the opening, Zoe introduces the first useful geographic split from the Watchtower.

Central is around the FBC's current operations.

The **Evacuation Zone is east**.

The **West Incursion Zone is west**.

That briefing can make the game sound more linear than it actually becomes.

Once Dylan has reached the Field Office and collected the needed access, more than one direction can become useful at the same time. A mission marker in one zone does not mean every other accessible zone should be ignored until that mission is finished.

<h2 id="downtown">
  Downtown
</h2>

**Downtown** is the starting Manhattan zone.

The Oldest House sits here, and the opening sequence teaches most of the navigation systems Dylan needs before the map opens up farther.

This is where the first Map Kit setup is introduced.

The early route is still heavily directed, so there is little value in trying to clear Downtown as though it were a full open-world district immediately. Get the mapping system working, restore the route through the Central Checkpoint, then follow the story into Central.

<h2 id="central">
  Central
</h2>

**Central** becomes the practical hub for a large part of the game.

Important locations here include the FBC **Field Office**, the Watchtower, several subway routes, and connections used by major story objectives.

Central also hosts parts of **Power Lines**, including the Vanished Station sequence.

Because so many missions start or end around FBC operations here, a new call from Pope, Zoe, or Arish often means Central is worth revisiting even when your last objective finished somewhere else.

The area itself is not fully static. Patterning can change routes and later objectives can reuse familiar parts of the zone in altered states.

<h2 id="evacuation-zone">
  Evacuation Zone
</h2>

The **Evacuation Zone** lies east of Central.

Its defining early landmark is the evacuation site, where Dylan investigates the resonance tremors and finds the trapped civilians.

The other major geographic feature is the **Sinkhole**.

That descent eventually becomes much more important than the surface streets. The mission route sends Dylan far below Manhattan, and later story progression reaches deeper underground areas that are no longer treated as part of the normal Evacuation Zone map.

If your objective has already moved into the Sinkhole, do not expect the surface map to represent every interior route below it.

<h2 id="west-incursion">
  West Incursion Zone
</h2>

The **West Incursion Zone** is west of Central and is one of the earliest areas associated with severe Patterning.

Zoe describes it as the part of the city suffering some of the worst spatial distortion, while Pope identifies it as an early place to look for Resonant activity.

Navigation here becomes less dependable if you think in normal street-grid terms.

Buildings and roads can fold into unnatural orientations, so the map is most useful for keeping track of your general position and objective direction rather than assuming every visible road behaves like an ordinary Manhattan street.

<h2 id="the-park">
  The Park
</h2>

**The Park** becomes available as the wider Manhattan exploration opens.

Mold is the dominant environmental threat here, and the region also connects into underground and sewer routes.

It is not required in exactly the same way as every main-story zone. You can reach important late-game progression without treating The Park as a mandatory step between every other area.

That makes it a good place to return to when an objective, Resonant hunt, side activity, or cleanup specifically sends you there rather than forcing it into the main route too early.

<h2 id="underpass">
  Underpass
</h2>

The **Underpass** is a later subterranean zone reached after the Sinkhole and CFD progression has moved forward.

It is not simply a lower floor of the Evacuation Zone.

Zoe explicitly names the deeper area the Underpass once Dylan descends far enough, and its navigation works differently from the earlier Sinkhole run.

The zone includes:

- deep vertical drops;
- FBC research camps;
- time fluctuations;
- overlapping versions of the same expedition;
- routes beneath the Oldest House.

The <Link href="/control-resonant/underpass">Underpass route</Link> becomes especially confusing around the Research Site because the environment and the people inside it can appear in different iterations.

<h2 id="unknown">
  Unknown
</h2>

**Unknown** is the final major Manhattan zone to open.

It is a heavily Patterned area made from fractured pieces of the city suspended in altered space. Normal ground-level navigation matters much less here than reading the floating geometry around Dylan.

Unknown is tied to late-game Hedron progression.

By the time you reach it, the game expects you to be comfortable with traversal abilities and with Patterning changing the usable route in front of you.

Do not assume that every structure visible in the distance can be reached directly from the island or platform you are currently standing on. Later objectives use manipulated Pattern geometry, relay equipment, and other progression requirements to bridge otherwise impossible gaps.

<h2 id="zone-order">
  What order should you explore the zones?
</h2>

For main-story progression, the practical order is roughly:

**Downtown → Central → Evacuation Zone / West Incursion Zone → Underpass → Unknown**

**The Park** sits outside that straight line and can be explored when its objectives become relevant.

This is not a strict level-select sequence.

After Central, several objectives begin running in parallel. The game may send you into one zone for a story mission, another for a Resonant, and another for a fault or traversal unlock.

So I use the map to answer **where an objective is**, not to decide that I must completely finish one zone before entering another.

<h2 id="blocked-route">
  A revealed map does not mean every route is already accessible
</h2>

The map and Dylan's actual movement options are separate systems.

You can reveal a zone and still encounter:

- gaps that require a later traversal ability;
- Patterned barriers tied to story progress;
- locked FBC access;
- Mold-blocked routes;
- interiors whose geometry has changed;
- dungeon-like spaces with no local map.

When that happens, do not assume the Map Kit is incomplete.

Check the current mission and Dylan's available traversal abilities first.

Several places are deliberately visible before you have the power or story state needed to reach them.

<h2 id="map-not-revealing">
  Why the map is still blank after finding a Map Kit
</h2>

There are three common states that look like a broken Map Kit.

**You only activated the main Pylon.**  
Find the three Sensors around it. The Pylon alone does not finish the scan.

**All three Sensors are active, but nothing changed.**  
Return to the central Pylon and use it again. The final interaction is what brings the collected map data online.

**You are inside a dungeon-like location.**  
Some interiors do not have their own usable maps. Revealing the surrounding Manhattan zone does not create a floor plan for every Patterned interior.

Once the Pylon updates successfully, use the map for zone-scale navigation and rely on objective markers, environmental clues, and the actual geometry for enclosed spaces.
