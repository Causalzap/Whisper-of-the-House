The first hours of **CONTROL Resonant** can make Manhattan feel more open than it really is.

Central quickly becomes the place Dylan keeps returning to, while the Evacuation Zone, West Incursion Zone, The Park, Underpass, and later distorted areas open through story progress, FBC access, and traversal abilities. Several missions can also run at the same time, so the closest marker is not always the objective that moves the story forward.

The most reliable way to recover when I lose the route is to read the exact quest name and objective in the upper-left corner before moving anywhere.

<h2 id="where-to-go-next">
  Start with the quest name, not the nearest marker
</h2>

CONTROL Resonant begins fairly linearly, but that changes once Dylan reaches Central.

From there, the game starts mixing three kinds of progress:

- the main crisis around Manhattan and the Oldest House;
- Jesse's Faults, which unlock important traversal;
- regional Resonants, some of which are required before the story can continue.

That is why finishing one objective does not always produce another obvious marker in the same district.

If you only want the order of the main campaign, follow the <Link href="/control-resonant/walkthrough">main story walkthrough</Link>.

If the Quest Log has several active missions and you are trying to work out which one is blocking the next step, use the <Link href="/control-resonant/quests">CONTROL Resonant quest list</Link>. It separates the main story, Jesse Faults, Resonant quests, and Side Stories so you can see which chain still needs attention.

<h2 id="early-manhattan">
  Central, Evacuation, and West Incursion are the first major split
</h2>

After the opening, Central becomes the practical center of Dylan's operations.

The Watchtower briefing establishes the early geography:

- **Central** around the FBC's current operations;
- **Evacuation Zone** to the east;
- **West Incursion Zone** to the west.

Those regions do not behave like three chapters that must be completely cleared one after another. Objectives begin overlapping fairly early.

If you are still restoring access through Central, follow the <Link href="/control-resonant/central-checkpoint">Central Checkpoint route</Link>. The important mechanic there is moving the electrical charge through Power Core relays rather than trying to carry it directly to the Checkpoint.

Once Zoe has opened the larger Manhattan objectives, follow the active mission instead of repeating the Central route.

<h2 id="evacuation-zone">
  Evacuation Procedures leads into the larger containment plan
</h2>

The Evacuation Zone begins with the search for the source of the resonance tremors.

Follow the southeast road from Central and use the <Link href="/control-resonant/evacuation-zone">Evacuation Procedures walkthrough</Link> when your objective sends Dylan to the Evac Building.

The important result is larger than simply clearing that building. Dylan finds Arish, gains **Security Clearance Level 2**, and learns that the tremor source cannot be solved by a normal strike team.

That discovery helps set up the next group of major objectives.

<h2 id="cfd-plan">
  Power Lines and Into the Sinkhole can run alongside Resonant work
</h2>

Once the FBC begins preparing the Cancellation Field Device, the game stops feeling like one mission leading cleanly into the next.

Two large routes become especially important.

### Power Lines

Use the <Link href="/control-resonant/power-lines">Power Lines walkthrough</Link> when Dylan is sent through the subway network.

The mission covers **Factory Station, East Park Station, Perimeter Station, and the Vanished Station**. The stations all belong to the same power-routing job, so there is no reason to treat each one as a separate story branch.

### Into the Sinkhole

When the objective changes to **Into the Sinkhole**, the surface power route is no longer the problem.

Follow the <Link href="/control-resonant/into-the-sinkhole">Sinkhole route</Link> for the diving bell descent, Bedrock, Probe Relays, and the escape after the original route shifts away.

Finishing one of these does not always trigger the next major story event immediately. Check the other active objectives before repeating a completed mission.

<h2 id="required-resonants">
  Check the Deserter and Dancer when Enemy of My Enemy will not start
</h2>

Resonants are not all equally urgent when their quests first appear.

The two that matter most to the early story gate are:

- **Deserter** — the Central Resonant in the Patterned Factory;
- **Dancer** — the West Incursion Zone Resonant in the Theater.

Both need to be dealt with before **Enemy of My Enemy** can move forward.

For the full list of six Resonants, their regions, ability rewards, and how Resonant Cycles change Manhattan, use the <Link href="/control-resonant/resonants">all Resonants guide</Link>.

If your active objective is specifically the Central Resonant, follow the <Link href="/control-resonant/patterned-factory">Patterned Factory and Deserter route</Link>.

If it is the West Incursion Resonant and you have already reached the Theater, use the <Link href="/control-resonant/dancer">Dancer guide</Link>.

<h2 id="west-incursion">
  West Incursion changes once Dylan unlocks Shift
</h2>

West Incursion is where normal street navigation starts becoming unreliable.

The theater spotlights provide a useful large-scale landmark, but seeing the Theater does not mean Dylan can reach it immediately. The route eventually breaks around the Parking Garage and forces progress through Jesse's story.

Follow the <Link href="/control-resonant/west-incursion-zone">West Incursion Zone route</Link> until the Parking Garage becomes the blocker.

When the active objective moves into **The Incursion Fault**, switch to <Link href="/control-resonant/search-for-jesse">Search for Jesse</Link>.

Completing that Fault unlocks **Shift**, which turns walls, rotated roads, and other gravity-altered surfaces into usable paths. Once Shift is available, return to the Theater route rather than continuing to search for a conventional street-level entrance.

<h2 id="search-for-jesse">
  Jesse's Faults are part of traversal progression
</h2>

The Jesse chain is not just a parallel story.

The first two Faults give Dylan movement options that other regions eventually expect him to have:

**Incursion Fault → Subway Fault → Underpass Fault**

The <Link href="/control-resonant/search-for-jesse">Search for Jesse route</Link> covers all three together.

If a route looks physically impossible even though the objective marker is clearly beyond it, check whether a Jesse Fault is still unfinished before spending time looking for another staircase or hidden door.

That becomes especially important in West Incursion and later in the Underpass.

<h2 id="underpass">
  The Underpass can contain three different objectives at once
</h2>

The Underpass is where reading the active quest title matters most.

Use the <Link href="/control-resonant/underpass">Underpass walkthrough</Link> when you are following the research team, restoring Power Cores, crossing the chasm, dealing with time fluctuations, or moving beneath the Oldest House.

If the objective changes to Jesse's Fault, use <Link href="/control-resonant/search-for-jesse">Search for Jesse</Link> instead.

If it changes to **The Underpass Resonant**, the route branches toward **The Spire** and the Astral Plane. The <Link href="/control-resonant/physicist">Physicist guide</Link> covers the three Astral Keys, three locked doors, the boss, and the Spike / Astral Rebuke choice.

All three routes can exist in the same region without being the same task.

<h2 id="hedron">
  The Hedron crisis takes over the late story
</h2>

Once the crisis turns back toward the Oldest House and the Hedron becomes the immediate threat, earlier Manhattan objectives stop being the best indicator of where the main story is going.

Follow the <Link href="/control-resonant/hedron-link">Hedron Link route</Link> when your objective names **The Hedron Link** or **Sever the Hedron Links**.

The important rule in this stage is that cutting the Hedron's connections is how Dylan weakens its hold. Research balloons and Probe Relays then help create routes through Patterned space as the mission moves into increasingly distorted areas.

If your Quest Log still says Power Lines, Into the Sinkhole, a Jesse Fault, or an earlier Resonant quest, stay with that objective instead of jumping ahead to the Hedron route.

<h2 id="unknown">
  Unknown mixes late story, Pope's Research, and the Co-Director
</h2>

Unknown is one of the easiest regions to misread because several late objectives can point into the same distorted space.

Floating islands and broken geometry are not always reachable just because you can see them. Some routes depend on progression gained through **Pope's Research**.

The region also contains the **Unknown Resonant**.

If that is your active objective, use the <Link href="/control-resonant/co-director">Co-Director guide</Link> once Dylan reaches the encounter.

If the active quest still concerns the Hedron, follow the Hedron objective rather than treating every FBC structure in Unknown as part of the Co-Director route.

<h2 id="park">
  The Park is worth returning to when its own objectives become active
</h2>

The Park is dominated by Mold and has its own surface, parking, sewer, and underground routes.

Use the <Link href="/control-resonant/the-park">Park and Collective route</Link> when the active objective sends Dylan there.

The region's Resonant is the **Collective**. Reaching it requires the Mold Gateway and four Mold samples rather than a normal path directly into a boss arena.

If the main story is currently pulling Dylan elsewhere, The Park does not need to interrupt every other objective the moment it becomes accessible.

<h2 id="map">
  Reveal the map when the problem is location rather than progression
</h2>

If you know the objective but cannot place it in Manhattan, use the <Link href="/control-resonant/map">CONTROL Resonant map guide</Link>.

Each mapped zone uses a Map Kit with one main Pylon and three Sensors. Activate all three Sensors, return to the Pylon, and use it again to bring the local data online.

The map is most useful for questions such as:

- which zone a location belongs to;
- how Central connects to surrounding districts;
- where you entered a region before gaining later traversal;
- which areas still need their Map Kit completed.

It is less useful inside Faults, the deeper Sinkhole, the Astral Plane, or other spaces where the active objective and local geometry matter more than Manhattan streets.

<h2 id="side-stories">
  Side Stories are easier when you separate local tasks from long cleanup chains
</h2>

Some Side Stories are compact enough to finish while you are already in the area. Others deliberately send Dylan across several regions.

For a complete view of active and optional missions, use the <Link href="/control-resonant/quests">quest list</Link>.

A few Side Stories are especially easy to recognize from the world itself.

### Crossing the Threshold

The investigation around the Field Office, Dig Site, Field Hospital, Loading Docks, Wilson, and AVP-3 belongs to <Link href="/control-resonant/crossing-the-threshold">Crossing the Threshold</Link>.

Once that investigation is active, follow its evidence instead of clearing unrelated Central markers.

### The Party Won't Stop

If you find **Singing Reported?** in West Incursion, it leads to <Link href="/control-resonant/party-wont-stop">The Party Won&apos;t Stop</Link>.

The karaoke machine uses nine timed choices, and a wrong answer can kill a partygoer even though the Side Story continues.

### The Last Taxi

An anomalous taxi begins <Link href="/control-resonant/last-taxi">The Last Taxi</Link>.

There are seven taxis across Manhattan's major zones, so there is no need to stop the campaign and chase the whole chain the moment you find the first one. Solve the locations that are naturally reachable and return for the rest as more regions open.

<h2 id="lost-right-now">
  If the route suddenly stops making sense
</h2>

When CONTROL Resonant feels as though it has stopped giving directions, I use the same checks in this order:

1. read the exact quest name;
2. read the current objective beneath it;
3. check whether a Jesse Fault is blocking traversal;
4. check whether the Central or West Incursion Resonant is still required;
5. only then use the district name to decide which route to follow.

Being physically inside **Central**, **West Incursion**, **Underpass**, or **Unknown** does not tell you enough by itself.

For example, the Underpass can hold the research-team story, a Jesse Fault, and the Physicist route at the same time. Unknown can hold Hedron progression, Pope's Research, and the Co-Director.

The quest name tells you which problem Dylan is actually solving.

If the objective itself is clear but you still do not know where the story reconnects, return to the <Link href="/control-resonant/walkthrough">main walkthrough</Link>. If the problem is deciding which active mission matters, use the <Link href="/control-resonant/quests">quest list</Link>.
