Fields of Mistria rewards me more for connecting several systems than for
finishing one system in isolation.

I do not clear the whole farm before entering town, complete the Museum before
opening the Mines, or wait until every relationship is finished before raising
Town Rank. I use the same route to advance requests, Renown, Museum sets,
resources, Mine progression, town repairs, and relationships together.

My main progression order is:

**Eastern Road Bridge → Stone Star → Mines → Water Seal → later Mine seals →
Dragon's Breath → Deep Woods → Copper and Gold Star projects → Saturday Market
Plaza → Bell Tower → 10 Hearts → Engagement Ring → wedding → completion
cleanup.**

The most important decision is knowing when a day has already produced
permanent progress. Once I activate an elevator checkpoint, open a seal, clear a
blocked route, finish a Donation Box, or schedule a wedding, I usually return
home instead of risking the whole day for one more resource.

<h2 id="opening-priorities">
  Open the town routes before perfecting the farm
</h2>

<AdUnit placement="article_after_first_h2" />

The starting farm contains enough weeds, rocks, branches, and trees to consume
most of the first week. I do not clear all of it.

I make space for:

- the starting crop patch;
- one or two storage chests;
- a direct path between the farmhouse and the farm exit;
- and the first animal or crafting buildings when they become relevant.

Then I move into town and start the systems that unlock the rest of the game.

My early priorities are:

1. meet Adeline and unlock the Request Board;
2. visit the Museum;
3. repair Caldarus' Shrine;
4. begin the Eastern Road Bridge project;
5. donate first copies of new items;
6. ship ordinary duplicates;
7. attend town events that appear in the Journal or mailbox.

The Eastern Road Bridge requires **60 Wood and 60 Stone**. I reserve that stack
before using the same materials on fences, paths, furniture, or a decorative
farm layout.

Once the bridge donation is complete, I speak to Adeline instead of assuming
the project will finish automatically. The repair reopens the Saturday Market
route, but the vendors do not necessarily appear on the same day the bridge is
finished.

I do not buy something from every Saturday vendor just to prove that I visited.
Early Tesserae are more useful for seeds, food, inventory space, and other
permanent improvements.

<h2 id="first-spring-routine">
  Use the first Spring to combine Renown, relationships, and seasonal progress
</h2>

The first Spring contains several activities that are easy to miss when I spend
every day clearing the farm.

On Friday evening, many villagers gather at the Sleeping Dragon Inn. I use that
weekly visit to:

- speak to several villagers in one building;
- distribute simple gifts;
- progress the Inn's continuing Friday scenes;
- and avoid spending an entire day following individual map markers.

Missing one Friday does not make me restart the save. I return on a later Friday
and continue from the current point.

The Spring Festival collection also rewards normal play rather than one narrow
grind. After Seline introduces the event, **Breath of Spring** can appear as a
bonus while farming, fishing, mining, and completing other activities.

I continue progressing the farm and Mines instead of searching for the flowers
as ordinary ground forage. The collected total affects festival placement,
rewards, decorations, and available booth stock, but I do not postpone the main
story just to force a perfect first-year result.

At the end of Spring, I check for seasonal Museum items and forage before
sleeping into Summer. A missed seasonal item can cost more time than an
unfinished ordinary town request.

<h2 id="raise-renown">
  Raise Town Rank through work I already need to do
</h2>

The Mines remain closed until Mistria reaches **Stone Star Town Rank**.

The game identifies three repeatable Renown sources:

- completed Museum sets;
- Request Board requests;
- shipped items.

Town repairs and major progression quests add further Renown as the save
continues.

I combine these sources instead of grinding one of them.

My normal town route is:

1. check the Request Board;
2. collect items that match the route I am already taking;
3. donate the first copy of a new Museum item;
4. finish requests using storage or nearby resources;
5. ship unneeded duplicates overnight.

<h2 id="unlock-mines">
  Reach Stone Star before returning to the Mine entrance
</h2>

The first Mine discussion takes place at the Museum. Errol refuses to reopen the
damaged Mines until the town reaches Stone Star.

That is a hard progression gate.

I do not repeatedly visit the Mine entrance, search for a hidden key, or upgrade
the Pickaxe in the hope that one of those actions will bypass Town Rank.

After reaching Stone Star, I read the new message and follow the objective to
the **Mine entrance west of town**.

objective from resource gathering to story progression.

I inspect the Water Tablet, take its rubbing, and complete the translation route
before returning with the required offerings.

The Water Seal uses four Upper Mines items:

- Ruby;
- Stone Loach;
- Lantern Moth;
- Upper Mines Mushroom.

I search storage before starting another full Mine run. By Floor 20, I may
already own several of the offerings without remembering where they came from.

The items must be in my carried inventory when I return to the seal chamber.
Items left in a farmhouse chest cannot be submitted from underground.

<h2 id="later-mine-progression">
  Keep moving through the seals instead of clearing every Mine room
</h2>

The Water Seal is the first of five major seals.

The later Mine route continues through additional biomes, tablets, offerings,
enemies, artifacts, and permanent story rewards.

I use the same general method in each region:

1. activate every available elevator checkpoint;
2. prioritize the tablet or seal chamber;
3. record the actual offering requirements;
4. check home storage;
5. farm only what is missing;
6. carry the full set back together;
7. stop after opening the seal if continuing would be unsafe.

I do not restart at Floor 1 every time a new objective appears.

The Fire Seal is an especially important transition. Completing it begins an
escape sequence and grants **Dragon's Breath**.

Once I receive that spell, I stop treating Mine resources as the immediate
priority and follow Caldarus toward the Deep Woods.

<h2 id="copper-star-summit">
  Complete the Copper Star town event to reopen the Summit
</h2>

Copper Star triggers another town-development event rather than a normal
material bundle.

After reaching the rank, I attend the Manor House meeting. The town works
together to clear earthquake debris and repair damaged public areas.

The remaining stone is used to restore the stairs leading to the Summit.

I complete this event when it appears because it reopens the upper route and
restores access to the Summit area, including the Lemon Tree.

I do not search for a separate Summit Donation Box or prepare another large
stone stack. The stairs are repaired as part of the Copper Star town-cleanup
event.

This is a good example of why I keep raising Renown while exploring the Mines.
Town Rank does not only unlock reward chests; several ranks trigger permanent
map and community changes.

<h2 id="midgame-resource-planning">
  Protect materials before the late town projects begin
</h2>

As Town Rank rises, Mine materials begin serving several competing purposes.

The same general resource pool may be needed for:

- town repairs;
- Forge recipes;
- Museum donations;
- Stillwell missions;
- equipment;
- relationship progression;
- and optional furniture.

I begin keeping at least one copy of unfamiliar items and larger stacks of

The resources I am most careful with are:

- Mistril materials;
- Void resources;
- Monster Cores and other uncommon enemy drops;
- Hardwood;
- Refined Stone;
- Essence Stones;
- Perfect-quality gemstones;
- Mine insects and forage from later biomes.

I check active quests and Forge recipes before using the last copies on
decorations or duplicate equipment.

An optional cosmetic purchase can wait. A permanent town vendor, repaired
building, or required crafted component normally cannot be replaced by simply
spending more Tesserae.

Filling the Donation Box is not the final step. I still find Adeline and report
that the materials are ready.

After she completes the project, I wait for the next Saturday Market.

<h2 id="marriage">
  Treat 10 Hearts as the start of the marriage route
</h2>

Eight Hearts lets me choose the dating relationship. It is not the marriage
threshold.

I continue to **10 Hearts** with the person I am dating. When the last heart
moves slowly, I use a date instead of relying only on another normal
conversation and gift.

After reaching 10 Hearts, I sleep. Elsie visits the farmhouse and gives me the
Engagement Ring Recipe.

The ring is crafted at the Forge and takes 30 in-game minutes to make. I check
the current recipe panel directly rather than identifying materials only from
their icons.

Using the ring begins a private relationship scene. It does not force an
immediate engagement, and I still receive a final decision before selecting a
spouse.

Once I accept the proposal, I choose a wedding date. I leave several days
before the ceremony because the flower, cake, and meal choices appear through
separate conversations with my fiancé.

I speak to them every day until those choices are complete.

<h2 id="achievement-cleanup">
  Let the achievement list identify the remaining systems
</h2>

Fields of Mistria has **69 Steam achievements**.

I do not organize the entire save around completing them in menu order. Most
early achievements unlock naturally through:

- story progress;
- Town Ranks;
- repairs;
- Mine seals;
- relationships;
- skills;
- animals;
- festivals;
- Museum sets.

An established save can receive credit for conditions completed before the
achievement system was added, so I load the old save fully before deciding to
restart.

The achievements that require active long-term planning are more likely to
include:

- every villager's birthday gift;
- Legendary Fish and Insects;
- complete Museum progress;
- all Stillwell Mission Quests;
- maximum skills and Tier 5 perks;
- Tier 5 animal breeding;
- Mistril Star Rank;
- marriage and later family milestones.

I begin birthday tracking early because missing one date can create a long
wait. I leave the complete Museum, Stillwell, highest Town Rank, and family
milestones as established-save cleanup instead of blocking the main story.

<h2 id="what-to-keep">
  Keep progression materials before selling the last copy
</h2>

My storage rule is simple:

> The first unfamiliar copy is progression until I prove otherwise.

Before selling or crafting with a rare item, I check:

1. Museum donation status;
2. active seal offerings;
3. town-repair requirements;
4. Forge recipes;
5. Stillwell missions;
6. relationship gifts;
7. seasonal availability.

I keep at least one copy of:

- new fish and insects;
- artifacts;
- each gemstone quality;
- biome-specific Mine forage;
- uncommon enemy drops;
- Essence Stones;
- Mistril and Void materials;
- seasonal crops and forage.

This does not mean I hoard every ordinary stack forever.

Once the Museum has the item, no active quest needs it, and its replacement is
easy to obtain, I ship or process the excess.

I also compare optional spending with permanent progress.

A decorative item or extra music track may be appealing, but inventory space,
a new route, a vendor, or a repaired building usually changes more of the save.

<h2 id="fields-of-mistria-faq">
  Fields of Mistria progression FAQ
</h2>

### Do I need a new save for Fields of Mistria 1.0?

No. I can continue an established save and access new progression from the
point that save has already reached.

Previously completed achievement conditions may also be recognized after the
save is loaded and related systems are processed.

### What is the first spell I receive?

The first spell in this route is **Full Restore**.

Caldarus adds it to the Journal, and casting it restores Health and Stamina.
Mana recovers gradually over subsequent nights, so I save it for a day when the
full refill can secure a Mine checkpoint, major resource target, or another
permanent objective.

### Do I need to complete the Museum before getting married?

No. Museum completion and marriage are separate progression paths.

Marriage depends on dating, 10 Hearts, the Engagement Ring route, the proposal,
and the wedding.

### Should I finish every Mine floor before moving on?

No. I prioritize the next ladder, elevator platform, tablet, or seal.

Once permanent progress is active, I can return later for ore, enemies,
artifacts, fish, and insects without repeating the entire route.

### Should I sell the first copy of a rare item?

Usually not.

I check the Museum, active quests, Forge recipes, seal offerings, Stillwell
missions, and seasonal availability first.

### Is having a child required after marriage?

No. The wedding completes the marriage route. Later family progression is
optional and separate.

### When should I stop a Mine or resource run?

I return home when I have secured permanent progress and the remaining health,
energy, time, or inventory space makes the next step unsafe.

One extra resource rarely has more value than preserving the completed
checkpoint and starting the next day prepared.
