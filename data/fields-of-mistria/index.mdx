My Fields of Mistria progression route is built around **permanent access**, not
trying to finish every available activity before moving on.

I clear only enough farm space to function, raise Renown while doing work I
already need, unlock the Mines at Stone Star, push elevator checkpoints and
seals, open the Deep Woods, then return to the later town projects and
relationship progression.

The route I use is:

**Eastern Road Bridge → Stone Star → Mines → Water Seal → later Mine seals →
Dragon's Breath → Deep Woods → Copper Star → Gold Star → Saturday Market Plaza
→ Bell Tower → 10 Hearts → Engagement Ring → marriage → completion cleanup.**

The decision that saves me the most time is knowing when a day has already
produced permanent progress. Once I unlock a route, activate an elevator
checkpoint, break a seal, finish a town project, or secure another permanent
upgrade, I am usually willing to go home instead of risking that progress for
one more resource.

<h2 id="opening-priorities">
  Open the town routes before perfecting the farm
</h2>

The starting farm can consume most of the first week if I let it.

I do not clear every tree, rock, weed, and branch before starting the rest of
the game.

I make enough room for:

- a usable crop patch;
- one or two storage chests;
- a direct route from the farmhouse to the exit;
- and the first buildings I actually need.

Then I leave the farm and start opening systems.

My first priorities are:

1. meet Adeline and begin using the Request Board;
2. visit the Museum;
3. repair Caldarus' Shrine;
4. begin the Eastern Road Bridge project;
5. donate first copies of unfamiliar items;
6. ship ordinary duplicates;
7. attend Journal and mailbox progression events.

The Eastern Road Bridge requires **60 Wood and 60 Stone**.

I reserve that amount before spending the same materials on fences, furniture,
paths, or a decorative farm layout.

That is one of the first places where I separate **progression materials** from
**optional materials**.

Once the bridge is ready, I follow the active town objective rather than
assuming that filling a construction requirement always completes the project
on its own.

I also do not buy something from every Saturday vendor just because the route
is open. Early Tesserae are more useful when they improve inventory capacity,
food supply, seeds, tools, or another permanent bottleneck.

<h2 id="first-spring-routine">
  Use the first Spring to advance several systems at once
</h2>

I treat the first Spring as a progression season, not a farm-decoration season.

There are several recurring activities that fit naturally around the main
route.

On Friday evenings, many villagers gather at the Sleeping Dragon Inn. I use
that visit to speak to several people in one place instead of following
individual map markers for half the day.

It is also a convenient time to:

- hand out simple gifts;
- continue Friday Night at the Inn scenes;
- check relationship progress;
- and finish nearby town errands.

If I miss a Friday, I do not restart or delay the whole route. I continue the
following week.

The Spring Festival works the same way.

Once **Breath of Spring** becomes available, I let it accumulate through normal
activities instead of abandoning Mine, farm, or town progression to grind only
festival drops.

At the end of each season, however, I do make one deliberate check for seasonal
Museum items, forage, fish, or insects that I have not secured yet.

A missed seasonal collectible can create a much longer delay than an unfinished
ordinary request.

<h2 id="raise-renown">
  Raise Town Rank through work I already need to do
</h2>

My first major Renown target is **Stone Star** because that is the progression
gate for reopening the Mines.

I do not grind Town Rank from one source.

I combine:

- Museum donations;
- Request Board jobs;
- shipping;
- town repairs;
- and normal story progression.

My usual town loop is:

1. check the Request Board;
2. choose requests that overlap with the route I was already taking;
3. donate the first copy of new Museum items;
4. complete easy requests from storage;
5. ship useful duplicates overnight.

resource room and start following the seal objective.

Before making another full Mine run, I check storage.

By this point, there is a good chance I already collected one or more required
items without realizing they would later become seal offerings.

<h2 id="later-mine-progression">
  Push the Mine story through checkpoints and seals
</h2>

After the Water Seal, I keep using the same progression logic.

My order inside each later Mine region is:

1. activate elevator checkpoints;
2. find the tablet or story gate;
3. confirm what the next seal requires;
4. check home storage;
5. return only for missing items;
6. open the seal;
7. stop if continuing would put the completed day at risk.

I do not restart from Floor 1 for every objective.

I use the deepest reliable checkpoint and treat optional mining as a separate
resource run.

The most important later transition is the **Fire Seal**.

Once that sequence gives me **Dragon's Breath**, I stop treating deeper Mine
resources as the immediate priority.

Dragon's Breath opens a new permanent route outside the Mines, so I follow that
lead first.

<h2 id="unlock-deep-woods">

- another Town Rank;
- a better Axe;
- a Donation Box;
- or clearing more ordinary Eastern Road obstacles.

After obtaining Dragon's Breath, I travel into the Eastern Road and take the
northern route to the blocked entrance.

<h2 id="copper-star-summit">
  Keep raising Renown while the Mine route expands
</h2>

Opening new Mine regions does not make Town Rank irrelevant.

I continue turning in Museum items, requests, shipments, and town objectives
while pushing the underground story.

At **Copper Star**, another town-development sequence restores damaged public
areas and reopens the Summit route.

I complete these rank events when they appear because they create permanent map
changes.

I do not postpone them for a perfect farm day.

This is why I avoid treating Renown as an early-game-only system. Town Rank
continues changing access long after the Mines have opened.

<h2 id="midgame-resource-planning">
  Start protecting late-game materials before the big projects
</h2>

The middle of the game is where resource conflicts become more expensive.

The same rare items may begin competing between:

- Forge recipes;
- Museum donations;
- town repairs;
- Stillwell missions;
- equipment;
- relationship progression;
- and optional crafting.

This is when I stop selling the last copy of an unfamiliar late-game material.

I am especially careful with:

- Mistril materials;
- Void materials;
- Monster Cores and uncommon enemy drops;
- Hardwood;
- Refined Stone;
- Essence Stones;
- Perfect-quality gemstones;
- later Mine insects and forage.

My rule is not to hoard everything forever.

It is:

> **Do not spend the last hard-to-replace copy until I know the next progression
> project does not need it.**

If the choice is between an optional cosmetic craft and a permanent route,
vendor, or repaired town building, I normally delay the cosmetic.

<h2 id="achievement-cleanup">
  Leave completion goals for the established save

progression route around completing them in menu order.

Most early achievements overlap naturally with:

- Town Rank;
- Mine progression;
- repairs;
- relationships;
- skills;
- animals;
- festivals;
- and Museum progress.

The goals I deliberately leave for the established save are the ones that
benefit from long-term access and accumulated resources.

That includes things like Museum completion, higher Town Rank, Stillwell
missions, maximum-skill progression, breeding goals, relationship milestones,
and other collection-heavy achievements.

I begin time-sensitive habits early, especially seasonal collection and
birthdays, but I do not delay a new permanent route just to clean up the

<h2 id="what-to-keep">
  What I keep instead of selling the last copy
</h2>

My storage rule is:

> **The first unfamiliar copy is progression until I prove otherwise.**

Before selling or consuming a rare item, I check whether it might still belong
to:

1. the Museum;
2. an active seal;
3. a town project;
4. a Forge recipe;
5. a Stillwell mission;
6. a relationship requirement;
7. a seasonal collection.

I am most cautious with:

- new fish and insects;
- artifacts;
- gemstones;
- later Mine forage;
- unusual enemy drops;
- Essence Stones;
- Mistril and Void materials;
- seasonal crops and forage.

Once the Museum has the item, no active progression uses it, and I know I can
replace it easily, I stop hoarding the excess.

This lets storage support progression without turning every chest into a
permanent archive.

<h2 id="when-to-stop-for-the-day">
  When I stop a Mine or resource run
</h2>

I return home when the day has already produced meaningful permanent progress
and continuing would put that result at risk.

For me, the strongest stopping signals are:

- a new elevator checkpoint is active;
- a seal has opened;
- a blocked route has permanently disappeared;
- a rare quest component is secured;
- a Donation Box category is finished;
- a major relationship or town event has progressed;
- health or stamina is becoming unsafe;
- the remaining inventory space is too small for the next objective.

I do not judge a successful day by how empty the stamina bar is.

If I unlocked something that will still be there tomorrow, the day already
worked.

That approach is what keeps the progression route moving forward without
turning every objective into a full-day grind.
