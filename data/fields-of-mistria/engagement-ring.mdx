The **Engagement Ring in Fields of Mistria is not something I buy from a shop**.
I unlock its recipe after reaching **10 Hearts with someone I am dating**. Once
that relationship requirement is met, Elsie visits and gives me the Engagement
Ring Recipe.

I then craft the ring at a **Blacksmithing station** with **1 Dragon-Forged
Core, 2 Diamonds, 4 Gold Ingots, and 4 Mistril Ingots**. Using the ring starts
a conversation about the relationship; it does not force me to confirm the
engagement immediately.

<h2 id="unlock-engagement-ring-recipe">
  How I unlock the Engagement Ring recipe
</h2>

The important checkpoint is **10 Hearts with someone I am dating**.

I do not treat every 10-Heart friendship as marriage-ready. The Engagement Ring
is specifically used with a romantic partner, so I make sure the character is
already in the dating state before waiting for the recipe.

Once I have the relationship where I want it, I finish the day and advance
time instead of searching the Saturday Market, General Store, or other vendors.
Elsie comes to me and explains that she has noticed how close I have become
with my partner before giving me the Engagement Ring Recipe.

That makes this an easy progression check:

**dating → 10 Hearts → advance the day → Elsie gives the recipe**

If Elsie has not appeared yet, I check the relationship first. I do not start
farming expensive ring materials just because a character has reached a high
friendship level.

<h2 id="engagement-ring-recipe">
  Engagement Ring recipe and materials
</h2>

I need exactly:

- **1 Dragon-Forged Core**
- **2 Diamonds**
- **4 Gold Ingots**
- **4 Mistril Ingots**

The **Dragon-Forged Core** is the material I check first. The ingots and
Diamonds are easy to count from storage, but I do not assume I have a spare
Dragon-Forged Core just because I have already progressed deep into the game.

If that slot is empty, I stop there and restock before trying to rush the
proposal. There is no reason to stand at the forge with three completed
material slots and then discover that the one specialized component is missing.

I craft a ring when I am ready to see the relationship discussion, not as a
general-purpose blacksmithing product.

<h2 id="where-to-craft-engagement-ring">
  Craft the Engagement Ring at a Blacksmithing station
</h2>

Learning the recipe does not put a finished ring into my inventory.

I take the materials to a **Blacksmithing station** and select the Engagement
Ring from the available recipes. I do not waste time checking cooking,
carpentry, or ordinary inventory crafting menus.

<h2 id="how-to-use-engagement-ring">

10 Hearts**.

The key distinction is dating status. Reaching 10 Hearts by itself does not
turn the ring into a universal item that I can use on anyone.

I use the ring to ask that romantic partner to **discuss our future together**.

<h2 id="postpone-engagement-proposal">
  I can postpone the proposal without losing the chance
</h2>

This is the part I would check before clicking through quickly.

Starting the Engagement Ring scene **does not mean I have to propose during
that attempt**. I can postpone the conversation and come back later.

The game also allows the discussion scene to be triggered repeatedly until I
make the final decision.

instead of clicking through just because I already spent the crafting materials.

<h2 id="before-confirming-engagement">
  Check your other dating relationships before confirming
</h2>

There is one setting I check before the final engagement.

By default, other characters I was dating can change to **Best Friend** after I
get engaged. The game allows that behavior to be changed in the Gameplay
settings, but I need to make the choice **before confirming the engagement**.

<h2 id="engagement-ring-not-working">
  Why the Engagement Ring is not unlocking or working
</h2>

### Elsie has not given me the Engagement Ring recipe

I check two things before doing anything else:

1. the character is someone I am actually **dating**;
2. that relationship has reached **10 Hearts**.

If either condition is missing, I go back to relationship progression. If both
are complete, I advance the day and watch for Elsie's visit instead of searching
stores for the recipe.

### I learned the recipe but cannot find the ring in normal crafting

I go to a **Blacksmithing station**.

The recipe is a Blacksmithing recipe, so I do not expect it to appear beside
ordinary hand-crafted items or cooking recipes.

### I crafted the ring but cannot start the proposal discussion

I check the target character rather than the ring.

The person needs to be a **romantic partner I am dating at 10 Hearts**. A
10-Heart character who is not in the correct romantic state is not the target
for this interaction.

### I used the ring but I am not ready to get engaged

I choose the option to postpone the discussion.

I do not reload the save or craft another ring just because I backed out of one
conversation. The proposal discussion can be started again until I make the
final choice.

<h2 id="engagement-ring-route">
  The shortest route from 10 Hearts to the proposal
</h2>

Once my relationship is ready, I keep the sequence simple:

**dating at 10 Hearts → advance the day → get the recipe from Elsie → check 1
Dragon-Forged Core + 2 Diamonds + 4 Gold Ingots + 4 Mistril Ingots → craft at
Blacksmithing → use the ring on that partner → discuss the future → postpone or
confirm**

The biggest time loss is solving the wrong problem.

If Elsie has not appeared, I work on the relationship requirement instead of
shopping for a ring. If the recipe is learned but I cannot craft it, I check my
materials and the Blacksmithing station. If the ring is finished but the
interaction will not start, I check dating status and 10 Hearts.

Once those three checkpoints are separated, the Engagement Ring progression is
much easier to troubleshoot.
