<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
To get started in **Playrooms**, open `light.exe` on the computer in your starting room, collect balls, and unload them into the ball pits. Stored balls earn money for upgrades and keys. Focus on **Ball Magnet**, **Quick Grab**, **Vacuum Collect**, and carrying capacity early; fill the first pit to unlock **Sprint**. Return to your starting room before the lights go out. You can reach the ending without finding every Sparrow, office item, or loose ball.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

The first problem in Playrooms is not finding the next door. It is discovering how little work you can get done with a bag that holds only **50 balls** and a shift that ends before you are ready.

Once collection gets faster, that problem changes. You can work farther from the starting room, but reaching more valuable piles means longer journeys home. The trick is to spend money on whichever part of the job is slowing you down, rather than buying every available upgrade as soon as it appears.

## What Should You Do on Your First Day in Playrooms?

Your day starts at a computer in a small room. Read the opening message, then launch **`light.exe`** to turn on the lights and begin work.

1. Leave the starting room and find a nearby group of loose balls in the Playroom.
2. Pick them up until your bag is full. At the start, it holds **50**.
3. Walk to a ball pit and unload the bag. Balls count toward a pit when they are deposited, not merely picked up.
4. Repeat while you still have a comfortable route back.
5. Return to the starting room when the work period is ending. Check the computer for purchases and the next shift.

Don't try to clean the whole Playroom on your first day. A successful opening shift is one where you learn the pickup and unloading rhythm, earn something to spend, and get back safely.

<section id="money-and-pits" className="scroll-mt-24">

## How Do You Earn Money and Unlock More Areas?

**Deliver balls into the collection system.** Picking them up is only the first half of the job. Your earnings come from storing them, and you spend those earnings on skills, equipment, and keys.

There are three different progress checks worth keeping separate:

- **Your bag or equipment tank:** how many balls you are carrying right now
- **A ball pit's required total:** how much must be delivered before that pit is finished and its reward becomes available
- **The area's remaining balls:** what is still scattered around, even after a pit has been filled

This distinction explains why you can unlock a new room while the previous area is still messy. **Filling a pit does not automatically mean every ball in that area has been collected.**

The first pit is especially useful because completing it unlocks **Sprint**. Another early pit rewards **Crouch**. Before choosing where to unload, look at the pit's remaining requirement and reward instead of spreading your first deliveries across every pit.

### Should You Buy the Next Key or Finish Cleaning the Current Area?

Usually, buying the next key is worth considering once you can collect efficiently and return safely. A new area gives you more balls to earn from and eventually access to better equipment. You can come back for the scattered leftovers later.

The main progression takes you through the **Waiting Area, Backroom, Poolroom, School, Office, and Hotel**. If you need the exact key prices, doors, or the route to the ending, follow the <Link href="/playrooms/walkthrough">Playrooms walkthrough</Link>.

After your early shifts, open **`skills.exe`**. The best purchase depends on whether you are spending too long picking up balls, unloading a full bag, or simply walking back and forth.

For ordinary cleanup, improve pickup speed first; increase bag capacity when those extra balls start filling it too quickly. If you are carrying large loads but standing at the pit waiting to empty them, **Quick Hands** becomes more important than another capacity increase.

**Long Arms** is useful when you need more reach, but it is not an automatic first purchase if your real bottleneck is slow collecting or carrying.

One exception: **Ball Magnet** may collect unwanted colours during a single-colour pit attempt. Plan that challenge separately using the <Link href="/playrooms/achievements">achievements checklist</Link>.

### Are Movement and Light Upgrades Worth Buying?

Yes, especially once your work takes you beyond the starting Playroom.

**Sprint Speed** and **Sprint Stamina** help with longer trips. **Longer Days** gives you more time to work, while **Slow Blackout** makes the end of a shift easier to manage. They solve a different problem from collection upgrades: getting home with the work you have already done.

If you regularly lose a nearly finished shift because you stayed too far away, prioritize working time and your return journey before spending more on collection speed.

Open **`shop.exe`** when the equipment catalog becomes available. Tools don't all solve the same collection problem, and some become available only after further progress.

The **Push Broom** becomes useful when you begin working with larger piles and suction equipment in the Waiting Area. The portable **Ball Vacuum** offers another way to move balls into the collection system after further progression. By the time you reach the School, the **Hand Vacuum** makes scattered cleanup much less repetitive.

The **Scanner** is most valuable near the end of an area's cleanup. It costs **€10** when available through `shop.exe`. When an area looks empty but the remaining count says otherwise, scan before spending another shift searching the same floor.

Vacuum Robots are a later investment. Check the available charging equipment when setting them up; do not assume they can keep working indefinitely without a way to recharge.

### Should You Use the Broom or the Hand Vacuum?

Use the broom when there is a large, connected pile you can push into an intake. Use the Hand Vacuum for smaller clusters, corners, and balls spread around furniture. If you are spending more time moving a machine than collecting balls, switch methods or move on to a denser area.

A tool that is efficient in one room may be frustrating in another. You do not need to commit to a single method for the entire game.

<section id="night" className="scroll-mt-24">

## What Happens When the Lights Go Out in Playrooms?

Your workday has a limit. When the lights begin shutting down, **stop collecting and head back toward your starting room**. The opening instructions tell you to be there during the rest period.

The most common mistake is deciding to finish one more pile while you're already deep inside another area. A longer journey, a confusing corridor, or an unexpected detour can cost far more than the few extra balls you picked up.

A useful habit is to divide every shift into two parts: work outward while you have plenty of time, then collect only along your return route once you need to head home.

The School has its own additional hazard: **don't sprint through its corridors**, even if you are hurrying back. The stairs are an exception to the School's rule. The <Link href="/playrooms/walkthrough">room-by-room walkthrough</Link> explains where that matters during progression.

</section>

<section id="difficulty" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Should You Play Cozy, Normal, or Hardcore Mode?

Choose based on whether you want relaxed cleanup, the ordinary horror experience, or the risk of losing an entire run.

<GuideTable columns={playroomsModeColumns} rows={playroomsModeRows} />

For a first playthrough, **Normal** lets you learn the hazards without risking the entire save. Cozy is an option if your main interest is cleaning and upgrading rather than the scares.

Hardcore makes route knowledge much more important. Getting caught there is not just a bad shift; it means starting over. If you want its associated achievement, plan that run after learning the facility in another mode.

</section>

<section id="stuck" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Why Is My Progress Stuck Even Though the Room Looks Empty?

First, check what the game is asking you to complete.

**If a pit is still short of balls**, you need more delivered units. Balls in your bag, a machine, or the surrounding room do not yet count toward that pit's total.

**If the pit is full but the area isn't clean**, look for loose balls elsewhere. Desks, corners, lockers, and less obvious surfaces are easier to miss than the large piles. Use the Scanner rather than walking the same corridor repeatedly.

**If the missing balls are in the Hotel**, the numbered rooms and Cleaning Schedule require a different approach. Use the <Link href="/playrooms/hotel">Hotel cleaning walkthrough</Link> for room tracking and stuck-ball recovery.

**If your question is about a missing achievement**, first distinguish finishing a pit, fully cleaning an area, and discovering a room. They are not the same condition. The <Link href="/playrooms/achievements">30-achievement checklist</Link> separates those goals.

</section>

## Do You Need Every Ball, Sparrow, and Office Item to Finish Playrooms?

No. The normal ending follows the main ball-pit progression and final exit. You do not need to rescue every bird, collect every decoration, or remove every last loose ball just to see it.

If you want to keep playing after the ending, there are several separate things to complete:

- **20 Sparrows:** birds hidden on furniture, high ledges, and in guest rooms. Check the <Link href="/playrooms/sparrows">Sparrow locations</Link> if you're missing one.
- **14 office items:** collectible decorations, some hidden well away from the usual cleaning path. Use the <Link href="/playrooms/office-items">office item locations</Link> to find them.
- **The Whale:** a secret in the green-carpeted maze reached from the Waiting Area. Follow the <Link href="/playrooms/green-office-map">Green Office maze route</Link> if you want to locate it.
- **Achievements and full cleanup:** different objectives beyond the ending. Track them with the <Link href="/playrooms/achievements">achievements checklist</Link>.

For the final ball pit and exit sequence itself, continue with the <Link href="/playrooms/walkthrough">complete Playrooms walkthrough</Link>.
