My first IDLE_DIRECTIVE run slowed down because I treated every new machine as
progress.

I filled the Body Grid with small Batteries, kept Salvagers that could not use
the current location, upgraded a Spinner group I was about to replace and
visited a Trader before I could accept Tasks.

The machine looked busier, but I was still waiting for Metal, Machinery and a
reason to use System Reset.

I now follow one progression loop:

> stabilize Energy → repair the Recycler and Navigation → collect only the
> resource blocking Mobility → repair a Spire → reveal useful destinations →
> take a purposeful Prestige → rebuild around the newly unlocked system.

IDLE_DIRECTIVE does not reward keeping every item or clearing the entire map in
one run. Most Body upgrades, equipment, Wiring, stored resources, Mobility and
Trader Reputation disappear during System Reset.

The permanent progress comes from recognizing when another temporary upgrade is
less valuable than the Augments waiting behind Prestige.

This page covers the complete progression route from a fresh start through
Traders, Labs, Blueprints, the Replicator and the first Terminal.

The detailed systems are separated into focused guides:

- machine connections and resource throughput;
- first-Prestige and Augment decisions;
- Body Grid layout examples;
- all 43 achievements.

> The route was tested across fresh starts and multiple Prestige runs. Terminal
> repair time is dynamic in the current version, so I check the value displayed
> in the current save instead of relying on an older fixed timer.

<h2 id="first-15-minutes">What I Do During the First 15 Minutes</h2>

A fresh machine begins with little room and no movement.

In one unmodified opening, Diagnostics showed:

- a **4×3 Body Grid**;
- **60% Energy Efficiency**;
- **400 meters of Sensor Range**;
- **0% World Explored**;
- **0 kilometers traveled**;
- four Terminals remaining.

Those numbers tell me what the first run needs.

I do not need a large factory yet. I need enough Energy, Metal and Machinery to
restore the systems that let me leave the starting location.

My opening sequence is:

1. run Diagnostics;
2. reboot the Scanners;
3. start the Repair Protocol;
4. Scavenge;
5. inspect the results;
6. keep useful Energy equipment, Metal and Machinery;
7. discard items that cannot contribute to the opening route;
8. Scavenge again;
9. repair the Recycler;
10. build toward Navigation and Mobility.

I keep the Scanners running even though they consume Energy. They are required
for the normal Scavenge loop.

### I take usable Energy before specialized storage

The first passive Energy Cell, Spinner setup or fuelled Biogenerator is usually
more important than a specialized storage container.

I install enough generation to make total Energy positive.

That early power is not the final layout. Its job is to pay for:

- the Recycler repair;
- Body Space when required;
- the Navigation Computer;
- the first Mobility level.

I do not spend all available Metal upgrading one weak Energy Cell.

Once the machine can support Navigation, another temporary power upgrade must
justify the delay it creates.

### I separate Body Space from Inventory Space

Body Space solves an installation problem.

Inventory Space solves a loot problem.

I expand Body Space when I cannot fit something required, such as:

- the Navigation Computer;
- the Recycler and a working power route;
- a Spinner beside its Repeater;
- a Biogenerator beside its Biocell.

I expand Inventory Space when useful Scavenge items are being discarded because
there is nowhere to hold them.

That distinction prevents me from spending early Metal on the wrong type of
space.

In one fresh opening, the Actions panel showed:

- Body expansion at **50 Metal and 300 Energy**;
- Inventory expansion at **50 Metal and 125 Energy**;
- the first Mobility level at **50 Metal and 200 Energy**;
- the Recycler still waiting for its third Machinery item.

Permanent Augments can change later costs and starting conditions, but the
decision remains the same:

> spend toward movement before filling every available tile.

<h2 id="repair-recycler">Repair the Recycler Before Protecting Every Drop</h2>

The damaged Recycler creates the first Inventory trap.

Scavenge produces Batteries, Repeaters, Spinners, storage, Machinery and upgrade
components faster than the opening Inventory can hold them.

Without the Recycler, weak duplicate equipment has little value after the Body
Grid is full.

The first tested Recycler repair required **three Machinery**.

Machinery is a resource category. I do not search the Scavenge results for a
large device literally named Machinery.

After the Recycler is running, I divide items into three groups.

### Items that open a system

I usually keep:

- one useful Battery;
- one or two usable Repeaters;
- a Spinner for temporary power;
- a Biogenerator when I can also fuel it;
- Machinery and Processors;
- one Materials Case;
- a Furnace when Scrap is available;
- larger or Mark II equipment.

### Items that duplicate a temporary solution

I normally recycle:

- extra weak Batteries;
- Spinners I cannot automate;
- storage replaced by a better container;
- equipment that will not fit before the next planned objective;
- low-level duplicates of stronger installed machines.

### Items that block the next Scavenge

I discard common low-value items when:

- the Recycler is already occupied;
- Inventory is full;
- the item does not open a system;
- waiting would delay another Scavenge attempt;
- the next result may contain Machinery, a Repeater or a stronger Battery.

I do not preserve every item because it might become useful several Prestiges
later.

The current run only needs the equipment that supports its next objective.

<h2 id="repair-navigation">Repair Navigation Before Perfecting the Power Grid</h2>

The starting location cannot provide every resource efficiently.

Once the Recycler is available and total Energy is positive, I focus on
Navigation.

Navigation lets me see the map.

Mobility lets me cross it.

I need both before the run can reach resource POIs, Spires or Traders.

My route is:

1. keep the temporary power setup Energy-positive;
2. repair Navigation;
3. save the first Mobility cost;
4. purchase one Mobility level;
5. inspect the nearest revealed POIs;
6. choose between a resource stop and a Spire.

I do not wait for:

- maximum Battery capacity;
- a clean Body Grid;
- fully automated Biomass;
- several upgraded generators;
- every Inventory slot to contain something valuable.

The opening machine has completed its first job when it can leave.

<h2 id="first-destination">Choose a Resource POI or Head Directly to a Spire</h2>

I do not always choose the same first destination.

The correct route depends on what is blocking the machine.

### I stop for resources when movement is not yet sustainable

I make a short stop at Ruins, Wreckage, Craters or another useful location when
I still need:

- Metal for Mobility;
- Machinery for the Recycler or later repairs;
- Processors;
- Amplifiers;
- a better Repeater;
- storage;
- a Furnace or Decomposer;
- enough Energy equipment to remain positive while moving.

I do not stay at the first POI simply because it is nearby.

If its useful resources are exhausted, I collect what matters and continue.

### I go directly to a Spire when the machine is stable

I skip ordinary resource stops when:

- Navigation and Mobility are online;
- Energy remains positive during travel;
- the Recycler can handle the expected loot;
- another temporary machine will not change the route;
- the next permanent decision depends on map information.

The Spire is the first POI that changes what I can plan.

It reveals the surrounding map, contributes to future Augment Points and can
expose Trader locations after Signal Decryption is available.

### I leave when the current POI stops supporting the objective

I leave when:

- Metal or Biomass has been exhausted;
- storage is full;
- the important items have already been collected;
- repeated Scavenge results contain only weak duplicates;
- staying does not advance a repair, Task or nearby achievement.

I do not upgrade an inactive Salvager at a location with no Metal.

I do not add another Harvester after the local Biomass is gone.

<h2 id="repair-first-spire">Repair, Charge and Decrypt the First Spire</h2>

At the first Spire, I complete the whole sequence before leaving:

1. repair the Spire;
2. charge it when required;
3. reveal the nearby map;
4. start Signal Decryption;
5. remain at the Spire until useful locations appear.

An early Spire repair took roughly **two and a half to three minutes** in the
tested fresh routes.

Signal Decryption pauses when I travel away.

I do not begin it and immediately leave for an ordinary resource pile.

Once the map updates, I ask:

- Is another Spire already visible?
- Does a Trader sell something needed immediately?
- Are Tasks unlocked?
- Is a resource POI blocking Mobility or repairs?
- Is the current Reset total already enough for a useful Augment package?

I do not automatically travel to the nearest Trader.

When Tasks are still locked and the Trader stock does not solve an immediate
problem, another Spire may produce more permanent value.

<h2 id="when-to-prestige">Prestige When Temporary Growth Stops Opening Systems</h2>

The first System Reset often feels premature because the current machine still
has many possible upgrades.

That does not mean the run is still efficient.

I open the Reset preview after:

- repairing the first Spire;
- revealing important destinations;
- completing a nearby achievement;
- reaching an expensive Body expansion;
- identifying Tasks, Auto Explore or another Augment as the real bottleneck.

I use two broad first-run routes.

### Fast reset

I take the faster route when:

- the opening has become slow;
- the next temporary purchase opens no new system;
- the useful early Augment path has begun;
- finding another repair would require blind exploration.

### Longer first Prestige

I continue when:

- more Spires are already visible;
- the machine can repair them without a major rebuild;
- one nearby achievement is almost complete;
- another repair crosses a known Augment cost;
- a stronger 20–30-point package is realistically close.

<h2 id="traders-tasks-reputation">Use Traders After Tasks Have a Purpose</h2>

IDLE_DIRECTIVE has four Traders:

- Habitat;
- Reservoir;
- Outpost;
- Enclave.

Traders use Energy as currency and can offer resources, machines, components,
keys and later progression items.

Reaching a Trader does not automatically create a Reputation route.

During the tested first visits:

- buying an item did not increase Reputation;
- selling Metal did not replace Task progress;
- entering the location could trigger the relevant Trader contact achievement.

The useful Reputation loop begins after unlocking **Tasks**.

### Do not buy a Transponder before Tasks

The Transponder marks a destination for a Trader Task.

Without the Tasks Augment, there is no usable Task location to mark.

I buy a Transponder when:

- Tasks are unlocked;
- I have accepted a suitable Task;
- the reward or Reputation is worth the travel;
- the current run is intended to build Trader progress.

I do not buy one simply because it appears in stock.

### Focus one useful Trader at a time

Trader Reputation normally resets during System Reset unless later Augments
preserve part of it.

During an early Reputation run, I focus on the Trader whose stock supports the
current objective.

Examples include:

- Reservoir when I need access to the stronger 4×2 Battery shown in the
  Databank;
- a Trader selling a Lab Access Key;
- a Trader carrying a machine or component blocking automation;
- the faction whose maximum-Reputation achievement I am completing.

I do not divide a short run across four incomplete Reputation bars.

<h2 id="blueprints-replicator">Unlock Crafting Through Traders, Labs and Blueprints</h2>

The crafting route has several dependencies:

> reveal Traders → unlock Tasks and Trader progress → obtain the correct Lab key
> → enter a Lab → find Blueprints → use the Replicator.

Unlocking or finding a Replicator does not automatically provide every recipe.

Blueprints provide recipes.

Labs provide Blueprints.

Labs require the appropriate access key, and those keys are connected to Trader
progression.

<h2 id="terminals">Start Terminal Progression After the Normal Machine Is Stable</h2>

Terminals are not an early replacement for Spires.

The first captured Terminal repair required:

- **150,000 Energy**;
- **10,000 Metal**;
- **25 Machinery**;
- a displayed repair time of **one hour**.

    Mobility, processing or the next Prestige route.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

Terminal repair time is dynamic in the current version.

An older save that began a repair before the timing change may retain the older
value until the next Terminal. I therefore use the timer shown in the active
save.

I begin Terminal progression when:

- automated Metal production is stable;
- Machinery has a dedicated reserve;
- Energy capacity can absorb the payment;
- the machine no longer needs those resources for basic survival;
- the current run can continue through additional Prestiges.

I wait when paying for the Terminal would:

- empty every Machinery container;
- prevent the next Mobility purchase;
- shut down Metal or Biomass processing;
- delay a stronger Prestige package;
- leave no resources for rebuilding the normal machine.

The game tracks four Terminals.

Repairing one advances the Terminal achievement route. Repairing all of them is
part of the late-game Directive progression.

I do not assume that every later Terminal has the same universal cost or timer.
I check the active stage before committing resources.

<h2 id="common-stalls">Check One Missing Condition Before Rebuilding Everything</h2>

Most early stalls are caused by one missing condition.

### Spinner receives power but does not stay active

The Repeater must touch the active generator directly.

### Biogenerator has power but produces no Energy

Its Biocell must be directly connected and contain Biomass.

### Salvager or Harvester reports no valid location

The current POI does not contain its resource, or that resource has been
exhausted.

### Collector or Grabber moves nothing

I check for compatible storage and free capacity.

### Trader offers no Tasks

The Tasks Augment is still locked.

### Auto Explore visits useless locations

The route includes POIs the current machine cannot use, or repair and waiting
conditions are too broad.

<h2 id="progression-loop">Repeat the Same Progression Decisions at a Larger Scale</h2>

After the first Prestige, I do not recreate the old machine tile for tile.

I ask what the new permanent purchase changed.

If I bought more Scavenge results, I repair the Recycler and prepare storage
earlier.

If I bought Signal Decryption, I move from Navigation to a Spire instead of
wandering through ordinary POIs.

If I bought Tasks, I visit a Trader with a Task objective rather than buying
random stock.

If I bought Auto Explore, I first build enough Mobility, storage and resource
processing to sustain the selected route.

If I bought Auto Repair, I begin with Spires before including expensive
Reactors.

If I unlocked the Replicator, I craft the exact item blocking the current
production chain.

If Terminal progression is active, I maintain Metal, Machinery and Energy
reserves without delaying every useful Prestige.

The complete loop becomes:

> wake the machine → restore movement → repair the POIs that fund the next
> permanent breakpoint → automate one bottleneck → complete one nearby
> achievement → advance Trader, Lab or Terminal progression → reset before
> temporary optimization becomes the objective.

That is where IDLE_DIRECTIVE stops feeling like a slow opening puzzle and becomes
a controlled progression system.
