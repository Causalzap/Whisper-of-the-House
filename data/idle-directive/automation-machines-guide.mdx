If a machine is powered but inactive, I check three things before moving it:

1. does its input need to touch it directly?
2. does it need a specific point of interest?
3. does it have compatible storage with free capacity?

Those three checks solve most early automation failures.

My first Spinner stopped because its Repeater was connected through Wire instead
of touching it. My first Biogenerator had Biomass nearby but no directly
connected Biocell. My Salvager was powered at a location with no Metal, and my
Collector had nowhere to place the items it could see.

I now build automation in this order:

> create temporary power → repair Navigation → move to a useful POI → add
> matching storage → automate one resource chain → replace temporary machines →
> test Auto Explore with one controlled route.

map automation. Exact tile-by-tile arrangements are kept in the{" "}

> source → storage → processor → output storage or consumer.

I do not upgrade the final machine until I know the problem is actually at the
final machine.

<h2 id="spinner-repeater">Use a Spinner and Repeater as Temporary Power</h2>

A Spinner is useful because it can provide opening Energy before I have a
complete fuel chain.

A level-one Spinner in the tested opening generated **0.500 Energy per second**
while spinning.

The limitation is that the Spinner is an active generator. It needs repeated
activation.

A Repeater automates that activation, but it must touch the Spinner directly.

Wire can connect both machines to the wider power network. Wire does not transmit
the Repeater trigger across the grid.

My opening route is:

1. place the Spinner where at least one activation side remains open;
2. connect one Repeater directly to it;
3. connect the setup to powered Wiring;
4. watch whether the Spinner slows down between activations;
5. add or replace the Repeater only when the timing is the bottleneck;
6. stop investing once the setup supports Navigation and Mobility.

A standard early Repeater can trigger every four seconds.

The later **Repeater (2s)** automatically triggers a directly connected active
generator every two seconds. When I find one, it can replace weaker Repeater
coverage and recover Body Space.

I keep the Spinner setup when:

- the Recycler and Navigation Computer would otherwise create negative Energy;
- I do not have a reliable Biomass source;
- the grid still has room for storage and processing;
- one inexpensive upgrade makes total Energy income positive.

I stop expanding it when:

- another Spinner requires another large Repeater group;
- storage or a Furnace no longer fits;
- a Biogenerator can replace several temporary pieces;
- an amplifier needs the same neighbouring tiles;
- the machine already has enough Energy to reach the next POI.

The opening Spinner has completed its job once movement is stable.

<h2 id="biogenerator-not-working">Fix a Biogenerator That Produces No Energy</h2>

The Biogenerator needs two different connections:

- powered Wiring;
- a directly connected Biocell containing Biomass.

Biomass does not travel through ordinary Wire.

A Biomass container placed elsewhere in the Body Grid does not fuel the
Biogenerator automatically.

My troubleshooting order is:

1. confirm the Biogenerator is switched on;
2. confirm it is connected to the powered Wiring network;
3. place a Biocell directly against the required side;
4. fill the Biocell from Biomass storage;
5. check the Biocell's current contents;
6. compare generation with the Energy consumption of the complete machine.

A normal Biocell can be refilled and emptied manually.

One tested Biocell had **3,520 Biomass capacity** after substantial upgrading.

A Refilling Biocell begins with much less capacity—the captured level-one version
held only **20 Biomass**—but it automatically refills when empty, with a
**30-second cooldown**.

I use the normal Biocell when:

- the generator is still temporary;
- Biomass consumption is low;
- I am watching the machine;
- its much larger upgraded capacity provides a useful buffer.

I use the Refilling Biocell when:

- the Biogenerator is the main power source;
- Auto Explore will move the machine unattended;
- manual refilling is repeatedly stopping the whole system;
- the cooldown and available Biomass supply can support consumption.

A large generation number is not enough by itself.

One tested Biogenerator II produced **437.5 Energy per second** with a **25%
Energy Amplification** bonus, but it also consumed **7 Biomass per second**.

I therefore check both:

- net Energy after every active machine is included;
- whether Biomass storage is rising or falling.

<h2 id="collection-machines">Give Each Collection Machine One Job</h2>

Collector, Grabber, Salvager and Harvester solve different bottlenecks.

I do not keep all four active at every location.

### Collector

The Collector clears resources and upgrade materials from completed Scavenge
results when matching storage is available.

I use it when the Scavenge grid remains full long enough to slow down the next
Scavenge action.

I check four things when it appears inactive:

1. are there completed Scavenge results?
2. does matching storage exist?
3. does that storage have free capacity?
4. is the Collector powered and charged?

The Collector does not harvest Metal or Biomass from the current map location.

### Grabber

The Grabber moves compatible items from the current POI into matching storage.

It is useful for resources such as Scrap and Carcasses.

It cannot place:

- Scrap into a Machinery container;
- Carcasses into Scrap storage;
- an item into a full container;
- a resource that no longer exists at the current POI.

When its tooltip reports no materials, I check the POI and storage before adding
another Grabber.

I move when the location is exhausted.

### Salvager

The Salvager harvests Metal from a compatible location.

I install it when:

- Metal is the current bottleneck;
- the POI still contains Metal;
- I plan to remain long enough for the machine to recover its space and Energy
  cost;
- storage or spending capacity can absorb the output.

I do not upgrade it at a location with no Metal.

### Harvester

The Harvester collects Biomass from a compatible location.

I prioritize it when the Biogenerator is draining the Biomass buffer faster than
the existing chain can replace it.

I turn it off or leave it behind when:

- the POI has no Biomass;
- Biomass storage remains full;
- Metal, Machinery or Rare Metal is the actual bottleneck;
- the machine is moving to a Trader, Spire or Terminal.

<h2 id="automated-metal">Automate Metal From Scrap Without Overbuilding</h2>

My basic Metal chain is:

> Scrap source → Scrap Box → Furnace → Metal.

The storage step prevents intermittent collection from disrupting the Furnace.

Without Scrap storage:

- a Grabber may have nowhere to place Scrap;
- a Scrap Scavenger cannot maintain a useful buffer;
- the Furnace waits whenever the current POI is empty.

An early Furnace converted:

- **1 Scrap**
- into **50 Metal**
- every **60 seconds**.

A developed Furnace II converted:

- **1 Scrap**
- into **60 Metal**
- every **15 seconds**.

The captured level-58 Furnace II could process up to **58 Scrap at a time**.
It was processing **20 batches** and consuming **120 Energy per second** at that
load.

A level-20 Scrap Scavenger generated:

- **20 Scrap**
- every **30 seconds**;
- while consuming **2.10 Energy per second**.

That equals **40 Scrap per minute**.

With 20 concurrent 15-second Furnace batches, the tested Furnace configuration
could consume up to 80 Scrap per minute. One level-20 Scrap Scavenger therefore
did not justify adding another Furnace.

I add Scrap production when:

- the Furnace is powered but regularly waits for input;
- the Scrap Box remains nearly empty;
- Metal production stops whenever I leave a Scrap-bearing POI.

I improve Furnace throughput when:

- Scrap storage stays full;
- the Furnace remains active continuously;
- Metal output is still slower than current spending.

I add storage when:

- the source and Furnace are both fast enough;
- short interruptions cause Scrap to be lost;
- Auto Explore will move away before the Furnace finishes the buffer.

I stop investing when Metal accumulates faster than I can spend it.

At that point, another resource is limiting progression.

<h2 id="automated-biomass">Balance Carcass Production Against Biomass Consumption</h2>

A sustainable Biomass chain can be built as:

> Cultivator or Carcass POI → Carcass storage → Decomposer → Biomass storage →
> Biocell → Biogenerator.

A Harvester can add direct Biomass income at a valid POI.

A Cultivator and Decomposer provide a stationary route that does not depend on
remaining at a Biomass location.

In the tested developed setup:

- a level-17 Cultivator produced **17 Carcasses every 60 seconds**;
- a level-25 Decomposer II converted **1 Carcass into 12 Biomass every 10
  seconds**;
- the Decomposer II could process up to **25 Carcasses at a time**;
- the Biogenerator II consumed **7 Biomass per second**.

Seventeen Carcasses produce:

> 17 × 12 = 204 Biomass per minute.

That equals approximately:

> 3.4 Biomass per second.

One level-17 Cultivator therefore did **not** sustainably fuel that
Biogenerator II by itself.

The generator consumed 7 Biomass per second while the Cultivator supplied the
equivalent of about 3.4. Stored Biomass, POI harvesting, another Cultivator or
another Carcass source had to cover the deficit.

I watch the storage trend for several minutes while:

- Mobility remains active;
- the Furnace and Recycler are running;
- the machine leaves a Biomass POI;
- the Biogenerator remains at normal load.

I increase Biomass input when storage trends downward.

I upgrade the Biogenerator when:

- Biomass storage remains stable or rises;
- Energy output is the actual bottleneck;
- the amplifier and fuel chain can support the higher level.

I add storage when:

- average production is sufficient;
- short interruptions still empty the Biocell;
- Auto Explore will leave the source behind.

<h2 id="amplifiers">Match Each Amplifier to the Correct Target</h2>

Amplifiers are adjacency machines.

Connecting them to the same Wiring network is not enough. The supported target
must occupy a valid neighbouring tile.

### Concentrator

The Concentrator supports neighbouring Energy Cells.

I use it when enough strong Energy Cells can occupy its active sides.

I do not preserve several weak cells simply to fill the shape. I compare the
complete cluster's output and occupied tiles with the generator that could use
the same space.

### Energy Amplifier

The Energy Amplifier increases the output of supported neighbouring Energy
producers.

The captured level-five version provided **25% amplification**.

I use it beside the strongest Biogenerator or other compatible producer.

One later Energy Amplifier upgrade required:

- 10 Rare Metal;
- 7 Amplifiers;
- 7 Magnets;

and added another 5% in that setup.

I do not pay that cost while the fuel chain is already running dry. Increasing
generation also increases the importance of maintaining its input.

### Power Amplifier

The Power Amplifier improves neighbouring Harvesters and Salvagers.

The tested level-one version provided a **25% output increase**.

I place the strongest valid location machine beside it first.

I do not expect it to:

- amplify a distant machine;
- make a Salvager work at a POI without Metal;
- make a Harvester work at a POI without Biomass;
- solve full storage;
- improve a Collector or Furnace.

<h2 id="upgrade-duplicate-replace">Upgrade, Duplicate or Replace Based on the Bottleneck</h2>

Before spending resources, I ask where the chain is slowing down.

### Upgrade the source when processors wait empty

Examples:

- the Furnace waits for Scrap;
- the Decomposer waits for Carcasses;
- Biomass storage falls while the Biogenerator remains active;
- a Collector clears the grid faster than I can generate new results.

I improve collection or production first.

### Upgrade processing when input storage remains full

Examples:

- the Scrap Box remains full while Metal production is slow;
- Carcass storage remains full;
- completed Scavenge results accumulate;
- the current processing cycle is slower than incoming material.

I improve or duplicate the processor.

### Add storage when both sides are fast enough

I add capacity when:

- input production and processing are both strong;
- the chain fails only during brief interruptions;
- Auto Explore leaves the location unattended;
- several machines share the same buffer.

### Replace equipment when the stronger item saves real space

I do not replace every Mark I item immediately.

I replace it when the new version solves a measurable problem:

- a 2-second Repeater replaces multiple weaker Repeaters;
- Furnace II removes a full Scrap backlog;
- Collector II clears results before the next Scavenge;
- a larger container replaces several small containers;
- a larger Battery opens enough tiles for another machine.

I do not protect temporary equipment because it was useful earlier.

<h2 id="storage-bottlenecks">Treat Storage as an Active Part of Automation</h2>

Storage tells me which part of the chain is failing.

I keep separate containers for resources with separate processing paths:

- Scrap;
- Carcasses;
- Biomass;
- Machinery;
- Magnets;
- Amplifiers;
- Processors;
- Wires when I am frequently rebuilding.

A Materials Case normally stores one selected category. Placing Magnets inside
one does not make it accept Machinery or Processors automatically.

When a machine stops moving items, I check storage before assuming the machine
is broken.

### Empty input storage

The source is too slow or the current POI is exhausted.

### Permanently full input storage

Processing is too slow.

### Falling fuel storage

The generator consumes more than the chain produces.

### Full output storage

Adding more collection or processing will not help until I spend, move or expand
the output capacity.

### Empty storage during Auto Explore

The route may be leaving the required resource too early or visiting the wrong
POI types.

Storage is the visible buffer between each stage of the machine. I use it as a
diagnostic tool, not only as additional capacity.

<h2 id="replicator-taskmasters">Use the Replicator for a Known Bottleneck</h2>

I do not craft random machines because the Replicator has an empty slot.

Before starting a recipe, I check:

1. is the recipe or Blueprint already unlocked?
2. which exact missing machine is blocking the current chain?
3. do I have every required component?
4. can the machine remain powered during crafting?
5. will one completed item improve throughput immediately?

In one Power Amplifier attempt, the recipe displayed **288 seconds**, but one
Amplifier component was still missing.

Starting or unlocking the recipe did not solve that missing input.

I craft one copy, test it and then decide whether more copies are useful.

I prioritize extra Replicator slots when multiple confirmed recipes are waiting.

I prioritize Replicator speed when long crafting times are the bottleneck.

I do not prioritize either when:

- the required Blueprint is still locked;
- rare components are missing;
- the completed machine has no valid place in the current chain;
- basic Metal or Biomass production is still unstable.

### Taskmasters are a late Replicator investment

Taskmasters provide passive Trader Reputation and Augment progress, but their
early return is slow.

In the tested setup:

- one Taskmaster required **3,600 seconds** to craft;
- the Replicator could queue up to five at a time in that configuration;
- one deployed Taskmaster produced about **one Augment Point per hour**;
- six Taskmasters running overnight produced only about **31 points**.

That means the first Taskmaster does not quickly repay the crafting time,
Replicator occupancy and Blueprint progression used to create it.

I delay Taskmasters until:

- Replicator speed has been improved;
- several crafting slots are available;
- essential machines are no longer waiting in the queue;
- Auto Repair and repair rewards are already producing stronger active progress;
- the current run is intended to continue for many hours.

I do not use Taskmasters as the first solution to Trader Reputation.

Manual Tasks and Reputation Augments create more immediate progress during the
earlier runs.

I craft Taskmasters when the alternative is leaving an upgraded Replicator idle
overnight.

<h2 id="auto-explore">Configure Auto Explore Around the Current Machine</h2>

Auto Explore can automate movement, but it cannot correct a bad resource chain.

It may move away from the POI that fuels the machine, visit a Lab without a key,
fill every storage container or repeatedly reach repairs the machine cannot
afford.

I open:

> Map → Options → Automation Settings

My first controlled setup is:

1. choose one exploration pattern;
2. enable only POI types the machine can currently use;
3. disable Labs until I have a Lab Access Key;
4. choose when to leave based on the resource I am collecting;
5. leave Wait for Grabbers off until collection and storage are proven;
6. leave Auto Repair off until the resource buffers can pay for repairs;
7. watch one complete route before leaving the machine unattended.

### When to move after Metal is exhausted

I use this when:

- Salvagers are active;
- Metal is the current bottleneck;
- the POI's remaining Biomass does not support the current machine;
- storage and processing can absorb the collected Metal.

### When to move after Biomass is exhausted

I use this when:

- Harvesters support the Biogenerator;
- Biomass storage is falling;
- Metal is already sufficient;
- the machine needs to rebuild its fuel buffer.

### When to leave immediately

I use this only when:

- exploration or travel is the current objective;
- no local collection machine needs time;
- the next Spire, Trader or repair target matters more than resources;
- continuous movement remains Energy-positive.

### When to wait for Grabbers

I enable this after confirming:

- the Grabber is powered;
- the POI contains compatible items;
- matching storage has enough free capacity;
- waiting does not trap the route at an exhausted location.

I turn Auto Explore off and return manually when:

- Scrap, Carcass or Biomass storage is full;
- the fuel buffer trends downward;
- the machine visits unusable POIs;
- a specific Trader, Spire or Terminal becomes the next objective;
- Auto Repair repeatedly fails to start;
- movement consumes the Energy safety margin.

In the tested launch build, maximum movement speed could occasionally cause Auto
Explore to stall. Reducing the speed slightly helped movement resume.

I treat that as version-specific troubleshooting, not a universal rule.

<h2 id="automation-readiness">Know When the Machine Is Ready to Run Unattended</h2>

I consider a chain automated only when it survives a complete cycle without
manual rescue.

For a stationary Metal chain, that means:

> generate or collect Scrap → store it → process it → receive Metal → continue
> without clearing a full box.

For a Biomass power chain, that means:

> produce or collect Carcasses → convert them → store Biomass → refill the
> Biocell → power the generator without a falling long-term buffer.

For Auto Explore, that means:

> leave one POI → travel → collect → store → process → leave again without
> running out of Energy or filling storage.

Before stepping away, I check:

- total Energy remains positive while every required machine is active;
- direct-contact machines still touch after the latest rebuild;
- the current POI matches the active Salvager or Harvester;
- input and output containers have room;
- Biomass production covers average consumption;
- processing capacity matches incoming materials;
- the exploration route includes only usable destinations;
- the stop condition prevents the machine from consuming its final safety
  buffer.

My automation loop is:

> identify the current bottleneck → automate only that stage → watch one complete
> cycle → compare source and processing rates → expand the slower side → remove
> temporary equipment when it blocks the next system.

That keeps the Body Grid functional without turning every new item into another
machine that looks active but produces no permanent progress.
