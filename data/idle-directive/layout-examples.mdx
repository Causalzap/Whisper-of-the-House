I stopped trying to build one perfect IDLE_DIRECTIVE layout.

The items I find after Scavenge change from run to run. Body Space grows at
different times, and a machine that saves the opening can become the exact thing
blocking the next upgrade.

I now copy the purpose of a layout rather than every tile.

A working layout should answer four questions:

- what does this machine need to accomplish next?
- which pieces must touch directly?
- which neighbouring tiles must stay open?
- what event tells me to dismantle it?

The four examples below cover the layouts I actually reuse:

1. a compact post-reset machine for reaching Navigation;
2. a Concentrator cluster for passive Energy;
3. a Power Amplifier wing for Metal or Biomass collection;
4. a stationary Metal and Biomass automation base.

These are not final blueprints. I rotate, move or replace pieces when the current
run gives me different Batteries, storage or Mark II equipment.

I do not build the largest layout I can fit.

I choose the smallest layout that opens the next system.

Before placing anything, I divide the Body Grid into four functional areas.

### Power bridge

This is the temporary group that keeps the Recycler, Navigation and early
Mobility online.

I keep it compact because it is usually the first section I replace.

### Processing lane

I keep the Recycler, Furnace and Decomposer somewhere I can reach without
removing the main generator.

A processing machine may need to be switched off when its input is empty or
Energy is needed for a repair.

### Storage edge

I place ordinary storage along walls and irregular spaces that do not provide
valuable amplifier adjacency.

I avoid using the best neighbouring tiles for small containers that can easily
be moved later.

### Expansion lane

I keep at least one clear route for Wiring and one open area large enough to
accept a future machine.

My earliest layouts failed because I turned every empty tile into a Battery. The
machine worked until I found a stronger generator or amplifier, then half the
grid had to be dismantled just to install it.

<h2 id="bootstrap-layout">Layout 1: Post-Reset Bootstrap</h2>

I use the bootstrap layout after a fresh start or early Prestige.

Its job is not to automate the game. Its job is to restore movement with the
fewest temporary pieces possible.

My placement order is:

1. install the first useful passive Energy source;
2. place one Spinner where at least one Repeater side remains open;
3. connect the Repeater directly;
4. add a Battery without blocking the control side;
5. reserve a practical area for the Recycler;
6. reserve another area for Navigation;
7. place a Biogenerator only when both it and its Biocell can fit;
8. repair Navigation;
9. save Metal and Energy for Mobility;
10. leave the starting area before expanding the temporary power group again.

### Where I place the temporary generator

I place the Spinner near an edge, but not inside a corner that removes every
usable Repeater side.

I do not surround it with Batteries first.

The direct connection is more important than making the opening grid look
compact.

If I find a better Repeater, I replace the weaker control setup instead of
building another complete Spinner group.

### Where I reserve Navigation space

Navigation is the reason this layout exists.

I do not let storage, duplicate Batteries or optional processing equipment fill
the only area large enough to install it.

When the grid becomes crowded, I ask which item is delaying movement.

A duplicate Battery or unused storage container is normally easier to remove
than the power source currently supporting the repair.

### When I install the first Biogenerator

I install it only when I can preserve room for its directly connected fuel
source.

I do not place the generator first and hope the Biocell will fit later.

During this stage, manual refilling is acceptable because I am still watching the
machine. I do not wait for a perfect automated fuel solution before repairing
Navigation.

### What I keep from Scavenge

I keep items that help me leave:

- one or two useful Repeaters;
- a practical Battery;
- a Biogenerator I can actually fuel;
- Machinery and Processors needed for repairs;
- a limited number of Wires;
- one flexible Materials Case.

I recycle or discard duplicate low-level equipment when keeping it would require
another Inventory or Body expansion before Navigation is repaired.

### When I dismantle the bootstrap layout

I rebuild after reaching a POI that supports a real production chain.

The bootstrap layout has completed its job when:

- Navigation is repaired;
- Mobility is available;
- Energy remains positive while moving;
- I can reach a Metal, Biomass, Spire or Trader objective.

I do not add another temporary generator simply because one more empty tile
exists.

<h2 id="concentrator-layout">Layout 2: Concentrator and Energy Cell Cluster</h2>

I build around a Concentrator only when I already have enough useful Energy Cells
to justify its neighbouring positions.

I place the cluster before storage and Batteries because its shape is less
flexible.

My order is:

1. choose a clean open section;
2. place the Concentrator first;
3. identify every valid neighbouring position;
4. put the strongest Energy Cells in those positions;
5. connect the cluster to the main Wiring route;
6. keep Batteries and storage outside the useful sides;
7. compare the complete cluster with the generator that could occupy the same
   area.

### I use this layout when

- I have several good Energy Cells;
- the Concentrator appears before a stable Biomass chain;
- the machine needs passive generation without fuel;
- the Body Grid has a large open section;
- I can upgrade the cells without delaying a more important system.

### I skip this layout when

- I only have one or two weak Energy Cells;
- the cluster blocks the only Wiring route;
- storage and processing no longer fit;
- a stable Biogenerator produces more useful output from less space;
- I am preserving weak cells only because the Concentrator exists.

The correct question is not “Can I fill every side?”

It is:

> Does the full cluster justify the tiles that it prevents me from using
> elsewhere?

### When I return to this layout

I reconsider it when:

- stronger Energy Cells appear;
- an Upgrader can improve one selected cell;
- Body Space expands;
- an Energy achievement needs additional generation;
- the main fuel chain becomes unreliable.

I do not dismantle a stable Biomass system just to test one newly found
Concentrator.

<h2 id="harvesting-layout">Layout 3: Power Amplifier Harvesting Wing</h2>

I build the harvesting wing when resource collection is the current bottleneck.

The Power Amplifier's neighbouring positions are reserved for the best
Harvesters and Salvagers I can use at the current POI.

My placement order is:

1. identify whether Metal or Biomass is limiting progress;
2. choose the strongest matching collection machine;
3. place the Power Amplifier beside it;
4. place another useful collection machine on a second active side;
5. connect the group to Wiring;
6. move storage or processing equipment away from the remaining useful sides;
7. remove temporary equipment when it blocks the amplified group;
8. upgrade the strongest machine before adding weak duplicates.

### Why I removed the Spinner group

The Spinner was still producing some Energy.

That did not make it worth protecting.

By this stage:

- stronger power sources were available;
- the Spinner needed several supporting tiles;
- the Power Amplifier needed those same positions;
- Metal and Biomass collection were limiting progression more than opening
  Energy.

I removed the entire temporary group rather than compressing the new layout
around it.

This is the main rule of the harvesting wing:

> an old machine does not keep priority just because it still works.

### When I build the Metal version

I use Salvagers beside the amplifier when:

- Body Space or Mobility upgrades are waiting for Metal;
- the current POI still contains Metal;
- I plan to remain longer than one Scavenge action;
- storage can absorb the additional output;
- the current processing or spending route can use it.

I do not reorganize the entire grid for a POI that is almost exhausted.

### When I build the Biomass version

I use Harvesters beside the amplifier when:

- the Biogenerator is draining the fuel buffer;
- one Harvester is not keeping up;
- the POI still contains Biomass;
- I intend to remain long enough to rebuild storage.

I do not add more Harvesters when Biomass storage remains full and Energy output
is already sufficient.

### When I leave the wing behind

I stop using the layout when:

- the POI is exhausted;
- storage is permanently full;
- processing is slower than collection;
- the run moves toward Traders, Spires or Terminals;
- a stationary production chain replaces location-based collection.

An amplifier does not create a resource that is no longer present.

<h2 id="stationary-base-layout">Layout 4: Stationary Metal and Biomass Base</h2>

I build a stationary base only after the machine can sustain two separate
resource chains.

The layout combines:

> Scrap source → Scrap storage → Furnace → Metal

and:

> Carcass source → Carcass storage → Decomposer → Biomass storage → Biocell →
> Biogenerator

The two sides share power and Body Space, but I keep their input storage visually
separate.

### The Metal side

I place the Scrap source and Scrap Box close enough that the input route remains
obvious.

The Furnace sits where it can be disabled or moved without disconnecting the
main generator.

I add another Scrap source when the Furnace repeatedly waits empty.

I improve Furnace capacity when Scrap storage remains full and Metal production
is still too slow.

I do not add another Furnace merely because Metal is important.

The input buffer tells me whether more processing is actually needed.

### The Biomass side

I keep Carcass storage between the source and the Decomposer.

I keep Biomass storage between the Decomposer and the Biogenerator.

That gives the layout two visible buffers:

- Carcass storage shows whether the Decomposer is keeping up;
- Biomass storage shows whether fuel production is keeping up with consumption.

In the tested setup, one Cultivator did not sustainably supply the developed
Biogenerator by itself.

I do not repeat the entire throughput calculation here. The practical layout
signal is simpler:

> if Biomass storage falls during normal operation, I need more input before I
> upgrade the generator again.

### Where I place the buffers

I preserve:

- direct Biocell contact;
- amplifier adjacency;
- one clear Wiring route;
- enough storage for a temporary interruption;
- access to switch off or move a processor.

The containers do not need to touch every machine in the chain.

Their most important purpose in this layout is showing which stage is rising,
falling or staying full.

### When I use this base

I use it when:

- the run will remain in one area for a long period;
- Metal and Biomass are both required;
- crafting or repairs need a sustained resource supply;
- Body Space is large enough to separate the two chains;
- temporary collection from one POI is no longer sufficient.

I do not build it immediately before Prestige.

<h2 id="battery-placement">Keep Batteries Away From Valuable Adjacency</h2>

Batteries are easy to place because they only need access to power.

That makes them the easiest objects to put in the wrong position.

I place them along:

- exterior walls;
- irregular corners;
- spaces that cannot support a useful amplifier group;
- areas that do not interrupt the Wiring lane.

I avoid placing Batteries:

- beside a Concentrator;
- beside an Energy Amplifier;
- beside a Power Amplifier;
- between a Biogenerator and its Biocell;
- inside the only expansion lane.

During the opening, I accept inefficient Battery placement when I urgently need
capacity.

Later, I compare capacity per occupied tile.

I replace several weak Batteries when one larger model can:

- preserve the same capacity;
- open an amplifier side;
- make room for a processor;
- reopen the Wiring path.

I do not keep every Battery installed for a future capacity achievement.

When I am ready to attempt a capacity milestone, I can temporarily rebuild around
Batteries and then restore the normal layout.

<h2 id="storage-placement">Use Storage to Show Which Stage Is Failing</h2>

I place important input storage near the chain it belongs to.

Examples include:

- Scrap Box near the Furnace;
- Carcass storage near the Decomposer;
- Biomass storage near the Biocell and generator;
- Machinery and component storage near the upgrade area;
- one flexible case near the Recycler and Scavenge workflow.

This makes a stalled layout easier to read.

### Empty Scrap storage

I need more Scrap collection or production.

### Permanently full Scrap storage

I need more Furnace throughput or more Metal spending.

### Full Carcass storage

The Decomposer is too slow, unpowered or blocked.

### Falling Biomass storage

The Biogenerator consumes more than the chain produces.

### Full Scavenge results with empty storage

The Collector is too slow or not connected correctly.

Storage is not only spare capacity.

It is the most visible signal of where the layout is unbalanced.

<h2 id="leave-open-space">Reserve Space for the Machine I Have Not Found Yet</h2>

I cannot predict every item drop, but I can avoid making future upgrades
impossible.

Before Navigation, I preserve an area large enough to install it.

Before a strong generator, I preserve room for its direct fuel connection.

Before a Concentrator, I avoid filling the largest open section with storage.

Before a Power Amplifier, I keep the strongest collection machines within moving
distance of one another.

Before the Replicator, I preserve enough open space to test a crafted machine
without switching off the whole grid.

I still use temporary space during emergencies.

The difference is that I avoid spending rare materials upgrading an item that I
already expect to remove.

<h2 id="when-to-rebuild">Rebuild Only for a Measurable Reason</h2>

I do not rebuild because the grid looks untidy.

I rebuild when the current layout prevents progress.

### A direct connection no longer fits

Examples include:

- no open tile for the Biocell;
- no usable Repeater side;
- an amplifier surrounded by storage;
- no route to extend Wiring.

### One machine replaces several weaker pieces

I remove the old group after confirming the replacement can support the active
machine.

I do not remove temporary power first and hope the new generator will work after
the rebuild.

### A buffer moves in the wrong direction

- falling Biomass means more input is needed;
- full Scrap means more processing may be needed;
- an empty Furnace means more Furnace capacity is useless;
- full output storage means the chain has nowhere to send its result.

### The next POI changes the machine's job

At a Metal POI, I activate Salvagers.

At a Biomass POI, I activate Harvesters.

At a Trader, Spire or Terminal, location collection machines may have no useful
input. I switch off unnecessary consumers rather than preserving every previous
POI layout at full load.

### Prestige is already the stronger upgrade

System Reset removes the current Body upgrades, Wiring, installed equipment and
stored resources.

Before a planned Prestige, I only rebuild for:

- one reachable achievement;
- one final repair;
- one exact capacity threshold;
- the machine required to complete the current objective.

I do not spend another hour improving a layout that is about to disappear.

<h2 id="layout-loop">The Layout Loop I Use on Every Run</h2>

I no longer judge a machine by how full the Body Grid looks.

I judge it by whether every occupied section supports the current objective.

My layout loop is:

1. build the smallest power bridge that opens Navigation;
2. move to the POI that supplies the missing resource;
3. add matching storage before automated collection;
4. add processing only when its input exists;
5. keep direct-contact machines together;
6. reserve amplifier sides for the correct targets;
7. replace weak equipment when the new item saves real space or throughput;
8. move when the current location is exhausted;
9. stop reorganizing when Prestige becomes the stronger improvement.

A working layout has:

> one clear power route → one visible buffer for each resource chain → one open
> expansion area → no temporary machine protected after its job is finished.
