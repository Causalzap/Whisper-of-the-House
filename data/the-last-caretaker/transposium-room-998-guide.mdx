<h2 id="start-with-transposium-security">
  Start with Security before searching for Room 998
</h2>

Room 998 is easy to turn into a much bigger mystery than the game currently supports.

The useful part of the Update 5.5 Transposium changes starts at the **Security system**. From there I can move through camera nodes and compare what the monitors show with the rooms I can physically walk into.

That order matters. Searching the maze first gives me dozens of doors, room numbers, flooded spaces, paintings, pipes, and old secrets with no way to know which one is connected to the new 99X material. Security gives the search a reference point.

Once I open the camera feed, I cycle through the available nodes slowly rather than treating them like ordinary surveillance views. The interesting information is not always a moving object. Room numbers, closed doors, a broken robot, markings in the background, and apparently inaccessible spaces can all matter.

Update 5.5 clearly wants me to notice that the camera system knows about places the normal Transposium route does not simply hand over.

What it does **not** give me is a clean objective saying:

> Go through this door and enter Room 998.

That difference is the key to the entire search.

  Room 998 appears in Security before it exists as a usable destination

While cycling the feeds, **998** becomes visible as part of the Security investigation.

That distinction saved me a lot of wasted searching later.

The Security state associated with 998 showed a **broken robot and two closed doors**. At that point I had something concrete: Room 998 was not just a number pulled from a community theory. The game itself was exposing a 998 state through the Transposium system.

The problem was getting from that virtual reference to a physical entrance.

There was no obvious 998 door in the route I could walk, no new quest marker pointing to one, and no normal interaction that turned the camera feed into an accessible room.

So I treat 998 as a **real in-game breadcrumb with an incomplete normal route**, rather than as a solved secret room.

That wording matters in Early Access. “Room exists” and “player can currently enter it” are two very different claims.

<h2 id="room-72">
  The Room 72 instructions did not match my physical Transposium
</h2>

The most convincing-looking solution I tested claimed that the Security cameras were only the first step.

The proposed chain was roughly:

1. cycle the camera nodes;
2. find a hidden three-digit code in a camera view;
3. go to **Room 72**, described as a large flooded room with a broken robot;
4. use a keypad or terminal there;
5. synchronize the physical room with the hidden room matrix.

That sounds specific enough to be a real puzzle.

It also fell apart when I tried to reproduce it.

The room numbers I could inspect included numbers such as **51, 53, 56, 52, 33, 11, 37, 69, 86, and 36**, while another flooded-space check showed **76**. I never reached a physical state where the supposed flooded Room 72 and its obvious code terminal appeared together.

More importantly, I could not find the interaction the solution depended on.

That is where I draw the line between useful experimentation and writing a fake solution around somebody else's instructions.

A guide saying “enter the code in Room 72” is only useful if a normal player can actually identify Room 72 and find the corresponding keypad. On this Update 5.5 save, I could do neither.

Could another game state expose different room numbers? Possibly. I do not have enough evidence to state that as a mechanic.

What I can say is narrower and much more useful:

**If your own Transposium does not contain the Room 72 / keypad state described by a solution, do not spend hours assuming you overlooked one tiny switch.**

A camera clue is worth checking. A physical Room 72 is worth checking if the game actually gives you one. When the required room and keypad are absent from your playable state, the rest of that solution is no longer verified for your save.

<h2 id="room-998-status">
  What Room 998 looks like in Update 5.5
</h2>

After going back through Security, the strongest 998 state I could establish was still informational rather than physical.

The feed could identify **998**, the broken robot, and the closed doors. The Transposium itself could show me plenty of ordinary numbered rooms. What I could not produce was a legitimate transition between those two states.

References to players getting inside with developer tools do not change that conclusion for me.

Developer clipping can prove that geometry or unfinished content exists behind a wall. It does not prove that the normal game currently contains the switch, quest state, code path, or door logic needed to reach it.

For an Early Access guide, that distinction is more important than showing what is behind the wall.

I would rather know that I can safely stop searching than spend another hour checking every flooded room because somebody entered 998 with a console command.

So for the 5.5 state represented here:

**Room 998 is discoverable as a clue. I would not call it normally accessible or solved.**

If a later patch adds a real door, keypad, objective, or state transition, that conclusion can change immediately. Until one of those appears, the Security feed is the end of the verified route.

<h2 id="other-99x-rooms">
  998 is part of a wider 99X trail
</h2>

The strange part is that 998 is not isolated.

By the end of the 5.5 investigation, the game was surfacing multiple **99X room references** rather than a single hidden Transposium room.

The set I could account for included:

- **Room 994**
- **Room 995**
- **Room 997**
- **Room 998**

They are not all presented as normal rooms inside one Transposium corridor.

For example, **Room 997** was associated with **Dock 37**, while a later Pico check associated **Room 994** with **Nomad's Tower**. Another 99X reference appeared elsewhere, and 998 was the one drawing attention through the Transposium investigation.

That changes how I interpret the mystery.

If these numbers belong to different locations, hunting for four adjacent doors numbered 994 through 998 inside Transposium is probably the wrong mental model. They behave more like distributed breadcrumbs tied to different facilities or Pico/security records.

It also explains why physically searching every Transposium door feels so unproductive.

The mystery is wider than one building.

What I do with those references right now is simple: record the number and location where the game exposes it, then move on. Until a normal interaction connects those entries into an actual quest or traversal chain, there is nothing gained by trying to brute-force a connection the game has not exposed.

<h2 id="security-camera-code">
  A camera code is only useful when the physical half of the puzzle exists
</h2>

One detail deserves special caution because it sounds exactly like the kind of secret The Last Caretaker would hide.

The suggested solution described a **three-digit code visible through a specific camera feed**, perhaps on a wall or whiteboard. I could inspect the cameras and search the corresponding rooms, so the first half of that theory was testable.

The failure came at the second half.

A code without a usable keypad is not a solution.

Even if a camera frame genuinely contains three digits, I still need the game to provide somewhere meaningful to enter them. Without that second interaction, writing down every number in every feed only creates more candidate combinations.

This is where Transposium can consume unlimited player time because almost every unusual object starts looking intentional:

- paintings;
- broken robots;
- numbered doors;
- flooded rooms;
- inactive machinery;
- wall markings;
- camera angles that do not move;
- old secret mechanisms from previous updates.

Some of those absolutely are deliberate environmental clues. That still does not make every clue part of a currently completable 998 puzzle.

I keep evidence and interpretation separate.

A visible number is evidence.

“A visible number opens Room 998” needs a working interaction before I treat it as fact.

<h2 id="old-transposium-secrets">
  Do not mix the older Transposium secrets with the new 99X trail
</h2>

Another source of wasted time is combining every strange thing ever added to Transposium into one giant puzzle.

The location already had older hidden mechanics, including the maze/tubing area and its hidden-button secret. Those can make the 5.5 camera material feel like the missing final layer of the same puzzle.

I have not found evidence that pressing the older hidden buttons suddenly opens Room 998.

Once an older secret has already produced its expected result, I leave it solved. Re-running it because 998 exists creates a loop where every old mechanism becomes a suspect again.

The new 5.5 trail has enough identifiable pieces of its own:

**Security → camera feeds → 99X references → inaccessible/closed states.**

That is the chain I follow.

Anything older needs a new state change before I bring it back into the investigation.

<h2 id="rising-tide">
  Rising Tide is where the current 5.5 progression actually moves forward
</h2>

There is one place where I stop investigating 998 and follow a different branch completely: **Rising Tide**.

The cartridge is available through the Pico system, and unlike the speculative Room 72 path, it produces a real progression result. Once its hidden data is exposed, the game can move into the Aaron Voss quest chain.

I keep that route separate because it has its own destination, objective, and completion state. If Rising Tide has already revealed Aaron's information, the next action belongs in the <Link href="/the-last-caretaker/memory-for-aaron-guide">Memory for Aaron guide</Link>, not in another circuit around Transposium.

That separation is useful when both mysteries are on screen at the same time.

Seeing 998 does not mean I need to solve 998 before leaving.

A real quest marker beats an unresolved room reference.

<h2 id="when-to-stop-searching">
  When I stop searching Transposium for 998
</h2>

I leave the 99X investigation alone once I have done the parts the current game lets me verify:

Security is working. I have cycled the relevant feeds. Room 998 has appeared as a system reference. I have compared the claimed Room 72 instructions with the physical rooms in my save. No usable Room 72 keypad or normal 998 entrance has appeared.

At that point another lap around the same corridors is not progress.

A later patch could add one tiny interaction and make the whole chain suddenly solvable, which is normal for an Early Access mystery. Until that happens, my save benefits more from moving on to a quest that already has a working objective.

The practical stopping rule is:

**If the game gives me new state, I follow it. If I am only repeating the same camera feeds and the same inaccessible doors, I leave.**

That keeps Transposium interesting without letting an unfinished secret consume the rest of the run.
