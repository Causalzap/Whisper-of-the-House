<h2 id="choose-the-human-first">
  Choose the profession before you build the human
</h2>

The easiest way to waste food and Memories is to start improving a pod before deciding what that human is supposed to become.

That barely matters for the first story human. Early progression only needs a viable person so the main quest can move forward, and learning how Lazarus works is more valuable than trying to produce a perfect specialist on the first attempt. If you are still recovering the Human Seed or trying to grow that first person, follow the <Link href="/the-last-caretaker/walkthrough">main walkthrough</Link> until the Human Growth system is online.

Council production is where the decision changes.

I open the committee screen first, find a role that is still empty, then track that profession in Human Growth before adding anything. On my later 5.5 save, four committee groups were still unfinished, so there was no reason to create another generic high-stat human and hope somebody needed them.

Once a profession is tracked, the growth screen tells me what actually matters. Weight may already be sufficient while Height is still red. A physically valid human may still sit at 81% because Creativity, Empathy, or Communication is missing.

That turns the pod from a guessing game into a build.

The sequence I use is:

1. choose the missing profession;
2. track it;
3. clear the physical requirements;
4. fill the missing mental traits;
5. check Growth Prediction again;
6. begin only when the intended role reaches 100%.

A high number in the wrong stat is not progress toward the role I need.

<h2 id="growth-system-ready">
  Get Lazarus support working before spending growth resources
</h2>

There are two layers to Human Growth, and mixing them together makes troubleshooting much harder.

The chamber itself needs functioning infrastructure. The human inside it also needs valid biological inputs.

Fresh water is one of the first hard dependencies. During the early Lazarus setup, getting water into the system did not immediately make the human viable; instead, the growth interface became useful enough to reveal what was still wrong.

The base screen showed:

- **Weight: 0 / 20 kg**
- **Height: 0 / 30 cm**
- **Life Expectancy: 0 / 10 years**

It also displayed an estimated **10.0 kWh** and **10,000 L** for the growth setup.

Those numbers are useful because they separate an infrastructure failure from a bad human configuration. If the chamber cannot maintain its basic support, there is little value in spending rare Memories trying to perfect the profession first.

Food production is the next piece.

The **Soil Forge** converts biomass into food materials, while the **Food Processor** turns those materials into usable growth inputs. Once both machines were supplied with fresh water, I could produce the nutritional components the pod needed instead of depending entirely on whatever had been scavenged earlier.

That is when fat, protein, carbohydrates, and the more specialized growth foods stop looking like random consumables. They become stat tools.

I keep enough raw material available to correct the current pod, but I do not manufacture enormous stacks in advance. The next profession may need a different physical balance, and inventory space disappears quickly when every growth food gets produced “just in case.”

<h2 id="physical-requirements">
  Fix Weight, Height, and Life Expectancy one gate at a time
</h2>

Human Growth gets much easier once the physical panel is treated as three separate problems rather than one overall quality score.

An early configuration had already reached **48 kg**, yet Height was still below requirement and Life Expectancy was only **23 years**. Adding more Weight at that point would have consumed resources without fixing the red gate.

Food effects also vary enough that the choice matters.

During the same progression I had options that could provide combinations such as:

- **+8 Height**
- **+2 Height and +8 Weight**
- **+3 Height, +8 Weight, plus Life Expectancy**

So the best food is not automatically the one with the largest total numbers. It is the one that closes the requirement currently blocking the human.

If Height is the only red stat, I compare foods by Height first. If Weight and Height are already safe but lifespan remains poor, then Life Expectancy becomes the useful value. Once all physical minimums are cleared, continuing to pump body stats only makes sense if the tracked profession or my own lifespan target gives me a reason.

<GuideCallout title="Fix the requirement that is still red">
A pod can be heavy enough and still fail Height. It can be tall enough and still fail Life Expectancy. Read the failed gate first, then choose the food that changes that number.
</GuideCallout>

This is also where I decide whether to keep working or leave Lazarus.

A missing food that I can manufacture locally is not a reason to sail away. A missing biomass supply is. Once the processors are working and the resource itself is gone, the next useful action has moved outside the growth room.

<h2 id="memories-and-traits">
  Memories are profession resources, not just collectibles
</h2>

Physical requirements only get the human into a viable body. Profession matching happens through the traits layered on top of it.

That is where the pile of books, toys, games, manuals, cameras, sports items, and other Memories collected during exploration suddenly becomes important.

The effects are concrete.

During the earlier collection pass I found examples such as a **Programming Manual with 10 Logic**, a teddy bear contributing Empathy, books carrying traits such as Logic, Adaptability, Creativity, Focus, or Patience, another Memory with Leadership and Discipline, and items that could cover Empathy and Communication. Even a basketball had a useful trait contribution.

That changes how I manage the storage boxes.

I no longer think of Memories as things to feed into the next human simply because I have them. Each one is part of a limited trait inventory that may solve a much more important profession later.

The tracked profession tells me what to search for.

If Creativity is missing, I search the Memory stockpile for Creativity. If Communication is still red, Communication becomes the filter. A strong Logic item can stay exactly where it is if the current profession does not ask for Logic.

This is where exploration starts feeding directly into the Council system. A new Memory is not merely completion progress; it can unlock another viable profession combination.

The route to 105 years was gradual. Life Expectancy had been sitting in the 60s before repeated improvements moved it through **73 → 81 → 89 → 97 → 105 years**.

That is more useful than chasing a universal “best human” number. Another profession can want a very different combination of physical and mental traits, so copying the exact Visual Technician setup into every pod wastes resources.

A later human reached **140 years** while I was configuring another role. That did not make 140 the new minimum. It only proved that lifespan can be pushed much farther when I have the food and see a reason to spend it.

For profession work, the reliable endpoint is the percentage:

**100% means I have solved the job I came to solve.**

Anything beyond that is optional optimization.

<h2 id="council-professions">
  Use the Council as the production queue
</h2>

Once Human Growth is reliable, the hardest question changes.

I no longer ask whether I can make a human. I ask which profession deserves the next pod.

The Council screen answers that immediately.

In this save, several committees were already **4/4**, while four groups still sat at **0/4**. The selected **Culture & Memory** committee specifically required:

- Visual Technician
- Sculptor
- Cultural Archivist
- Manual Holder

That is a much better production list than “make four good humans.”

It also changes how I spend Memories.

A Visual Technician may use Creativity, Empathy, and Communication items that another Culture & Memory profession could also need. If I burn the strongest copies on unnecessary over-optimization, the next pod may become harder for no progression benefit.

So after every completed human, I return to the Council screen before starting another.

The next empty slot determines the next profession. The next profession determines the next trait search. Only then do I decide which food and Memories can safely be spent.

The Council system is also still developing in the 5.5 state represented here. Not every committee has a full quest chain attached to it yet, so there is limited value in rushing to fill every possible slot purely for completion.

Roles tied to something I am actively pursuing come first.

<h2 id="when-to-stop-optimizing">
  Stop optimizing when another input no longer changes the job
</h2>

Human Growth makes it very easy to spend resources simply because another number can still rise.

Life Expectancy is the clearest example. Moving a human from 65 to 105 years feels meaningful because the number keeps responding. Height, Strength, and Intellect can tempt the same behavior.

The important question is whether that additional input changes what the human can do.

For a profession human, my comfortable finish line is:

- the minimum physical gates are satisfied;
- all required profession traits are present;
- Growth Prediction shows the intended profession at **100%**;
- Life Expectancy is high enough that I am satisfied with the result.

After that, every extra item has an opportunity cost.

A Memory spent adding another trait to a finished Visual Technician could be the exact Memory needed by a Sculptor. A food item used to push an already-safe Height value farther may be the cleanest answer to the next pod's physical requirement.

That matters more as the Council fills up.

Early on, almost every useful growth input feels abundant. Several humans later, the uncommon trait combinations become much more valuable than another few centimeters or another eight years on somebody who was already complete.

<h2 id="missing-profession-trait">
  Missing a profession trait can turn exploration into the next objective
</h2>

Some Human Growth problems cannot be solved inside Lazarus.

Food shortages usually have a production answer. Trait shortages may not.

During later profession work, I reached builds where the remaining characteristic depended on a Memory I had never collected. The map still contained locations I had not visited, which meant another pass through the Food Processor was irrelevant.

That gives me a simple way to decide when to leave:

**Missing physical stat:** check food.

**Missing Life Expectancy:** check the available growth inputs.

**Missing profession trait with no matching Memory in storage:** go exploring.

The goal is not to collect every Memory before creating any humans. That would delay progression for no reason.

I collect enough to solve the profession currently in front of me.

When a later role exposes a new trait gap, that gap gives the next exploration trip a purpose. A location that once looked optional may suddenly contain exactly the Creativity, Communication, Wisdom, Patience, or Empathy source the Council needs.

<h2 id="human-growth-stuck">
  Human Growth stuck? Diagnose the layer that failed
</h2>

**Begin is disabled and the minimum requirements are red**

Read Weight, Height, and Life Expectancy separately. The base growth screen requires at least **20 kg**, **30 cm**, and **10 years** before those three checks clear.

**The chamber has water, but the human still will not grow**

Water only solves the infrastructure layer. The biological requirements and growth inputs still need to be valid.

**Height is the only physical requirement still failing**

Compare the food effects instead of adding whatever has the biggest Weight number. During the early run, available foods included +8 Height and mixed Height/Weight options.

**I cannot produce the food that fixes the stat**

Make sure the Soil Forge and Food Processor are operating with fresh water and that there is enough biomass or other source material available.

**The physical requirements are green but the profession is below 100%**

Stop adding body stats and read Growth Prediction. The unresolved profession traits are the next problem.

**Visual Technician is stuck around 81%**

In the setup shown here, Creativity, Empathy, and Communication were still missing at 81%. Matching Memories moved the build toward the final profession state.

**I have the right Memory but do not want to waste it**

Check the Council queue first. If another unfinished profession needs that trait more urgently, use a weaker valid combination or postpone the current pod until another Memory is found.

**The profession is already 100%. Should I keep increasing Life Expectancy?**

Only if the extra lifespan matters to you. The profession is already solved; additional growth is optimization, not another hidden requirement.

**I cannot find the trait the profession asks for**

That usually means the next progression step is exploration rather than more growth-room work. Search unexplored locations and stored Memories for that characteristic.

<h2 id="after-growth">
  Verify the finished profession, then choose the next job
</h2>

When the chamber finishes, I verify the resulting profession before transporting the human or committing another pod.

The Visual Technician configuration produced the intended Visual Technician, which is the confirmation I want before considering that Council slot solved. The same rule applies to Sculptor, Cultural Archivist, Manual Holder, or another specialist: check the result first, then move on.

The first story human follows a different path. Once that human exists, the main progression points away from Lazarus and toward Exodus, so there is no reason to keep polishing their stats just because the chamber can.

Council humans stay part of a production loop:

**check empty role → track profession → clear physical gates → add the required Memories → reach 100% → grow → verify → check the next role.**

Once I started treating Human Growth this way, the system stopped feeling like experimentation.

Food solves the body. Memories solve the profession. The Council tells me which profession is worth spending those resources on.
