<h2 id="reach-pishon">
  Reach Pishon, then decide whether this is a scout run or a build run
</h2>

The first Project Eden trip gets much easier once I stop treating Pishon like a normal location that needs to be cleared in one visit. The marker only gets me into the right stretch of ocean. What matters is finding the actual construction platform, checking where **Eden Frame - Small 1** belongs, and seeing what the current build wants from me.

I approach slowly enough that I can identify the platform without immediately jumping into the water. Rain and fog can hide its shape surprisingly well, and once I am swimming beside the structure it becomes harder to judge which surface the frame is supposed to use.

The build prompt decides what happens next.

If the boat already carries a useful amount of Stainless Steel and the requested support parts, I stay. If the frame reveals a large material shortage, I take that information back to the Fabricator instead of scattering half a project around Pishon.

That first visit only needs to answer a few practical questions:

1. Where does the first frame sit?
2. How much Stainless Steel does the current build require?
3. Which smaller components are actually missing?
4. Can the boat support a full construction trip without running short on power or cargo space?

Once I know those answers, the next loadout stops being guesswork.


<h2 id="stainless-steel">
  Stainless Steel is the first real Project Eden resource wall
</h2>

Project Eden starts consuming serious cargo space when Stainless Steel enters the picture.

The Fabricator recipe is:

- **1 Iron**
- **10L oil**
- for **1 Stainless Steel**

The Iron requirement is obvious because I can see the stack disappearing. Oil is easier to miss because the same reserve may also be supporting fuel production and other ship systems.

A frame requirement of **50 Stainless Steel** translates into **50 Iron and 500L of oil** just for that steel. The Farming Platform adds another **8 Stainless Steel**, which pushes those two known steel costs to **58 Iron and 580L of oil** before Connection Ports, pumps, tanks, generation, or other construction parts are counted.

That number changes how I prepare the trip.

If the frame wants fifty and the boat has twelve, I do not carry the twelve back to Pishon just to watch an unfinished structure sit there. I stay at the Fabricator long enough to make the quantity useful, free enough storage to move it comfortably, then sail once with a load that can actually advance the build.

<GuideCallout title="When I turn back for materials">
A large Stainless Steel shortage ends the Pishon trip for me. One deliberate 500L-oil crafting session is faster than several journeys caused by discovering the same shortage piece by piece.
</GuideCallout>

Project Eden does not need an Oil Whale to start. If Stainless Steel production eventually turns oil into the real bottleneck, that is when the <Link href="/the-last-caretaker/oil-whale-guide">Oil Whale route</Link> becomes relevant. Until then, adding a second logistics project usually slows Eden down rather than helping it.

  Place the Eden Frame first, then let its prompt decide the rest of the cargo

I bring the boat close enough that waves and swimming are not fighting the build interface, then work around the intended construction surface. When the ghost refuses to validate, clicking harder does nothing useful.

Floating crates, loose panels, platform edges, support geometry, and even slight boat movement can interfere with placement. My fastest recovery has been to stop, look at what the ghost is clipping, clear loose objects, and approach the platform from another side.

Once the ghost turns valid, I check the material panel before committing.

Besides Stainless Steel, the first frame can call for parts such as **Connection Ports**, **Compression Pump components**, and **Gas Tank or storage components**. Those are the items I prefer to verify at Pishon before mass-producing them.

There is no advantage in arriving with ten pumps when the missing item was a Connection Port.

This is one of the rare places where a light scouting trip can save more time than a perfectly prepared first trip. Seeing the actual frame requirements converts “bring construction stuff” into an exact list.

I also leave some inventory capacity unused before returning. Project Eden involves heavy components, repeated movement between the boat and the platform, and enough construction pieces that starting the trip already overloaded makes every correction slower.

<h2 id="farming-platform">
  The Farming Platform changes Project Eden from construction to life support
</h2>

The completed frame looks like major progress, but **Eden Frame - Small 1 is not the end of First Roots**.

The next important piece is the **Farming Platform**, which attaches to the frame walkway. It is not meant to be placed independently beside the structure.

The known build requires **8 Stainless Steel**. At the normal recipe, that is another **8 Iron and 80L of oil**.

Placement is more likely to waste time than the materials.

I walk along the completed frame and watch for the correct snap point. When the Farming Platform ghost floats near the structure without locking in, I stay close to the walkway and change my position gradually. Trying to move it farther out into open space only takes me away from the connection it needs.

Once the Farming Platform snaps into place, the nature of the job changes completely.

The remaining question is no longer:

> Do I have enough metal?

It becomes:

> Is this structure actually capable of supporting growth?

That is a much better point for the second article ad, because the player has finished one complete phase of the build before entering the water-and-power phase.

<h2 id="support-first-roots">
  Get fresh water and power working before blaming the soil or seeds
</h2>

Fresh water is the connection I verify first because a purifier sitting nearby can look finished even when the frame is receiving nothing.

The useful route starts at the **Water Pump on the lower part of the Eden Frame**. I connect a **Water Purifier** into that system so freshwater reaches the frame itself. Once that connection is working, the Farming Platform can receive water automatically instead of depending on repeated manual watering.

If the platform still behaves as though it is dry, I follow the route from the lower Water Pump back through the purifier instead of rebuilding the Farming Platform. The important question is whether freshwater is entering the frame network, not whether the water machine exists somewhere on Pishon.

Electricity is easier to prove. A wind turbine or solar setup works as long as power is actually reaching the frame socket and the connected equipment stays alive.

For the first Eden build, I care more about stable generation than elegant cable work. A temporary clean connection is enough. If the boat itself already has a tangled electrical network, I solve that separately through the <Link href="/the-last-caretaker/power-fuel-wall-outlets">Power, Fuel & Wall Outlets Guide</Link> rather than turning Pishon into a second ship-rewiring project.

Only after water and power are confirmed do I load **soil** and then the required **seeds**.

That order matters because planting into an unsupported platform makes several possible failures look identical. With freshwater and electricity already proven, a stalled growth state has far fewer causes left.

I also stay at Pishon after the last input goes in.

A Farming Platform existing on the frame does not mean First Roots has progressed. I want to see the growing system accept its resources and actually begin working before putting the boat in reverse.

<h2 id="first-roots-stuck">
  First Roots stuck? The symptom tells you which stage failed
</h2>

Most Project Eden failures become much easier once I stop treating them as one generic “First Roots is broken” problem.

**The Eden Frame ghost never turns valid**

This happens before materials matter. Clear debris around the platform, stabilize your position, check whether the ghost is clipping the structure, and approach the intended build surface from another side. A larger Stainless Steel stack cannot solve invalid placement.

**The frame is in position but construction will not complete**

Read the missing-material panel again. Stainless Steel is the big number, but one Connection Port, pump part, storage component, or other requested piece can hold the entire frame at an unfinished state.

**The Farming Platform refuses to snap**

Stay on the completed frame and search its walkway. The Farming Platform attaches to the frame; it is not a free-standing agricultural machine.

**The platform is finished but nothing is growing**

Work through the support chain in order:

**freshwater → power → soil → seeds**

I fix the first missing stage I can prove instead of dismantling the completed platform.

**A Water Purifier is running, but the platform still appears dry**

Trace the freshwater connection into the lower frame Water Pump. The purifier and frame need an actual route between them; physical proximity does not supply water.

**Everything looks connected, but First Roots still has not advanced**

Wait long enough to see the system begin operating. Leaving immediately after the final input can make a normal transition look like a failed quest.

That sequence catches nearly every early Pishon problem without forcing me to rebuild work that was already correct.

<h2 id="when-to-leave-pishon">
  Leave Pishon when the next trip has a specific job
</h2>

I abandon an unfinished First Roots attempt for two reasons: the structure has exposed a material shortage I cannot solve locally, or the boat no longer has enough reserve to support another round of work safely.

Everything else is usually cheaper to diagnose while I am standing beside the frame.

Once freshwater, power, soil, and seeds are active, I stop unloading unrelated cargo. Project Jonah, Theta 9, distant Samples, and other New Horizons objectives all compete for the same storage space and attention, but none of them makes First Roots grow faster.

After the objective progresses, I collect unused construction material and leave the working support infrastructure alone. Then I wait for the next Eden requirement before filling the boat with another batch of specialized components.

Project Eden gets expensive when I try to predict several stages ahead. It stays manageable when every trip has one clear purpose: arrive with the parts the current structure has already asked for, solve that stage completely, and sail again only when Pishon gives me a new reason.
