<h2 id="when-oil-whale-is-worth-it">
  When is the Oil Whale worth using?
</h2>

A full Whale charge is a serious commitment. I plan around **100kW to fill it**, and a successful dive brings back about **10,000L of oil**. Those two numbers decide whether I use the Whale at all.

If my boat is already running close to empty batteries, I leave the Whale alone. Ten thousand liters of oil is not useful enough to justify arriving at the next stop with no electrical reserve for tools, pumps, lights, charging, or another mistake.

The calculation changes once oil itself becomes the bottleneck. Project Eden construction, fuel production, and a boat carrying more permanent machinery can consume enough resources that a prepared 10,000L pickup saves a longer supply trip. At that point I want three things before heading out: room in my oil storage, a healthy electrical bank, and a route across the deck that will still be usable once the Whale is connected.

A refinery or fixed oil source still wins when it is nearby and I want a predictable refill with minimal setup. The Whale wins when I can afford the charge and would rather bring a large oil source into my current route than detour back to infrastructure I have already left behind.

<GuideCallout title="My go / no-go check">
If charging the Whale would leave the boat unable to handle another normal stop, I postpone it. If power is comfortable and I have somewhere for 10,000L to go, the trip starts making sense.
</GuideCallout>


<h2 id="start-project-jonah">
  Start with Project Jonah and bring a grown human
</h2>

The Whale is not something I search for at random before the quest chain is ready. The practical route runs through **Project Jonah** around **Omega Nursery / Platform Omega**, and the important cargo is a **grown human**, not a Human Seed.

If you are still working toward your first Human, finish that progression first in the <Link href="/the-last-caretaker/walkthrough">main walkthrough</Link>. Lazarus needs to be far enough along that you can produce a usable Human and physically transport them.

I use a duplicate or a Human I am not relying on for an immediate profession or Council plan. Committing an important Human to another location and then discovering I need them elsewhere is an avoidable detour.

Around Omega, the shark-related route is much more useful than circling open water looking for the silhouette of a Whale. Once Project Jonah has progressed far enough, I save before leaving the Human and before doing the first Whale setup. Early Access behavior around spawned Whales is inconsistent enough that a save here is cheap insurance.

<h2 id="dock-and-charge-whale">
  Park beside the Whale before you connect power
</h2>

The easiest Whale setup I have used starts with the boat alongside the body rather than nose-first into it. Slow down before the final approach, get close enough to work the side interaction points, then stop fighting the helm.

The Whale has usable points along its sides, and there is Whale-side interaction involved in starting the process. When everything appears connected but nothing happens, I check the Whale itself before tearing apart the boat grid.

Here the Whale-specific requirement is simpler: the connection must stay live long enough to charge the Whale and survive the transfer back.

<h2 id="oil-whale-power">
  Budget 100kW for the charge, then keep a reserve
</h2>

The Whale can absorb **100kW when filled**, so I treat that charge as an expense rather than background power consumption.

A boat showing 100kW available is not automatically ready. If that 100kW is effectively the entire bank, spending it all leaves nothing for the systems around the Whale. I would rather arrive with more capacity than the Whale itself requires or have generation already restoring the bank while the charge is happening.

This is where oversized electrical storage starts to feel useful. It is not because the Whale needs a complicated special grid. It is because a large one-time draw exposes a boat that normally survives on a narrow margin.

Before charging I look at the whole situation rather than one battery display: free oil capacity, current generation, essential loads, and how much power remains after the Whale is full. Nonessential machines can stay off until the oil is back on board.

<h2 id="oil-whale-oil-storage">
  One dive returns about 10,000L — plan the storage before it surfaces
</h2>

The number I use for a normal Whale run is **about 10,000L of oil per dive**.

That sounds straightforward until the Whale returns and the receiving tanks are already full. I decide where the oil is going before I start the dive, not while 10,000 liters are waiting beside the boat.

Permanent working storage still makes the most sense on the boat for me. Anything stored there leaves with me, stays connected to the rest of the ship, and does not require another transfer just to become useful.

Update 5.5 makes Whale-side infrastructure more interesting, though. Building directly on the Whale now works for equipment that previously could not be placed there. A medium battery and local generation were both successfully built on the Whale platform, which opens up cleaner remote setups instead of forcing every component back onto the ship.

The same change makes extra oil buffering practical.

In the 5.5 setup I tested against, added storage reached roughly **20,000L**. That does not turn one dive into a 20,000L haul; the Whale still returns about 10,000L. What changes is how much can remain buffered across repeated cycles.

That distinction matters when deciding where to spend materials. For a one-off refill, building a miniature oil base on the Whale is unnecessary. If I expect to reuse the same Whale repeatedly, local battery, generation, and storage become much easier to justify.

<h2 id="transfer-oil-to-boat">
  Wait for the oil to reach the boat before you disconnect
</h2>

A surfaced Whale is not the finish line. I leave only after the recovered oil is visible inside the storage I intend to travel with.

A working transfer can move oil at roughly **70,000L per hour**, so 10,000L should not become an all-night operation. When the number barely moves, the first suspects are the connection and destination capacity rather than the Whale itself.

I check four things in that order:

1. **Is the Whale still close enough for the connection to stay live?**
2. **Does the oil route actually point toward the receiving tank?**
3. **Is that tank able to accept more oil?**
4. **Has a cable or socket silently disconnected while the boat moved?**

The rate is useful as a diagnosis. If I am nowhere near the expected transfer speed, I fix the physical route instead of simply waiting longer.

Once the oil is on the boat, I disconnect deliberately. Pulling away first and hoping every cable sorts itself out is a good way to leave part of the setup hanging behind me.


<h2 id="three-oil-whales">
  There are three Oil Whales, but they do not get permanent map markers
</h2>

The awkward part of the Whale system is not the number of Whales. There are **three** available in a save. The awkward part is finding them again.

They do not behave like ordinary locations with a convenient permanent marker. Once I have found a Whale I expect to reuse, I remember the nearby named location rather than relying on the world map to save me.

In one developed save, Whales had been left around **Exodus** and **Fuel Station Gamma**, which made those landmarks much more useful than trying to memorize open-ocean coordinates.

If one of the expected Whales refuses to show up, I do not immediately assume the save only has two. A working recovery in 5.5 has been:

1. save the game;
2. return to the main menu;
3. load the save again;
4. check the Whale area once more.

One missing Whale appeared after that reload. I treat this as an Early Access workaround, not a guaranteed spawn rule, but it is worth trying before spending another hour searching empty water.

<h2 id="move-oil-whale">
  You can move an Oil Whale with the gyrocopter cable
</h2>

Moving a Whale is possible, and Update 5.5 is exactly why I would no longer tell players that towing simply does not work.

The useful trick is not the normal mooring cable.

Back the ship toward the Whale, use the **gyrocopter as the cable link**, attach one end around the rear of the ship and the other end to the **Whale's nose**, then drive the ship forward. The gyrocopter swings around violently while the cable loads up, but the Whale does move.

The movement comes in stages rather than as a clean tow. I pull until the cable reaches its stretch, let the Whale creep forward, then repeat. Dropping the ship anchor can also let the retracting gyrocopter cable draw the Whale closer before the next pull.

There is an important catch: **the new position is not completely dependable after another dive or reload cycle**.

A Whale previously moved near Exodus stayed there for a long time, while another 5.5 test showed a moved Whale appearing back toward its earlier position after a later cycle. That makes towing useful for convenience, but not something I would build an essential progression plan around.

My threshold is simple. Moving a Whale a short distance toward a base I use constantly can pay off. Dragging one across a large part of the map just because it is technically possible is a very slow way to save future travel time.

<h2 id="oil-whale-wont-work">
  If the Oil Whale is not working, match the fix to the failure
</h2>

Different Whale problems look similar when the result is simply “no oil,” but they are not the same failure.

**No Whale appears near the Project Jonah route**

First make sure the quest state is correct and that a grown Human was used where the route expects one. If the route is correct and the Whale still appears missing, save, reload from the main menu, and check again. The absence of a map marker makes this problem look worse than it is.

**The Whale is beside the boat but will not start**

Check the Whale-side interaction and available electrical charge. A cable touching the Whale does not mean the Whale is ready for the dive.

**The Whale dives but returns less useful oil than expected**

A normal target is around 10,000L. Make sure the receiving storage was not already occupied and that added Whale-side storage is not holding part of the oil before it reaches the boat.

**The transfer starts and then stops**

Look for a drifting boat, broken cable range, a wrong routing direction, or a full receiving tank. A healthy transfer is fast enough that a nearly stationary number is a routing problem.

**A moved Whale is no longer where I parked it**

Check its previous/original area before assuming it has vanished. Towing works, but position persistence after later Whale cycles has not been perfectly consistent in 5.5.

<h2 id="oil-whale-or-refinery">
  Whale or refinery: I choose based on the next hour, not the maximum yield
</h2>

A Whale is at its best when I already have the electrical reserve, I want roughly 10,000L now, and its location fits the route I am running. Building local storage can make repeated dives more efficient, and moving one closer to a frequently used base can eventually cut travel time.

A refinery is less dramatic but often easier to live with. When batteries are marginal, the boat is already carrying another project's materials, or I only need enough oil to finish one small job, I would rather use the predictable source than create a second logistics problem.

The Whale becomes especially attractive later, when 100kW is a manageable charge instead of most of the boat's stored electricity. At that point the bottleneck shifts away from power and toward how quickly I can store, transfer, and use the 10,000L it brings back.

That is the point where the Oil Whale stops feeling like an expensive novelty and starts functioning as infrastructure.
