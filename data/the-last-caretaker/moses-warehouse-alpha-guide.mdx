<h2 id="where-moses-starts">
  Where does the MOSES Field Reserve Program start?
</h2>

The confusing part of MOSES is that the reward can appear before the route is obvious. I could already see the new storage equipment in Skills, but the build options were still locked behind **MOSES Field Reserve Program**, and there was no useful reason to stand on the boat opening the map repeatedly waiting for a new marker to solve it for me.

The route that moved the quest forward was **Central Warehouse Alpha**.

That distinction matters if you are returning on an established save. Warehouse Alpha may already look familiar because it existed before the Deck Drop content, so it is easy to dismiss it as a location you have already cleared. Update 5.5 adds a new field-logistics route inside the existing site rather than giving you an entirely separate warehouse to discover.

I sail there with enough room in my inventory to move through a large industrial interior, but I do not arrive prepared to strip the whole place. MOSES needs exploration and a specific design recovery; turning the first visit into a full salvage operation only makes the actual objective harder to track.

The reward is worth prioritizing once storage has become a real limitation. The quest is the gate for the new **portable barrel and bulk-storage family**, including the larger battery and petrol/diesel storage options. Their current capacities, network behavior, Fill / Equalize problem, and where I actually build them belong in the <Link href="/the-last-caretaker/power-fuel-wall-outlets">Power, Fuel & Wall Outlets Guide</Link>.

<h2 id="prepare-warehouse-alpha">
  Bring enough power and ammo to stay inside Warehouse Alpha
</h2>

Warehouse Alpha is not a difficult combat destination in the same way as a dedicated deep-station run, but it is large enough to punish a weak return setup.

On the save I used for this route, bringing much of the existing warehouse electrical system back online showed roughly **21 kW of draw**. That number will not be identical on every save — an already-scrapped Warehouse Alpha can have a different active load — but it was enough to make me stop treating site power as free background electricity.

The character's own energy became the more immediate problem later. After pushing through the production section for a while, I had to leave, recharge at the boat, and come back. That trip would have been much more annoying without a clear exit route.

Ammo is similar. The first new rooms can be surprisingly quiet, then the route starts introducing spiders and armed warehouse enemies deeper inside. I bring a normal combat loadout that can clear several enclosed rooms rather than an oversized boss kit. There is no reason to fill half the inventory with explosives when that same space becomes useful for parts and salvage on the way out.

What matters before I leave the deck is:

- enough energy reserve to explore without immediately retreating;
- a weapon and ammo for spiders and warehouse security enemies;
- free inventory rather than a preloaded salvage haul;
- a recent save before disturbing the new section.

The warehouse contains so much material that arriving almost full is backwards. There will be more than enough temptation once you are inside.

<h2 id="find-field-logistics-facility">
  Find the old field-logistics facility inside Warehouse Alpha
</h2>

The breakthrough came while working through the lower Warehouse Alpha interior near the older garage and stair areas. Once I reached the added section, the AI finally gave the line that matters: this was an **old field logistics facility**, and **MOSES tested new storage prototypes here**.

The objective then changed to finding the **production area**.

From that point forward I stop spending much time on old warehouse clutter. If the objective says to find the production area, another pile of scrap beside the forklift is not progression.

The added logistics section also has its own navigation quirks. A hatch can be opened from inside that reconnects toward the familiar **basketball-hoop area**, giving the route a much cleaner way back out. I make a mental note of that shortcut immediately because the production area goes far enough into Warehouse Alpha that retracing the entire entrance every time wastes energy.

There is another practical limitation: the new section is not designed around driving the forklift all the way through it. Warehouse Alpha may encourage forklift thinking in the older storage space, but once the MOSES route narrows down, I plan around carrying the important objective items and using the available exits rather than expecting the Donk to solve every hauling problem.

<h2 id="reach-production-area">
  The production area gets more dangerous after the quiet first rooms
</h2>

The first part of the MOSES section can feel almost suspiciously empty. That is not a signal that you have gone the wrong way.

I continue through the industrial rooms and keep an eye on the equipment rather than immediately grinding everything down. The storage prototypes start becoming visually obvious: larger tanks, portable containers, dispensers, racks, and test equipment are mixed into a part of Warehouse Alpha that clearly exists for storage development rather than ordinary cargo handling.

The quiet does not last forever. Spiders start appearing, and some rooms use warehouse security enemies that can lock the space into a fight when you cross the sensor. Clearing them before hunting for the quest interaction keeps the room readable; otherwise every terminal, wall board, and side opening becomes harder to inspect while something is firing across the shelves.

There is no special reason to destroy every security unit completely if it has already stopped being a threat. The objective is still the MOSES design, not a warehouse extermination counter.

This is also the point where I resist the urge to take apart every prototype rack I see. The next quest step depends on recovering a specification, and scrapping the room before finding that interaction adds visual noise without bringing the unlock any closer.

<h2 id="find-moses-blueprint">
  Do not scan the big tanks — find the prototype specification
</h2>

This was the real MOSES card for me.

The production area is full of exactly the objects I want to build, so the natural reaction is to point the Replica Scanner at the **Bulk Battery, tanks, dispensers, or other large prototypes** and expect one of them to become a blueprint.

That is not what moved the quest.

The large hardware itself can return no valid scan target. Once that happened, I stopped testing every tank and started searching the production rooms for the **prototype blueprint / container specification** instead.

The quest language is the clue. When it asks for the prototype blueprint, it wants the design record associated with the storage project, not a replica scan of the finished physical tank.

I search the room differently at that point: wall boards, specification displays, work areas, upper sections, and anything that looks like documentation becomes more important than another piece of machinery sitting on the floor.

That shift saves a lot of pointless scanner use.

<GuideCallout title="Scanner says no target?">
If the big battery or fuel tank refuses to scan, leave the prototype alone. Search the production area for the design/specification interaction instead. The MOSES unlock comes from recovering the storage design, not from repeatedly scanning the finished hardware.
</GuideCallout>

<h2 id="unlock-moses-storage">
  “The designs are available” is the confirmation you want
</h2>

The clean completion signal comes when the system reports that the **container specifications have been recovered** and **the designs are available**.

That message is more useful than judging success from the prototype models around the room. Later, the quest state also reflected that the storage options could now be unlocked and additional skill points had been awarded.

At that stage I stop searching the production floor for a second copy of the same blueprint.

The important distinction is:

**Warehouse Alpha unlocks the designs. The Power / Fuel systems decide whether those designs are worth building.**

A newly available Bulk Battery is not automatically an instruction to tear apart a working Medium Battery bank. The same applies to the larger fuel tanks and portable barrel equipment. Unlocking them gives me options; it does not make the old equipment obsolete.

That separation is especially useful on an established save. I complete MOSES as soon as I am already capable of handling Warehouse Alpha, then decide on the actual boat rebuild later when there is a storage or routing problem worth solving.

<h2 id="warehouse-alpha-shortcut">
  Open the shortcut before hauling anything heavy out
</h2>

The new interior has a much better return route than walking the entire warehouse backward.

A hatch / small exit route can be opened from inside, reconnecting toward the familiar exterior area near the basketball court and boat approach. Once I find it, that becomes my preferred recharge and unloading path.

Warehouse Alpha is dense enough that the character can become overloaded long before the building is remotely empty. Once movement starts slowing, the shortcut lets me drop material at the boat, recharge, and return without losing track of the MOSES rooms.

Trying to move everything in one pass creates the worst version of the route: slow movement, low remaining energy, an inventory full of generic materials, and the actual blueprint still somewhere ahead.

<h2 id="loot-warehouse-alpha">
  Finish MOSES before turning Warehouse Alpha into a salvage project
</h2>

Central Warehouse Alpha has enough material to become its own project.

On this developed save, so much of the location was still intact that a full strip-down was estimated at around **two hours** of dedicated clearing. More importantly, clearing the warehouse itself was not moving MOSES progression forward.

That makes the priority easy.

On the first MOSES run I take high-value items that are directly in the way, memories or obviously useful pieces I would regret leaving behind, and whatever I can carry without affecting movement. The shelves, containers, loose industrial parts, and generic scrap can survive until after the storage design is unlocked.

The quest gives Warehouse Alpha a useful dividing line:

**before the specification:** navigation matters more than salvage.

**after the specification:** the building becomes a resource reserve I can clean out whenever I actually need those materials.

There is no bonus for finishing both jobs in the same visit.

A player who arrives short on common crafting material may reasonably spend longer salvaging. On a mature save that already has crates full of resources, grinding down every rack before finding the blueprint is mostly trading time for materials that are already abundant.

<h2 id="moses-stuck">
  MOSES stuck? Check which part of the route has actually stopped
</h2>

**The new storage skills are visible but still locked**

That is expected before MOSES. Go to Central Warehouse Alpha and look for the added field-logistics route rather than trying to unlock the equipment from the Skills screen alone.

**Warehouse Alpha looks exactly like the old location**

Move through the existing warehouse instead of stopping at the familiar first rooms. The MOSES objective appears when the route reaches the field-logistics section where the AI identifies the storage prototype facility.

**The objective says “find the production area”**

Continue through the newly opened industrial section. The added storage equipment, tank dispensers, prototype hardware, and later enemy rooms are signs that you are following the correct branch.

**A large tank or Bulk Battery says it cannot be scanned**

Stop using the physical prototype as the target. Search the production rooms for the prototype blueprint / container specification interaction.

**The production area seems empty**

The first added rooms can be quiet. Push farther before assuming nothing has spawned; later sections introduce spiders and warehouse security encounters.

**I keep running out of energy before finding the design**

Use the inside-opened exit as a shuttle route back to the boat. Recharge and return rather than trying to finish the whole warehouse on a depleted reserve.

**I recovered the specification but cannot tell if it worked**

Look for the confirmation that the **container specifications were recovered** and **the designs are available**. The later quest state should then allow the new storage family to be unlocked.

**I finished the quest but the new storage behaves strangely**

That is no longer a MOSES progression problem. Bulk storage network behavior, current capacities, and Fill / Equalize belong in the <Link href="/the-last-caretaker/power-fuel-wall-outlets">Power, Fuel & Wall Outlets Guide</Link>.

<h2 id="leave-warehouse-alpha">
  Leave once the designs are unlocked and the shortcut is usable
</h2>

MOSES does not require Central Warehouse Alpha to become an empty shell.

My completion point is much earlier: the field-logistics area has been found, the production route is open, the container specification has been recovered, the storage designs are available, and I know the short route back out.

Anything left after that is optional value.

If the character still has comfortable inventory capacity, I take another pass through nearby rooms. If I am already slow, low on energy, or carrying material I have no immediate use for, the boat wins. Warehouse Alpha is one of the easiest places to convince myself that one more shelf is worth another ten minutes.

The better reward is already unlocked.

I leave with MOSES complete, build only the storage equipment that solves a real problem on the current save, and keep the rest of Warehouse Alpha as a resource cache for the trip when those materials actually matter.
