<h2 id="fix-the-bottleneck">
  Fix the Failing Network Before Rebuilding Your Boat
</h2>

A boat with cables running across the deck is not necessarily a boat with an electrical problem. If the batteries recover after heavy use, the engines receive their fuel, and every important machine starts reliably, there is little reason to dismantle a working network just to make it look cleaner.

The point where I start changing the layout is when the existing connections make a real problem difficult to solve. A machine may be losing power despite nearby charged batteries, a refinery may not be receiving the correct fuel, or another piece of equipment may have been placed beyond the range of the nearest outlet.

These problems require different repairs.

- **A machine loses electricity:** Follow its power input back toward generation or charged storage.
- **Fuel is available but does not reach the machine:** Check the fluid type, connection direction, and receiving port.
- **A cable cannot reach its destination:** Move the connection point before adding several extenders.
- **A larger battery stays partially charged:** Check how it is connected to smaller batteries and whether the network uses Fill or Equalize.
- **Several machines fail after changing one connection:** Restore the previous working route before rebuilding anything else.

The ship becomes more complicated after the larger New Horizons expeditions and the additional equipment introduced in Deck Drop. More batteries, tanks, generators, and resource processors may all share limited space.

I keep the existing installation running while changing one section. Replacing several unrelated connections at once makes it much harder to identify what caused a new failure.

<h2 id="what-wall-outlets-do">
  What Power, Fuel, and Gas Wall Outlets Actually Do
</h2>

Buildable wall outlets allow you to position connections where machines need them instead of relying entirely on the fixed sockets already installed on the ship.

They are especially useful when several machines occupy one section of the deck or when a connection must pass between floors.

The main electrical and resource-routing options include:

- **Power Wall Outlets:** Organize electrical connections between generation, batteries, and powered equipment.
- **Fuel Wall Outlets:** Provide connection points for liquid fuel networks, including oil, petrol, and diesel.
- **Gas Wall Outlets:** Help route gases such as oxygen and methane.
- **Electric Cable Extenders:** Bridge distances that a direct electrical connection cannot cover.
- **Grid Scan:** Helps identify electrical network problems when the physical cables appear connected.

An outlet does not generate electricity, manufacture fuel, or move resources without the required network behind it. It still needs an appropriate source and a usable connection to the destination.

For example, placing a power outlet near a Fabricator does not make the Fabricator operational unless that outlet is connected to a functioning electrical supply.

Gas and fuel connections also remain separate. A gas route that carries oxygen or methane should not be treated as interchangeable with a liquid fuel route simply because both use hoses and wall-mounted connectors.

<h2 id="build-from-the-load">
  Build Connections from the Machine Back Toward the Source
</h2>

When replacing a long electrical connection, I start at the machine that needs power rather than moving generators and batteries first.

Suppose a machine on the rear deck receives electricity through a cable that crosses several walkways. Instead of disconnecting the original route immediately, place a power outlet closer to the machine and establish a new connection back toward the existing supply.

Test the new arrangement before removing the original cable.

The sequence is straightforward:

1. Identify the machine and its required network type.
2. Choose an outlet position that leaves enough clearance for the connection.
3. Connect the outlet to the existing source or distribution network.
4. Connect the machine and confirm that it operates.
5. Remove the old cable only after the replacement works under load.

This is particularly useful on a working ship where disconnecting a shared power route could also interrupt batteries, processing equipment, or essential controls.

Electric extenders are still useful for short gaps.

<h2 id="range-blocked-output">
  Why a Wall Outlet Shows Blocked Output or Runs Out of Range
</h2>

Two outlet failures are particularly easy to mistake for problems with the electricity supply.

The first is insufficient cable range.

A connection may appear short when viewed directly, but floors, deck structures, and equipment placement can prevent it from reaching the desired point. If the game will not accept the connection, moving the outlet to a more accessible position is often more useful than extending the same awkward route.

The socket's physical model can appear to fit even when the actual connection side is obstructed. Tanks, walls, structural edges, and nearby machinery can all leave insufficient room for the cable or hose.

Before changing the network, rotate the socket or move it away from the obstruction. Then inspect which side accepts the input and which side must connect to the destination.

A blocked output is a placement problem. Adding generation or charging more batteries will not remove the physical obstruction.

<h2 id="bulk-storage-update-55">
  Bulk Battery and Fuel Tank Capacities in Update 6
</h2>

The Bulk Battery, Bulk Petrol Tank, and Bulk Diesel Tank were introduced with Update 5.5 and unlocked through the **MOSES Field Reserve Program**. Their storage capacities have since increased.

In the Update 6 Beta, the larger containers have these capacities:

- **Bulk Battery: 30 kWh / 30,000 Wh**
- **Medium Battery: 6 kWh / 6,000 Wh**
- **Bulk Petrol Tank: 20,000 L**
- **Bulk Diesel Tank: 20,000 L**
- **Standard Petrol and Diesel Tanks: 2,000 L each**

A Bulk Battery now holds five times as much energy as one Medium Battery. A 20,000 L Bulk Fuel Tank has the nominal capacity of ten standard 2,000 L tanks.

These are substantial increases over the previous Update 5.5 values of 20 kWh for the Bulk Battery and 10,000 L for the large fuel tanks.

The MOSES Field Reserve Program remains important because unlocking the relevant skill alone is not enough to obtain the bulk-storage equipment. Complete the required program at Warehouse Alpha before planning around those containers. For the Warehouse Alpha objectives, see <Link href="/the-last-caretaker/moses-warehouse-alpha-guide">MOSES Warehouse Alpha</Link>.

The smaller Battery Barrel also has a different purpose from the large storage units. Its roughly 1 kWh capacity is useful for temporary or portable electrical needs, but it does not replace the reserve provided by a Medium or Bulk Battery.

<h2 id="bulk-battery-fill-equalize">
  Why a 30 kWh Bulk Battery Stops Charging Around 5–6 kWh
</h2>

The Bulk Battery's capacity does not guarantee that every connected electrical network will fill it correctly.

The important distinction is between **Fill** and **Equalize**.

With different battery sizes connected together, Equalize can cause the larger battery to settle near the absolute energy level of the smaller batteries instead of using its full storage capacity.

This behavior is especially noticeable when a 30 kWh Bulk Battery shares a network with 6 kWh Medium Batteries.

During an earlier mixed-bank setup, Medium Batteries contained approximately **5.3 kWh** each. Connecting a much larger Bulk Battery through Equalize held the larger container near that level rather than continuing toward its own maximum.

The result is a battery that appears connected and functional but is storing only a small fraction of its available energy.

If the Bulk Battery stops charging well below its displayed maximum, inspect the connection mode before adding more generators.

A reliable way to diagnose the problem is to separate charging from normal power distribution:

1. Check the Bulk Battery's current energy against its maximum.
2. Inspect the connection to the Medium Battery bank.
3. Use **Fill** on the charging route intended to replenish the Bulk Battery.
4. Watch whether the stored energy continues rising beyond the smaller batteries' level.
5. Reconnect the large reserve deliberately, rather than assuming Equalize will preserve its full charge.

Equalize can be useful for sharing electricity, but it may also pull stored energy back toward the levels of the connected smaller batteries.

This is why I do not leave a large reserve permanently dependent on a mixed bank without checking its actual behavior.

For a fixed installation, a bank composed mainly of Bulk Batteries is easier to plan around than one large battery surrounded by many smaller units. On a boat, retaining the original Medium Battery layout and adding a separately managed reserve may be less disruptive.

<h2 id="bulk-tanks">
  How to Fill 20,000 L Bulk Petrol and Diesel Tanks
</h2>

The higher capacity of Update 6 Bulk Fuel Tanks makes them considerably more useful for long journeys.

A single 20,000 L tank can replace ten standard 2,000 L tanks by capacity. That reduces the number of separate storage containers and potentially the number of connections required to maintain a large reserve.

However, the size increase does not remove the need to configure the transfer route correctly.

Mixed-container networks can show behavior similar to mixed batteries. If a large tank stops filling around the level imposed by smaller containers, inspect the transfer mode before assuming the Bulk Tank cannot hold its advertised capacity.

For a large reserve that you intend to fill before leaving port, I prefer an explicit **Fill** connection.

Once the container reaches the required amount, its connection to the working fuel network can be arranged around the equipment that needs the resource.

Keep different liquids identifiable:

- Petrol storage should connect to petrol consumers.
- Diesel storage should serve equipment requiring diesel.
- Oil should have its own appropriate collection and processing route.

A large tank receiving the wrong liquid or connected to the wrong destination does not become more useful simply because it has additional capacity.

The same applies to transfers involving the Oil Whale. The critical step is getting the collected oil into a suitable storage network; the encounter and transfer procedure are covered in <Link href="/the-last-caretaker/oil-whale-guide">Oil Whale</Link>.

<h2 id="boat-or-fixed-base">
  Should You Use Bulk Storage on the Boat or at Lazarus?
</h2>

The difference between a working boat and a fixed facility is available space.

On the ship, Medium Batteries can fit along walls and into smaller gaps between existing machines. A Bulk Battery has greater capacity, but it also occupies a larger physical volume. Replacing a compact bank may require moving equipment or changing walkways.

The 30 kWh capacity improves that trade-off considerably, especially when the original batteries cannot support heavy electrical loads for long enough.

Still, I would not remove a functioning Medium Battery layout merely to install a taller container. If the existing equipment fits the ship well, a separate Bulk Battery reserve can be the less disruptive choice.

Bulk fuel tanks are easier to justify when they replace several smaller tanks and their associated connections. One large tank may leave more usable space than a collection of ten standard tanks, provided there is sufficient room to install it and reach its connections.

Fixed locations offer more flexibility.

At **Lazarus**, the larger equipment can support heavy electrical projects without taking space away from the ship's normal working deck. That becomes particularly valuable when preparing for Project Crossing.

At Exodus and other facilities with repeated processing or power requirements, large storage can also reduce the amount of temporary equipment that must be moved between visits.

The best location is one where the container can be filled, connected, inspected, and accessed without obstructing the equipment around it.

<h2 id="fuel-gas-routing">
  Keep Petrol, Diesel, Oil, and Gas Networks Separate
</h2>

A resource network can look connected while moving the wrong substance or feeding equipment that does not accept it.

This becomes a bigger issue when the boat has several neighboring tanks, wall outlets, and processing machines.

For liquid fuel networks, identify the source and destination before connecting the hoses. Oil, diesel, and petrol are not interchangeable, and sending material toward the wrong container or machine does not resolve a supply shortage elsewhere.

Gas networks require the same attention.

Oxygen systems and other gas-consuming equipment may use similar-looking connections, but the receiving machine still needs the correct gas.

I keep each important route easy to trace. A tank or outlet should have an identifiable source, a destination, and a clear reason for being connected to that part of the ship.

There is no advantage in filling an entire wall with adjacent outlets if inspecting a failed machine still requires tracing several unidentified hoses.

Project Eden can create additional construction and supply requirements. Its building objectives are covered in <Link href="/the-last-caretaker/project-eden-guide">Project Eden</Link>; the electrical and resource networks supporting those machines should remain understandable as the installation expands.

<h2 id="transfer-gate-power-update-6">
  How to Prepare Enough Power for the Lazarus Transfer Gate
</h2>

Update 6 introduces a much heavier electrical task at Lazarus: powering the rooftop **Transfer Gate** used for Project Crossing.

Ordinary Lazarus equipment can be running normally while the Transfer Gate still lacks enough stored energy to activate.

The gate must receive electricity through its power inputs and accumulate sufficient charge before it can be used. Its interface shows the current energy level, the charging requirement, and the available power information.

This makes a large storage bank useful, but adding batteries without checking their charge or the actual supply connections can leave the gate waiting for electricity.

Before trying to activate it, inspect three things:

- **Generation:** Are your solar panels, wind turbines, or other connected generators producing electricity?
- **Stored energy:** Do the batteries contain usable electricity, or are they almost empty?
- **Connections:** Is power actually reaching the Transfer Gate rather than stopping at an unconnected outlet or a separate network?

The gate has multiple electrical inputs, so a single slow connection does not have to be the only route supplying it. Check the power indicators while adding connections to see whether charging improves.

Also distinguish between the figures displayed for incoming electrical power and accumulated energy. A large power input can charge the system more quickly, but the gate still needs sufficient stored energy before activation.

This is where Update 6 Bulk Batteries become particularly useful. Their increased capacity allows substantial energy storage at Lazarus without requiring the same number of smaller units.

I would establish the gate's electrical supply before installing the final equipment and beginning the transfer sequence. A charged system gives you more freedom to move supplies into position without interrupting other essential machinery.

For the Continuity Token, destination selection, and travel to North Bastion, follow <Link href="/the-last-caretaker/walkthrough">The Last Caretaker Walkthrough</Link>.

<h2 id="north-bastion-methane">
  How to Power the Methane Extractor at North Bastion
</h2>

North Bastion introduces an additional kind of resource dependency. The new heating equipment needs more than a working electrical connection.

The **Methane Extractor** draws methane trapped beneath the ice. It requires electrical power to operate, while the extracted methane must travel through a suitable gas connection to equipment that consumes it.

This creates two separate things to check: whether the extractor can run, and whether the collected gas reaches its destination.

If the extractor does not operate, start with its electrical connection. Check the nearby power source, the available charge, and the cable route to the extractor.

Once it is operating, inspect the methane output separately. Electricity reaching the extractor does not mean methane automatically arrives at the Boiler.

The nearby heating equipment introduces another requirement: the Boiler needs **Methane and Fresh Water** to generate steam for the heaters.

As a result, several failures may appear to be the same heating problem:

- **Extractor inactive:** Check electrical power and the extractor's connection.
- **Extractor working but no methane reaches the Boiler:** Inspect the gas output and hose route.
- **Methane available but Boiler cannot run:** Check the fresh-water supply.
- **Boiler operating but the room remains cold:** Inspect whether steam reaches the intended heater.

The fresh-water requirement is important because finding a water supply is not necessarily the same as providing processed Fresh Water. A Water Purifier may be needed to prepare the water used by the Boiler.

Do not replace the extractor's electrical connection with a gas hose, or assume a powered Boiler can manufacture its own methane. Each part of the system has a separate role.

The gas and water supply routes should also be easy to access for repairs. Running short of cable or hose material in the frozen region can leave essential equipment disconnected while cold continues affecting your systems.

For the initial Boiler activation, Bring Back the Heat objective, and route toward the North Bastion Laboratory, continue with <Link href="/the-last-caretaker/walkthrough">North Bastion Progression</Link>.

<h2 id="grid-scan">
  Use Grid Scan When a Power Network Looks Connected but Does Not Work
</h2>

Grid Scan becomes useful when visible inspection does not explain why a powered machine is inactive.

A broken cable or blocked outlet is usually straightforward to identify. Problems are harder when a machine appears connected to a working electrical network but still receives no usable supply.

I use Grid Scan most often after changing a large battery bank or reorganizing the ship's power distribution.

It is also useful when a connection has been replaced but the expected machine does not start, or when several machines are supposed to share electricity and only some of them respond.

The important question is whether the equipment is actually part of the intended electrical network.

For example, a new outlet may be connected to the machine but not to the charged battery network you expected it to use. Looking at the cable near the machine alone would not reveal the missing supply upstream.

Grid Scan helps narrow that investigation rather than dismantling the entire installation.

It does not, however, turn an incorrect gas or fuel connection into the right resource. If the machine is receiving electricity but requires a liquid or gas input, inspect that resource network separately.

<h2 id="power-fuel-troubleshooting">
  Troubleshooting Power and Fuel Problems by Symptom
</h2>

When an installation stops working, the fastest repair usually starts with the specific symptom instead of the size of the network.

**The machine has power cables attached but will not start**

Inspect the actual power supply and whether the machine's network is connected to charged storage or active generation. Then check for other missing resource inputs. A Fabricator or processor may require more than an electrical connection.

**A power outlet shows Blocked Output**

Move or rotate the socket so the connection side is clear. Do not replace batteries or generators until the physical output is usable.

**A large battery will not charge beyond the Medium Battery level**

Look at Fill and Equalize. Try charging the Bulk Battery through Fill and check whether it rises toward its own maximum capacity.

**A Bulk Petrol or Diesel Tank stops filling early**

Confirm the resource type and container capacity, then inspect the transfer mode. A dedicated Fill route is a better test than repeatedly rearranging tanks in the same mixed network.

**The Transfer Gate charges too slowly**

Check incoming generation, available stored energy, and the connections supplying the gate. Adding a battery that is not charged or not connected to the gate will not improve its charging rate.

**The Methane Extractor is connected but does not produce methane**

Check electrical power at the extractor itself. Once it operates, inspect the methane output separately from the power connection.

**The Boiler has methane but the heaters still do not provide heat**

Confirm that the Boiler has the required Fresh Water and that steam is reaching the heater. An active methane supply alone is insufficient.

**The boat stopped working after reorganizing the network**

Restore the last working connection first. Once the affected machine runs again, change one outlet or cable at a time until the problem can be reproduced and corrected.

<h2 id="when-to-stop-rebuilding">
  When the Power and Fuel Layout Is Good Enough
</h2>

The purpose of organizing the ship's electrical and resource systems is to make equipment dependable without turning every later repair into a major reconstruction.

A functioning layout gives you clear routes between generators, storage, and consumers. The different fuel and gas networks can be identified without following every hose, and the important machines remain accessible for repair.

Storage should also work at the capacity you intended. A 30 kWh Bulk Battery that never charges beyond a few kilowatt-hours is not providing the reserve you installed it for. The same applies to a 20,000 L fuel tank whose connection prevents it from receiving more than a small amount.

I leave usable space for additional equipment instead of packing every remaining wall and deck section with outlets.

The ship does not need to be perfectly symmetrical or free of visible cables. It needs to deliver electricity and resources reliably, remain easy to repair, and have enough reserve for the next expedition.
