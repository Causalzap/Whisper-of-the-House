<h2 id="start-memory-for-aaron">
  Start Memory for Aaron through the Rising Tide cartridge
</h2>

Memory for Aaron does not begin by sailing around the north edge of the map hoping to discover another station. The trigger starts much closer to home: **Rising Tide**, one of the Pico cartridges.

If the cassette has been sitting in storage since I picked it up, I take it to a **Pico Terminal** and install it. The terminal keeps track of the cartridges that have already been collected, so the important step is getting Rising Tide running rather than carrying the physical cassette around afterward.

At first it looks like another self-contained Pico game. That impression is what makes this quest easy to miss.

Rising Tide has score-gated sectors. During my run the progression climbed through requirements of **50, 100, 150, 200, and 250**, and I eventually pushed the score to **300**. After that, the information attached to the cartridge changed and the terminal exposed something far more useful than another score attempt: **coordinates, an access code, and the name Aaron Voss**.

That is the point I care about.

I do not keep replaying Rising Tide once Aaron's data has surfaced. The game has already handed me the progression clue.

<GuideCallout title="No Memory for Aaron quest yet?">
If Rising Tide is installed but Aaron Voss has never appeared, keep working through the cartridge's gated progression first. Simply owning the cassette is not the same as exposing the hidden data that starts the quest.
</GuideCallout>

<h2 id="get-memory-for-aaron-quest">
  Wait for the quest to point to North Deep Intake Station
</h2>

After the Aaron Voss data appeared, the next useful confirmation was explicit:

**Memory for Aaron**

**Travel to North Deep Intake Station**

**Discover what information Alina Voss was hiding.**

The map gains the destination after the quest is active, so I use that marker rather than trying to reverse-engineer the coordinates from the cartridge text.

That also avoids mixing two completely different 5.5 mysteries together.

Rising Tide appears while players are already investigating the new Transposium material, and the same session can expose references to the 99X rooms. **Memory for Aaron is not solved by finding Room 998 or a hidden Room 72.** Once the quest says North Deep Intake Station, I leave that puzzle behind and follow the quest marker.

This matters because the 99X material can become a serious time sink. Some of those rooms are visible through security systems or can be discussed from inaccessible areas, but that does not make them part of Aaron's route.

For this quest, the marker is the reliable next action.

<h2 id="prepare-for-north-deep-intake">
  Bring a fighting loadout, but leave room to explore
</h2>

North Deep Intake Station looks more like an overrun industrial facility than one of the cleaner habitat interiors. That changes what I carry.

A **Flamethrower** is useful because slugs and smaller biological threats show up around the structure, but it is not a reason to fill every weapon slot. I also keep a rifle or another dependable ranged weapon available because the station has enough open and vertical space that not every target is conveniently inside flame range.

The larger risk is arriving overpacked.

There are paint cans, food, containers, common materials, Quantum Crystals, nuggets, and plenty of other objects that can pull attention away from the quest. If my inventory is nearly full before I even dock, the station turns into inventory management long before Aaron's trail becomes interesting.

My threshold is simple: enough ammo and energy to search without immediately retreating, plus several free inventory slots.

I also save before going deep into the structure.

This is not because the route requires some brutal boss fight. It is because North Deep Intake invites side exploration: ladders, upper platforms, underwater sections, loose containers, machinery, and rooms that look like they ought to hide something. A save lets me investigate those without worrying that one bad fall or combat mistake will erase the useful part of the trip.

<h2 id="reach-north-deep-intake">
  Reach North Deep Intake Station and orient yourself before looting
</h2>

The station becomes obvious once I approach the quest marker. Its silhouette reads more like an industrial intake or refinery structure than a living space, which helped me identify that I had reached the correct location.

I resist the first instinct to strip the outer platforms.

There is enough ordinary loot here that a full cleanup can easily become the longest part of the quest, even though none of that material tells me what Alina was hiding. On the first pass I use the station's big structural features as landmarks and concentrate on finding access between the upper platforms, interior rooms, and lower areas.

The ladders deserve attention because the station has vertical routes that are easy to overlook from the deck. Some ladder access can be manipulated from below, and once I am on the upper structure I use the height to understand the station rather than immediately climbing down after the first container.

Underneath, there are also submerged sections and additional materials. They are worth remembering, especially for resources such as **Quantum Crystals**, but I treat them as secondary until the Aaron objective has moved.

The question guiding the first pass is not “what can I salvage?”

It is:

**Which part of this station reacts to the quest?**

<h2 id="find-aaron-grave">
  The grave does not look like a grave until the game identifies it
</h2>

This is the strongest real-world card in the whole quest.

I expected Aaron's location to be obvious: a memorial marker, a carved name, some recognizable grave decoration, or at least a room that visually announced its purpose.

North Deep Intake does not give that away.

The relevant interior can look like another industrial room. There was no traditional headstone, no giant RIP marking, and nothing that made me instantly think “this is where Memory for Aaron ends.”

That is why the AI line matters so much.

When I entered the correct space, the game identified it as **the first grave we have ever found** and explained that Alina had refused to let Aaron's life mean nothing.

That means visual guesswork is a poor way to decide whether you have found it.

Move through the accessible station interior and let the location trigger fire. If the game has not acknowledged a grave yet, keep checking the connected rooms rather than searching the walls for a conventional memorial.

There is also a deceptively simple interaction around this part of the route: a door can look like it needs a power solution when it actually has a usable physical handle. I nearly turned that into an electrical problem because so many earlier locations train me to think in terms of generators and cables.

Before building power equipment beside an apparently dead door, walk right up to it and check whether it simply opens.

That saves a surprising amount of unnecessary construction.

<h2 id="north-deep-intake-access">
  Keep pushing after the grave trigger instead of leaving immediately
</h2>

Finding the grave is the emotional reveal, but I do not sprint back to the boat the moment the AI recognizes it.

There is still accessible structure beyond the first discovery. I continue through the nearby doors, including the route where the game gives **Access granted**, and check the connected underwater space below.

That lower section contains more industrial material and some less-common resources. On my run I found **Quantum Crystals** among the things worth picking up, which made that detour more attractive than another stack of generic scrap.

The important choice is whether the character is still moving comfortably.

If inventory weight has started to drag the run down, Aaron's quest takes priority over another load of titanium or paint material. The station remains available after completion; the quest reward does not depend on stripping every lower pipe and platform.

If I still have room, this is a good moment to take the rarer resources because the route is already open and I am standing beside them.

That is a better salvage rule than “take everything until the character can barely walk.”

<h2 id="complete-memory-for-aaron">
  Stay until Memory for Aaron actually completes
</h2>

The quest completed after the grave route and the remaining nearby exploration had registered, awarding progression rewards including **enhancement / skill points**.

The story payoff also clarifies what Alina was preserving.

Aaron died without learning that he was going to become a father. Alina preserved the truth so that their daughter could inherit more than his name.

That reveal changes the earlier wording of the quest. The “memory” is not just a collectible attached to a dead caretaker; the location exists to preserve a personal history that would otherwise have disappeared with Aaron.

Once the quest-complete state lands, I consider the progression job finished.

North Deep Intake can still hold ordinary loot, paint variants, containers, lower-level materials, and pieces I skipped on the way in. None of those are required to prove that Memory for Aaron has completed.

This is where I separate story completion from salvage completion.

If the boat is nearby and the inventory still has space, another sweep is cheap. If energy is getting low or movement has slowed, I leave. The destination is now known and the path has already been opened, which makes a future resource run much less expensive than forcing one more overloaded trip on the first visit.

<h2 id="memory-for-aaron-stuck">
  Memory for Aaron stuck? Check the trigger before searching the station
</h2>

**I have the Rising Tide cassette, but no quest**

Install it in a Pico Terminal and work through its gated progression. Owning the cassette alone did not trigger Memory for Aaron on my run.

**Rising Tide keeps showing score requirements**

Keep progressing through the gated sectors. The sequence exposes higher thresholds, and my run reached **300** before the hidden Aaron Voss information appeared.

**Aaron Voss appeared, but I do not know where to sail**

Wait for the actual **Memory for Aaron** quest state and then use the new **North Deep Intake Station** marker. There is no need to manually navigate from a guessed coordinate.

**I am searching Transposium for Room 998 or Room 72**

That is a different mystery. Once Memory for Aaron points to North Deep Intake, leave the 99X investigation alone.

**I reached North Deep Intake but cannot find a headstone**

Do not search for a conventional-looking grave. Move through the station interior until the AI recognizes the correct area as the grave.

**A door looks unpowered**

Check for a physical handle before building or rewiring anything. At least one apparent power problem on this route is much simpler than it first looks.

**I found the grave, but the quest has not completed yet**

Keep working through the connected grave / access area and let the station state register. Do not leave the instant the first grave line plays.

**I am overweight before finishing**

Leave common salvage. Aaron's objective does not require cleaning North Deep Intake, and the rarer materials are easier to collect once the route is already understood.

<h2 id="after-memory-for-aaron">
  After Aaron, treat North Deep Intake as a known resource stop
</h2>

The useful thing about completing Memory for Aaron is that the station stops being an unknown quest destination.

I now know its vertical layout, where the grave route sits, which doors actually matter, where the lower section opens up, and which areas are mostly ordinary salvage. A return visit can therefore be built around resources instead of discovery.

That makes the second trip very different from the first.

If I need Quantum Crystals or want to strip more of the industrial materials, I arrive with empty storage and enough power to haul. If those resources are already abundant, there is no reason to return just because several shelves remained untouched.

The story route has already done its job.

Aaron's name appeared in a cartridge, the cartridge exposed a place Alina had hidden, and North Deep Intake turned that clue into one of the more personal discoveries in Update 5.5.
