<h2 id="starting-dock-beacon-49">
  Power the starting dock, then wake Beacon 49
</h2>

The opening hours feel much less confusing once I stop treating every piece of scrap as equally important. The route is really about getting enough power online to leave the starting area, then using the navigation relay to expose the next major destination.

At the dock, the first blocker is the **dock outlet / upper-deck power objective**. The wording can make it sound as if there is a special item called a Dock Outlet somewhere in the building. What actually matters is restoring power through the available batteries, connectors, and cables until the upper deck comes back online.

I keep extra cable on me from this point onward. Cable shortages repeatedly turn a five-minute repair into a trip back across the structure, and almost every major facility after the dock asks for more routing.

Once the ship is usable, the first important trip is **Navigational Beacon 49**. The beacon is dark when you arrive. Restore its power, charge the relay, and activate it. When the relay finally comes alive, it reveals the Lazarus Complex and gives the first clear answer to “where do I go next?”

Early skill points are more valuable when they remove a real bottleneck. I favor power management, carrying capacity, and anything that makes repeated repair work easier before spending heavily on convenience gear I have not needed yet. If the boat already has stable generation and I am not constantly overweight, I am comfortable holding points until the next facility exposes a specific problem.

<GuideCallout title="Leave the dock when">
The ship can travel without immediately running out of power or fuel, you have spare cable, and Beacon 49 is active. Clearing every crate before leaving only delays the first real progression destination.
</GuideCallout>

<h2 id="lazarus-complex">
  Restore Lazarus before chasing the Seed Vault
</h2>

Lazarus is where the game stops feeling like a tutorial and starts asking whether the boat can support a real expedition.

The first time I enter, I am not trying to strip the whole complex. I want three things done:

1. restore the main power;
2. gain access to the Hall of Humanity;
3. repair the Lazarus systems far enough to receive the Seed Vault objective.

The most common dead end is reaching the Hall of Humanity while the facility still has no usable main power. I wasted time checking doors and terminals before the objective made the actual problem clear: **the Hall stays inaccessible until the Lazarus power route is restored, with the main power route leading upward toward the roof systems.**

I also keep a small material reserve on the boat instead of converting everything into random upgrades. During the Lazarus repair work, one of the required repair points called for **plastic, carbon fiber, and titanium**. Running short on something basic such as plastic is a good reason to go back to the boat; wandering deeper and hoping the exact material appears is usually slower.

Once the Lazarus Rack is working again, the objective changes. The game points toward a Human Seed Vault **far to the north**. That is the moment I leave Lazarus rather than continuing to scrap the place room by room.

The rack is the progression gate. Extra loot is optional.

<h2 id="seed-vault-prometheus">
  Go north to Seed Vault Prometheus and leave once the Seed is live
</h2>

The trip north leads to **Seed Vault Prometheus**. When I approach the structure, I park on the usable side of the platform, save before entering, and make sure I am not already overloaded.

There is plenty to loot inside, which is exactly why this section can go wrong.

Before retrieving the Seed, I am happy to explore, fight, and collect materials. After the Seed is secured, priorities change completely.

The first Human Seed run gives you a **two-hour survival window**. On my return trip, the remaining time had already dropped to about **1 hour 33 minutes** before I got the Seed back into Lazarus.

That changes how I handle the Vault.

For the first trip, I take **one Seed and leave**.

Extra biomaterial and scrap can make the character painfully slow, and overloading yourself after starting a timed return trip is a bad trade. The other loot will still be there. The Seed timer will not wait while you dismantle another room.

The important sequence is:

1. reach the Seed chamber;
2. secure a Human Seed in the transport pod;
3. stop treating the Vault as a loot run;
4. return to the boat;
5. sail back to Lazarus;
6. place the Seed into a functional Lazarus Rack.

That is the safest point to resume scavenging.

<h2 id="human-seed-lazarus">
  Back at Lazarus, solve upkeep before trying to grow the Human
</h2>

Getting the Seed home is not the end of the timed problem.

Once the Seed is placed into the Lazarus system, the rack immediately exposes the next weakness: **human growth needs support infrastructure**.

Fresh water was the first practical failure point for me. The Seed was back safely, but the rack still showed that the Human was in danger because the support requirements were not ready.

This is where I stop spending resources on unrelated exploration gear.

A basic growth setup needs the systems that can provide:

- fresh water;
- food;
- memories;
- enough power to keep the equipment running.

The **Soil Forge** and **Food Processor** become useful here because the rack is not just checking whether food exists. Human growth also cares about things such as **weight, height, and life expectancy**.

If the rack says those minimum requirements are not met, throwing more power at the machine will not fix the problem. Food and memory inputs are what change the growth result.

I also avoid starting another Seed while the first setup is still unstable. One half-supported Human is enough to diagnose the system; two only double the upkeep pressure.

<h2 id="grow-first-human">
  Grow one workable Human before chasing a perfect one
</h2>

The first Human does not need to be a masterpiece.

The rack itself gives the useful rule: initialize growth only after supplying **fresh water, memories, and food**, then wait for the growth cycle to finish.

When I first tested the system, it was tempting to keep adjusting every input and chase better stats before allowing the Human to finish. That is useful later, when a profession or Council role has a specific target. For the first progression Human, the larger goal is proving that the whole chain works.

A bad first result teaches more than another hour spent staring at ingredients.

Once the Human finishes growing, the objective changes immediately.

The game tells you to escort the grown Human to **Exodus Station, southeast of the Lazarus Complex**.

That direction matters because Exodus may not yet feel like an obvious next destination on a crowded map.

Before carrying the Human onto the boat, I clear a safe spot on deck and save. A grown Human is heavy enough to noticeably affect movement while being carried, and dropping one around the boat is much easier than expected.

This is also a good point to check fuel.

A long sea trip with the main progression objective physically sitting on deck is not when I want to discover that the ship is nearly empty.

<h2 id="exodus">
  Take the Human southeast to Exodus
</h2>

From Lazarus, the next route is southeast.

The practical goal is simple: transport the Human until the Exodus / rocket facility comes into view.

I treat the trip differently from a normal scavenging run.

The Human is the objective. Random platforms are not.

If the boat is short on petrol, badly damaged, or carrying so much loose storage that moving around the deck is awkward, I fix that before leaving Lazarus. There is little value in creating a rescue trip halfway to Exodus just because a fuel stop looked inconvenient five minutes earlier.

Reaching Exodus is also where the early progression route begins to hand off into the larger EA systems. The game has already taught the core loop by this point:

**restore infrastructure → reveal the next destination → bring back what the next system needs → prepare the boat → move humanity forward.**

That same loop keeps showing up later, but the destinations become more specialized.

<h2 id="later-progression">
  After the early route, choose one later objective at a time
</h2>

Once the early Lazarus and Human chain is behind me, I stop trying to clear every marker in one sweep.

Update 5 adds several large routes that compete for the same boat space, power, fuel, and attention.

If I want the large construction path, I move into <Link href="/the-last-caretaker/project-eden-guide">Project Eden at Pishon</Link>.

If oil supply is becoming the problem, I follow the <Link href="/the-last-caretaker/oil-whale-guide">Oil Whale route</Link> instead of treating the Whale like a random map encounter.

For a deeper underwater objective, <Link href="/the-last-caretaker/research-outpost-theta-9-guide">Research Outpost Theta 9</Link> is its own trip and deserves proper preparation before leaving the boat.

Returning players coming into the broader Update 5 progression can use the <Link href="/the-last-caretaker/new-horizons-guide">New Horizons Guide</Link> to decide which of those routes is worth doing first.

The mistake I avoid is combining all of them into one overloaded expedition.

Project Eden wants construction materials. Theta 9 rewards mobility and combat preparation. Oil Whale work cares about power and storage. Trying to prepare for all three at once usually produces a boat full of equipment with no clear purpose.

<h2 id="progression-rule">
  The progression rule that keeps me from getting lost
</h2>

When the map starts filling with optional locations, I use the current objective as the anchor.

A major facility normally ends by pointing somewhere specific:

- Beacon 49 reveals Lazarus;
- Lazarus points north toward the Human Seed Vault;
- the Seed returns to Lazarus;
- completed Human growth points southeast toward Exodus;
- later systems branch into their own routes.

If I have finished a large objective and nothing obvious is happening, I first check whether I actually completed the facility’s power, relay, rack, or terminal step.

Most of my “where do I go now?” moments have not come from missing a secret island.

They came from leaving a required system half-finished.
