<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
For a new save, restore the starting dock and Beacon 49, reach Lazarus, recover a Human Seed from Prometheus, grow your first Human, and carry them to Exodus. For Update 6, return to Lazarus to start Project Crossing, power the Transfer Gate, and enter North Bastion. Restore heat before searching the frozen settlement. If another objective is already active, prepare for that specific location rather than trying to complete every expedition at once.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

<h2 id="where-to-go-next">
  What Should You Do Next in The Last Caretaker?
</h2>

The Last Caretaker doesn't always send you toward the newest location. As your save develops, the map fills with research stations, construction projects, abandoned facilities, and signals that lead in different directions. Some objectives require a long voyage, while others are waiting for a single machine, resource connection, or terminal interaction.

The useful starting point is the objective your save has already reached.

- **Still repairing the starting dock or looking for Lazarus?** Concentrate on the navigation and Human Seed sequence. You do not need to investigate every platform along the way.
- **A Human is growing, but progression has stopped?** Check the Lazarus Rack's supplies, growth requirements, and the current transport objective.
- **The First Crossing is active?** Return to Lazarus. Project Crossing uses the rooftop Transfer Gate and leads to North Bastion.
- **First Roots is active at Pishon?** Prepare for construction and life support rather than another combat expedition.
- **MOSES, Memory for Aaron, or an underwater survivor signal is active?** Each points toward a particular facility with its own equipment requirements.
- **Your ship has fuel and electricity but machines keep shutting down?** Repair the resource network before taking on another distant project.

These are not all stages of one continuous mission. Some can remain unfinished while you pursue a different objective. The important distinction is whether a destination advances your current task or merely gives you somewhere else to explore.

<h2 id="core-progression">
  From Beacon 49 to Your First Human at Exodus
</h2>

The first major journey takes you from repairing a barely functional ship to transporting a grown Human toward space.

It follows a recognizable sequence:

**Starting Dock → Beacon 49 → Lazarus Complex → Seed Vault Prometheus → Lazarus Rack → Exodus Station**

At the beginning, restore enough electrical power to operate the ship and reach Navigational Beacon 49. Once its relay is active, Lazarus becomes the next important destination. The Hall of Humanity and Lazarus Rack introduce the systems needed to recover and grow Humans.

Seed Vault Prometheus adds a more immediate problem: retrieving the first Human Seed and returning it safely before its survival time expires. Extra salvage is less valuable than an intact Seed if you are already making the journey back to Lazarus.

Once the Seed is installed, the challenge changes from navigation to life support. The rack needs fresh water, food, Memories, and electricity. If it reports missing upkeep, deal with that requirement before attempting to grow another Human.

For the full sequence of facilities and objectives, follow the <Link href="/the-last-caretaker/walkthrough">route from Beacon 49 through Lazarus and Prometheus</Link>.

The first Human does not need to have ideal late-game attributes. Meeting the immediate growth requirements is enough to continue. When you begin preparing Humans for specific roles, profession percentages, physical characteristics, and memory combinations become much more important. Those decisions are covered under <Link href="/the-last-caretaker/human-growth-professions-guide">Human Growth and Professions</Link>.

<h2 id="update-6-project-crossing">
  Update 6: How to Reach North Bastion Through Project Crossing
</h2>

Update 6 introduces **Project Crossing**, a new journey beginning at the Lazarus Complex. Its Transfer Gate leads to North Bastion, a frozen region where electrical power, gas supply, and protection from cold become immediate concerns.

If **The First Crossing** is your current objective, the place to investigate is Lazarus rather than another distant island.

The structure on the roof is a Transfer Gate. To use it, you need to examine the Project Crossing room, scan the **Continuity Token**, reproduce the token with a Fabricator, and provide the gate with sufficient electrical charge.

This is a much larger electrical demand than an ordinary powered door or crafting machine. Before committing materials to additional storage, check whether electricity is actually reaching the gate and whether its connected batteries contain enough energy.

The trip changes considerably after you reach the other side.

North Bastion is not another warm, open-water settlement. The cold can damage your systems and make movement increasingly dangerous. Your first useful task is to establish a heated shelter.

The initial heating system relies on a **Boiler** that uses **Methane and Fresh Water** to produce steam for heaters. A nearby Methane Extractor can draw gas from beneath the ice, but the extractor itself requires electricity. Powering a machine and supplying its output are separate problems: a working extractor still needs a gas connection to deliver methane to the equipment that consumes it.

After **Bring Back the Heat** begins progressing, the journey leads north toward the settlement inside the mountain. Anna then directs your attention to a Laboratory deeper within the complex.

There are two different kinds of problems here:

- If you cannot open the gate, do not have the Continuity Token, or are unsure where to go after arriving, follow the <Link href="/the-last-caretaker/walkthrough">Project Crossing and North Bastion progression</Link>.
- If the gate will not charge, the Methane Extractor has no power, or gas is not reaching the heating equipment, inspect the connections in <Link href="/the-last-caretaker/power-fuel-wall-outlets">Power, Fuel, and Wall Outlets</Link>.

Prepare for the cold before traveling deeper into North Bastion. Spare repair materials, healing supplies, and a usable electrical reserve matter much more there than they do on a routine salvage run.

<h2 id="returning-new-horizons">
  Returning to New Horizons: Pishon, Oil Whale, or Theta 9?
</h2>

A developed save can have several major objectives available without being ready to complete all of them. The ship may already have extensive storage, advanced equipment, and access to older facilities, but the newer destinations still impose different costs.

If you have returned after an earlier update and are unsure what to tackle, <Link href="/the-last-caretaker/new-horizons-guide">New Horizons</Link> explains the larger destinations and their progression.

Once a specific objective is active, choose the supplies and equipment around that task.

**Pishon and Project Eden**

Pishon is the construction route. The first visit is useful for locating the platform and inspecting the **Eden Frame - Small 1** requirements. If you are short on Stainless Steel or the required structural components, repeatedly sailing back with a small amount of material will not solve the shortage efficiently.

The next major stage is **First Roots**, which depends on the Farming Platform receiving water, electricity, soil, and seeds. For construction quantities, placement problems, and the actual support connections, use <Link href="/the-last-caretaker/project-eden-guide">Project Eden at Pishon</Link>.

**Project Jonah and the Oil Whale**

The Oil Whale becomes relevant when oil production or supply is limiting what you can build and operate. It requires more preparation than an ordinary salvage encounter, so having the objective available is not by itself a reason to reorganize your ship around it.

If your current oil supply already supports your work, you can continue with another task. When the Whale becomes the priority, <Link href="/the-last-caretaker/oil-whale-guide">Project Jonah and the Oil Whale</Link> covers its operation, resource transfer, and storage requirements.

**Research Outpost Theta 9**

Theta 9 is better approached as a sustained underwater expedition. Travel equipment, weapons, ammunition, and free inventory matter before entering the station, because locked areas and authorization problems can force backtracking.

If the survivor signal is active, prepare for that route rather than taking the same cargo you would use for Project Eden. Players already stopped by **Ventilation 3.3** or authorization requirements can continue with <Link href="/the-last-caretaker/research-outpost-theta-9-guide">Research Outpost Theta 9</Link>.

These destinations can be approached with different preparations. A cargo loadout built for Pishon is not automatically suitable for Theta 9, and the equipment used for the Oil Whale may not help with a construction shortage.

<h2 id="update-55">
  MOSES Warehouse Alpha, Memory for Aaron, and Transposium
</h2>

Update 5.5 adds reasons to return to places that may already be familiar. A warehouse, cartridge activity, or security system can gain new importance even when you explored it during an older save.

The clearest example is the **MOSES Field Reserve Program**.

If the Skills screen shows Bulk Storage equipment but the designs are still unavailable, the missing step is connected to **Central Warehouse Alpha**. The field logistics objective leads to the equipment unlock rather than requiring you to keep buying unrelated skills.

Follow <Link href="/the-last-caretaker/moses-warehouse-alpha-guide">MOSES Warehouse Alpha</Link> when that unlock is preventing you from building the larger storage containers.

A different progression path begins with **Memory for Aaron**. Rising Tide and the Pico cartridge activities eventually reveal Aaron Voss and lead toward **North Deep Intake Station**. Once the quest points there, it becomes a concrete destination rather than another score challenge. The route continues in <Link href="/the-last-caretaker/memory-for-aaron-guide">Memory for Aaron</Link>.

**Transposium Room 998** presents a less straightforward situation. The security system can show the room and nearby 99X clues without providing an obvious physical route into every visible location. That distinction matters when you are repeatedly checking corridors or doors that never change state.

If Room 998 is the specific location you are investigating, review the observed access points and blocked routes in <Link href="/the-last-caretaker/transposium-room-998-guide">Transposium Room 998</Link> before spending another trip searching the same passages.

These three objectives are not interchangeable. A locked bulk storage design points toward Warehouse Alpha; Aaron Voss points toward North Deep Intake; and the Room 998 mystery begins with the Transposium security information.

<h2 id="system-blockers">
  When the Problem Is Power, Fuel, or Human Growth
</h2>

Sometimes the location is correct and the objective has not advanced because the supporting system is failing.

A machine can have a cable attached without receiving power. A large battery can be connected to a network but remain far below its storage capacity. A tank can contain fuel while a downstream consumer receives nothing.

These problems become harder to identify as more equipment is installed.

Update 6 Beta also changes the scale of storage planning. Bulk Batteries now hold **30 kWh**, and Bulk Petrol and Diesel Tanks hold **20,000 L** each. Those increases are useful for power-intensive projects, but capacity alone does not correct an unsuitable Fill / Equalize configuration or a disconnected outlet.

If you are repeatedly moving cables, adding extenders, or charging storage that never reaches the expected level, diagnose the existing network through <Link href="/the-last-caretaker/power-fuel-wall-outlets">electrical, fuel, and gas routing</Link> before building more equipment.

Human production has its own version of the same issue.

A Lazarus Rack may be operating correctly but still produce a Human who does not satisfy the requirements for the role you want. Extra electricity does not compensate for missing Memories, nutrition, or unsuitable attributes.

For Council and specialist production, decide which profession you need before choosing the inputs. The relationship between growth characteristics, Memories, and profession outcomes is covered in <Link href="/the-last-caretaker/human-growth-professions-guide">Human Growth and Professions</Link>.

The distinction is worth making early: a failed machine needs a supply or connection fix; an unsuitable Human needs a different growth plan.

<h2 id="unfinished-searches">
  What to Do With Unresolved Locations and Side Objectives
</h2>

Not every marker needs an immediate expedition. Some destinations are worth investigating only after a specific quest gives you enough information to make progress.

**Salvage Gyro Lost Blueprints** can draw you toward old service platforms. If the objective identifies a platform along your existing route, it is reasonable to investigate. When repeated searches stop revealing new equipment, locations, or quest progress, record what you found and return with better information or a clearer objective.

**Eden Cradle and later Committee work** become more relevant as you develop Human production. An empty role alone is not a reason to spend scarce Memories on a speculative build. Look at the requirements of the role or objective you are actually trying to complete, then prepare the necessary Human.

The wider **Transposium 99X clues**, including references to Rooms 994, 995, and 997, can also remain unresolved while you pursue another mission. A room appearing in a security feed does not necessarily mean the same room is accessible through the corridor you are standing in.

For smaller Samples, isolated Memories, and unusual machinery, keep track of anything that changes the game state: a newly unlocked interaction, an item accepted by a terminal, a revealed destination, or an objective that advances. Repeatedly revisiting an unchanged door or object rarely becomes more productive just because you brought a larger inventory.

<h2 id="when-to-turn-back">
  When Should You Return to the Boat?
</h2>

A long trip is worth continuing while it is moving your objective forward. Once you know the missing requirement, staying at the destination without the means to satisfy it may only consume fuel, ammunition, health, or time.

Before continuing deeper, check:

- **Objective progress:** Has a terminal, quest, door, or construction stage changed since you arrived?
- **Missing materials:** Can the next required part be made with what you brought?
- **Survival reserves:** Do you have enough electricity, fuel, ammunition, and healing supplies to complete the return journey?
- **Cargo weight:** Is additional salvage making travel or combat more difficult than the reward justifies?
- **New information:** Have you found a useful route, unlock condition, or destination that changes what you should bring next time?

If the mission cannot advance without supplies stored on the ship, return and prepare those exact items. If a station needs authorization, further repairs to an unrelated power socket will not solve the locked door. And if you are traveling into North Bastion, do not sacrifice your remaining heat and health simply to explore one more unpowered corridor.

The ship gives you a place to repair equipment, reorganize storage, recharge, and build the parts the current objective needs.

Return with a specific requirement in mind, then complete the task that originally brought you there.
