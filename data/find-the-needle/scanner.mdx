Scanner Mk I sits on a hay belt and treats needles differently from scrap. Scrap leaves through an adjustable cannon; a detected needle stays in the drawer and stops the belt until you collect it. Those two outputs are why the Scanner needs both a clear access point and a place for rejected metal to land.

## How Do You Unlock the Scanner in Find The Needle?

Scanner Mk I becomes available when you complete **First Bales (Stage 1, Order 1)**. Deliver the required **20 Bales**; finding a needle is not part of this unlock. The Scanner will then appear among the newly available buildings.

The Scanner Mk I entry lists a build cost of $250 and describes a belt-mounted machine that checks 90 strands of hay per batch. Unlocking the entry does not mean you can place it immediately: the build also requires Straw Panels from your Depot.

## Why Does Scanner Mk I Say the Depot Is Short of Straw Panels?

A Scanner Mk I costs $250 plus two Straw Panels to construct. Cash alone will not cover the requirement. A red placement preview can explicitly report "the Depot is short Straw Panel 0/2" even when you have unlocked the machine.

## How Do You Put a Scanner on a Conveyor Belt?

Choose a section of the conveyor that hay passes through before it reaches the processing equipment or a sale exit. Turn off the line while you rearrange that section, then select the Scanner in the build menu and place it directly onto the belt route.

The first installation should be simple enough to inspect. Keep the incoming and outgoing belt sections connected, leave room beside the Scanner for its metal output, and check that the building has power before restarting the line.

After mounting the Scanner, face it and press **E** to open the Scanner Drawer panel. Leave space beside the scrap cannon for a receiving belt or other destination; the detailed throw settings are on this control screen.
</section>

## Where Should You Aim the Scanner's Metal Output?

Use Show Range to preview where the cannon can throw scrap metal, then adjust Tube Direction and How Far It Throws. The control panel may initially show a throw distance of 3.00 m, but that is a setting, not a required distance for every factory. A Smelter is one possible destination for scrap, while a short receiving conveyor can help bridge an awkward gap. Needles are collected in the drawer; they are not ejected from this tube.

The important test is where the material actually lands, not just where the tube appears to point. The Scanner can throw scrap onto a receiving conveyor, including one at a different height. Position the receiving belt inside the landing area and check a real discharge before relying on the route.

If scrap misses the receiving belt, change one cannon setting at a time and watch the next ejected piece. Hay added to a belt downstream of the Scanner skips inspection, so pay attention to where any new feed joins the route.

You do not need to rebuild the whole factory to correct a poor landing spot. First give the rejected metal a reliable route and keep the outgoing hay moving toward its intended destination.

<section id="needle-drawer" className="scroll-mt-24">

## What Happens When the Scanner Finds a Needle?

The Scanner Mk I checks 90 strands of hay at a time, with a listed throughput of 1,200 strands per minute. Its needle drawer holds eight needles. When it detects a needle, it stops the belt until the drawer is emptied; the scrap cannon does not shoot that needle out.

That pause is not the same as an electrical failure. If the line stops at the Scanner, press E to inspect its drawer, check whether a needle is present, and clear the drawer as directed by the machine. Check whether the line resumes before changing your generators or dismantling the conveyor. A status such as "0 needles in the tray" only describes the current drawer, not the contents of the whole haystack.

This is also why the Scanner should sit before a route that could send unexamined material straight into production or sale. Its purpose is to interrupt the flow when there is something you need to recover, not simply to make hay move faster.

An idle Scanner does not prove there are no needles left in the pile. It only examines hay that actually passes through it, so a moving belt is not the same as having searched the entire haystack.

## Is the Metal Detector the Same as the Scanner?

No. The handheld **Metal Detector** costs **$390** and beeps faster as you approach a hidden needle. Its stated range is about **seven meters in open space**, but the hay muffles the signal to roughly **one meter inside the pile**. A belt-mounted Scanner examines passing material instead of helping you choose where to dig.

That limitation matters when you are standing beside a huge stack. Silence does not mean the pile is empty. Remove a layer and sweep the exposed area again if you are checking a promising location. Walking around the outside once cannot rule out needles buried farther inside.

You do not need to wait for the Metal Detector to notice every visible needle. A glowing Cactus Needle can be picked up during early digging, before the detector is purchased. Its listed effect makes Rakes, Arms, and Drones work 15% faster. The handheld detector can help with the next search; the fixed Scanner protects hay that is already moving through your factory.

Check your Needle Cabinet after collecting a new type. The Scanner's drawer is a temporary place to recover detected needles; it is not the same screen as your overall needle collection.

If you are collecting all 24 needles, use the <Link href="/find-the-needle/achievements">needle collection achievements and 60-achievement checklist</Link> to track the remaining goals.

## Why Is My Scanner Not Working?

A Scanner can be locked, short of building materials, backlogged with hay, or deliberately stopped by a detected needle. Each state calls for a different fix.

**The Scanner is missing from your available buildings.** Check whether you have finished the first 20-Bale order and reopened the build menu. Unlocking early conveyor equipment alone is not the same as completing the delivery.

**It is available, but you cannot afford to place it.** Check both the $250 cash cost and the two Straw Panels. Make sure the panels have entered the Depot; panels still traveling down a sale belt are not necessarily available for construction.

**Metal lands on the floor.** Use Show Range and adjust the output tube's direction or distance. Watch a real discharge before deciding the receiving belt is correctly aligned.

**The belt stops while the Scanner is in use.** Press E on the Scanner and check the needle drawer before troubleshooting electricity. A detected needle intentionally stops the belt until the drawer is emptied.

**The machine has no power, or another processing machine reports a jam.** These are separate from the Scanner holding a belt for a detected needle. Follow the <Link href="/find-the-needle/automation">power and machine-jam troubleshooting steps</Link> to restore the affected line.

**Hay is queuing before the Scanner, but its drawer is empty.** The Mk I is rated for 1,200 strands per minute; a busy line can feed it faster than it can scan. Check its Scan Batch Size and Scan Cycle Speed upgrades before treating the slowdown as a power failure.

If the machine is scanning but you still have not found a needle, continue working through the hay instead of assuming that a single pass or the absence of a warning means every needle is accounted for.

## How Many Scanners Should You Build First?

Start with **one Scanner** on a route that carries hay toward your processing equipment or sale point. Confirm that its hay input, needle drawer, and scrap output work as intended before adding another. Four Scanners can be used in a larger layout, but that is an example rather than a required starting configuration.

Add another Scanner when you create a separate hay route that needs inspection. Check that each route passes through a Scanner before it reaches a destination, and test the scrap output and drawer on every new unit. The number of collecting machines does not, by itself, tell you how many Scanners to build.

### Can You Upgrade the Scanner?

The technology entry lists **Scan Batch Size** and **Scan Cycle Speed** upgrades. Initially, each next upgrade costs **$40**: the displayed batch-size upgrade adds **25%**, while the cycle-speed upgrade makes scanning **9% faster**. Higher levels can remain locked until you complete a later order such as Wrapped Bales. If hay is building up at an otherwise working Scanner, check these upgrades before expanding to another scanning route.
