<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
To start Main Sequence, scan nearby asteroids and collect Titanium Ore, Nickel Ore, and Water Ice with your starting ship. Craft basic plates, Hull Plates, Lightweight Frames, and Hydrogen Fuel aboard the ship. Build your Starter Station within 1 km of a large Titanium asteroid, fuel its Reactor, and construct a Material Science Lab. Research Logistics I to unlock Conveyor Belts, then Mining Drones to automate Titanium collection. Connect your first Refineries and Assemblers before expanding toward Silica, Basic Circuits, and Computation Science.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

**Main Sequence starts with a mining ship, a small collection of resources, and a mission to establish humanity's presence in the solar system.** Your first challenge is not building the largest factory possible. It is creating a station that can produce materials, power itself, and continue researching without requiring you to mine every asteroid manually.

The opening missions introduce these systems in a useful order, but it is easy to get stuck when the next machine needs a component you have not crafted, or when a new production line stops because the station has run out of fuel.

<section id="first-steps" className="scroll-mt-24">

## What Should You Do First in Main Sequence?

Follow the opening missions rather than trying to unlock every available technology immediately.

The early objective is to establish a reliable source of Titanium, produce basic construction materials, and unlock the equipment that replaces manual mining and crafting.

The opening has two turning points. First, collect enough materials to build and power a Starter Station. Then use Logistics I and Mining Drones to replace the manual Titanium supply.

Once Hull Plates and Material Science are being produced at the station, you can spend more time exploring new resources instead of returning to the starting asteroids for every construction project.

You do not need a completed Shipyard, a large combat ship, or a remote production network before your first station can function.

Get the initial manufacturing and research systems working first. Your later expansion will be much easier when the station is already producing the materials needed to build new equipment.

<section id="first-mining-trip" className="scroll-mt-24">

## How Do You Mine Your First Resources?

You begin aboard a small ship in an asteroid field.

The initial objectives introduce resource scanning, shooting resource asteroids, and collecting the resulting materials.

### Find Titanium, Nickel, and Water Ice

Enter the pilot seat and follow the current mission objective.

Hold **V** to scan for nearby resources.

The first materials to prioritize are:

- **Titanium Ore:** Used to produce Titanium Plates and several early construction components.
- **Nickel Ore:** Needed for Nickel Plates and additional machinery components.
- **Water Ice:** Used to produce Hydrogen Fuel.

Start with the nearby Titanium asteroid, then collect Nickel and Water Ice.

You do not need to empty every asteroid before returning to your ship's Workstation. Collect enough materials for the current objective, then make a little extra for the machines you will construct next.

### How Do You Collect the Ore?

Approach a resource asteroid and fire your **Plasma Cannon with LMB** to break off mineable materials.

Use the **Tractor Beam with RMB** to collect the floating resources into your ship's inventory.

If the material is not being collected, check whether your ship is close enough and has available inventory capacity.

Press **Tab** to inspect what you have collected.

When you have the required resources, leave the pilot seat and head toward the crafting equipment aboard the ship.

### Should You Mine Extra Resources Before Building?

Collecting a modest reserve of Titanium, Nickel, and Water Ice is useful.

The first Reactor, Material Science Lab, station modifications, and Conveyor connections all require manufactured materials.

If you mine only the exact amount requested by each mission, you may need to undock and repeat the same short trip several times.

However, do not fill every inventory slot before your first station is built. Leave room to collect and move finished construction materials.

</section>

<section id="ship-crafting" className="scroll-mt-24">

## How Do You Craft Your First Materials?

Your starting ship contains equipment for refining and assembling items.

Use it to turn collected resources into the components required for the Starter Station and its first machines.

### Craft Plates Before Advanced Components

Open the ship's crafting equipment.

Begin by producing the plates needed for the current missions.

Typical early materials include:

- Titanium Plates
- Nickel Plates
- Hull Plates
- Lightweight Frames
- Magnetic Coils
- Capacitors
- Basic Motors

Some components depend on materials produced by the other crafting machine.

If a recipe is unavailable or you cannot craft the requested quantity, check its ingredients before collecting more raw ore.

You may simply need to finish an earlier plate recipe.

### Make Hydrogen Fuel From Water Ice

Water Ice provides the starting source of Hydrogen Fuel.

Produce a reserve before building the station's first Reactor.

This matters because the Reactor needs fuel before it can power your Science Lab and other machines.

Hydrogen Fuel is also used by the ship's ordinary power system.

Do not spend your entire Water Ice supply without leaving enough fuel for the next stage.

### Why Are Your Crafting Materials Missing?

Check where the items are stored.

Materials can be in your personal inventory, the ship's inventory, or one of the onboard machines.

If you cannot build a station component even though you previously crafted its ingredients, confirm that the materials are accessible to the building operation.

Check completed production queues as well.

An item that has finished crafting may still need to be collected from its machine.

</section>

<section id="starter-station" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Where Should You Build Your First Station?

**Build your Starter Station within 1 km of a large Titanium asteroid.**

This is both an opening mission requirement and an important decision for your early mining operation.

The station will eventually use Mining Drones to collect resources from nearby asteroids. A station placed too far from its intended Titanium source will make that transition harder.

### Check the Area Before Placing the Station

Open the map and inspect the surrounding asteroid field.

Look for a location with access to:

- A large Titanium asteroid
- Nearby Nickel
- Reachable Water Ice
- Enough space to extend the station later

You do not need every resource immediately beside the station, but having the early essentials nearby reduces repeated manual trips.

Avoid placing the station right against an asteroid or another object if that would make docking and expansion awkward.

### Place and Dock With the Starter Station

Return to the pilot seat and open the building interface.

Select **Starter Station**.

Position it within the required distance of the large Titanium asteroid, then complete construction.

Access the station's design interface.

The Station Designer allows you to add and rearrange station modules.

Early expansion can include a Command Module and additional rooms such as a 2×1 station section.

Before applying a modification, check the building materials required.

If you are short of Hull Plates or Lightweight Frames, return to your ship or crafting equipment and produce the missing items.

**Leave walking space between early machines.**

It is tempting to fill every available floor tile with Refineries, but you will soon need room for Conveyors, Assemblers, and more production equipment.

An empty strip of floor can be more useful than squeezing in one extra machine that blocks your route through the station.

## How Do You Power the Station and Start Research?

Your first station needs two important machines:

**Reactor → Material Science Lab**

The Reactor provides the power. The Science Lab converts supported research items into Material Science.

### Build and Fuel the First Reactor

Open the station's building menu and select a Reactor.

If the Reactor requires Magnetic Coils or other components you do not have, craft those items before returning.

Place the Reactor in an accessible location.

Then open its interface and insert **Hydrogen Fuel**.

Do not assume that building a Reactor automatically provides electricity. The Reactor must have fuel.

Once it is running, the station has a power source for its first research and production equipment.

### Build the Material Science Lab

Next, construct a Material Science Lab.

The opening construction process uses materials such as Lightweight Frames and Capacitors.

Leave space near the lab for a future Conveyor connection.

Initially, you can supply its research materials manually.

Later, you can connect an automated production line and stop carrying items into the lab yourself.

### How Do You Start Logistics I Research?

Open the Research Tree with **T**.

Select **Logistics I** and add it to the Research Queue.

In one Early Access build, **Logistics I costs 5 Material Science**. Check the requirement in your current Research Tree before adding it to the queue.

Supply the Material Science Lab with a supported item, such as Hull Plates, and wait for the required Science to be generated.

Once the research completes, Conveyor equipment becomes available.

If the progress bar is not moving, check three things:

1. The lab has power.
2. It is receiving an accepted research item.
3. Logistics I is selected in the Research Queue.

A technology sitting in the queue does not generate research points on its own.

When Logistics I is finished and the next technology requires an unfamiliar Science type, [check what unlocks it and which lab produces it](/main-sequence/research-progression).

<section id="first-automation" className="scroll-mt-24">

## How Do You Build Your First Automated Production Line?

After Logistics I is unlocked, you can begin replacing manual material transfers with Conveyor Belts.

The first useful application is keeping your station supplied with Hydrogen Fuel.

### Connect Water Ice to Hydrogen Fuel Production

Place a Refinery and select its Hydrogen Fuel recipe.

    Water Ice before connecting its output to
    the Reactor.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

The basic production flow is:

**Water Ice → Refinery → Hydrogen Fuel → Reactor**

Use Conveyor Belts to connect the appropriate inputs and outputs.

Check that the source is supplying Water Ice and that the Refinery has the required power.

If Water Ice is being unloaded from your ship, inspect the Dock's resource connection and ensure that it supplies the intended line.

You should not need to refill the Reactor by hand every time if the connected fuel production is working.

### Why Is Hydrogen Fuel Not Reaching the Reactor?

Check that the Refinery has Water Ice, its recipe is set to Hydrogen Fuel, and the output connection leads toward the Reactor.

If the Reactor has already run out of fuel, insert Hydrogen Fuel manually to restore power while you repair the supply.

For incorrect ports, Conveyor connections, and recurring fuel shortages, use the [Main Sequence factory automation guide](/main-sequence/factory-automation#power-and-fuel).

### When Should You Expand the Room?

Expand once the first equipment is operational and you know which additional machines you need.

Leave space for multiple Refineries, one or more Assemblers, and Containers that collect finished materials.

Do not build a complicated production network before confirming that a single input reaches the correct machine.

A simple working line is a better starting point than a large layout that is difficult to diagnose.

</section>

<section id="mining-drones" className="scroll-mt-24">

## How Do You Unlock and Use Mining Drones?

**Mining Drones are the next important step because they reduce repeated manual asteroid mining.**

After Logistics I, research Mining Drones in the Research Tree.

In the same Early Access research tree, **Mining Drones costs 15 Material Science**. Use the requirement displayed in your current game if it differs.

### Build Your First Mining Drone Bay

After researching the technology, open the Station Designer and add a Mining Drone Bay.

Position it so that its resource target is within range.

Your first Titanium asteroid should already be close to the Starter Station if you followed its placement requirement.

Make sure the Drone Bay has power before expecting it to collect resources.

### Select Titanium Ore

Open the Mining Drone Bay's configuration and select **Titanium Ore**.

Once the equipment is powered and the target is accessible, the drones can begin collecting the selected resource.

Watch whether ore arrives at the Drone Bay.

This is the important transition from manual resource collection to continuous station production.

### Why Are Mining Drones Not Working?

Check the basics before moving the entire station.

- **No power:** Inspect the Reactor and station power supply.
- **Wrong resource:** Open the Drone Bay and select the intended ore.
- **No useful target:** Check whether the correct asteroid is within range.
- **No ore leaving the Bay:** Inspect its resource output and connected Conveyor.
- **Production stops downstream:** Check the receiving Refinery rather than assuming the drones have failed.

If the drones are collecting ore but your factory is not producing anything, the missing step is likely between the Drone Bay and the processing machines.

</section>

<section id="first-working-factory" className="scroll-mt-24">

## What Should Your First Working Factory Produce?

Once Titanium arrives automatically, use it to support construction and ongoing research.

The first goal is not to manufacture every available item.

Concentrate on the basic components you repeatedly need.

### Automate Titanium Plates and Hull Plates

Set up Refineries to process incoming Titanium Ore.

Then connect the resulting plates to an Assembler that produces Hull Plates.

A basic flow is:

**Mining Drone Bay → Refinery → Titanium Plates → Assembler → Hull Plates**

Add a Container where you need stored materials.

Hull Plates can then support construction and Material Science production.

After the line begins working, inspect both the input and output.

If the Assembler receives material but its products accumulate without reaching the intended destination, check its output connection.

### Keep Material Science Running

Your research will slow whenever the Science Lab stops receiving suitable items.

A useful next step is to connect a reliable Hull Plate supply to the Material Science Lab.

This lets you continue researching without constantly moving items between machines.

However, do not send every finished Hull Plate into research if you still need some for station construction.

Keep a useful reserve available for your next expansion.

To keep Hull Plates available for construction, [set a Container Buffer](/main-sequence/factory-automation#storage-buffer-limit) before sending the rest into research.

### What Else Should You Produce Early?

Once the Titanium line works, prepare supplies of components you frequently use:

- Lightweight Frames for construction
- Basic Motors for logistics equipment
- Magnetic Coils for machines and power-related construction
- Capacitors for additional equipment
- Nickel Plates for recipes that require Nickel

You do not need to automate all these items simultaneously.

Choose the component you are repeatedly running out of and expand that supply first.

### How Can You Tell the First Production Loop Is Working?

Let the Mining Drone Bay deliver another batch of Titanium Ore without manually refilling the machines. The Refinery should continue producing Titanium Plates, the Assembler should turn them into Hull Plates, and the Material Science counter should increase when the lab receives its research items.

A full Container does not prove that production is continuing. If the next batch fails to arrive, find the stalled connection before expanding the station.

</section>

<section id="silica-and-circuits" className="scroll-mt-24">

## What Should You Do After Automating Titanium?

Your next major requirement is moving beyond the opening Titanium production system.

**Silica, Silicon Wafers, and Basic Circuits** become important as you prepare for Computation Science and more advanced equipment.

### Find a Silica Asteroid

Return to your ship and use the resource scanner to locate Silica.

If the nearest useful asteroid is too far from the main station for reliable local mining, consider establishing another small outpost.

Do not assume every new resource must be processed at your original station.

### Build a Small Silica Outpost

Find a suitable location near the resource and construct another Starter Station.

At first, this outpost only needs the systems required for its job.

Avoid spending materials on a large factory before you have confirmed that it can collect the intended resource.

A useful naming convention is to identify stations by their purpose, such as **Silica 1**.

That makes them easier to recognize later when configuring cargo deliveries.

### Why Does the Second Outpost Have No Production?

A newly built Silica Outpost may have a Mining Drone Bay but no working power supply. The station itself being constructed does not mean the drones can start mining.

Check that the Drone Bay is powered, Silica is selected, and the intended asteroid is within range. Confirm that the station begins collecting material before arranging regular deliveries to your main factory.

Once the resource supply works, use the [ship logistics guide](/main-sequence/ship-logistics) to set up automated transport.

### Prepare Silicon Wafers and Basic Circuits

Silica supports Silicon Wafer production.

Basic Circuits then become an important component for the next stage of research and station equipment.

You can begin with a small manually supplied operation before committing to a permanent transport network.

Once you have the necessary materials, unlock the next research technologies and prepare for Computation Science.

If Computation Science is your next target, [check its prerequisite research and lab requirements](/main-sequence/research-progression#early-research) before expanding the electronics factory.

</section>

<section id="ship-and-transport" className="scroll-mt-24">

## When Should You Build Another Ship or Automate Deliveries?

A second ship becomes useful when your original vessel is handling too many unrelated jobs.

For example, you may want to explore new sectors while a cargo ship moves Silica between stations.

You do not need a large combat vessel to establish the first cargo route.

### Build a Shipyard When You Need More Than the Starting Ship

Research the Shipyard technology and prepare the construction materials for a Shipyard Core.

The Shipyard allows you to construct or modify ships for different roles.

A mining ship benefits from resource collection equipment, while a transport vessel benefits from Containers, propulsion, and docking capability.

If your upgraded ship exceeds the available construction limit, [check the Shipyard mass limit and module choices](/main-sequence/ship-design#mass-and-thrusters).

### When Is a Cargo Ship Worth Building?

Your first few Silica deliveries can be made manually. A cargo ship becomes more useful when repeated trips between the outpost and the main station interrupt mining, research, or construction.

Once both stations can load and receive cargo, a transport ship can collect Silica while you work elsewhere. Configure the pickup Dock, unloading destination, and any necessary fuel stops using the [Autopilot cargo route steps](/main-sequence/ship-logistics#first-shipping-route).

Test the first delivery before depending on the imported Silica for Silicon Wafer production.

</section>

<section id="beginner-troubleshooting" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Why Are You Stuck in the Main Sequence Tutorial?

The opening missions combine mining, crafting, research, construction, and power.

If progress stops, identify which system the current objective is asking you to use.

Press **G** to review the mission requirements before changing your station.

  columns={beginnerProblemColumns}
  rows={beginnerProblemRows}
/>

### Why Does the Tutorial Still Say a Conveyor Is Not Connected?

If the mission does not advance after you place a Conveyor Belt, open the objective with **G** and check which connection is required.

Inspect the belt between the Dock, Refinery, and Reactor. Make sure the correct resource is moving through the intended ports rather than merely passing beside the machine.

If the required connection appears complete but the objective still does not register, check the final belt segment. Replacing an incorrectly connected segment resolved this issue during an early station setup.

Do not rebuild the entire production room before checking the specific connection named by the mission.

For wider production problems, see the [factory automation guide](/main-sequence/factory-automation). If research is the remaining obstacle, use the [research progression guide](/main-sequence/research-progression).

</section>

<section id="where-to-go-next" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Where Should You Go Next After the First Factory?

Your next destination depends on what the Starter Station can already supply.

If Titanium Plates and Hull Plates are accumulating but Silicon Wafers are unavailable, locate Silica and establish a way to bring it back. When several machines begin competing for the same materials, inspect their input supply and use [Buffer and production controls](/main-sequence/factory-automation#storage-buffer-limit) to prevent one line from consuming everything.

When your next machine is still locked, open the Research Tree and identify the missing Science type. Computation Science leads into more advanced technologies, while later Electromagnetic, Quantum, and Astronomic research requires additional equipment and materials. Follow the [research requirements and unlock order](/main-sequence/research-progression) when the next Science type becomes your obstacle.

Travel creates a different set of decisions. Upgrade your existing ship when it only needs a Mining Laser or Warp Drive; consider a new design when cargo storage, weapons, and propulsion no longer fit comfortably together. The [Shipyard and module options](/main-sequence/ship-design) become important at that point. If repeated journeys between outposts consume too much time, [automate those deliveries](/main-sequence/ship-logistics) rather than continuing to carry every load yourself.

</section>
