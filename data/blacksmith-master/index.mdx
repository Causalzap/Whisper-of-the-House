<GuideQuickAnswer title="What Should You Do First in Blacksmith Master?">
Hire your first Blacksmith, complete the tutorial's **Mace Merchant Offer** for 80 Gold, then unlock **Carpentry** and place a Carpentry Workbench. Keep accepting orders you can finish with your available materials. Expand into Assistants and resource gathering as your workload grows, but avoid hiring several employees before you can cover their wages. Open the Store when you can supply products consistently, then add Racks, a Checkout Counter, a Cashier, and workers assigned to produce retail stock.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

Your first Mace order pays **80 Gold**, while the Carpentry Workbench costs **300 Gold**. That difference makes the opening decisions important: hiring another worker or buying a new station can use money you still need for materials and wages.

Keep the first Blacksmith working on achievable Merchant Offers before expanding. Once the workshop is earning consistently, decide whether its next limitation is the available equipment, raw materials, or the number of items workers can finish each day.

## What Should You Do on Your First Day?

The opening tutorial teaches the basic production and selling loop.

Follow these steps before attempting a large expansion:

### Step 1 — Hire Your First Blacksmith

Open the hiring menu when the tutorial asks you to recruit a Blacksmith.

You will see candidates with different skills and traits.

For your first worker, favor someone who can handle the equipment and materials already available in the workshop.

A faster worker can spend less time traveling between tasks. A worker with better Metalworking can be more useful when much of your initial output involves metal items.

However, a candidate with one excellent trait may also have an inconvenient negative trait.

Compare the complete profile before hiring.

This is your first dependable way to earn money.

The amount is small, but it establishes the process you will use throughout the game:

**Accept an offer → craft the requested goods → complete the offer → claim the reward.**

Later orders require more items and sometimes additional production capabilities.

### Step 3 — Unlock Carpentry

After the first order, open the Mastery Tree.

The tutorial introduces Mastery progression and provides an early unlock for **Carpentry**.

Carpentry makes wooden items available.

Place a Carpentry Workbench so your Blacksmith can begin using the new capability.

The tutorial's Carpentry Workbench is priced at **300 Gold** in the 1.0 opening sequence.

Once it is installed, wooden products give your workshop another source of items for Merchant Offers.

You no longer have to rely entirely on metalworking.

### Step 4 — Continue With Achievable Merchant Offers

Accept orders that match your current production capabilities.

At the beginning, products such as Maces, Wooden Clubs, and other basic items are easier to supply than advanced items whose materials or equipment are still locked.

Before accepting an order, check the requested item and quantity.

If an offer requires a material you cannot produce or purchase conveniently, another available order may be more practical.

You can revisit more demanding products after unlocking the necessary equipment.

## How Do You Choose the Best Workers Early?

Hiring decisions become important long before you reach the advanced employee systems.

Every worker has a hiring cost, an ongoing wage, skills, and traits.

A worker who looks inexpensive at first can become costly if you hire several people before you have enough work for them.

### Which Blacksmith Stats Matter Most?

Early Blacksmiths can differ in:

- **Metalworking:** How efficiently they work with metal.
- **Carpentry / Woodworking:** How efficiently they handle wooden products.
- **Movement:** How quickly they travel between tasks.
- **Traits:** Positive and negative effects that change performance or costs.

A useful starting approach is to hire one reasonably balanced Blacksmith, then specialize later as your production expands.

If you add a second Blacksmith, you can choose someone whose strengths complement the first.

For example, a stronger Woodworking employee can concentrate on wooden items while a Metalworking specialist handles metal products.

This keeps the two workers from always competing for the same crafting jobs.

However, do not immediately hire a third or fourth worker because you have unlocked additional space.

Check whether the current Blacksmiths are busy and whether the workshop can provide enough materials for more production.

### Should You Choose Faster Workers?

Movement can be valuable because workers repeatedly travel between equipment, materials, and finished items.

A faster worker is often attractive when the workshop is growing and tasks require frequent walking.

But movement is not the only consideration.

For an employee spending most of the day performing a specialized action, the skill or trait affecting that action can also matter.

Choose the worker whose strengths match the job you actually need completed.

### Should You Use Manual or Automatic Level-Ups?

Workers gain experience by performing their jobs.

As they level up, their available skills improve.

The hiring and worker information screens let you choose a level-up strategy, including manual distribution and automatic options.

Manual leveling is useful when you want to concentrate improvements on a particular task.

For example, a Blacksmith who primarily makes wooden products can benefit from focusing on Woodworking rather than spreading every improvement equally.

Automatic leveling is more convenient when you do not want to manage each employee individually.

You can start with a simple automatic strategy and switch when an important worker needs more specialized development.

### When Should You Hire an Assistant?

Assistants become useful once your business has more carrying and resource-handling work.

They can transport completed items, restock resources, and operate Smelting equipment.

The important question is not how many Assistants you can afford, but what task currently needs assistance.

For the full explanation of Assistant jobs, material transport, and production assignments, see the <Link href="/blacksmith-master/workshop-layout">workshop layout and production guide</Link>.

## How Do You Make Money in Blacksmith Master?

At the beginning, **Merchant Offers are your primary source of income**.

The Store provides another income source later, but your first workshop must be supported through orders.

The key is to consider both the reward and what it costs to produce the requested goods.

### Choose Offers You Can Finish Reliably

Not every Merchant Offer deserves the same priority.

When choosing between available orders, consider:

1. **Reward:** How much Gold or other progress you receive.
2. **Materials:** Whether you already have enough resources.
3. **Crafting requirements:** Whether the required equipment is available.
4. **Production time:** How much of your workforce the order will occupy.
5. **Other rewards:** Whether the offer provides a new Blueprint, worker, or decoration.

An offer with a large Gold reward is not necessarily the best immediate choice if it will prevent your workers from completing anything else for several days.

Smaller orders can be useful when you need money quickly or want to continue progressing toward Merchant Offer milestones.

As your workshop becomes more capable, larger orders become easier to accept.

### Should You Focus on Wooden Items Early?

Wooden items can be a useful early production option because Carpentry becomes available very quickly.

They also allow you to use a different workstation while Metalworking is occupied.

Once you have access to a dependable Wood supply, wooden products can support regular orders without requiring every item to consume Ingots.

That does not mean you should abandon Metalworking.

A workshop producing both wooden and metal items has more flexibility when available Merchant Offers change.

Use whichever group of items you can manufacture efficiently with your current resources.

### Are Merchant Offers Only for Gold?

No.

Some offers reward more than money.

Depending on the available offer, rewards can include new Blueprint opportunities, Elite Workers, decorations, or other progression benefits.

This matters when you are deciding whether to take a smaller payment.

If your business is already profitable but you need new products, an offer with a useful Blueprint reward may be worth more than an ordinary Gold-only offer.

Inspect the reward before accepting the order.

Do not assume that completing more of the same inexpensive order will automatically unlock every new item.

### Should You Take Timed Merchant Offers?

Timed offers add a deadline to the order.

They are useful when you have the necessary materials, available workers, and enough time to produce the requested goods.

If the order is too large for your current capacity, you risk receiving a reduced reward after missing the deadline.

A practical approach is to accept a timed offer when the production requirements are already familiar.

Do not make an unfamiliar advanced item your first timed order merely because its displayed reward looks attractive.

One successful timed order also contributes to the corresponding Steam achievement.

For that completion requirement, see the <Link href="/blacksmith-master/achievements">Achievements checklist</Link>.

## Why Are You Losing Money Every Day?

A negative daily result does not always mean your workshop has stopped working.

You may have spent money on equipment, recruitment, or resources that will help later.

However, repeated losses are a warning when the business is not producing enough completed orders to pay its ongoing expenses.

### Check Wages Before Hiring More Employees

Every additional worker increases ongoing costs.

Higher-level or more specialized employees can cost more than basic workers.

Before recruiting someone, ask what new work they will perform.

If you already have two Blacksmiths waiting for materials, a third Blacksmith is unlikely to solve that immediate problem.

If your Merchant Offers are completed regularly but finished goods are waiting for delivery, an Assistant may be more useful.

Spend money where it can remove the limitation you have actually observed.

### Separate One-Time Spending From Daily Expenses

A new Workbench, Furnace, or building expansion is a one-time purchase.

Worker wages and repeated resource purchases continue to affect your finances.

This distinction is important.

You might have a temporarily expensive day because you built additional equipment. That is different from operating with too many employees and continually failing to earn enough to pay them.

Watch the daily financial summary and compare income with ongoing expenses after the expansion is complete.

For example, one Day 30 report shows **2,430 Gold from Merchant Offers** and **305 Gold from item sales**, but also **2,218 Gold in wages**, **800 Gold in purchases**, and **49 Gold in amenities**. The workshop earned 2,735 Gold and spent 3,067 Gold, leaving a **332 Gold loss**.

This does not automatically mean the business is permanently unprofitable. Check what was purchased that day, then compare ordinary income against recurring wages and other operating costs before paying for another expansion.

### Avoid Purchasing Resources Indefinitely

Purchasing basic materials is useful while your resource production remains locked.

It can also keep an important order moving when you temporarily run short.

But if you must repeatedly buy the same material to maintain ordinary production, consider whether it is time to establish a renewable supply.

The Oak Forest and Iron Mine become increasingly valuable as the workshop grows.

Their unlock order and later resource progression are covered in the <Link href="/blacksmith-master/mastery-tree">Mastery Tree guide</Link>.

### What If You Have Almost No Gold Left?

Start with the work you can finish without another major purchase.

Check for an accepted Merchant Offer that is close to completion.

Use your existing staff and equipment to finish products whose resources are already available.

Avoid refreshing hiring candidates or buying new equipment unless that expense is required to finish an order.

Once a payment arrives, restore enough operating funds for your essential materials and wages before expanding again.

If you have entered debt, treat returning to sustainable production as the immediate goal rather than opening another expensive system.

## What Are the Best First Upgrades?

After Carpentry, choose your next unlock based on what is currently limiting your workshop.

- **Assistants:** Prioritize them when carrying finished goods or handling materials is interrupting crafting.
- **Construction:** Choose it when the starting room cannot accommodate the equipment you need next.
- **Oak Forest:** Consider it when you regularly use Oak Wood and want to reduce repeated purchases.
- **Iron Mine:** Prioritize it when Iron Ore purchases are becoming an ongoing expense and you are ready to process the Ore.
- **Store:** Open retail sales when you can regularly produce items without leaving existing Merchant Offers unfinished.

You do not need to unlock all five in one fixed order. For example, a workshop that makes mostly wooden products may benefit from the Oak Forest before investing heavily in retail.

Before choosing the next node, check the connected prerequisites and your current crafting-tier requirement. The <Link href="/blacksmith-master/mastery-tree">Mastery Tree guide</Link> covers the full unlock order, resource branches, and later progression decisions.

</section>

## How Do You Open Your First Store?

After unlocking **Store** in the Mastery Tree, you can begin selling finished items to customers.

This is a separate activity from Merchant Offers.

Customers purchase items displayed on Racks, and a Cashier handles payment at the Checkout Counter.

### Step 1 — Place a Checkout Counter

Open the Store building options and place a Checkout Counter.

Make sure customers can reach it.

You will need a Cashier to handle purchases.

Having a Counter without an available Cashier does not create a functioning checkout.

### Step 2 — Place a Suitable Rack

Choose a Rack that can display an item you already know how to make.

Different display types support different products.

For example, a basic tool or utensil may require a different type of display from a Shield.

Once the Rack is placed, select the product you want it to sell.

A Rack without a suitable assigned item will not help your Store earn money.

### Step 3 — Hire a Cashier

Hire a Cashier and make sure they can work at the Checkout Counter.

Cashiers have skills related to checkout performance and customer service.

At the beginning, one working Counter and Cashier provide a sensible starting point.

Add more checkout capacity when actual customer queues justify it.

### Step 4 — Produce Items for Racks

Your Blacksmiths can work toward different destinations.

After opening the Store, make sure some crafting output is assigned to **Craft for Racks**.

Otherwise, workers may continue filling Merchant Offers while the Store has no products available.

Completed retail items also need to be delivered to the correct Racks.

For the exact worker assignments and transport checks, see the <Link href="/blacksmith-master/workshop-layout">Store Rack restocking section</Link>.

### Step 5 — Confirm the First Sale

Watch the Store after the required items are available.

A successful sale requires a customer to find an item, choose to buy it, and complete checkout.

Check whether the number of items sold increases.

If customers enter but the Store earns nothing, first confirm that products are displayed and the Cashier is operating.

Do not buy several more Racks until this basic selling loop works.

## How Should You Price Items in Blacksmith Master?

Once you can edit product prices, use the **Blueprints** menu to adjust the selling price of individual items.

  />

The price you set affects how attractive the product is to customers.

Higher prices can increase revenue per item sold, but they can also make purchases less likely.

Lower prices can encourage sales, although each sale brings in less money.

The best decision depends on actual demand and your ability to keep the product stocked.

### Should You Increase Every Price Immediately?

Not necessarily.

Start by observing how your current products sell.

If a Rack empties quickly and your workshop struggles to replace the items, the product may be selling faster than you can supply it.

A small price increase can be worth testing.

If products remain on display for a long time despite having customers in the Store, a lower price may be more useful.

Do not adjust every item at the same time.

Change a small number of products, observe the next selling period, and compare the results.

This makes it easier to identify whether the change helped.

### Which Products Should You Sell First?

Start with products that meet three conditions:

1. You already own the Blueprint.
2. Your workshop can supply the required materials.
3. You can restock the product regularly.

You do not need the most expensive item in the game to operate a profitable small Store.

A simple product that sells consistently can be more useful than an advanced item whose materials remain unavailable.

As your research progresses and Blueprint quality improves, revisit the products you sell.

Improved products may support better selling prices, but remember that customers still respond to the price.

### How Do You Adjust Multiple Prices?

The Blueprints menu includes price controls for individual items and options to adjust prices across the selected tab.

This can be convenient when you have many products.

However, changing an entire category simultaneously makes it harder to judge individual demand.

For your first Store, start with a few products and make controlled adjustments.

Once you understand which products sell reliably, broader changes become easier to manage.

## How Do You Attract More Customers and Stop Them Leaving?

Opening a Store does not guarantee that every visitor will buy something.

Customer traffic, available stock, prices, and checkout service all affect how the Store performs.

The first step is to determine whether you have too few visitors or whether the visitors you already attract are leaving without purchasing.

### How Do You Increase Customer Numbers?

**Decorations** contribute to the Store's customer bonus. The building interface shows your current decoration value, its target, and the corresponding customer attraction bonus.

For example, a Store with 600 decoration value against a 500 target shows a **+100% customer bonus** in the building menu. Other available progression upgrades can also improve customer arrivals.

Add these when you have enough products available to benefit from more visitors.

If your Store is already running out of stock, increasing customer traffic may create additional pressure without producing a matching increase in sales.

Expand your retail inventory alongside customer attraction rather than focusing only on making the Store more decorative.

### Why Do Customers Leave Angry?

One possible cause is waiting too long at checkout.

Inspect the Checkout Counter when you see customers leaving after joining a queue.

Check whether the Cashier is actually serving visitors and whether the existing checkout capacity is appropriate for the number of customers.

If customers are not reaching checkout at all, check the availability and prices of your displayed products before adding another Cashier.

The late-game **Master Cashier** has a special ability that prevents patience loss at the register, but your first Store can operate without that specialist.

### More Customers but No More Sales?

Compare the number of visitors with the items actually sold. If decoration bonuses are bringing more shoppers into the Store but purchases remain low, inspect the products on your Racks and their prices before adding more decorations.

If the Racks empty quickly, your problem is different: the workshop may not be replacing stock fast enough. Restore the available products before attempting to attract another wave of customers.

</section>

## How Do You Keep Workers Happy?

Employee Happiness becomes increasingly important as your business grows.

Your staff need appropriate working conditions, and improving one factor may not compensate for neglecting every other part of their day.

Check the Happiness information instead of assuming that wages alone determine whether workers are satisfied.

### What Happens When Workers Become Excited?

The highest Happiness state shown in Staff Settings is **Excited**.

Excited workers lose the effect of their negative Trait and gain experience **20% faster**.

This makes Happiness useful for more than keeping employees satisfied. It can improve the performance of workers who would otherwise be limited by a negative Trait, while helping them level up faster.

Check the Happiness rating and its individual contributing factors before spending more Gold on additional staff.

### Which Happiness Setting Should You Improve First?

Open Staff Settings and compare the scores shown for working hours, salary bonuses, job security, seating comfort, and food and drinks.

For example, a workshop with **100% for working hours**, **84% for salary bonuses**, **100% for job security**, **56% for seating comfort**, and **67% for food and drinks** has a clearer problem with comfort and amenities than with its working schedule.

Start with the weakest score. Improve the available seating or food and drink options, then inspect the Happiness report again after workers have used the facilities. Raising a setting that is already at 100% will not address the lower scores elsewhere.

### How Does Job Security Affect Happiness?

Frequently firing workers reduces Job Security and makes remaining employees feel uncertain.

The Staff Settings screen tracks recent dismissals as part of the Happiness calculation.

If you are replacing several employees, consider the effect on the workers who remain. Hiring decisions are easier to manage when you avoid unnecessary recruitment and repeated dismissals.

### Working Hours and Wages

Working hours and wages are additional factors to monitor.

Longer working periods may increase available production time, but they can also make maintaining Happiness more difficult.

Likewise, reducing wages can improve short-term expenses while creating pressure elsewhere.

If your staff is becoming unhappy, examine the complete Happiness status before changing a single setting repeatedly.

A useful approach is to improve the weakest condition, then check how the status changes after the next working day.

### Should You Prioritize Happiness Before Expansion?

You do not need a perfectly decorated workshop before hiring your second employee.

However, a larger workforce requires more attention to comfort, food, wages, and working conditions.

If Happiness is already declining, fix the underlying issue before recruiting several additional workers.

A stable workforce is easier to manage than one that requires constant corrective spending.

## Can You Craft Items Yourself?

Yes. Blacksmith Master includes **Direct Control**, allowing you to take control of a Blacksmith and perform crafting actions yourself.

Select a worker and choose **Take Control**.

You can then move through the workshop and interact with the equipment required to make an item.

Direct Control is optional for normal progression.

You can manage the workshop through the regular employee assignments without manually crafting every product.

However, using it early can help you understand the actions that make up an item.

For example, seeing a Blacksmith heat an Ingot, work at an Anvil, and quench the result makes the basic metalworking process easier to understand.

You can return to normal workshop management after finishing the activity.

There is also a Steam achievement for crafting an item through Direct Control. Its requirements are included in the <Link href="/blacksmith-master/achievements">Achievements guide</Link>.

## What Should You Do After Your First Few Days?

After the first few profitable days, the next investment should address a limitation you can identify. Missing materials, equipment, or product categories point toward the <Link href="/blacksmith-master/mastery-tree">Mastery Tree unlocks</Link>; long Furnace queues, empty Ingot Storage, and undelivered products are problems to solve in the <Link href="/blacksmith-master/workshop-layout">production chain</Link>.

Later, the <Link href="/blacksmith-master/master-workers">six Master Workers</Link> open specialized abilities and Siege Machine production. If you want to finish every objective, the <Link href="/blacksmith-master/achievements">72-achievement checklist</Link> tracks the remaining requirements. A reliable workshop can move toward any of these goals without expanding every system at once.
