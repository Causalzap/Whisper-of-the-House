<section id="quick-answer" className="scroll-mt-24">
  <GuideQuickAnswer title="Spend Your Stars on Something That Wins the Fight">

Regent's strongest direction depends on what his current cards can support. Stars pay for powerful attacks, Forge builds Sovereign Blade, and card creation can provide additional Block, Strength, or damage. These systems can work together, but collecting setup cards without enough immediate output makes the deck unreliable.

Check which resource is limiting your turns. If Stars remain unused, look for a way to spend them. If Sovereign Blade is powerful but cannot be played alongside your defense, improve Energy efficiency. If card creation is already triggering useful payoffs, strengthen that interaction rather than starting another unfinished engine.

  </GuideQuickAnswer>

Regent plays differently from the other characters in *Slay the Spire 2* because Stars are a second resource that persists between turns. His Majesty can save them for expensive attacks, convert them into Energy, or build an entirely different damage plan through Forge and Sovereign Blade.

That flexibility is also where many Regent runs go wrong. A deck may generate plenty of Stars but have no useful way to spend them. Another may build an enormous Sovereign Blade but struggle to draw or pay for it. The decisions below focus on recognizing those problems before they end a run.

<h2 id="stars-vs-energy" className="scroll-mt-24">
  When Does Regent Need More Stars Instead of More Energy?
</h2>

Stars do not disappear at the end of your turn. Regent begins combat with three Stars from Divine Right, and cards such as Venerate can generate more.

Energy works differently. It refreshes each turn, and many useful cards still require Energy even when the deck has a large Star reserve.

This creates two distinct problems:

- **Star shortage:** You draw a powerful Star-cost card but cannot afford its Star requirement.
- **Energy shortage:** You already have enough Stars, but cannot play the attacks, defensive cards, or Powers that would spend them effectively.

Adding Venerate can solve the first problem. It does little for the second.

An early Regent deck often benefits from some additional Star generation, especially when its strongest cards spend Stars faster than the starting relic supplies them. However, it is possible to reach a point where another generator contributes nothing to the turn.

If you finish several turns with unspent Stars while important cards remain in your hand, the problem is unlikely to be Star production.

Look instead at Energy, card access, and the amount of damage or Block your current cards produce.

<h2 id="orbit-energy" className="scroll-mt-24">
  When Is Orbit Worth Taking or Upgrading?
</h2>

Orbit provides an answer when Regent can spend Stars effectively but needs more Energy to play the rest of the hand.

Its effect restores one Energy for every four Energy spent after Orbit is active. The regular card costs two Energy to establish, while Orbit+ costs one.

The upgrade matters because the Power can begin helping your deck with a smaller initial investment.

In one Regent A10 run, the player selected Orbit at a Merchant rather than spending Gold on an unrelated reward. The deck already had useful cards, but needed a better way to play them together.

Orbit is especially attractive when your deck regularly spends enough Energy to trigger it. Multiple useful cards, draw effects, and expensive attacks can all make that additional Energy valuable.

But Orbit is not another Star generator.

If your hand is full of Star-cost cards you cannot afford, Orbit will not directly fix that shortage. And if the deck has very little card draw, it may struggle to spend the additional Energy efficiently.

Before buying or upgrading Orbit, check whether your current turns are constrained by Energy rather than by the cards available to play.

<h2 id="sovereign-blade-forge" className="scroll-mt-24">
  When Should Regent Build Around Sovereign Blade?
</h2>

The first time you Forge during combat, Sovereign Blade is created directly in your hand. It has Retain, costs 2 Energy (1 when upgraded), and starts with 10 base damage.

The Forge effect that creates it immediately increases that damage. For example, Forge 8 creates a Sovereign Blade dealing 18 damage. Every later Forge adds more damage to the Blade during that combat.

This means Regent can build a large attack without necessarily adding multiple conventional damage cards.

However, increasing the Blade's damage is only half of the problem.

You must still be able to play it.

### Forge is useful when its cards do more than increase one attack

Cards such as Refine Blade, Spoils of Battle, and Bulwark allow Regent to develop the Blade while gaining other benefits.

Refine Blade provides Forge and additional Energy on the next turn. Spoils of Battle combines Forge with card draw. Bulwark supplies Block while increasing the Blade's damage.

Those secondary effects are important.

A deck that spends several turns Forges but cannot defend may die before the Blade becomes useful. A deck that gains Block, draws cards, or prepares Energy while Forging has a much more realistic path to using the attack.

### When is more Forge worse than making Sovereign Blade playable?

Because Sovereign Blade is created directly in your hand and has Retain, drawing it is not the initial problem. The real question is whether you can afford to play it without sacrificing defense.

If playing Sovereign Blade leaves too little Energy to survive the enemy's attack, another Forge card may increase its damage without making the turn safer.

Once Sovereign Blade has been played, access becomes a separate issue. Summon Forth can return existing Sovereign Blades to your hand while adding more Forge damage.

Before taking another Forge reward, check which problem remains:

- **Not enough damage:** More Forge or a damage-enhancing Blade interaction may help.
- **Not enough Energy:** A cost reduction or Energy support may make the existing Blade usable.
- **Difficulty playing the Blade again:** A retrieval effect such as Summon Forth can matter more than additional Forge.
- **Insufficient defense:** Bulwark can develop the Blade while protecting the turn.

A larger damage number is only useful when the deck can turn it into a successful attack.

<h2 id="regent-act-one" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Can Regent Use His Stars Effectively Before an Early Elite?
</h2>

Regent begins combat with three Stars, but those Stars do not guarantee a strong opening. The important question is whether the hand contains an attack or defensive card that can use the available resources.

In an A10 Underdocks run, the opening decisions included a choice between Fishing Rod's earlier value and Lava Rock's slower payoff. The route also offered several possible Elite encounters.

If Star generation occupies much of the hand but there is no useful spender, the extra resource does not solve the fight. If Sovereign Blade requires setup, make sure another attack or defensive card can keep the deck functional until Forge becomes useful.

A route containing several Elites may be rewarding, but the deck first needs a reliable way to convert its resources into immediate damage or Block.

<h2 id="block-versus-stars" className="scroll-mt-24">
  When Should Regent Take Block Instead of More Stars?
</h2>

Star generation loses value when your deck already has enough Stars to pay for its important cards. At that point, a defensive reward may improve more turns than another resource generator.

In one Regent run, the choice came down to additional Star production or a one-Energy card that could provide 22 Block in the current situation. The defensive card was selected because the deck already had the Stars it needed but could not reliably protect a weak draw.

The distinction matters when comparing Regent's defensive options.

**Bulwark** is particularly useful in a Forge deck because it provides Block while increasing Sovereign Blade's damage. **Particle Wall** can provide repeatable Block when the deck has enough Energy to use it. Other defensive cards may be more appropriate when a single immediate response is needed.

Before taking another Star generator, check whether your current turns already end with Stars left over. If they do, the next reward should usually improve how you spend your cards, survive dangerous attacks, or reach an existing payoff.

The same decision changes with the deck. A strong Block card is not automatically better than Venerate when your most important attacks still cannot be paid for.

<h2 id="regent-card-creation" className="scroll-mt-24">
  When Is Regent's Card Creation Engine Worth Building?
</h2>

Card creation becomes a dependable strategy when the deck already rewards you for creating or transforming cards.

**Spectrum Shift** adds a random Colorless card to your hand at the start of each turn. That can provide new options, but the card created is not guaranteed to solve the current fight.

The payoff becomes clearer when other cards are present:

- **Arsenal** gains Strength whenever you create a card.
- **Pillar of Creation** provides Block whenever you create a card.
- **Supermassive** gains damage from cards created during combat.

Transforming cards with **CHARGE!!** or **BEGONE!** can also activate card-creation effects. This allows Regent to combine a useful transformation with an existing Strength, Block, or damage payoff.

### When does CHARGE!! help an unfinished deck?

CHARGE!! transforms two chosen cards in your Draw Pile into Minion Dive Bombs. The transformed cards remain in the Draw Pile, so this does not immediately put two attacks into your hand.

In the November 2026 public beta, Minion Dive Bomb deals 11 damage, or 13 when upgraded, down from 13 and 16 previously.

Choose CHARGE!! when replacing weaker upcoming draws improves your damage or activates card-creation payoffs you already have.

Do not rely on it to repair an immediate hand that lacks Block. The new attacks still need to be drawn before they can be played.

A creation engine is strongest when its generated cards and trigger effects work together. Without Arsenal, Pillar of Creation, Supermassive, or another usable payoff, adding more creation effects may simply consume Energy without improving the dangerous turn.

<h2 id="regent-removal" className="scroll-mt-24">
  When Should Regent Stop Prioritizing Venerate and Other Star Generators?
</h2>

Venerate is valuable when your important cards need more Stars than the deck can currently produce. It becomes less useful when the deck routinely finishes turns with a large Star reserve and no way to spend it.

This can happen in a Forge-focused deck that relies more heavily on Sovereign Blade, or in a card-creation deck whose main payoffs use Energy instead of Stars.

If a Star generator no longer contributes to important turns, avoid taking additional copies. A low-impact generator may eventually become a removal candidate, but only after confirming that the remaining deck can still pay for its essential Star-cost cards.

<h2 id="regent-rest-upgrade" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Is Upgrading Pale Blue Dot Worth More Than Resting?
</h2>

Pale Blue Dot draws an additional card at the start of your next turn if you played five or more cards during the current turn. Upgrading it increases that bonus from one card to two.

That makes the upgrade attractive for a Regent deck already capable of playing five cards consistently. It can improve access to later attacks, defense, and resource spenders.

In one A10 run, the player approached a dangerous encounter with Pale Blue Dot as an appealing upgrade. Resting with Regal Pillow was chosen instead because the current HP total made surviving the next fight the immediate priority.

<h2 id="regent-boss-matchups" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Why Is BEGONE! Useful Against Soul Fysh?
</h2>

Soul Fysh adds Beckon Status cards to your deck. Leaving a Beckon in your hand at the end of a turn causes HP loss, while playing it normally costs Energy.

BEGONE! provides a Regent-specific answer: it transforms a card in your hand into Minion Strike. That card can be a Beckon, allowing you to remove the Status from the current combat while gaining a zero-cost attack that draws a card.

This is particularly useful when your Regent deck already has enough damage but risks losing important turns to Beckon.

BEGONE! can also activate effects that reward card creation, including Arsenal and Pillar of Creation, when those cards are present.

Its value here comes from solving Soul Fysh's specific Status problem. You do not need to change your entire Regent build to benefit from it.

<h2 id="regent-run-problems" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Why Does a Strong-Looking Regent Deck Keep Losing?
</h2>

A Regent deck can have powerful cards and still fail when its resources do not match what the hand needs.

- **Stars remain unused:** Stop adding Star generators until you have a worthwhile spender.
- **Sovereign Blade stays in hand:** Check whether its Energy cost prevents you from defending and attacking on the same turn.
- **Orbit produces Energy that goes unused:** Improve access to playable cards rather than stacking more Energy effects.
- **Card creation produces little value:** Look for a payoff such as Arsenal, Pillar of Creation, or Supermassive before adding more generators.
- **Damage is strong but survival is inconsistent:** Favor defense that works before the main engine is ready.

Fix the failure that appears most often in your actual turns. A deck with enough Stars does not need more Stars, and a deck with enough damage does not necessarily need another attack.

After your first Regent win, keep the resource problem that mattered most in mind: unspent Stars, a Sovereign Blade you could not afford to play, or card creation without a reliable payoff.
