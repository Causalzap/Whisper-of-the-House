<h2 id="where-to-start" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Which Slay the Spire 2 Guide Should You Start With?
</h2>

The right answer depends on where the run stops working.

If you cannot survive the early Acts, check whether your deck has enough immediate damage, dependable Block, and a way to handle the next Elite. If ordinary clears are consistent but higher difficulty causes repeated losses, identify which Ascension modifier changed the decision that previously worked.

Character and mode problems require different answers. Regent can run out of useful ways to spend Stars, Necrobinder can lose Osty before reaching Unleash, and The Fool can create long Arrow or Melody sequences without enough protection. Blitz removes the normal map and Gold decisions, while Dread adds a different enemy modifier depending on the upcoming boss.

Even the first reward can change which problem matters most. One Ancient offered +11 Max HP, a card reward with a potion, or Cursed Pearl's 333 Gold in exchange for adding Greed.

Before choosing, consider whether the next encounter, the next Merchant, or the deck's current weakness makes one reward useful sooner than the others.

<h2 id="first-win" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Why Do Your First Runs Keep Dying in Act 1?
</h2>

An early loss often begins before the final combat. A deck can have plenty of cards but lack enough damage to finish an Elite, or spend too much HP on a route that its current rewards cannot support.

Before taking another card, ask what it improves in the next few fights. Before entering an Elite, compare your damage, Block, HP, and potions with the likely cost of surviving it.

The <Link href="/slay-the-spire-2/beginner-guide">Beginner Guide</Link> covers card picks versus Skip, Ancient rewards, early routes, Merchant purchases, Elite fights, and Rest Site decisions from the opening floor to the first clear.

<h2 id="ascension" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Why Does Your Deck Win Normally but Lose at Higher Ascension?
</h2>

Ascension modifiers stack. A deck that wins at lower difficulty may fail when Gold becomes scarcer, Merchant removals become more expensive, enemies grow stronger, or a bad opening draw costs more HP.

The important question is which earlier decision no longer works. If you repeatedly lose after taking an early Elite, reconsider whether the current deck can survive that fight. If the deck reaches Act 3 but cannot finish both bosses, check whether its damage and defense remain reliable across multiple difficult encounters.

For the complete A1–A10 modifiers and difficulty-specific decisions, see the <Link href="/slay-the-spire-2/ascension-guide">Ascension Guide</Link>.

<h2 id="regent" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Why Does Regent Have Plenty of Stars but Still Struggle?
</h2>

Stars persist between turns, but stored resources do not help if the deck cannot spend them. A Regent deck can also build an enormous Sovereign Blade yet struggle to play it while defending.

When Stars remain unused, the next reward may need to improve Energy, card access, or a worthwhile spender rather than generate more Stars. Forge and card-creation effects can provide other damage plans, but they also need reliable support.

If Sovereign Blade is strong but cannot be played safely, compare Forge and Energy options in the <Link href="/slay-the-spire-2/regent-guide">Regent Guide</Link>.

<h2 id="necrobinder" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Why Does Necrobinder Struggle Before Osty or Doom Can Finish a Fight?
</h2>

Necrobinder has several different damage plans, but they do not become useful at the same time.

Osty can absorb incoming attacks and strengthen Unleash. Doom can kill an enemy at the end of its turn, provided Necrobinder survives that turn. Souls and Ethereal cards improve access to different combinations, while Eradicate can turn stored Energy into a powerful finishing attack.

If a run keeps failing, identify whether Osty needs more protection, Doom needs another safe turn, or the deck cannot reach its main damage card consistently.

The <Link href="/slay-the-spire-2/necrobinder-guide">Necrobinder Guide</Link> shows how Osty, Doom, Souls, and stored Energy can turn those difficult opening turns into a workable damage plan.

<h2 id="the-fool" className="scroll-mt-24">
  How Do You Make The Fool's Arrows and Melody Work Together?
</h2>

The Fool combines a bow with musical card effects, creating damage and defensive sequences that depend on how cards are played.

Aim improves Arrow damage, while Impaled can make later attacks more valuable. Melody cards offer another direction, and Sadness-to-Joy conversions can support longer turns when the deck has the resources to use them.

The main danger is building an impressive combination that cannot survive long enough to work. If your Arrow attacks are weak, check the Aim setup. If Melody turns stall, check the Energy and card draw required to complete the sequence.

For Aim timing, Melody sequences, and ways to handle Sadness before it overwhelms your hand, use the <Link href="/slay-the-spire-2/the-fool">The Fool Guide</Link>.

<h2 id="blitz-vs-dread" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Should You Play Blitz or Dread After Reaching Ascension?
</h2>

Blitz and Dread change the game in very different ways. Both were introduced in the November 2026 `public-beta` update.

### Choose Blitz when you want faster runs and concentrated rewards

Blitz removes the normal map and Gold economy. You begin by choosing three cards, then work through a shortened sequence of encounters with unusual Merchant rewards.

You cannot rely on the same route or purchasing decisions used in a normal run. Your starting cards and later reward choices need to form a workable deck much sooner.

The <Link href="/slay-the-spire-2/blitz">Blitz Mode Guide</Link> covers the encounter order, starting-card choices, Merchant rewards, and successful or failed character runs.

### Choose Dread when normal A10 no longer feels difficult enough

Dread activates all ten Ascension modifiers and adds Boss Dominions that change the enemies encountered before each boss. Additional Ascender's Banes and stronger bosses make both deck consistency and combat survival more demanding.

A Dominion can make ordinary attacks less effective, strengthen enemies over time, or interfere with your opening turn. The right response depends on the upcoming boss rather than on one fixed strategy for every Act.

The <Link href="/slay-the-spire-2/dread">Dread Mode Guide</Link> explains the twelve Boss Dominions, Sloth versus Amnesia, additional Banes, and the decisions that changed actual high-difficulty runs.

<h2 id="aeonglass" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Why Does Aeonglass Kill Decks That Were Winning Earlier?
</h2>

Aeonglass punishes decks that need many card plays before dealing meaningful damage.

Withering Presence creates a Wither after every six cards played. Withers left in your hand deal damage at the end of the turn, while Increasing Intensity makes later turns more dangerous. The boss also begins with Artifact, preventing early debuffs from working until that protection is removed.

Before extending a turn, check whether the next card will trigger another Wither and whether you can remove or survive it. If Poison, Weak, or Vulnerable is central to your plan, account for Artifact before expecting those effects to apply.

The <Link href="/slay-the-spire-2/aeonglass">Aeonglass Boss Guide</Link> covers the attack sequence, Wither timing, Discard versus Exhaust, Artifact removal, and the mistakes that caused otherwise powerful decks to lose.

<h2 id="multiplayer" className="scroll-mt-24">
  What Changes When You Play Slay the Spire 2 With Friends?
</h2>

Multiplayer changes how damage, Block, debuffs, and rewards should be used. A defensive card that protects the team can allow another player to spend more Energy attacking. A debuff applied by one player may also improve the damage dealt by several teammates.

That makes communication important. Before committing to attacks, identify which enemy should die first, whether the team already has enough Block, and who benefits most from a shared reward.

<h2 id="exact-data" className="scroll-mt-24">

If you need to compare a card's Energy cost, a relic's effect, or an enemy's abilities, you can browse the <Link href="https://spiregenius.com">SpireGenius game database</Link>.

For a difficult combat decision, the displayed card text and enemy effects in your current game version should take priority over data from an older build.

<h2 id="what-to-play-next" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Why Can't You Find The Fool, Blitz, or Dread?
</h2>

The Fool, Blitz, and Dread were added to the Steam `public-beta` branch in November 2026. If your game does not show the new character or modes, check which version you are playing.

In Steam, open **Library → Slay the Spire 2 → Properties → Game Versions & Betas**, then select `public-beta`.

Changing branches provides access to the beta content, but it does not bypass the mode unlock requirements.

- **The Fool:** Available as a playable character on the supported beta branch.
- **Blitz:** Unlocks after completing an Ascension run at any difficulty.
- **Dread:** Requires reaching Ascension 10 with all six characters, including The Fool.

If Blitz is locked, continue progressing through normal Ascension. If Dread is locked, check which characters have not yet reached A10.

These requirements apply to the November 2026 beta content and may change in later updates.
