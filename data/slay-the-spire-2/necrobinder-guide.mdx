<section id="quick-answer" className="scroll-mt-24">
  <GuideQuickAnswer title="Keep Osty Alive and Reach Your Damage Cards Reliably">

  Necrobinder starts with Bodyguard, Unleash, and Bound Phylactery, giving her an immediate way to build Osty's HP and convert it into damage. Strengthen that opening before committing to Doom, Souls, Ethereal cards, or an expensive finisher.

  Remove weak cards when doing so improves access to the attacks and defense your deck actually uses. Doom needs time to finish enemies, while Eradicate needs enough Energy to deliver lethal damage. A powerful combination only works if you can survive until it is ready.

  </GuideQuickAnswer>
</section>

Necrobinder has several ways to win in *Slay the Spire 2*, but they depend on different resources. Summon strengthens Osty, Doom threatens delayed kills, Souls provide card draw, and Energy-heavy attacks can finish fights in one turn.

The first few rewards determine which of these effects can carry the current deck. A successful run does not need every combination. It needs a way to survive dangerous turns and reach its chosen damage source reliably.

<h2 id="reach-real-cards" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Should Necrobinder Remove Strikes Early?
</h2>

In one Ascension 10 Underdocks run, the player removed two Strikes immediately and planned to remove a third at the next Merchant. That left only one basic Strike competing with Bodyguard, Unleash, and the stronger cards added during the run.

The removal made sense because Necrobinder already had a useful starting combination. Bodyguard increases Osty's HP, while Unleash deals additional damage based on Osty's current HP.

Drawing these cards instead of another Strike can improve both survival and damage. Additional cards such as Negative Pulse or Pull Aggro can cover problems the starting combination does not solve alone.

Removing Strikes is less attractive when it leaves the deck without enough damage for its next encounter. The goal is to reach functioning cards sooner, not to minimize deck size regardless of the result.

<h2 id="first-floors" className="scroll-mt-24">
  How Do You Decide Whether Osty Should Defend or Attack?
</h2>

Osty takes unblocked attack damage for Necrobinder while he is alive. Her starting relic, Bound Phylactery, Summons 1 at the beginning of each turn.

Summon increases Osty's maximum HP during combat, giving cards such as Bodyguard and Reanimate a lasting defensive purpose. Unleash also deals additional damage based on his current HP.

That creates an important timing decision: if Osty is about to take a large hit, playing Unleash before that damage can produce a stronger attack.

But using every available card to grow Osty is not always correct. Sometimes immediate Block prevents more damage than additional Summon, especially when his current HP is already sufficient for the next attack.

In one early run, the deck was a single point of Block short of the intended defensive interaction. The problem was not a lack of theoretical late-game damage. The cards available on that turn could not fully cover the attack.

Before taking another Osty-related card, check whether the deck needs more HP for Osty, a stronger Osty attack, or an independent defensive answer.

<h2 id="do-not-force-doom" className="scroll-mt-24">
  When Is Doom Better Than Direct Damage?
</h2>

Doom does not immediately reduce an enemy's HP. At the end of that enemy's turn, it dies if its Doom is at least equal to its remaining HP.

That timing matters because the enemy normally gets to act before the Doom check. Applying enough Doom to finish an enemy is not the same as killing it with an Attack before its next move.

Necrobinder can use Doom effectively when Osty, Block, or Weak already protects the turn in which the enemy will act.

Negative Pulse is useful when several enemies need Doom and the deck also needs Block. Other Doom cards may produce more finishing pressure, but they are less attractive when the immediate attack will kill Necrobinder before Doom resolves.

Before committing another reward to Doom, check whether the current deck can survive the remaining enemy action. If it cannot, a direct attack, defensive card, or faster damage source may be the better choice.

A deck can also use Doom alongside Osty or Eradicate rather than choosing one damage type for the entire run.

<h2 id="souls-and-ethereal" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Should Necrobinder Build Around Souls or Ethereal Cards?
</h2>

Souls and Ethereal cards can both help Necrobinder reach a stronger turn, but they reward different actions.

A Soul costs 0 Energy, draws two cards, and Exhausts when played. Soul+ draws three cards instead. Cards such as Reave, Grave Warden, and Dirge can add Souls to your Draw Pile.

Souls become more valuable when your deck has a payoff for playing or exhausting them:

- **Haunt:** Playing a Soul causes an enemy to lose HP.
- **Devour Life:** Playing a Soul increases Osty's HP through Summon.
- **Soul Storm:** Gains damage from Souls already in your Exhaust Pile.

If your deck only creates Souls without a way to use the additional cards, the extra draw may not solve its problem. Check whether the hand has enough Energy and useful actions after the Souls are played.

### When does Banshee's Cry become a realistic attack?

Banshee's Cry deals 33 damage to all enemies but starts at 9 Energy, or 7 when upgraded.

Each Ethereal card played during the combat reduces its cost by 2 Energy. That makes the number of Ethereal cards your deck can actually play more important than the number it contains.

An Ethereal card left unplayed in your hand is Exhausted at the end of the turn. Simply allowing it to disappear does not count as playing it for Banshee's Cry.

If several Ethereal cards can be played without sacrificing survival, Banshee's Cry can become an efficient area attack later in the fight.

Do not take it only because the deck contains several Ethereal cards. Check whether those cards can be played and whether the fight will last long enough for the discount to matter.

<h2 id="remove-until-it-hurts" className="scroll-mt-24">
  How Does Forbidden Grimoire Help Necrobinder Thin Her Deck?
</h2>

Forbidden Grimoire is an Ancient Power card, not a relic. When played during combat, it allows you to remove a card from your deck after that combat ends.

The removal is optional. If you do not play Forbidden Grimoire during the fight, you do not receive its removal reward from that fight.

Forbidden Grimoire also has Eternal, so it cannot be removed or transformed from your deck. Repeatedly using it can thin the rest of your deck, but the Grimoire itself will remain.

In one Necrobinder run, obtaining Forbidden Grimoire changed the value of future Merchant visits. The deck could remove unwanted cards through combat rewards instead of spending Gold on every removal.

Later, the deck was reduced to 14 cards during combat. That made it easier to reach Reanimate, Banshee's Cry, Unleash, and other important effects.

The result was not valuable simply because the deck was small. It was valuable because fewer unnecessary draws separated the player from a working combination.

### When should you stop removing cards?

Removing a card is useful only when the remaining deck can still cover its important fights.

Keep Bodyguard or another defensive option if losing it makes Osty vulnerable before the main attack is ready. Keep a card-recovery effect if the deck depends on finding one important card again.

Forbidden Grimoire also has an immediate Energy cost. Spending Energy to play it may be unsafe in a difficult combat if the current hand still needs to defend.

Once the remaining cards consistently provide damage, Block, draw, and access to the finisher, additional removal can become less valuable than an upgrade, potion, or other useful reward.

<h2 id="osty-buys-time" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Why Is Pull Aggro Useful in an Osty Build?
</h2>

Pull Aggro combines two defenses in one card: it Summons 4 and gains 7 Block. Upgrading it increases those values to Summon 5 and 9 Block.

This matters because Block protects Osty from incoming attacks before his HP is consumed. At the same time, Summon increases the current HP used by Unleash for damage.

In one Necrobinder run, repeated Pull Aggro effects helped maintain Osty while the player waited for the main attack. The deck did not need to choose between protecting its existing damage source and strengthening it.

The next decision is whether additional protection is still necessary. If Osty and the available Block already cover the incoming attack, spending another card on Summon may be less useful than drawing or playing Unleash.

At a later Merchant, defensive options such as Friendship, Defy, and a Block Potion were considered in the context of this existing Osty setup. Their value depended on whether they helped the deck survive its next dangerous turn.

<h2 id="second-kill-condition" className="scroll-mt-24">
  When Is Eradicate Better Than Waiting for Unleash?
</h2>

Eradicate is an X-cost Attack with Retain. It deals 11 damage X times, or 14 damage X times when upgraded.

Retain lets Necrobinder keep the card while preparing enough Energy for a decisive attack. This makes Eradicate useful when a deck has strong Energy generation but cannot reliably reach its usual damage card at the right time.

In one Ascension 10 run, the player already had Energy support and Lethality before using Eradicate as an additional finishing option.

A recorded attack reached **8 × 24 = 192 damage**.

<h3 id="energy-needs-an-outlet" className="scroll-mt-24">
  How Do Borrowed Time and Ice Cream Help Eradicate?
</h3>

Borrowed Time grants 4 Energy, or 6 when upgraded, but increases the cost of other cards for the current turn.

Eradicate is different because its X cost is not increased by Borrowed Time. That allows Necrobinder to convert the extra Energy directly into more Eradicate hits.

Ice Cream preserves unspent Energy between turns. With both effects available, the deck can build a larger Energy reserve and wait until Eradicate is ready.

One recorded calculation reached **26 × 9 = 234 damage**, but that still was not enough to kill the target.

The player had to defend and preserve the setup instead of spending the turn on an attack that would leave the enemy alive.

Before playing Eradicate, multiply its displayed damage per hit by the number of hits and compare the result with the enemy's remaining HP and Block.

If the attack does not finish the enemy, calculate whether enough Block remains to survive. Retaining Eradicate for another turn can be better than committing all your Energy to an incomplete kill.

The important question is not how much Energy the deck can generate. It is whether the stored Energy produces lethal damage before the next dangerous attack.

### What if Ice Cream will not remain available?

One Eradicate run also had to account for changing relic availability. Pocket Watch, Ice Cream, and Stone Calendar did not provide equal value to the current combination.

Ice Cream was central because it preserved the Energy needed for Eradicate. The player counted the remaining combats before the relic arrangement would change, then chose a path that kept Ice Cream available for the important fight.

This does not mean Necrobinder should always avoid extra encounters. It shows that an Energy-based finisher can become much weaker if the relic supporting its stored Energy is no longer available.

<h2 id="when-the-deck-is-finished" className="scroll-mt-24">
  How Can Necrobinder Deal 533 Damage With Unleash?
</h2>

Unleash is part of Necrobinder's starting deck. Its damage increases with Osty's current HP, so repeated Summon effects can turn a basic starting Attack into a major late-game finisher.

One Ascension 10 run developed enough Summon, card access, and supporting effects to produce a **533-damage Unleash**.

The damage was the result of a developed combination, not a starting-deck attack dealing that much on its own.

Summon effects increase Osty's maximum HP during combat. Keeping Osty alive also preserves the current HP that Unleash uses for its additional damage.

This creates a choice between spending a turn on more Summon and using Unleash immediately.

If the enemy is about to remove a large amount of Osty's HP, playing Unleash before that attack can be better than waiting for another small increase.

If Osty is protected and the deck can survive, additional Summon may produce a much stronger attack later.

That successful run finished with nine Elite kills, but those rewards were possible because the deck could already defend, draw, and reach its main damage cards. The 533-damage attack was the result of that progression rather than a reason to take every Elite.

<h2 id="why-necrobinder-runs-collapse" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Why Does Your Necrobinder Build Stop Working?
</h2>

A powerful Necrobinder deck can still fail when its damage source is not available at the right time.

- **Osty dies before Unleash becomes useful:** Strengthen his HP earlier, protect the dangerous attack, or play Unleash before Osty loses the HP that contributes to its damage.
- **Doom is high but the enemy still attacks:** Doom normally checks for a kill at the end of the enemy's turn. You still need enough protection to survive that turn.
- **Souls draw cards but do not improve the fight:** Check whether the extra cards can be played and whether Haunt, Devour Life, or another effect rewards your Soul generation.
- **Banshee's Cry remains too expensive:** Play the Ethereal cards needed to reduce its cost. Letting those cards Exhaust in hand does not provide the discount.
- **Eradicate cannot finish the enemy:** Calculate the full attack before spending your Energy. If the damage is insufficient, retain the card and preserve enough defense to try again.
- **Forbidden Grimoire gives no removal reward:** Make sure you played the Power during that combat. Owning the card alone does not activate its effect.
- **The deck is thin but unreliable:** Keep enough damage, Block, and access to your finishing cards. Removing another card is not always the solution.

The most reliable improvement is the one that fixes the turn where the current combination fails. Necrobinder does not need every damage engine in one deck, but she does need a way to survive until the chosen engine can finish the fight.
