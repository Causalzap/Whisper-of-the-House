<section id="quick-answer" className="scroll-mt-24">
  <GuideQuickAnswer title="Build for the Boss Before You Run Out of Rewards">

  In Blitz Mode, choose three starting cards that can survive the first fights and support a realistic damage plan. You have no normal map or Gold, and the Merchant offers a limited number of free selections rather than a conventional shop.

  Use those selections to fix what your deck cannot do: win a long boss fight, survive a dangerous opening turn, or draw and pay for its strongest cards. Do not rely on finding several missing combo pieces later. Blitz offers far fewer opportunities to repair an incomplete deck.

  </GuideQuickAnswer>
</section>

Blitz Mode in *Slay the Spire 2* replaces much of the usual journey with a shorter sequence of encounters and more concentrated reward decisions. A run can finish quickly, but that does not make every fight easier. The problem is often reaching an Elite or boss before the deck has enough cards to solve it.

Blitz was introduced in the November 2026 `public-beta` update alongside The Fool and Dread. These strategies apply to the early beta version of the mode; its balance and rewards may change during Early Access.

<h2 id="unlock-blitz" className="scroll-mt-24">
  How do you unlock Blitz Mode in Slay the Spire 2?
</h2>

Blitz unlocks after you complete an Ascension run with any character. An A0 clear alone does not meet this requirement.

If Blitz is missing after an Ascension victory, check that you are playing on Steam's `public-beta` branch under **Properties → Game Versions & Betas**. If you have not unlocked Ascension yet, follow the <Link href="/slay-the-spire-2/ascension-guide">Ascension Guide</Link>. For character and mode progression, see the <Link href="/slay-the-spire-2">Slay the Spire 2 guide</Link>.

Blitz has five Ascension levels shared across characters, with different modifiers from the normal A1–A10 climb.

<h2 id="blitz-rules" className="scroll-mt-24">
  What changes in Blitz Mode?
</h2>

Blitz removes the ordinary route-planning phase and gives the player a concentrated sequence of fights, rewards, and preparation opportunities.

The important changes are:

- **Three cards are added at the beginning of the run.** You select them before the first combat.
- **Each Act contains four encounters.** The shortened structure leaves much less time to develop a deck.
- **There is no normal map.** You cannot choose a safer or more rewarding path through the Act.
- **Gold is no longer a useful resource.** The Merchant offers selectable benefits instead of ordinary purchases.
- **Treasure Chests contain an additional relic.** In solo play, both chest relics can be collected.
- **Blitz uses five shared Ascension levels.** The normal character-specific climb does not carry over as an identical difficulty ladder.

Every Blitz Act follows the same sequence: **Ancient → two normal encounters → Merchant → Elite → Treasure Chest → Rest Site → Boss**. The four combats are the two normal encounters, the Elite, and the Boss.

This fixed order changes when you must solve a problem. Your opening cards have to survive two fights before the Merchant. The Merchant's three selections must prepare you for the forced Elite, and the Chest and Rest Site are your final preparation opportunities before the Boss.

There is no alternate path to take if your deck is missing an important answer. Choose rewards for the encounters you know are coming.

<h2 id="starting-three-cards" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Which three starting cards should you choose in Blitz?
</h2>

The opening selection is one of the most important decisions in the entire run.

  className="my-8 rounded-xl"

A strong opening does not have to contain three cards from the same archetype. It needs a way to win the immediate fights, enough defense to avoid arriving at the Elite nearly dead, and a plausible direction for later damage.

Consider what happened in an early Defect Blitz attempt.

The opening choice included **Ice Lance, Cold Snap, and Hot Fix**. The first fights were manageable, and the deck picked up additional cards and rewards. But the next major encounter exposed a problem: the deck could survive some turns without dealing enough sustained damage to finish the Waterfall Giant.

The run ended in Act 1.

The mistake was not necessarily choosing an individual weak card. The opening had not developed a sufficiently reliable answer to the boss before the available reward opportunities ran out.

### Choose for the two fights before the Merchant

Your opening trio must work before you can make another concentrated set of reward choices. You cannot change paths to avoid an unfavorable second encounter, so check whether the cards provide enough immediate damage and Block without drawing a particular combination.

A strong attack does not necessarily require another attack beside it. If the first two cards already provide damage, the third may be more valuable as defense, draw, or Energy that lets you play both cards on the same turn.

### Check whether the opening can develop into boss damage

The early Defect attempt with Ice Lance, Cold Snap, and Hot Fix survived its initial encounters but did not develop enough damage to defeat Waterfall Giant. Its weakness only became decisive after the limited reward opportunities had passed.

Before confirming the three cards, identify which one can contribute against a long boss fight. If none can, your Merchant selections will need to solve that problem before the forced Elite and Boss.

<h2 id="blitz-merchant" className="scroll-mt-24">
  What should you take from the Blitz Merchant?
</h2>

The Blitz Merchant does not work like an ordinary Shop.

Instead of spending Gold, you select **three benefits from the offered choices**. The available options change between visits.

<Image
  src="/images/slay-the-spire-2/slay-the-spire-2-blitz-merchant-three-choices.webp"
  alt="Blitz Merchant reward selection showing three remaining item choices"
  width={1158}
  height={882}
  className="my-8 rounded-xl"
/>

Depending on what appears, you may be offered card rewards, upgraded or enchanted cards, relics, potions, card removal, transformation, upgrades, or benefits that require accepting a Curse.

Some Curse-related offers provide additional selections or several rewards. Read the specific offer rather than assuming every Curse option works the same way.

The best Merchant choice depends on the deck that arrives at the counter.

/>

### When should you choose more card rewards?

Choose card rewards when the deck is missing an essential answer.

In one Silent run, the deck reached a Merchant with insufficient confidence in its upcoming fights. The player accepted an offer that added a Curse in exchange for multiple card rewards, looking for cards that could solve the next encounters.

The resulting choices included **Adrenaline**, **Escape Plan**, and **Echoing Slash**. Those additions offered more card access and a way to improve damage.

The important part was not simply obtaining more cards. The deck had identifiable weaknesses, and the extra selections offered possible solutions.

That choice also carried a real downside. The Curse was now in the deck, and its removal could not be guaranteed.

Accept a Curse trade when the extra selections can realistically solve a problem that would otherwise lose the upcoming Elite or Boss fight. The Curse enters your deck immediately, and another removal opportunity is not guaranteed. If the deck already has a dependable way to win, do not add a bad draw simply to collect more rewards.

### When is removing a card better than adding one?

Removal is valuable when starter cards interfere with an engine that already works.

It is much less convincing when the deck does not yet have enough damage to beat the next boss.

For example, a deck with a reliable, repeatable attack and enough defensive tools may benefit greatly from removing an unwanted Strike. The same removal does little for a deck that has no way to defeat a high-HP enemy.

The question is whether the removal makes your strong turns appear more often or merely reduces the number of cards in an unfinished deck.

### When are potions better than relics?

Potions can solve an immediate combat problem without requiring your deck to become stronger first.

During one Silent Blitz victory, the player repeatedly selected additional potions and potion capacity. By the final Aeonglass fight, the available consumables provided a significant amount of emergency damage and flexibility.

That run did not prove that potions are always the strongest Merchant option. It demonstrated that a short mode can reward consumables unusually well when dangerous fights arrive before a complete card engine can be assembled.

If you are about to fight an Elite and your deck lacks enough immediate output, a useful potion may have more practical value than a relic whose effect requires several more combats to matter.

### When is an upgraded or enchanted card worth taking?

The reward should improve a card you already want to play.

Upgrading a strong attack can be excellent when damage is the limiting factor. A card enchantment can be even more influential if it adds draw, improves timing, or changes how reliably the deck reaches its payoff.

But an impressive modification on the wrong card does not repair the deck.

In a Regent run, the player looked for rewards that supported the existing high-damage setup rather than collecting unrelated cards. That allowed the deck to become stronger without waiting for an entirely different combination to appear.

<h2 id="best-blitz-strategies" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Which strategies have worked for different characters in Blitz?
</h2>

Different characters can win Blitz, but their successful runs reveal different ways to handle its limited rewards.

These are examples of working strategies, not fixed card lists that every run can reproduce.

### Silent: use flexible damage and potions to reach the final fight

A successful Silent Blitz run combined early attacks with card access and several potion-related rewards.

Instead of relying entirely on a slow Poison engine or collecting every Shiv-related card, the deck used the rewards actually available to improve both its immediate turns and its boss damage.

By the final encounter, the player had enough consumables to support the decisive turns against Aeonglass.

The lesson is not that Silent should always draft the same attack cards. It is that **an unfinished damage package can be supported by better draw and consumables rather than replaced with a completely new package**.

### Ironclad: establish damage before adding too much setup

A separate Ironclad Blitz victory began with direct attacks and card access, including **Pommel Strike**, **Pillage**, and **Anger**.

Those cards helped the deck function early instead of waiting for an elaborate Power engine.

Ironclad can benefit from concentrating on attacks that already work together, but this does not mean ignoring Block in every fight.

The important advantage is that a direct damage plan may need fewer specific rewards to become effective. In Blitz, needing fewer missing pieces can matter as much as having a higher theoretical damage ceiling.

### Necrobinder: combine early survival with a repeatable source of damage

In a successful Necrobinder run, the starting selection included **Deathbringer**, **Defy**, and **Poke**.

The player built around Doom, defensive support, and later upgrades rather than hoping to find a completely different engine.

The run came close to failure. Its survival depended on defensive resources, including **Tungsten Rod**, and a later combination involving **Shroud** and **Reaper Form**.

The final Queen encounter was won with a functioning damage plan, defensive support, and appropriate use of consumables.

There were several narrow survival turns along the way. This was not a case of one strong starting card automatically winning Blitz.

The broader <Link href="/slay-the-spire-2/necrobinder-guide">Necrobinder Guide</Link> explains Osty and Doom decisions in normal runs. Blitz changes how soon those tools must become effective.

### Regent: focus limited rewards on an existing Star payoff

One Regent Blitz victory developed around a high-damage combination involving **Supermassive** and supporting cards.

Later, **Monarch's Gaze** and Replay interactions contributed to a successful final fight.

The player did not need every possible Regent synergy. The deck needed to make the existing payoff strong and playable before the remaining encounters ran out.

That is particularly important for Regent because cards that spend Stars can look impressive without a dependable way to generate and use those resources.

The <Link href="/slay-the-spire-2/regent-guide">Regent Guide</Link> covers the underlying Star and card-selection decisions in more detail.

### Defect: prepare the final damage engine before the last boss

A later Defect Blitz victory ended against Aeonglass.

The deck used an Orb-focused setup with **Refract**, and **Liquid Memories** allowed Refract to contribute again during the final fight.

The decisive turns did not begin with an overwhelmingly strong position. The player was uncertain whether the available damage and defense would be sufficient.

The run succeeded because the key pieces were finally accessible when needed.

That example also demonstrates why taking another speculative Power shortly before the final boss may be worse than improving the engine already in the deck.

The next reward should address a known weakness, not create a second plan that cannot be finished.

<h2 id="fool-blitz-failure" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Why does The Fool struggle to finish some Blitz runs?
</h2>

The Fool provides an especially useful example of what can go wrong when a deck needs too many specific pieces.

In one Blitz attempt, the player developed a combination involving Melodies, Arrow damage, and supporting cards. The early and middle fights were manageable, but the deck eventually reached Queen without enough reliable card access.

The resulting turns were awkward. The necessary cards did not arrive together often enough, while Queen's debuffs made defensive recovery harder.

The run ended in defeat.

<Image
  src="/images/slay-the-spire-2/slay-the-spire-2-blitz-fool-queen-failure.webp"
  alt="The Fool lying defeated on the Devastation screen after a failed Blitz battle"
  width={1750}
  height={682}
  className="my-8 rounded-xl"
/>

The player identified the lack of suitable draw options as a major problem. Blitz had simply offered too few chances to assemble the missing support.

A different Fool Blitz attempt exposed a related failure. The deck could generate Block, but it lacked a reliable damage engine. Sadness accumulated in hand, and the available Anguish scaling was insufficient to finish the encounter.

More turns did not solve the problem because the damage plan was not getting stronger quickly enough.

These failures show two distinct weaknesses:

- **A deck with damage but unreliable access** may never assemble the required hand before the boss becomes dangerous.
- **A deck with defense but insufficient scaling** may survive several turns without moving meaningfully closer to victory.

When choosing The Fool's opening cards, favor an Arrow or Melody package that already functions. Do not add Sadness generators merely because a conversion card might appear later.

For complete Arrow, Melody, and Sadness interactions, use the <Link href="/slay-the-spire-2/the-fool">The Fool Guide</Link>.

Blitz changes the value of the setup time those strategies require. It does not remove their normal card dependencies.


<h2 id="later-acts" className="scroll-mt-24">
  What should you fix before the Blitz Elite and Boss?
</h2>

Each Act gives you two normal fights before the Merchant, followed by an Elite, a Treasure Chest, a Rest Site, and the Boss. By the time you reach the Merchant, you have already seen how well your current deck handles ordinary enemies.

The important question is what happens when those enemies have much more HP or deal enough damage to force several defensive turns.

### At the Merchant: prepare for two mandatory fights

Your three choices must help against both the Elite and the Boss. If the deck can clear normal encounters but has no repeatable damage, another small attack may not solve the next fight. Look for scaling, access to your strongest attack, or a way to play it more often.

If damage is sufficient but your opening turns are dangerous, a defensive reward or useful potion can be more valuable than another speculative Power.

### At the Elite: spend resources when survival requires it

You cannot avoid this Elite by taking another route. Use a potion if it prevents a dangerous HP loss or keeps the run alive.

A Treasure Chest and Rest Site follow the Elite, so surviving with enough HP to reach them matters more than preserving every consumable for the Boss.

### At the Chest and Rest Site: prepare for the visible Boss

The Chest offers relic choices before the final Rest Site. Evaluate those relics against the Boss already shown for the Act.

At the Rest Site, decide whether your remaining HP is sufficient to survive the Boss's opening turns. If it is, an upgrade that makes your existing damage or defensive engine more reliable may be stronger than healing. Otherwise, Rest.

In Acts 2 and 3, the same sequence repeats, but there are fewer remaining opportunities to replace a failing damage plan. By the last Merchant, improve the engine you already have rather than starting an unrelated combination.

<h2 id="blitz-ascension" className="scroll-mt-24">
  What changes at each Blitz Ascension level?
</h2>

Blitz has five Ascension levels. Completing one level unlocks the next for every character, rather than maintaining a separate ladder for each character.

The modifiers accumulate as you climb:

1. **Blitz A1 — Weary Traveler:** Ancients restore only 80% of your missing HP.
2. **Blitz A2 — Tight Belt:** Start with one fewer potion slot.
3. **Blitz A3 — Tough Enemies:** Enemies become harder to kill.
4. **Blitz A4 — Deadly Enemies:** Enemy attacks become more dangerous.
5. **Blitz A5 — Ascender's Bane:** Begin the run with an additional Curse.

The short encounter sequence makes each modifier more noticeable. At A5, the Curse can disrupt a crucial opening hand, while the reduced potion capacity from A2 limits how many consumables you can carry into the next forced fight.

These are Blitz-specific modifiers, not the normal A1–A10 ladder. For ordinary Ascension progression, use the <Link href="/slay-the-spire-2/ascension-guide">Ascension Guide</Link>.

<h2 id="blitz-troubleshooting" className="scroll-mt-24">
  Why do you keep losing Blitz runs?
</h2>

Blitz often exposes the same weaknesses sooner than a normal run would.

A deck that might gradually recover in a longer game may reach its final boss before that recovery happens.

<GuideTable
  caption="Blitz Mode: common failure patterns and their practical fixes."
  columns={blitzFailureColumns}
  rows={blitzFailureRows}
/>

After a failed Blitz run, identify the earliest decision that could have changed the outcome: the starting trio, the Merchant's limited selections, a potion used too late, or the Rest Site choice before the Boss.

The next run does not need an entirely different archetype. It needs an answer to the encounter that the previous deck could not survive or finish.
