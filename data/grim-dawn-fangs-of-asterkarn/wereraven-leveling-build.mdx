I level this character as a **self-cast Cold and Frostburn Wereraven**, not as
a pet build.

Berserker supplies the transformation, the attacks I use in every fight, and
most of the early attributes. I select Occultist as the planned second mastery,
but I do not slow the leveling route by investing heavily in Summon Familiar
before the equipment for that later hybrid is available.

The build is simple when its priorities stay in the correct order: unlock
Wereraven, keep Ice Talons sustainable, use Cold Snap for close-range clearing,
maintain Rallying Cry, and repair Energy Regeneration and defenses before
stacking more damage.

<h2 id="skill-order">
  Wereraven leveling skill order
</h2>

<h3 id="unlock-wereraven">
  Levels 1–10: unlock Wereraven first
</h3>

Wereraven becomes available after advancing the **Berserker mastery bar to its
10-point threshold**. This is a mastery-bar requirement, not a requirement to
wait until character level 10.

I place the opening points into the Berserker bar until the Wereraven node is
available, then take the transformation immediately. I skip Werewolf,
weapon-pool skills, and unrelated dual-wield attacks because they do not
support the combat loop used by this route.

<h3 id="early-points">
  Early checkpoint: Wereraven, Ice Talons, and Rallying Cry
</h3>

One useful early checkpoint in my run was:

<div className="my-5 rounded-xl border border-slate-200 bg-slate-50 px-5 py-4 font-mono text-sm leading-7 text-slate-700">
  <p>Wereraven: 6 points</p>
  <p>Ice Talons: 3 points</p>
  <p>Rallying Cry: 1 point</p>
</div>

This is not a rigid level-by-level allocation. Equipment can add skill ranks,
and advancing the mastery bar sometimes gives more value than immediately
adding another point to an attack.

The priority is:

<ol className="my-5 list-decimal space-y-2 pl-6">
  <li>Unlock Wereraven.</li>
  <li>Raise Ice Talons until normal enemies die comfortably.</li>
  <li>Take one point in Rallying Cry.</li>
  <li>Continue advancing the Berserker mastery bar.</li>
  <li>Add Cold Snap when close-range packs become uncomfortable.</li>
</ol>

I use **Ice Talons** as the repeatable attack against single targets,
spread-out enemies, and anything I want to hit while repositioning.

I use **Cold Snap** when several enemies close the distance. I do not spend its
Energy on one distant target when Ice Talons can finish the same enemy.

<h3 id="rallying-cry">
  Keep Rallying Cry active
</h3>

Rallying Cry is an active buff and heal, not a passive bonus.

Even one early point gives enough value to justify a hotbar slot. In the tested
setup, rank 1 restored a portion of Health and added movement speed and
Offensive Ability for a long duration.

I activate it:

- before bosses and Hero enemies;
- before entering a dense room;
- after returning from town;
- and whenever the previous buff expires.

I do not immediately max every Rallying Cry modifier. The mastery bar and the
two Wereraven attacks affect every fight and normally deserve the early points
first.

<h3 id="levels-10-25">
  Levels 10–25: strengthen the core without spreading points
</h3>

After unlocking the form, I keep most new points inside Berserker.

My priorities during this phase are:

1. keep Ice Talons strong enough for normal enemies;
2. continue raising the mastery bar;
3. add points to the useful Wereraven nodes;
4. maintain one point in Rallying Cry;
5. raise Cold Snap when pack pressure becomes the problem.

I select Occultist at character level 10 only when I am committed to the later
hybrid. Selecting it does not mean I must immediately invest in Summon
Familiar.

Splitting the opening points evenly between Wereraven and Familiar produces
two underdeveloped damage packages. The self-cast Wereraven is already doing
the leveling work, so I finish that foundation first.

<h3 id="levels-25-50">
  Levels 25–50: keep advancing Berserker and solve the real wall
</h3>

Once Ice Talons clears normal enemies, it is tempting to stop advancing the
mastery bar and place every point into visible damage nodes.

That improves easy packs but can delay:

- mastery attributes;
- later Wereraven nodes;
- Health and survivability;
- and the utility needed for Heroes and bosses.

During the middle campaign, I use this order:

<ol className="my-5 list-decimal space-y-2 pl-6">
  <li>Keep Ice Talons at a comfortable clearing rank.</li>
  <li>Advance the Berserker mastery bar.</li>
  <li>Improve Wereraven and its relevant modifiers.</li>
  <li>Add Cold Snap for pack clear.</li>
  <li>Add defensive or boss utility only when a specific problem appears.</li>
</ol>

If normal enemies die quickly but a boss barely moves, I check Energy,
Offensive Ability, devotion bindings, and resistance reduction before adding
more area damage.

If normal enemies die quickly but I am being burst down, I stop adding damage
and repair the defensive layer that is failing.

<h2 id="rotation">
  Wereraven leveling rotation
</h2>

The route does not need a crowded hotbar.

My standard sequence is:

<ol className="my-5 list-decimal space-y-2 pl-6">
  <li>Enter Wereraven before engaging.</li>
  <li>Activate Rallying Cry.</li>
  <li>Use Ice Talons on the first priority target.</li>
  <li>Reposition while Frostburn and devotion damage continue ticking.</li>
  <li>Use Cold Snap when multiple enemies reach close range.</li>
  <li>Use Evade to leave ground effects instead of finishing one extra cast.</li>
</ol>

Ice Talons is the repeatable skill. Cold Snap is the close-range answer, not a
replacement for every Ice Talons cast.

<h3 id="weapon-stats">
  Which weapon stats actually help Wereraven?
</h3>

Wereraven has its own basic attack and cannot trigger weapon-pool skills.
Ice Talons and Cold Snap also do not rely on a normal weapon-damage component
in this leveling setup.

That changes how I compare weapons.

I prioritize:

- global Cold and Frostburn bonuses;
- casting speed;
- Offensive Ability;
- bonuses to Wereraven, Ice Talons, or Cold Snap;
- useful skill modifiers;
- Energy Regeneration;
- resistances and defensive statistics;
- and helpful granted skills.

I place less value on:

- the weapon's displayed DPS;
- flat damage that only applies through weapon damage;
- weapon-pool skill bonuses;
- and an attack-speed increase when the build needs casting speed.

A weapon with lower sheet DPS can still be the clear upgrade when it provides
global Cold damage, casting speed, and skill ranks.

<h3 id="winds-of-asterkarn">
  Is Winds of Asterkarn required?
</h3>

No. Winds of Asterkarn can work with Wereraven and can be useful against dense
groups or targets that allow safe channeling.

I do not make it the center of this leveling route because remaining in place
is dangerous in areas filled with ground effects. Ice Talons lets me attack
and reposition more freely.

I test Winds when:

- Energy sustain is already stable;
- enemies stay inside the channel;
- and the encounter does not punish standing still.

I skip it when the existing Ice Talons and Cold Snap package already clears
comfortably.

<h2 id="devotions">
  Wereraven leveling devotion route
</h2>

My devotion plan has three jobs:

1. add an early damage proc;
2. improve Cold damage against tougher enemies;
3. add recovery when incoming damage becomes the wall.

<h3 id="tsunami">
  Take Tsunami first
</h3>

Tsunami is the first offensive devotion I use because it can trigger from the
attack I am already pressing throughout every map.

I bind **Tsunami to Ice Talons**.

I do not bind it to a utility skill that may go several packs without being
pressed.

<h3 id="viper-blizzard">
  Add Viper and move toward Blizzard
</h3>

Viper becomes important when Heroes and bosses take much longer than normal
packs. More percentage damage is not always the missing piece; reducing enemy
Elemental Resistance can improve the damage already on the build.

The later route I tested moves through:

- Viper;
- Sailor's Guide;
- Scholar's Light;
- and then Blizzard.

Blizzard fits the Cold and Frostburn theme but requires critical hits to
trigger. I do not abandon a reliable Tsunami setup simply because Blizzard is
available on the devotion map.

If Blizzard rarely activates, I check Offensive Ability and its skill binding
before changing the route.

<h3 id="behemoth">
  Take Behemoth earlier when survival becomes the problem
</h3>

I move toward Behemoth when:

1. normal enemies still die at an acceptable speed;
2. repeated hits force immediate potions, retreats, or deaths.

Giant's Blood adds an emergency recovery layer. It does not replace capped
resistances or a reasonable Health pool.

If the build is dying because one resistance is far below the others, I fix
that resistance before expecting Behemoth to solve the encounter.

<h2 id="energy">
  How I solve Wereraven Energy problems
</h2>

Energy is the most persistent mechanical issue in this leveling route.

Ice Talons is used repeatedly, and unnecessary Cold Snap casts can empty the
bar during long packs and bosses.

One Ectoplasm helps but is not the entire solution.

<figure className="my-8">
  <Image
    src="/images/grim-dawn-fangs-of-asterkarn/grim-dawn-wereraven-ectoplasm-energy-fix.webp"
    alt="Grim Dawn Ectoplasm component providing Energy and Energy Regeneration"
    width={246}
    height={215}
    className="mx-auto h-auto w-full max-w-sm rounded-xl"
  />
  <figcaption className="mx-auto mt-3 max-w-sm text-sm text-slate-400">
    Ectoplasm is a useful early source of Energy Regeneration, but the rest of
    the equipment still needs to support sustained casting.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

I fix Energy in this order:

<ol className="my-5 list-decimal space-y-2 pl-6">
  <li>Add Ectoplasm to an eligible jewelry slot.</li>
  <li>Keep Energy Regeneration on at least one useful equipment piece.</li>
  <li>Do not replace that item for a minor Cold Damage increase.</li>
  <li>Stop recasting into enemies already dying from Frostburn.</li>
  <li>Use Cold Snap only when its area coverage is needed.</li>
  <li>Carry Elixirs of Spirit for bosses and unusually dense areas.</li>
</ol>

Elixirs are an emergency resource. If Energy empties during every normal pack,
the permanent equipment setup needs more regeneration.

I do not place every attribute point into Spirit to hide the problem. Spirit
can satisfy caster requirements and enlarge the Energy pool, but Physique,
Health, and Defensive Ability matter more when the character is dying before
the larger pool becomes useful.

<h3 id="components">
  Components I use to solve specific problems
</h3>

I use components to repair the weakest part of the current character rather

- **Ectoplasm** when Energy Regeneration is insufficient;
- **Aether Soul** in an eligible Amulet or Medal when Aether damage is the

- broader resistance components when several defenses are low;
- Health, Armor, or regeneration components after the major resistances are
  stable.

<h3 id="vendor-gear">
  When I buy or keep vendor gear
</h3>

I buy or stash an item when it solves a defined problem:

- a ring with a large bonus to Ice Talons;
- an Amulet with Frostburn and Energy Regeneration;
- an item that caps the resistance needed in the next region;
- a caster weapon or off-hand that improves both damage and sustain;
- or a component blueprint that fixes a known weakness.

A future item is worth keeping when I can equip it soon and it directly
improves the main attack. It is not a reason to dismantle the current setup
before meeting its requirement.

I skip an item when:

- it adds damage but removes the resistance keeping me alive;
- its granted skill does not help the normal combat loop;
- it mainly supports weapon attacks or weapon-pool skills;
- or it is an expensive temporary purchase shortly before another planned
  upgrade.

<h2 id="attributes">
  Wereraven attribute points
</h2>

I use **Physique as the default**.

Physique helps equipment requirements, Health, and Defensive Ability. Those
benefits remain useful in every region and difficulty.

I add Spirit only when:

1. an important caster item requires it;
2. the Energy pool remains too small after fixing regeneration;
3. the defensive panel is already stable.

Offensive Ability is important because Blizzard and other critical-hit effects
cannot trigger reliably without critical hits.

Defensive Ability solves the opposite problem. If enemies begin landing
frequent or large critical hits, another Cold Damage roll will not protect the
character.

<h2 id="occultist">
  When I select Occultist
</h2>

I select **Occultist at character level 10** only because it is the planned
second mastery for the later hybrid.

I do not immediately divide the leveling points evenly between the two
masteries.

Berserker already supplies:

- Wereraven;
- Ice Talons and Cold Snap;
- Rallying Cry;
- mastery attributes;
- and the damage used in every fight.

My early Occultist plan is:

<ol className="my-5 list-decimal space-y-2 pl-6">
  <li>Select Occultist when the character plan is confirmed.</li>
  <li>Continue spending primarily in Berserker.</li>
  <li>Consider Blood of Dreeg when its heal and support justify the mastery investment.</li>
  <li>Consider Aspect of the Guardian when its defenses solve a current problem.</li>
  <li>Delay the complete Summon Familiar package.</li>
</ol>

Summon Familiar is not a one-point leveling companion that automatically
carries the route. It needs skill investment and pet-supporting equipment to
become a real damage package.

The planned level 94 Wereraven and Familiar setup is a separate endgame
transition. Until that equipment, the Familiar investment, and the final
damage conversion are equipped and tested together, I continue leveling as a
self-cast Cold and Frostburn Wereraven.

<h2 id="faq">
  Grim Dawn Wereraven leveling FAQ
</h2>

### Can Wereraven trigger weapon-pool skills?

No. Wereraven has its own basic attack and cannot trigger weapon-pool skills.
Do not spend leveling points on WPS nodes expecting them to activate in the
form.

### Should I use weapon DPS to compare upgrades?

Not by itself. This route values global Cold and Frostburn bonuses, casting
speed, Offensive Ability, Energy Regeneration, skill ranks, and defenses more
than the displayed weapon DPS.

### Is Tsunami or Blizzard better while leveling?

Tsunami is the more reliable early proc. Blizzard becomes stronger after the
build has enough Offensive Ability to land critical hits consistently. I use
Tsunami first and transition toward Blizzard later.

### Why choose Occultist without leveling Summon Familiar?

Occultist preserves the planned Blood of Dreeg, Aspect of the Guardian, and
endgame Familiar route. It does not require splitting the early skill points
before the Wereraven package is complete.

### Should I use Winds of Asterkarn?

It is optional. Use it when the build can sustain the Energy cost and the
encounter allows safe channeling. Ice Talons remains the more mobile core of
this route.

### Can I turn Veteran off after starting?

Yes. Veteran can be toggled. Switch to Normal when the extra enemy durability
or repeated deaths remove the value of its experience bonus.

### When should I take Behemoth?

Take Behemoth when normal clearing remains comfortable but repeated hits force
immediate potions, retreats, or deaths. Fix exposed resistances first.

### When does the Familiar hybrid begin?

The full hybrid begins after the required endgame equipment and Familiar
investment are available. Until then, this guide remains a self-cast Cold and
Frostburn Wereraven leveling route.
