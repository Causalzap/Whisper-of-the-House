<h2 id="unlock-services">
  How I unlock the Ascension and Awakening services
</h2>

I first reach the Kurn settlement and continue its story until I can complete
the **Altar of Ascension** objective.

The first time I found the sacred site, the game told me to earn the Kurn&apos;s
trust. I did not try to unlock it by reaching Level 100 or placing random
materials on the platform. I continued the local quests and returned when the
altar objective became available.

    interactions.

I separate the services by what they actually do:

- the **Altar of Ascension** changes campaign loot and enables selected
  Ascendant mechanics;
- **Jornn the Runekeeper** adds the first Ascended Affix to an item;
- an **Inventor** rerolls the item seed, prefix, suffix, or existing
  Ascended Affix;
- the **Runed Orb** processes Soul Echoes and displays supported Epic
  Awakening recipes;
- **Elder Noltukari** is the named NPC I find inside the hidden Elderveil
  area, not the crafting interface itself.

I do not look for an Epic recipe in the center of the altar. The altar affects
the world; the Runed Orb handles the hidden-area recipes.

The exact reputation or quest threshold for every Elderveil recipe was not
shown clearly in this run, so I do not invent a faction rank. If I can reach
the Orb but a service is missing, I continue the local progression and return
after the next reputation or quest update.

<h3 id="full-mode-versus-altar">
  Full Ascendant Mode versus individual altar mechanics
</h3>

Full Ascendant Mode requires a **Level 100 character on Ultimate**.

I turn it on only after the build can already clear ordinary Ultimate Heroes
and bosses without repeated deaths. A harder mode does not improve my material
income when every run becomes much slower.

The Ascension Altar can also enable individual Ascendant mechanics on other
difficulties. That lets me target particular encounters and rewards without
turning on the complete mode.

I use the altar when I want more chances at features such as:

- Tempest Totems;
- Resplendent Troves;
- enemies or containers Marked by the Void;
- enhanced Roguelike dungeon rewards;
- or other specific mechanics shown in the current altar interface.

I add only the mechanics my build can clear efficiently. Turning on every
option at once is not automatically the fastest material farm.

<h2 id="soul-materials">
  Soul Echoes, Soul Fragments, and Ashes of Awakening
</h2>

These are separate materials with different roles.

<h3 id="soul-echoes">
  How I get Soul Echoes
</h3>

Heroes and bosses can drop echoes of their souls while the relevant Ascendant
mechanics are active.

I therefore prioritize named enemies instead of clearing every ordinary pack.
A useful route gives me several Heroes, bosses, Totems, or high-value
encounters without long empty sections between them.

My basic farming loop is:

<ol className="my-5 list-decimal space-y-2 pl-6">
  <li>Check the current Ascension Altar settings.</li>
  <li>Select only the mechanics the build can clear consistently.</li>
  <li>Choose a route with several named targets.</li>
  <li>Kill enough normal enemies to reach those targets safely.</li>
  <li>Collect every soul-material drop before opening a portal.</li>
  <li>Reset after the valuable encounters are complete.</li>
</ol>

I do not claim that one route is permanently best without recording repeated
runs. The real comparison is time spent, named targets killed, materials
received, and deaths per run.

<h3 id="soul-fragments">
  How I turn Soul Echoes into Soul Fragments
</h3>

Soul Echoes can be processed into Soul Fragments through the **Runed Orb** in
Elderveil.

I open the Orb and use the conversion recipe shown in the current UI. I do
not hard-code an Echo-to-Fragment ratio into this guide until I have captured
the live recipe clearly, because conversion requirements can change between
development versions and hotfixes.

When an Ascension or Alteration service says I am missing Soul Fragments, I
check:

- whether the Echoes are still unprocessed;
- personal inventory;
- personal stash;
- transfer stash;
- another character;
- and the exact Runed Orb recipe requirements.

I do not resume farming until I confirm that I genuinely lack the materials
rather than merely storing them in another location.

<h3 id="ashes-awakening">
  How I get Ashes of Awakening
</h3>

**Ashes of Awakening** are a key material for turning supported Epic items into
their Awakened Legendary versions.

They are primarily associated with Ascendant content. The Altar can also
enable individual mechanics such as Resplendent Troves and Marked by the Void
on other difficulties, giving me ways to look for Ashes without enabling the
complete Ascendant Mode.

My decision is:

- use full Ascendant Mode when the Level 100 Ultimate build clears it
  efficiently;
- use selected altar mechanics when full mode makes each run too slow;
- prioritize Heroes, bosses, upgraded containers, Troves, Totems, and enhanced
  dungeon rewards;
- and save the Ashes until I have inspected the complete Legendary output.

Different Inventor operations may show different costs. I use the current
Alteration panel for each seed, prefix, suffix, or Ascended Affix reroll
instead of assuming that every reroll consumes the same material.

<h2 id="item-ascension">
  How item Ascension works
</h2>

I take the existing item to **Jornn the Runekeeper** near the Ascension Altar.

Ascension does not change an Epic into a Legendary. It adds one extra
**Ascended Affix** to the item I already own.

Ascended Affixes fall into two broad groups:

- regular combat attributes, such as Offensive Ability, Defensive Ability,
  critical damage, or another stat appropriate to the item slot;
- mastery modifiers that affect skills associated with the masteries of the
  character performing the Ascension.

That class connection matters. When I want modifiers from a specific mastery
combination, I perform the Ascension on the character using those masteries.

<h3 id="ascended-affix-tiers">
  Why the item base matters
</h3>

The item&apos;s base category affects the mastery modifiers it can receive.

A common, non-MI base that has normal affixes and is displayed as a Magic item
can access the stronger tier of certain mastery modifiers.

Monster Infrequents, Epics, and Legendaries use the standard tier of mastery
modifiers. They remain valuable because their base statistics and existing
skill support are far more predictable.

I do not Ascend only purple items by habit. I compare the base item, existing
affixes, available modifier tier, and the role the final item needs to fill.

<h3 id="reroll-affix">
  How I reroll an Ascended Affix
</h3>

After the initial Ascension, I take the item to an **Inventor** and open
Alteration.

I choose the exact part I want to reroll rather than replacing the entire item:

- item seed;
- prefix;
- suffix;
- or Ascended Affix.

I read the displayed materials and Iron Bits before confirming. I do not treat
the cost of a seed reroll, normal affix reroll, and Ascended Affix reroll as
one universal recipe.

I stop rerolling when the current result already supports the build or when
another attempt would consume the materials needed to Ascend a more important
slot.



<h2 id="awaken-epic">
  How I Awaken an Epic item
</h2>

Awakening consumes a supported Epic and creates its designed Legendary
version.

The original blue item is not simply recolored. I treat the output as a new
piece of equipment and do not expect an attached Ascended Affix to survive.

My order is:

<ol className="my-5 list-decimal space-y-2 pl-6">
  <li>Preserve any component or augment I do not want to lose.</li>
  <li>Take the unascended Epic to the Runed Orb.</li>
  <li>Find the recipe matching the exact Epic version.</li>
  <li>Inspect the Legendary output before spending anything.</li>
  <li>Pay the Ashes, additional materials, and Iron Bits.</li>
  <li>Compare and equip the newly created Legendary.</li>
  <li>Take that Legendary to Jornn for Ascension afterward.</li>
</ol>

<h3 id="eligible-epics">
  Which Epic items can be Awakened?
</h3>

A blue rarity and a high Required Player Level do not guarantee an Awakening
recipe.

Earlier development information referenced item-level 84 and 94 Epics, while
current live discussions do not fully agree on whether every earlier category
remained in the release build.

I therefore use one reliable rule:

> An Epic is currently Awakenable when its exact version has a visible Runed
> Orb recipe.

I verify:

- the complete item name;
- Normal, Empowered, Mythical, or other version;
- the base item required by the recipe;
- the Legendary output;
- and every material listed by the current UI.

I keep one uncertain copy until I have searched the live recipe list. I do not
collect an entire set based only on an old item-level rule.

I did not find it by remaining on the obvious central path. I moved toward

<ol className="my-5 list-decimal space-y-2 pl-6">

  <li>Test the narrow dark opening instead of trusting only the minimap.</li>
  <li>Follow the concealed route into Elderveil.</li>
  <li>Speak with Elder Noltukari.</li>

<h2 id="inventory">
  How I store Epics and soul materials
</h2>

I keep one dedicated stash section for this system.

I separate:

- confirmed Awakening base items;
- unsupported or uncertain Epics awaiting a recipe check;
- duplicate Epics safe to consume;
- finished Awakened Legendaries;
- Soul Echoes;
- Soul Fragments;
- and Ashes of Awakening.

I do not Ascend the only copy of an Epic that I still intend to Awaken.

Once the current Runed Orb confirms that an item has no recipe and it does not
support my active build, I can sell or dismantle duplicate copies without
checking the same item every session.

<h2 id="faq">
  Soul Echoes and Awakened Epic items FAQ
</h2>

### Can I use another character to Ascend the same item?

Yes, but the character performing the Ascension affects the available mastery
modifier pool. I use the mastery combination that I want the final item to
support.

### Can the Inventor reroll only one part of an item?

Yes. Alteration can target the item seed, prefix, suffix, or Ascended Affix
separately. I check the material cost for the selected operation before
confirming it.

### Why did I receive a defensive-looking mastery modifier?

Mastery modifiers can affect defensive, passive, support, or offensive skills.
A defensive result tied to one of the character&apos;s masteries is not
automatically a regular affix or a bug.
