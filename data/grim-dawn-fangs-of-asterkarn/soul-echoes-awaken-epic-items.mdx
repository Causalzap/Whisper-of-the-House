**Soul Echoes, Soul Fragments, item Ascension, and Epic Awakening are connected,
but they are not interchangeable.**

The mistake that costs the most materials is Ascending a blue Epic first and
Awakening it afterward. Awakening consumes the original Epic and creates a new
Legendary, so I always **Awaken first and Ascend the finished Legendary
second**.

<h2 id="ascension-altar">
  Where I find and unlock the Ascension Altar
</h2>

I reach the Kurn settlement and continue its local story until the **Altar of
Ascension** objective becomes available.

The first time I reached the sacred site, the game still told me to earn the
Kurn&apos;s trust. I did not try to solve that lock with Level 100, random
materials, or repeated interaction with the platform. I returned to Kurnhold,
continued the local quest chain, and came back when the altar objective was
active.

The Altar changes the world and enables selected Ascendant mechanics. It is not
the interface I use to Awaken an Epic, process Soul Echoes, or reroll an item.

<h3 id="full-mode-versus-altar">
  When I use full Ascendant Mode and when I use individual mechanics
</h3>

Full Ascendant Mode requires a **Level 100 character on Ultimate**.

I enable it only when the build can already clear ordinary Ultimate Heroes and
bosses without repeated deaths. A mode that doubles the danger but triples the
run time is not a better material farm.

On other difficulties, I use individual Altar mechanics when I want targeted
encounters such as:

- Tempest Totems;
- Resplendent Troves;
- enemies or containers Marked by the Void;
- upgraded Roguelike dungeon rewards;
- and other options shown in the current Altar interface.

I add mechanics one at a time. When a new option causes repeated deaths or
turns a short route into a slow clear, I disable it before adding another.

<h2 id="jornn-runekeeper">
  Where I find Jornn the Runekeeper
</h2>

**Jornn the Runekeeper** is the Kurnhold service I use to add the first
Ascended Affix to an existing item. He is near the Ascension Altar, but his
service and the Altar itself do different jobs.

My service check is simple:

- **Altar of Ascension:** changes campaign mechanics and rewards;
- **Jornn:** adds the first Ascended Affix;
- **Inventor:** rerolls the seed, prefix, suffix, or an existing Ascended Affix;
- **Runed Orb:** processes Soul Echoes and Awakens supported Epics.

If I can see the Altar but Jornn does not offer the expected service, I finish
the current Kurnhold objective and re-enter the settlement before spending time
searching another region.

<h2 id="soul-materials">
  Soul Echoes vs Soul Fragments
</h2>

Soul Echoes are farming drops. Soul Fragments are processed materials used by
the upgrade services. Owning Echoes does not automatically satisfy a Fragment
cost.

<h3 id="soul-echoes">
  How I farm Soul Echoes
</h3>

Heroes and bosses can drop Soul Echoes while the relevant Ascendant mechanics
are active. I therefore build the route around named targets instead of
clearing every ordinary pack.

My farming loop is:

1. Check the current Altar settings.
2. Enable only the mechanics the build can clear consistently.
3. Choose a route with several Heroes, bosses, Totems, Troves, or other valuable encounters.
4. Kill enough normal enemies to move safely between those targets.
5. Pick up every soul-material drop before opening a portal.
6. Reset after the valuable encounters are complete.

I do not call one route permanently best after a single lucky run. I compare
time spent, named targets killed, materials received, and deaths over several
runs.

I turn back or disable a mechanic when:

- one encounter repeatedly kills the build;
- the route contains long empty sections;
- the extra difficulty produces no useful target density;
- or the run takes so long that an easier route gives more attempts per hour.

<h3 id="soul-fragments">
  How I turn Soul Echoes into Soul Fragments
</h3>

I process Soul Echoes through the **Runed Orb** in Elderveil.

I use the conversion recipe shown in the current interface rather than
hard-coding a permanent Echo-to-Fragment ratio. Before farming again, I check:

- whether the Echoes are still unprocessed;
- personal inventory;
- personal stash;
- transfer stash;
- another character;
- and the complete Runed Orb recipe.

This prevents the common mistake of farming more Echoes while the required
Fragments are already stored elsewhere.

<h3 id="ashes-awakening">
  How I get Ashes of Awakening
</h3>

**Ashes of Awakening** are used to turn supported Epic items into their
Awakened Legendary versions.

I look for them through Ascendant content and selected Altar mechanics such as
upgraded Troves, Totems, marked enemies or containers, Heroes, bosses, and
enhanced dungeon rewards.

My decision is:

- use full Ascendant Mode when the Level 100 Ultimate build clears it efficiently;
- use selected Altar mechanics when full mode makes each run too slow;
- save Ashes until the exact Runed Orb output has been inspected;
- and stop farming one route when deaths or travel time erase the benefit of its extra rewards.

<h2 id="item-ascension">
  How item Ascension and Ascended Affixes work
</h2>

I take the existing item to **Jornn the Runekeeper**.

Ascension does not turn an Epic into a Legendary. It adds one extra
**Ascended Affix** to the item I already own.

Ascended Affixes can provide regular combat attributes or mastery modifiers.
The character performing the Ascension affects the available mastery-modifier
pool, so I use the mastery combination I want the finished item to support.

<h3 id="ascended-affix-tiers">
  Why I compare the item base before Ascending it
</h3>

The base category affects the mastery modifiers an item can receive.

A common non-MI base with normal affixes and Magic rarity can access stronger
versions of some mastery modifiers. Monster Infrequents, Epics, and
Legendaries use the standard tier, but they begin with more predictable
build-specific stats and skill support.

I do not Ascend an item only because it is purple. I compare:

- the base item;
- its current affixes;
- the mastery modifier I actually want;
- the resistance or skill slot it fills;
- and the Soul Fragment cost relative to other upgrades.

<h3 id="reroll-affix">
  How I reroll an Ascended Affix
</h3>

After the first Ascension, I take the item to an **Inventor** and open
Alteration.

I select only the part I want to change:

- item seed;
- prefix;
- suffix;
- or Ascended Affix.

I read the displayed materials and Iron Bits before confirming. I stop
rerolling when the current result already supports the build, or when another
attempt would consume the materials reserved for a more important slot.

<h2 id="awaken-epic">
  How I Awaken Epic items
</h2>

Awakening consumes a supported Epic and creates its designed Legendary
version. I treat the output as a new item and do not expect an Ascended Affix
on the original blue item to survive.

My order is:

1. Preserve any component or augment I do not want to lose.
2. Take the unascended Epic to the Runed Orb.
3. Search for the exact item version.
4. Inspect the Legendary output and every listed cost.
5. Pay the Ashes, additional materials, and Iron Bits.
6. Compare the new Legendary with the item it will replace.
7. Take the finished Legendary to Jornn for Ascension.

<h3 id="eligible-epics">
  Which Epic items can be Awakened?
</h3>

Blue rarity and a high level requirement do not guarantee an Awakening recipe.

I use one reliable rule:

> An Epic is currently Awakenable when its exact version has a visible Runed
> Orb recipe.

I verify the complete item name, its Normal, Empowered, Mythical, or other
version, the required base item, the Legendary output, and every material
listed by the current interface.

I keep one uncertain copy until I have checked the live recipe list. I do not
collect an entire set based only on an older item-level rule.

<h2 id="stuck">
  Soul Echo, Ascension, and Awakening stuck fixes
</h2>

### The Altar of Ascension is still locked

I continue Kurnhold progression until the altar objective becomes available.
Level 100 and random materials do not replace the local quest unlock.

### Heroes and bosses are not dropping Soul Echoes

I check that a relevant Ascendant mechanic is active, then test several named
encounters. One dry kill is not enough evidence that the system is broken.

### Jornn or the Inventor says I am missing Soul Fragments

I check the Runed Orb conversion recipe, inventory, personal stash, transfer
stash, and other characters before farming more Echoes.

### I cannot find Elderveil

I return to Guardian&apos;s Sorrow, move west, and hug the outer wall. The
entrance is a narrow dark opening that is easier to miss when I stay on the
central path.

### The Runed Orb has no recipe for my Epic

I search the exact item name and version. If the matching recipe is not visible,
I keep one copy and continue the relevant local progression instead of
dismantling it immediately.

### My Ascended Affix disappeared after Awakening

That happens because Awakening consumes the original Epic and creates a new
Legendary. I Awaken an unascended Epic first, then Ascend the finished
Legendary.

<h2 id="faq">
  Ascension Altar and Soul Echoes FAQ
</h2>

### Are Soul Echoes and Soul Fragments the same material?

No. Soul Echoes are farming drops. Soul Fragments are processed materials used
by Ascension and Alteration services. I use the Runed Orb in Elderveil to
convert them according to the current recipe.

### Should I Awaken an Epic before Ascending it?

Yes. Awakening consumes the Epic and creates a new Legendary item. I Awaken
first and take the finished Legendary to Jornn afterward.

### Can another character Ascend the same item?

Yes, but the character performing the Ascension affects the mastery-modifier
pool. I use the mastery combination I want the final item to support.

### Can the Inventor reroll only one part of an item?

Yes. Alteration can target the seed, prefix, suffix, or Ascended Affix
separately. I check the displayed cost for the selected operation before
confirming it.
