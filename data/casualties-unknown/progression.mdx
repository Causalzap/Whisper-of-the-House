<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
Descend through **Layers 1–9** to reach **Where We Must Reach (Layer 10)**. Retrieve the cargo, use the **high-powered drill pod**, and climb upward through **Crimson Ascent (Layer 11)** to reach the surface. Because the caves are procedurally generated, focus on the hazards and objectives of each layer rather than following fixed left-or-right directions.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

The first five layers teach you how easily a fall, lost light, or untreated injury can ruin your run. Layers 6–9 introduce freezing temperatures, constant rain, unpredictable crystal effects, and volcanic heat. After that comes the cargo retrieval and a final climb back toward the surface.

Reaching the deepest layer is not the same as finishing the game. You must still collect the cargo, leave the bottom of the cave, and survive Crimson Ascent.

Your route through individual caves can change between runs. A shaft that offered a safe landing before may now contain a turret, dangerous terrain, or an opening that leaves you stranded.

The normal journey goes downward through the first ten layers. Crimson Ascent reverses the direction: you must travel upward to escape.

<h2 id="what-to-prepare-before-descending">
  What Should You Prepare Before Descending?
</h2>

Before entering a new layer, prepare for its specific hazards. Keep reliable lighting for the darker caves, secure enough protection against the cold before Frozen Chasm, and preserve your ability to move and recover before entering the hotter lower layers.

If your current injuries make climbing or landing unsafe, recover before crossing into the next area. For equipment recipes and INT requirements, see the <Link href="/casualties-unknown/crafting">crafting guide</Link>. For treating bleeding, infections, or fractures, use the <Link href="/casualties-unknown/medical">medical guide</Link>.

<h2 id="layer-1-gravel-lands">
  Layer 1 — Gravel Lands: Get Equipped Before Going Deeper
</h2>

Gravel Lands gives you time to establish the essentials, but its apparently manageable terrain can still punish careless movement.

Gravel, soil, pools of groundwater, wooden structures, and natural openings create opportunities to explore sideways before making a major descent. Some areas receive natural light, but you should not depend on that lighting farther down.

**Check the reachable ledges before your first major descent.** Search for a landing point you can see and a route that will not leave you trapped below.

Because the layout changes between runs, follow the safest available terrain rather than repeating a fixed search pattern. For the first-run equipment and inventory priorities, see the <Link href="/casualties-unknown">beginner guide</Link>.

<h2 id="layer-2-deeper-gravel-lands">
  Layer 2 — Deeper Gravel Lands: Prepare for Darkness
</h2>

The second layer resembles the first, but natural lighting becomes less reliable and the terrain introduces more hazards.

Bear traps, barbed fences, cave ticks, and other dangers make poor visibility especially costly.

**Keep your light accessible and inspect the ground ahead before stepping onto an unfamiliar platform.**

If you enter a dark passage while already injured, even a small trap may create enough damage to threaten the rest of your run.

Water crossings also require care. Do not use a pool as a landing target without knowing whether you can climb out.

If you encounter a repairable drill pod, check whether skipping ahead would leave you short on supplies. See <Link href="#should-you-skip-layers">when to use drill pod shortcuts</Link> before committing to the jump.

### Before leaving Layer 2

Check your light, food, water, and basic medical supplies. The next layer offers more exposed terrain and fewer convenient water sources.

<h2 id="layer-3-dried-desert">
  Layer 3 — Dried Desert: Manage Water and Exposed Terrain
</h2>

Dried Desert replaces the smaller gravel passages with wider spaces, sand, hard stone, and longer drops.

Groundwater is less common. Turrets, landmines, and other machinery can threaten routes that initially appear open.

The difficulty comes from combining exposure with movement. A large gap may have a usable crossing, but the safest-looking platform can be in a turret's firing line.

### How Do You Cross Open Ground Under Turret Fire?

Dried Desert has exposed crossings where turrets and landmines can make an otherwise reachable platform dangerous. Identify the turret's firing line before leaving cover, and inspect the landing area for explosives.

If a turret controls the direct route, look for solid terrain that breaks its line of sight or choose another crossing. Do not stop to rearrange equipment while exposed to its fire.

<h2 id="layer-4-wasteland">
  Layer 4 — Wasteland: Survive Sound Cannons and Radiation
</h2>

Wasteland combines radioactive material with mechanical traps.

Toxirock, spent fuel, landmines, turrets, and sound cannons can make an exposed path hazardous before you have even reached the next drop.

The safest approach is to inspect suspicious terrain and avoid spending more time than necessary beside dangerous materials.

<h3 id="how-to-avoid-sound-cannons">
  How Do You Avoid Sound Cannons?
</h3>

A sound cannon charges when it detects you within its effective range and line of sight.

**When you notice the warning, move out of its line of sight instead of continuing into open terrain.**

Release 1.0 added a directional indicator for sound cannons. Use that information to identify the threat and choose nearby cover.

The response is straightforward:

1. Stop approaching the detected threat.
2. Identify the direction of the cannon.
3. Get behind solid terrain when possible.
4. Avoid jumping blindly into another trap.
5. Check your condition before continuing.

Destroying the cannon is not always necessary. If another route lets you avoid the encounter, saving your health and supplies may be more useful.

<h3 id="wasteland-radiation">
  Avoid Unnecessary Radiation Exposure
</h3>

Radiation creates a problem that may continue affecting your run after you have left the immediate hazard.

Do not stand beside suspicious materials while organizing supplies or treating an unrelated injury if a safer position is available.

If you are already badly hurt, stabilize your condition before entering another exposed area.

<h2 id="layer-5-overgrown-depths">
  Layer 5 — Overgrown Depths: Avoid Getting Trapped by Thornbacks
</h2>

Overgrown Depths contains dense vegetation, plant resources, and hostile creatures.

Grabber plants, skullcrushers, wall biters, cave ticks, and Thornbacks make movement more dangerous than the abundant foliage might suggest.

The extra plant material can help you rebuild supplies after the dry layers. However, the same environment creates places where your character can be grabbed or trapped.

<h3 id="elder-thornback">
  What Should You Do When an Elder Thornback Appears?
</h3>

The Elder Thornback is a particularly dangerous creature that can pursue you through terrain.

Do not assume a thin wall will permanently block it. The creature can dig through material while chasing you.

For a run focused on reaching the cargo, **escaping an unnecessary fight is often more valuable than forcing a kill**.

Keep moving toward usable terrain, avoid being pinned against a wall, and use the distance you create to continue your descent.

A prepared player may choose to fight, but doing so while injured or low on supplies can leave you unable to survive the next layer.

<h3 id="prepare-for-frozen-chasm">
  Prepare for the Frozen Chasm
</h3>

This is a good place to check your food, clothing, medical supplies, and available tools before entering a much colder environment.

The next layer continuously pressures your body temperature. Avoid leaving while exhausted, seriously injured, or unable to recover from cold exposure.

<h2 id="layer-6-frozen-chasm">
  Layer 6 — Frozen Chasm: How to Stay Warm
</h2>

Frozen Chasm introduces persistent cold and icy terrain.

Slippery surfaces, freezing water, and long periods away from shelter can make your temperature fall dangerously low.

You may also encounter Snow Striders, gunmines, and sharp ice formations. These hazards matter, but the continuous cold should shape your movement through the whole layer.

<h3 id="frozen-chasm-heat-sources">
  Find Heatferns and Other Warm Places
</h3>

Heatferns warm the surrounding air, making them useful recovery points when your body temperature drops. However, prolonged exposure to their spores may affect your immune system. Recover enough warmth to continue, then move toward the next safe section.

Ion space heaters and life pods can also provide opportunities to recover when you find them.

Do not assume that every warm location is suitable for staying indefinitely. Inspect your condition while recovering, then plan the next part of your descent.

A practical sequence is to reach warmth, check the route ahead, and continue once you are stable enough to travel.

<h3 id="frozen-chasm-cold-water">
  Avoid Freezing Water
</h3>

Cold water can make an already difficult layer significantly worse.

A shorter route through water may expose you to wetness and temperature loss, leaving you struggling to recover afterward.

If your temperature is falling rapidly, prioritize reaching a safe heat source rather than repeatedly stopping in exposed terrain.

Check whether your character is wet, whether your insulation is sufficient, and whether there is a reachable source of warmth.

### Before leaving Layer 6

Recover enough temperature and mobility for the next area. Fungal Pools introduces constant rain, so your ability to rest safely will remain important.

<h2 id="layer-7-fungal-pools">
  Layer 7 — Fungal Pools: Rain and Mushroom Traps
</h2>

Fungal Pools is a wet, mushroom-filled environment where the threats are not always immediately recognizable.

Rain makes recovery difficult, while fungal growths may affect movement or attack players passing nearby.

You may encounter Bouncecaps, Side Stabbers, grabber-shrooms, and other mushroom hazards.

<h3 id="fungal-pools-rain">
  Find Shelter Before Resting
</h3>

Sleeping on an exposed platform while wet and cold may make your condition worse.

Look for a suitable enclosed or protected location before committing to a long rest.

A stable platform is not necessarily a safe recovery position if rain continues reaching your character.

The same applies to treatment. If you need to recover from an injury, first try to reach a location where the environment is not continuously making the situation worse.

<h3 id="fungal-pools-traps">
  How Do You Recognize Mushroom Traps?
</h3>

Side Stabbers can attack passing players with a large spike. Their position can make them difficult to notice until you are close.

Inspect unfamiliar growths along the walls before moving past them.

Bouncecaps can change your movement unexpectedly. They may help you cross terrain, but relying on an uncontrolled bounce near a large gap is risky.

Avoid moving blindly beneath unfamiliar ceiling growths as well.

You do not need to interact with every mushroom to reach the bottom of the layer. Identify a safe route and continue.

Do not assume unfamiliar fungal flesh is safe to eat. Some mushrooms can cause sickness or temperature changes, so inspect them before using them as emergency food.

<h2 id="layer-8-crystalline-hollow">
  Layer 8 — Crystalline Hollow: Understand Crystal Effects
</h2>

Crystalline Hollow contains large open spaces and crystals with different properties. Some alter movement, while others can teleport your character or cause dangerous effects.

**Hover over unfamiliar crystals before approaching them.** Check whether the crystal is marked as anomalous, and avoid touching it until you know what it does. Color and brightness alone are not reliable indicators of safety.

<h3 id="crystalline-hollow-gravity">
  Watch for Gravity Crystals
</h3>

Gravity effects may pull or push your character, changing the movement you expected from a normal jump.

Before entering the effect, inspect the terrain around you. A crystal that looks useful in an open space can become dangerous when there is a large drop nearby.

If a movement effect causes you to lose control, avoid attempting another difficult jump until you understand what is happening.

<h3 id="crystalline-hollow-teleportation">
  Be Careful with Teleportation Crystals
</h3>

Some crystals can move you to another position.

Do not treat an unfamiliar crystal as a shortcut without considering where your character may end up.

A poorly timed interaction could leave you in a location where your light, equipment, or current injuries make the next movement harder.

<h3 id="crystalline-hollow-open-spaces">
  Do Not Rush Across Open Gaps
</h3>

Large spaces can make it difficult to judge safe landing distances.

Inspect the available platforms and breakable terrain before dropping. If a direct jump is unreliable, find a more controlled path.

The layer may also contain unusual hostile manifestations. Avoid prolonged encounters around unstable crystals when you can safely continue downward.

<h2 id="layer-9-geothermal-vents">
  Layer 9 — Geothermal Vents: Avoid Magma and Overheating
</h2>

Geothermal Vents adds extreme environmental heat to an already dangerous descent.

Magma, hot surfaces, geysers, sawblades, and moving machinery can make a narrow route deadly.

The heat can become dangerous even when you are not standing directly in magma.

<h3 id="geothermal-vents-temperature">
  Watch Your Body Temperature
</h3>

Moving, climbing, and digging can add exertion while the surrounding environment is already hot.

If you notice dangerous temperature conditions, find a safer place and reduce unnecessary activity.

Do not stop directly beside magma or active machinery merely because you need to rest. The location must be safe enough for recovery.

Before digging through a thick wall, check whether another route would require less exertion and fewer tool resources.

<h3 id="geothermal-vents-cooling">
  Look for Cooling Opportunities
</h3>

Cold Puffs can provide cooling in this layer.

They can be valuable when your temperature becomes dangerous, but the interaction may also produce other effects. Check your condition afterward instead of assuming that cooling solves every problem.

Air-conditioned rooms can also provide relief from the surrounding heat when encountered.

An Ice Pack is another useful temporary resource if you already have one available.

The goal is to reduce heat exposure enough to continue through the region, not remain indefinitely in dangerous terrain.

Inspect narrow passages for geysers, pistons, sawblades, and other mechanical traps before jumping or climbing into them.

Some objects that look like ordinary environmental structures may be hostile machinery. Approach unfamiliar objects cautiously.

If you rely on a powered digging tool, monitor its remaining power. Losing access to it while trapped near extreme heat can force you into risky manual digging.

<h2 id="layer-10-where-we-must-reach">
  Layer 10 — Where We Must Reach: Find the Cargo
</h2>

Where We Must Reach is the deepest part of the normal descent and contains the cargo you were sent to recover.

The area combines several dangerous elements from previous layers, including turrets, sound cannons, landmines, sawblades, and difficult terrain.

Your priority is to reach the cargo and complete the high-powered drill pod sequence.

<h3 id="layer-10-find-cargo">
  Step 1: Reach the Cargo
</h3>

Continue downward through the available cave route, but do not assume the shortest drop is safe.

Inspect machinery and exposed terrain before passing through. If a route is blocked, check for breakable material or an alternative way around the obstruction.

Avoid wasting your remaining equipment on an unnecessarily difficult passage.

When you reach the final cargo area, identify the objective and the nearby high-powered drill pod.

<h3 id="layer-10-drill-pod">
  Step 2: Use the High-Powered Drill Pod
</h3>

The high-powered drill pod is tied to the cargo retrieval objective.

**Retrieve the cargo before attempting to leave.** Once the objective has been obtained, use the drill pod to move away from the deepest part of the cave.

The cargo becomes embedded in your character during the final mission sequence.

<h2 id="layer-11-crimson-ascent">
  Layer 11 — Crimson Ascent: How to Reach the Surface
</h2>

The high-powered drill pod brings you into Crimson Ascent, but it does not carry you to the surface. From here, search upward for reachable ledges and climbable terrain rather than continuing the descent.

<h3 id="crimson-ascent-climbing">
  Step 1: Find a Safe Upward Route
</h3>

Look for accessible ledges, breakable terrain, and structures that can help you gain height.

If a jump repeatedly fails, do not keep forcing it until you are exhausted or injured.

Search the surrounding terrain for another approach. A slightly longer climb is preferable to losing your remaining health near the end of the run.

<h3 id="crimson-ascent-resources">
  Step 2: Use Available Resources
</h3>

The area contains unusual red vegetation and other structures that may provide useful materials or climbing opportunities.

Collect what you need to continue, but avoid unnecessary detours.

If your character is injured, use a stable position to recover enough mobility before attempting another difficult climb.

<h3 id="crimson-ascent-surface">
  Step 3: Break Through to the Surface
</h3>

Keep moving upward until you reach the final barrier between the cave and the surface.

Breaking through completes the cave traversal and begins the surface sequence.

**Reaching the surface is the progression goal.** The events afterward depend on your character's preparation and the ending conditions you have satisfied.

For Bio-pod, Escape, and M.E.R.C. requirements, see <Link href="/casualties-unknown/endings">how to get all three endings</Link>.

<h2 id="should-you-skip-layers">
  Should You Use Drill Pods to Skip Layers?
</h2>

Repairable drill pods can let you bypass part of the normal descent.

This may save time and reduce exposure to danger, but it can also mean missing valuable supplies or equipment.

Use a shortcut when your current inventory and condition are suitable for the layer ahead. If you still need food, medical supplies, or essential tools, skipping into a more dangerous region may leave you worse prepared.

If you are collecting M.E.R.C. components, check the <Link href="/casualties-unknown/endings">ending-specific requirements</Link> before taking a shortcut.

The high-powered drill pod in Layer 10 serves a different purpose. It is part of the final cargo retrieval sequence rather than an optional shortcut through early areas.
