<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
For your first **Casualties: Unknown** run, choose a manageable difficulty, inspect the area around the starting pod, and **search nearby ledges before dropping into a deep shaft**. Keep your light accessible, secure your inventory, collect useful early materials, and avoid entering the next layer while badly injured. You can **Save and Quit at a layer transition**; quitting in the middle of a layer is not the same thing. The full game has **11 layers**, with the cargo near the bottom and a final ascent back to the surface.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

One bad fall can create several problems at once. You may injure a leg, drop your light, land beside a trap, and discover that the only way forward requires another jump. That makes preparation more important than reaching a particular depth quickly.

You do not need to master every recipe or medical item before descending. Start with the controls and a stable inventory. Once you can reliably move, loot, and identify danger, the remaining systems become much easier to manage.

<h2 id="first-run-settings">
  Which Settings Should You Choose for Your First Run?
</h2>

The default **Tough** preset is a challenging introduction. If you are still learning the movement and inventory system, **Relaxed** or **Paradise** gives you more room to experiment. You can move to a harder preset once you know how to survive a bad fall and recover your equipment.

<h2 id="first-five-minutes">
  What Should You Do in the First Five Minutes?
</h2>

Your first priority is not digging as far down as possible. Establish a route that lets you collect basic supplies **without committing to a fall you cannot recover from**.

1. **Check your landing area.** Look around the starting pod for reachable objects, vegetation, containers, and materials. Do not immediately break the floor below you.
2. **Find and secure a light source.** Put it in a hand or inventory position you can reliably access. Keep a backup if you find another suitable light-emitting item.
3. **Search sideways first.** Check nearby ledges and short passages before choosing the next drop. The map is generated differently between runs, so this is a habit rather than a fixed route.
4. **Collect useful materials, but do not overload yourself.** Foliage, plastic, and other common items become valuable when you have an immediate use for them. Leave unneeded bulk behind.
5. **Check your hands and storage before jumping.** An item you are carrying loosely can be lost or become difficult to recover after a fall.
6. **Descend only when you can identify a landing.** If the bottom is not visible, look for a ledge, rope, or another controllable way down.

<h2 id="hands-mouth-inventory">
  How Do Hands, the Mouth Slot, and Inventory Work?
</h2>

Casualties: Unknown does not treat every item as weightless loot. What you hold, where you store it, and how you move it can change the outcome of a jump or a fight.

Your hands are active equipment positions. You can switch between them, but you may still need to free a hand to interact with the environment. Certain objects can also be held in the **mouth**, which can make it easier to carry something while your hands are busy. It is useful, but it is not a replacement for a bag.

A good routine is to finish sorting your inventory **before** stepping onto a narrow ledge. Keep the item you need for the next action easy to reach, and move important supplies into a suitable container rather than leaving them loose on the ground.

### Where did your item go?

If something slips out of your hands or lands behind terrain, **hold Ctrl to show nearby item labels**. Search the ground around the last place you handled it, including lower ledges and the area beside a wall.

If you find the item but cannot pick it up, check for a blocked path or whether your hands and accessible container slots are already occupied. Moving to a better position is often safer than repeatedly grabbing at it beside a large drop.

<h2 id="survive-first-drop">
  How Do You Avoid Losing a Run to One Fall?
</h2>

You can survive a drop and still be in serious danger. A fractured limb makes the next jump harder, while a lost light makes it difficult to tell whether the next surface is safe. Water, moving machinery, and an enemy below the ledge can turn the landing into another problem.

Before a large drop, **look for a place to land and a way to move again afterward**. If there is no obvious platform, check the surrounding walls for a safer route or a place where you can descend in smaller steps.

If the screen shakes or the ground begins moving, avoid making unnecessary corrections while you are off balance. First regain a stable position, then assess where your equipment has landed.

### Is water a safe landing spot?

Not automatically. A pool may slow a fall, but wetness, temperature, depth, and your remaining mobility all matter. You may land safely only to become trapped without enough strength to climb out.

Do not assume that water found underground is safe to drink either. Read the liquid or item description before consuming it, and keep the need for drinking water separate from a decision about crossing a pool.

If you are injured after landing, inspect your condition before trying another jump. Use <Link href="/casualties-unknown/medical">the medical guide</Link> to identify bleeding, fractures, dislocations, or other injuries rather than guessing which item will fix them.

<h2 id="survival-warnings">
  Which Survival Warnings Matter Most?
</h2>

Watch the condition warnings while exploring. Hunger, thirst, fatigue, pain, body temperature, and blood loss can combine until an otherwise manageable room becomes difficult to escape.

Three checks are worth making before entering an unfamiliar shaft:

- **Can you still move reliably?** Severe pain, exhaustion, and damage to a leg or arm can make a safe-looking jump unreliable.
- **Can you see the route and recover your equipment?** A light that is about to fail or a full bag increases the cost of a mistake.
- **Are conditions already getting worse?** Ongoing bleeding, cold exposure, or another increasing warning is a reason to stabilize before dropping farther down.

Open the health panel when a warning does not match what you can see on the character. The body diagram and overall vital signs help distinguish an ordinary wound from a deeper problem. The <Link href="/casualties-unknown/medical">medical treatment guide</Link> explains which items address each condition.

<h2 id="open-crates-lockpicking">
  How Does Lockpicking Work in Casualties: Unknown?
</h2>

Crates can contain food, medicine, equipment, or a better way to carry your supplies. They are worth checking when it is safe to stop, but lockpicking can injure the hand you are using.

To open a locked crate, start the interaction and look at the **semicircular arc above the keyhole**. Choose a point on the arc and try turning the lock. The closer the position is to the correct spot, the farther the lock turns.

1. **Try a position on the arc.** Watch how far the lock turns instead of holding the attempt blindly.
2. **Release when the lock resists or shakes.** Continuing to force it can damage your claws and hand.
3. **Adjust the selected position.** Try a nearby angle and compare the result.
4. **Repeat until the lock opens.** The goal is to rotate it fully, not to win by applying more force.
5. **Secure the reward.** Move useful items to a safe container before continuing through unstable terrain.

A **Lock-picking Kit** can make repeated attempts safer by taking damage instead of your claws. In Release 1.0, a usable kit can be selected from anywhere in your inventory rather than needing to sit in a particular slot. You still need to approach the crate from safe ground; lockpicking while bleeding or being attacked is a poor trade.

<h2 id="loot-code-crates">
  How Do You Open Code Crates?
</h2>

When you reach a **Code Crate**, take time to read its combination and enter the digits carefully. Split a long number into small groups rather than rushing through it, and confirm that the interface is accepting the input method you are using.

A code crate may reward useful storage or other equipment. Once it opens, check the floor and nearby geometry before moving the reward. An item can fall into a narrow space or become blocked by terrain even when you have successfully unlocked the container.

Blueprints and ePDAs are also worth examining rather than carrying indefinitely without reading them. They can expand what you know about the world and available equipment. If a new recipe appears but you cannot make it yet, open the crafting menu and check its actual material and INT requirements rather than gathering ingredients blindly.

Avoid using an unidentified medicine or unfamiliar item solely because the tooltip looks promising. In a survival run, discovering an item through a bad side effect is not always worth the gamble.

<h2 id="save-quit">
  How Do You Save and Quit a Run?
</h2>

**The normal Save and Quit opportunity appears at the transition between layers.** When you reach the bottom of a layer, the game presents a choice to continue or save and exit. Choose **Save and Quit** if you want to resume the run later.

Do not mistake an ordinary menu exit in the middle of a layer for that save opportunity. If you intend to stop playing, aim to reach the next layer transition before leaving the run.

There is also a practical reason to prepare before reaching the very bottom: the transition prompt interrupts normal movement until you choose how to proceed. Secure essential equipment and assess your condition before committing to the exit.

<h2 id="characters-modes">
  How Do You Unlock Milky and Dune?
</h2>

You begin with **Experiment**. Completing runs unlocks the remaining playable characters in sequence:

**Experiment → Milky → Dune.**

The characters have different abilities and dialogue, but you do not need to wait for another character before learning the basic movement and survival systems. A completed run with Experiment unlocks Milky; completing a run with Milky then unlocks Dune.

If you are working toward a particular ending, check the run settings before starting. **Escape** requires its Unchipped preparation, while **M.E.R.C.** has separate conditions involving a later chipped run and items collected across multiple layers.

The exact requirements for Bio-pod, Escape, and M.E.R.C. are in <Link href="/casualties-unknown/endings">how to get all three endings</Link>.

<h2 id="how-far-do-you-go">
  How Far Do You Need to Descend to Finish the Game?
</h2>

The full game has **11 layers**. The cargo is located in Layer 10, **Where We Must Reach**, and the return journey continues through **Crimson Ascent**, where you must climb toward the surface.

The caves change between runs, so the exact paths and obstacles will not always appear in the same positions. For the hazards and objectives of each area, follow the <Link href="/casualties-unknown/progression">11-layer progression walkthrough</Link>.

<h2 id="demo-vs-full-game">
  Does the Demo Have the Same Content as the Full Game?
</h2>

No. The Demo covers the opening five layers, while the **full game released on November 9, 2026** and added Layers 6–11, endings, two more playable characters, Endless mode, and other changes.

The Demo remains available, but it is not kept in step with the full-release mechanics. If you see an older recipe, run-setting name, or saving tip that conflicts with your current game, use the information displayed in the version you are playing.
