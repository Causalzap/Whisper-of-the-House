<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
For your first run, turn foliage into **Rope** and **Canvas**, then make a **Foliage Bag** for storage. Prepare a **Dressing** using spare rippable materials, and work toward a **Canteen** with plastic, Rope, and Bio-chem Fluid from Glowplant Fruit. After those essentials, pursue a cutting tool and a **Material Pouch** when you have the required parts. Press **C** to check the crafting menu, and always inspect the recipe's **INT, item condition, and tool-quality requirements** before committing materials.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

Before converting all your foliage into Canvas, reserve the Rope needed for the item you intend to finish. Several early recipes share the same materials, and spending a component on the wrong craft can leave you unable to complete the equipment you actually need.

<h2 id="how-crafting-works">
  How Do You Use the Crafting Menu?
</h2>

Press **C** to open crafting. The menu separates different kinds of recipes, including materials, tools, equipment, and medical supplies. Selecting a recipe shows its ingredients, any required tool properties, and the Intelligence needed to craft it reliably.

You can also drag an item onto the crafting button to see recipes that use it. This is useful when you find unfamiliar scrap, plants, or machinery and want to know whether the item has value beyond selling it.

Crafting can use some materials lying nearby on the ground. You do not always need to place every ingredient into your hands first, although the items must still be accessible to the crafting system. When you find a recipe you plan to make soon, **pin it** so you can track missing ingredients without repeatedly reopening and searching the list.

### Read the ingredient labels, not just their pictures

A recipe may ask for a **specific item**, such as Canvas, or an item carrying a particular **quality**, such as Foliage, Cutting, Hammering, or Rippable.

That distinction explains many early failures. Three items with Foliage quality can satisfy Canvas even if they are not all the same plant. Conversely, an ordinary object that looks sharp does not automatically meet a particular Cutting requirement unless the game lists that quality.

Some recipes also impose a minimum condition. A damaged ingredient can have the correct name but still fail the recipe check.

### INT changes how risky the craft is

The displayed **INT Needed** value is an important requirement, not decorative information. When your Intelligence is lower than the recipe asks for, attempting it can produce a worse result or fail, and more difficult attempts can carry additional penalties.

For essential equipment, avoid repeatedly attempting a recipe beyond your current INT merely because you have the materials. Reading blueprints and ePDAs, interacting with traders, and making new items can help you progress your knowledge and crafting options. Check the actual recipe value before deciding whether to attempt it.

<h2 id="rope-canvas-foliage-bag">
  How Do You Make a Foliage Bag?
</h2>

The Foliage Bag is an early way to stop juggling supplies between your hands and the ground. Its core crafting chain is simple:

1. Gather **Foliage-quality materials** from reachable vegetation.
2. Make **one Rope** from two Foliage-quality ingredients.
3. Make **two Canvas** from three Foliage-quality ingredients each.
4. Combine the **Rope + two Canvas** to create a **Foliage Bag**.
5. Equip the bag, move your important supplies into it, and protect it from accidental use in another recipe.

If all the ingredients come from ordinary foliage, that chain uses eight Foliage-quality items in total. You can complete the steps as you collect the materials instead of storing a large pile of loose foliage first.

<h3 id="canvas-ingredients">
  Why Won't Canvas Craft?
</h3>

Canvas requires **three items with Foliage quality** and has an **INT 2** requirement. The key is the ingredient property. Having three miscellaneous plants is not enough if an item does not actually meet the displayed Foliage quality.

Once the bag is finished, **favourite it with F** when possible. Some storage items are also Rippable crafting materials. Protecting your active bag prevents the crafting menu from treating it as disposable material for a later recipe.

Do not make three Foliage Bags simply because an older build or a particular loadout suggested it. Make another only when your current storage is genuinely stopping you from carrying necessary equipment. A good found backpack or duffel bag may solve that problem without further crafting.

<h2 id="craft-dressing">
  How Do You Craft a Dressing?
</h2>

Once you have storage, prepare a way to carry basic first aid. The regular **Dressing** recipe takes **two Ripped Dressings**, each meeting a **greater-than-90% condition requirement**, and asks for **INT 5**.

The Ripped Dressing is made using an appropriate Rippable item. Use spare materials rather than sacrificing the bag or clothing you currently depend on.

A practical sequence is to identify spare Rippable material, make the required Ripped Dressings, check their condition, and then craft the finished Dressing. If a piece is too worn, adding another low-condition piece will not satisfy the threshold automatically.

<h2 id="canteen-bio-chem-fluid">
  How Do You Make a Canteen and Bio-chem Fluid?
</h2>

A Foliage Bag stores items, but it does not replace a liquid container. A **Canteen** lets you carry water or useful crafting liquids rather than depending on whatever container happens to be nearby.

The early Canteen chain uses **two Chunks of Plastic**, **one Rope**, and **10 mL Bio-chem Fluid**. The common Bio-chem Fluid recipe turns a **Glowplant Fruit** into 10 mL of fluid.

1. Save plastic chunks you find near the starting area or salvage from suitable objects.
2. Keep one Rope available rather than turning every piece of foliage into Canvas.
3. Collect a Glowplant Fruit; keep a backup light source if you are relying on the fruit for illumination.
4. Produce the Bio-chem Fluid needed for the recipe and avoid leaving important liquid in temporary storage.
5. Craft the Canteen, then decide whether it is better used for water or further Bio-chem Fluid in your current run.

**Do not confuse liquid storage with water purification.** A Canteen gives you a place to put liquid; it does not automatically make unsafe groundwater drinkable. Producing clean water requires a separate process and appropriate heat source.

If you find an intact container early, you can postpone a second Canteen. The point is to have enough reliable liquid storage to support the next stage of the run, not to craft every container variation.

<h2 id="material-pouch">
  Is the Material Pouch Worth Crafting?
</h2>

Yes, once you have the tools and materials for it. The **Material Pouch** attaches to the thigh and is designed to carry **crafting materials**. It is not a general-purpose replacement for a Foliage Bag or duffel bag.

That distinction matters when you are carrying scrap metal, wood pieces, processed materials, and ingredients for multiple recipes. A regular bag can hold the items you want to use or consume; the Material Pouch is valuable because it makes the material side of your inventory more manageable.

### How do you work toward a Material Pouch?

The pouch is a more involved project than a Foliage Bag. It uses a chain of Rope or String, processed wood materials, Canvas, and Nails, together with a tool that provides Hammering quality. Its recipe asks for a higher INT value than the opening bag.

If the recipe is still blocked, work backward from the missing part. A typical route involves gathering wood scraps for processed wood, obtaining usable scrap metal for metal intermediates, and making Nails once you have suitable Cutting capability. In a release-version crafting run, producing a Scrap Tube also provided access to a useful Hammering-quality tool.

Before assembling the Material Pouch, check that you still have the required Rope. Making another cutting tool or intermediate can consume materials you had reserved, leaving you with the Wood Panels and Nails but no way to finish the pouch.

**Release 1.0 removed the Material Pouch's previous low item-weight restriction.** It can now hold eligible crafting materials such as Scrap Metal, Chunks of Plastic, and Circuit Boards. It is still intended for materials rather than food, medicine, or general equipment.

The Wood Scraps recipe also changed in the full release, so check its current ingredient requirements before processing your remaining wood.

This is an upgrade to work toward while your run is stable. If you already have reliable storage and urgently need a light, first aid, or a usable cutting tool, solve that immediate problem first.

<h2 id="knives-wood-metal">
  Which Tools and Materials Should You Make After the First Bag?
</h2>

A **Flimsy Knife** is a useful next objective because Cutting quality opens further recipes. The crafting interface requires suitable scrap metal; importantly, the material must meet the recipe's condition requirement. Collecting a large amount of badly damaged metal is not the same as having an accepted ingredient.

Once you have a cutting tool, several material chains become more practical:

- **Wood Scraps → Wood Cube → Wood Panel:** useful for structures, equipment, and more advanced recipes. Check the current Wood Scraps recipe, because the 1.0 release changed it.
- **Scrap Metal → Scrap Cube → Nails / Scrap Tube:** useful when recipes call for Nails or a component with a tool property.
- **Rope → String:** useful for equipment and storage upgrades requiring more refined binding materials.

Do not convert all your raw materials into one intermediate immediately. A later recipe may need the original scrap, Rope, or Canvas instead of the processed version.

A tool-quality requirement works differently from an ingredient that gets consumed. When the menu asks for Cutting or Hammering, the tool may be needed to perform the craft rather than become part of the finished object. Inspect the recipe before assuming a tool will be destroyed.

### How Do You Craft a Drill Repair Kit?

Release 1.0 simplified the **Drill Repair Kit** recipe. Inspect its current ingredient and tool requirements before spending materials on repairs, and check <Link href="/casualties-unknown/progression">whether taking a drill pod shortcut is worth it</Link> before committing those supplies.

<h2 id="crafting-not-working">
  Why Is Crafting Not Working?
</h2>

If every ingredient appears to be present but the craft still will not work, read the requirement panel one line at a time. The most useful clues are usually an unchecked ingredient, a condition threshold, or an unmet tool quality.

<h2 id="how-to-get-int">
  How Do You Get More INT for Crafting?
</h2>

INT is worth improving because it determines how reliably you can make more demanding items. The benefit is practical: better tools, more sophisticated storage, and advanced equipment become easier to obtain without wasting materials.

Look for **blueprints and ePDAs** as you explore, and use the opportunities available from new crafting discoveries or traders. Rather than collecting every raw ingredient in advance, choose one useful recipe just above your current level and work toward both its requirements and the INT needed to make it reliably.

If you plan to make the M.E.R.C. Scarf, check its <Link href="/casualties-unknown/endings">required chips and INT threshold</Link> before using those components in another recipe.

Once your essential equipment is ready, check the hazards and preparation requirements for your next destination in the <Link href="/casualties-unknown/progression">layer-by-layer progression guide</Link>.
