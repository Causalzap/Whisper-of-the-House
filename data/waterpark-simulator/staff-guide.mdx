I do not use one fixed hiring order in Waterpark Simulator.

My first worker depends on the job that is already pulling me away from the
rest of the park. A Ticket Worker makes sense when I keep abandoning food,
cleaning and repairs to clear the entrance. A Janitor is better when trash,
puddles and dirty facilities are spreading faster than I can reach them.
Maintenance becomes valuable when several attractions are breaking during the
same operating day.

The most important rule is that **unlocking a staff role and increasing the
number of employees I can hire are separate Research upgrades**. I can unlock
Vendors, Janitors, Maintenance or Security and still have no free staff slot.

In my larger Hard Mode Beach park, a practical six-worker roster became:

- two Janitors;
- two Vendors;
- one Ticket Worker;
- one Maintenance Worker.

That is not a universal best build. It matched a park where cleaning, two food
stalls, ticketing and repairs were all active bottlenecks at the same time.

> Tested in Waterpark Simulator 1.0 on a Hard Mode Beach Map save through four
> stars. Candidate stats, wages and staff pressure can differ between saves,
> difficulties and later balance updates.

<h2 id="who-to-hire-first">Who Should I Hire First?</h2>

I wait for the park to expose a real bottleneck.

Hiring too early is dangerous on Hard Mode. When Cashier Staff first became
available, one Ticket Worker candidate cost about **$270 per day**, while I had
only **$63** left.

The role was useful, but the park did not yet have enough cash to pay the wage
and safely restock the next day.

I returned later with roughly **$300**, hired the worker and started the next
day with only **$9**.

That hire worked because the entrance had become a repeated interruption. It
was still a risky purchase, and I do not treat that cash level as a safe
routine.

### Hire a Ticket Worker first when

- I leave food, cleaning or repairs several times per day to sell tickets;
- the queue remains after the opening rush;
- guests wait outside while I am on the far side of the park;
- the park can pay the wage and still afford tomorrow's stock;
- freeing the entrance lets me operate another income-producing facility.

### Hire a Janitor first when

- trash is visible on several guest paths;
- full bins and puddles appear faster than I can clear them;
- “I saw something disgusting” repeats in the Daily Report;
- cleaning is the main failure while I can still repair attractions myself;
- remote service areas are creating long trash routes.

### Hire Maintenance first when

- several attractions become damaged during the same day;
- breakdowns reduce attraction uptime;
- I keep leaving tickets or food to use the hammer;
- a distant expansion is outside my reliable repair route;
- one broken facility can disable a large part of the current capacity.

The decision is about the job I am failing to complete, not which staff node
looks strongest in the Research Tree.

<h2 id="how-staff-stats-work">How I Compare Staff Stats and Pay</h2>

The Hiring List shows more than daily Pay.

I compare:

- **Efficiency**;
- **Precision**;
- **Movement Speed**;
- **Endurance**;
- **Recovery Time**;
- **Pay**.

I do not assume the highest Efficiency candidate is automatically the best.

### Efficiency

Efficiency matters most when two candidates perform the same repetitive job.

My first Ticket Worker had close to **200% Efficiency**, which helped the
entrance move quickly while the worker was active.

The problem was not processing speed. It was fatigue and downtime.

### Movement Speed

Movement Speed matters most for roles that cross the park:

- Janitors;
- Maintenance Workers;
- Restockers;
- employees covering separated themed sections.

A stationary Ticket Worker or Vendor spends less time travelling, so Movement
Speed is less important for those jobs than it is for a cleaner or repair
worker on a large Beach Map.

### Endurance and Recovery Time

Endurance helps a worker stay active through a busy period.

Recovery Time matters because the employee stops working while resting.

For one later Janitor choice, I compared:

- **154% Efficiency**, Movement Speed 4, Endurance 150, 69-second Recovery;
- **134% Efficiency**, Movement Speed 3, Endurance 198, 39-second Recovery.

The second candidate had lower Efficiency, but much better Endurance and almost
half the Recovery Time.

For a long operating day, that can be more useful than the higher headline
Efficiency.

### Precision

I use Precision as a tie-breaker rather than the first deciding stat.

I have not isolated a reliable universal formula showing exactly how much one
extra point changes every role. I prioritise the stats whose operational effect
I can see clearly: work speed, travel time, stamina, rest duration and wage.

### Pay

I compare Pay against the problem the worker removes.

A cheap employee is poor value when they spend most of the day walking,
resting or standing without an assignment. A more expensive worker can be worth
the wage when they keep the entrance open or prevent several attractions from
remaining broken.

<h2 id="ticket-worker">When a Ticket Worker Is Worth the Salary</h2>

My first Ticket Worker removed one of the most repetitive opening jobs.

My operating route became:

1. check the ticket booth;
2. confirm the employee has arrived;
3. open the park;
4. leave the entrance;
5. restock food;
6. inspect pools and slides;
7. return only when the worker rests or the queue becomes abnormal.

The hire did not make the entrance permanently automatic.

Staff take breaks, and their work stops while they are resting.

### When I cover the booth myself

I return to ticket sales when:

- the Ticket Worker is resting;
- a queue has already formed;
- a star or Museum objective requires personal ticket sales;
- one short rush is close to causing departures;
- the newly hired worker has not started yet.

I do not hire a second Ticket Worker because one break created a small queue.

I first check whether I can cover that short gap without abandoning a larger
problem.

### Why a new employee may not appear immediately

A late-day hire may begin work on the following day.

When a Maintenance Worker did not appear immediately, I:

1. checked the Manage screen;
2. confirmed the employee was still hired;
3. changed to the next day;
4. checked the normal work area again.

The employee arrived after the day changed.

<h2 id="janitor-or-maintenance">Janitor or Maintenance: Which One Do I Need?</h2>

This was the hardest early staffing choice because both jobs were useful.

I chose a Janitor when cleaning was the work I could no longer finish while
repairs were still manageable with the hammer.

That decision changed as the park expanded.

After removing a Maintenance Worker, damaged attractions began appearing
alongside dirty water, tickets, food and trash. The saved wage returned every
repair to me during the busiest part of the day.

### Choose a Janitor when the failure is spread across the park

Trash and puddles appear around:

- the entrance;
- food and vending;
- toilets and showers;
- pool edges;
- loungers;
- remote themed sections.

A Janitor removes the constant need to stop for every small mess while moving
between critical jobs.

My first Janitor candidate had roughly **159% Efficiency** and was also one of
the cheaper available options.

### Choose Maintenance when downtime is the larger loss

Maintenance becomes more important when:

- several slides and pools wear down together;
- the park contains distant attractions;
- repairs interrupt tickets or food;
- damaged facilities generate complaints or injuries;
- one breakdown removes a large part of current capacity.

In one comparison, the faster Maintenance candidate had:

- 92% Efficiency;
- Movement Speed 7;
- Endurance 157;
- 60-second Recovery Time.

The other had:

- 172% Efficiency;
- Movement Speed 3;
- Endurance 198;
- 111-second Recovery Time.

For a wide park, I may prefer the faster worker who returns from rest sooner.
For a compact section, the higher-Efficiency candidate may complete more work
before stopping.

### When I add a second Janitor

I add one when:

- the first regularly rests while trash is still spreading;
- several themed sections are open;
- rain creates puddles across a large area;
- bins remain full through most of the day;
- cleaning complaints persist despite the first assignment;
- I still spend most of my own day cleaning.

Two Janitors can also remove the actions I need for personal quests and
Research progress.

<h2 id="vendors-and-restocker">When Vendors and a Restocker Become Worth It</h2>

A Vendor does not automatically operate every food location.

Each Vendor needs a specific stall assignment.

I delay the first Vendor while:

- ticketing is the more serious bottleneck;
- I can serve food during quieter periods;
- the staff limit is still low;
- one employee slot has more value as a Janitor or Ticket Worker;
- the park cannot safely support another wage.

I added Vendors after Lemonade and Popcorn both created repeated queues.

The working split became:

- Vendor 1 on Lemonade;
- Vendor 2 on Popcorn;
- I covered Hot Dogs and emergencies.

### How I assign a Vendor

When a Vendor shows `Not Assigned`, I check:

1. the worker is still employed;
2. the worker is not resting;
3. the stall has an assignment control;
4. the correct Vendor is selected;
5. the stall has stock.

I add a second Vendor when two separate stalls form queues on most days and the

### When I hire a Restocker

One vending machine does not justify another daily wage.

I hire a Restocker when:

- several machines sell different products;
- food, drinks and inflatables empty during most days;
- restock trips interrupt tickets, safety or paid attractions;
- the larger park makes every stock run too long;
- the salary and any tax cost less than the income protected.

In the larger park, Inflatable demand became difficult to keep stocked while
other employees were resting and guests were already waiting for food.

<h2 id="security-and-mascot">When I Add Security or a Mascot</h2>

I treat Security and Mascot as later hires, not replacements for the essential
opening roles.

### Security

Security becomes useful when disruptive guests are appearing often enough to
create a real workload.

I consider the hire when:

- vandals or disruptive guests repeatedly interrupt normal operation;
- manually dealing with them pulls me away from safety or service;
- the park can support another salary and staff slot;
- I am working toward the **Order Restored** achievement, which requires
  Security to remove 50 disruptive guests.

I do not hire Security while the entrance, cleaning or repairs are still the
larger daily failure.

### Mascot

I do not prioritise Mascot over Ticket Workers, Janitors, Maintenance or proven
food coverage.

The current Hard Mode route did not isolate a reliable revenue or Satisfaction
gain that would justify a universal “hire Mascot at this star level” rule.

I treat Mascot as an optional late-game slot after:

- the core operating jobs are covered;
- the normal staff cap has room;
- the park can absorb the wage;
- I have a specific reason to test the role.

That is safer than replacing an essential worker with a role whose benefit has
not yet been measured in the current park.

<h2 id="staff-limit">Why I Cannot Hire More Employees</h2>

Unlocking a new role does not increase employee capacity.

The Research Tree separates:

- role unlocks such as Vendor, Janitor, Maintenance, Security or Restocker;
- **Hiring Level Up**, which increases the normal employee limit;
- **Staff Overflow**, which permits over-hiring with extra tax.

### Hiring Level Up

Hiring Level Up increases the normal staff cap.

In the larger park, one upgrade cost **6 Research Points** and created room for
two additional employees.

I prioritise it when:

- the current roster is full;
- I know exactly which jobs are missing;
- Staff Overflow tax is repeating;
- the park can afford the added wages.

### Staff Overflow

Staff Overflow allows hiring beyond the normal limit, up to **200% of the
current maximum**, but adds an end-of-day tax.

One early over-limit setup showed an expected tax of roughly **$215**.

- I am close to the next Hiring Level upgrade;
- the park can cover both the salary and tax;
- the bottleneck already exists today.

I avoid it when:

- I am staffing a future expansion that is not open;
- the worker has no consistent assignment;
- the park loses money on no-build days;
- available Research Points can increase the normal cap.

<h2 id="employees-not-working">Why an Employee Is Not Working</h2>

When a worker appears idle, I check the cause in this order.

### The employee is resting

Staff stop working while on break.

I temporarily cover the most important failure:

1. drowning or injured guest;
2. entrance queue;
3. food queue close to causing a departure;
4. broken paid attraction;
5. trash and puddles;
6. low-priority restocking.

### The employee has no assignment

This is common with Vendors.

I walk to the specific stall and assign the worker there. Hiring the employee
does not automatically choose Lemonade, Popcorn or Hot Dogs.

### The employee starts next day

A late-day hire may not appear immediately.

I confirm the hire, move to the next day and check again.

### The staff cap is full

Unlocking a role does not create a free slot.

I check Hiring Level Up or Staff Overflow in the Research Tree.

### The worker is too far away

A Janitor, Maintenance Worker or Restocker may technically be working but spend
too much time travelling.

I respond by:

- choosing better Movement Speed;
- delaying another remote expansion;
- splitting the park between two workers;
- grouping related facilities together;
- creating complete service areas instead of scattered attractions.

### The station has no stock

A Vendor cannot sell an item the stall does not have.

I check inventory before firing or replacing the employee.

<h2 id="staff-checklist">My Checklist Before Hiring or Firing</h2>

Before hiring, I ask:

- Which repeated job is currently failing?
- Is the failure caused by workload, distance, rest or missing assignment?
- Can the park pay the wage and still restock tomorrow?
- Is there a free normal staff slot?
- Will Staff Overflow add a recurring tax?
- Does the role have a specific station or route?
- Will the hire remove actions I still need for a quest or Research?
- Would a better layout solve the same problem?

Before firing, I run one ordinary day and check whether the role is truly
underused.

Removing Maintenance once saved salary but returned every repair to me during
an already chaotic day. I now fire only after confirming that the worker's job
is not a recurring bottleneck.
