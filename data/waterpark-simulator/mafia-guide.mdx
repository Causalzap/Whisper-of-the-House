The Mafia looked like free emergency money the first time I met them. My park was
still early in its climb toward the second star, cash was tight, and the first
offer gave me **$750** with no contract or paperwork.

That money was useful. The mistake would have been treating it as free income.

In the same save, the Mafia returned later and asked me to settle the
arrangement. I refused. At the start of a later park day, the game warned that
the Mafia would send people to damage attractions, and a group entered the park
and started breaking equipment.

So I treat the Mafia as a delayed risk decision:

**take cash now, then be ready for a later demand and possible sabotage.**

The park was not yet a mature five-star operation. I was working toward the
second star, buying basic pools and slides, keeping food service running and
trying to unlock the next objectives. A $750 injection could immediately move
one of those jobs forward.

- I already know exactly what I need to buy;
- that purchase removes an immediate progression or service bottleneck;
- I can survive a later disruption without the park collapsing.

If the park already has broken attractions, uncovered service stations, dirty

<h2 id="what-happens-after-accepting">What Happens After You Accept the Mafia Offer?</h2>

The important part is that nothing terrible happened immediately.

That is what makes the offer easy to misread.

I accepted the $750, kept operating the park and spent money on normal
progression. Later, when I needed a Lemonade Stand for the current objective, I
still had enough cash to build it, and the extra early money had clearly helped
my buying power.

The Mafia did not immediately remove the cash, shut an attraction or force me
into a new screen.

I simply continued playing.

That delay is the real trap. Once the next several objectives start taking your
attention, it is easy to forget where the extra money came from.

### What I do immediately after accepting

I do not start a large expansion just because the balance increased.

Instead I:

1. finish the purchase I accepted the money for;
2. keep the current compact park operational;
3. leave enough cash for food stock and repairs;
4. avoid adding unnecessary salary costs;
5. assume the Mafia can return later.

The $750 was useful because it helped an early park that was still buying
relatively small facilities. It would have been much less important later when
individual themed attractions and major upgrades cost thousands.

<h2 id="mafia-repayment-demand">When Does the Mafia Ask for the Money Back?</h2>

In this save, the Mafia disappeared for several park days before returning.

When they came back, the conversation changed completely. The friendly offer
was over. The Mafia said the park was doing better and that it was time to
settle the arrangement.

This is the point where I stop treating the Mafia as a money mechanic and start

I also avoid starting a major build while that decision is unresolved. If I am
about to spend my reserve on a new pool, Park Size expansion or themed area, I
handle the Mafia first.

The exact repayment formula is not something I have confirmed, so I would not
plan a budget around a guessed interest rate or assumed repayment amount.

<h2 id="what-happens-if-you-refuse">What Happens If You Refuse the Mafia?</h2>

I refused the settlement demand.

The response was not neutral. The Mafia warned me that refusing them would have
consequences.

In my run, the park did not instantly explode and the attack did not happen
inside that same conversation. I was able to continue operating.

That matters because the safest reaction is **not** to panic and destroy the
park layout.

Instead, I finish the current day and make the next opening easier:

- repair attractions before sleeping;
- clean anything already near failure;
- avoid spending the last of my cash;
- make sure the most profitable part of the park is easy to reach;
- delay a large expansion if the current area is already hard to maintain.

I would not assume the exact delay is identical in every save. What I can
confirm is the sequence I saw:

**accepted money → later settlement demand → refusal → later Mafia sabotage.**

The biggest mistake here is continuing whatever build project I was doing before
the event started.

When several objects are being damaged at once, every minute I spend placing
decoration or opening another area creates more repair work.

### My priority during the attack

I use this order:

1. stop building;
2. identify where the crew is moving;
3. keep the active park from completely falling apart;
4. watch the condition of attractions guests are already using;
5. leave optional construction until the attack is finished;
6. repair the worst damage immediately afterward.

I do not chase every small problem on opposite sides of the map.

If one remote lounger is damaged but my main slide, food service and busiest pool
are still functioning, I stay near the high-traffic area until the immediate
chaos is under control.

<h2 id="mafia-dropped-money">Can Mafia Attackers Drop Money?</h2>

I saw cash appear during the sabotage.

That does not make the attack profitable enough for me to ignore the damage.

Some of the money was awkward to reach while the crew was still moving through
the park, and stopping to collect it while attractions were being destroyed was
the wrong priority.

My rule is simple:

**protect the operating park first, collect convenient cash second.**

If money is sitting directly on my route, I take it. I do not run across the
park for a small pickup while equipment behind me is being pushed toward zero
durability.

I also would not assign a fixed cash reward to each attacker. This run confirms
that money can appear during the event, not a guaranteed amount per Mafia
member.

<h2 id="mafia-offer-amounts">Is the Mafia Offer Always $750?</h2>

I would not treat **$750** as a universal Mafia amount.

The clearest full chain I completed started with $750, but other saves and later
progression states showed Mafia offers of **$5,000** and **$15,000**.

That tells me the important decision is not:

> "Is $750 worth taking?"

It is:

> "Is the amount being offered worth the later risk to my current park?"

A few hundred dollars can be meaningful when I am still buying basic pools,
slides and food service.

Several thousand dollars can sound much more attractive later, but by that
point the park is also larger, more expensive and harder to repair if several
attractions are damaged at once.

I therefore judge the offer against the **current bottleneck**, not the number
alone.

<h2 id="what-is-not-confirmed">What I Have Not Confirmed About the Mafia</h2>

There are two things I would not write into a fixed strategy yet.

### The exact repayment formula

I confirmed that the Mafia returns to settle the earlier arrangement, but I
have not confirmed a universal interest rate or formula that applies to every
offer amount.

I would not save a specific percentage and assume that guarantees a safe
repayment.

### The full pay-back branch

The complete route I tested was:

**accept the initial money → refuse the later settlement → receive the sabotage
warning → survive the Mafia attack.**

I have not completed the alternate branch far enough to say exactly what every
later event looks like if I agree to settle immediately.

That means I am comfortable explaining the refusal-and-sabotage route, but I
would not claim that paying always removes every future Mafia event until that
branch is tested separately.

<h2 id="final-takeaway">My Mafia Rule on Hard Mode</h2>

The Mafia offer is most useful when the park has a **temporary cash bottleneck**
and the money immediately completes something important.

It is worst when I use it to hide a park that already cannot keep up with its
own repairs and service load.

The sequence I plan around is:

**cash offer → keep the park stable → Mafia returns → settlement decision →
possible sabotage → repair before expanding again.**

If I accept, I spend the money on a real bottleneck and keep a reserve.

If I refuse later, I stop treating the Mafia as a conversation and start
preparing for attraction damage.

And after an attack, I do not resume expansion until the existing guest route is
clean, repaired and earning again.
