The jump from four stars to Diamond was much easier once I stopped treating it
as one giant late-game grind.

There are really two separate checkpoints.

At **4.5 stars**, my next Park Prestige objectives were:

- earn **$500,000**;
- build a slide with at least **4 Prestige**.

The important detail is that the $500,000 objective counted money I had already
earned during the save. I did not need $500,000 sitting in my cash balance.

After I placed a qualifying four-Prestige slide, the park reached **five
stars**. The next target then changed to Diamond, which required:

- earning **$1,000,000**;
- building one **Wonder Attraction with at least 6 Prestige**.

I used the same Hard Mode Beach Map save for both milestones.

The real late-game bottlenecks were not the stars themselves. They were deciding
when to stop spending Research, when to use a temporary qualifying slide, which
Museum branch to push far enough for a Wonder Attraction, and where to put an
enormous final attraction without wrecking an already-working park.

<h2 id="four-prestige-slide">Use a Qualifying Slide Instead of Rebuilding the Park</h2>

I was tempted to treat the four-Prestige requirement as an excuse to build a
beautiful permanent late-game slide.

I did not.

Once the required Prestige Slides became available, I chose a relatively
compact design and treated it as a **progression slide first**.

That was the right decision because the star trigger cared about the Prestige
requirement. It did not require me to redesign an entire themed district around
that one slide before progressing.

This is one of the biggest late-game time savers.

If I am only one qualifying attraction away from the next star, I separate two
questions:

**Does this satisfy the progression requirement?**

and:

**Do I want this exact attraction here permanently?**

Those do not have to have the same answer.

In my run, I placed the slide, triggered five stars, then immediately tested
the landing because the pool was small enough that I was worried guests could
overshoot it.

I adjusted the setup after the milestone instead of delaying the milestone
until the whole area looked finished.

### When I build the slide

I build it once:

- the $500,000 counter is complete or close enough to finish naturally;
- the required slide tier is unlocked;
- I have open space near a usable pool;
- buying it will not consume the cash needed to keep the park operating.

### When I wait

I wait if the slide itself is still locked behind Research.

I do not spend Research on unrelated decorations or small convenience upgrades
while the only thing blocking five stars is the required attraction tier.

<h2 id="what-unlocks-after-five-stars">What Changes After Five Stars</h2>

Five stars did **not** finish the park.

As soon as I completed the final five-star condition, the game pointed me toward
a higher target: the legendary six-star level, shown through the Diamond Park
progression.

The message specifically told me to continue Museum progression and build a
Wonder Attraction.

The new objective panel then made the requirements much more concrete:

**earn $1,000,000 and build one Wonder Attraction with at least 6 Prestige.**

<h2 id="one-million-requirement">You Do Not Need $1 Million in Cash</h2>

The Diamond money requirement behaved like the five-star money requirement: it
tracked earnings rather than the amount currently sitting in my wallet.

At the beginning of my Diamond push, I had only about **$75,000 in cash**, while
the progression counter was already only a couple hundred thousand dollars away
from $1 million.

Later, I was less than **$50,000** away from the $1 million objective while
still spending money normally around the park.

Eventually the objective completed.

What I stop doing is wasting money on optional rebuilding that does not help me
reach the Wonder Attraction.

If the $1 million counter is already moving steadily, I let normal operation
finish it.

<h2 id="unlock-wonder-attraction">How I Unlocked a Wonder Attraction</h2>

The second Diamond requirement is where the Museum becomes important.

I did not need to finish every themed area before reaching Diamond.

In this save I pushed the **Pirate branch** far enough to unlock its large
flagship attraction. The Ocean and Aztec sections were still unfinished later,
even after Diamond had already triggered.

That gave me a much cleaner strategy:

**pick one Wonder Attraction route and finish that route first.**

I do not spread the same limited resources equally across Pirate, Ocean and
Aztec if Diamond is my immediate goal.

<h2 id="wonder-attraction-space">The Real Diamond Bottleneck Was Space</h2>

Unlocking the Wonder Attraction was not the end of the problem.

The attraction was enormous.

When I first previewed it, my existing Pirate section had already been built
around smaller pools, slides and decoration. The new attraction did not politely
fit into the space I had left.

This is where I would change my route on a second playthrough.

Once I am close to the final Museum reward, I stop filling every empty patch of
land.

I leave a large central area flexible until I have seen the Wonder Attraction.

In this run I eventually had to consider:

- removing an existing pool;
- shifting other pools toward the sides;
- moving the large Pirate attraction toward the middle;
- rebuilding paths and decoration around it.

I even delayed some smaller Pirate additions because fitting the main attraction
mattered more than completing the visual theme.

That is the correct late-game priority.

A small pool is replaceable.

The attraction that clears the Diamond objective is not the thing I design
around last.

<h2 id="when-to-tear-down">When I Tear Down an Existing Pool</h2>

I do not demolish a working pool the first time the Wonder Attraction preview
turns red or looks awkward.

First I rotate the attraction and check the complete footprint.

Then I look at what is actually blocking it.

If moving one low-value pool opens a clean central footprint, I remove the
pool.

If I would need to destroy an entire profitable district, I check another area
or another expansion first.

In my final layout, one existing pool became the sacrifice because it was the
cleanest way to make the large Pirate attraction the focal point of the themed
section.

That decision was easier because I was no longer treating every earlier build
as permanent.

Late-game parks change.

The mistake is not rebuilding something.

The mistake is spending another hour decorating around a layout that I already
know cannot fit the final progression attraction.

Once the cumulative $1 million requirement was complete, the attraction became
the last meaningful gate in my run.

I cleared the necessary space, rebuilt the Pirate section around the large
attraction and placed it.

The Diamond completion triggered immediately afterward.

**4.5 stars → cumulative $500,000 → 4-Prestige slide → five stars →
cumulative $1,000,000 → Museum route to a Wonder Attraction → clear enough
space → place the 6-Prestige attraction → Diamond.**

I do not add another grind between those stages unless the game is specifically
blocking the attraction through Museum progression or Research.

<h2 id="diamond-not-unlocking">What I Check If Diamond Is Not Unlocking</h2>

If Diamond does not trigger, I check the two displayed requirements separately.

### The money counter is incomplete

I keep operating.

I do not sell attractions simply to raise cash because the objective is based
on earnings, not cash held.

A stable park that keeps selling tickets and attractions will finish the counter
naturally.

### I cannot find a Wonder Attraction

I return to the Museum.

I do not assume normal Research alone unlocks the final attraction.

The themed progression needs to be far enough to expose the large reward.

### The attraction is unlocked but I cannot afford it

I stop optional decoration and side upgrades.

At this point I already have the thing I need. My next job is protecting enough
cash to place it.

### The attraction will not fit

I stop expanding the decoration around it.

I preview the footprint, choose the least valuable existing pool or attraction
blocking the space, remove that first and test again.

### I built something expensive but Diamond did not trigger

I verify that it is actually a **Wonder Attraction with at least 6 Prestige**.

A normal expensive slide or pool is not enough just because it looks like a
late-game attraction.

<h2 id="final-takeaway">My Five-Star and Diamond Rule</h2>

I do not make the late game harder than the objective panel asks me to.

For five stars, I let cumulative income do its work and place a qualifying
four-Prestige slide without redesigning the entire park around it.

For Diamond, I keep the park earning, concentrate Museum progress on one viable
Wonder Attraction, save the Research needed to reach it and leave enough space
to fit it.

The biggest lesson from this run was layout timing.

I could earn the money while doing almost anything.

I could earn Research while maintaining the park.

But once I had filled a themed area with smaller permanent-looking builds, the
Wonder Attraction forced me to undo that work.

On another run, I would reserve the biggest piece of land for the biggest
progression attraction first, then decorate around it after Diamond is already
secured.
