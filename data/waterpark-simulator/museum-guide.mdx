The Museum stopped feeling like a side activity once I realised that it uses a
separate progression system.

Goldie, Poseidon and Yogi do not simply award normal Research Points. Their
quests advance Pirate, Ocean and Wellness routes, while a later Museum branch
opens Aztec rewards.

The confusing part is that quest progress and reward access are not always the
same thing. I could continue completing some tasks, but the next Museum node
still remained locked until the park reached three or four stars.

My working rule is simple: I accept one task, complete it during normal park
operation, return after closing, collect the reward and stop if the next step is
star-gated or too expensive to build safely.

> Tested in Waterpark Simulator 1.0 on a Hard Mode Beach Map save through four
> stars. This guide covers the Museum routes and costs I completed or directly
> verified; it does not invent the unfinished five-star or Diamond sequence.

<h2 id="unlock-museum">How to Unlock and Reach the Museum</h2>

The Museum is visible near the Beach Map opening before its full quest system is
available.

I wait for the notification announcing that the Museum has opened. It explains
that the building contains unique characters, quests, special resources,
exclusive attractions and a long-term Wonder Attraction route.

    I wait for this notification before trying to progress the Museum. Seeing
    the building earlier does not mean every quest and reward branch is active.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

From the park, I use this route:

1. return to the main entrance;
2. leave the active park area;
3. cross the street;
4. enter the Museum;
5. speak to the NPC with an available task;
6. accept one objective;
7. return to the park.

The main cost is not an entry fee. It is travel and attention.

Running back after every single counter increase wastes most of the operating
day. I combine Museum objectives with work the park already needs:

- save injured or drowning guests;
- clean dirty buildings;
- empty bins and clear path trash;
- mop puddles after rain;
- sell vending snacks;
- clean smelly guests.

I normally return after closing or before the next opening, when the park is
stable and I am not abandoning the entrance or a safety alert.

<h2 id="museum-currencies">How Museum Rewards and Research Work</h2>

Museum progression is separate from the normal Research Point tree.

The Museum contains distinct branches for:

- Pirate rewards;
- Ocean rewards;
- Aztec rewards;
- Wellness facilities through Yogi's quest line.

    branch.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

I treat every theme reward as limited.

A completed Goldie or Poseidon quest does not unlock an entire branch. One
reward may buy one node while the next item needs another quest, another reward
or a higher park rating.

One early Pirate choice showed:

- one Pirate reward available;
- two required for Pirate Decoration Level 2.

That changed how I spent the first reward. I did not assume another one would
arrive immediately.

### What I unlock first

My usual order is:

1. a utility or facility required by the next quest;
2. the pool or slide needed for progression;
3. showers, toilets or trash coverage for the themed area;
4. decoration;
5. cosmetic alternatives that do not solve a current task.

I do not spend the first theme reward on decoration when the next objective
requires a functional pool, shower or other utility.

### Museum rewards do not replace normal Research

Theme rewards unlock themed objects, but the main Research Tree still controls
important support systems such as:

- Park Size;
- Hiring Level and staff capacity;
- ticket upgrades;
- vending support;
- cleaning tools;
- general facility quality.

Before starting a large Museum build, I check whether the park first needs more
space, another employee slot or a utility upgrade.

<h2 id="star-gates">Why a Museum Quest or Reward Is Locked</h2>

The first major Museum roadblock was park Prestige, not a missing NPC trigger.

After one of Goldie's early objectives, the Pirate branch showed that further
rewards required a **three-star park**.

  <Image

  />
  <figcaption className="mx-auto mt-3 max-w-4xl text-sm text-slate-400">
    I stopped searching for a hidden trigger when the branch itself showed the
    three-star requirement. The correct next step was normal park progression.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

A star gate can create several confusing states at once:

- the NPC may still offer an action quest;
- I may still complete or prepare a task;
- an earned reward may remain unspendable;
- the next branch node may not open until the required rating.

Pirate and Ocean progression later reached another stopping point before four
stars.

When a route appears stuck, I check:

1. current Park Prestige;
2. the relevant Museum branch;
3. visible three-star or four-star requirements;
4. whether the NPC still offers another action objective;
5. the current Road to the Next Star tasks.

I do not repeatedly reload the area or search for a hidden object before
checking the star gate.

### What I continue doing behind a gate

I still complete a quest when it is cheap and overlaps normal play:

- rescue guests;
- clean graffiti;
- mop puddles;
- clear trash;
- clean buildings;
- sell vending snacks;
- clean smelly guests.

I pause when the next task requires an expensive facility that the park cannot
yet support.

<h2 id="goldie-pirate-quests">Goldie and the Pirate Quest Route</h2>

Goldie's route focuses on rescue, cleaning, vandalism and other hands-on park
actions.

One early task required saving injured visitors with the Injector.

My route was:

1. keep the Injector accessible;
2. leave active attractions open;
3. watch for injury notifications;
4. go directly to the affected guest;
5. use the Injector;
6. continue normal operation until the objective completes;
7. return to Goldie after closing.

Other Goldie objectives in this route included:

- saving **five drowning guests**;
- mopping puddles personally;
- cleaning graffiti;
- selling snacks through a vending machine;
- cleaning **10 smelly guests** with the water gun;
- later using Pirate or security-related tools against troublesome guests.

### Saving five drowning guests

I complete this naturally in an active park with several pools and slides.

I keep the life-saving tool ready, patrol from the busiest pool toward the
newer themed areas and respond immediately to the drowning alert.

I do not expand only to create more incidents. A larger park makes every rescue
route longer.

### Mopping puddles before Janitors take them

The main obstacle can be my own staff.

With two Janitors active, they often reached puddles before I could complete a
manual quest counter.

I stay close during rain, mop new puddles near the active area and try to finish
the task before adding another Janitor. I do not fire an essential cleaner when
the park is already failing.

### Cleaning graffiti

Graffiti is not guaranteed to appear beside me.

I check:

- entrance walls;
- service buildings;
- toilets and showers;
- themed facilities;
- the outer side of remote structures.

The objective is completed in the active park, not by running around the
Museum.

### Vending snacks and smelly guests

I accept these tasks after the required systems already make sense for the
park.

I do not buy a vending machine solely for one small counter unless I can afford
the machine, its first stock and nearby trash coverage.

For smelly guests, I keep the water gun available and clean marked guests
during normal patrols.

<h2 id="poseidon-ocean-quests">Poseidon and the Ocean Quest Route</h2>

Poseidon's route begins with cleaning and guest-interaction objectives before
opening the expensive Ocean build options.

The sequence I completed included:

- helping panicking guests enter the water;
- clearing trash personally;
- painting guests;
- cleaning **15 buildings**;
- earning Ocean rewards for utilities and decoration.

### Clearing trash quickly

Trash removed from existing bins counted as personal progress.

I used the bins already spread around the park:

1. entrance;
2. food and vending area;
3. loungers;
4. first pool section;
5. remote themed areas;
6. back toward the Museum route.

This was faster than waiting for random litter to appear.

### Cleaning 15 buildings

I accept this task before a busy day and clean eligible facilities as they
become dirty:

- ticket facilities;
- food stands;
- vending machines;
- toilets and showers;
- pools and slides;
- other service buildings.

If I wait until after closing, Janitors may already have removed every useful
target.

### Spending four Ocean rewards

At one stage, I had **four Ocean rewards** available and could finish the
currently open Ocean choices, including decoration and a shower.

### Why I delay the Ocean pool and slide

The Ocean Ripple Slide cost **$3,000**, while its supporting Ocean Pool cost
**$5,250**.

That is at least **$8,250** before adding:

- showers and toilets;
- trash bins;
- loungers;
- paths;
- decoration;
- staff coverage.

I build the section only when:

- the active park can complete a no-build day without collapsing;
- I have more than the minimum $8,250;
- staff can reach the new area;
- ticketing and food service are stable;
- I can build a complete section instead of one isolated pool.

The reward may be unlocked while the park is still financially unready.

<h2 id="yogi-wellness-quests">Yogi and the Wellness Quest Route</h2>

Yogi became the most expensive Museum route because it moved from cheap
decoration tasks into interactive Wellness facilities.

The route I completed or directly verified included:

- building **10 fountains**;
- relaxing guests in a Wooden Hot Tub;
- relaxing guests in Saunas;
- building **20 loungers with at least three Prestige**;
- mopping puddles personally;
- serving Hot Tub guests consecutively without mistakes;
- unlocking larger Wellness and Thermal facilities.

### Build 10 fountains for about $1,500

Some eligible fountains cost about **$500 each**, but a cheaper option cost
**$150**.

Ten of the cheaper version completed the objective for approximately
**$1,500** instead of $5,000.

My route was:

1. find the cheapest eligible fountain;
2. place one and confirm the counter increases;
3. place the remaining nine together;
4. complete the objective;
5. return to Yogi;
6. move or remove them when the permanent Wellness layout is ready.

I keep temporary quest objects together instead of scattering them across the
whole park.

### Wooden Hot Tub

The Wooden Hot Tub cost approximately **$3,000**.

Its quest involved relaxing guests, with dialogue referencing roughly
**15 minutes at 38°C**.


I delay the purchase when:

- the park lacks one safe operating day's reserve;
- ticket or food queues are still failing;
- I cannot manually manage or staff the facility;
- another star objective requires a different expensive build.

After construction, I keep the Hot Tub near an area I already patrol. Guest
requests need attention, and a remote Hot Tub creates another long route.

### Saunas

Sauna guests can request settings, so I do not place the facility at the far
edge of the map while also operating another manual service.

I build it near:

- an existing staff route;
- food and basic services;
- a maintenance path I already use;
- the developing Wellness area.

### Twenty three-Prestige loungers

This is not an early purchase.

Twenty high-Prestige loungers consume both cash and space. I wait until:

- the required lounger is researched;
- attraction income is stable;
- I have a permanent seating area;
- the purchase will not block wages or the next required facility.

Unlike the temporary fountains, these loungers can become part of the final
Wellness layout.

### Consecutive Hot Tub requests

I attempt this when the rest of the park can run without me for several
minutes.

Before starting, I:

1. restock food and vending machines;
2. confirm the Ticket Worker is active;
3. repair nearby attractions;
4. keep cleaning staff on the active route;
5. remain beside the Hot Tub;
6. read every requested setting carefully.

I do not leave midway for a low-priority alert. One incorrect request can delay
or reset the chain.

This later Wellness route opens larger facilities such as the Thermal Hot Tub
and Wellness Pool.

<h2 id="aztec-route">When I Start the Aztec Route</h2>

I moved into the Aztec branch after finishing the Ocean rewards available at
that stage.

The first Aztec task I encountered was **clear trash**.

That made it a useful transition because it required no expensive themed
facility.

My route was:

1. finish the current Ocean reward turn-in;
2. speak to the Aztec quest giver;
3. accept the trash objective;
4. return to the park;
5. empty existing bins and clear path trash personally;
6. return after the objective completes.

A later task required a slide with at least three Prestige.

That is not only the displayed slide price. It can also require:

- the correct research;
- a compatible pool;
- enough park space;
- showers, bins and paths;
- staff coverage.

I complete the cheap cleaning objective immediately but postpone the
three-Prestige build until the park can afford the complete section.

<h2 id="museum-quest-route">How I Run a Museum Quest Day</h2>

I do not return to the Museum every time a counter increases.

### Before opening

I:

1. check the active Museum objective;
2. equip the required tool;
3. restock the park;
4. repair active facilities;
5. confirm staff assignments;
6. choose the section most likely to produce the required actions.

Examples include:

- Injector for Goldie's injury task;
- life-saving equipment for drowning guests;
- water gun for smelly guests;
- mop for Yogi's puddles;
- cleaning tools for Poseidon's buildings;
- enough vending stock for snack sales.

### During the day

I follow the normal guest route:

1. entrance;
2. food and vending;
3. first pools;
4. toilets and showers;
5. themed sections;
6. remote expansion;
7. back toward the entrance.

I complete qualifying Museum actions while keeping ticketing, safety and
service alive.

I do not trade ten refunds for one quest counter.

### After closing

I:

1. finish any safe manual cleaning objective;
2. repair active facilities;
3. confirm the task is complete;
4. return to the Museum;
5. speak to the correct NPC;
6. spend or save the reward;
7. inspect the next star gate;
8. decide whether the next task is affordable.

This removes most of the inefficient cross-street trips.

<h2 id="museum-staff-problems">How Staff Can Slow Manual Museum Tasks</h2>

Employees can solve park problems so efficiently that they remove the actions I
need to perform personally.

This affects tasks such as:

- mop puddles yourself;
- clear trash yourself;
- clean buildings;
- sell tickets or food personally;
- operate a specific facility;
- restock particular items.

I do not permanently weaken the park only to force progress.

Instead, I:

- stay near new puddles during rain;
- empty bins before Janitors reach them;
- accept cleaning tasks before a busy day;
- cover one food station while Vendors handle the others;
- use a Ticket Worker's break for required personal ticket sales;
- finish manual tasks before adding another specialist.

In a large park, I keep the necessary staff and accept that a manual objective
may take more than one day.
