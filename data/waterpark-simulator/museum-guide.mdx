The Museum stopped feeling like a side activity once I realised that it uses a
separate progression system.

Goldie, Poseidon and Yogi do not simply award normal Research Points. Their
quests advance Pirate, Ocean and Wellness routes, while a later Museum branch
opens Aztec rewards.

The confusing part is that **quest progress, reward access and Park Prestige
are connected without being the same system**.

I could continue completing some Museum tasks while the next reward node stayed
locked behind three or four stars. Later, reaching five stars pushed the Museum
back into the main progression route because the next step toward the legendary
park required more Museum progress and a Wonder Attraction.

My working rule is simple:

**accept one useful quest, complete it during normal park operation, return after
closing, spend the matching theme reward, and stop when the next step is
star-gated or too expensive to build safely.**

This page owns the Museum itself:

- Goldie and Pirate quests;
- Poseidon and Ocean quests;
- Yogi and Wellness quests;
- Aztec progression;
- Pirate, Ocean and Aztec rewards;
- Museum star gates;
- themed facilities and quest-specific build decisions.

Once the question becomes **how to reach five stars or Diamond**, I switch to
the dedicated Prestige guide instead of repeating that complete progression
route here.

From the park, I use this route:

1. return to the main entrance;
2. leave the active park area;
3. cross the street;
4. enter the Museum;
5. speak to the NPC with an available task;
6. accept one objective;
7. return to the park.

The main cost is not an entry fee.

It is **travel and attention**.

Running back across the street after every single counter increase wastes too
much of the operating day.

I combine Museum objectives with work the park already needs:

- save injured or drowning guests;
- clean dirty buildings;
- empty bins and clear path trash;
- mop puddles after rain;
- sell vending snacks;
- clean smelly guests.

I normally return after closing or before the next opening.

That way I am not abandoning:

- the entrance;
- a food queue;
- a broken attraction;
- a drowning guest;
- an urgent cleaning problem.

<h2 id="museum-currencies">How Museum Rewards and Research Work</h2>

Museum progression is separate from the normal Research Point tree.

The Museum contains distinct routes for:

- Pirate rewards;
- Ocean rewards;
- Aztec rewards;
- Wellness facilities through Yogi's quest line.

I treat every theme reward as limited.

A completed Goldie or Poseidon quest does not automatically unlock an entire
branch.

One reward may buy one node while the next item still needs:

- another quest;
- another theme reward;
- a higher Park Prestige;
- another prerequisite node.

One early Pirate choice showed:

- one Pirate reward available;
- two required for Pirate Decoration Level 2.

That changed how I spent the first reward.

I stopped treating theme rewards like normal currency that I could immediately
farm back.

### What I unlock first

My usual order is:

1. a utility or facility required by the next quest;
2. the pool or slide needed for progression;
3. showers, toilets or trash coverage for the themed area;
4. decoration;
5. cosmetic alternatives that do not solve a current task.

I do not spend the first theme reward on decoration when the next objective
requires a functional pool, shower or other utility.

### Museum rewards do not replace normal Research

Theme rewards unlock themed objects, but the main Research Tree still controls
important support systems such as:

- Park Size;
- Hiring Level and staff capacity;
- ticket upgrades;
- vending support;
- cleaning tools;
- general facility quality.

Before starting a large Museum build, I check whether the park first needs:

- more space;
- another employee slot;
- better ticketing;
- a utility upgrade;
- more operating cash.

<h2 id="star-gates">Why a Museum Quest or Reward Is Locked</h2>

The first major Museum roadblock was Park Prestige, not a missing NPC trigger.

After one of Goldie's early objectives, the Pirate branch showed that further
rewards required a **three-star park**.

    progression.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

A star gate can create several confusing states at once:

- the NPC may still offer an action quest;
- I may still complete or prepare another task;
- an earned reward may remain unspendable;
- the next branch node may not open until the required rating.

Pirate and Ocean progression later reached another stopping point before four
stars.

When a Museum route appears stuck, I check:

1. current Park Prestige;
2. the relevant Museum branch;
3. the visible star requirement;
4. whether the NPC still offers another action objective;
5. whether the previous reward has actually been spent;
6. the current Road to the Next Star tasks.

I do not repeatedly reload the area or search for a hidden item before checking
the branch itself.

### What I continue doing behind a gate

I still complete a quest when it is cheap and overlaps normal play:

- rescue guests;
- clean graffiti;
- mop puddles;
- clear trash;
- clean buildings;
- sell vending snacks;
- clean smelly guests.

I pause when the next task requires an expensive facility that the park cannot
yet support.

A locked Museum reward is not a reason to bankrupt the main park.

Goldie's route focuses heavily on rescue, cleaning, vandalism and other

One early task required saving injured visitors with the Injector.

My route was:

1. keep the Injector accessible;
2. leave active attractions open;
3. watch for injury notifications;
4. go directly to the affected guest;
5. use the Injector;
6. continue normal operation until the objective completes;
7. return to Goldie after closing.

Other Goldie objectives in this route included:

- cleaning graffiti;

### Saving five drowning guests

I complete this naturally in an active park with several pools and slides.

I keep the life-saving equipment accessible, patrol from the busiest pool toward
the newer themed areas and respond immediately to the drowning alert.

I do not expand only to create more incidents.

A larger park also makes every rescue route longer.

### Mopping puddles before Janitors take them

The main obstacle can be my own staff.

With two Janitors active, they often reached puddles before I could complete a
manual quest counter.

I respond by:

- staying close during rain;
- watching for new puddles near the active area;
- taking the nearby puddles myself;
- trying to finish the task before adding another Janitor.

I do **not** fire an essential cleaner when the park is already losing control.

### Cleaning graffiti

Graffiti is not guaranteed to appear beside me.

I check:

- entrance walls;
- service buildings;
- toilets and showers;
- themed facilities;
- outer sides of remote structures.

I continue normal park operation while waiting for it.

The objective is completed inside the active park, not by repeatedly running
around the Museum.

### Vending snacks and smelly guests

I accept these tasks after the required systems already make sense for the
park.

I do not buy a vending machine solely for one small counter unless I can afford:

- the machine;
- its first stock;
- nearby trash coverage.

For smelly guests, I keep the water gun available and clean marked guests during
normal patrols.

<h2 id="poseidon-ocean-quests">Poseidon and the Ocean Quest Route</h2>

Poseidon's route begins with cleaning and guest-interaction objectives before
opening the expensive Ocean build options.

The sequence I completed included:

- helping panicking guests enter the water;
- clearing trash personally;
- painting guests;
- cleaning **15 buildings**;
- earning Ocean rewards for utilities and decoration.

### Clearing trash quickly

Trash removed from existing bins counted toward personal progress.

I used bins already spread around the park:

1. entrance;
2. food and vending area;
3. loungers;
4. first pool section;
5. remote themed areas;
6. back toward the Museum route.

That was faster than waiting for random loose litter to appear.

### Cleaning 15 buildings

I accept this task before a busy operating day.

Then I clean eligible facilities as they become dirty:

- ticket facilities;
- food stands;
- vending machines;
- toilets and showers;
- pools and slides;
- other service buildings.

If I wait until after closing, Janitors may already have removed too many useful
targets.

### Spending four Ocean rewards

At one stage I had **four Ocean rewards** available and could finish the
currently open Ocean choices, including decoration and a shower.

- the active park can complete a no-build day without collapsing;
- I have more than the minimum $8,250;
- staff can reach the new area;
- ticketing and food service are stable;
- I can build a complete section instead of one isolated pool.

The reward can be unlocked while the park is still financially unready.

<h2 id="yogi-wellness-quests">Yogi and the Wellness Quest Route</h2>

Yogi became the most expensive Museum route because his progression moved from
cheap decoration tasks into interactive Wellness facilities.

The route I completed included:

- building **10 fountains**;
- relaxing guests in a Wooden Hot Tub;
- relaxing guests in Saunas;
- building **20 loungers with at least three Prestige**;
- mopping puddles personally;
- serving consecutive Hot Tub requests without mistakes;
- unlocking larger Wellness and Thermal facilities.

### Build 10 fountains for about $1,500

Some eligible fountains cost about **$500 each**, but I found a cheaper option
for **$150**.

Ten of those completed the objective for approximately **$1,500** instead of
$5,000.

7. move or remove the temporary fountains later.

I keep temporary quest objects together instead of scattering them across the

### Wooden Hot Tub

The Wooden Hot Tub cost approximately **$3,000**.

Its quest involved relaxing guests, with dialogue referencing roughly
**15 minutes at 38°C**.

I delay the purchase when:

- the park lacks one safe operating day's reserve;
- ticketing or food queues are still failing;
- I cannot manually manage or staff the facility;
- another star objective requires a more important expensive build.

After construction, I keep the Hot Tub near an area I already patrol.

Guest requests need attention.

A remote Hot Tub creates another long route I must repeatedly cross.

### Saunas

Sauna guests can request settings.

I therefore do not place the facility at the far edge of the map while also
operating another manual service.

I build it near:

- an existing staff route;
- food and basic services;
- a maintenance path I already use;
- the developing Wellness area.

### Twenty three-Prestige loungers

This is not an early purchase.

Twenty high-Prestige loungers consume both cash and space.

I wait until:

- the required lounger is available;
- attraction income is stable;
- I have a permanent seating area;
- the purchase will not block wages;
- the next required facility is already funded.

Unlike the temporary fountains, these loungers can become part of the permanent
Wellness layout.

### Consecutive Hot Tub requests

I attempt this when the rest of the park can operate without me for several
minutes.

Before starting, I:

1. restock food and vending machines;
2. confirm the Ticket Worker is active;
3. repair nearby attractions;
4. keep cleaning staff on the active route;
5. remain beside the Hot Tub;
6. read each requested setting carefully.

I do not leave midway for a low-priority alert.

One incorrect request can delay the chain and make the entire attempt take
longer.

The later Wellness route opens larger facilities such as the Thermal Hot Tub
and Wellness Pool.

<h2 id="aztec-route">When I Start the Aztec Route</h2>

I moved into the Aztec branch after finishing the Ocean rewards available at

The first Aztec task I encountered was **clear trash**.

That made it a useful transition because it required no expensive themed

My route was:

1. finish the current Ocean reward turn-in;
2. speak to the Aztec quest giver;
3. accept the trash objective;
4. return to the park;
5. empty existing bins and clear path trash personally;

That is not only the displayed slide price.

A usable themed slide can also require:

- the correct unlock;

three-Prestige build until the park can afford the entire section.

- complete more Museum quests;

<h2 id="museum-quest-route">How I Run a Museum Quest Day</h2>

I do not return to the Museum every time a counter increases.

That creates too many unnecessary cross-street trips.

### Before opening

I:

1. check the active Museum objective;
2. equip the required tool;
3. restock the park;
4. repair active facilities;
5. confirm staff assignments;
6. choose the section most likely to produce the required actions.

Examples include:

- Injector for Goldie's injury task;
- life-saving equipment for drowning guests;
- water gun for smelly guests;
- mop for Yogi's puddles;
- cleaning tools for Poseidon's building task;
- enough vending stock for snack sales.

### During the day

I follow the normal guest route:

1. entrance;
2. food and vending;
3. first pools;
4. toilets and showers;
5. themed sections;
6. remote expansion;
7. back toward the entrance.

I complete qualifying Museum actions while keeping:

- ticketing;
- safety;
- food;
- cleaning;
- repairs

alive.

I do not trade ten refunds for one extra Museum counter.

### After closing

I:

1. finish any safe manual cleaning objective;
2. repair active facilities;
3. confirm the task is complete;
4. return to the Museum;
5. speak to the correct NPC;
6. spend or save the reward;
7. inspect the next branch node;
8. check the next star gate;
9. decide whether the next task is affordable.

This removes most of the inefficient Museum travel from the operating day.

<h2 id="museum-staff-problems">
  How Staff Can Slow Manual Museum Tasks
</h2>

Employees can solve park problems so efficiently that they remove the actions I
need to perform personally.

This affects tasks such as:

- mop puddles yourself;
- clear trash yourself;
- clean buildings;
- sell tickets or food personally;
- operate a specific facility;
- restock particular items.

I do not permanently weaken the park just to force quest progress.

Instead, I:

- stay near new puddles during rain;
- empty bins before Janitors reach them;
- accept cleaning tasks before a busy day;
- cover one food station while Vendors handle the others;
- use a Ticket Worker's break for required personal ticket sales;
- finish manual tasks before adding another specialist.

In a large park, I keep the necessary employees and accept that a manual Museum
objective may take more than one day.

The same tradeoff appears when I need another specialist.

A Security Guard, Restocker, Janitor or Vendor can solve a real operating
problem, but every added employee also changes which manual actions remain
available to me.
