Research Points stopped feeling slow once I stopped trying to earn them during
the busiest part of the day.

My reliable route is to keep the park operating, handle only the problems that
can cause injuries, refunds or blocked service, then use closing time for one
complete cleaning-and-repair circuit.

That decision mattered on Hard Mode. At one point, the remaining route through
**Modern Toilets**, **Mop Upgrade** and **Chlorine** still represented about
**16 Research Points**. Spending those points on an unrelated attraction would
have left me draining dirty pools by hand for several more days.

I therefore treat Research as a bottleneck-removal system. I do not buy the
most exciting node. I buy the upgrade that removes the job currently costing
the most time, money or Satisfaction.

> Tested in Waterpark Simulator 1.0 on a Hard Mode Beach Map save through four
> stars. Research costs and unlock order may change with difficulty or later
> balance updates.

<h2 id="how-research-points-work">How Research Points Actually Work</h2>

The repeatable progress in my park came from ordinary maintenance actions I
performed myself:

- cleaning dirty attractions;
- repairing damaged attractions;
- mopping puddles;
- clearing messes created during a busy day;
- repeating the circuit after guests had left.

I do not use a fixed claim such as “one repair equals one Research Point.”

The bar moves through many small actions, while the amount of available work
changes with:

- park size;
- guest traffic;
- weather;
- the number of active attractions;
- how much work employees complete first.

The consistent part is the route.

After closing, I can clean and repair without stopping every few seconds to
sell a ticket, serve food or respond to a safety alert.

My circuit begins at the entrance and ticket facilities. I continue through
food and vending, toilets and showers, then follow the pool-and-slide route to
the farthest active attraction before returning toward the Research Table.

The early mistake was ending the day as soon as the financial report appeared.

That skipped the calmest maintenance window and left the same dirty or damaged
facilities waiting for the next morning.

I move to the next day only after:

1. important attractions are repaired;
2. obvious dirt, trash and puddles are cleared;
3. I have checked the Research Table and know what the next points are for.

<h2 id="fast-research-route">The Fastest Research Route I Use Each Day</h2>

I divide every day into three different priorities.

### Before opening

I walk from the entrance to the outer edge of the active park.

I repair anything already close to breaking, check each pool for dirty water
and remove visible objects before considering Chlorine.

This prevents a research session from becoming a bad operating day.

A broken slide may create another repair opportunity, but it can also create
injuries, refunds and lost attraction income. I do not deliberately leave
critical facilities broken to manufacture Research progress.

### During business hours

Once guests enter, I prioritise:

1. drowning or injured guests;
2. broken attractions;
3. pools with dirty water;
4. ticket or food queues;
5. overflowing bins and large puddle clusters;
6. cosmetic cleaning that can wait until closing.

I do not cross the whole map for one small dirty mark while the entrance queue
is stalled.

When several facilities become dirty during the same period, I remember their
locations and collect them into one route after closing.

### After closing

This is where most of my controlled progress happens.

My order is:

1. entrance and ticket facilities;
2. nearby food stands and vending machines;
3. toilets and showers;
4. the first pool, diving board and slide;
5. outer attractions and expanded sections;
6. remaining puddles, dirt and damaged buildings;
7. Research Table;
8. next day.

The layout can change, but the principle does not: one continuous circuit is
faster than bouncing between isolated alerts.

<h2 id="chlorine-research-route">When I Rush Chlorine—and When I Do Not</h2>

Chlorine became the first major Research target when dirty water started
forcing repeated drain-and-refill cycles.

In this Hard Mode save, the remaining route through **Modern Toilets** and
**Mop Upgrade** into **Chlorine** represented roughly **16 Research Points** at
the moment I checked the tree.

I rush Chlorine when:

- several pools become dirty during the same operating day;
- draining one pool removes too much usable capacity;
- dirty-water complaints produce refunds;
- refill trips interrupt tickets, food or safety work;
- a progression or Museum task requires it.

I delay Chlorine when the actual bottleneck is:

- a blocked entrance;
- empty vending machines;
- an exhausted Ticket Worker;
- an employee limit;
- a park that cannot afford another utility branch.

Chlorine does not solve those problems.

In this route, it became available at **Research Level 7**.

Its important limitation is that it cleans dirty water without requiring a full
drain, but it does not remove bottles, trash or other physical objects from the
pool.

My Chlorine route is:

1. confirm that the pool water is actually dirty;
2. remove visible objects from the pool;
3. buy Chlorine from the shop;
4. carry it back to the affected pool;
5. place it into the dirty water;
6. keep the pool operating instead of draining it completely.

I do not throw Chlorine into every pool on a schedule. I use it when the dirty
water state is present.

<h2 id="research-order">The Best Research Order Depends on the Bottleneck</h2>

I do not use one fixed Research order for every park.

Before spending, I ask which problem is currently costing the most time, money
or Satisfaction.

### Dirty water controls the day

I work toward the utility branch and Chlorine.

This is the correct choice when I repeatedly leave tickets, food or repairs to
drain and refill pools.

The upgrade removes a recurring manual job and protects attraction capacity
during business hours.

### Guests are waiting at the entrance

I prioritise ticket processing before buying another attraction.

Cashier upgrades and the Ticket ATM matter when guests are physically waiting
to enter.

The Ticket ATM can automate supported card customers, but cash customers still
need a staffed counter. I do not assume one machine completely replaces ticket
staff.

I also do not increase guest traffic while the entrance is already the
bottleneck.

### Food and vending queues keep appearing

I move toward Vendor Training, vending support or Restocker access.

I prioritise this branch only after demand is strong enough to justify it.

One machine that empties occasionally is still manageable. Several machines
emptying while I am also running tickets, cleaning pools and completing Museum
tasks is the point where automation starts earning its cost.

### The employee limit blocks the next hire

I buy **Hiring Level Up** before unlocking another role I cannot employ.

This is a common mid-game trap. Lifeguard, Security, Restocker and other nodes
can appear while the park is already at its staff cap.

Unlocking the role does not create an employee slot.

In the larger park, **6 Research Points** bought the next hiring-limit upgrade
and opened room for two more employees. That was more useful than another
attraction while trash and maintenance were already falling behind.

<h2 id="staff-and-research">Why Employees Can Slow Personal Research Progress</h2>

The most surprising Research bottleneck was competent staff.

Two Janitors kept the park cleaner, but they also reached puddles before I
could mop them.

That was good for Satisfaction and bad when I needed a personal maintenance
counter or a short Research push.

The same problem appeared after hiring Maintenance.

When I later needed roughly **6 more Research Points** for a required facility,
the worker was already repairing attractions before I reached them.

I do not fire useful employees every time I need Research.

My rule is:

- keep fewer cleaners while the park is compact and manageable;
- add another Janitor when trash and puddles are escaping the first;
- hire Maintenance when breakdowns interrupt service;
- accept slower personal Research once the park is too large to maintain alone.

There is no value in preserving every repair for myself while refunds, safety
and Satisfaction deteriorate faster than I can complete the route.

When I need a short Research push, I also postpone a major expansion until the
points are secured. A distant new section creates more travel and emergencies,
but it does not guarantee that I will reach the available work before staff.

<h2 id="research-checklist">My Checklist Before Spending Research Points</h2>

Before confirming a purchase, I ask:

- What interrupted me most during the last operating day?
- Did guests leave because of water quality, queues, dirt or boredom?
- Is the problem caused by a missing upgrade or poor placement?
- Can I afford the facility or employee unlocked by this node?
- Does the next star objective require a specific purchase?
- Will the upgrade remove a repeated job or add another one?
- Is the current area stable enough for Park Size?
- Am I spending only because the points are available?

If the node does not remove a current bottleneck or unlock the next required
step, I keep the points.

I avoid four expensive mistakes:

1. buying another attraction while a utility problem repeats;
2. ending the day before the maintenance circuit;
3. unlocking staff roles without increasing the employee limit;
4. expanding only to create more cleaning and repair work.

Research Points are slow enough that one convenient purchase can delay the
upgrade that actually changes the park.
