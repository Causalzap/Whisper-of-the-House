I stopped treating Satisfaction as a decoration score after one Hard Mode day
ended at **60%**.

The Daily Report showed the real failure: **32 guests complained that a pool
had no water**, while **22 guests were relying on only two pools**. Buying more
decoration would not have repaired either problem.

I recovered by restoring every active pool, fixing the service route from the
entrance to the farthest attraction, and adding capacity only after the
existing park could survive a full operating day.

That same Hard Mode Beach Map park later handled **103 guests at 94%
Satisfaction**. The improvement did not come from one hidden upgrade.
Ticketing, stock, water, trash, staff and attraction uptime all had to keep
moving together.

> Tested in Waterpark Simulator 1.0 on a new Hard Mode Beach Map save through
> four stars. Prices and staffing pressure may differ on Normal difficulty.

Satisfaction is separate from Park Prestige. Fixing guest complaints makes the
park easier to grow, but it does not replace the Road to the Next Star
objectives.

<h2 id="read-satisfaction-report">Why Satisfaction Is Low or Stuck Around 80%</h2>

I have not found a hidden 80% Satisfaction cap.

When my park moved from **70% to 78% and then 82%**, the score was still
responding to the mix of service, cleanliness, capacity and quality complaints.

When the percentage appears stuck, I look for one problem that is repeating
across several guests rather than buying another random decoration.

The percentage tells me whether the day was good or bad. The complaint list
tells me what to fix.

I group the messages into five practical problems:

- **service:** waiting for tickets, food, drinks, inflatables or staffed
  facilities;
- **cleanliness:** trash, vomit, dirty attractions, puddles and dirty water;
- **basic needs:** no nearby shower, toilet, trash bin, lounger or food;
- **capacity:** too many guests sharing too few usable attractions;
- **quality:** weak decoration, low-Prestige facilities or boredom.

I do not try to solve every line before the next day. I first address the
problem affecting the most guests or causing an immediate refund.

One early report listed:

- “I can't find a trash bin”;
- “Decoration needs improvement”;
- “I saw something disgusting”;
- “I need a shower.”

Trash coverage and showers came before decoration because they affected basic
operation across the whole day.

### Why 100% Satisfaction can still hide problems

I had a **100% Satisfaction** day that still included a refunded ticket.

The percentage summarises the overall day. It does not prove that every guest
was served correctly or that every route worked without interruption.

Even at 100%, I still check:

- ticket refunds;
- injuries;
- guests who left early;
- repeated complaint categories;
- queues that formed while an employee was resting.

<h2 id="fix-satisfaction-losses">What to Fix First When Satisfaction Drops</h2>

My priority is not whichever complaint sounds most annoying. It is whichever
problem can make a guest leave, request a refund or stop using the park.

The order I use is:

1. pools without water;
2. broken or unsafe attractions;
3. guests waiting too long for service;
4. dirty water, vomit and visible trash;
5. missing toilets, showers or bins;
6. boredom and decoration.

A missing decoration reduces the quality of the visit. A pool without water
removes the attraction entirely.

That difference caused the worst day in my opening park.

When this happens, I stop construction and close the park if necessary. A third
pool does not help while the two existing pools are empty, dirty or connected
to broken attractions.

I stabilise the current area in this order:

1. refill every usable pool;
2. remove physical trash from the water;
3. use Chlorine on pools that are actually dirty;
4. repair the connected slide or diving board;
5. confirm that guests can enter and leave safely;
6. add another pool only after the current pools are working again.

Chlorine cleans dirty water, but it does not remove bottles, trash or other
physical objects. I treat those as separate problems.

<h2 id="satisfaction-check-route">My Pre-Opening Satisfaction Check</h2>

I use one entrance-to-attraction route before every operating day.

### 1. Entrance

I confirm that the Ticket Worker is present, the booth is assigned and the
opening queue can move.

I also check that the Ticket ATM is operating and that the remaining queue
still has a staffed counter available.

I do not activate Poster Marketing while guests are already waiting outside.

### 2. Food and vending

I restock every active food, drink and Inflatable seller before opening.

An empty machine is a predictable service failure, not an emergency I should
discover during the busiest part of the day.

### 3. Toilets, showers and trash bins

I place these facilities along the paths guests actually use:

- near food and seating;
- between the entrance and first pool;
- beside active pool areas;
- inside remote themed sections after they open.

Better trash bins may cover a wider area, but I still place them where litter
is generated. Coverage does not help much when every bin is far from food and
seating.

### 4. Pools and connected attractions

For every active pool, I check:

- water is present;
- dirty water and physical trash separately;
- connected slides and boards are repaired;
- a shower and trash bin are nearby;
- staff can reach the area.

Only after this route is complete do I open the park.

<h2 id="waiting-for-service">How I Fix “Waiting for Service”</h2>

“Waiting for service” does not automatically mean I need another employee.

I go to the failed location and check:

1. Is the employee resting?
2. Is the employee assigned to this exact station?
3. Does the station have stock?
4. Is one worker expected to cover several facilities?
5. Did guest traffic increase faster than the service setup?

I once watched a guest leave because the wait became too long while the park
was already dealing with heavy trash and food demand.

<h2 id="trash-and-disgusting-complaints">How to Fix “I Saw Something Disgusting”</h2>

This complaint does not point to one specific object.

I check the busiest guest route for:

- vomit;
- loose trash;
- floating objects in pools;
- dirty water;
- overflowing trash bins;
- dirty toilets and showers;
- smelly guests;
- waste near seating or food.

I do not assume another toilet automatically fixes it.

One guest left immediately after seeing vomit and received a refund. The useful
lesson was not that vomit can be prevented completely. It was that a visible
mess near the main route must be cleaned before more guests walk past it.

My cleaning priority follows guest traffic:

1. entrance;
2. ticket and food queues;
3. paths around toilets and showers;
4. pool edges and diving-board landings;
5. seating areas;
6. remote expansion zones.

A remote puddle can wait a few seconds. Vomit between the ticket booth and the
first pool cannot.

I also empty bins before they overflow. Adding more bins does not solve the
problem if every existing bin remains full.

<h2 id="capacity-and-satisfaction">When More Capacity Helps—and When It Makes Satisfaction Worse</h2>

In the 60% day, **22 guests were relying on two pools**, so another complete
pool area was a reasonable fix.

I expand only after one stable day where:

- existing pools remain filled and clean;
- entrance and food queues clear;
- staff finish the current route;
- wages and restocking leave cash available;
- hygiene and service no longer dominate the complaints.

I do not expand while the current pools are empty, dirty or broken.

Another pool also adds:

- cleaning;
- repairs;
- showers and trash bins;
- loungers;
- longer staff routes;
- potentially more guests.

A new pool is not only its purchase price.

I also avoid placing it so far from the active park that Janitors, Maintenance
Workers and the player spend most of the day travelling.

I do not expand because the next parcel is available. I expand because the
current parcel has stopped being the bottleneck.

<h2 id="decoration-and-boredom">When Decoration Actually Increases Satisfaction</h2>

Decoration matters after the basic service route works.

I do not spend the last of my cash on a themed area while guests are still
asking for showers, trash bins or usable pools.

Decoration moves higher in my priority order when:

- service and hygiene complaints are under control;
- guests are leaving from boredom;
- a star objective requires Decoration Points;
- I can finish one concentrated themed area;
- enough cash remains for wages and restocking.

Basic loungers cost **$30 each** in this Hard Mode save, so adding two beside an
active pool was a safer early improvement than starting a large decorative
project.

I decorate where guests already spend time:

- beside pools;
- around loungers;
- along food and vending routes;
- inside a complete themed section.

A random statue does not repair a broken visitor route.

<h2 id="recover-low-satisfaction">My One-Day Satisfaction Recovery Test</h2>

After a bad report, I change one major system at a time.

### Before the next day

I identify the complaint affecting the largest number of guests, then make one
targeted correction:

- no water → restore and keep the existing pools open;
- no trash bin → add coverage on that route;
- no shower → place one beside the affected pool;
- waiting for service → restock, reassign or cover the station;
- overcrowded pools → add one complete pool area;
- boredom → add a usable attraction or concentrated decoration.

### During the test day

I keep guest traffic close to the previous day.

I do not combine the repair with:

- new Poster Marketing;
- a higher guest limit;
- another park expansion;
- several new employees;
- a second expensive attraction.

If I change several systems together, I cannot tell which one removed the
complaint.

### After the test day

I compare:

- the repeated complaint count;
- Satisfaction;
- ticket refunds;
- injuries;
- service departures;
- whether the same queue or pool failed again.

This process moved the early park from **70% to 78% and then 82%**. The
developed park later handled **103 guests at 94% Satisfaction**.
