My Waterpark Simulator route is built around one rule:

**I do not expand until the section I already own can survive a full operating
day.**

On Hard Mode, the fastest way I found to lose control was to unlock a new pool,
hire another employee and increase guest traffic at the same time.

The park became larger, but tickets, food, cleaning, repairs and pool water all
started failing together.

The route that worked was slower and much more sustainable:

- finish one compact entrance-to-pool service loop;
- complete the current Park Prestige objectives;
- use closing time for cleaning, repairs and Research Points;
- run a stable operating day before expanding;
- hire only for work that is already being missed;
- build themed areas only when I can afford the complete section;
- stop construction when a new area starts breaking the old one.

This Hub owns the continuous **Waterpark Simulator 1.0 Hard Mode Beach Map route
from the tutorial through four stars**.

After four stars, I hand the exact 4.5-star, five-star and Diamond requirements
to the dedicated Prestige guide rather than repeating that late-game route
here.

<h2 id="tutorial-to-one-star">
  Tutorial to One Star: Build the Smallest Working Park
</h2>

I do not try to make the opening area beautiful.

I build the smallest park that can finish the tutorial and serve its first
guests:

1. one functioning pool;
2. one usable slide;
3. a diving board when required;
4. a working ticket entrance;
5. a Hot Dog Stand;
6. at least one toilet;
7. enough loungers for the opening capacity;
8. cleaning and repair tools close enough to reach quickly.

The opening section becomes chaotic before it becomes profitable.

Guests need:

- tickets;
- food;
- clean water;
- toilets;
- showers;
- rescues;
- working attractions

while I am still learning the route.

The answer is not another expansion.

I keep walking the same compact loop until I can operate it without losing
track of the entrance.

After the tutorial, this Hard Mode route reaches the first clear Prestige
objectives:

- sell **15 tickets**;
- build one trash bin;
- build **two showers**.

### Where I place the first services

I put the first trash bin on the route between:

- the entrance;
- food;
- the pool.

That is where guests already travel and where most early litter appears.

I place the two showers beside the pool route rather than at the far edge of the
park.

The first toilet also stays near the pool and food area.

Spreading essential facilities across unused land only makes the opening route
longer.

After these facilities were placed, this park could hold **eight guests** at the
same time.

### My pre-opening route

Before opening the next day, I walk:

1. ticket booth;
2. Hot Dog Stand;
3. toilet and showers;
4. pool;
5. slide and diving board;
6. trash bin;
7. back to the entrance.

I repair anything already damaged and remove visible trash before the first
queue forms.

### How long one star took

This continuous Hard Mode run reached one star after roughly **37 minutes**,
when the fifteenth ticket was sold.

That is a reference, not a required speed.

I would rather spend several extra minutes building a stable service loop than
reach one star with a park that immediately collapses.

### Upgrade the active route before building far away

One early pool upgrade changed:

- guest price from **$2 to $4**;
- maximum capacity by **three additional visitors**;
- hygiene, durability and safety;
- Fun from +4 to +5;
- daily maintenance upward.

I accepted the higher maintenance because guests were already using that part of
the park.

The supporting infrastructure already existed:

- guests could reach it;
- showers were nearby;
- I already cleaned the area;
- the slide and diving board were already on my patrol;
- ticket and food service did not need to be duplicated.

That is much safer than unlocking a new pool and immediately dropping it at the
far edge of the map.

### Do not destroy several old facilities together

Removing an old object can return some money, but I do not plan around getting
the entire original cost back.

I rebuild in stages:

1. replace or upgrade one facility;
2. finish the replacement;
3. run a normal operating day;
4. check cash and maintenance;
5. replace another item only after the park recovers.

Destroying several usable facilities at once can leave me with:

- less capacity;
- less income;
- too little cash to complete the rebuild.

<h2 id="daily-operating-loop">
  The Daily Operating Loop I Use Before Expanding
</h2>

Once the park has several attractions, I split each day into three routes.

### Before opening

I start at the entrance and move outward:

1. check the ticket station and assigned worker;
2. restock food, drinks and vending machines;
3. empty bins already close to full;
4. inspect toilets and showers;
5. check each active pool for water and physical trash;
6. repair slides, diving boards and service buildings;
7. equip the tool needed for the current objective;
8. open only when the route is ready.

### During the operating day

I prioritise failures that can immediately stop a guest from spending money or
remaining in the park:

1. drowning or injured guest;
2. broken attraction;
3. blocked ticket queue;
4. food or service queue;
5. empty pool or dirty water;
6. visible vomit and high-traffic trash;
7. stock shortage;
8. decoration and low-priority cleaning.

I do not cross the map to clean one remote mark while people are still waiting
outside the entrance.

### After closing

Closing time becomes my maintenance and Research shift.

My route is:

1. entrance and ticket area;
2. food and vending;
3. bins, toilets and showers;
4. pools and slides;
5. themed or remote sections;
6. remaining puddles and dirty facilities;
7. Research Table;
8. next day.

This prevents me from ending the day while easy repairs and Research progress
are still available.

<h2 id="two-to-three-stars">
  Two to Three Stars: Finish Cheap Objectives Before Expansion

- repairing **20 buildings**;
- cleaning and operating more facilities;
- selling snacks through vending machines;
- cleaning smelly guests;
- removing graffiti;
- increasing Park Size;
- meeting decoration and facility requirements.

I do not necessarily complete them in the order they appear.

### Complete action objectives first

I finish low-cost work such as:

- repairs;
- cleaning;
- graffiti removal;
- vending sales;
- water-gun objectives;
- flyers;
- rescues

before starting another expensive pool or land purchase.

These actions keep the park operating and often create Research progress at the
same time.

### Save for the required Park Size node

The final three-star gate in this run required the next Park Size expansion.

I considered buying it during an operating day, then waited.

I did not want:

- new land;
- longer guest routes;
- longer cleaning routes;
- another construction project

appearing while I was already handling ticketing, food and pool problems.

The park reached three stars when I purchased the expansion.

<h2 id="guest-capacity">
  Why Guests Today Can Exceed Maximum Capacity
</h2>

Pools increase maximum park capacity.

One upgraded Basic Pool added **three visitors**.

Later pools, upgrades and Park Size pushed active capacity through values such
as:

- 30;
- 47;
- 50;
- the mid-60s.

I do not treat these two values as identical:

- current maximum capacity during operation;
- `Guests Today` in the Daily Report.

One mature day showed maximum capacity around **66**, while the Daily Report
recorded **103 guests**.

My practical interpretation is:

- maximum capacity = roughly how many visitors can be in the park at once;
- Guests Today = total guests processed across the operating day.

The interface did not give me a detailed calculation, so I treat that as an
observed operating interpretation rather than a confirmed formula.

### Capacity is not traffic

A park may have room for 30 or 50 guests while fewer actually arrive.

Traffic can also depend on:

- current Prestige;
- flyers;
- Poster Marketing;
- ticket processing;
- how long the park remains open.

I do not add another pool just because the capacity number is not full.

I first check whether guests are actually reaching the entrance and getting
inside.

### Why can Guests Today be higher than maximum capacity?

In my park, the most useful interpretation is that maximum capacity represents
the active simultaneous limit while `Guests Today` counts visitors processed
across the whole operating day.

A park with maximum capacity in the mid-60s still recorded **103 guests** in one
Daily Report.

<h2 id="final-checklist">
  My Checklist Before Leaving the Four-Star Route
</h2>

Before I push the park into the dedicated late-game Prestige route, I want the
existing build to pass these checks:

- the entrance clears the opening queue;
- food and vending machines stay stocked;
- active pools stay filled and clean;
- remote areas have showers, bins and toilets;
- every Vendor has a real assignment;
- the employee limit and Staff Overflow tax are understood;
- salaries are covered on a normal no-build day;
- Janitors and Maintenance can reach outer sections;
- Museum rewards are not blocked by an ignored star gate;
- I can complete a closing maintenance circuit;
- repeated service failures are no longer crushing Satisfaction;
- one normal day leaves enough cash for wages and restocking.

If the park fails several of those checks, I do not force the next star merely
because the objective is visible.

I return to the oldest failing service loop, fix it and run another ordinary
day.

That approach took this Hard Mode park from an early **60% Satisfaction** failure
to a mature operating day with:

- **103 guests**;
- **94% Satisfaction**;
- **$3,206 in ticket income**;
- **$6,260 from attractions**;
- **$2,819 in tips**.

The larger park still had injuries, refunds, staff expenses and occasional
queues.

The difference was that one failure no longer caused every other system to
collapse.
