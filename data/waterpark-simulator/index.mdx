My Waterpark Simulator route is built around one rule: I do not expand until
the section I already own can survive a full operating day.

On Hard Mode, the fastest way I found to lose control was to unlock a new pool,
hire another employee and increase guest traffic at the same time. The park
became larger, but tickets, food, cleaning, repairs and pool water all started
failing together.

The more reliable route was slower but much easier to sustain:

- finish one compact entrance-to-pool service loop;
- complete the current star objectives;
- use closing time for cleaning, repairs and Research Points;
- run one stable day before expanding;
- hire only for work that is already being missed;
- build themed areas only when I can afford the complete section.

This guide follows one continuous **Waterpark Simulator 1.0 Hard Mode Beach Map
save from the tutorial through four stars**. It keeps the main progression route
in the Hub and sends deeper money, staff, Museum, Satisfaction, Research and
achievement questions to their focused guides.

<h2 id="tutorial-to-one-star">Tutorial to One Star: Build the Smallest Working Park</h2>

I do not try to make the opening area beautiful.

I build the smallest park that can finish the tutorial and serve its first
guests:

1. one functioning pool;
2. one usable slide;
3. a diving board when required by the current objective;
4. a working ticket entrance;
5. a Hot Dog Stand;
6. at least one toilet;
7. enough loungers for the opening capacity;
8. cleaning and repair tools close enough to reach quickly.

The opening section becomes chaotic before it becomes profitable. Guests need
tickets, food, clean water, toilets and rescues while I am still learning where
every tool is stored.

The answer is not another expansion. I keep walking the same compact route
until I can operate it without losing track of the entrance.

After the tutorial, the park reaches half a star and receives the first clear
Prestige objectives:

- sell **15 tickets**;
- build one trash bin;
- build **two showers**.

### Where I place the first services

I put the first trash bin on the route between the entrance, food stand and
pool. That is where guests already carry food and generate most early litter.

I place the two showers beside the pool route rather than beside the entrance.

The first toilet also stays near the pool and food area. Spreading these
facilities across unused land only makes the opening maintenance route longer.

After these facilities were placed, this park could hold **eight guests** at
the same time.

### My pre-opening route

Before opening the next day, I walk:

1. ticket booth;
2. Hot Dog Stand;
3. toilet and showers;
4. pool;
5. slide and diving board;
6. trash bin;
7. back to the entrance.

I repair anything already damaged and remove visible trash before the first
queue forms.

### How long one star took

This continuous Hard Mode run reached one star after roughly **37 minutes**,
when the fifteenth ticket was sold.

That is a reference, not a required speed. I would rather spend several extra
minutes on working showers and bins than reach one star with a park that
immediately collapses.

### Upgrade the active route before building far away

One early pool upgrade changed:

- guest price from **$2 to $4**;
- maximum capacity by **three additional visitors**;
- hygiene, durability and safety ratings;
- Fun from +4 to +5;
- daily maintenance upward.

I accepted the higher maintenance because guests were already using this part
of the park.

Its supporting infrastructure already existed:

- the guest route reached it;
- showers were nearby;
- I was already cleaning the area;
- the slide and diving board were already on my patrol;
- ticket and food service did not need to be duplicated.

I removed the old screenshot from this section because the available image was
too blurred to prove the displayed price and upgrade values clearly. The
numbers remain part of the tested route, but I would only restore the figure
with a readable original capture.

### Do not destroy several old facilities together

Removing an old object returns some money, but not necessarily its complete
purchase price.

I replace one section at a time:

1. remove or upgrade one facility;
2. complete its replacement;
3. run one normal day;
4. compare income and maintenance;
5. replace the next item only after cash recovers.

Destroying several usable objects together can leave the park with less
capacity and too little money to rebuild.

### Unlocking staff does not mean I can afford staff

One and a half stars opened Cashier Staff in this route.

A Ticket Worker candidate initially cost roughly **$270–$294 per day**, while
the park sometimes held less than that amount in total cash.

I continued selling tickets myself until:

- the entrance repeatedly interrupted food or maintenance;
- the park could cover the salary;
- enough money remained for tomorrow's stock.

<h2 id="daily-operating-loop">The Daily Operating Loop I Use Before Expanding</h2>

Once the park has several attractions, I divide the day into three routes.

### Before opening

I start at the entrance and move outward:

1. check the ticket station and assigned worker;
2. restock food, drinks and vending machines;
3. empty bins that are already close to full;
4. check toilets and showers;
5. inspect every active pool for water and physical trash;
6. repair slides, diving boards and service buildings;
7. equip the tool required by the current objective;
8. open only after the route is complete.

### During the operating day

I prioritise failures that can end a guest's visit:

1. drowning or injured guest;
2. broken attraction;
3. ticket queue;
4. food or service queue;
5. empty pool or dirty water;
6. visible vomit and high-traffic trash;
7. stock shortages;
8. decoration and low-priority cleaning.

I do not cross the map for one remote dirty mark while guests are still waiting
outside.

### After closing

Closing time is my maintenance and Research shift.

My route is:

1. entrance and ticket area;
2. food and vending;
3. bins, toilets and showers;
4. pools and slides;
5. themed or remote sections;
6. remaining puddles and dirty buildings;
7. Research Table;
8. next day.

This prevents me from ending the day while easy repairs and Research progress
are still available.

<h2 id="two-to-three-stars">Two to Three Stars: Finish Cheap Objectives Before Expansion</h2>

The two-star stage is where the park stops feeling like a tutorial area and
starts behaving like a management game.

The Road to the Third Star included objectives such as:

- repairing **20 buildings**;
- cleaning and operating more facilities;
- selling snacks through vending machines;
- cleaning smelly guests;
- removing graffiti;
- increasing Park Size;
- meeting decoration and facility requirements.

I do not complete these in the order they appear when that order would damage
the park.

### Complete action objectives first

I finish low-cost work such as:

- repairs;
- cleaning;
- graffiti removal;
- vending sales;
- water-gun objectives;
- flyers;
- rescues;

before starting another expensive pool or expansion.

These actions keep the park operating and often build Research progress at the
same time.

### Save for the required Park Size node

The final three-star gate in this run required the next Park Size expansion.

I considered buying it during an operating day, then waited because I did not
want new land and new guest movement appearing while I was already handling
tickets, food and pool problems.

The park reached three stars when I purchased the expansion.

<h2 id="when-to-buy">When I Buy, Hire, Expand or Turn Back</h2>

### I buy another pool when

- existing pools are filled, clean and operational;
- guests are using the current capacity;
- entrance and food service are stable;
- I can afford the pool and supporting facilities;
- staff can reach the location;
- cash remains for wages and stock.

### I wait when

- an existing pool has no water;
- dirty water is already causing complaints;
- the entrance queue is blocked;
- the new pool would sit alone in a remote area;
- I cannot afford nearby showers, bins and seating;
- I am buying only because the attraction was just unlocked.

### I hire when

- the same task fails on several days;
- doing it myself causes another important job to fail;
- the salary is smaller than the income or Satisfaction being lost;
- the role has a clear assignment;
- enough cash remains for the next day.

### I do not hire when

- the employee has no permanent workload;
- the park cannot afford tomorrow's stock;
- the normal staff cap is full and Staff Overflow tax is not justified;
- I only need the role for one small objective;
- a better layout would solve the problem.

### I return to the older area when

- a new section creates long rescue routes;
- employees spend most of the day travelling;
- vending stock remains empty;
- trash and water failures spread faster than I can fix them;
- Satisfaction drops after expansion;
- the new area earns less than its recurring costs.

Returning does not mean destroying the expansion. I stop construction and run
the older park until cash, staff and Research catch up.

<h2 id="guest-capacity">Why Guests Today Can Exceed Maximum Capacity</h2>

Pools increase maximum park capacity.

One upgraded Basic Pool added **three visitors**. Later upgrades, additional
pools and Park Size pushed the active limit through values such as 30, 47, 50
and eventually the mid-60s.

I do not treat these two values as the same:

- the active maximum capacity shown during the day;
- `Guests Today` shown in the Daily Report.

One mature day showed maximum capacity around **66**, while the Daily Report
recorded **103 guests**.

My operating interpretation is:

- maximum capacity = visitors who can be present at one time;
- Guests Today = total visitors processed across the full day.

The interface available in this run did not provide a detailed formula, so I
treat that as an observed interpretation rather than a confirmed calculation.

### Capacity is not traffic

A park may have room for 30 or 50 guests while fewer actually arrive.

Traffic can also be affected by:

- current Prestige;
- flyers;
- Poster Marketing;
- ticket processing;
- how much time remains in the operating day.

I do not add another pool only because the capacity meter is not full. I first
check whether guests are reaching the entrance and being processed.

<h2 id="small-systems">Smaller Systems That Stay in the Hub</h2>

### Chlorine

Chlorine cleans dirty pool water without requiring the whole pool to be
drained.

It does not remove bottles or physical trash.

I remove visible objects first, then use Chlorine only when the pool has the
dirty-water state.

### Poster Marketing

Poster Marketing increases guest traffic by placing posters around town.

In this save, the posters remained present on the following day. I still avoid
using them while the entrance, vending stock or cleaning route is failing.

### Night Shift

Night Shift lets the park continue operating after sunset with additional staff
costs.

A longer day can produce more ticket and attraction income, but it also creates
more:

- staff fatigue;
- stock consumption;
- trash;
- dirty water;
- repairs.

I activate it only after the normal operating day is stable.

### Zipline

I treat the Zipline as a late transport or attraction project.

I first build and test a short connection before trying to cross a large part
of the Beach Map. That confirms the start, end and interaction work before I
spend the operating reserve on a larger network.

### Custom Slides

Custom Slides use a dedicated editor with route, distance and placement
controls.

<h2 id="hub-faq">Waterpark Simulator Progression Questions</h2>

### Can Waterpark Simulator be played in multiplayer?

Waterpark Simulator 1.0 includes multiplayer, but this playthrough did not
verify the system deeply enough for a dedicated guide.

The current material does not establish:

- who owns the save;
- whether money and Research are shared;
- what non-host players retain;
- reconnect behaviour;
- cross-platform support;
- the practical player limit in a live park.

I do not fill those gaps with assumptions.

### Why can Guests Today be higher than maximum capacity?

The most likely explanation is that maximum capacity measures simultaneous
visitors, while Guests Today counts everyone who entered during the operating
day.

A park with maximum capacity in the mid-60s still recorded 103 total guests in
one report.

### Is City Map progression identical to Beach Map progression?

I do not assume it is identical.

The achievement list includes separate three- and five-star City Map
achievements, while this route was completed on Beach.

The management principles transfer, but layout, expansion direction and build
decisions can differ.

### Does this guide cover five stars and Diamond Prestige?

Not yet.

This Hub verifies the route through four stars. The game includes:

- general five-star Prestige;
- Beach Map five stars;
- City Map five stars;
- Hard Mode five stars;
- Diamond Star Prestige.

Their existence is covered in the achievements guide, but this page does not
invent the missing four-and-a-half-star, five-star or Diamond objectives.

<h2 id="final-checklist">My Checklist Before Continuing Past Four Stars</h2>

Before pushing farther, I want the existing park to pass these checks:

- the entrance clears the opening queue;
- food and vending machines remain stocked;
- active pools stay filled and clean;
- remote areas have showers, bins and toilets;
- every Vendor has a stall assignment;
- the employee limit and Staff Overflow tax are understood;
- salaries are covered on a no-build day;
- Janitors and Maintenance can reach the outer sections;
- Museum rewards are not blocked by an ignored star gate;
- I can complete a closing maintenance circuit;
- repeated service failures are no longer holding down Satisfaction;
- one ordinary day leaves enough cash for wages and restocking.

If the park fails several checks, I do not force another star objective merely
because it is visible.

I return to the oldest failing service route, fix it and run one ordinary day.

That approach took this Hard Mode park from an early **60% Satisfaction**
failure to a mature day with:

- **103 guests**;
- **94% Satisfaction**;
- **$3,206 in ticket income**;
- **$6,260 from attractions**;
- **$2,819 in tips**.

The larger park still had injuries, refunds, staff expenses and occasional
queues. The difference was that one failure no longer caused every system to
collapse at the same time.
