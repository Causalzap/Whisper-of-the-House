  <GuideQuickAnswer title="What Should You Do First in Permafrost?">
    Choose **Hunter + Engineer** for a first run, get your
    Campfire working, and follow Anders's objectives to
    Horizon. After reaching New Home, complete the current
    construction and radio requirements before spending
    supplies on optional upgrades.
  </GuideQuickAnswer>
</section>


Permafrost rarely leaves you with only one thing to do. A
settlement may need repairs while your equipment is wearing
out, a new recipe may consume materials saved for another
structure, and a promising route can lead into cold severe
enough to make the return journey dangerous.

The next objective matters more than how many supplies you
can collect. At New Home, a better mining tool can be more
useful than another weapon. At House Camp, salvaged metal
becomes more important than Wood. Later, reaching Crow's
Nest brings new requirements for Moonrock, advanced
equipment, and the journey toward White Hell.

Your main journey takes you through **Horizon, Bone Cave,
New Home, Hunter's Glade, House Camp, Logan's Camp, Solar
Facility, and the Urban District** before the preparations
for Last Stand.

The decisions below help you identify which task should
come first, which resources are worth keeping, and when
an optional detour can wait.


<h2
  id="starting-class"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Which Starting Class Should You Choose?
</h2>

**Hunter + Engineer** is a useful first-playthrough
combination. Hunter begins with a Wolf Head Hat and
Improvised Coffee, while Engineer provides starting
bonuses to Running, Axes, and Toughness.

Those advantages suit the opening journey, where you
spend considerable time walking between objectives,
gathering Wood, and surviving encounters with wildlife.

Other choices make sense for different priorities.
Hunter + Tracker favors hunting and animal resources,
while Smuggler + Scavenger offers starting bonuses
better suited to looting and stealth.

The choice does not permanently restrict your skills.
You can still improve mining, crafting, combat, and
exploration abilities regardless of your starting
combination.

For the complete starting equipment, all four
Specializations, skill progression, and Blueprint
Points, see <Link href="/permafrost/classes-skills">Permafrost's starting classes and skills</Link>.


<h2
  id="first-steps"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  What Should You Do Before Leaving for Horizon?
</h2>

After the opening collapse, Anders contacts you over
the radio. The nearby Wood and Stone are enough to
begin preparing for the journey without immediately
searching the surrounding wilderness.

The first priority is getting warm. Build a Campfire,
add fuel, and recover Body Warmth before moving into
the next exposed area.

Then finish the requested basic equipment. The opening
objectives introduce gathering tools, Rope, and a
Crude Bow. These give you ways to collect materials,
hunt, and handle early encounters.

Keep a little Wood after building the Campfire.
Using every piece on tools and unnecessary structures
can leave you without fuel when the next stretch
of travel becomes colder.

### Should You Search Every Container Before Horizon?

Not yet. Some nearby objects are locked, and some
mineral deposits require equipment you do not have.

Collect useful food, basic materials, and anything
needed for your immediate crafting objectives.
Then follow the route toward Horizon instead of
spending your remaining warmth and supplies
exploring every side path.

Horizon provides beds, trading opportunities,
and a safer place to prepare for the next journey.

A new weapon is worth buying when your existing
one is badly damaged or no longer handles ordinary
enemies reliably. Otherwise, keep your money
available until you encounter a problem that
actually requires an upgrade.


<h2
  id="progress-blockers"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Where Are You Stuck in Permafrost?
</h2>

Reaching the marked location does not always finish
the active objective. Some quests require another
conversation, a particular crafted item, or a repair
counter that has not reached its target.

The Journal usually reveals which of those
requirements is still unfinished.

### Horizon or Bone Cave — The Next Quest Won't Start

Robyn asks you to prepare food at Horizon. Cooking
the requested Meat is only part of the assignment;
return to her and finish the conversation before
leaving.

The next major objective involves rescuing the
dog in Bone Cave.

Bring a Bandage and Raw Meat. Treat the dog first,
then interact again to feed it. A cooked Dish
does not replace the required Raw Meat.

After the rescue, continue through the cave
toward the Woods.

### New Home — Your Shelter Is Built, but Progress Has Stopped

Check the exact construction objective rather than
judging the shelter by its appearance.

A wooden house can look complete while one required
piece is missing. The Bed also needs to be claimed
after construction.

The radio introduces a different problem:
**collecting Copper is not the same as producing Wire**.

The solution depends on what is missing:

- **Copper Boulder cannot be mined:** Check the
  tier of your equipped pickaxe.
- **Copper collected but no usable Ingots:** Process
  the ore using the appropriate station.
- **Ingots available but radio still cannot be built:**
  Produce the required Wire and make sure the
  finished items are in your inventory.

You do not have to repair the damaged road bridge
just to unlock Copper mining. The crossing can
make later travel more convenient, but the mining
tool and finished materials determine whether
you can complete the radio.

For mineral locations, pickaxe tiers, Iron,
Moonrock, and processing requirements, use the
<Link href="/permafrost/mining-guide">Permafrost mining guide</Link>.

Once the Radio Station Bench is working, listen
to Icarus's transmission to continue toward
Hunter's Glade.

### Hunter's Glade — The Lookout Still Needs Work

Speaking with Skinwalker begins the lookout
renovation, but repairing only the radio does
not finish the assignment.

Inspect the damaged cabin and upper walkway
with your Building Tool. Complete the listed
repairs, listen to the restored radio, and
speak with Skinwalker again.

Before leaving, collect the available quest
reward. It provides equipment needed for
the next stage of progression.

Robyn's recording handoff and Skinwalker's
optional hunting contracts do not need to
be completed before continuing the main journey.

### House Camp — You Keep Running Out of Materials

Anna's assignments change the type of resources
you need.

The defensive repairs require processed metal
materials rather than more wooden construction
pieces. Search damaged vehicles, abandoned
buildings, and salvageable objects for Scrap.

Follow the equipment and renovation objectives
in order. Repair the marked defenses, return
to Anna, and complete the subsequent cooking
and church communication tasks.

If the next assignment does not appear,
check whether Anna is waiting for another
conversation or whether a required reward
has not been collected.

For the exact quest sequence, repair locations,
construction requirements, and NPC interactions,
follow the <Link href="/permafrost/main-quests-walkthrough">Permafrost main quests walkthrough</Link>.


<h2
  id="survival"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  When Is Your Equipment Too Weak for a Cold Zone?
</h2>

The road near New Home can feel manageable with
starting winter clothing, but that does not mean
the same outfit will protect you near Bunker
Checkpoint or Finn's radio tower.

**Body Warmth and Health are different resources.**
A full Health bar provides little reassurance
when your Body Warmth is already falling rapidly.

Check the environment before leaving shelter.
Cold Zones, storms, and exposed terrain can
make an otherwise short journey dangerous.

A Campfire is useful in areas where construction
is permitted. Some dangerous locations are
No-Build Zones, so carrying Wood does not
guarantee you can create an emergency fire.

### Should You Turn Back or Keep Going?

Consider how much protection remains for
the return journey, not just the distance
to the next objective.

A rapidly falling Body Warmth meter is a
reason to reconsider the crossing. So is
a nearly broken Makeshift Heater when there
is no heated settlement nearby.

The best preparation depends on the immediate
weakness:

- **Already freezing:** Recover Body Warmth
  before departure.
- **Heater nearly worn out:** Repair it or
  prepare another usable source of portable heat.
- **Clothing insufficient for the temperature:**
  Compare Cold Resistance before committing
  materials to replacement equipment.
- **Long crossing ahead:** Carry suitable food,
  drinks, and enough supplies to return safely.

Do not assume an entire new outfit is better
than the clothing you already wear. Individual
pieces can offer different protection, even
when they belong to a later equipment set.

The <Link href="/permafrost/staying-warm">Permafrost cold survival guide</Link>
explains Body Warmth, Makeshift Heater repairs,
warming food and drinks, equipment comparisons,
and the conditions that make a crossing unsafe.


<h2
  id="after-logan"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  What Should You Do After Reaching Logan's Camp?
</h2>

After House Camp and Finn's tower, the main
journey leads toward Logan's warehouse in
New Town.

This is where ordinary preparation begins
to overlap with longer assignments and
higher-tier crafting.

### Logan Wants 900 Bucks — Should You Pay?

Logan offers scavenger membership for
**900 Bucks**, but that purchase is separate
from asking him about the radio tower.

You can begin helping his group without
buying membership immediately.

Keep your money when you still need equipment,
ammunition, or supplies. Pay for membership
when its benefits are worth more to you
than those immediate needs.

The first major favor sends you to the
Rat Queen at Solar's Crossing.

### Parking Zoo — Why Does the Quest Require More Crafting?

Talking to the Rat Queen is only the start.

Her assignment leads into Weapons Bench
preparation, ammunition crafting, and
hunting the creatures threatening her
settlement.

You may need to return to a suitable
construction area to prepare the required
equipment before the hunting objectives
can be completed.

Once the Rat Queen acknowledges your
work, return to Logan rather than
continuing to search the area for
another objective.

Logan's next assignment involves
a doctor and medical supplies.

### Solar Facility — Where Does the Story Continue?

After reporting the doctor's assignment
to Logan, the next communication objective
takes you to Solar Facility.

The radio is in an upper part of the
installation. Listen to the required
transmission, then return to Anna
at House Camp.

Anna directs you toward the Urban District
and Crow's Nest.

This changes the next priority from
improving the old shelter to preparing
for new enemies, advanced materials,
and harder travel.

### Crow's Nest — What Does Jeremiah Need?

Find Jeremiah at Crow's Nest and speak
with him about the equipment needed
for the journey ahead.

He asks you to recover stolen supplies
from a Moon Cultist outpost south of
Crow's Nest.

The frozen billboard and nearby
scaffolding help identify the area.
After recovering the Supplies Container,
return to Jeremiah to hand it over.

The next progression introduces advanced
mineral processing and equipment preparation
for White Hell.

At this point, Iron, Moonrock, suitable
pickaxes, and the correct processing stations
are more important than filling your
inventory with ordinary low-tier materials.

### Dressed for White Hell — What Comes Next?

Prepare the clothing pieces listed by
the active quest and collect the processed
materials required by their recipes.

Check the exact item names before
committing valuable materials. Not every
piece of winter clothing satisfies a
late-game armor objective.

Completing **Dressed for White Hell**
advances the Journal to **Go to the
Last Stand**.

That is the point where armor preparation
gives way to the next story objective.


<h2
  id="equipment-priorities"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Which Supplies Should You Save, and What Can Wait?
</h2>

Not every upgrade deserves the resources
needed to craft it. The useful question
is whether the item removes the obstacle
preventing your next objective.

At New Home, a pickaxe upgrade has more
immediate value than an optional weapon
because it makes the required mineral
materials obtainable.

Before a difficult Cold Zone, a working
heater and better cold protection may
be more important than another
Workbench or storage chest.

When you reach later construction
and armor objectives, processed Iron
and advanced materials become more
valuable than another large supply
of basic tools.

A few resource choices are especially
worth watching:

- **Wood:** Keep enough for heating
  and required construction instead
  of converting every piece into fuel
  or optional structures.
- **Copper Ingots:** Make the Wire you
  need, but do not automatically
  convert every remaining ingot.
  Later crafting also uses the
  ingots directly.
- **Scrap and Iron:** Reserve materials
  for the current workstation or
  armor requirement before spending
  them on optional equipment.
- **Rare Whiskey:** Keep a bottle for
  the trader achievement instead of
  consuming it as an ordinary drink.

The same principle applies to exploration.
Hunting contracts, distant locked
containers, decorative construction,
and mineral deposits above your current
tool tier can wait until you have a
reason to return.

An optional detour becomes worthwhile
when it provides the equipment, resource,
or protection that your next objective
actually needs.


<h2
  id="achievements"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Should You Plan for All 42 Achievements From the Start?
</h2>

Most Permafrost achievements can advance
alongside ordinary play. Traveling,
building, crafting, repairing equipment,
and collecting recordings all contribute
toward different completion goals.

A few achievements require decisions
that are easier to make before committing
to a particular run.

**The Hell Has Frozen** requires completing
the main story with **Temperature Around
Player** set to minimum in the difficulty
settings. Choose that modifier before
starting when you intend to attempt
the challenge.

**Hell Runner** requires completing
White Hell without equipping Lunar Armor.
Remember the equipment restriction before
preparing for that section of the game.

Other achievements involve specific
actions, such as selling rare whiskey,
learning workstation recipes, or
making a long-distance bow kill.

You do not need to abandon the main story
to pursue every ordinary cumulative goal
as soon as it appears. Keep track of
what has already been completed, then
return for the remaining objectives
when you have suitable equipment.

Use the <Link href="/permafrost/achievements">Permafrost 42-achievement checklist</Link>
to track completed goals and find
the requirements for individual
achievements.
