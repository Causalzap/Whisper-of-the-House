Your starting choices in Permafrost affect the equipment you
carry into the opening area and which skills begin with a
small advantage. They do not lock you into a particular
weapon, crafting style, or progression route.

Character creation has two separate decisions:

- **Background:** Hunter, Survivor, or Smuggler.
  This determines your starting clothing and supplies.
- **Specialization:** Engineer, Tracker, Scavenger, or
  Marksman. Each provides +1 to three specific skills.

Any Background can be combined with any Specialization.
A Hunter can begin with Engineer's construction-oriented
bonuses, while a Survivor can choose Marksman for ranged
combat.

The difference matters most during the opening journey,
when Wood, food, tools, and cold protection are limited.
Later, every character can develop skills outside the
three selected starting bonuses.


<h2
  id="best-starting-class"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Why Hunter + Engineer Is a Good Starting Combination
</h2>

Hunter + Engineer is a practical choice when you want
to establish New Home and continue the main story
without concentrating on one type of combat.

Hunter provides:

- Wolf Head Hat
- Improvised Coffee
- Instant Oatmeal
- Makeshift Bandage
- Other starting winter clothing

Engineer adds:

- **Running +1**
- **Axes +1**
- **Toughness +1**

The advantage is the combination of useful supplies
and skill bonuses for activities you will perform
throughout the opening hours.

Wood is needed for Campfires, tools, construction
pieces, and crafting stations. Running is involved
in almost every journey between settlements.
Toughness becomes relevant when wildlife or hostile
survivors attack.

The Wolf Head Hat gives Hunter useful cold protection
from the beginning, while Improvised Coffee provides
a temporary Cold Resistance benefit and restores Energy.

### What Do You Give Up by Choosing Engineer?

Engineer has no starting bonus to Pickaxes, Bows,
Skinning, or Lockpicking.

That does not prevent you from using those skills.
It simply means they begin without the additional
level granted by another Specialization.

For example, Axes and Pickaxes are separate skills.
Engineer's Axes bonus does not make a Stone Pickaxe
capable of mining Copper. You still need the proper
tool upgrade.

Likewise, choosing Engineer instead of Tracker means
you will develop Bows and Skinning through normal
hunting rather than receiving their initial +1 levels.

For a first character focused on travel, construction,
and ordinary survival, that is a reasonable exchange.
Players planning to hunt frequently may benefit more
from Tracker's bonuses.

  Hunter vs. Survivor vs. Smuggler: Starting Gear
</h2>

All three Backgrounds begin with the basic equipment
needed to survive the opening area.

The important differences are the clothing and
consumables available before you reach Horizon.

### Hunter — Wolf Head Hat and Improvised Coffee

Hunter starts with a distinctive Wolf Head Hat
and useful supplies:

- **1 Makeshift Bandage**
- **1 Improvised Coffee**
- **1 Instant Oatmeal**
- **Wolf Head Hat**
- Remaining Casual clothing pieces

The hat is Hunter's main equipment advantage.
It remains worth comparing against later headwear
rather than replacing automatically when a new
armor set becomes available.

Improvised Coffee is useful during travel when
Energy and cold exposure both need attention.
Saving it for a longer journey can be more valuable
than consuming it immediately beside a Campfire.

Hunter is a good choice for players expecting
frequent outdoor gathering and exploration.

The Wolf Head Hat is not permanently exclusive
to this Background. Skinwalker's wolf hunting
challenge at Hunter's Glade can also provide one,
so selecting Survivor or Smuggler does not mean
you can never obtain it.

For the hat's exact equipment comparison and
the effects of warming consumables, see
<Link href="/permafrost/staying-warm">how to stay warm in Permafrost</Link>.

### Survivor — Improvised Tea and Winter Clothing

Survivor begins with:

- **1 Makeshift Bandage**
- **1 Improvised Tea**
- **1 Granola Bar**
- Survivor Hood
- Survivor Uniform
- Survivor Trousers
- Survivor Gloves
- Survivor Boots

Improvised Tea is the most useful distinction
between Survivor's consumables and Hunter's.

Both drinks provide Cold Resistance, but Coffee
restores Energy while Tea restores Health.

Survivor is a sensible alternative when you prefer
carrying a healing drink from the beginning.
The choice does not prevent you from selecting
Engineer, Tracker, or another Specialization.

**Survivor + Engineer** keeps Engineer's three
skill bonuses while giving you Survivor's outfit
and starting consumables.

### Smuggler — Energy Drink and Chocolate Bar

Smuggler starts with:

- **1 Makeshift Bandage**
- **1 Energy Drink**
- **1 Chocolate Bar**
- Casual winter clothing, including a Casual Hat

The Energy Drink and Chocolate Bar are useful
for managing your supplies during the opening
journey.

Smuggler does not automatically begin with
Lockpicking or Sneaking bonuses.

Those come from the **Scavenger Specialization**,
which is selected separately.

A Smuggler can choose Engineer for gathering
and survival or Marksman for ranged combat
without losing the starting Smuggler equipment.

Its supplies are less directly focused on
cold protection than Hunter's Wolf Head Hat
and Improvised Coffee. The Background becomes
more appealing when you intend to explore
optional buildings and collect resources
rather than rush through the early construction
objectives.


<h2
  id="specializations"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  All Four Permafrost Specializations and Their Bonuses
</h2>

Specializations affect your starting skills,
not your starting clothing or consumables.

Each provides **+1 to three skills**, with
no requirement to choose a particular Background.

### Engineer — Running, Axes, Toughness

**Starting bonuses:**

- Running +1
- Axes +1
- Toughness +1

Engineer suits a character who spends considerable
time traveling, gathering Wood, and working through
the construction tasks at New Home.

The Running bonus is relevant to frequent movement
between objectives. Axes is used during Wood gathering,
and Toughness helps develop a skill associated with
surviving damage.

The main limitation is its lack of bonuses to
hunting, ranged combat, and mineral gathering.

Engineer is useful throughout the game, but it
does not bypass equipment requirements or
automatically unlock better crafting recipes.

### Tracker — Skinning, Bows, Knives

**Starting bonuses:**

- Skinning +1
- Bows +1
- Knives +1

Tracker covers two connected activities:
hunting an animal and processing its carcass.

A bow helps you attack wildlife from a distance.
Afterward, a suitable knife or Shiv is needed
to gather Meat, Hide, and other materials.

Tracker becomes particularly useful when hunting
is a regular part of your survival routine.

Its bonuses also remain relevant during
Skinwalker's hunting contracts.

Choosing Tracker does not provide free ammunition
or eliminate the need to maintain your tools.
You still need the appropriate weapons,
arrows, and crafting materials.

### Scavenger — Lockpicking, Sneaking, Running

**Starting bonuses:**

- Lockpicking +1
- Sneaking +1
- Running +1

Scavenger favors exploration of abandoned
buildings, locked containers, and hostile areas.

Lockpicking gives you a starting level in
that skill, while Sneaking and Running support
movement through dangerous locations.

The important limitation is that a Lockpicking
bonus does not guarantee success with every lock.

Some containers require an appropriate tool,
and failed opening attempts can still occur.

Scavenger becomes more useful when you spend
time searching optional locations. It offers
less direct help with early Wood gathering
than Engineer.

### Marksman — Accuracy, Firearms, Bows

**Starting bonuses:**

- Accuracy +1
- Firearms +1
- Bows +1

Marksman concentrates its three bonuses
on ranged combat.

Bows is immediately relevant because the
opening objectives introduce a Crude Bow.

Firearms becomes useful once you obtain a
gun and ammunition later in the journey.

**Marksman does not give you a firearm
at character creation.**

The early survival tasks still require
ordinary gathering, construction, and
crafting, none of which receive an initial
bonus from Marksman.

Choose it when ranged combat is a deliberate
priority rather than expecting it to make
the opening shelter objectives faster.


<h2
  id="best-combinations"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Which Background and Specialization Should You Combine?
</h2>

The most useful pairing depends on whether
your immediate priority is shelter construction,
hunting, optional exploration, or combat.

### First Playthrough — Hunter + Engineer

Use this combination when you want a useful
starting hat and consumables alongside bonuses
to Running, Axes, and Toughness.

It works well for the early journey through
Horizon, Bone Cave, and New Home.

**Survivor + Engineer** is a close alternative
when you prefer the healing benefit of
Improvised Tea.

### Frequent Hunting — Hunter + Tracker

Hunter supplies the Wolf Head Hat and Coffee,
while Tracker improves the starting levels
of Bows, Skinning, and Knives.

This combination is most attractive when
you regularly hunt for food and animal
materials or plan to complete hunting
contracts as you travel.

### Looting and Stealth — Smuggler + Scavenger

Smuggler provides its starting consumables,
and Scavenger adds Lockpicking, Sneaking,
and Running.

Choose it when abandoned buildings, locks,
and optional exploration are a major part
of how you intend to play.

You will still complete the same opening
construction objectives as other characters,
without Engineer's starting Axes bonus.

### Ranged Combat — Hunter + Marksman

Hunter's starting equipment works well
with Marksman's Accuracy, Firearms,
and Bows bonuses.

Your Crude Bow makes one of those
bonuses relevant early, while firearms
become useful after you acquire a gun.

The pairing does not remove the need
to craft ammunition or gather materials
for better weapons.

None of these combinations prevents you
from developing skills outside your
starting choices. A character who begins
with Scavenger can still become proficient
with bows, mining tools, or construction
through ordinary play.


<h2
  id="skill-progression"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  How to Level All 16 Skills in Permafrost
</h2>

Permafrost has **16 character skills**.
They develop through their associated
activities as you play.

Your Specialization adds one starting
level to three of them. It does not
prevent the other skills from gaining
experience.

Open the **Skills** screen to inspect
your current levels and progress toward
the next level.

### Movement: Running and Jumping

The movement skills are:

- Running
- Jumping

Running develops as you move through the world.
It is one of the easier skills to use consistently,
since the main journey requires repeated travel
between settlements.

The skill screen also displays the effects associated
with each skill. For Running, that includes a
Stamina Usage attribute.

Jumping develops through movement involving jumps,
although ordinary travel will not necessarily
advance it as quickly as Running.

Neither skill requires a particular Background
or Specialization.

### Combat: Bows, Firearms, Knives, Accuracy, Toughness

The five combat-related skills are:

- Bows
- Firearms
- Knives
- Accuracy
- Toughness

Weapon use determines much of your combat
progression. Players who rely heavily on bows
will spend more time developing bow-related
skills than those who primarily use firearms
or knives.

Accuracy is a separate skill from Bows and
Firearms, which is why Marksman includes
bonuses to all three.

Toughness is associated with taking damage
and surviving hostile encounters.

Its progression does not make warm clothing,
healing supplies, or appropriate weapons
unnecessary.

You can improve these skills after character
creation even without choosing Tracker
or Marksman.

### Gathering: Axes, Pickaxes, Skinning

Three skills are closely associated with
collecting resources:

- **Axes:** Chopping trees and collecting Wood.
- **Pickaxes:** Mining Stone and mineral deposits.
- **Skinning:** Processing animal carcasses.

They advance through different tools
and activities.

Axes receives frequent use during New Home's
construction tasks, while Pickaxes becomes
important when gathering Flint, Copper,
Coal, and later minerals.

Tool restrictions still apply regardless
of the skill level.

For example, a higher Pickaxes level
does not let a Stone Pickaxe mine Copper.
The actual mining capability depends
on the equipped tool.

The <Link href="/permafrost/mining-guide">Permafrost mining guide</Link>
covers the pickaxe tiers and the mineral
requirements for Copper, Iron, and Moonrock.

### Exploration and Production: Six More Skills

The remaining skills are:

- **Lockpicking:** Opening locks.
- **Sneaking:** Moving stealthily.
- **Building:** Constructing structures.
- **Renovating:** Repairing damaged structures.
- **Crafting:** Producing items and equipment.
- **Cooking:** Preparing food.

Building and Renovating are separate skills.

Building receives experience while you
construct structures at New Home and other
settlements. Renovating becomes relevant
when repairing damaged walls, workstations,
and defenses.

This distinction matters during the
Hunter's Glade lookout repairs and
House Camp renovation objectives.

Crafting develops as you make equipment,
ammunition, and other items. Cooking
progresses through preparing food.

Lockpicking and Sneaking are more closely
connected to optional exploration and
encounters around abandoned settlements.

The character you create can improve
all six regardless of starting
Specialization.

### Which Skills Should You Focus On?

Let your regular activities determine
most of your skill progression.

Wood gathering and construction will
develop Axes and Building. Frequent
travel contributes to Running, while
hunting and resource collection develop
their corresponding skills.

There is little reason to repeatedly
perform an unrelated activity simply
because its skill started one level
lower.

For achievements such as **Jack of All Trades**
and **Master of One**, use the
<Link href="/permafrost/achievements">42-achievement checklist</Link>
to track the required levels and
other completion goals.


<h2
  id="blueprint-points"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  How to Get More Blueprint Points in Permafrost
</h2>

Blueprint Points and ordinary skill
levels serve different purposes.

Skills improve through gameplay.
Blueprint Points unlock recipes
and construction options in the
blueprint trees.

For example, increasing Pickaxes
does not automatically unlock a
new Pickaxe recipe.

You still need to learn the
appropriate blueprint before
crafting the tool.

### What Should You Do When You Run Out of Blueprint Points?

**Building and Crafting are particularly useful
when you need more blueprint unlock points.**

These skills gain experience when you perform
their associated activities.

When they reach another level, additional
points become available for the corresponding
blueprint progression.

For Building:

1. Open the Skills screen and inspect
   your Building progress.
2. Construct useful structures that
   contribute experience.
3. Continue until Building levels up.
4. Return to Blueprints and check
   your available unlock points.

For Crafting, the process is similar.
Producing ordinary items adds Crafting
experience.

Arrows and required equipment can be
useful choices when you already have
the materials.

There is no need to waste an entire
inventory of supplies on unwanted
items. Craft what you still need,
then check whether the next skill
level has provided enough points.

### Which Blueprints Are Worth Unlocking First?

The next unfinished objective is
the most useful way to decide.

Early on, Rope and basic equipment
are important. New Home then
introduces wooden construction,
the Workbench, and additional
production stations.

Later, the Tribal Clothes and
Scrap tool objectives require
other blueprint unlocks.

Some of these recipe groups can
require **2 Blueprint Points**,
so check the displayed cost before
spending your remaining points
on an optional item.

An unlocked blueprint also does
not guarantee you can craft its
item immediately.

You may still need a particular
workstation, processed materials,
or a suitable construction area.

When resources are limited,
unlock the recipe that removes
your current obstacle rather
than spending points on
equipment you cannot yet make.


<h2
  id="difficulty-settings"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Does Your Background Change the Game Difficulty?
</h2>

**No. Background and difficulty are separate
character and world settings.**

Selecting Survivor does not automatically
put the game on Story difficulty.
Choosing Hunter does not force you
to play with harsher survival conditions.

The available difficulty choices include:

- **Story:** A less demanding setting
  for following the narrative,
  exploring, and building.
- **Survival:** More demanding conditions
  involving survival and resource management.
- **Custom:** Lets you adjust individual
  world settings.

Your Background changes starting equipment.
Your Specialization changes three starting
skill levels.

Neither replaces the world difficulty setting.

For example, you can play Hunter + Engineer
on Story difficulty, or combine Survivor
with Scavenger in a more demanding world.

Custom difficulty also contains settings
that can matter for particular completion
goals. Choose those world conditions
before starting when you intend to meet
a specific challenge requirement.


<h2
  id="after-character-creation"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  What Happens After Character Creation?
</h2>

Once you have chosen your Background
and Specialization, finish the
character appearance and companion
settings.

You can select the dog's appearance
and name during character creation.

The companion does not join you
immediately. You rescue it later
during **Dog Days** in Bone Cave.

After confirming your character
and world settings, the opening
begins near a collapsed shelter.

Anders contacts you over the radio,
and your first objectives involve
collecting Wood and Stone,
starting a Campfire, and preparing
basic equipment.

You then travel toward Horizon,
continue through Bone Cave,
and establish New Home.

The <Link href="/permafrost/main-quests-walkthrough">Permafrost main quest walkthrough</Link>
covers the required interactions,
construction objectives, and
routes through the later settlements.
