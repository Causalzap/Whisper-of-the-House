  <GuideQuickAnswer title="What Is the Best Starting Class in Permafrost?">
    Choose **Hunter + Engineer** for your first run. Hunter starts
    with a Wolf Head Hat and Improvised Coffee, while Engineer
    gives +1 Running, Axes, and Toughness. Pick **Hunter + Tracker**
    for frequent hunting or **Smuggler + Scavenger** for looting.
  </GuideQuickAnswer>

Wood, warmth, and travel time are the first pressures you face in
Permafrost. Before you can work on Icarus's communication network,
you need to reach Horizon, survive Bone Cave, and build a shelter
in the Woods. The equipment you start with and the skills you
improve can make those opening hours easier.

Character creation separates these advantages into two choices:

- **Background:** Determines your starting clothing, food,
  drinks, and other supplies.
- **Specialization:** Gives +1 to three particular skills.

There are **3 Backgrounds and 4 Specializations**, and you can
combine them freely. Hunter does not have to choose Tracker,
and Smuggler does not have to choose Scavenger.

For a first playthrough, Hunter + Engineer offers a particularly
useful combination of starting cold protection, movement, Wood
gathering, and survivability. Other combinations become more
attractive when you plan to spend most of your time hunting,
opening containers, or fighting at range.


<h2
  id="best-starting-class"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Why Choose Hunter + Engineer for Your First Run?
</h2>

Hunter provides a **Wolf Head Hat**, Improvised Coffee, and
Instant Oatmeal. Engineer adds **+1 Running, +1 Axes, and
+1 Toughness**.

Those bonuses are relevant to several activities you cannot
avoid during the opening journey.

Running is useful throughout the trip from the starting area
to Horizon and then toward New Home. Axes supports the repeated
Wood gathering required for shelter construction, fuel, tools,
and crafting stations. Toughness gives you an initial advantage
in surviving damage during dangerous encounters.

The Wolf Head Hat is another reason to consider this pairing.
It provides useful cold protection without requiring you to
find or craft a replacement immediately.

Unlike a combination focused entirely on combat, Hunter +
Engineer gives you advantages during both exploration and
construction.

### What Is the Tradeoff?

Engineer does **not** increase Pickaxes.

Axes and Pickaxes are separate skills, so the Engineer bonus
does not help your Stone Pickaxe break Copper or Coal Boulders.
You still need to craft a Flint Pickaxe before collecting
those minerals.

Likewise, Hunter + Engineer does not begin with Tracker's
bonuses to Bows, Skinning, and Knives. You can improve those
skills while hunting, but you will not receive their initial
+1 levels.

The pairing is a good default because Wood gathering,
travel, and basic survival are unavoidable early activities.
Players who expect to spend much more time hunting may
prefer Hunter + Tracker instead.


<h2

  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Hunter, Survivor, or Smuggler: Starting Gear Compared
</h2>

All three Backgrounds begin with basic equipment for surviving
the opening area, including a Building Tool, backpack, winter
clothing, and Makeshift Bandage.

The meaningful differences are the clothing pieces and
consumables provided at character creation.

### Hunter: Wolf Head Hat, Coffee, and Oatmeal

Hunter begins with:

- **1 Makeshift Bandage**
- **1 Improvised Coffee**
- **1 Instant Oatmeal**
- **Wolf Head Hat**
- Remaining Casual clothing pieces

The Wolf Head Hat is the main equipment advantage. Cold
protection matters from the first journey, and the hat
remains useful when you begin comparing clothing upgrades
later in the game.

Improvised Coffee also provides Cold Resistance and Energy
recovery, making it a useful item to save for a longer
journey away from shelter.

Hunter is particularly appealing when you expect to
travel frequently or spend time gathering resources
outside settlements.

You do not need to select Hunter to obtain a Wolf Head
Hat eventually. Skinwalker's wolf hunting challenge
at Hunter's Glade also rewards one.

For the hat's later equipment comparison and the actual
benefits of warming consumables, see <Link href="/permafrost/staying-warm">how to stay warm in Permafrost</Link>.

### Survivor: Tea, Granola Bar, and Winter Clothing

Survivor starts with:

- **1 Makeshift Bandage**
- **1 Improvised Tea**
- **1 Granola Bar**
- **Survivor Hood**
- **Survivor Uniform**
- **Survivor Trousers**
- **Survivor Gloves**
- **Survivor Boots**

Survivor is a useful alternative when you prefer starting
with a healing drink. Improvised Tea provides both Health
recovery and Cold Resistance, allowing it to address two
problems during an exposed journey.

The Background does not automatically improve Building,
Crafting, or Toughness. Those skills depend on your
Specialization and subsequent activity.

Survivor + Engineer is a reasonable pairing if you prefer
this starting equipment but still want the benefits of
Running, Axes, and Toughness.

Choosing Survivor does not change the world's difficulty.
That setting is selected separately.

### Smuggler: Energy Drink and Chocolate Bar

Smuggler begins with:

- **1 Makeshift Bandage**
- **1 Energy Drink**
- **1 Chocolate Bar**
- **Casual winter clothing**, including a Casual Hat

Smuggler's consumables are useful when Energy and Hunger
need attention during the opening journey.

However, this Background does not grant a permanent
Lockpicking or Sneaking bonus. Those advantages belong
to the Scavenger Specialization.

A Smuggler can choose Engineer for gathering or Marksman
for combat just as easily as another Background.

Smuggler becomes most appealing when paired with skills
that support frequent exploration of abandoned buildings
and containers.

Its starting supplies are less compelling if your main
priority is establishing New Home as quickly as possible.


<h2
  id="specializations"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  All Four Specializations and Their Starting Skills
</h2>

Your Specialization provides **+1 to three skills** at
character creation.

These bonuses do not consume Blueprint Points, and
selecting one Specialization does not prevent you from
improving the other skills through normal play.

### Engineer: Running, Axes, and Toughness

Engineer starts with:

- **Running +1**
- **Axes +1**
- **Toughness +1**

  <Image

  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">

Engineer suits the first major construction tasks.

You need Wood for the opening Campfire, weapons, New Home's
wooden structures, and several crafting stations. Axes
therefore receives frequent use long before advanced
equipment becomes available.

Running also matters because your early objectives involve
travel between the starting area, Horizon, Bone Cave, and
the Woods.

Toughness supports survival during encounters with animals
and hostile survivors.

The most important limitation is **no initial Pickaxes
bonus**. Mining capability still depends on the pickaxe
you have equipped, regardless of how much Wood you
have already chopped.

### Tracker: Skinning, Bows, and Knives

Tracker starts with:

- **Skinning +1**
- **Bows +1**
- **Knives +1**

This is the most directly useful Specialization for
hunting-focused play.

The opening journey introduces a bow, and wildlife
provides food and animal materials. After killing
an animal, you also need a suitable knife or Shiv
to process its carcass.

Tracker supports both parts of that activity:
the weapon used for hunting and the tool used
to collect resources afterward.

The bonuses remain relevant when Skinwalker
begins offering hunting contracts at Hunter's Glade.

Tracker does not give you extra arrows, a stronger
starting bow, or a different animal-loot inventory.
You still need to craft weapons, maintain their
condition, and gather the required materials.

### Scavenger: Lockpicking, Sneaking, and Running

Scavenger starts with:

- **Lockpicking +1**
- **Sneaking +1**
- **Running +1**

Scavenger favors players who search buildings,
investigate locked containers, and try to avoid
unnecessary fights.

Running is useful during travel, while Sneaking
becomes relevant around dangerous locations.
Lockpicking gives an initial advantage when
interacting with locks.

It does not guarantee that every locked container
can be opened immediately.

Some interactions also depend on the tool being
used, and an unsuccessful opening attempt may
still occur with the Scavenger bonus.

This makes Scavenger more attractive during
exploration-heavy play than during the opening
construction objectives, which require substantial
Wood gathering and crafting.

### Marksman: Accuracy, Firearms, and Bows

Marksman starts with:

- **Accuracy +1**
- **Firearms +1**
- **Bows +1**

Marksman concentrates all three bonuses on combat,
particularly ranged weapons.

You can benefit from Bows during the opening quests
because the game introduces a Crude Bow early.
Accuracy is also relevant to ranged attacks.

Firearms become useful later, once you have acquired
a gun and ammunition.

Choosing Marksman does **not** place a firearm in your
starting inventory.

For someone planning a combat-focused character,
the initial skill bonuses may be worth taking.
However, Marksman provides no direct starting
bonus to Running, Axes, Pickaxes, or Building.

That makes it less immediately helpful for New
Home's resource and construction requirements.


<h2
  id="best-combinations"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Which Background and Specialization Work Best Together?
</h2>

A Background and Specialization do not have to share the
same theme. Their advantages can complement each other
without repeating the same activity.

The most useful combinations depend on what you expect
to do after leaving the starting area.

### For Your First Shelter and General Survival

**Hunter + Engineer** is the default recommendation.

The starting clothing and consumables help with exposure,
while Running, Axes, and Toughness support frequent early
activities.

This pairing is especially suitable when your main goal
is moving through Horizon and Bone Cave, then establishing
New Home without taking unnecessary detours.

**Survivor + Engineer** is the alternative for someone who
prefers Improvised Tea and Survivor's starting outfit.

Both choices receive the same Engineer skill bonuses,
so the decision comes down to which initial equipment
you would rather carry.

### For Hunting and Animal Materials

**Hunter + Tracker** gives you a hunting-oriented set
of initial bonuses alongside Hunter's equipment.

Bows, Skinning, and Knives all become useful during the
opening story, and they remain relevant when collecting
food, hides, and other animal resources.

This is a sensible choice when hunting will be a regular
part of your survival strategy rather than an occasional
way to obtain Meat.

### For Looting and Avoiding Encounters

**Smuggler + Scavenger** pairs the Energy Drink and
Chocolate Bar with Lockpicking, Sneaking, and Running.

It is more attractive when you intend to explore
optional structures, examine containers, and move
between locations frequently.

The tradeoff appears during New Home: you still need
to complete the same Wood gathering and construction
tasks without Engineer's starting Axes bonus.

### For Ranged Combat

Any Background can use **Marksman**.

Hunter + Marksman is an option when you want Hunter's
starting equipment while concentrating on bows and
eventual firearms.

Your early access to a Crude Bow makes the Bows bonus
immediately relevant, although the Firearms bonus must
wait until you obtain an appropriate weapon.

There is no need to restart simply because you chose
a combination with different strengths. All characters
can develop skills outside their starting Specialization.


<h2

  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  How Do You Level Up Skills in Permafrost?
</h2>

Permafrost has **16 skills** covering movement, combat,
resource gathering, exploration, and construction.

Skills develop through related activities. Your starting
Specialization determines which three begin with an
additional level, but the others remain available
through ordinary play.

### Movement and Combat

The movement skills are **Running** and **Jumping**.

Running is useful because long trips between settlements
consume Stamina. Jumping also relates to movement and
Stamina management.

Combat includes **Bows, Firearms, Knives, Accuracy,
and Toughness**.

Your weapon choice determines which combat skills
receive the most attention. A player who uses a bow
through most encounters will develop differently
from someone who regularly fights with knives
or firearms.

Toughness is associated with surviving damage, but
better skill levels do not replace equipment, healing,
or adequate protection against the cold.

### Gathering Resources

Three skills are particularly relevant to obtaining
early supplies:

- **Axes:** Chopping trees and gathering Wood.
- **Pickaxes:** Mining Stone and mineral deposits.
- **Skinning:** Processing animal carcasses after hunting.

Axes and Pickaxes are separate because gathering Wood
and extracting minerals involve different tools.

Higher skill levels do not remove a tool's mining
restrictions. A Stone Pickaxe still cannot break
Copper simply because you have spent more time mining.

You need an appropriate pickaxe for the deposit.

The <Link href="/permafrost/mining-guide">Permafrost mining instructions</Link>
cover the tool upgrades and mineral requirements
encountered at New Home.

### Exploration, Construction, and Crafting

The remaining skills are:

- **Lockpicking:** Opening locks.
- **Sneaking:** Moving stealthily.
- **Building:** Constructing structures.
- **Renovating:** Repairing damaged structures.
- **Crafting:** Producing equipment and other items.
- **Cooking:** Preparing food.

These activities become relevant at different stages
of the journey.

Building receives use while establishing New Home.
Renovating becomes more important when repairing
the lookout at Hunter's Glade and damaged structures
around House Camp.

Crafting and Cooking are used throughout the game,
although the available recipes depend on the
stations and blueprints you have unlocked.

You can inspect the skill interface to see which
activities your character has developed through
regular use.


<h2
  id="blueprint-points"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Do Skill Levels Unlock Blueprints?
</h2>

**Skill Levels and Blueprint Points are separate.**

Performing an activity develops its related skill.
Blueprint Points are used to unlock crafting recipes
and structures.

For example, running improves your movement-related
progress, but unlocking Rope for the opening equipment
requires using the blueprint system.

Similarly, increasing Pickaxes does not automatically
unlock the next pickaxe recipe.

You must obtain the necessary recipe and materials
before crafting the upgraded tool.

### Which Blueprints Should You Unlock First?

The next unfinished objective should determine where
you spend your points.

During the opening journey, Rope and basic equipment
are important. New Home then introduces wooden
building pieces, the Workbench, and other stations
needed for its construction tasks.

Later objectives require more specialized equipment
and processing.

Before unlocking an optional recipe, check whether
you have the materials and station needed to use it.
A clothing blueprint provides little immediate value
if you have not yet obtained the equipment required
to craft the outfit.

Blueprint Points are most useful when they remove
the requirement preventing you from completing
your current task.


<h2
  id="difficulty-settings"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Does Hunter, Survivor, or Smuggler Change Difficulty?
</h2>

Background and world difficulty are selected independently.

The available difficulty choices include:

- **Story:** A less demanding option for exploring,
  building, and following the narrative.
- **Survival:** More demanding environmental conditions
  and resource management.
- **Custom:** Allows individual world settings to
  be adjusted.

Choosing Survivor does not automatically select Story
difficulty, and Hunter does not force the world into
a harder mode.

Your Background controls starting supplies, while
the difficulty setting controls the conditions
under which you play.

Story is a reasonable choice when you want to learn
the building and survival systems with less pressure.
Survival is more suitable when cold exposure,
resource scarcity, and equipment preparation
are intended to remain major challenges.


<h2
  id="after-character-creation"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  What Happens After Character Creation?
</h2>

After choosing your Background, Specialization,
appearance, and companion settings, confirm
the character and create your world.

The opening begins beside a collapsed shelter,
where Anders contacts you over the radio.

Your immediate objectives are to collect basic
materials, start a Campfire, prepare equipment,
and reach Horizon.

You can customize your dog's appearance during
character creation, but the companion joins
you later during the Dog Days quest in Bone Cave.

The <Link href="/permafrost/main-quests-walkthrough">Permafrost main quest walkthrough</Link>
covers the journey through Horizon and Bone Cave,
building New Home, and reaching the later settlements.
