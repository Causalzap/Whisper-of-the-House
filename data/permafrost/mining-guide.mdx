  <GuideQuickAnswer title="Where Do You Get Copper, Coal, and Flint?">
  Mine Flint in Bone Cave and craft a **Flint Pickaxe**.
  For the nearby Copper ravine, repair the broken road bridge
  with Wood and Rope, then cross it and mine Copper Boulders.
  Make Coal from Wood at the Stone Furnace or mine Coal Boulders.
</GuideQuickAnswer>
</section>


Copper is the first major resource obstacle at New Home. The
Radio Station Bench requires Wire, but collecting Copper alone
will not let you finish it. You need a pickaxe capable of mining
Copper Boulders, a Stone Furnace to process the ore, and a
Workbench to turn the resulting ingots into Wire.

Coal is the material that often sends players on an unnecessary
second mining trip. It can be mined, but the Stone Furnace also
makes **2 Coal from 5 Wood**. Once you have enough Copper, you
can finish the processing chain without searching another cave
just for Coal.

Building all three Wire from scratch requires **9 Copper
and 15 Coal**. Check your inventory first, though: any
Copper Ingots or Wire collected during exploration reduce
the amount you still need to craft.


<h2
  id="copper-location"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Where to Find Copper Near New Home
</h2>

The nearby Copper deposits are in the ravine beyond the
road bridge connecting the Woods to the route toward Horizon.

From New Home, head toward the damaged bridge. You will find
broken wooden stairs and bridge sections that can be repaired,
but you need to restore the crossing before using this route.

### How to Repair the Broken Bridge

Bring **Wood and Rope** to the damaged bridge. Rope can be
crafted from **2 Cloth** after unlocking its recipe in the
Crafting blueprint tree.

Approach the broken wooden structure and equip your Building
Tool. The damaged sections use the Renovating interaction,
rather than the normal building placement menu.

Repair the bridge one section at a time:

1. Face the damaged wooden stairs or bridge section and
   check the materials displayed by the repair interaction.
2. Use the Building Tool to renovate that section once
   you have enough Wood and Rope.
3. Continue along the bridge and repair the remaining
   damaged sections.
4. Cross only after the wooden path is fully connected.

The bridge requires more than one repair. Finishing the
first section may leave another gap that still needs Wood.

If ordinary construction reports a No-Build Zone, do not
try to place new foundations over the damaged bridge.
Use the renovation interaction on the existing structure.

### Where to Mine Copper After Crossing

Continue to the far side of the bridge and enter the
nearby ravine. Follow the rocky slope toward the frozen
creek bed, inspecting the deposits along the exposed
rock faces.

Look for the name **Copper Boulder** when you approach
a deposit. Equip your Flint Pickaxe and mine the Copper.

Coal and other minerals can also appear in this area.
Check each deposit's interaction label rather than
relying on the color of the rock.

This is a useful early collection route because it
allows you to gather Copper and Coal before committing
to a longer journey toward Solar Fields.

### More Copper Toward Solar Fields

The mineral deposits continue beyond the first ravine.

Follow the narrow canyon into the misty chasm that leads toward
Solar Fields. Additional Copper Boulders can be found along
the rock formations and cliff faces.

Farther ahead, **Landscar Cave**, near the Solar Fields approach,
contains another Copper deposit.

This longer route is useful once you have a reliable pickaxe,
enough inventory space, and suitable protection for traveling
farther from New Home.

For the first Radio Station Bench, start with the nearby
ravine. There is little benefit in pushing toward a more
dangerous area if the closer deposits already provide the
Copper required for your current recipe.

### How Much Copper Should You Gather?

Each Copper Ingot requires **3 Copper**, and each Wire costs
one ingot. Making all three Wire for the New Home radio
therefore requires **9 Copper**.

Check for any Wire or Copper Ingots you already collected
before mining the full amount. You may need less than
nine raw Copper to finish the radio.

Gather more when your tool condition and carrying capacity
allow it, particularly if you expect to build the Tailoring
Bench afterward. That station requires Copper Ingots directly,
so it is useful to have some left after finishing the radio.

Do not automatically convert your entire ingot supply into
Wire. Keep the material in its ingot form until you know which
recipe you are preparing next.


<h2
  id="flint-location"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Where to Find Flint in Permafrost
</h2>

**Bone Cave is the first reliable Flint location.** It lies
on the route between Horizon and the Woods where you establish
New Home.

You can mine Flint using the starting Stone Pickaxe, making
it the first material needed for upgrading your mining
equipment.

From New Home, return toward the Bone Cave exit and search
the rocky passage. Flint Boulders appear along the route,
including the lower cavern near the bear encounter and
the passage toward the Woods exit.

Flint can also be found on slopes around the Woods and
in the rocky area beyond the repaired bridge. Once you have
a Flint Pickaxe, there is no need to return to Bone Cave
for every small amount you need.

### Make the Flint Pickaxe First

The Stone Pickaxe can mine Stone and Flint, but it cannot
break Copper or Coal Boulders.

Crafting a Flint Pickaxe removes that restriction.

The early tool recipes are:

- **Stone Pickaxe:** 2 Stone + 4 Wood, crafted from inventory.
- **Workbench:** 10 Wood, built from the Utilities menu.
- **Flint Pickaxe:** 2 Flint + 4 Wood, crafted at the Workbench.

The Workbench also offers a Flint Axe and Flint Shiv, which
are useful for the New Home construction objectives. For
mineral gathering, however, the Flint Pickaxe is the essential
upgrade.

Craft it, equip it, and return to the Copper deposits.

A newly crafted pickaxe left in your backpack will not change
which boulders your currently equipped tool can break.


<h2
  id="pickaxe-upgrades"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Which Pickaxe Can Mine Copper, Coal, and Iron?
</h2>

Every pickaxe has a list of materials it can mine. Open
the tool's description and look for its **Mines** entry
before attempting a higher-grade deposit.

The two important early tiers are:

- **Stone Pickaxe:** Stone and Flint.
- **Flint Pickaxe:** Stone, Flint, Coal, Copper, and Sulfur.

The difference becomes obvious in the opening caves.
You can discover Coal and Iron Boulders before you
have equipment capable of extracting their contents.

A red crossed-out mining indicator means the equipped
tool cannot break the targeted deposit. Swinging again
will not overcome that restriction.

**Iron, Gold, and Moonrock are beyond the Flint Pickaxe's
early mining capabilities.** Leave those deposits alone
until you obtain equipment whose description explicitly
lists the required material.

You may find a Refined Flint Pickaxe while looting a
Military Crate around the Bunker Checkpoint area. It is
an optional equipment discovery, not something you
need to collect before mining the first Copper deposits.

That area can be dangerously cold. Crafting an ordinary
Flint Pickaxe at New Home is a more direct way to begin
collecting Copper and Coal.

### Does Improving Pickaxes Skill Unlock More Ores?

Tool tier and skill progression are different.

The Pickaxes skill develops through mining, but the
deposits available to you still depend on the equipped
tool's mining capabilities.

A high amount of mining experience does not make a
Stone Pickaxe equivalent to a Flint Pickaxe.

For the early Copper requirement, obtaining and
equipping the correct tool is the important step.


<h2
  id="coal-location"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  How to Get Coal: Mine It or Make It From Wood?
</h2>

Coal has two practical early-game sources:

- Mine Coal Boulders with a Flint Pickaxe.
- Produce Coal from Wood at a Stone Furnace.

Which method is better depends on what you already have.

### Make Coal From Wood at the Stone Furnace

The Stone Furnace can produce:

**5 Wood → 2 Coal**

This is the simplest option when you already have Copper
but not enough Coal to smelt it.

You can collect Wood around New Home using an axe, return
to the furnace, and make Coal without traveling to a
separate mineral deposit.

One Copper Ingot consumes **5 Coal**, so producing three
batches of Coal gives you six pieces. Five go into the
ingot recipe and one remains.

For the three Copper Ingots required by the New Home radio,
you need **15 Coal**. If none is available through mining
or looting, eight Coal batches use **40 Wood** and produce
16 Coal, leaving one spare.

That is a noticeable amount of Wood, but it may still be
preferable to a dangerous trip when your shelter already
has an operating furnace.

Keep Wood for the other construction requirements as well.
Converting your entire supply into Coal can leave you
unable to finish a building or keep your Campfire running.

### Where to Mine Coal Boulders

Coal deposits appear before you can mine them.

The cave passage on the opening route toward Horizon
contains Coal Boulders, as does Bone Cave. You can return
to these areas after crafting a Flint Pickaxe.

Additional Coal locations include:

- The cliff path around **Liam's favorite spot** above Horizon.
- Rocky passages inside Bone Cave.
- The ravine beyond the repaired road bridge near New Home.
- The canyon and chasm extending toward Solar Fields.

Mining is a good choice when you are already collecting
Copper nearby. You can bring back both resources on
one trip and preserve Wood for construction and fuel.

For a quick decision:

**Already carrying Copper and standing near a Stone Furnace?**
Make the missing Coal from Wood.

**Heading into the Copper ravine with a Flint Pickaxe?**
Mine the Coal Boulders you encounter along the way.

There is no reason to treat Coal mining as a mandatory
step when the furnace can produce the amount you need.


<h2
  id="stone-furnace"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  How to Build the Stone Furnace
</h2>

The Stone Furnace processes raw metal and produces Coal
from Wood. You will need it before you can reliably make
Copper Ingots for the Radio Station Bench.

Its construction requirement is **40 Stone**.

The blueprint is found in the **Building** tree and costs
**2 Blueprint Points** to unlock. After learning it, open
the Building menu and find Stone Furnace under Utilities.

Stone is available as loose Rock Shards and from ordinary
Rock Boulders. The starting Stone Pickaxe can break those
boulders, so you do not need a Flint upgrade just to
gather the furnace's building material.

The small wooden shelter at New Home can become crowded
once you add a bed, chest, and Workbench. The furnace
can be placed outside beside the hut, provided the
ground is suitable for construction.

Position it close enough to your Workbench and storage
that you can transfer materials without making repeated
trips across the base.

Once it is built, you can process Copper and produce
the Coal needed for smelting.


<h2
  id="copper-ingot"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  How to Make Copper Ingots
</h2>

Open the Stone Furnace and select **Copper Ingot**.

The recipe is:

**3 Copper + 5 Coal → 1 Copper Ingot**

Use the furnace's production controls to select the
quantity you want, start crafting, and wait for the
processing job to finish.

### Where Do Finished Copper Ingots Go?

Finished furnace products can appear **on the ground
beside the Stone Furnace** rather than automatically
entering your inventory.

After processing finishes, inspect the ground near
the station and collect the ingots.

This is an easy step to miss. The furnace can finish
working while your backpack still shows no Copper
Ingots, leaving the next recipe unavailable.

Once collected, the ingots are ready for use in
equipment recipes or Wire production.

### Can You Find Copper Ingots Without Smelting?

A loose **Copper Ingot** can be collected at **Nomad Camp**,
inside a Cold Zone near New Home.

Picking up the ingot can satisfy the early Smelt Copper
Ingot objective, even though you did not produce it
yourself. It also gives you enough metal for one Wire
at the Workbench.

The limitation is the temperature. Nomad Camp is much
colder than the area around your first shelter, and
spending too long searching it can leave you without
enough warmth for the return trip.

Take the ingot and leave promptly. For repeated Copper
production, the nearby mineral deposits and Stone Furnace
provide a more dependable supply.

Bring suitable protection before entering the Cold Zone.
See <Link href="/permafrost/staying-warm">how to stay warm in Permafrost</Link> for heaters,
clothing, and recovering Body Warmth.


<h2
  id="copper-ingots-and-wire"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  How to Craft Wire From Copper Ingots
</h2>

Wire is produced at the Workbench, not at the
Stone Furnace.

The recipe requires **1 Copper Ingot for 1 Wire**.

Once your ingots have finished processing,
collect them and return to the Workbench.
Select Wire and craft the quantity needed
for your next construction objective.

The New Home Radio Station Bench requires:

- **3 Wire**
- **10 Wood**

Making three Wire requires three Copper Ingots,
which consume a total of **9 Copper and 15 Coal**.

There is no need to produce extra Wire just because
you have additional Copper Ingots available.

The Tailoring Bench later requires **3 Copper Ingots
and 15 Wood**. Keeping three ingots unprocessed into
Wire saves another smelting job when that equipment
becomes available.

For that reason, finish the radio's three Wire
first and keep any remaining Copper Ingots in
their original form until the next recipe
requires them.

### Why Does the Workbench Say You Have No Copper Ingots?

Check that the finished ingots are actually
in your character's inventory.

There are three places worth checking:

- Beside the Stone Furnace, where completed
  products may have appeared.
- Inside your storage chest at New Home.
- In your dog's inventory, if you used
  your companion to carry materials.

Move the ingots into your backpack and reopen
the Workbench recipe.

This also applies to some construction tasks.
Having the right materials somewhere in your
base does not always mean the active crafting
interface can use them.

Once the Wire is ready, return to the Radio
Station Bench objective. The
<Link href="/permafrost/main-quests-walkthrough">Permafrost main quest walkthrough</Link>
covers the radio interaction and the journey
toward Hunter's Glade.


<h2
  id="mining-problems"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Why Can't You Mine or Process the Materials You Found?
</h2>

Most early mining problems can be traced to
one of four conditions: the wrong tool,
missing ingredients, incomplete processing,
or a damaged piece of equipment.

### Copper Ingots Finished, but Wire Is Still Unavailable

Collect the furnace's finished products
from beside the station, move them
into your backpack, and open the Wire
recipe at the Workbench.

Raw Copper will not satisfy a recipe
that requires a Copper Ingot.

Likewise, having an ingot in storage
or the dog's inventory may leave
the active recipe showing missing
materials.

### Your Pickaxe Breaks Before You Finish Mining

Mining damages your tools over time,
so repeated trips are easier with
a Repair Bench available at New Home.

The early Repair Bench requires
**10 Flint and 10 Wood**.

Repair important tools before leaving,
or bring a usable replacement when
planning to gather a large amount
of Copper.

A mining trip becomes considerably
less efficient when the only pickaxe
capable of breaking the deposit
fails halfway through the route.

### You Keep Running Out of Inventory Space

Mining gives you raw materials, while
the furnace and Workbench turn those
materials into more useful components.

Move ordinary Stone, surplus Flint,
and other materials you do not need
immediately into storage before
leaving for the ravine.

Your dog can carry additional supplies,
but keep the materials needed for
the next active craft accessible
in your own backpack.

For a Radio Station Bench, collecting
the required Copper, processing
the ingots, and making three Wire
is enough. Extra minerals can wait
until the next construction task
reveals what you need.
