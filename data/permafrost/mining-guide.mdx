<section id="quick-answer" className="scroll-mt-24">
  <GuideQuickAnswer title="Where Do You Find Copper, Coal, and Flint in Permafrost?">
    Mine **Flint in Bone Cave** and craft a Flint Pickaxe
    at your Workbench. Look for Copper and Coal Boulders
    along the rocky routes near New Home. Make any missing
    Coal from Wood, then process Copper into Ingots and
    craft Wire for the Radio Station Bench.
  </GuideQuickAnswer>
</section>


Copper is the first mineral that forces you to improve
your equipment in Permafrost. The Stone Pickaxe you
make near the beginning can collect Flint, but it
cannot break the Copper and Coal Boulders you
encounter while exploring.

The next obstacle comes after mining. Copper must
be processed into Ingots before it can become Wire,
and the New Home radio needs three completed Wire.
That requires a working furnace and enough Coal,
which you can either mine or produce from Wood.

Later, the same progression continues with Iron,
Quality Metal, and Moonrock. The important difference
is that a Copper Pickaxe can handle Iron but cannot
harvest Moonrock. Higher-tier processing equipment
also becomes necessary when the story starts asking
for advanced armor materials.


<h2
  id="flint-location"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Where to Find Flint and Make Your First Flint Pickaxe
</h2>

**Bone Cave** is an early, accessible source of
Flint. You pass through it between Horizon and
the Woods, where New Home begins.

The starting Stone Pickaxe can mine Flint Boulders,
so there is no need to obtain another tool before
collecting your first pieces.

From New Home, return toward the Bone Cave exit
and enter the rocky passage. Search the lower
cavern and the route toward the Woods opening.
Flint Boulders can be found in those areas,
including near the section where you previously
encountered the Bear.

Flint also appears on rocky slopes around the
Woods. Once you know those nearby deposits,
returning to Bone Cave is not necessary for
every small amount.

### Craft the Flint Pickaxe

The first mining equipment recipes are:

- **Stone Pickaxe:** 2 Stone + 4 Wood
- **Workbench:** 10 Wood
- **Flint Pickaxe:** 2 Flint + 4 Wood

Craft the Stone Pickaxe from your inventory
if you do not already have one.

After collecting Flint, build the Workbench
and use it to craft a Flint Pickaxe.

Equip the new pickaxe before returning to
the mineral deposits. Having it in your
backpack does not change the mining
capabilities of your currently equipped tool.

The Flint Axe and Flint Shiv are also
required during New Home's equipment
objectives, but the Pickaxe is the tool
that removes the early Copper bottleneck.


<h2
  id="copper-location"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Where to Find Copper Near New Home
</h2>

The closest practical Copper search area
is the rocky ravine near the road
between the Woods and Horizon.

From New Home, head toward the damaged
road bridge and the surrounding rocky terrain.
Follow the slopes and exposed rock faces
toward the frozen creek bed.

Look for the interaction label
**Copper Boulder**.

Copper deposits can resemble other
mineral formations from a distance,
so check the name displayed when
you approach a rock before spending
time trying to mine it.

A Flint Pickaxe can harvest these deposits.

Coal and other minerals also appear
around the rocky passages, allowing
you to collect multiple materials
on the same trip.

### Do You Have to Repair the Broken Bridge?

**No. Repairing the bridge is not a required
step for unlocking Copper mining.**

The bridge provides a more convenient
crossing between the Woods and the
route toward Horizon. It becomes
especially useful when you are making
repeated trips between settlements.

You can repair it with Wood and Rope,
using the Building Tool's **Renovating**
interaction on the damaged structures.

Rope can be crafted from **2 Cloth**
after unlocking its recipe.

To restore the crossing:

1. Approach a damaged wooden section
   with the Building Tool.
2. Inspect its Wood and Rope requirements.
3. Renovate the damaged stairs or
   bridge section.
4. Continue repairing the remaining
   gaps until the path is connected.

The crossing involves more than
one repair, so finishing the first
section may still leave another gap.

A normal building placement attempt
may report **No-Build Zone**. That
does not mean the bridge cannot be
repaired. Use Renovating on the
existing damaged pieces instead
of placing new foundations.

Save the bridge repairs for when
the shortcut is worth the Wood
and Rope. Getting a Flint Pickaxe
and harvesting accessible Copper
should take priority when your
immediate objective is making Wire.

### Additional Copper Toward Solar Fields

Copper deposits continue farther
through the canyons and rocky
approaches toward Solar Fields.

The narrow passages, cliff faces,
and the area around **Landscar Cave**
offer additional mineral deposits.

These are useful when you need
more Copper for later equipment
or construction.

They are not the first places
you need to visit for the New
Home radio.

The longer journey uses more
supplies, exposes you to hostile
wildlife, and can take you into
colder terrain. Collect enough
Copper from the nearer deposits
before committing to that route.


<h2
  id="pickaxe-upgrades"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Which Pickaxe Can Mine Copper, Coal, Iron, and Moonrock?
</h2>

Mining capability depends on the
pickaxe you have equipped.

Open the tool description and
inspect its **Mines** entry.
That list tells you which
materials the tool can harvest.

The important progression is:

- **Stone Pickaxe:** Stone and Flint.
- **Flint Pickaxe:** Stone, Flint,
  Coal, Copper, and Sulfur.
- **Scrap and Copper Pickaxes:** Higher-tier
  tools capable of collecting Iron.
- **Quality Pickaxe:** Can harvest
  Moonrock deposits that resist
  lower-tier tools.

The Copper Pickaxe is not enough
for Moonrock. A Quality Pickaxe
has successfully harvested it.

A red crossed-out mining indicator means
the current tool cannot break the rock.
Repeated strikes do not bypass the
restriction.

Switch to a suitable pickaxe or return
after obtaining a higher-tier tool.

### Do Pickaxes Skill Levels Unlock More Ores?

Skill progression and tool capability
are separate.

Mining develops the Pickaxes skill,
but a Stone Pickaxe does not gain
the ability to mine Copper simply
because your skill level increases.

The same applies to Moonrock:
improving your skill cannot replace
the required tool tier.

### Is the Refined Flint Pickaxe Worth Getting?

A Refined Flint Pickaxe can appear
among the loot around Bunker
Checkpoint, including military
equipment containers.

It is an optional upgrade,
not a prerequisite for obtaining
your first Copper.

The checkpoint has dangerously
cold sections. There is little
reason to make a difficult detour
for an optional pickaxe when
you can craft a standard Flint
Pickaxe at New Home.

Later, a Scrap or Copper Pickaxe
becomes more relevant once you
start collecting Iron.


<h2
  id="coal-location"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  How to Get Coal: Mine Coal Boulders or Use Wood?
</h2>

There are two ways to obtain Coal:

- Mine Coal Boulders using a Flint
  Pickaxe or better equipment.
- Produce Coal from Wood at
  a Stone Furnace.

The best choice depends on
what resources you already have.

### Make Coal From Wood

The Stone Furnace can produce:

**5 Wood → 2 Coal**

This is particularly useful after
returning from a Copper trip
with plenty of ore but not enough
Coal to process it.

Wood is available around New Home,
so you can produce the missing
Coal without traveling back
to a mineral deposit.

One Copper Ingot requires
**5 Coal**.

Making Coal only from Wood
therefore has a noticeable
resource cost.

For example, producing
**15 Coal** from an empty
supply requires eight batches:

- 8 batches consume **40 Wood**.
- Those batches produce **16 Coal**.
- 15 Coal are used to make
  three Copper Ingots.
- 1 Coal remains.

This is enough Coal for the
three Copper Ingots used by
the New Home radio.

However, 40 Wood is also
a substantial amount of
building and heating fuel.

Use your existing Coal before
converting another large
Wood supply.

### Where to Mine Coal Boulders

Coal deposits are present
along several early routes:

- The opening cave toward Horizon
- Rocky passages in Bone Cave
- The cliff path near
  **Liam's favorite spot**
  above Horizon
- The ravine near the damaged
  bridge outside New Home
- Later canyon routes leading
  toward Solar Fields

Some of these deposits are
visible before you own
a pickaxe capable of
mining them.

After crafting a Flint Pickaxe,
you can return and harvest
the Coal.

Mining is the better choice
when Coal appears near
Copper you are already
collecting.

Producing it from Wood is
more convenient when the
Copper is already at home
and a dangerous second
journey would only be for fuel.


<h2
  id="stone-furnace"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  How to Unlock and Build the Stone Furnace
</h2>

The **Stone Furnace** is required
for processing early metal
materials and producing Coal
from Wood.

Unlock its recipe in the
Building blueprint tree.

The blueprint costs
**2 Blueprint Points**.

Once learned, open Utilities
in the Building menu
and select Stone Furnace.

Construction requires:

**40 Stone**

  <Image
    src="/images/permafrost/permafrost-stone-furnace-requirements.webp"
    alt="Permafrost Stone Furnace construction entry showing a requirement of 40 Stone"
    width={300}
    height={100}
    className="rounded-xl"
  />

  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">
    The Stone Furnace requires 40 Stone
    after its blueprint has been unlocked.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

Stone can be collected from loose
Rock Shards and ordinary Rock Boulders.

Your starting Stone Pickaxe can
mine those boulders, so gathering
materials for the furnace does
not require a Flint upgrade.

### Where Should You Place the Furnace?

New Home's first shelter can
become crowded after adding
a Bed, storage, and Workbench.

The furnace can be placed
outside beside your shelter
when the terrain allows
construction.

Keep it reasonably close
to your storage and
Workbench.

This reduces the amount
of time spent transferring
Copper, Coal, and finished
Ingots between stations.

Leave enough open space
around the furnace to
collect finished materials.
Some completed products
appear beside the station
rather than entering your
inventory automatically.


<h2
  id="copper-ingot"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  How to Make Copper Ingots
</h2>

Each Copper Ingot requires:

**3 Copper + 5 Coal → 1 Copper Ingot**

Use the Stone Furnace to
process your collected Copper.

Select the Copper Ingot
recipe, choose the required
quantity, and start the
production job.

<figure className="my-8">
  <Image
    src="/images/permafrost/permafrost-copper-ingot-smelting.webp"
    alt="Permafrost crafting interface displaying the Copper Ingot recipe with Copper and Coal material requirements"
    width={300}
    height={100}
    className="rounded-xl"
  />

  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">
    Each Copper Ingot uses 3 Copper
    and 5 Coal.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

Once the job finishes, collect
the processed ingots before
moving to the next recipe.

### Where Did Your Finished Copper Ingots Go?

Finished furnace products
may appear **on the ground
beside the furnace**.

They do not always move
directly into your backpack.

Look around the station
after processing finishes
and pick up the completed
Copper Ingots.

This can explain a situation
where the furnace has finished
working but your next recipe
still reports missing metal.

If the ingots were moved to
a storage chest or your
dog's pack, retrieve them
before crafting.

### Can You Find Copper Ingots Without Smelting?

A loose Copper Ingot can
be found at **Nomad Camp**
inside a Cold Zone near
New Home.

This provides an early
alternative to processing
every ingot yourself.

Collecting an ingot may
also satisfy the relevant
inventory requirement
during early progression.

The disadvantage is the
cold exposure.

Nomad Camp is considerably
colder than the area
around your shelter, so
a quick pickup is more
sensible than prolonged
exploration without
appropriate equipment.

Bring portable heating
protection and recover
Body Warmth before
entering.

For the actual warmth
and equipment mechanics,
see <Link href="/permafrost/staying-warm">how to stay warm in Permafrost</Link>.


<h2
  id="copper-ingots-and-wire"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  How to Craft Wire and Finish the New Home Radio Materials
</h2>

Wire is crafted at the
**Workbench** using
Copper Ingots.

The recipe is:

**1 Copper Ingot → 1 Wire**

The New Home Radio Station
Bench needs:

- **3 Wire**
- **10 Wood**

Starting without any
Copper Ingots or Wire,
the total metal-processing
requirement is:

- **9 Copper**
- **15 Coal**
- **3 Copper Ingots**
- **3 Wire**

First produce the ingots,
collect them from the
furnace, and carry them
to the Workbench.

Craft three Wire and
keep the finished items
in your backpack before
attempting to build
the radio.

### Should You Turn Every Copper Ingot Into Wire?

No. Later construction
also requires Copper
Ingots directly.

The Tailoring Bench,
introduced during
The Way Home, requires:

**3 Copper Ingots + 15 Wood**

If you want enough
Copper for both the
New Home radio and
the later Tailoring
Bench, prepare a total
of six Copper Ingots.

Starting from raw
materials, that means:

- **18 Copper**
- **30 Coal**
- **6 Copper Ingots**

Convert three Ingots
into Wire for the radio.
Keep the other three
as Ingots for the
Tailoring Bench.

There is no advantage
in processing all six
into Wire when the
later recipe specifically
requires Ingots.

### Why Does the Workbench Show Missing Copper Ingots?

Check where the finished
ingots are stored.

They may be:

- On the ground beside
  the Stone Furnace
- Inside your storage
  chest at New Home
- In your dog's inventory

Move them into your
character's backpack
and reopen the Wire
recipe.

Raw Copper cannot
replace a Copper Ingot,
and finished Wire
cannot replace an
Ingot in a recipe
that specifically
requires processed metal.

Once the three Wire
are ready, build the
Radio Station Bench
and continue the
transmission objective.

The <Link href="/permafrost/main-quests-walkthrough">Permafrost main quest walkthrough</Link>
covers the radio interaction
and the journey toward
Hunter's Glade.


<h2
  id="iron-mining"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  How to Mine Iron and Upgrade Beyond Flint Tools
</h2>

Iron becomes important
after the early New Home
construction sequence.

Later equipment,
workstations, and
weapon-related crafting
require more advanced
metal supplies than
Copper alone.

Iron deposits can
be encountered along
rocky passages and
caves in the larger
Solar Fields area.

You may discover Iron
before your equipment
is ready to harvest it.

### Which Pickaxe Mines Iron?

A **Scrap Pickaxe or
Copper Pickaxe** can
harvest Iron.

Check the **Mines**
list on your equipped
tool to confirm that
Iron is supported.

The Flint Pickaxe you
used for Copper is
not the final mining
upgrade.

After gaining access
to Scrap tools at
House Camp, examine
the higher-tier options
before returning to
Iron deposits.

A Copper Pickaxe
also lists Iron among
its mineable materials.

### Do You Need a Copper Pickaxe for Moonrock?

No. A Copper Pickaxe
still cannot harvest
Moonrock.

This limitation becomes
noticeable when you reach
later areas containing
Moonrock deposits.

A **Quality Pickaxe**
is a higher-tier
option capable of
mining them.

Do not spend materials
crafting another
Copper Pickaxe solely
because you have found
Moonrock.

### Making Iron Ingots

Iron is used in
more advanced crafting
and construction.

The Iron Ingot recipe
uses:

**3 Iron + 3 Coal → 1 Iron Ingot**

Process Iron before
attempting structures
that require Iron Ingots
rather than raw Iron.

For example, the
Weapons Bench introduced
during Parking Zoo
requires **10 Iron Ingots**.

The tool and material
preparation for that
quest can therefore
take longer than
collecting its other
supplies.

Keep enough Coal
available for Iron
processing instead
of using every piece
on Copper.

The <Link href="/permafrost/main-quests-walkthrough">main quest walkthrough</Link>
covers the actual
Parking Zoo objectives
and the required
weapon and ammunition
crafting sequence.


<h2
  id="moonrock-smelting"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Where to Find Moonrock and Make Quality Metal
</h2>

Moonrock becomes relevant
during later exploration,
especially when you begin
preparing the advanced
equipment needed for
White Hell.

Finding a glowing or
unusual deposit is only
the first step.

You need a pickaxe
capable of harvesting
Moonrock and the
appropriate processing
station before you can
turn the material into
usable Ingots.

### Where to Find Moonrock Deposits

Moonrock deposits have
been encountered near:

- Bunker areas reached
  during later exploration
- Meteor impact sites
- Rocky ground near a
  previously visited
  tower and recording
  locations

These are useful areas
to inspect once you have
a suitable pickaxe.

Look around exposed rock
formations and lower
sections of the terrain.
Not every deposit is
immediately visible from
the main path.

Some areas also contain
Copper or ordinary Rock
Boulders, so confirm
that the deposit is
actually Moonrock before
assuming the trip has
supplied the required ore.

### Use a Quality Pickaxe

A Quality Pickaxe has
successfully harvested
Moonrock.

One has also been
obtained as exploration
loot in the later
Solar Facility area.

That makes checking
valuable containers
worthwhile before
spending resources
on another tool.

However, the exact
loot location should
not be treated as a
guaranteed source for
every visit.

Once you have a
Quality Pickaxe, equip
it and confirm that
the Moonrock deposit
can be harvested.

Bring enough inventory
space to collect the
ore. Mining the correct
deposit is not useful
when your character
cannot carry the
materials home.

### Why Can't You Craft Moonrock Ingots Yet?

Raw Moonrock is not
the same item as a
Moonrock Ingot.

The basic Stone Furnace
does not provide the
later processing options
required for all
advanced materials.

You need access to the
**Professional Smelter**.

The Professional Smelter
recipe becomes available
during the later
Jeremiah-related
progression at Crow's Nest.

Once the recipe is
learned, return to a
suitable building area
and construct the
station.

The Professional Smelter
can process Moonrock
into Moonrock Ingots
and make Quality Metal.

Check your available
Coal before starting
a large processing
batch. Advanced
production can consume
a substantial fuel
supply.

### How to Make Quality Metal

Quality Metal is
produced using Iron
and Scrap at the
Professional Smelter.

Plan for **2 Iron
per Quality Metal**,
along with the other
materials shown by
the selected recipe.

For example, producing
16 Quality Metal
requires **32 Iron**,
before accounting for
the additional Scrap
and other inputs.

This becomes important
when you are planning
several advanced tools
and armor pieces in
the same session.

Crafting a complete
outfit and upgrading
every tool at once
can consume a large
amount of Iron.

Finish the equipment
needed for your next
objective first,
then use remaining
materials on optional
upgrades.

### Moonrock or Quality Tools: Which Should Come First?

Quality tools provide
an intermediate upgrade,
but Moonrock equipment
becomes available later
and can offer stronger
performance.

The resource decision
depends on your current
progress.

A working Quality
Pickaxe is valuable
because it lets you
harvest Moonrock.

Replacing every other
tool with Quality
equipment is less
important when you
are close to producing
Moonrock Ingots.

Keep the necessary
Iron, Scrap, Coal,
and Moonrock materials
for the equipment
your current quest
requires.

The advanced armor
crafting objectives
are covered in the
<Link href="/permafrost/main-quests-walkthrough">main quest walkthrough</Link>.


<h2
  id="mining-problems"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Why Can't You Mine, Smelt, or Use the Materials You Collected?
</h2>

Several failures can
look similar even
though they need
different fixes.

### The Boulder Won't Break

Look at the deposit
name and the equipped
pickaxe's **Mines**
entry.

A red mining restriction
means the tool cannot
harvest that material.

Use a higher-tier
pickaxe rather than
continuing to strike
the same rock.

For Copper, Coal,
and Sulfur, the Flint
Pickaxe is the important
early upgrade.

Iron requires a
more capable tool.
Moonrock requires
a higher tier again.

### The Furnace Finished but the Material Is Missing

Inspect the ground
beside the furnace
or smelter.

Finished products
may be placed outside
the station rather
than directly in
your inventory.

Collect them before
opening the next
crafting recipe.

### You Have Copper but Cannot Make Wire

Wire requires
**Copper Ingots**,
not raw Copper.

Process the ore,
collect the finished
Ingots, and use the
Workbench to produce
Wire.

Also check that the
Ingots are in your
character's inventory
rather than storage
or your dog's pack.

### The Pickaxe Breaks During a Mining Trip

Mining consumes tool
durability.

A Repair Bench helps
keep your equipment
usable between journeys.

The early Repair Bench
requires:

- **10 Flint**
- **10 Wood**

Repair the pickaxe
before leaving for
a long collection
route.

A replacement tool
is worth carrying
when you expect
several deposits
and cannot easily
return to New Home.

### You Have Moonrock but No Ingot Recipe

Check whether you
have unlocked the
Professional Smelter.

Finding raw Moonrock
does not automatically
make all advanced
processing recipes
available at a
Stone Furnace.

Once the appropriate
station is constructed,
prepare the additional
fuel and materials
required by the
selected recipe.
