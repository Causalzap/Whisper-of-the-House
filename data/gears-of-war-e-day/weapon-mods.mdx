You do not need every weapon mod in Gears of War: E-Day to make the Campaign
easier.

The upgrades worth going out of your way for are the ones that noticeably change
how a weapon behaves: giving the Lancer a clear hip-fire point, turning a Gut
Puncher Perfect Reload into a much larger explosion, making Torque Bow blind fire
usable from cover, or removing a reload problem from the Incinerator.

Most of the strongest early choices appear in **Act 2, Chapter 2: Hold the Line**.
Act 4 then adds another group of upgrades for the Hammerburst, Torque Bow,
Incinerator, Gut Puncher, and Ripper.

There is no currency cost to weigh up. The real cost is **travel time plus the
combat required by the Secondary Objective**, so the best choice depends on which
weapons you actually intend to keep.


<h2
  id="act-2-best-mods"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Which Weapon Mods Should You Get in Act 2?
</h2>

Act 2, Chapter 2 is where optional markers start having very different values.

Four Secondary Objectives are especially useful for weapon upgrades:

- **Lost in Translation** — Lancer Laser
- **Vanguard** — Hammerburst Bayonet
- **Trapped Family** — Gnasher Bayonet + Breechshot Precision Long Barrel
- **Black Raven Down** — Gut Puncher Overcharged Ammo

There is no required order. Check where you already are in the district and clear
the worthwhile objective when it fits your current route instead of repeatedly
crossing the map for one attachment at a time.

If your problem is the main Hold the Line route rather than the optional rewards,
use the <Link href="/gears-of-war-e-day/act-2-walkthrough">Act 2 walkthrough</Link> for the Campaign progression.


<h2
  id="lancer-laser"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Where Do You Get the Lancer Laser Mod?
</h2>

The **Lancer Laser** comes from **Lost in Translation** in Act 2, Chapter 2.

Go to the Secondary Objective marker and meet the civilians. This is an escort
rather than a search puzzle: get them from the starting location to the second
objective point and keep the group alive until the escort finishes.

Completing it gives you:

- **Lancer Laser**
- **Melee Stunner armor mod**

The Laser adds a clear point of aim during hip fire. That becomes useful as soon
as something fast reaches your cover, because you can keep the Lancer centered on
the target while moving instead of stopping to aim down sights.

It also makes **blind fire over cover** much easier to place.

Those are two situations that happen throughout the Campaign, which is why I rate
this above minor capacity upgrades early on. If the Prototype Lancer is staying
in your loadout, Lost in Translation is one of the first optional detours worth
making.


<h2
  id="hammerburst-bayonet"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Where Is the Hammerburst Bayonet?
</h2>

The **Hammerburst Bayonet** comes from **Vanguard**, also in Act 2, Chapter 2.

Look for the **car showroom** and start the Secondary Objective there. The combat
stays around the showroom and surrounding car park, so once you find the landmark
there is no long route to solve.

Finishing Vanguard rewards:

- **Hammerburst Bayonet**
- **Lancer Cover Brace**

The Bayonet makes the most sense when the Hammerburst is already part of your
regular loadout. A weapon you normally prefer at range now has a useful response
when something breaks through your firing lane and reaches close range.

It becomes even more attractive with an aggressive playstyle where Smoke or
cover lets you close distance intentionally.

I would not cross the whole district solely for this reward if the Hammerburst
has already been dropped for another weapon. The mod is good because it extends
a gun you are using, not because every Campaign run needs it.


<h2
  id="trapped-family"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Are the Gnasher Bayonet and Breechshot Long Barrel Worth Getting?
</h2>

**Trapped Family** is close to the forward operating area where this part of the
mission begins.

The objective is mostly a defensive hold. Once it starts, prepare for repeated
**Wretch waves** and keep the area under control until the attack ends.

You get two weapon rewards:

- **Gnasher Bayonet**
- **Breechshot Precision Long Barrel**

The two mods suit opposite ends of a loadout.

The **Gnasher Bayonet** fits a close-range player who is already carrying a
shotgun and regularly ends fights near the enemy. It adds another useful option
without asking you to change the way the weapon is being used.

The **Precision Long Barrel** supports accurate ranged shooting instead.

That split makes Trapped Family easier to justify than an objective that rewards
two upgrades for weapons you may never equip. There is a good chance at least one
of these two fits the loadout you already have.


<h2
  id="black-raven-down"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Where Do You Get Gut Puncher Overcharged Ammo?
</h2>

The **Gut Puncher Overcharged Ammo** upgrade comes from **Black Raven Down**.

Use the **crashed Raven** as your landmark. Near the crash is a burger restaurant;
go inside and interact with the person on the floor to start the Secondary
Objective.

    width={1600}
    height={900}
    className="rounded-xl"
  />

  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">
    Use the crashed Raven as the landmark, then check the nearby restaurant to
    start Black Raven Down.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

The objective turns into a defensive fight that includes a **Bilefrog**. Finish
the defense to receive:

- **Proximity Reload armor mod**
- **Gut Puncher Overcharged Ammo**

These two rewards work especially well together rather than acting as unrelated
pickups.

A close-range kill can trigger **Proximity Reload**, giving you a Perfect Reload.
The Gut Puncher's Overcharged Ammo then makes that Perfect Reload shot produce a
much larger explosion.

The useful combat loop becomes:

1. Get the close-range kill.
2. Trigger the Perfect Reload.
3. Spend the enhanced Gut Puncher round on the next clustered group or tougher
   target.

The upgraded blast is noticeably larger than the normal shot, which is why Black
Raven Down is one of the Act 2 detours I would still make when trying to move
through the chapter efficiently.


<h2
  id="act-4-mods"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Which Weapon Mods Are Worth Getting in Act 4?
</h2>

Act 4 shifts the useful rewards toward more specialized weapons.

The upgrades worth watching for are:

- **Hammerburst Overpacked**
- **Torque Bow Mobile Targeting Laser**
- **Incinerator Spring-Loaded Mag + extra mod slot**
- **Gut Puncher Acid Rounds**
- **Torque Bow Primed Coils**
- **Gut Puncher and Ripper Incendiary Rounds**

Most of these are concentrated around **Hope and Ruin** and **Tough as Nails**.

If you are trying to solve the actual route through the ruined city, power
system, communications path, or refinery rather than chasing optional upgrades,
use the <Link href="/gears-of-war-e-day/act-4-walkthrough">Act 4 walkthrough</Link> instead.


<h2
  id="blood-mag-overpacked"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Where Do You Get Blood Mag and Hammerburst Overpacked?
</h2>

One Act 4, Chapter 1 optional encounter leads to a device being used to map the
Locust tunnel system.

Once you reach it, defend the device while waves attack, including **Boomers and
Wretches**.

Completing the defense gives you:

- **Blood Mag**
- **Hammerburst Overpacked**

Blood Mag rewards executions with a full Perfect Magazine, so its value rises
sharply if you already finish weakened enemies at close range. A player who spends
most fights behind long-range cover will get much less from it.

Overpacked is more straightforward: the Hammerburst can keep more ammunition
ready before another reload interrupts the fight.

For a Hammerburst-focused loadout, these Act 4 rewards stack nicely with what you
may already have picked up from Vanguard. If the weapon left your loadout back in
Act 2, the detour becomes much harder to justify.


<h2
  id="torque-bow-laser"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Where Is the Torque Bow Mobile Targeting Laser?
</h2>

The **Mobile Targeting Laser** comes from the **Missing Convoy** Secondary
Objective in **Act 4, Chapter 1: Hope and Ruin**.

Find the survivor calling from a balcony. His convoy has been ambushed, and the
objective becomes a defense against several airborne enemies before the reward
unlocks.

The important change is what happens during blind fire. The normal Torque Bow
gives you very little information about where a blind-fired shot will land; the
Mobile Targeting Laser adds a visible line so you can place the shot without
fully exposing yourself.

Missing Convoy rewards:

- **Torque Bow Mobile Targeting Laser**
- **Braced Capacitor**

This is one of the largest behavior changes among the optional upgrades. Without
the Laser, a blind-fired Torque Bow shot commits valuable ammunition with poor
information. With it, the weapon remains usable from cover.

If the Torque Bow is one of your regular weapons, Missing Convoy belongs near the
top of the Act 4 detour list.


<h2
  id="trapped-locust"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Where Are the Incinerator, Acid Rounds, and Primed Coils Mods?
</h2>

Several upgrades are clustered around **Act 4, Chapter 3: Tough as Nails**, which
makes this one of the most efficient places to collect multiple useful parts
without spreading the detour across different chapters.

Start with **Trapped Locust** near the factory.

Clear the enemies around the objective to receive:

- **Extra Incinerator mod slot**
- **Incinerator Spring-Loaded Mag**

Spring-Loaded Mag fixes one of the Incinerator's practical annoyances. Stow the
weapon for a short period, bring it back out, and the magazine replenishes without
forcing you through the usual reload cycle.

The easy mistake happens after Trapped Locust is already complete: **leaving the
area too early**.

Instead, continue from the objective area:

1. Drop down to the lower path.
2. Follow it away from the completed objective.
3. Enter the nearby **train carriage**.
4. Collect both upgrades inside before returning to the main route.

The carriage contains:

- **Gut Puncher Acid Rounds**
- **Torque Bow Primed Coils**

Acid Rounds add another specialized ammunition option to the Gut Puncher, while
Primed Coils make the Torque Bow's targeting system focus more quickly.

This is one detour I would not skip after already completing Trapped Locust. You
have paid almost all of the travel and combat cost just by reaching the factory,
so walking away before checking the carriage leaves two nearby upgrades behind.


<h2
  id="fire-in-the-hole"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Where Do You Get Incendiary Rounds?
</h2>

Stay in **Act 4, Chapter 3** for **Fire in the Hole**.

The objective puts you above a survivor who needs protection while enemies attack
below. Incendiary grenades in the area help control groups pushing toward his
position.

Completing the defense gives **Incendiary Rounds** to:

- **Gut Puncher**
- **Ripper**

The effect is straightforward: shots from those weapons can ignite targets.

This detour makes much more sense when one of those guns is already part of your
loadout. If neither is being used, I would take the train-carriage pickups or
Torque Bow upgrade first rather than collecting the fire effect simply because
the objective marker is available.


<h2
  id="best-priority"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Which Mods Are Actually Worth the Detour?
</h2>

You can finish the Campaign without collecting every optional attachment, so I
would base the final priority on **how often the upgrade changes an action you
already perform**.

My highest-value choices are:

- **Lancer Laser** — the easiest early recommendation because it improves both hip
  fire and blind fire on a weapon that remains useful throughout the Campaign.
- **Gut Puncher Overcharged Ammo** — makes the Perfect Reload explosion
  substantially more useful against groups and tougher targets.
- **Torque Bow Mobile Targeting Laser** — turns blind fire from guesswork into a
  practical cover option.
- **Acid Rounds + Primed Coils train carriage** — costs very little extra travel
  once you have already finished Trapped Locust.

The **bayonets, Overpacked, Incinerator parts, and Incendiary Rounds** are more
loadout-dependent. They can be excellent upgrades when you are already using the
weapon they support, but there is little reason to spend extra travel time
collecting an attachment for a gun you have already decided not to carry.
