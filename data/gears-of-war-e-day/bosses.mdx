Counting repeat encounters separately, the Campaign has **seven major boss
encounters**:

1. **Brumak**
2. **Corpser**
3. **Weaponized Brumak**
4. **Brumak**
5. **Scoria**
6. **Corpser**
7. **Vraahk**

That is fewer unique boss types than the encounter count suggests because the
Brumak and Corpser both return. The important distinction is the Weaponized
Brumak: the armored Fort Vigil fight does not play like the first Brumak battle.

The fights also punish very different mistakes. The first Brumak lets you deal
damage normally but pressures your position. The Corpser makes the arena part of
the solution. The Weaponized Brumak asks you to dismantle the dangerous parts
instead of pouring fire into armor, while the Scoria encounter becomes difficult
because two large airborne targets are active at once.


<h2
  id="first-brumak"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  How Do You Beat the First Brumak?
</h2>

The first Brumak arrives late in **Act 1, Chapter 4: Back in the Fold**, during
the fighting around City Hall.

There is an important preparation window before it appears. The defenders tell
Bravo to stock up, and **Gut Punchers** are available during the City Hall
defense. Take the ammunition and heavier weaponry while the immediate wave is
clear rather than entering the next fight with half-empty rifles.

  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">
    Use the space around City Hall instead of letting the Brumak pin you against
    one piece of cover.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

Unlike the later armored version, this Brumak does not require you to solve a
specific armor sequence before dealing useful damage. The fight is mostly about
maintaining enough space to keep firing without getting trapped directly in
front of it.

Do not choose one cover position and try to stay there for the entire fight.
Fire while the Brumak is at a manageable distance, then rotate to another lane
when it closes the gap. The open area matters more than squeezing another few
seconds of damage out of a bad position.

The Gut Puncher is worth spending here. There is no benefit in carrying every
heavy round out of the encounter while using only rifle ammunition against one
of the largest targets in Act 1. Use the stronger shots when you have a clean
angle, then reload while repositioning rather than standing still beside an
empty weapon.

Smaller enemies can also steal your attention during the encounter. Clear
anything that reaches your immediate cover before returning fire to the Brumak;
getting surrounded is more dangerous than briefly stopping boss damage.

Once the Brumak drops, the City Hall sequence can continue and Act 1 moves into
the formation of Bravo Squad.


<h2
  id="corpser"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  How Do You Beat the Corpser With the Pillars?
</h2>

The first full Corpser fight happens in **Act 3, Chapter 2: Enemy Lines**, after
Bravo has fallen into the Locust tunnel network and worked through Cooper
Station.

This is the fight where wasting ammunition is easiest.

The Corpser can make it look as though you simply need more damage, but the
opening section is built around the **large support pillars** surrounding the
arena. Positioning the creature correctly advances the fight far more reliably
than firing continuously into its body.

<figure className="my-8">
  <Image
    src="/images/gears-of-war-e-day/gears-e-day-enemy-lines-pillar-boss.webp"
    alt="Gears of War E-Day Corpser lined up with a support pillar during Enemy Lines"
    width={1600}
    height={900}
    className="rounded-xl"
  />

  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">
    Put a standing pillar behind your position, wait for the Corpser to commit,
    then move out of the line before it reaches you.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

Watch the Corpser instead of the reticle. After its ground pressure and movement
settle, it eventually commits to a forward attack. Stand where that attack will
carry it into a pillar, then move before impact.

A successful hit damages the support and puts the arena itself to work.

The sequence is:

**line up a pillar → trigger the charge → move clear → let the Corpser hit the
support → reset at another pillar**

When the Corpser burrows, stop trying to follow it with gunfire. Move away from
the emergence line and wait for it to surface before rebuilding your position.

The later part of the encounter adds smaller Locust around the arena. Deal with
anything that can force you out from behind a pillar before setting up the next
charge. A Ticker reaching you at the exact moment the Corpser commits is much
more likely to ruin the attempt than losing a few seconds of boss damage.

Heavy ammunition is therefore a resource I would **not** burn early. Save it for
the sections where weapon damage is actually useful instead of emptying it into
the first phase and reaching the later fight nearly dry.

If you have not reached the boss yet, the route through Fairlight Outpost,
Cooper Station, and the underground sections is in the
<Link href="/gears-of-war-e-day/act-3-walkthrough"> Act 3 walkthrough</Link>.


<h2
  id="weaponized-brumak"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  How Do You Beat the Weaponized Brumak at Fort Vigil?
</h2>

The Fort Vigil encounter changes the Brumak problem completely.

This one arrives with substantial armor and mounted weapons. When it pushes
through the defenses, Bravo is sent after it while the remaining Gears hold the
rest of the assault.

There is a **Supply Cache immediately before the main engagement**. Use it.

Running past that resupply point with low ammunition creates an unnecessary
problem because this fight asks you to break several durable targets rather than
simply survive a short burst of combat.

Once Bravo reaches the firing position, the squad attacks from **above**.

  <Image
    src="/images/gears-of-war-e-day/gears-e-day-weaponized-brumak-armor.webp"
    alt="Gears of War E-Day Weaponized Brumak armor during the Fort Vigil fight"
    width={1600}
    height={900}
    className="rounded-xl"
  />

  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">
    Strip the Weaponized Brumak's armor from the elevated firing position before
    wasting sustained fire on protected sections.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

Start by attacking the **armor** that blocks the useful targets underneath. If
your rounds are disappearing into intact protection without changing the state of
the fight, move your aim rather than assuming you need a different gun.

As parts open up, switch priorities to the Brumak's mounted weapons. The squad
specifically calls out the **launcher**, and the machine gun is another dangerous
system worth removing when you have the angle.

I prioritize them in this order:

1. Break the armor blocking the current target.
2. Hit the **launcher** as soon as it becomes vulnerable.
3. Remove the exposed mounted gun.
4. Continue damaging the Brumak once the immediate weapon pressure is reduced.

The payoff is not just a shorter health bar. Every weapon you disable removes
one way the Brumak can force you out of position.

That is why spreading bullets evenly across every visible part is inefficient.
Finish one useful target, change what the boss can do, then move to the next.

Keep moving when its fire reaches your current position. The elevated route
gives you enough room to shift, so staying planted after your cover has become
unsafe is unnecessary.

Once the Weaponized Brumak falls, the Fort Vigil defense is effectively over and
the Campaign transitions toward the evacuation plan.


<h2
  id="second-brumak"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  What Changes in the Later Brumak Fight?
</h2>

Another Brumak appears later during the evacuation push.

Do not carry the Fort Vigil armor logic into this encounter. When there is no
mounted armor sequence to dismantle, return to the simpler rule from the first
fight: **create space, deal damage, and move before the creature takes away your
escape route**.

The surrounding battle can make this encounter feel busier than the Act 1
version, but standing directly in front of the Brumak remains the worst trade.
Use the widest safe part of the area and force it to spend time turning or
closing distance while you continue firing.

There is no reason to search the arena for a hidden switch or wait for a special
armor-break prompt. If the Brumak is taking normal damage, keep pressure on it
while preserving enough room to relocate.


<h2
  id="scoria"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  How Do You Beat the Scoria Fight at the Refinery?
</h2>

The Scoria encounter happens during the refinery crisis in **Act 4: Critical
Failure**, after Bravo reaches the light mass converter area.

The important number here is **two**.

Two Scoria enter the fight, and the converter objective cannot continue while
the pair is still controlling the arena.

  <Image
    src="/images/gears-of-war-e-day/gears-e-day-scoria-pair.webp"
    alt="Two Scoria attacking Bravo near the light mass converter in Gears of War E-Day"
    width={1600}
    height={900}
    className="rounded-xl"
  />

  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">
    Two Scoria are active around the converter. Concentrate damage on one until it
    drops instead of leaving both alive at partial health.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

They spend much of the fight airborne, which makes a close-range loadout much
less comfortable than it was in the corridors leading into the refinery. Keep a
weapon that can put consistent damage onto a moving target at range.

The most important decision is **not splitting damage evenly**.

If both Scoria remain alive, both can keep forcing movement and breaking your
aim. Focus the target that gives you the cleanest angle and finish it before
switching. Once one goes down, the amount of incoming pressure drops
immediately.

Keep moving between firing opportunities rather than tracking one Scoria from a
fixed point while the other attacks from outside your view. The fight has enough
space to reposition; use it.

After the first Scoria falls, do not start looking for a converter control or
assume the objective has advanced. Bravo still has to kill the **second Scoria**.
Only after both are down can the squad return to dealing with the failing
converter.

The surrounding refinery is already unstable, so this is also a bad place to
chase an angle through environmental hazards just to maintain damage. Let the
Scoria move back into a clean firing lane instead of trading safety for a few
extra rounds.

Once both are dead, return to the converter objective and continue the escape
sequence.


<h2
  id="second-corpser"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Does the Corpser Appear More Than Once?
</h2>

Yes. The Corpser returns later in the Campaign, so defeating the Enemy Lines
encounter is not the last time Bravo sees one.

The first fight is still the one that teaches the important lesson: do not assume
a giant target is asking for continuous center-mass fire. Read the space around
the creature before spending heavy ammunition.

When a later Corpser blocks the route, keep enough distance to read the next
attack and move before committing to a long reload. The encounter is shorter
than the original underground fight, so there is little reason to drain every
heavy weapon at the first possible opening.


<h2
  id="vraahk"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Is Vraahk the Final Boss?
</h2>

Yes. **Vraahk** is the final named boss encounter in the Campaign.

By the time Bravo reaches him, the fight is part of one continuous final
operation: the east bridge controls fail, the route changes, Marcus and Dom have
to keep moving through the damaged bridge and Skytrain approach, and the squad
eventually reaches the detonator.

Do not treat reaching the final arena as the end of the objective. Vraahk still
has to go down before the detonator can be used.

The complete route from the failed bridge controls through the final fight,
detonator, escape, and Epilogue is in the
