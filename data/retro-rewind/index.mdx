Your first challenge in Retro Rewind is keeping a small video store running, not building a perfect collection. Customers need movies they can actually rent, returned tapes have to make it back onto shelves, and each new purchase competes with the cash needed to operate tomorrow.

Once that daily routine works, you can broaden your movie selection, add concessions and employees, and eventually run video game and entertainment-system rentals alongside VHS movies.

## What Should You Do on Your First Day in Retro Rewind?

Start with the stock and genres your store already has. You do not need to unlock every movie category or buy an additional room before serving your first customers.

A sensible opening order is:

1. **Check the office computer and calendar.** See what you can order now and whether a release or event is coming up.
2. **Put your available movies on the correct shelves.** A tape left in storage cannot fill an empty spot on the sales floor.
3. **Open for business and handle customer requests.** Read the requested title or genre rather than handing over the first nearby case.
4. **Check in returns and restock them.** Getting a rented movie back into circulation matters as much as buying more inventory.
5. **Review what you could not supply.** Use those gaps to decide what to buy or rearrange before the next trading day.

Early progress comes from completing this cycle consistently. A new shelf can help when you have stock with nowhere to put it; it does very little when the shelves you already own are mostly empty.

## What Should You Check Before Opening Each Day?

After your first trading day, the morning routine changes with what happened yesterday. Check the office calendar for upcoming releases before committing money to ordinary stock. A new movie needs available shelf space when its release day arrives.

Next, inspect the Return Station and backstore. A popular movie may appear sold out on the sales floor even though several copies are waiting to be checked in. Restock those copies before paying for duplicates.

Use yesterday's unanswered customer requests to decide what is genuinely missing. If you already own the movie, find out why it is unavailable. If the title or genre is missing entirely, make it a purchasing priority.

During business hours, concentrate on customer requests and returns. Before closing, check which shelves are running low so the next morning starts with a clear restocking plan.

## What Should You Buy First: Movies, Shelves, Staff, or Expansions?

The best purchase depends on what is actually stopping your store from serving customers.

- **Empty shelves and missing titles:** Spend on movies customers can rent now. More furniture will not fix a shortage of usable copies.
- **Boxes of movies with nowhere to display them:** Add or rearrange shelves before placing another large order.
- **Returns or checkout work taking too long:** Consider hiring staff when your cash flow can support their wages.
- **A busy section that repeatedly runs out of copies:** Add stock for that demand rather than filling an unrelated genre.
- **No room for stock you already own:** Reorganize the current floor first; an expansion makes sense once you can use the space.

There is also a difference between buying more of a popular title and finding a replacement for a movie that barely rents. A well-stocked section should match customer requests, not just look full.

The Black Market can help when you need a particular VHS, but it is not necessary for every ordinary stock purchase. Use <Link href="/retro-rewind/is-the-black-market-worth-it">the Tape Dealer when an exact title justifies the purchase</Link>; look up a <Link href="/retro-rewind/sku-codes">movie's SKU code</Link> when you already know what you want.

## How Should You Handle Returns, Damage Fees, and Problem Customers?

A returned tape has to be checked in before it is useful again. Sort ordinary returns from damaged movies, and return working copies to their shelves instead of treating every empty position as a reason to purchase more stock.

**Damaged VHS tapes can be repaired.** The Repair Desk gives you a way to restore a cassette you still want to rent, so a broken return does not always require a fresh order. Whether to repair, replace, or discard it depends on the title's demand and collection value. <Link href="/retro-rewind/when-to-replace-damaged-tape">Compare those choices before throwing away a valuable copy</Link>.

The checkout monitor lets you review customer profiles and fee history when someone disputes a charge. You can also ban a member, but reserve that option for customers whose future business you are willing to lose.

Rental devices have a different return process from movie cassettes. Test returned equipment before renting it again; damaged VHS players and game consoles are repaired through <Link href="/retro-rewind/tech-shed-electronics">Tech Shed Electronics</Link> rather than the tape Repair Desk.

## When Can You Add Video Games and Console Rentals?

Video games become available as your store levels up, but cartridges and complete entertainment systems use different rental equipment. Buying games does not automatically make consoles available for customers to rent.

Keep your VHS returns and restocking under control before investing heavily in another rental category. When you're ready to expand into gaming, <Link href="/retro-rewind/video-game-rentals">unlock the consoles, prepare the game shelves, and set up equipment rentals</Link>.

</section>

## Why Isn't Your Retro Rewind Store Making Progress?

The same symptom can have several different causes, so check where the normal store cycle has stopped.

**Customers keep asking for movies you cannot supply.** Check which genres are unlocked and whether the missing copies are waiting in storage or the returns area. Buy new stock when those checks do not solve the gap.

**Your store is crowded but the shelves are still empty.** More floor space will not restock a movie. Check in returned copies and move available stock onto shelves before expanding again.

**You have money but cannot find the next movie genre.** Open the Store Level Unlocks calendar. New categories arrive through progression; buying random inventory does not bypass their unlock levels.

**You bought video games but customers are not renting them.** Games require their own display shelves, while complete console rentals require a different backstore installation. Check which part of that setup is missing.

**A damaged movie has disappeared from your rental selection.** Check whether the cassette is still repairable before ordering another copy of the same title.
