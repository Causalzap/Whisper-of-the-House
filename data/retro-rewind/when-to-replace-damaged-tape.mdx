A broken VHS tape no longer has to mean a permanent loss. Retro Rewind lets you restore damaged movies using the Repair Desk in your backstore, so paying for another copy is not always the first or cheapest solution.

The real decision comes after you identify the damaged title. A popular New Release, a holographic collectible, and an ordinary movie that rarely gets rented do not deserve the same amount of attention. Repairing one might protect your busiest shelf; repairing another might only preserve inventory you no longer need.

## How Do You Repair Broken VHS Tapes in Retro Rewind?

First, unlock the **Repair Desk** in your backstore. The installation costs **$100** through **Bob's Repair Services**.

Check the available station upgrades rather than looking for another movie in the ordering computer. The Repair Desk is a work area for existing damaged inventory, not a replacement-movie purchasing option.

Once it is installed:

1. **Check in the damaged return.** Separate broken movies from tapes that only need rewinding or restocking.
2. **Take the broken VHS to the Repair Desk** in your backstore.
3. **Start the repair interaction** and complete the on-screen repair process.
4. **Collect the restored movie** once the repair is finished.
5. **Return it to the appropriate shelf** so it becomes available for rental again.

The important result is getting your existing movie back into circulation. A repair does not require you to order a fresh copy of the same title.

### Where Is the Repair Desk?

Look in the **backstore storage area**, where your returned movies and other inventory can be handled away from the sales floor.

If you cannot find an active Repair Desk, check Bob's Repair Services for the installation rather than assuming your store needs another room expansion.

The $100 charge is the installation price. Do not confuse that one-time unlock with the cost of replacing individual movies.

## Do Damage Fees Pay for a Broken VHS Tape?

Not necessarily. A customer returning a broken VHS can be charged a damage fee, but collecting that fee does not restore the movie or guarantee that it covers the cost of another copy.

Check whether the damaged tape is worth repairing before spending money on a replacement. If the customer disputes the fee at checkout, handle that interaction separately from the decision to keep or discard the movie.

</section>

## Should You Repair, Replace, or Throw Away a Damaged Tape?

Start with the movie's value to your store, not the inconvenience of seeing a broken cassette in your returns.

### Repair Movies You Still Need

Repair is usually the sensible first choice when the damaged copy belongs to a category that customers regularly request.

A working tape can go straight back into your rental collection after restoration. Buying another copy instead uses cash and adds a new purchasing task without necessarily improving your selection.

This is especially important when your store already has several damaged movies waiting in the backroom. Check what can be restored before ordering a large batch of replacements.

### When Is Buying Another Copy Better Than Repairing?

Repairing a damaged VHS restores your existing copy; it does not increase the number of movies you own. Once the original is back in circulation, decide whether another purchase would improve your rental selection.

- **Buy the exact title** when customers frequently request it and your available copies keep running out.
- **Buy another movie in the same genre** when the original title has little demand but the category needs more variety.
- **Buy a stronger title instead** when the damaged movie rarely rents and a better-performing alternative is available.

For a popular movie, the extra copy gives more customers a chance to rent it. For a weak title, buying something different may be more useful than replacing the original.

If you need a specific hard-to-find movie, <Link href="/retro-rewind/sku-codes">check its SKU code</Link> before placing another order.

### Discard Movies You No Longer Want

Not every damaged movie is worth keeping forever.

An ordinary title with little demand may be taking up space that could serve a stronger movie. In that case, removing the copy is a reasonable inventory decision, even if repairing it would have been possible.

Make the decision deliberately. Once a collectible or difficult-to-replace movie has been discarded, obtaining that exact version again may take more effort than restoring it would have.

## Should You Repair a Damaged New Release Immediately?

**Yes, when customers are still actively renting it.**

A damaged New Release can be more urgent than an older movie because its strongest rental demand may be concentrated around its release period.

Check whether the title is still marked as a New Release and how many working copies remain.

- **You have several working copies:** Repair the damaged one when the return workload allows.
- **Only one copy remains:** Repairing another becomes more urgent, especially before a busy trading day.
- **Every copy is unavailable:** Restore one as soon as possible instead of ordering blindly and leaving customers without the title.

Repair a popular title before opening when recent rental requests are strong and only a few working copies remain. A busy Friday alone is not a reason to buy duplicates; the number of available copies matters more than replacing every damaged tape ahead of the weekend.

### What If the New Release Period Is Almost Over?

Check the calendar and the movie's current status before paying for extra copies.

A title nearing the end of its New Release period may no longer justify the same purchasing priority as one that just launched. Repairing an existing copy can preserve the movie for ordinary rentals without committing to another large order.

When the release period ends, you can still keep the movie as part of your regular collection.

</section>

## Should You Repair Rare or Holographic VHS Tapes?

**Prioritize keeping them.**

A holographic movie is worth keeping even when rental demand is low, especially if you're collecting special editions or filling a display case.

Before discarding a damaged copy, try restoring it at the Repair Desk. This preserves the collectible version you already own without requiring another difficult-to-find purchase.

</section>

<section id="cash-and-order-limits" className="scroll-mt-24">

## What If You Cannot Afford to Replace Every Broken Tape?

Early in the game, buying replacement movies competes with employee wages, shelves, and stock for upcoming releases. Repairing a movie you already own can free up that purchasing budget for something your collection is missing.

Keep valuable damaged copies until you can restore them. Low-demand movies are different: when you no longer want a title, clearing it out may be more useful than spending time and money maintaining it.

### Does Replacing Broken Tapes Use Your Movie Order Capacity?

Buying a replacement requires a normal movie purchase, so it competes with other stock you plan to order. Repairing an existing cassette uses the Repair Desk instead of placing a new movie order.

When your purchasing options are limited, restore the movies customers already want and reserve new orders for missing titles or additional copies that serve proven demand.

</section>

<section id="repair-not-working" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Why Can't You Repair a Damaged VHS Tape?

Start with the item you're trying to repair. A damaged VHS movie belongs at the backstore Repair Desk. If the station isn't available, unlock it through Bob's Repair Services before attempting the repair.

A damaged VHS player or gaming console is different equipment. Take electronic hardware to <Link href="/retro-rewind/tech-shed-electronics">Tech Shed Electronics</Link> rather than trying to repair it at the tape workbench.

If the movie has entered the repair interaction but still cannot go back on the shelf, complete the on-screen repair and collect the restored cassette first. Repairing and restocking are separate actions.

A movie that has already been thrown away cannot be restored through the Repair Desk. You'll need another copy if you still want that title.

</section>
