## Where Is Tech Shed Electronics, and When Is It Open?

Leave your video store and head toward the nearby shopping area. Look for **Tech Shed Electronics** along the road near the barbershop.

The shop operates on this schedule:

- **Monday:** Closed
- **Tuesday:** Closed
- **Wednesday–Sunday:** 8:00 AM to midnight

Check the day before making a purchasing trip. A closed shop does not mean you need to unlock another console, purchase a store expansion, or wait for a particular game release.

A visit late on Tuesday won't help, even if your next new-release game is approaching. Finish your current store business and return when Tech Shed opens Wednesday morning.

Tech Shed is separate from your own store's Catalogue. You can order certain equipment from the Catalogue without visiting the electronics shop, but ordinary game shopping requires checking its physical stock.

## How Do You Buy Games from Tech Shed Electronics?

Enter during business hours and inspect the game cartridges currently displayed on the shop's shelves. Check the title and console family before buying anything.

You'll find games for **Super Tenko** and **Saga Origin**. These are different cartridge libraries, so collecting a game for one system doesn't automatically add an equivalent title for the other.

### Does Tech Shed Get New Games Every Day?

Missing a trading day does not necessarily mean losing every game you wanted. Check the shelves when Tech Shed reopens; unsold cartridges may still be available, but there is no reliable fixed restock time or daily quantity to plan around.

If the title you want is missing, keep your money for now and return on another trading day. Buying duplicates you do not need will not help you find that particular game.

## How Do You Find Colored Game Cartridges at Tech Shed?

Inspect cartridges with unusual colors instead of looking only at their titles.

Tech Shed can occasionally offer limited-edition games with metallic-colored cartridges. Check unusual copies carefully: a different-looking case is worth inspecting, but cartridge color alone does not tell you how rare or valuable that edition is.

Red, gold, and green-colored cartridges can appear as part of a game collection. Before purchasing a copy for its appearance, check the actual game and edition rather than assuming a particular color represents a higher rarity tier.

There is no established color-based price ranking to rely on when comparing these variants.

### Should You Buy Every Colored Cartridge?

Look at the title first, then decide whether the colored copy adds something you actually want.

For a small game collection, a missing title can be more useful than another edition of a game you already own. Once you've built a broader selection and your shop has money to spare, collecting unusual cartridge variants becomes a more reasonable expense.

Three questions help with that decision:

- Do you already own the ordinary version?
- Is the colored copy for a console family you collect?
- Can you afford it without delaying an immediate store purchase?

Don't discard an existing edition simply because you've found another color. Inspect the copies before deciding which one to keep.

## Can Tech Shed Repair Broken Consoles and VHS Players?

Tech Shed Electronics handles repairs for damaged **VHS players and gaming consoles**.

If a returned entertainment system fails its condition test, take care of the hardware repair before offering it for rent again. Tech Shed is the place to check for electronic repairs during its regular opening hours.

A damaged **VHS tape** is different. Movie tapes are repaired at your video store's Repair Desk, not at Tech Shed. For damaged movie copies, <Link href="/retro-rewind/when-to-replace-damaged-tape">check when to repair or replace a VHS tape</Link>.

### Is Repairing a Console Cheaper Than Buying Another?

Check the repair charge at Tech Shed before ordering replacement equipment. A working console can handle another rental after being returned, so restoring an existing device may cost less than purchasing a second one.

Compare the available repair price with the replacement cost shown in your Catalogue. If you already have enough equipment for customer requests, getting the damaged unit working again may be more useful than expanding your stock.

Do not assume every console or VHS player has the same repair cost. Check the actual charge before deciding.
