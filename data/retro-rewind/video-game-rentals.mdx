Video games and consoles use two different rental setups in Retro Rewind. Game cartridges go on video game shelves where customers can browse and rent them. Entire consoles are kept on the System Rental Shelf in your backstore and handed out when customers request them at the help desk.

You can start building a game collection before you unlock console rentals. That distinction matters when your store has reached Level 9 or 15 but you still cannot find anywhere to place a rental console.

## What Level Unlocks Video Games in Retro Rewind?

Check the unlock calendar in your office. The two console families become available at different store levels:

- **Level 9 — Super Tenko:** Unlocks the first video game console family alongside the Christmas genre.
- **Level 15 — Saga Origin:** Unlocks the second console family alongside the Comedy genre.
- **Level 21 — System Rentals:** Unlocks the separate system-rental feature used to rent entire consoles and VHS players.

The first two unlocks give you access to their respective consoles and games. They do not automatically install the System Rental Shelf.

## Where Do You Get Your First Video Games?

Head to **Tech Shed Electronics** after unlocking your first console family. Buy a few compatible cartridges, bring them back to your video store, and put them on a Video Game Shelf.

Start with games for the console family you have unlocked. Check your available shelf space before spending heavily on cartridges; anything you cannot display will remain in storage instead of becoming available to customers.

For Tech Shed's opening hours, available game stock, and special editions, <Link href="/retro-rewind/tech-shed-electronics">check what you can buy at Tech Shed Electronics</Link> before making another trip.

## How Do You Put Video Games on Rental Shelves?

Open the Catalogue and look under **Shelves**. Retro Rewind includes shelving specifically designed for video games, including a separate type for new releases.

A normal video game shelf is the first thing to buy. Place it on your sales floor, unpack your cartridges, and stock them in the available positions.

The Catalogue offers three sizes of each video game shelf:

- **Standard Shelf:** 5 rows for **$130**, 6 rows for **$180**, or 7 rows for **$220**.
- **New Releases Shelf:** 5 rows for **$150**, 6 rows for **$200**, or 7 rows for **$230**.

For your first game section, the **$130 Standard Shelf** is the cheapest starting option. Move up to a larger shelf when the collection needs more room. The 7-row New Releases Shelf costs only $10 more than the equivalent Standard Shelf, but it is most useful when you have new titles ready to display.

### Should You Separate Super Tenko and Saga Origin Games?

A shared shelf is enough for a small collection. Once both console families are available, give Super Tenko and Saga Origin separate shelf areas so you can find compatible cartridges quickly when a customer requests a particular platform.

The games have their own qualities, including review ratings and other features that can matter when recommending a title. Start by sorting by console compatibility rather than trying to build individual shelves for every game characteristic.

### Do You Need a Store Expansion Immediately?

No. Place your first Standard Video Game Shelf in unused sales-floor space. Consider another room only when the existing VHS and game sections no longer have enough space for the inventory you want to display.

## How Do You Get New-Release Video Games?

Check the **office calendar** regularly. Video game release dates appear alongside the existing movie releases and special events.

The Video Game Rental update adds **16 scheduled video game releases**. These are different from the wider selection of ordinary collectible titles at Tech Shed.

Prepare for a release before opening your store:

- Check which game is scheduled to launch and when.
- Leave space on a New Release Video Game Shelf.
- Order available release copies through the game's purchasing system.
- Stock the new title so customers requesting a new-release game have something suitable to rent.

Each new-release game order also provides a promotional ceiling banner. You can use it to decorate the corresponding game area.

## How Do You Give Customers the Right Video Game?

Customers can browse game shelves, reserve titles, and ask you to recommend a game at the help desk.

Read the request before picking up a cartridge. The console family matters just as much as the game's other qualities.

For example, a customer may say they play on a **Super Tenko** and want something critics liked. A well-reviewed Saga Origin title still won't meet the console requirement.

Handle recommendations in this order:

1. Identify the customer's console: Super Tenko or Saga Origin.
2. Check any additional preference, such as a good review rating or a new release.
3. Select a matching game from the correct shelf and return it to the customer.

If a customer rejects your recommendation, check the cartridge's platform label before choosing another title. A strong critic rating cannot compensate for a game made for the wrong console.

Reservations work with video games as well as VHS movies. A customer may arrive asking for a particular game that was previously reserved, so keep reserved copies distinguishable from your ordinary shelf stock.

## How Do You Rent Out Super Tenko and Saga Origin Consoles?

Renting an entire console requires the **System Rental Shelf**, not the regular video game shelving.

System Rentals becomes available at Level 21. Call Bob's Repair Services and pay $150 to unlock the System Rental Shelf in your backstore. The shelf allows you to rent out VHS players and gaming consoles.

After installing the System Rental Shelf, prepare your equipment:

1. Open the Catalogue's **Equipment** section.
2. Buy a Super Tenko, Saga Origin, or both.
3. Place the purchased systems on the backstore rental shelf.
4. Open your store and fulfill customer requests for the available consoles.

### How Does a Customer Actually Rent a Console?

The customer requests the system at the help desk. Unlike a cartridge rental, you retrieve the requested entertainment system from the backstore rental stock.

A customer can explicitly ask for a **Super Tenko** or **Saga Origin**. Retrieve that console, complete the interaction, and collect the rental payment.

## What Should You Do When a Customer Returns a Console?

Console rentals return through the store's customer-service process. Handle the returned system separately from ordinary VHS and cartridge stock.

Returned entertainment systems need to be tested before going back into rental stock. Accept the returned console and complete its testing process before making it available to another customer.

Follow the return process in order:

1. Accept the returned console at the help desk.
2. Complete the returned system's condition test.
3. Return a working system to your available rental stock.
4. Send damaged equipment for repair before renting it again.

A damaged console needs <Link href="/retro-rewind/tech-shed-electronics">repair at Tech Shed Electronics</Link>. The backstore Repair Desk fixes damaged VHS tapes, not console hardware.

Keeping a returned console in circulation without checking its condition risks putting damaged equipment back into the rental pool.

The store can also receive returned game cartridges alongside movies. Process those through the ordinary returns workflow and restock the appropriate game shelves.

## Why Can't You Rent Video Games or Consoles Yet?

A few situations look similar but have different fixes.

**The Catalogue shows games, but you have nowhere to display them.**

Buy a regular video game shelf from the Shelves section. Game cartridges need their own display space; unlocking the console family does not create a shelf automatically.

**Super Tenko is unlocked, but Saga Origin isn't available.**

Check your store level. Super Tenko unlocks at Level 9, while Saga Origin follows at Level 15. Continue normal store operations until the second family unlocks.

**You can buy consoles, but the backstore rental shelf is missing.**

Reach Level 21, then pay $150 through Bob's Repair Services to unlock the System Rental Shelf. The consoles themselves and the rental station are separate unlocks.

**A customer rejects the game you selected.**

Check the console requirement before trying another title. A request for a Super Tenko game cannot be fulfilled with a Saga Origin cartridge, regardless of its review score.

**Customers aren't requesting your new consoles.**

First confirm that the System Rental Shelf is installed and the purchased consoles are actually stocked. Console requests happen through customer interactions, so owning equipment alone doesn't guarantee an immediate rental.

There is no established fixed waiting period that guarantees a particular console will be requested. Keep both the equipment and compatible game stock available while continuing normal trading.

**You have games but customers keep asking for new releases.**

Check the office calendar and the New Release Video Game Shelf. Older collectible games and scheduled new-release titles serve different requests.
