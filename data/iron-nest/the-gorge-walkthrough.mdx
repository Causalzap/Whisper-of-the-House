<h2 id="the-gorge-route">
  The Gorge route at a glance
</h2>

The Gorge is not a normal firing-solution mission.

Iron Nest has already been hit, the engine needs a manual restart, and the
ballistic computer is unavailable. Command gives the position of the Nest and
says the enemy is holding the pass to the east, but it does not provide exact
target coordinates.

The reliable route is:

1. Complete the manual engine restart.
2. Read the damage report and stop trying to restore the ballistic computer.
3. Mark Iron Nest at the printed coordinate near K4 9:0.
4. Send one reconnaissance flight east from the Nest.
5. Mark the blue convoy and the visible enemy groups.
6. Choose an isolated opening target away from the convoy.
7. Load one gun and fire one ranging shot.
8. Mark the impact and adjust only the elevation.
9. Repeat the observed-fire process across the forward enemy element.
10. Stand down when the pass is clear and the column begins moving.

The <Link href="/iron-nest">Iron Nest guide hub</Link> covers normal calculator
use, shell roles, map controls, and the standard firing loop. This mission page
only explains what changes when the calculator cannot provide an elevation.

<h2 id="restart-engine">
  Restart the damaged engine first
</h2>

The opening damage report confirms that Iron Nest was hit on the port
underside. The armor held, but pressure lines ruptured and the engine requires a
manual restart.

Go to the damaged engine station before planning the fire mission.

    width={1600}

    className="rounded-xl"
  />
  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">
    Restore the engine so the Nest can operate. The ballistic computer remaining
    offline is part of the mission rather than an unfinished repair step.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

After the restart, confirm that the systems needed for manual fire still work:

- tactical map;
- reconnaissance requisition;
- turret rotation;
- left and right loaders;
- powder-charge loading;
- manual gun elevation;
- firing controls.

The calculator or targeting station can remain red or unavailable.

Do not repeat the engine restart because the targeting computer is still
offline. The mission order explicitly says the affected system is not expected
to be restored and that the operation must be completed without it.

<h2 id="read-gorge-order">
  Mark K4 9:0 and read what command actually knows
</h2>

The escort has already dealt with an initial attack near the column. Your
required target is a larger forward element holding the gorge east of Iron
Nest.

The order gives three useful pieces of information:

- Iron Nest is near **K4 9:0**;
- the required enemy force is east of that position;
- reconnaissance is available at the operator's discretion.

It does not give a clean enemy coordinate or a normal calculated firing card.

Place the Iron Nest marker at the printed position before drawing the
reconnaissance line. Do not use the convoy as the firing origin simply because
its blue icons appear near the K4 sector.

Also distinguish between:

- the initial attackers already handled by the escort;
- the forward enemy element blocking the route;
- secondary elements behind the column that may remain after completion.

Only the forward element is required for the primary objective.

<h2 id="send-east-recon">
  Send one reconnaissance flight east
</h2>

Place the reconnaissance origin at Iron Nest and aim the flight east at
approximately **90°**.

    Command gives a search direction rather than a target coordinate, making
    one narrow eastbound reconnaissance flight worth the requisition.

Keep the flight aligned with the gorge instead of photographing a broad section
of unrelated terrain.

In one operation, the resulting image showed hostile positions spread roughly
from **4.80 km to 8.50 km** east of the Nest. The main visible group was near:

- **90.7° bearing**
- **5.32 km range**

Those are examples from that operation, not fixed mission coordinates.

Do not immediately buy another reconnaissance flight. The first image already
shows the corridor that must be cleared. More photographs are only useful when
part of the forward route remains genuinely uncovered.

<h2 id="mark-convoy-and-enemies">
  Mark the convoy before selecting the first target
</h2>

The reconnaissance image can show a dense collection of enemies east of the
blue convoy.

    width={1600}

    className="rounded-xl"
  />
  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">
    The first target should provide a useful ranging impact without placing the
    convoy inside the expected effect area.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

Mark the blue positions first. Then mark each distinct hostile group along the
gorge.

For the opening ranging shot, choose a group that is:

- clearly part of the forward element;
- separated from the convoy;
- large enough to tolerate an imperfect first impact;
- close to the main eastbound firing line;
- useful as a reference for the other enemy groups.

Do not begin with the hostile group closest to the convoy. The first manual
shot is the least predictable shot of the mission.

The visible concentration justified a large-area destructive shell in the
documented run, but only because its effect area remained clear of the blue
icons. For a target nearer the convoy, use the smaller safe option available in
your current magazines rather than forcing the same shell choice throughout the
mission.

<h2 id="first-manual-shot">
  Treat the first shell as a ranging shot
</h2>

The map can still give you a useful bearing and approximate target distance.
What it cannot give you is the calculated elevation.

In one operation, the enemy corridor stayed close to **90–91°**, so the turret
bearing was known even while the elevation had to be estimated manually.

The first ranging shot used approximately:

- **90–91° bearing**
- **two powder charges**
- **49.28° elevation**

That shot landed much farther along the gorge than intended.

The miss was still useful because it answered a specific question:

> Where does a two-charge shot at approximately 49.28° land on this eastbound
> line?

Fire only one gun for the first test.

Do not prepare a second guessed elevation and fire both guns together. Two
different impacts would make it harder to identify which elevation produced
which crater.

<h2 id="correct-impact">
  Correct one variable at a time
</h2>

Once the impact appears, place a temporary marker at the center of the crater.

Then compare the crater with the intended enemy marker.

If the shell landed on the correct eastbound line:

- keep the turret bearing;
- keep the same powder-charge count;
- change only the elevation.

If the shell landed left or right of the hostile corridor, correct the bearing
before changing the range.

The documented clear used several manual elevations, including approximately:

- **44°** after the opening overshoot;
- **28.5°** against another part of the enemy line;
- **31°** for the final required group.

These are not three steps of a fixed elevation sequence. They belonged to
different target points along the gorge.

Do not copy `44 → 28.5 → 31` into another run. Instead, preserve the relationship
between:

- target marker;
- impact marker;
- bearing;
- charge count;
- elevation used.

The useful rule is:

> Fire once, observe once, change one control, then fire again.

<h2 id="clear-forward-element">
  Work through the forward element from known impacts
</h2>

After a successful correction, the last crater becomes a local reference for
the next enemy group.

Continue when:

- the impact is visible;
- the hostile group survives;
- the next target lies on or close to the same eastbound line;
- the convoy remains outside the effect area;
- you know which elevation produced the previous crater.

Return to the map when:

- the crater lands left or right of the hostile corridor;
- the next group lies on a different bearing;
- the target marker was confused with a blue convoy icon;
- the previous image no longer shows the surviving unit clearly;
- the firing origin was not K4 9:0.

For each remaining group:

1. Mark the intended impact point.
2. Compare it with the most useful previous crater.
3. Keep two charges when continuing the same documented manual sequence.
4. Adjust elevation in the direction suggested by the observed impact.
5. Fire one gun.
6. Wait for the updated reconnaissance image.
7. Remove destroyed target markers.

Do not alternate between one, two, and three charges while also changing
elevation. That removes the value of the previous shot as a reference.

The point of the mission is not to invent a universal artillery table during
the operation. It is to use repeatable observations to move the shell impact
through the required enemy line.

<h2 id="protect-convoy">
  Recheck the convoy before every shot
</h2>

The convoy does not need smoke support or direct fire. It needs the blocking
force removed.

Before every shell, confirm that:

- the target is east of Iron Nest;
- the effect area does not overlap a blue icon;
- the shot is aimed at the forward element rather than the already-cleared
  initial attack position;
- the next elevation change will not pull the impact back toward the convoy;
- the friendly column has not begun moving into the target area.

The safest first target is usually farther from the convoy. Once the first
crater establishes how the gun is behaving, work back toward the remaining
required groups with smaller, controlled corrections.

Do not use a wider blast merely to reduce the number of shells when the circle
reaches the convoy route. The documented safe clear earned a strong kill-per-shot
result without sacrificing the minimal-casualty result.

<h2 id="pass-clear">
  Stand down when the pass is clear
</h2>

The primary objective is complete when command reports:

- `Pass is clear`
- `Column is moving`
- forward element neutralized;
- route open;
- primary objective complete;
- Iron Nest may stand down.

The same message says the secondary elements at the rear of the column are not
required targets.

For the efficient route:

- stop requisitioning shells;
- do not order another reconnaissance flight;
- clear the active firing controls;
- stand down.

The rear targets are behind the convoy rather than blocking the pass. Continuing
fire changes the goal from mission completion to optional medal cleanup.

<h2 id="gorge-medals">
  Choose between the safe clear and optional full elimination
</h2>

The documented safe-clear route earned results for:

- a high kill-per-shot ratio;
- minimal allied casualties.

It did not complete the optional results tied to:

- eliminating every enemy;
- maintaining consecutive kills across multiple fire missions.

That creates two different approaches.

### Efficient safe clear

Stop when the forward element is neutralized.

This route protects:

- allied safety;
- requisition efficiency;
- kill-per-shot performance;
- a clean stand-down after the primary objective.

### Optional cleanup route

Continue only when:

- you are deliberately pursuing all enemies;
- the rear positions have been reconfirmed;
- the column is clear of the intended impact area;
- the additional shots will not invalidate the result you value more.

Do not order another plane after `Pass is clear` unless you have already decided
to pursue the optional targets.

<h2 id="gorge-stuck">
  The Gorge mistakes that waste the run
</h2>

### Trying to repair the ballistic computer

The mission expects you to complete the operation without the affected system.

### Drawing from the convoy instead of K4 9:0

The convoy is the protected column. Iron Nest remains the firing origin.

### Firing east without reconnaissance

East defines the search direction, not the safe impact point.

### Choosing the target closest to the convoy first

Use an isolated group for the opening ranging shot.

### Firing both guns before seeing the first impact

One observed crater gives a useful reference. Two simultaneous guesses do not.

### Changing charges, bearing, and elevation together

Keep the known values fixed and change one variable at a time.

### Removing the crater marker too early

The crater is the replacement for the missing calculator result.

### Copying 49.28°, 44°, 28.5°, or 31°

Those elevations belong to specific shots in one operation. Use the observed
impact in your own run.

### Building a permanent lookup table during the mission

A quick reference may be useful later, but the current objective only requires
repeatable corrections along this gorge.

### Continuing after the pass-clear message

Rear elements are optional once the column is moving.
