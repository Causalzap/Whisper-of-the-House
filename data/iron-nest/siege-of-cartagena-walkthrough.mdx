<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
**Siege of Cartagena has four required phases.** First, use the field reports
to locate the enemy signal station, assembly area, and field command. Check the
signal-station marker carefully because the other two targets depend on chained
measurements from earlier calculated positions.

After destroying all three installations, locate the two coastal batteries from
the listening-post reports. Treat “south” and “southeast” as search sectors,
not exact firing bearings. Confirm the battery icons, prepare two AP solutions,
and destroy both before the Counter-Battery Timer expires.

If Iron Nest must use Emergency Move, request two Position Reports and mark the
intersection of their bearing lines. Recalculate both battery shots from that
new firing origin.

Once the batteries are gone, destroy at least eight additional enemy units,
prepare two Smoke Shells, sound the War Horn, and complete the requests at
**K6 8:5** and **K8 5:1**. Stand down when Mendoza confirms that the breakout
succeeded.

<h2 id="siege-of-cartagena-route">
  Siege of Cartagena route at a glance
</h2>

Siege of Cartagena is difficult because one inaccurate marker can damage
several later shots.

The opening targets are connected. The enemy signal station becomes a reference
for the assembly area and field command, so an error in the first target does
not remain isolated.

My safe route is:

1. Mark Iron Nest and all three spotters.
2. Locate the enemy signal station.
3. Use the confirmed station marker to locate the assembly area.
4. Use the station and assembly area to locate the field command.
5. Prepare and fire three H-shell solutions.
6. Read the coastal-battery reports immediately.
7. Use the directions and distances to define two search sectors.
8. Confirm both coastal-battery icons before firing.
9. Prepare two AP solutions.
10. Use Emergency Move when another reliable volley cannot beat the CBT.
11. Use two Position Reports to locate the moved Iron Nest.
12. Recalculate and destroy both batteries.
13. Destroy at least eight additional enemy units.
14. Prepare two Smoke Shells and sound the War Horn.
15. Complete both smoke requests.
16. Stand down after `Breakout successful`.

The <Link href="/iron-nest">Iron Nest guide hub</Link> covers the normal map,
calculator, ammunition, and loading systems. The
<Link href="/iron-nest/counter-battery-mission">
  Counter-Battery walkthrough
</Link>
covers the basic CBT loop. This page only explains Cartagena's chained targets,

<h2 id="mark-cartagena-reference-points">
  Mark Iron Nest and the three spotters
</h2>

The opening briefing warns that the field bearings can carry an error of up to
**0.5°**.

In one operation, the starting reference points were:

- Iron Nest: **D10 4:6**
- Spotter 1: **K7 0:2**
- Spotter 2: **L7 9:2**
- Spotter 3: **D7 2:5**

Your own operation can use different values.

Place all four markers before drawing any target measurements. I also delete
the completed Iron Road lines at this point because old railway and AP lines can
look like part of the Cartagena chain.

Do not manually add or subtract 0.5° from every bearing. The warning means the
reported line has a possible margin of error.

The danger is not usually the first H shell. The danger is placing the first
target slightly wrong, then using that wrong marker as the origin for another
measurement.

<h2 id="locate-three-concealed-installations">
  Locate the three concealed installations in order
</h2>

The three opening installations are hidden from aerial photography, so solve
them from the field reports.

### 1. Enemy signal station

Locate the signal station first.

In the documented operation, its reports included several distances that
converged on the same small area. Place the marker at the most credible common
intersection rather than at the edge of one measurement circle.

Do not continue to the chained targets when:

- the reports do not meet in one compact area;
- the marker falls outside the enemy backline;
- one distance was drawn from the wrong spotter;
- the point was chosen because it “looks right” rather than because the reports
  agree.

### 2. Enemy assembly area

The assembly-area chain used:

- approximately **221° from the enemy signal station**
- approximately **229° from Spotter 2**

The target belongs near the crossing of those two lines.

This is the first point where the signal-station error can carry forward. Zoom
in before placing the assembly-area marker.

### 3. Enemy field command

The field command used:

- approximately **235° from the enemy signal station**
- approximately **2.41 km from the enemy assembly area**

The 2.41 km measurement begins at the assembly area, not at the signal station.

<h2 id="prepare-three-installation-shots">
  Prepare all three opening shots before firing
</h2>

After all three installation markers are confirmed, draw a separate final
firing line from Iron Nest to each target.

One operation produced:

- **149.9° / 6.64 km**
- **135.5° / 8.16 km**
- **98.4° / 9.67 km**

The calculated elevations in that operation were approximately:

- **39.84°**
- **48.96°**
- **58.02°**

These values are examples from one operation, not fixed Cartagena answers.

Use H shells for the three opening installations. Do not buy speculative AP
rounds for them merely because fortified targets appear later in the mission.

Before firing:

- number every target marker;
- number every firing card;
- record the physical charge count;
- assign a gun side;
- preserve enough requisition for two AP shells and possible movement recovery.

Fire one confirmed solution at a time. After each impact, read the destruction
report before deleting its marker.

When the third installation is destroyed, clear the completed construction
lines and return to the teleprinter immediately.

<h2 id="locate-coastal-batteries">
  Use the coastal reports as search sectors
</h2>

The coastal-battery report identifies a listening post and describes the
batteries relative to it.

In one operation:

- listening post: approximately **K7 7:8**
- first battery: approximately **5.25 km south**
- second battery: approximately **5.27 km southeast**

The real trap is treating those words as exact firing bearings:

- south = exactly 180°
- southeast = exactly 135°

That produced points in implausible locations and wasted CBT time.

Use each report to define a search sector:

1. Mark the listening post.
2. Draw the reported distance.
3. Inspect the southern or southeastern part of that radius.
4. Check the latest developed reconnaissance covering the coastal strip.
5. Confirm the actual battery icon.
6. Only then draw the final Iron Nest-to-battery line.

The opening briefing says the first three installations are concealed from
aerial photography. It does not say the later coastal batteries are invisible.

In the documented operation, both coastal-battery icons were already visible in
developed reconnaissance, but they were noticed only after several incorrect
shots.

Do not fire at the center of the directional estimate when the actual battery
icon is available elsewhere on the map.

<h2 id="prepare-two-ap-battery-shots">
  Confirm both coastal batteries and prepare two AP shots
</h2>

Once the two coastal-battery icons are confirmed, prepare both AP solutions
before firing.

Cartagena punishes three mistakes here:

- using the broad-direction endpoints instead of the icons;
- loading H instead of AP;
- reversing the left and right firing cards.

Write the gun side directly on both cards.

Do not start the first AP shot while the second battery is still only a guess.
The first confirmed hostile action can leave too little time to stop, remeasure,
procure another shell, and reload.

If both targets are confirmed from the original Iron Nest position, calculate
both before committing the volley. If the timer is already too low, use
Emergency Move rather than forcing another uncertain attempt.

<h2 id="when-to-use-emergency-move">
  Use Emergency Move when another reliable volley will not fit
</h2>

Emergency Move is a recovery tool, not an automatic failure.

In one operation, the left and right firing cards were reversed. After another
incorrect volley, only about **96 seconds** remained. That was not enough to
diagnose both targets, prepare corrected AP shots, reload, and wait for impact.

Use Emergency Move when:

- the battery markers are still uncertain;
- the previous AP volley clearly missed;
- the two cards were reversed;
- the remaining time cannot cover a full corrected sequence;
- the next shell would be another guess.

Do not wait until the CBT impact is already arriving.

After movement:

- keep the confirmed coastal-battery markers;
- delete the old Iron Nest marker;
- delete both old final firing lines;
- discard the old bearings, ranges, charges, and elevations;
- request Position Reports before firing again.

A coastal battery does not move merely because Iron Nest moved. Your firing
origin does.

<h2 id="locate-iron-nest-with-position-reports">
  Use two Position Reports after Emergency Move
</h2>

A Position Report gives:

- a reference coordinate;
- a bearing from that reference toward Iron Nest;
- a broad grid containing the Nest.

One report creates a line of possible positions. It does not identify one exact
point.

The failed recovery attempt used only one report:

- somewhere inside B8;
- approximately **153° from B8 0:6**

A guessed point on that line produced another pair of misses.

After the next move, two reports were used:

- approximately **336° from A9 4:7**
- approximately **303° from B9 7:5**

Draw both bearings from their printed reference coordinates. Their intersection
is the new Iron Nest position.

After marking the new Nest:

1. Keep the two confirmed battery markers.
2. Draw two new final firing lines from the intersection.
3. Recalculate both ranges.
4. Recalculate both charge counts and elevations.
5. Label the left and right cards again.
6. Confirm both AP shells physically loaded.
7. Fire the prepared volley.

Do not preserve the old elevation because the target did not move. Elevation is
based on the new range from the moved Nest.

For a cleaner route:

- identify both battery icons before the first AP volley;
- label the cards by gun side;
- prepare both shells;
- avoid Emergency Move unless the timer truly requires it.

When both coastal batteries are destroyed, the Counter-Battery pressure ends
and the mission moves to the city-clearing stage.

<h2 id="destroy-eight-additional-units">
  Destroy at least eight additional enemy units
</h2>

The next order requires at least **eight additional enemy units** around
Cartagena before the garrison can begin its breakout.

The same order warns that units will request supporting smoke after the War
Horn is sounded.

One operation showed approximately twelve possible targets.

Group the safe targets that can be covered without touching friendly positions.
Use smaller individual shots where the enemy icons are separated or close to
the breakout route.

Do not continue clearing the entire map by habit.

Continue firing until:

- eight additional kills are confirmed;
- the breakout indicator turns green;
- the War Horn becomes the next required action.

At that point, stop buying destructive ammunition and prepare **two Smoke
Shells**.

<h2 id="prepare-smoke-and-sound-war-horn">
  Load two Smoke Shells before sounding the War Horn
</h2>

The completed breakout generated two separate smoke requests.

Buy two Smoke Shells after the eight-unit requirement is confirmed. Do not buy
four as insurance; extra smoke contributed to the inefficient resource result
in the documented run.

Before sounding the horn:

- confirm both coastal batteries are destroyed;
- confirm the eight-unit requirement;
- clear old destructive firing lines;
- keep the current Iron Nest marker;
- load or stage two Smoke Shells;
- preserve enough charges for two shots around 11 km.

Now sound the War Horn to commence the breakout.

Do not precompute the Smoke Shell solutions. The exact coordinates arrive only
after the advance begins.

<h2 id="second-smoke-request">
  Recalculate the second Smoke Shell at K8 5:1
</h2>

The second request was:

- coordinate: **K8 5:1**
- bearing: **103.5°**
- range: **10.80 km**
- elevation: **43.20°**

The second coordinate is closer and uses a different bearing and elevation.

Do not reuse the first Smoke Shell firing card merely because both requests
belong to the same breakout.

After the second impact, wait for Mendoza's final report rather than returning
to destructive fire.

  Stand down after Breakout Successful
</h2>

The completion order confirms:

- `Breakout successful`
- the city is held;
- Iron Nest may stand down;
- remaining enemy elements are no longer objectives;
- continued fire is optional.

Stand down immediately on the safe route.

Continuing to fire after this message can:

- consume ammunition that no longer advances the objective;
- endanger the units that just completed the breakout;
- weaken the minimum-assets result;
- damage an otherwise clean casualty result.

The mission-completion achievement is **Breakout**. The separate
**Naval Commendation** requires Gold on every Cartagena medal, but the exact Gold
thresholds were not confirmed in the available run.

<h2 id="cartagena-resource-decisions">
  Preserve requisition for the stages that can force recovery
</h2>

Cartagena is one of the few missions where resource planning deserves its own
task-specific check.

The operation may require:

- three opening H shells;
- two AP shells;
- reconnaissance over the coastal search area;
- Emergency Move;
- two Position Reports after moving;
- replacement AP shells after an incorrect volley;
- destructive shells for eight additional units;
- two Smoke Shells.

Do not spend the opening budget on:

- speculative AP for the concealed installations;
- repeated shells at broad south or southeast estimates;
- overlapping reconnaissance flights;
- more than two Smoke Shells;
- optional targets after breakout success.

A completed but inefficient run used two unnecessary Smoke Shells, missed
multiple AP shots, and moved Iron Nest twice. It completed the mission but did
not represent a minimum-assets route.

<h2 id="cartagena-stuck">
  Siege of Cartagena mistakes that derail the mission
</h2>

### Continuing the chain from an uncertain signal station

The assembly area and field command depend on earlier calculated positions.
Verify the first marker before continuing.

### Measuring 2.41 km from the wrong point

The field command uses 235° from the signal station and 2.41 km from the
assembly area.

### Treating south and southeast as exact firing bearings

Use them to define coastal search sectors, then confirm the battery icons.

### Firing at broad-direction endpoints

Both batteries may already be visible in developed reconnaissance.

### Using H against the coastal batteries

The battery targets require precise AP hits.

### Reversing the left and right AP cards

Write the gun side directly on both firing cards before loading.

### Waiting too long to use Emergency Move

Move when another reliable volley cannot be completed before the CBT impact.

### Firing after only one Position Report

One report gives a line of possible Nest positions, not a precise firing origin.

### Reusing pre-move ranges and elevations

Keep the battery markers, but recalculate every firing value from the new Nest.

### Clearing every enemy before the War Horn

Only eight additional units are required to unlock the breakout.

### Buying more than two Smoke Shells

The documented route generated two support requests.

### Reusing the first smoke solution

K6 8:5 and K8 5:1 require separate firing cards.

### Continuing after Mendoza authorizes stand-down

Remaining enemies are optional once the breakout succeeds.
