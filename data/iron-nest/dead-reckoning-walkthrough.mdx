<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
**Mission 9 — Dead Reckoning is a reveal-first mission.** The Enemy Signal
Station, Enemy Assembly Area, and Enemy Field Command begin hidden inside
structures, so ordinary reconnaissance cannot confirm them.

Use the mission reports to narrow each target to a suspected area, then fire a
**Tear Shell** into that area. Check the updated map before buying the
destructive shell: once the target is exposed, its icon tells you whether a
normal destructive shot is enough or whether the position needs AP.

Do not sound the War Horn after only two primary targets. The Field Command can
be the real trap. In one operation, a **1.86 km northeast** correction only
became useful after the first two primary targets were destroyed.

Once all three are confirmed destroyed, sound the War Horn. That completes the
required route. Any hidden enemies reported afterward are optional unless you
are pursuing the full-elimination medals.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

<h2 id="dead-reckoning-route">
  Mission 9: Dead Reckoning route at a glance
</h2>

Dead Reckoning was the first mission where I could spend a lot of time looking
at a perfectly clear map and still learn almost nothing.

That is intentional.

The three primary positions are dug into structures and are not supposed to
appear through normal reconnaissance until the mission's reveal condition is
satisfied.

My clean route is:

1. Read the entire order before buying shells.
2. Mark the current Iron Nest position and all three spotters.
3. Build a suspected area for the Enemy Signal Station.
4. Build a suspected area for the Enemy Assembly Area.
5. Keep the Enemy Field Command as an approximate search area if its reports do
   not intersect cleanly.
6. Fire Tear Shells into the first two useful search areas.
7. Check the updated map after each Tear impact.
8. Identify the actual revealed target icon before buying H, AP, or another
   destructive shell.
9. Destroy the first two confirmed primary targets.
10. Recheck the latest impact information before searching for Field Command.
11. Apply any new correction to the last useful impact point.
12. Use another Tear Shell to expose the third position.
13. Destroy Field Command with the shell required by its revealed target state.
14. Confirm all three primary targets are destroyed.
15. Sound the War Horn.
16. Stand down, or continue only if you are deliberately pursuing the optional
   hidden enemies.

The <Link href="/iron-nest">Iron Nest guide hub</Link> already covers the
general map, bearing, range, calculator, ammunition, and requisition systems.
Dead Reckoning is about **when a target is knowable**, not how the basic
calculator works.

<h2 id="why-recon-shows-nothing">
  Why normal reconnaissance shows nothing
</h2>

The mission order is unusually explicit:

- the garrison has gone to ground;
- the positions are hidden in cellars and stonework;
- the three primary locations cannot initially be identified normally;
- Tear Shells force hidden units into the open;
- all three primary positions must be destroyed before the advance begins.

That changes my normal reconnaissance habit.

I do **not** start Dead Reckoning by buying scout flights across the whole city.

If the target is still in its hidden state, another ordinary photograph does
not solve the underlying problem.

Instead, I use the field reports to answer a different question:

> Where is the smallest believable area in which I can place a Tear Shell?

That area does not need to be accurate enough for AP yet.

It only needs to be accurate enough for the Tear effect to reach the hidden
position.

<h2 id="mark-three-primary-areas">
  Mark three search areas, not three fake exact coordinates
</h2>

The targets are:

- Enemy Signal Station;
- Enemy Assembly Area;
- Enemy Field Command.

Their actual reports can change between operations.

For example, one operation reported the Signal Station active somewhere in
**M2**. Another operation placed the same target in **G5**.

That is enough variation that I would never copy a fixed online coordinate for
this mission.

The Signal Station may begin with only a broad grid.

The Assembly Area usually has several constraints, such as:

- a distance from one spotter;
- a bearing from another;
- a general cardinal direction from a third.

Field Command can be even less comfortable, with a long distance and multiple
directional descriptions that define an area rather than one perfect point.

This distinction matters.

When three approximate lines form a small triangle instead of one perfect
intersection, I place the Tear target near the center of the most plausible
overlap.

I do **not** buy AP just because one of those points looks like a bunker.

The reveal happens first.

The destructive-shell decision comes second.

<h2 id="reveal-first-two-targets">
  Reveal the first two primary targets before solving Field Command
</h2>

The most reliable route I found was to work on the two better-defined targets
first.

I usually begin with:

1. Signal Station;
2. Assembly Area.

These two give me something concrete to progress while Field Command is still
ambiguous.

For each one:

1. Mark the center of the suspected area.
2. Draw a normal Iron Nest-to-area firing line.
3. Calculate the Tear Shell shot.
4. Fire one Tear Shell.
5. Wait for the map to update.
6. Look for a newly exposed hostile icon.
7. Replace the broad search marker with the confirmed target marker.

This is where I decide what to buy next.

Do not purchase three AP shells, three H shells, and three Tear Shells at the
start just because there are three primary objectives.

After reveal, the target icon can tell a different story.

<h2 id="buy-after-reveal">
  Buy the destructive shell after the target is visible
</h2>

The Tear Shell solves the **location problem**.

The revealed icon solves the **ammunition problem**.

After every reveal I check:

- Is the target underground?
- Does it show an armored or protected indicator?
- Is it ordinary infantry?
- Is another hostile standing inside the same effect area?
- Are friendly positions close enough to make a large blast risky?

One operation revealed targets with an underground-style marker that was easy
to miss because the hostile diamond sat over the smaller structural symbol.

That matters because I initially read it as normal infantry.

If the position is confirmed underground or hardened, I use AP.

If it is normal exposed infantry or another soft target, I use the smallest
destructive option that safely covers it.

The key buying rule for Dead Reckoning is:

> **Tear first. Read the icon. Buy second.**

That saves more requisition than trying to predict all three destructive shell
types from the briefing.

<h2 id="blind-fire">
  Can you destroy a hidden target without using Tear first?
</h2>

Yes — but I would not build the route around it.

The order says that being invisible to reconnaissance does not make the target
immune to artillery.

I tested that logic in another operation.

A destructive shot fired into the suspected Signal Station area **did destroy
the hidden Signal Station without revealing it first**.

That proves the mission text is literal.

It does **not** make blind fire the better route.

The remaining primary targets still forced me back to Tear Shells because I did
not know their precise positions.

Blind fire is worth considering only when:

- the target area is unusually small;
- I already have a highly confident intersection;
- I deliberately want to test a low-requisition route;
- missing does not compromise a medal route I care about.

I go back to Tear Shells when:

- the suspected area spans multiple blocks;
- AP would have too little radius to forgive an error;
- two or more plausible positions remain;
- the target must be distinguished from nearby infantry;
- I am starting to fire at the map instead of at an actual solution.

<h2 id="third-target-missing">
  The real Dead Reckoning trap: the third target looks missing
</h2>

This is the part that consumed the most time.

I had:

- repeatedly covered the suspected Field Command area;
- checked several reconnaissance results;
- re-read the original spotter reports;
- found and destroyed the other primary positions;

and Field Command still did not appear.

At that point, firing more Tear Shells across the entire map only made the map
messier and destroyed the minimum-assets route.

The useful trigger came **after the first two primary targets were confirmed
destroyed**.

A new correction tied to the previous northwest impact became relevant.

In that operation it said:

**1.86 km northeast of the last hit.**

This is the decision point I would use now:

**If Target 3 is missing, do not immediately buy another scout flight.**

First:

1. Confirm Signal Station is destroyed.
2. Confirm Assembly Area is destroyed.
3. Return to the latest relevant impact.
4. Look for a correction arrow or correction distance.
5. Apply that correction from the **impact point**, not from Iron Nest.
6. Fire the next Tear Shell at the corrected area.

The `1.86 km northeast` value came from one operation, so I would not assume
your run will print exactly the same number.

The important mechanic is that the late correction can be more useful than the
opening Field Command estimates.

<h2 id="reveal-field-command">
  Reveal Field Command before buying its final shell
</h2>

After applying the correction and using another Tear Shell, the missing
position finally appeared in the documented run.

At that point the target state made the next decision much easier: it presented
as a hardened or underground position, so AP became the sensible final shot.

    Once Field Command is visible, stop using area guesses. Mark the exact icon
    and solve a new precise firing line from Iron Nest.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

This is when I buy the final AP shell.

Not earlier.

My final sequence is:

1. Replace the Tear search marker with the revealed Field Command marker.
2. Delete obsolete search lines.
3. Draw a new line from the current Iron Nest position.
4. Measure the actual bearing and range.
5. Calculate the required charges and elevation.
6. Load AP.
7. Verify the physical charge count.
8. Fire one precise shot.
9. Wait for the destruction confirmation.

Do not reuse the Tear Shell firing card.

The Tear target was the center of a reveal area.

The AP target is the newly confirmed structure inside that area.

<h2 id="when-to-sound-war-horn">
  Do not touch the War Horn until all three primaries are dead
</h2>

The required primary targets are:

- Enemy Signal Station;
- Enemy Assembly Area;
- Enemy Field Command.

The mission specifically warns that sounding the War Horn before all three are
destroyed sends allied troops into contested ground.

I therefore ignore the horn until I have three separate destruction
confirmations.

Do not use:

- three explosions;
- three spent Tear Shells;
- three target markers;
- three reconnaissance areas

as a substitute for those confirmations.

Only after the third primary is dead do I sound the War Horn.

The resulting message confirms that the garrison has been crippled and the
primary objective is complete.

<h2 id="optional-hidden-enemies">
  The hidden enemies after the War Horn are optional
</h2>

The mission can reveal another group of hidden enemies after the advance begins.

The number is not necessarily identical in every clear.

One operation reported:

**10 enemies remain hidden throughout the city.**

Another operation reported:

**7 enemies remain hidden.**

The second run had already killed additional enemies before the War Horn, which
explains why I do not treat “10 remaining” as a fixed objective value.

For a normal clear, I stop here.

That is the point where continuing to buy Tear Shells stops being mission
progress and becomes medal cleanup.

For the optional route, command allows Forward Observers to help locate
remaining hidden enemies.

I only start spending again when I have deliberately chosen that route.

<h2 id="optional-cleanup">
  How I approach the optional cleanup
</h2>

The optional phase is not worth playing like the opening three primaries.

I no longer have three named installations to solve.

Instead, I have hidden enemy groups scattered around the city.

My cleanup process is:

1. Clear old primary-target construction lines.
2. Keep the current Iron Nest marker.
3. Use the remaining-enemy count as a progress check.
4. Requisition a Forward Observer when I need the nearest enemy direction.
5. Use Tear Shells over the part of the city where hidden groups are still
   suspected.
6. Wait for the reveal before buying destructive ammunition.
7. Group exposed enemies only when one safe shell can actually cover them.
8. Stop when the remaining-enemy count reaches the goal I am pursuing.

I do not blanket every district with Tear Shells before the War Horn.

One operation did exactly that and still had new hidden enemies appear after the
primary phase changed.

That is strong evidence that the optional enemies should be treated as a
**new mission state**, not as targets that must all be revealed during the
opening stage.

<h2 id="dead-reckoning-requisition">
  The minimum-assets route is mostly about waiting
</h2>

Dead Reckoning punishes speculative purchases more than most earlier missions.

The expensive mistakes are:

- buying ordinary reconnaissance before Tear has changed the target state;
- firing multiple Tear Shells at the same uncertain Field Command area;
- purchasing AP before seeing whether the revealed target needs it;
- blind-firing several buildings just because one blind shot worked;
- trying to uncover the optional enemies before the War Horn;
- continuing optional cleanup after deciding that mission completion is enough.

My lean route is:

- buy Tear Shells for the two primary areas I can actually justify;
- destroy those revealed targets;
- stop and re-read the map before spending on Field Command;
- use the late correction if it appears;
- buy the final destructive shell only after Field Command is visible;
- sound the horn;
- stand down.

The completion achievement is **Steady Hands**.

**Quiet Precision** requires Gold on all Dead Reckoning medals, but the exact
Gold thresholds are not exposed clearly enough for me to claim a fully verified
minimum-assets formula.

<h2 id="dead-reckoning-stuck">
  Dead Reckoning mistakes that make the mission feel broken
</h2>

### Buying scout flights because nothing appears

The opening primary targets are deliberately hidden. Use Tear Shells to change
their state first.

### Treating the broad grid as the final firing coordinate

A report such as `M2` only gives a Tear search area. It is not a precise AP
target.

### Trying to solve all three exact coordinates before firing anything

The third target can become easier only after the earlier primary targets are
destroyed.

### Buying AP before the Tear reveal

Wait until the icon confirms that the target is hardened or underground.

### Assuming blind fire means Tear Shells are unnecessary

One hidden Signal Station was destroyed by blind fire, but the remaining
primary targets still required the reveal workflow.

### Spamming Tear Shells when Field Command is missing

Confirm the first two kills and recheck the latest impact for a new correction.

### Measuring the 1.86 km correction from Iron Nest

That correction belongs to the previous impact point.

### Copying 1.86 km as a universal answer

It came from one operation. Use the correction printed in your own run.

### Reusing the Tear Shell firing card for AP

The reveal area and the confirmed underground target are not necessarily the
same exact point.

### Sounding the War Horn after two kills

The advance is safe only after Signal Station, Assembly Area, and Field Command
are all confirmed destroyed.

### Assuming ten hidden enemies are always left afterward

One completed route reported ten; another reported seven after additional
pre-horn kills.

### Trying to reveal the optional enemies before the phase changes

Some hidden enemies only become relevant after the primary objective is
complete.
