<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
**Do not fire until all three opening artillery positions are solved.** Prepare
one firing card for each target, load both guns, and arrange the opening shots
by turret bearing.

The Counter-Battery Timer begins when the **first shell lands**, not when the
first shell is fired. That lets you put two shells in the air before the
300-second countdown starts. Each confirmed artillery kill extends the timer.

Destroying the three opening guns does not finish the mission. Other hostile
units remain, and a newly deployed battery can appear later when prior
reconnaissance is no longer reliable. Clear that secondary battery, then destroy
the final hostiles before standing down.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

<h2 id="counter-battery-route">
  Counter-Battery route at a glance
</h2>

Counter-Battery becomes difficult when I treat the first visible artillery
position as permission to start shooting.

The opening order identifies three guns, but the timer does not begin
immediately. The safest route is:

1. Restore power and read the complete order.
2. Mark Iron Nest and the three observation assets.
3. Locate all three opening artillery positions.
4. Measure all three final firing lines from Iron Nest.
5. Calculate and label all three firing cards.
6. Load one prepared shell into each gun.
7. Fire both guns before the first shell lands.
8. Reload for the third artillery position.
9. Clear the remaining non-artillery hostiles.
10. Respond to the later secondary battery with fresh information.
11. Destroy the final hostiles and wait for `Sector is clear`.

The general coordinate, triangulation, calculator, and loading controls remain
in the <Link href="/iron-nest">Iron Nest guide hub</Link> This page focuses on
what changes once the mission introduces an active Counter-Battery Timer.

<h2 id="read-the-opening-order">
  Read the opening order before triggering the timer
</h2>

Go to the teleprinter before buying ammunition or touching the firing lever.

The opening message tells you:

- enemy artillery has deployed across the river;
- all identified artillery assets take priority;
- three opening positions can be solved from field reports;
- the Counter-Battery Timer begins when the first shell lands;
- other hostile units can be cleared after the immediate battery is gone.

That final distinction is important.

The artillery controls the CBT, but artillery is not the only thing that counts
toward mission completion. If the timer stops and the mission does not end, the
game is usually waiting for the remaining hostile units rather than another
shot at an already destroyed gun.

Do not spend requisition on an opening reconnaissance flight. The three initial
artillery positions already have field-intelligence solutions.

<h2 id="mark-three-artillery-positions">
  Mark all three opening artillery positions
</h2>

Place the current Iron Nest marker first, followed by the three spotters printed
in your order.

Then solve Artillery 1, Artillery 2, and Artillery 3 from their own reported
bearings or distances. Keep a numbered target marker for each position and
remove the temporary construction lines after the position is confirmed.

Do not copy another run's target coordinates.

In one operation, Iron Nest was at approximately **I6 5:3**, with observation
assets around **P10 4:3**, **L5 1:2**, and **K1 8:2**. Another operation used
different spotter coordinates while keeping the same mission structure.

The useful information is the method:

- field lines begin at the named spotters;
- the final red firing line begins at Iron Nest;
- each artillery marker needs its own firing card.

Counter-Battery assumes that you already know how to build the target point and
adds time pressure after the first impact.

<h2 id="calculate-three-opening-shots">
  Calculate all three opening shots before firing
</h2>

Once all three artillery markers are visible, draw one final firing line from
Iron Nest to each target.

If I already have the Iron Nest and target coordinates, I can use the
<Link href="/iron-nest/fire-mission-helper">
  {" "}Fire Mission Helper
</Link>
to turn each confirmed artillery position into bearing, range, powder charge,
and elevation before I assign the three cards to the guns.

For every shot, record:

- target number;
- turret bearing;
- range;
- shell;
- powder-charge count;
- elevation;
- intended gun side.

One operation produced these three opening solutions:

- **85.2° / 10.13 km / 3 charges / 40.52° elevation**
- **87.6° / 5.61 km / 2 charges / 33.66° elevation**
- **99.1° / 8.65 km / 2 charges / 51.90° elevation**

Those numbers belong to one operation. Your targets can move.

Their value is showing why I finish all three calculations first. Two shots used
two charges, while the 10.13 km shot used three. Remembering only the elevations
without matching them to the correct physical charge count is enough to send a
shell short.

Write the target number or gun side directly on each card. Three cards with
similar bearings are easy to reverse once the timer is running.

<h2 id="choose-opening-order">
  Choose the firing order by turret movement
</h2>

Do not automatically start with the nearest target.

Compare the three bearings and choose an order that keeps the turret moving in
one direction. In the example operation, an efficient sweep was:

1. **85.2°**
2. **87.6°**
3. **99.1°**

The first two targets were only 2.4° apart. That made them suitable for the two
preloaded guns. The third target required a larger rotation but could be handled
after the first reload.

My opening preparation is:

- left gun loaded for the first bearing;
- right gun loaded for the second bearing;
- both elevations set before firing;
- third shell and charge count written down;
- turret already aligned with the first target.

Do not load both guns and then forget which card belongs to which side. Check
the displayed left/right elevation before leaving the gun controls.

<h2 id="preload-both-guns">
  Preload both guns before the first impact
</h2>

The first shell's flight time is part of the mission's free preparation window.

Once both guns are ready:

1. Rotate to the first bearing.
2. Fire the first gun.
3. Rotate to the second bearing.
4. Fire the second gun while the first shell is still travelling.
5. Begin loading the third prepared shot.
6. Wait for the first destruction confirmation without stopping the reload.

Do not stand at the observation window to watch the first impact. The useful
action during that flight is firing the second gun and preparing the third
shell.

Before pulling either lever, verify the physical controls:

- correct shell loaded;
- correct charge count;
- correct gun selected;
- correct bearing;
- correct elevation for that gun.

A clipboard can show the right firing solution while the loader still contains
the wrong number of charges.

<h2 id="start-cbt-on-your-terms">
  Start the CBT on your terms
</h2>

The timer begins when the first shell lands.

In one clear, the first impact opened the Counter-Battery Timer at **300
seconds**. The next confirmed artillery kill immediately produced an
`Artillery destroyed — CBT extended` message.

  />
  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">
    Every opening artillery kill removes a threat and adds time, so the cleanest
    first volley creates its own recovery margin.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

After each confirmation:

1. Mark that artillery position as destroyed.
2. Check the timer extension.
3. Continue to the next prepared bearing.
4. Keep infantry and unrelated icons out of the opening sequence.

Do not interrupt the third artillery shot to clear a nearby infantry marker.
The ordinary unit may remain after the CBT stops, while an active artillery
piece continues to threaten the Nest.

<h2 id="after-opening-battery">
  What happens after the opening battery is destroyed
</h2>

Destroying the three identified artillery positions removes the immediate
Counter-Battery threat, but it does not necessarily complete the mission.

In one operation, the opening battery was cleared with **249 seconds**
remaining. The teleprinter then reported:

- `Counter-battery assets destroyed`
- `4 hostile units remain across the river`

The countdown stopped, but the mission continued.

This is the point to slow down.

Clear the completed artillery markers, inspect the latest reconnaissance, and
identify the remaining enemy icons. Do not continue firing at the old battery
coordinates simply because the mission banner has not appeared.

For the remaining visible hostiles:

- calculate fresh lines from Iron Nest;
- check whether close units can be covered safely;
- prepare both guns when two independent targets are known;
- buy only the shells needed for confirmed positions.

Preserve some requisition. The mission can deploy another artillery battery
after these visible units are engaged.

<h2 id="fresh-artillery">
  Find fresh artillery when prior reconnaissance is unreliable
</h2>

Later, the teleprinter can issue another Counter-Battery warning.

The important lines are:

- forward observers have no actionable fix;
- the hostile battery deployed recently;
- prior reconnaissance is not reliable;
- the reconnaissance card remains active;
- Star Shell ordnance is authorized.

Do not keep searching old photographs for a target that did not exist when
those photographs were taken.

My search order is:

1. Clear destroyed markers and expired firing lines.
2. Inspect the newest developed map sections.
3. Mark any newly visible artillery icons.
4. Identify the largest enemy-side strip not covered by current imagery.
5. Use the active reconnaissance card over that uncovered area.
6. Use a Star Shell only when a meaningful gap remains after the flight.

Do not spend several reconnaissance requests on overlapping strips. Each new
image should remove a large uncertain area.

Once a fresh artillery position is confirmed, treat it like a new firing
solution:

- new target marker;
- new Iron Nest-to-target line;
- new range;
- new charge count;
- new elevation.

The fact that the turret is already pointing across the river does not make an
old firing card reusable.

<h2 id="secondary-battery-and-final-hostiles">
  Clear the secondary battery and the final two hostiles
</h2>

After the newly deployed artillery is destroyed, the teleprinter can report:

- `Secondary counter-battery assets destroyed`
- `2 hostile units remain across the river`
- no further threats are anticipated

That message means the CBT problem is over, but the mission still needs two
ordinary targets removed.

Prepare the final pair before firing.

In one clear, the final two shots were close in bearing:

- approximately **112.6° bearing / 40.84° elevation**
- approximately **118.5° bearing / 42.64° elevation**
- three charges on both firing cards

That made a two-gun finish safer than firing one gun, walking back to the
loader, and rebuilding the second shot from memory.

For the final pair:

1. Mark both targets clearly.
2. Calculate both solutions.
3. Assign one card to each gun.
4. Load and elevate both guns.
5. Fire the first target.
6. Rotate directly to the second.
7. Fire the second target.
8. Wait for both destruction confirmations.

Read the last elevation digit carefully. Two nearby bearings can still require
different elevations.

<h2 id="counter-battery-completion">
  Wait for the sector-clear message
</h2>

Counter-Battery is complete only when the teleprinter reports:

- all hostile assets destroyed;
- sector clear;
- Iron Nest is to stand down.

Stop buying ammunition as soon as that message appears. Clear the active firing
cards, close the gun controls, and return to the bunk.

Do not keep firing at map icons after stand-down merely to empty the magazines.
Those shots no longer help mission completion and can damage an otherwise clean
resource result.

<h2 id="counter-battery-stuck">
  Counter-Battery mistakes that block or weaken the clear
</h2>

### Starting with only one artillery solution

The first impact starts the timer. Locate and calculate all three opening guns
before firing.

### Watching the first shell land

Use its flight time to fire the second preloaded gun and begin the third reload.

### Copying the example bearings

The `85.2°`, `87.6°`, and `99.1°` values belong to one operation. Use your own
field reports.

### Loading the charge count from the wrong card

One failed shot used one charge fewer than its prepared solution and landed
short. Check the physical charge counter before closing the loader.

### Treating 249 seconds as the end of the mission

The opening battery can be gone while four other hostile units remain.

### Looking for the secondary battery in old photographs

The order explicitly says prior reconnaissance is unreliable. Photograph the
uncovered area again.

### Leaving destroyed artillery markers on the map

Old icons and lines make the fresh battery look like a target you already
solved.

### Spending the reserve before the second warning

The later phase may require reconnaissance, Star Shell scouting, replacement
ammunition, and fresh powder charges.

### Standing down after secondary counter-battery is destroyed

Read the next line. Two ordinary hostile units can still remain after the new
battery is gone.
