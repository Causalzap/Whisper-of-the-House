<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
**Mission 10 — Fire on Call is about answering the support request you have now,
not clearing Sevilla in advance.**

Read the briefing, mark the current Iron Nest position, keep **Smoke, Tear, and
H** available, then sound the War Horn. Each frontline call gives you a friendly
unit, an enemy problem, a requested shell or sequence, and a deadline.

For a hidden strongpoint, follow the requested **Tear first → H second** order.
For a friendly unit sitting too close to the requested destructive impact,
place Smoke somewhere safer first; the allied unit will move to the Smoke impact
point.

Do not blindly obey an H request when the blast would clearly hit your own
troops. One successful operation replaced an extremely danger-close H shot with
**AP**, destroyed the mechanized target, preserved the friendly unit, and still
completed the mission.

Stop when command reports that Sevilla has fallen and the **primary objective is
complete**. Remaining enemies are optional.

<h2 id="fire-on-call-route">
  Mission 10: Fire on Call route at a glance
</h2>

Fire on Call is easier when I stop treating Sevilla like a map I need to clear.

The city contains plenty of visible and hidden enemies, but the mission does not
ask me to flatten every red icon before the infantry advances.

It asks me to support the units that call.

My route is:

1. Read the full assault order.
2. Mark the current Iron Nest position.
3. Keep H, Tear, Smoke, and enough powder available.
4. Do not spend requisition searching the entire city.
5. Sound the War Horn.
6. Read each new frontline message completely.
7. Mark the friendly unit before marking the requested impact.
8. Check whether the call needs H, Smoke, or Tear followed by H.
9. Move friendlies with Smoke before destructive fire when the requested point
   is unsafe.
10. Keep one gun flexible when I do not yet know the next support call.
11. Use AP instead of a large H blast when a precise danger-close hit is clearly
   safer.
12. Wait for the unit's confirmation before deleting its markers.
13. Repeat until command reports that the city has fallen.
14. Stand down unless I deliberately want optional cleanup.

The <Link href="/iron-nest">Iron Nest guide hub</Link> covers the shared firing
controls and ammunition roles. Fire on Call is about **sequencing support calls
while the friendly line is moving**.

<h2 id="prepare-before-war-horn">
  Prepare before sounding the War Horn, but do not solve fake targets
</h2>

The briefing tells me enough to prepare the Nest, but not enough to prepare every
shot.

It establishes that:

- the frontline waits for my signal;
- support calls begin only after the War Horn;
- some enemies are hidden inside buildings;
- lethal shells near friendly units cause casualties;
- Smoke Shells can reposition allied units;
- the assault succeeds by answering calls quickly and correctly.

That means I prepare **capability**, not firing solutions.

Before using the horn, I want:

- the current Iron Nest marker on the map;
- a clean map without Dead Reckoning construction lines;
- powder available;
- at least one normal destructive option;
- Smoke available;
- Tear available for hidden-building requests;
- both guns empty or deliberately assigned.

I do **not** map out random enemy formations and begin firing before the
assault.

One run considered spreading Tear across Sevilla before sounding the horn, but
the problem is obvious once the mission starts: support requests are generated
around the advancing allied units, and their positions change as they push
through the city. 

A pre-fired shell can solve the wrong problem and spend the shell I need five
minutes later.

<h2 id="sound-war-horn">
  Sound the War Horn only when the Nest is ready to answer
</h2>

Once the map is clean and I have basic ammunition available, I sound the War
Horn.

The mission immediately changes from planning to response.

Each call can give me some combination of:

- the friendly unit making the request;
- its current coordinate;
- the enemy's direction or relative position;
- the requested shell;
- an ordered sequence such as `Tear first, then H`;
- a deadline.

The first thing I mark is the **friendly unit**.

That sounds backwards in an artillery game, but it prevents the most expensive
mistake in this mission: building a technically correct firing solution without
noticing that my own troops sit inside the effect area.

Do not reuse the previous unit's marker just because the next call comes from

The advancing line changes throughout the mission.

and elevation while I keep the rest of the deadline for loading and checking
friendly safety.

<h2 id="tear-then-h">
  When the order says Tear first, then H, treat it as two different shots
</h2>

One of the first major support patterns is a friendly position pinned by a
strongpoint inside a building.

The order can explicitly require:

**Tear first → H second**

The Tear Shell is not the kill shot.

It exposes or forces out the hidden position so the destructive follow-up has a
real target.

In one completed operation, the first strongpoint call supplied a frontline
position around **K4**, followed by a bearing toward the hidden position and a
deadline. The Tear solution was prepared first, then a separate H solution was
built for the revealed target.

    Tear and H belong to two stages of the same support call. I do not treat the
    original approximate point as the final destructive target.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

My sequence is:

1. Mark the friendly caller.
2. Build the hidden strongpoint area from the report.
3. Calculate the Tear shot.
4. Fire Tear.
5. Wait for the target state to update.
6. Confirm the exposed enemy point.
7. Build a fresh H firing card.
8. Fire H.
9. Wait for the frontline confirmation.

Do not fire the H shell immediately behind the Tear shell just to save time.

The reason to use Tear first is to gain better target information.

If I fire both before the reveal exists, I have paid for the information and
then ignored it.

<h2 id="keep-second-gun-free">
  Keeping one gun free is often better than preloading both
</h2>

Earlier timed missions rewarded loading both guns before starting.

Fire on Call is different.

I do not always know whether the next request will need:

- H;
- Smoke;
- Tear;
- a precise danger-close shot;
- two stages in sequence.

During one strongpoint call, I could have prepared both sides for Tear and H,
but leaving the second gun free also meant I could react immediately if another
support request arrived before the first sequence was complete.

My normal choice is:

**One active call with plenty of time:**  
keep one gun flexible.

**A confirmed Tear → H sequence with no competing call:**  
prepare Tear on one side and H on the other.

**Two simultaneous known requests:**  
assign one card and shell to each side.

The mistake is not preloading.

The mistake is filling both guns with ammunition for a situation the mission has
not asked for yet.

<h2 id="move-friendlies-with-smoke">
  Use Smoke to move a friendly unit out of your H-shell blast
</h2>

This is the most important Fire on Call mechanic.

One support call requested H against an enemy position that was effectively on
top of a friendly formation.

The calculated target was valid.

The shot was not safe.

Instead of firing H immediately, I placed a **Smoke Shell at a safer nearby
point**.

The allied formation moved to the Smoke impact.

Only then did I fire the requested H shell at the original enemy position.

One operation's Smoke relocation used approximately:

- **7.31 km range**
- **216.2° bearing**
- **2 charges**
- **43.86° elevation**

The destructive follow-up used a different point and approximately
**45.18° elevation**.

Those values belong to that operation.

The reusable decision is:

> If the requested destructive effect overlaps a friendly position, move the
> friendly first instead of moving the enemy aim point until the shot stops
> solving the requested target.

This is much safer than simply offsetting the H marker and hoping the edge of
the blast still reaches the enemy.

<h2 id="smoke-can-move-more-than-one-unit">
  Check the new friendly positions after Smoke lands
</h2>

There is another reason I never queue the destructive follow-up blindly.

In one call, I expected Smoke to move one threatened unit.

Instead, **several nearby allied units shifted toward the smoke point**.

That turned the next H shot into a new danger-close problem.

The important moment is immediately after Smoke impact:

1. wait for the map update;
2. recheck every nearby blue icon;
3. confirm the original target is still hostile;
4. recheck the effect area;
5. only then fire the destructive round.

A Smoke Shell can fix one collision and create another if I assume only the
named caller will move.

This was one of the closest calls in the completed route: the H blast landed
very near the relocated friendlies, but the unit survived and the support call
completed successfully.

<h2 id="direct-smoke-calls">
  Some support calls only need Smoke
</h2>

Not every Smoke request is a setup for destructive fire.

The frontline can ask for Smoke at a direct coordinate simply to continue its
movement.

One operation received a request around **I8 8:1**.

The resulting Smoke solution was approximately:

- **258.8° bearing**
- **8.26 km range**
- **49.56° elevation**

Another later Smoke request used a completely different coordinate and firing
solution.

Do not follow every Smoke impact with destructive fire because enemy icons are

The support mission is complete when the frontline says it is complete.

Then I clear that firing card and wait for the next request.

<h2 id="hidden-strongpoint-later">
  Hidden strongpoints can appear again later in the assault
</h2>

Fire on Call does not teach Tear once and then stop using it.

A later unit can report being pinned by a strongpoint and explicitly request:

**Tear Shell first, then H Shell**

One operation received such a call while the friendly unit was around
**N3 9:1**.

The eventual firing line for the hidden position was approximately:

- **8.55 km**
- **209.7° bearing**
- **2 charges**
- about **51° elevation**

After the Tear impact, the exposed target justified the destructive follow-up.

That is why I keep at least one Tear option available during the assault.

I do not buy several Tear Shells at mission start, but I also do not spend the
last one on optional city cleanup while the primary objective is active.

<h2 id="danger-close">
  A requested H shell is not permission to kill your own troops
</h2>

The most important judgment call came near the end of the assault.

Spotter 3 reported taking fire.

The enemy position was only about **0.25 km** from the friendly position.

The request called for H.

At that spacing, the H effect area was clearly too large.

<h2 id="use-ap-danger-close">
  Use AP when precision matters more than the requested blast radius
</h2>

I would not turn this into a universal rule that says:

> Fire on Call H requests should always be replaced with AP.

Most should not.

I change to AP only when:

- the enemy point is precise;
- the friendly point is precise;
- the H effect clearly overlaps the friendly formation;
- the target can be killed by a direct precise hit;
- the AP impact stays outside the friendly position.

**Which shell gives me enough effect without touching the blue marker?**

<h2 id="deadlines">
  The deadlines are generous enough to stop and check the map
</h2>

Several calls in the completed operations gave roughly **seven or eight minutes**
to respond.

That is enough time to:

- read the full order;
- mark the caller;
- calculate one or two shots;
- reload;
- correct a dangerous friendly overlap.

The mission feels urgent because the message is live, but the correct response
is not to sprint through every station.

I use the deadline this way:

**More than several minutes remain:**  
verify the map before buying or firing.

**Tear impact still pending:**  
wait for the reveal instead of firing H at the approximate point.

**Friendly unit overlaps the effect area:**  
spend the extra minute on Smoke or a smaller precise shell.

**Firing card is ready:**  
load now rather than waiting for the last minute.

The timers create priorities.

They do not remove the need to make the shot safe.

<h2 id="fire-on-call-completion">
  Stop when Sevilla falls
</h2>

After the final required support call succeeds, command reports:

- the city has fallen;
- the southern front closes;
- the primary objective is complete;
- Iron Nest may stand down;
- street fighting continues below;
- remaining enemy elements are no longer required targets;
- additional fire support is optional.

This is where I stop on an efficiency route.

I do not fire at another cluster merely because the map still contains enemies.

At this stage:

- allied units are already throughout the city;
- every extra area shell creates casualty risk;
- every extra purchase hurts resource efficiency;
- none of those enemies are required for normal completion.

<h2 id="optional-cleanup">
  Optional cleanup is only worth it when one shot has a clear payoff
</h2>

Continued fire is authorized after completion.

That does not mean it is automatically worthwhile.

I consider another shot when:

- several confirmed enemies fit inside one safe impact area;
- no friendly formations are nearby;
- the kill supports a medal condition I am deliberately pursuing;
- the requisition return or target value justifies the shell.

I stop when:

- only scattered infantry remain;
- the friendly line has moved into the same blocks;
- I need another reconnaissance purchase just to find something to shoot;
- the mission already produced the result I wanted.

One completed route continued firing after the primary objective and destroyed
several clustered enemies, but that is optional optimization rather than the
answer to Fire on Call.

<h2 id="fire-on-call-medals">
  An all-medal clear does not require flattening Sevilla first
</h2>

One complete Fire on Call operation reached the result screen with
**all medals earned**.

That run did not begin by pre-clearing every building before the War Horn.

The useful behaviors were:

- answering the actual support calls;
- avoiding unnecessary allied casualties;
- revealing hidden strongpoints only when requested;
- using Smoke for movement;
- using a precise AP shot on an extreme danger-close target;
- avoiding unnecessary aerial reconnaissance;
- keeping shots productive.

The completion achievement is **The Gun Answers**.

Earning Gold on every Fire on Call medal unlocks **Iron Discipline**. The
achievement list confirms both, but it does not expose the exact numerical Gold
thresholds, so I would not invent a fixed shell count or requisition total.


<h2 id="fire-on-call-stuck">
  Fire on Call mistakes that usually cause the failure
</h2>

### Clearing Sevilla before sounding the War Horn

The important targets are generated through frontline calls. Save the
requisition for the actual requests.

### Filling both guns before knowing the next mission

A Smoke or Tear call can make the preloaded shell useless. Keep one side flexible
when the next request is unknown.

### Ignoring the friendly coordinate

Mark the caller before the enemy point. Fire on Call is as much about friendly
positioning as enemy targeting.

### Firing H before the Tear reveal

When the order says Tear first, let the first shell expose the strongpoint before
building the destructive shot.

### Using Smoke at the enemy target

When Smoke is being used to save a danger-close friendly unit, place it where
you want the friendly unit to move.

### Assuming only one friendly unit will move

Recheck the map after Smoke lands. Nearby allied units can shift as well.

### Following every Smoke Shell with H

Some calls request Smoke only. Wait for the next message.

### Blindly obeying an unsafe H request

At extreme danger-close spacing, compare the effect area with a precise AP hit.

### Using AP against an uncertain hidden point

Precision only helps once the target is actually precise.

### Waiting until the deadline to start loading

The timers give enough margin to calculate properly, but the gun should be ready
before the final minute.

### Continuing after the city falls

Remaining enemy elements are explicitly optional after primary completion.
