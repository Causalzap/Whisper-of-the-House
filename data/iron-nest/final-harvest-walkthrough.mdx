<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
**Mission 14 — Final Harvest is a scheduling mission disguised as a giant
target list.**

Do not solve one objective and then start the next.

I first calculate the moving train interception and write down its required
launch time. While I am waiting for that moment, I use the spare minutes to
locate **Reference Point Alpha**, both **Enemy Fire Direction Centers**, and the
anti-aircraft position.

In one operation, the train intercept point was roughly **14.48 km from Iron
Nest**. The calculated shell flight time was about **37 seconds**, so a planned
impact around **10:13:30** meant firing at approximately **10:12:58**.

The train strike is the transition point. Once it is away, I immediately switch
to the already-plotted priority positions instead of returning to the map from
scratch.

During the counter-battery phase, artillery threatening Iron Nest takes priority
over optional infantry. Later frontline fire requests can arrive at the same
time, so I keep one gun available rather than filling both sides with
nonessential cleanup shots.

When command reports the operation **complete** and allows you to **Stand
down**, stop unless you deliberately want optional kills. You do not need to
erase every enemy icon around Barcelona.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

<h2 id="final-harvest-route">
  Mission 14: Final Harvest route at a glance
</h2>

Final Harvest initially looked like a mission where I should clear the map from
left to right.

That was the wrong way to think about it.

The mission combines:

- a moving train with a timetable;
- chained positional intelligence;
- an anti-aircraft battery;
- enemy artillery;
- counter-battery pressure;
- live frontline fire support;
- ordinary enemy units that can still remain after the main operation is
  complete.

My clean route is:

1. Mark Iron Nest and the known spotters.
2. Read the train schedule first.
3. Pick the train intercept point.
4. Calculate its firing solution and shell flight time.
5. Write down the exact launch time.
6. Leave that gun prepared.
7. Use the waiting window to solve Reference Point Alpha.
8. Locate Enemy Fire Direction Centers 1 and 2.
9. Plot the anti-aircraft battery and other known priority positions.
10. Prepare a second firing card before the train deadline arrives.
11. Fire the train shot at the calculated launch time.
12. Immediately switch to the pre-plotted ground targets.
13. When counter-battery pressure begins, prioritize artillery that threatens
    Iron Nest.
14. Answer required frontline H-shell calls without abandoning the
    counter-battery problem.
15. Keep checking powder because the long-range targets consume charges quickly.
16. Stop when High Command gives the stand-down order.
17. Continue firing only if I deliberately want optional medal cleanup.

The
<Link href="/iron-nest/iron-road-walkthrough">
  Iron Road walkthrough
</Link>
covers the simpler moving-train problem.

The
<Link href="/iron-nest/counter-battery-mission">
  Counter-Battery walkthrough
</Link>
covers the earlier timer-focused mission.

Final Harvest is about **running both systems at the same time without losing
track of the next required shot**.

<h2 id="train-first">
  Solve the train first because its deadline will not wait for you
</h2>

The first thing that grabbed my attention was the train schedule.

That is also the first thing I solve.

A static AA battery can still be there five minutes later.

The train will not.

In my operation, I used a point about **6 km farther along the railway** as the
intercept.

That put the train at the chosen point roughly nine minutes later.

The final Iron Nest-to-intercept distance was approximately:

**14.48 km**

I used an HCH solution in this operation.

Once I have chosen the future intercept point, I can run its coordinates through
the
<Link href="/iron-nest/fire-mission-helper">
  {" "}Fire Mission Helper
</Link>
to get the bearing, range, powder charge, and elevation before I work backward
from shell flight time to the launch time.

The calculator then gave a shell flight time of roughly:

**37 seconds**

The intended train impact was around:

**10:13:30**

so my working launch time became approximately:

**10:12:58**

The exact numbers can change.

The workflow does not:

**train future point → firing solution → flight time → launch time**

Once I have the launch time written down, I stop recalculating the train unless
I discover an actual mistake.

<h2 id="use-waiting-window">
  Use the train waiting window to solve the static targets
</h2>

This is the biggest efficiency gain in Final Harvest.

After I solve the train, I usually have several minutes before I need to fire.

Those minutes are not downtime.

I use them to solve everything that can become dangerous immediately afterward.

The first reference in this operation was **Reference Point Alpha**.

Its observations were approximately:

- **320° from Spotter 1**
- **8.03 km from Spotter 2**

Once Alpha was stable, I could continue into the enemy fire network.

One Enemy Fire Direction Center used approximately:

- **109° from Alpha**
- another observation from Spotter 3.

A second center used:

- **273° from Alpha**
- **9.84 km from Spotter 1**.

I do not need to fire at any of these immediately.

The goal is to leave the map with usable targets ready before the train shot
changes the pace of the mission.

<h2 id="anti-aircraft-battery">
  Plot the AA battery before the train reaches the intercept
</h2>

The anti-aircraft position was another target I could solve while waiting.

In this run it was tied to Enemy Fire Direction Center 2, with a reported
bearing around **091°** and a distance of approximately **2.75 km**.

The important part is not the exact number.

It is the dependency:

**Alpha → Fire Direction Center 2 → AA battery**

That means I do not rush the AA marker if the Fire Direction Center still looks
wrong.

But once the chain is defensible, I create the final firing line from Iron Nest
and leave the solution ready.

I want to reach the train launch moment with:

- train shot ready;
- at least one ground target already solved;
- second gun assigned;
- powder checked.

I do **not** want to fire the train and then spend three minutes rebuilding
Reference Alpha from scratch.

<h2 id="prepare-before-train-shot">
  Prepare the next firing cards before launching at the train
</h2>

In one operation I had two ground firing solutions prepared before the train
deadline.

One was around:

- **11.94 km range**
- approximately **50.1° elevation**
- three charges.

Another used a different bearing and approximately **47.76° elevation**.

I treat those as observed firing states, not universal Final Harvest values.

This is one of the few missions where I am happy to do substantial preparation

The targets are already known.

The problem after the train strike is not lack of information.

It is lack of time.

<h2 id="fire-train">
  Fire the train shot, then immediately change priorities
</h2>

When the planned launch time arrives, I stop touching the other cards.

I fire the train solution.

Then I switch attention immediately.

I do not stand around waiting for a dramatic confirmation if I already have the
next required solution ready.

In the documented operation, the train strike succeeded.

At roughly the same time, the mission shifted into a much busier
counter-battery state.

That transition is where Final Harvest stops behaving like Iron Road.

The train is now old business.

The artillery threatening Iron Nest is the current problem.

<h2 id="counter-battery-phase">
  When counter-battery pressure starts, artillery becomes the priority
</h2>

Once the timed pressure was active, I had multiple known enemy artillery
positions and very little benefit in spending those seconds on ordinary
infantry.

Two long-range examples in this operation were roughly:

- **10.05 km**
- **17.27 km**

The longer one required four charges in that solution.

I prepared the two guns separately and fired them in sequence.

One mistake in this section was assuming I could improvise after a miss.

The mission was already busy enough that every additional recalculation competed
with:

- loading;
- gun elevation;
- turret rotation;
- new messages;
- another enemy artillery target.

If one priority shot fails, I first check whether I still have enough timer to
correct it safely.

If not, survival comes before preserving the original plan.

<h2 id="do-not-chase-every-target">
  Do not chase every visible target during the timed phase
</h2>

This is where the map becomes deceptive.

There are a lot of things I can shoot.

That does not mean they are all equally important.

During the highest-pressure section, my order is:

1. enemy artillery currently threatening Iron Nest;
2. required live fire-support request;
3. required AA or priority installation;
4. ordinary artillery that remains tactically dangerous;
5. infantry and cleanup targets.

I do not reverse that list because a nearby infantry icon is easier to hit.

A cheap easy kill can still cost the thirty seconds I needed to load a
four-charge long-range counter-battery shot.

Final Harvest rewards resisting the urge to clear the attractive targets first.

Final Harvest also borrows the live-support pressure from Fire on Call.

One request in this operation required an **H shell before about 10:26**.

The caller was around:

**A5 2:5**

and the enemy point was reported approximately:

- **78°**
- **1.98 km from the reported reference position**

This is why I try to keep one gun flexible.

If both sides are loaded with speculative cleanup ammunition, the support call
forces me to unload or waste time.

My decision is:

### If a counter-battery gun is already seconds from firing

Finish that verified shot, then service the H request.

### If the artillery solution is still uncertain

Use the ready gun for the support mission first.

### If the H deadline is becoming unsafe

Stop optional calculations and answer the live request.

This is the same support mechanic seen earlier in the campaign, but the
difficulty here is **competition between two valid priorities**.

<h2 id="load-while-solving">
  Load while solving, but only after the target role is known
</h2>

One habit saved a lot of time in this mission:

I start the loader while I am still finishing the map.

But I only do that once the shell role is clear.

For example, once a required H call is confirmed, I can put H in the free gun
while measuring its final point.

Likewise, once a priority artillery target is clearly a precise hardened target,
I can begin loading AP while I finish the elevation.

I do not use this habit to preload random shells.

Final Harvest generates too many different target types for that.

The useful version is:

**target role confirmed → begin loading → finish firing solution**

not:

**mission looks busy → fill both guns with something**

<h2 id="watch-powder">
  Powder charges become the hidden resource problem
</h2>

The number of shells was not what nearly caught me out.

It was powder.

Final Harvest mixes:

- medium-range support shots;
- long-range artillery;
- the train intercept;
- repeated two-gun firing.

Several solutions required three or four charges.

Late in the operation I had to stop and requisition more powder because the
remaining long-range solution could not be completed with what was beside the
guns.

I check powder at three moments:

1. after preparing the train;
2. before the counter-battery transition;
3. after firing two long-range solutions back-to-back.

I buy more before the count reaches zero.

Waiting until the gun is already loaded with AP and then discovering that I am
missing the fourth charge is one of the easiest ways to lose a live deadline.

<h2 id="when-to-buy">
  When I spend requisition in Final Harvest
</h2>

I buy aggressively only when the next task is known.

I buy when:

- the train solution is already fixed;
- a priority artillery position is confirmed;
- a live support message explicitly needs H;
- a long-range solution requires more powder than I have;
- I know which gun will use the purchase.

I wait when:

- the target is only an optional infantry group;
- I already have two active firing solutions;
- a current shell is still resolving;
- High Command may already be close to declaring the operation complete.

That last point matters.

This mission can leave a large number of visible targets on the map even after
the required operation is effectively over.

Buying another full set of ammunition before checking the teleprinter can turn a
clean clear into pointless expenditure.

<h2 id="small-targets-after-pressure">
  After the timer pressure breaks, precise targets become safer to clean up
</h2>

Once the immediate counter-battery problem was under control, I returned to a
few remaining artillery and priority targets.

One later precise shot used approximately:

- **5.2° bearing**
- **45.60° elevation**
- a precise shell choice.

Another longer target used approximately:

- **15.16 km range**
- **4 charges**
- bearing in the high-60° range.

Again, those are operation-specific examples.

The important change is tactical.

During the timer:

**shoot what is threatening the mission.**

After the timer:

**I can spend more attention on efficiency and target choice.**

That is when a smaller-impact shell can make sense for an isolated artillery
piece.

I do not spend time experimenting with a new shell while the Counter-Battery
Timer is about to expire.

<h2 id="final-harvest-complete">
  Stop when High Command says you may stand down
</h2>

This is where the mission title can mislead me.

The introduction describes the destruction of the last organized resistance in
very absolute terms.

But the actual operation can reach a state where command reports:

**Completed. You may stand down.**

while enemy units are still visible.

I trust the stand-down message.

I do not trust the visual instinct that says:

> There are still red icons, so I must not be finished.

After completion, continued fire can still be possible.

That is optional.

For a normal clear, I stop.

For a medal attempt, I decide whether the remaining cluster is worth the
ammunition and risk.

<h2 id="optional-cleanup">
  Optional cleanup is where Final Harvest can waste the most requisition
</h2>

I continued for a while after the main operation was already complete.

There were still infantry and other targets around the city.

It was fun to shoot them.

It was not necessary to finish the mission.

That difference matters if I am pursuing:

- minimum assets;
- ammunition efficiency;
- fewer unnecessary shots.

Late optional fire can even produce more counter-battery activity, which is
another reason not to assume “mission complete” means every remaining target is
free value.

I continue only when:

- the target is confirmed;
- one shot can remove several enemies;
- I specifically want the elimination or kill-ratio medals;
- I have already accepted the extra resource cost.

Otherwise I stand down.

<h2 id="final-harvest-stuck">
  Final Harvest mistakes that made the mission harder than it needed to be
</h2>

### Solving the train and then waiting beside the gun

Use the waiting window to plot Alpha, fire direction centers, AA, and priority
artillery.

### Starting the static targets before calculating the train

The train has the non-negotiable movement deadline.

### Forgetting shell flight time

The shell must arrive when the train reaches the future point.

### Recalculating the train repeatedly

Once the intercept and launch time are verified, write them down and move on.

### Firing the train with no ground targets prepared

The mission accelerates immediately afterward.

### Treating every red icon as the same priority

Counter-battery artillery comes before easy infantry kills.

### Filling both guns with cleanup ammunition

Keep one side usable for the next live support call.

### Waiting to buy powder until the loader is empty

Several long shots can use three or four charges each.

### Dropping a nearly finished counter-battery solution when an H request arrives

Compare the two deadlines before switching tasks.

### Ignoring a live H request because artillery still exists

The frontline support call is still a required mission task.

### Experimenting with unfamiliar ammunition under timer pressure

Use the known reliable solution first. Experiment after the threat is controlled.

### Continuing because Barcelona still contains targets

The stand-down message defines required completion.

<h2 id="final-harvest-achievements">
  Final Harvest completion and achievements
</h2>

Completing the mission unlocks **In the Dark — Reap the Final Harvest**.

Earning Gold on all Final Harvest medals unlocks **Night's Precision**.

I do not claim an exact Gold shell count or requisition threshold because the
achievement list confirms the unlock conditions but does not expose the hidden
numerical medal requirements.

The repeat-run priorities I would use are:

- solve the train immediately;
- use every waiting minute for static target preparation;
- enter the counter-battery phase with both guns organized;
- keep powder ahead of demand;
- answer required support calls;
- avoid optional cleanup until command has already confirmed the mission state;
- stand down when efficiency matters more than extra kills.
