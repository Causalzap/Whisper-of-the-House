<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
**Mission 8 — Rock of Gibraltar has one required target and one optional moving
ship.**

For HMS Rockingham, do not fire before **11:08**. Aerial reconnaissance is
unavailable while the cruiser is providing anti-aircraft cover, so locate it
through the mission's reference chain: **The Mole → Dockmaster's House → HMS
Rockingham**. Use a Forward Observer only when that final intersection still
looks ambiguous.

The cruiser requires **AP**. In one operation, the confirmed firing solution was
approximately **58.5° / 9.21 km / 2 charges / 55.26° elevation**.

Destroying Rockingham completes the primary objective and restores aerial
reconnaissance. You can stand down there.

For the optional vessel, wait for its actual position report, plot its
**12° course**, convert **9.7 knots** to roughly **1 km every 3 minutes 20
seconds**, choose a future point, then subtract shell flight time from the
vessel's arrival time before firing.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

<h2 id="rock-of-gibraltar-route">
  Mission 8: Rock of Gibraltar route at a glance
</h2>

Rock of Gibraltar looked simple at first: one cruiser, one AP shell, one hit.

The actual problem is that I cannot see the target normally.

Rockingham's anti-aircraft batteries shut down aerial reconnaissance, including
the normal map coverage I would use to confirm where a target really sits. The
mission instead gives a chain of reference points and expects me to locate the
ship before committing the AP shell.

My primary-objective route is:

1. Read the complete Rockingham order.
2. Note the **11:08** restriction before touching the firing control.
3. Mark Iron Nest and both spotters.
4. Locate **The Mole** from the two spotter bearings.
5. Use the Mole to locate **Dockmaster's House**.
6. Use Dockmaster's House and Spotter 2 to locate **HMS Rockingham**.
7. If the last intersection is still ambiguous, requisition one Forward
   Observer report.
8. Draw one final firing line from Iron Nest to Rockingham.
9. Load one AP shell and the calculated charges.
10. Fire only after 11:08.
11. Wait for `HMS Rockingham successfully destroyed`.
12. Stand down immediately, or continue into the optional vessel interception.

The <Link href="/iron-nest">Iron Nest guide hub</Link> already covers the normal
map, calculator, shell, and firing loop. This page only covers the parts that
are different in Rock of Gibraltar: the chained reference points, the
reconnaissance blackout, the firing window, and the moving ship.

<h2 id="read-rockingham-order">
  Do not fire at Rockingham before 11:08
</h2>

The first thing I write down is not a coordinate.

It is:

**Do not fire until after 11:08.**

The same order establishes three other restrictions:

- the primary target is an enemy heavy cruiser in Gibraltar Harbor;
- **Armor Piercing is required**;
- aerial reconnaissance and normal reconnaissance imagery are unavailable while
  Rockingham's batteries remain active;
- a Forward Observer can be requisitioned if I need more positional information.

This changes how I spend the opening minutes.

I do **not**:

- buy reconnaissance cards that cannot solve the current problem;
- fire a ranging shell to see where it lands;
- send a Star Shell hoping it will bypass the restriction;
- rush an AP shot because the current time is already near 11:08.

Instead, I use the protected period to finish the map.

If my Rockingham marker is still uncertain when 11:08 arrives, I wait. The
clock is a **not-before** condition, not a deadline.

<h2 id="locate-the-mole">
  Start with The Mole, not the cruiser
</h2>

The target chain begins with a fixed reference point called **The Mole**.

In one operation, the reports placed it at the intersection of:

- **096° from Spotter 1**
- **051° from Spotter 2**

Those values can change between operations, so I treat them as a worked example.

The important part is the order of operations.

I first place:

- Iron Nest;
- Spotter 1;
- Spotter 2.

Then I draw the two reported lines and mark the point where they intersect.

That marker becomes **The Mole**.

This is where I slow down.

Unlike a normal two-line target, the Mole is not the thing I will fire at. It is
the foundation for another point, which then helps locate the cruiser.

A sloppy first marker creates a believable but wrong Rockingham later.

<h2 id="locate-dockmasters-house">
  Locate Dockmaster's House before touching Rockingham
</h2>

Once the Mole is fixed, I move to **Dockmaster's House**.

One operation used approximately:

- **5.69 km from The Mole**
- **086° from Spotter 1**

That can produce more than one plausible-looking point if the circle and bearing
are drawn roughly.

I do not choose one just because it is nearer the coastline.

Instead I zoom in and make the distance circle and bearing line meet as cleanly
as possible.

This was one of the real sticking points in the mission: a Dockmaster marker
that is only slightly off can still produce a Rockingham point that looks
reasonable in the harbor.

When the two measurements do not meet cleanly, I go back one stage:

1. recheck The Mole;
2. recheck the Dockmaster distance;
3. recheck which spotter owns the Dockmaster bearing;
4. redraw only the bad construction line.

I do not start over from Iron Nest unless the first reference point itself was
wrong.

<h2 id="locate-hms-rockingham">
  Use two constraints to locate HMS Rockingham
</h2>

Rockingham is the third point in the chain.

In one operation, the target was described as roughly:

- **6.09 km from Dockmaster's House**
- **062° from Spotter 2**

The final cruiser marker belongs where those two measurements agree.

The most dangerous mistake here is replacing the actual intersection with the
place where I think a ship ought to be.

The harbor shape is useful as a sanity check, but it is not the solution.

Continue when:

- the Dockmaster-to-Rockingham distance meets the Spotter 2 bearing cleanly;
- the point is plausible for the harbor;
- both construction methods independently support the same small area.

Go back when:

- the cruiser point depends on only one line;
- the circle and bearing miss each other by a visible amount;
- the marker moved substantially when I redrew The Mole;
- the only reason I like the point is that it looks coastal.

<h2 id="forward-observer">
  Use a Forward Observer only when the final point is still ambiguous
</h2>

A Forward Observer is useful here because normal aerial reconnaissance is not.

In one operation, the reference chain already produced a convincing Rockingham
intersection, but I still had two plausible interpretations around the
Dockmaster area.

One Forward Observer report added another short-distance constraint around the
suspected cruiser position. That extra information lined up with the existing
intersection and gave me enough confidence to fire.

I requisition it when:

- the Rockingham intersection still has two believable points;
- the Dockmaster marker is visually uncertain;
- I have already rebuilt the chain once and do not want to spend an AP shell on
  another guess.

I skip it when:

- both constraints already meet cleanly;
- I am deliberately trying to minimize requisition;
- I can explain every reference marker without guessing.

The important resource decision is simple:

**do not buy positional help before discovering that you actually need
positional help.**

<h2 id="rockingham-firing-solution">
  Build one clean AP solution for Rockingham
</h2>

Only after the cruiser marker is fixed do I draw the final firing line from
Iron Nest.

One successful operation produced approximately:

- **58.5° bearing**
- **9.21 km range**
- **2 powder charges**
- **55.26° elevation**
- **AP shell**

Another completed operation produced different Rockingham coordinates and a
different final firing solution, which is exactly why I would not publish one
“magic” bearing as the answer.

Before firing I check:

- current time is later than 11:08;
- AP is physically loaded;
- physical charge count matches the current firing card;
- the selected gun is set to the current elevation;
- the turret bearing comes from **Iron Nest to Rockingham**, not from one of the
  reference reports;
- the target marker is the final cruiser point, not the Mole or Dockmaster's
  House.

I buy one AP shell for the primary objective.

I do not buy a second “insurance” AP unless the first shot actually fails and I
have diagnosed why.

<h2 id="destroy-rockingham">
  Destroy Rockingham before thinking about the optional ship
</h2>

Fire after the 11:08 restriction has passed and wait for confirmation.

A successful hit produces:

- `HMS Rockingham successfully destroyed`
- anti-aircraft cover over the harbor lifted;
- aerial reconnaissance restored;
- primary objective complete;
- permission to stand down.

If I only want mission completion, I stop here.

I do **not**:

- order a scout flight because reconnaissance has just come back;
- shoot another harbor target;
- buy more AP;
- wait for the optional ship.

The mission has already given me permission to stand down.

<h2 id="optional-vessel">
  The second ship is optional, so decide before spending anything
</h2>

Immediately after Rockingham is destroyed, command can issue a public sailing
notice for a vessel suspected of carrying ordnance.

The order says interception is **authorized at your discretion**.

That creates two valid routes.

### Fast completion route

Stand down after Rockingham.

This is the route I use when I care most about:

- finishing Mission 8 safely;
- conserving requisition;
- avoiding a moving-target miss;
- preserving a simple primary-objective clear.

### Optional interception route

Stay active and prepare to intercept the second ship.

I choose this when I specifically want:

- the optional kill;
- more target value;
- the moving-target challenge;
- medal progress that benefits from additional confirmed destruction.

Once I commit to the optional route, I stop wandering around the harbor. The
next useful information is the vessel's actual position, course, speed, and
timestamp.

<h2 id="moving-vessel-position-report">
  Wait for the actual vessel position report
</h2>

The first optional notice only says the ship will enter the strait somewhere
between **Q1 and T1** at about **11:26**.

That is not enough for a precise shell.

I wait for the follow-up report.

In one successful operation, the useful report was:

- position: approximately **R1 9:8**
- timestamp: **11:26:29**
- course: **12°**
- speed: **9.7 knots**

At 9.7 knots, the practical conversion used in that operation was approximately:

**1 km every 3 minutes 20 seconds.**

Now I can draw a line from the reported ship position on a **12° course**.

Do not draw that 12° line from Iron Nest.

It begins at the vessel's reported position.

This is the same conceptual trap as spotter bearings earlier in the campaign:
the report tells me where the target is going, not where the turret should
point.

<h2 id="choose-future-intercept">
  Choose a future point far enough ahead to prepare the gun
</h2>

I do not fire at R1 9:8.

By the time the shell reaches that coordinate, the vessel has moved.

Instead, I choose a future distance along the 12° course.

A first attempt used a point roughly **2 km** ahead. Since the ship needs about
3:20 per kilometre, that puts it there about **6 minutes 40 seconds** after the
reported timestamp.

That route looked mathematically sound, but the shot missed.

The problem with a short intercept is not just the formula. It gives less time
to:

- mark the course accurately;
- place the future point;
- draw the new Iron Nest-to-intercept line;
- calculate the real artillery range;
- load the gun;
- account for shell flight time;
- correct any drawing error.

I therefore prefer the later **4 km intercept** from the successful route.

At 1 km per 3:20:

**4 km = 13 minutes 20 seconds**

Starting from:

**11:26:29**

the vessel reaches the 4 km point at approximately:

**11:39:49**

Now I finally draw a normal firing line from Iron Nest to that future point.

<h2 id="calculate-moving-ship-shot">
  Do not confuse vessel travel distance with artillery range
</h2>

This is the biggest moving-ship trap.

The vessel moving **4 km** does **not** mean the artillery range is 4 km.

The 4 km measurement begins at the vessel's reported position.

The gun still fires from Iron Nest.

In the successful operation, the future point was roughly **17.6 km from Iron
Nest**, so the artillery calculator had to use that much longer distance.

That new solution produced a shell flight time of approximately **25 seconds**.

    The ship moves 4 km along its course, but the firing range must still be
    measured from Iron Nest to that future point.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

Now the firing time becomes:

**11:39:49 vessel arrival  
− 00:00:25 shell flight  
= 11:39:24 launch**

That is the number I care about.

The useful formula is:

> **Launch time = reported time + target travel time − shell flight time**

I calculate the shell flight time **after** choosing the future point, because
a farther intercept point changes the artillery range and therefore changes
flight time.

<h2 id="fire-optional-vessel">
  Be loaded before the launch second arrives
</h2>

By the time I reach the firing window, the work should already be finished.

Before **11:39:24** in the example operation:

- target course is drawn;
- future point is marked;
- final firing line is measured from Iron Nest;
- shell is loaded;
- charges are loaded;
- turret bearing is set;
- gun elevation is set;
- correct gun is armed.

Then I wait.

Do not begin loading when the clock reaches the launch time.

The successful shot was fired on the planned launch second and the impact
arrived at the predicted future position.

Command then confirmed the vessel destroyed and stated that no further ships
were expected through the strait.

AP worked for this optional ship in the completed route.

Unlike HMS Rockingham, however, the optional order does not explicitly state
`AP Required`, so I would describe AP here as the successful choice rather than
a mandatory mission rule.

<h2 id="rock-of-gibraltar-medals">
  Decide whether extra intelligence and the optional ship are worth the cost
</h2>

Rock of Gibraltar has several decisions that can pull in opposite directions.

If I am aiming for the leanest primary clear:

- solve the reference chain manually;
- skip the Forward Observer when the intersection is already certain;
- buy one AP shell;
- destroy Rockingham;
- stand down.

If I am aiming for more destruction:

- take the optional vessel;
- use the ship report instead of guessing its current location;
- avoid unnecessary scout flights;
- choose one future intercept with enough setup time;
- stop after the optional-destroyed confirmation.

The result screen includes medal conditions involving:

- allied casualties;
- enemy elimination;
- destroying targets without requisitioning aerial reconnaissance;
- minimum assets.

I would not turn those into exact Gold thresholds yet because the available
runs do not expose every hidden numerical requirement.

The useful lesson is more practical:

**every optional requisition needs to solve a real uncertainty.**

A Forward Observer that confirms an ambiguous Rockingham marker has a purpose.

A scout flight ordered after the moving vessel already gave me position, course,
speed, and timestamp usually does not.

<h2 id="rock-of-gibraltar-stuck">
  Rock of Gibraltar mistakes that waste the run
</h2>

### Firing as soon as Rockingham is located

The target can be solved before the firing window. Wait until after 11:08.

### Trying to use aerial reconnaissance on the cruiser

Rockingham's anti-aircraft cover disables it until the primary target is
destroyed.

### Treating The Mole as the target

The Mole is only the first reference point in the chain.

### Guessing Dockmaster's House from the coastline

Use the reported distance and bearing. A small Dockmaster error moves
Rockingham.

### Requisitioning a Forward Observer immediately

Build the supplied reference chain first. Use the observer when the last
intersection remains ambiguous.

### Firing H at Rockingham

The mission order explicitly requires AP.

### Copying 58.5° and 9.21 km

Those values belong to one operation. Other completed operations produced
different coordinates and firing solutions.

### Continuing automatically after Rockingham is destroyed

The primary objective is already complete. The second vessel is optional.

### Shooting the optional ship at its reported position

The ship continues moving after the position report.

### Drawing 12° from Iron Nest

The 12° course begins at the vessel position.

### Entering 4 km into the artillery calculator

Four kilometres is how far the ship travels along its course. Measure the real
range from Iron Nest to the future intercept point.

### Forgetting shell flight time

The target and shell must reach the future point at the same time.

### Choosing an intercept point with no setup margin

The successful route used a later 4 km point after a shorter attempt failed.
