<h2 id="what-this-helper-solves">
  What the IRON NEST Fire Mission Helper actually solves
</h2>

The normal firing process in *IRON NEST* is not difficult because no single
calculation is especially complicated.

It becomes difficult when several of them have to happen in sequence while new
reports are still arriving.

My normal chain is:

**find my own position → locate the target → measure bearing and distance → pick
powder → calculate elevation → set the gun**

The in-game ballistic calculator handles the last part well once I already know
the target distance.

The part that costs me time is everything before that.

That becomes most obvious in missions such as
<Link href="/iron-nest/phantom-battery-walkthrough">
  {" "}Phantom Battery
</Link>
where an Emergency Move can leave me knowing only the general sector containing
Iron Nest while listening posts are simultaneously reporting the enemy battery
from different reference positions.

<h2 id="fire-solution">
  Fire Solution: when I already know my position and the target
</h2>

This is the fastest mode.

I enter two coordinates:

- **Iron Nest position**
- **Target position**

The helper returns:

- target bearing from Iron Nest;
- distance in kilometers;
- the lowest powder charge that can reach that distance;
- the required gun elevation;
- other valid higher-charge arcs.

Coordinates can be written in the same format I already use while playing.

For example:

**H3 5:5**

and:

**Q4 4:2**

can also be entered compactly as:

**H355**

and:

**Q442**

The tutorial case is a useful check because the values are easy to compare with
the game.

From **H3 5:5** to **Q4 4:2**, my firing card reads approximately:

- **85.5° bearing**
- **8.92 km**
- **2 powder charges**
- **53.52° elevation**

The same workflow is useful anywhere I have already solved the target on the
map but still need to move the result to the gun quickly.

<h2 id="locate-target">
  Locate Target: combine listening-post and spotter reports
</h2>

The second mode is the one I care about most in
<Link href="/iron-nest/phantom-battery-walkthrough">
  {" "}Phantom Battery
</Link>

The game does not always hand me one clean coordinate.

Instead, I can receive combinations such as:

- a bearing from one listening post;
- a distance from another listening post;
- two bearings;
- or two distances.

The helper accepts any two of those report types.

For a bearing report, I enter:

**report origin + bearing**

For a distance report, I enter:

**report origin + distance**

It then finds the point where the two reports agree.

<h2 id="find-my-position">
  Find My Position after an Emergency Move
</h2>

The third mode solves a different problem.

Sometimes I know where the enemy is, but I no longer know exactly where **I**
am.

After an Emergency Move, the game can tell me that Iron Nest is somewhere
inside a sector without giving the exact point.

That is enough to continue if I request position information from known
references.

One working example gave me:

- reference at **P1 9:5**, distance **3.72 km**;
- reference at **L1 7:9**, distance **1.36 km**;
- Iron Nest somewhere inside **M3**.

Geometrically, each distance creates a circle around its reference point.

Two circles can intersect twice.

That is why the optional **Known Sector** field matters.

If High Command has already told me:

**Iron Nest new location somewhere within M3**

I enter **M3** so the helper can prefer the intersection inside that grid.

Position reports are rounded, so if neither mathematical intersection falls
cleanly inside the reported sector, I recheck the raw candidates instead of
assuming the calculation has failed.

Once I have the position, I press **Use as Iron Nest** and the result moves
straight into Fire Solution.

That removes the part of Phantom Battery where I would otherwise solve my
location, copy it down, clear the board, and then start a second calculation for
the target.

<h2 id="powder-and-elevation">
  How powder charge and elevation work in the helper
</h2>

For the firing solutions I verified in-game, the relationship is consistent:

**elevation = distance × 12 ÷ powder charges**

The normal range rule is also straightforward:

**one powder charge adds roughly 5 km of maximum range**

That gives the helper a simple default behavior:

> use the lowest charge count that can reach the measured target.

For example, the verified firing-card samples include:

- **8.92 km / 2 charges → 53.52°**
- **10.46 km / 3 charges → 41.84°**
- **13.40 km / 3 charges → 53.60°**
- **14.42 km / 3 charges → 57.68°**

The important detail is that the recommended charge is not necessarily the
**only** valid charge.

More powder can produce a lower firing arc at the same distance.

That is why the result card also shows **Alternative lower arcs**.

I normally use the lowest valid charge because it conserves powder and is the
simplest solution to carry straight back to the gun.

I use a higher charge when I deliberately want a flatter arc and have enough
powder to justify it.

<h2 id="moving-targets">
  Moving targets still need timing, not just a firing solution
</h2>

The helper can calculate the static firing solution for a future intercept
point.

It does **not** mean I can ignore time.

This matters in missions such as
<Link href="/iron-nest/iron-road-walkthrough">
  {" "}Iron Road
</Link>
and
<Link href="/iron-nest/final-harvest-walkthrough">
  {" "}Final Harvest
</Link>

For a landing craft or train, I still need to determine:

1. where the target will be;
2. when it will arrive there;
3. the firing solution to that future point;
4. how long the shell will be in flight;
5. when to pull the firing lever.

A real landing-craft solution at **13.40 km** used:

- **297.5° bearing**
- **3 powder charges**
- **53.60° elevation**
- approximately **35 seconds of shell flight time**

The helper currently handles the coordinate and ballistic side of that chain.

I still use the in-game timing information for the final launch moment.

That is intentional because getting the impact time wrong by twenty seconds is
more damaging than saving a few seconds on the map calculation.

<h2 id="common-mistakes">
  Fire Mission Helper mistakes that can still give me a bad shot
</h2>

### Entering the listening-post bearing as the final gun bearing

A listening-post bearing describes the direction **from that listening post**.

After locating the target, I still need the bearing **from Iron Nest to the
target**.

Fire Solution handles that second bearing automatically.

### Using a distance report from the wrong reference

A distance is only useful when I attach it to the exact position that generated
the report.

I do not enter the convoy position I wish had made the observation.

I enter the one printed in the actual position report.

### Forgetting the known sector after an Emergency Move

Two distance circles can produce two valid geometric intersections.

If the game says Iron Nest is somewhere in **M3**, I enter M3.

That small piece of information is often what turns two possible answers into
one useful answer.

### Treating an estimated intersection as perfect information

Reports and manual map reading can still contain rounding.

If an intersection lands very close to a grid boundary, I check the original
report before committing an expensive shell.

### Using more powder just because the helper lists it

Other valid arcs are alternatives, not automatic recommendations.

My normal solution is still the lowest charge that reaches the target.

### Expecting the helper to lead a moving target automatically

The helper solves the point I enter.

For a train, landing craft, or another moving target, I first calculate the
future intercept position.

### Forgetting that an Emergency Move invalidates the old firing origin

Once Iron Nest relocates, every old Iron-Nest-to-target bearing and distance is
obsolete.

I find my new position before reusing the target.
