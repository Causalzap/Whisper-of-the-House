<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
**White Shells has four endings, and you do not need four separate campaign
saves to see them.**

The four routes are:

- **PRPG / propaganda leaflet into Barcelona** → the peaceful city ending;
- **chemical or White Phosphorus route** → Barcelona survives but submits;
- **Atomic shell into Barcelona** → the city-destruction ending;
- **attack your own High Command, then destroy the forces that retaliate
  against Iron Nest** → the betrayal ending.

The fourth route is the only one I would prepare before firing anything.

Once I turned on my own command, I could no longer rely on requisitioning new
ammunition. My first attempt failed because destroying High Command by itself
was not enough.

On the successful attempt I located the friendly artillery that would become
hostile, calculated the shots in advance, bought the shells and powder first,
then attacked command and worked through the retaliation targets.

Do **not** spend the 666-requisition Atomic shell on your own headquarters if
you are trying for that route. Save the money for the follow-up fight.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

<h2 id="all-endings">
  Iron Nest White Shells: all four endings
</h2>

White Shells surprised me because I originally treated it like a normal final
mission.

It is not.

High Command places Barcelona in front of Iron Nest and essentially tells me
that I already know which shell should end the war.

In my run, Iron Nest was positioned around **A10**.

There was no normal chain of mandatory target coordinates to solve first.

Instead, the mission asks what I am going to do with the weapons I have spent
the campaign unlocking.

These are the four ending routes I confirmed:

| Ending achievement | Route I used |
| --- | --- |
| **A City in Celebration** | Fire a PRPG propaganda leaflet into Barcelona |
| **A City Subdued** | Use the non-atomic chemical / White Phosphorus route |
| **The Road Not Taken** | Turn on High Command and survive the retaliation |
| **A City Silent** | Fire the Atomic shell into Barcelona |

The first three city-directed routes are quick once the firing solution is
ready.

**The Road Not Taken is a completely different mission.**

That one needs preparation.

<h2 id="before-choosing">
  Before choosing an ending, do not waste the preparation window
</h2>

Unlike Final Harvest, I was not immediately racing a train timetable or a
Counter-Battery Timer when White Shells began.

That gave me time to inspect the map.

I use that time differently depending on the ending.

### For the propaganda ending

I only need a workable city firing point and a PRPG shell.

### For A City Subdued

I need the relevant chemical or White Phosphorus shell and a city target.

### For A City Silent

I need the Atomic shell and a safe firing solution into Barcelona.

### For The Road Not Taken

I do **not fire yet**.

I first scout the friendly military positions, work out which ones can answer
my betrayal with artillery fire, pre-calculate those shots, and buy the
ammunition before attacking High Command.

That distinction is the most important thing on this page.

Three endings are choices.

The betrayal ending is a **combat route**.

<h2 id="city-in-celebration">
  A City in Celebration: use the propaganda shell
</h2>

This was the first White Shells ending I found, and I found it almost by
accident.

I had already prepared to fire into Barcelona.

Before committing to the Atomic shell, I noticed the **PRPG propaganda leaflet
shell** and decided to fire that first.

In this run, the city solution was around **12.6 km** from Iron Nest, with an
elevation in the low-50° range.

Those values are not the important part.

The important part is the ammunition.

I fired **PRPG**, not Atomic.

I did not need to:

- destroy military positions first;
- clear the city;
- fire a second shell;
- wait for a counterattack.

The propaganda shot itself triggered the ending.

The resulting epilogue describes Castile no longer fighting itself and the two
sides standing together again.

This route unlocks:

**A City in Celebration — A city that comes out to meet you.**

### When I buy PRPG

Immediately, if this is the ending I am targeting.

### When I do not buy extra shells

Once PRPG is loaded and my city solution is ready.

There is no benefit in filling the other gun with expensive ammunition for a
fight this route never starts.

<h2 id="city-silent">
  A City Silent: fire the Atomic shell into Barcelona
</h2>

The most direct reading of High Command's final order is the Atomic route.

The Atomic shell costs **666 requisition** in the run I tested.

That is expensive, but for this ending I do not need to preserve a reserve for
a later artillery duel.

I reload White Shells, prepare the city solution, load Atomic, and fire into
Barcelona.

One repeated attempt used a firing state around:

- bearing in the high-60° range;
- approximately **51.72° elevation**.

Again, I would recalculate from the current Iron Nest position rather than copy
those values blindly.

After impact, High Command reports that the remaining resistance has been

This unlocks:

**A City Silent — A city wiped from the map.**

solutions for this ending.

Once I have decided to take the Atomic route, I keep it simple:

1. confirm the current firing origin;
2. choose a point inside Barcelona;
3. calculate the shell;
4. fire;
5. wait for the ending.

<h2 id="city-subdued">
  A City Subdued: use the chemical / White Phosphorus route
</h2>

After seeing the propaganda and Atomic outcomes, I knew there had to be a
middle route.

The achievement description gives the clue:

**A City Subdued — A city that kneels, and calls it peace.**

I confirmed this route with a **nerve-agent shell** fired into Barcelona.

The city remained rather than being erased by the Atomic blast, but the shot
still forced the alternate ending.

<h2 id="road-not-taken">
  The Road Not Taken: attack High Command and survive what comes next
</h2>

This is the difficult White Shells ending.

My first assumption was:

> Destroy my own High Command and the betrayal ending will trigger.

It did not.

I fired the Atomic shell at command.

The teleprinter began calling me a traitor.

Then the remaining friendly military assets turned on Iron Nest.

I had almost no useful requisition left and no prepared follow-up plan.

The run ended in failure.

That first attempt taught me two things:

1. **destroying High Command alone is not enough**;
2. I need my retaliation shots prepared **before** I betray them.

The achievement attached to the successful route is:

**The Road Not Taken — A city spared, and a road no one else will ever walk.**

<h2 id="do-not-nuke-own-command">
  Do not use the Atomic shell on your own command for this ending
</h2>

The Atomic shell costs so much that it is a terrible opener for this route.

In my failed attempt, spending almost everything on that first shot left me
unable to build the ammunition reserve I actually needed after the betrayal.

Worse, once I attacked High Command I could no longer depend on going back to
requisition and calmly shopping for the next shell.

So my second attempt was built around a different rule:

**buy first, betray second.**

I use cheaper appropriate shells against command and the retaliating military
targets.

The Atomic shell is unnecessary.

<h2 id="betrayal-preparation">
  Scout and pre-plan the retaliation before attacking High Command
</h2>

This is one of the rare missions where I am willing to spend a long time on the
map before the first shot.

I used reconnaissance to expose the relevant friendly positions and then
planned each military target that could threaten Iron Nest.

In the successful setup I ended up preparing a sequence of precise and
area-effect shots.

Some observed examples from that run included approximately:

- **19.9° bearing / 3.81 km / 11.43° elevation**;
- **40.1° bearing / 3.70 km / about 11.1° elevation**;
- **9.1° bearing / 7.66 km / 22.98° elevation**;
- **1.1° bearing / 6.31 km / 18.93° elevation**;
- **50.8° bearing / 6.42 km / 19.26° elevation**;
- **83.9° bearing / about 16.6 km / 48.99° elevation**;
- **83.5° bearing / 17.21 km / 51.63° elevation**.

Those numbers are useful as a picture of the workload.

They are not a fixed ending code.

I still build the solution from my own current firing origin.

What matters is that every major target already has:

- a marker;
- a shell role;
- a bearing;
- a charge plan;
- an elevation.

<h2 id="what-to-buy-before-betrayal">
  What I buy before starting The Road Not Taken
</h2>

I do not optimize down to the last requisition point on the first successful
attempt.

A spare shell is much cheaper than reaching the final artillery position with
an empty rack.

My preparation was roughly:

- armor-piercing shells for hardened positions;
- larger armor-piercing / destructive shells where several hardened targets
  could be covered together;
- H for softer artillery positions;
- enough powder to service the longest shots;
- at least one backup precision shell.

I bought the ammunition **before** firing on High Command.

That is the important part.

I also loaded both guns before starting.

The first hostile shot should begin a firing sequence, not a shopping trip.

<h2 id="betrayal-target-priority">
  After attacking command, shoot the units that can actually kill Iron Nest
</h2>

Once the betrayal starts, the map becomes visually busy.

I do not try to eliminate every friendly infantry marker.

I prioritize the artillery and other heavy positions that can retaliate against
Iron Nest.

That was the breakthrough.

Ordinary infantry was not what was ending my attempt.

The black-dot / artillery-type positions were.

So my route became:

1. strike High Command;
2. immediately switch to the first pre-planned retaliation target;
3. alternate the two guns where possible;
4. reload while rotating the other gun;
5. remove hardened artillery with the shell already assigned to it;
6. watch for newly revealed artillery that was not visible in the original
   planning pass;
7. destroy the final firing-capable position;
8. stop once the ending triggers.

<h2 id="why-betrayal-fails">
  Why destroying High Command can still end in failure
</h2>

If you destroyed High Command and then got killed, you did not discover a fifth
ending.

That happened to me.

It is simply a failed attempt at The Road Not Taken.

Destroying headquarters starts the betrayal state.

It does not automatically complete it.

The retaliating military assets still have to be dealt with.

Likewise, simply moving Iron Nest into Barcelona did not trigger a separate
ending in my tests.

I moved through the city and even continued far beyond it.

Nothing special happened.

So I would not spend time testing:

- walk into Barcelona;
- drive away;
- destroy command and wait;
- destroy Iron Nest itself.

The reproducible fourth route was:

**turn against command → survive → eliminate the retaliating artillery.**

It is also the ending that asks the most of the actual artillery systems.

The other three routes can finish with essentially one decisive city shot.

This one makes me prove that the pre-planning was good enough to survive the
consequences.

<h2 id="best-order">
  The order I use to unlock all four endings
</h2>

I would collect them in this order:

1. **A City in Celebration** — PRPG;
2. **A City Silent** — Atomic;
3. **A City Subdued** — chemical route;
4. **The Road Not Taken** — betrayal route.

The first three teach me where Barcelona sits relative to Iron Nest and confirm
that restarting White Shells is straightforward.

I save The Road Not Taken for last because it requires:

- reconnaissance;
- target planning;
- ammunition budgeting;
- two-gun sequencing;
- fast reloading;
- adapting when another artillery target appears.

That makes it much easier to treat the last attempt as a prepared combat
operation rather than experimenting with endings while under fire.

<h2 id="ending-not-triggering">
  If the White Shells ending does not trigger
</h2>

### Propaganda fired but nothing happened

Make sure the shell is actually **PRPG** and that the impact lands in the city.

Do not fire Atomic first.

### You wanted A City Subdued but got another ending

Do not lead with propaganda or Atomic.

Use the chemical route directly.

### You nuked Barcelona and expected another choice afterward

The Atomic impact resolves that route.

Reload White Shells to test another ending.

### You attacked High Command and died

That is not a completed ending.

Prepare the retaliation targets before turning hostile.

### You cannot requisition after betraying command

That is exactly why I buy the full combat load first.

Restart and prepare before firing the opening betrayal shot.

### You destroyed command but the ending still has not appeared

Look for artillery or other firing-capable retaliation targets that remain
active.

One of my late targets only became obvious after the fight was already under
way.

### You drove Iron Nest into Barcelona and nothing happened

Movement alone did not produce an ending in my run.

The four confirmed routes all depend on what I fire and whom I fight afterward.

<h2 id="white-shells-achievements">
  White Shells achievements
</h2>

White Shells has four ending achievements:

- **A City in Celebration** — A city that comes out to meet you.
- **A City Subdued** — A city that kneels, and calls it peace.
- **The Road Not Taken** — A city spared, and a road no one else will ever
  walk.
- **A City Silent** — A city wiped from the map.

There is also:

**Pride of Castile — Earn gold on all medals in "White Shells."**

I do not give a supposed guaranteed Gold formula here because the achievement
list confirms the unlock but does not expose the exact numerical medal
thresholds.

If I were making a Gold attempt, I would keep it separate from the
all-endings run rather than loading expensive experimental shells just to see
different outcomes.
