**Mission 13 — Phantom Battery starts with Iron Nest already under fire.**

Restart the engines first, then execute the required Emergency Move. The Nest
relocates to an unknown position, so do not reuse your old firing origin.

Use Position Reports to rebuild your own location while the three fixed
listening posts collect bearings on the Phantom Battery. Once two useful
observations agree, mark the battery and prepare the shot.

The real trap is that the Phantom Battery is **mobile**. A location that was
correct a moment ago can become stale after the battery fires or repositions.

Enemy spotters are your time reserve: **each destroyed spotter adds 90 seconds
to the Counter-Battery Timer**. I use them when the timer is too tight to
rebuild my own position and get a fresh AP solution safely.

In one successful final engagement, the confirmed battery was fired on at about
**277.4°**, with **2 charges** and approximately **27.72° elevation**. The exact
numbers change because both Iron Nest and the Phantom Battery can move.

<h2 id="phantom-battery-route">
  Mission 13: Phantom Battery route at a glance
</h2>

Phantom Battery was the first mission where I died before I had really started
solving the artillery problem.

The opening attack damages Iron Nest, the engine has to be restarted, and the
mission immediately expects an Emergency Move.

After that, the usual assumption that I know where my gun is no longer holds.

My reliable route is:

1. Restart the engine correctly.
2. Do not leave the controls on the starter position.
3. Execute the required Emergency Move.
4. Read the new coarse relocation area.
5. Request enough Position Reports to recover Iron Nest's actual position.
6. Mark all three fixed listening posts.
7. Wait for the Phantom Battery to fire and generate fresh bearing reports.
8. Intersect those reports to get a current battery position.
9. Decide whether the timer allows a battery shot immediately.
10. If the timer is too low, destroy an enemy spotter for another 90 seconds.
11. Use AP once the battery position is confirmed.
12. Move again whenever the Counter-Battery Timer becomes unsafe.
13. Delete every old Iron Nest firing line after movement.
14. Watch for `mobile` or `repositioning` before firing at an old battery marker.
15. Rebuild both positions when necessary.
16. Finish the current battery position with a fresh AP solution.
17. Stop after the threat is neutralized.

The basic geometry for finding Iron Nest from reports is already covered in
<Link href="/iron-nest/how-to-triangulate">
  How to Triangulate in Iron Nest
</Link>

The
<Link href="/iron-nest/counter-battery-mission">
  Counter-Battery walkthrough
</Link>
covers the earlier version of the timer mechanic.

Phantom Battery is different because **both sides of the firing line can
change**.

<h2 id="restart-before-moving">
  Restart the engine before trying to escape
</h2>

The opening attack puts Iron Nest into a damaged state immediately.

On my first attempt, I spent too long fighting the restart controls and then
tried to execute the move without being certain the engine was actually
running.

That run ended almost immediately.

The important sequence on the successful restart was:

1. use the starter;
2. get the engine operating normally;
3. move the engine controls out of the start state and into **RUN**;
4. confirm the Nest is actually running;
5. then execute Emergency Move.

I do not start repairing every minor damaged system before moving.

The hostile battery is already engaging me.

The first priority is:

**engine → movement → position**

not:

**engine → repair everything → organize ammunition → eventually move**

The engine controls themselves are the same system already encountered in The
Gorge, so I reuse the same reference image rather than creating another copy.

<h2 id="first-emergency-move">
  The first Emergency Move deliberately removes your firing origin
</h2>

After the successful restart, my first move relocated Iron Nest somewhere inside
**R2**.

That is not an exact coordinate.

It is only a coarse area.

This distinction matters because I can already receive useful information about
the Phantom Battery while still not knowing exactly where **I** am.

Knowing the enemy position alone is not enough to fire.

I still need a valid Iron Nest-to-target line.

So after moving, I immediately treat every old firing value as dead:

- old turret bearing;
- old range;
- old charge choice;
- old elevation;
- old red firing line.

I can keep fixed listening-post markers.

I cannot keep a firing solution built from a firing origin that no longer
exists.

<h2 id="locate-iron-nest">
  Rebuild Iron Nest's position before spending the expensive shot
</h2>

My first location recovery was messy because I tried to interpret the reports
while the battery was still firing and the Nest was shaking.

The lesson from that attempt was simple:

**a coarse relocation grid is not enough for a precise AP shot.**

Later in the same mission, a much cleaner recovery used two independent
Position Reports.

One reported a line from approximately:

- **T4 8:1**
- bearing about **256°**

Another used:

- **N4 5:5**
- bearing about **168°**

Their intersection gave me a workable new Iron Nest position.

When I already have two usable Position Reports, I can also enter them into the
<Link href="/iron-nest/fire-mission-helper">
  {" "}Fire Mission Helper
</Link>
instead of rebuilding the intersection manually. Once the new position is found,
I use **Use as Iron Nest** to carry it straight into the next firing solution.

I requisition the second report when the first one still leaves me somewhere

I do **not** try to save requisition by choosing an arbitrary point and hoping
the AP radius forgives it.

The entire mission is built around precise counter-battery fire.

A guessed firing origin defeats that purpose.

<h2 id="place-listening-posts">
  Mark all three listening posts before the battery speaks again
</h2>

The mission deploys **three listening posts** in the sector.

They are fixed reference points.

Their job is to give me bearings when the Phantom Battery re-engages.

That means I want all three on the map before I start interpreting any battery
report.

I do not need all three bearings every time.

I need enough independent information to support one current battery position.

A clean pair of useful bearings can be enough.

The mistake is drawing a battery bearing from Iron Nest.

Listening-post bearings begin at the listening post named in the report.

That is the same origin rule used everywhere else in the campaign, but Phantom
Battery makes mistakes much more expensive because the Counter-Battery Timer is
running while I solve them.

<h2 id="find-phantom-battery">
  Let the listening posts find the battery before you guess
</h2>

In one engagement, the battery re-opened fire and the listening posts began
producing fresh bearings.

One of the reports included a bearing around **258°** from its listening post.

Once another useful constraint was added, the reports produced a believable
crossing.

If I already have the listening-post reports in front of me, I can use the
<Link href="/iron-nest/fire-mission-helper">
  {" "}Fire Mission Helper
</Link>
to combine the bearing and distance or two bearings, then send the calculated
battery position straight into **Fire Solution**.

This is where I make the first big mission decision.

### If the battery point is clean and I have enough time

I build the firing solution immediately.

### If the battery point is still weak

I wait for another useful report instead of spending an armor-piercing round on
a guess.

### If the timer is already too low

I switch priorities and kill an enemy spotter for more time.

That last option is what makes Phantom Battery different from a simple “find the
intersection and shoot” puzzle.

My first confident battery solution produced a worked example around:

- approximately **42.54° elevation**

The shot reached the suspected Phantom Battery position.

It did not finish the mission.

Instead, the target state made it clear that the position required a precise
armor-piercing follow-up.

Not when the mission starts.

Not when I first see a vague bearing.

I buy it when I have:

- a current battery location;
- a current Iron Nest location;
- enough time to construct the final shot.

That ordering matters because the Phantom Battery can move before I use the
shell.

<h2 id="move-before-timer-expires">
  Move before the Counter-Battery Timer reaches the desperate stage
</h2>

The worst moments in this mission came when I tried to squeeze one more solution
out of a nearly dead timer.

At one point I was down to only a handful of seconds before impact.

That is too late to start thinking about movement.

My rule became:

**If I cannot finish a verified shot inside the remaining timer, I move first.**

After moving:

1. discard the old firing line;
2. keep the last known battery marker only as historical information;
3. recover Iron Nest's new position;
4. wait for current enemy information;
5. rebuild the shot.

This feels slower, but surviving the move gives me another solve.

Trying to save ten seconds with stale coordinates can end the entire run.

<h2 id="enemy-spotters">
  Enemy spotters are 90-second time extensions
</h2>

The opening orders give one of the most useful numbers in the mission:

**each destroyed enemy spotter adds 90 seconds to the Counter-Battery Timer.**

I treat those spotters as a time resource.

I do not automatically clear every spotter before touching the Phantom Battery.

That wastes the information advantage I already have when the battery itself is
well located.

I shoot a spotter when:

- the battery has repositioned and I need time to find it again;
- Emergency Move forced me to rebuild my own position;
- the timer is too short for another full AP calculation;
- a spotter is already well located and cheap to remove.

During one recovery, I prepared two known spotters across the two guns so I
could remove them quickly rather than handling them one at a time.

The purpose was not simply extra kills.

It was buying enough time to solve the moving battery again.

This is the decision that keeps the mission under control:

> **Battery position certain + enough time → shoot battery.**
>
> **Battery uncertain + timer collapsing → shoot a spotter.**

<h2 id="phantom-battery-moves">
  Do not assume the Phantom Battery stays where you found it
</h2>

This is the biggest trap in the mission.

After I finally had a good battery position, prepared AP, moved, rebuilt my own
position, and fired again, the expected target was no longer there.

The reason became clear:

**the Phantom Battery is mobile.**

Later reports explicitly described it as:

- `mobile`;
- `repositioning`.

One attempted follow-up used approximately:

- **300.2° bearing**
- **4.04 km range**
- **2 charges**
- **24.24° elevation**

The card itself was internally consistent.

The problem was the target.

The battery was still moving.

That is an important distinction.

When a well-built AP shot misses a mobile Phantom Battery, I do not immediately
assume:

- calculator error;
- wrong charges;
- wrong AP shell;
- broken gun.

I first ask:

**Was the target still at that marker when I fired?**

If the latest message says `repositioning`, I stop using the old target point.

<h2 id="when-to-wait-for-new-bearing">
  When I wait instead of firing another AP
</h2>

Once the battery is mobile, firing faster is not automatically better.

I wait when:

- the last report explicitly says the battery is repositioning;
- only one fresh listening-post bearing is available;
- my target marker is still based on the previous firing position;
- I just executed Emergency Move and have not recovered my own location;
- the AP shot would be based on two stale assumptions at once.

I fire when:

- Iron Nest's position is current;
- the battery's latest position is supported by fresh information;
- no `mobile` update has invalidated it;
- the timer still covers loading and shell flight.

The expensive mistake is trying to solve uncertainty by firing more AP rounds.

Phantom Battery rewards **current information**, not volume of fire.

<h2 id="off-map-position">
  What I did when a Position Report seemed to put Iron Nest off the map
</h2>

One later Emergency Move created another strange problem.

The reports put Iron Nest somewhere around **M1**, but plotting the information
made the position appear to fall beyond the useful map edge.

Rather than build a firing solution from a point I did not trust, I used a
controlled movement.

I moved Iron Nest roughly **2 km north**.

That brought the firing origin back into a part of the map I could work with.

Then I requested fresh positional information.

One recovery used distance constraints around:

- **3.72 km from P1 9:5**
- **1.36 km from L1 7:9**

Those measurements converged on the new firing position.

I would not treat those coordinates as a required Phantom Battery route.

The reusable lesson is:

> If the relocation data puts Iron Nest somewhere I cannot map reliably, I do
> not pretend the edge of the map is an exact firing origin.

A short controlled move plus new reports is safer than spending another AP
round from a guessed point.

<h2 id="final-phantom-battery-shot">
  Rebuild both ends of the line for the final AP shot
</h2>

The final successful engagement only happened after I stopped carrying old
solutions forward.

I had:

- a current Iron Nest position;
- a current battery point;
- AP loaded;
- enough time left to finish the setup.

In that operation, the final firing state used approximately:

- **277.4° bearing**
- **2 powder charges**
- **27.72° elevation**

Both gun controls were prepared around the same confirmed solution before the
shot.

<h2 id="phantom-battery-stuck">
  Phantom Battery mistakes that cost me entire attempts
</h2>

### Starting Emergency Move before the engine is actually running

The opening sequence is unforgiving. Finish the restart and leave the controls
in the running state first.

### Treating R2 as an exact Iron Nest coordinate

The first relocation only narrows the Nest to an area.

### Trying to solve only the enemy position

A perfect battery marker still cannot produce a firing solution when I do not
know my own current position.

### Drawing listening-post bearings from Iron Nest

Each bearing begins at the named listening post.

### Buying AP before the battery position is defensible

The battery can relocate while I am still solving the map. Buy the expensive
shot when I am ready to use it.

### Chasing spotters while the battery is already exposed

Spotters buy time. They are most useful when I actually need more time.

### Ignoring the 90-second extension

When the timer is too low for another full solution, a known spotter can be more
valuable than a rushed battery shot.

### Firing at the previous battery marker after a `mobile` update

The old point can be mathematically perfect and tactically useless.

### Assuming a missed AP means AP is wrong

In one attempted shot, the battery had simply moved away from the solved point.

### Keeping old firing lines after Emergency Move

The target may remain valid, but the bearing and range from Iron Nest do not.

### Guessing an off-map firing origin

If the positional reports produce a point I cannot reliably use, I reposition
and request new information.

### Waiting until the last few seconds to move

Emergency Move is useful only if I survive long enough to execute it.

<h2 id="phantom-battery-completion">
  Phantom Battery completion and achievements
</h2>

The mission-completion achievement is **Mirror Image — Hunt the Phantom
Battery**.

The all-Gold achievement is **Master Gunner — Earn gold on all medals in
"Phantom Battery."**

The available achievement data confirms those two unlocks, but it does not
expose the exact numerical Gold thresholds, so I do not treat any particular
shell count, Emergency Move count, or requisition total as a guaranteed
all-Gold formula.

The useful repeat-run priorities are:

- restart cleanly;
- avoid unnecessary Emergency Moves;
- use spotters as timer extensions rather than random side targets;
- do not waste AP on a repositioning battery;
- rebuild your own position correctly after every move;
- stop as soon as the Phantom Battery is confirmed destroyed.
