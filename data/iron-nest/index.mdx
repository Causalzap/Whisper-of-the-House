**Iron Nest is solved in this order: read, locate, calculate, prepare, fire,
and confirm.**

The most important rule is that **spotter bearings, distance reports,
corrections, railway waypoints, vessel courses, and Position Reports help you
locate a point — they are not automatically the bearing that goes into the
turret.**

Once the target is known, draw a fresh line from the **current Iron Nest
position** to that target. That final line gives the bearing and range for the
shot.

<h2 id="how-iron-nest-works">
  How Iron Nest actually works
</h2>

The gun usually does exactly what I tell it to do.

Most of my bad shots started several stations earlier:

- I copied a spotter bearing into the turret;
- I measured from an old Iron Nest position;
- I treated an approximate search area as an exact target;
- I reused a firing card after the Nest moved;
- I loaded a different charge count from the one used in the calculation;
- I prepared one gun and armed the other;
- I fired again before reading the report from the previous impact.

The campaign becomes much easier once I separate **finding the target** from
**firing at the target**.

<h2 id="triangulation">
  Stuck on triangulation?
</h2>

One of the easiest Iron Nest mistakes is treating a spotter bearing as a turret
bearing.

It is not.

A clean Fire and Light example used:

- **96° from Spotter 1**
- **53° from Spotter 2**

Those observations located the hostile position.

The final line from Iron Nest to that intersection was instead approximately:

- **65.7° bearing**
- **12.91 km range**

So if my turret bearing is identical to one of the spotter reports, I stop
before firing.

Later missions extend the same location logic into:

- bearing-and-distance intersections;
- chained reference points;
- Position Reports after Emergency Move;
- listening-post reports;
- moving-target course projections.

<h2 id="calibration-fire">
  Calibration Fire: learn the basic shot
</h2>

Calibration Fire stays on the Hub because it strips away almost every later
complication.

There is no moving target, Counter-Battery Timer, hidden garrison, chained
reference point, or Emergency Move.

My route is:

1. Mark the supplied Iron Nest position and target.
2. Draw the final line from Iron Nest to the target and record bearing and
   range.
3. Use that range to choose charges and elevation.
4. Load the same shell and charge count shown on the firing card.
5. Rotate, elevate, fire, and wait for confirmation.

<h2 id="read-the-order">
  Read the order before touching the gun
</h2>

I read every page of the teleprinter before visiting requisition or loading a
shell.

A later paragraph can completely change what the mission is asking me to do.

For example:

- Counter-Battery explains what actually starts the CBT;
- Rock of Gibraltar says **do not fire before 11:08**;
- High Tide defines the objective as holding until **07:14**;
- Dead Reckoning requires Tear to expose hidden positions reliably;
- Fire on Call changes the battle only after the War Horn;
- several missions allow stand down while optional targets still remain.

When I read positional information, I keep the origin attached to the number.

A bearing from a spotter begins at that spotter.

A correction begins at the previous impact.

A vessel course begins at the reported vessel position.

A Position Report begins at its named reference point.

Only the **final firing line** begins at Iron Nest.

<h2 id="build-final-solution">
  Build one final firing solution after the target is known
</h2>

Once the target is confirmed, I simplify the map.

I keep:

- the current Iron Nest marker;
- the confirmed target;
- nearby friendly or protected positions;
- one correction point if it is still active;
- the current final firing line.

I remove or stop trusting:

- rejected candidate intersections;
- old Iron Nest positions;
- destroyed targets;
- expired moving-target waypoints;
- pre-move firing lines;
- corrections that belonged to an earlier impact.

A complete firing state needs four linked values:

- **bearing**
- **range**
- **powder charges**
- **elevation**

After Iron Nest moves, I may keep a confirmed enemy marker if the enemy itself
did not move.

I do **not** keep the old bearing, range, charges, or elevation.

The firing origin changed.

The firing solution changed with it.

<h2 id="shell-choice">
  Choose the shell for the actual problem
</h2>

I stopped asking:

**Which shell is strongest?**

The better questions are:

- How certain is the target position?
- What type of target is it?
- What is close to it?
- What does the order require?
- Am I trying to destroy, reveal, illuminate, or reposition something?

The broad roles are simple:

- **H:** normal destructive fire against soft targets and installations.
- **HCHE:** useful when several soft targets fit safely inside a larger effect
  area.
- **AP / APHE:** armored, underground, hardened, or precision targets.
- **STAR:** illumination and correction missions.
- **SMK:** support calls and friendly repositioning.
- **TEAR:** expose hidden positions before the destructive follow-up.

<figure className="my-8">
  <Image
    src="/images/iron-nest/iron-nest-shell-menu-types.webp"
    alt="Iron Nest ammunition menu showing destructive illumination smoke and specialist shell options"
    width={1600}
    height={900}
    className="rounded-xl"
  />
  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-500 dark:text-slate-400">
    Specialized ammunition is useful when it solves a specific mission
    problem, not simply because it appears later in the ammunition list.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

The campaign itself gives better examples than a generic tier list.

**Liberation:**  
The underground cache becomes an AP problem once its target state is confirmed.

**Fire and Light:**  
The final sequence is an illumination and observer-correction problem, so STAR
is the actual mission tool.

**Siege of Cartagena:**  
SMK is required for the live breakout support.

**Fire on Call:**  
One H request put the enemy only about **0.25 km** from friendlies. A precise AP
hit was safer than using the larger requested blast blindly.

**Blind Fire:**  
A later chained target can have more positional uncertainty than an early
battery. More effect-area tolerance can be useful when the wider shot is safe.

<h2 id="requisition">
  Spend requisition only to remove a specific problem
</h2>

I get better results when every purchase answers a question.

### Rock of Gibraltar

If The Mole → Dockmaster's House → Rockingham chain already converges cleanly,
I do not buy a Forward Observer.

If two believable Rockingham points remain, the observer has a clear job:
remove that ambiguity before I spend AP.

### Dead Reckoning

I do not buy destructive ammunition for all three primary positions before Tear
has revealed what those targets actually are.

**Tear first. Read the target state. Buy second.**

### Phantom Battery

After Emergency Move, one Position Report can leave Iron Nest somewhere along
an entire line.

If my next AP shot depends on the exact firing origin, I pay for the second
report rather than guessing.

### High Tide

The mission already feeds me constant landing-craft movement reports.

Extra reconnaissance does not help much when my real bottleneck is keeping the
two guns and several moving-target solutions organized.

### Final Harvest

Some longer solutions use three or four powder charges.

I replenish powder before a priority gun is loaded and waiting for a charge I
no longer have.

<h2 id="dual-guns">
  Treat LEFT and RIGHT as separate firing states
</h2>

Once both guns matter, I stop thinking of Iron Nest as one cannon with two
triggers.

Each side can have its own:

- shell;
- powder charges;
- elevation;
- target;
- launch time;
- loaded state.

Different missions reward different two-gun habits.

**Counter-Battery:**  
Preloading two known artillery solutions can save critical CBT time.

**High Tide:**  
I prefer one live landing-craft solution per gun. New contacts stay on the map
until a gun becomes free.

**Fire on Call:**  
When the next support request is unknown, leaving one gun flexible can be more
valuable than filling both.

**Final Harvest:**  
One side can stay committed to a timed or counter-battery shot while the other
remains available for live support.

The dangerous mistake is not preloading.

It is allowing a new target to overwrite the shell, charges, or elevation that
belong to the target already assigned to that gun.

<h2 id="troubleshooting">
  If the shot is wrong, return to the station that owns the error
</h2>

I no longer restart the entire solve after every miss.

I first decide **what kind of information is wrong**.

### The target position is unclear

Return to the teleprinter and map.

Check:

- coordinate origin;
- correct spotter;
- distance origin;
- search sector;
- latest reconnaissance;
- impact correction;
- whether the objective changed.

If the problem is geometry, use the
<Link href="/iron-nest/how-to-triangulate">
  triangulation guide
</Link>

### The target is known but the turret bearing looks wrong

Redraw the final line from the **current Iron Nest marker**.

This catches:

- a spotter bearing copied into the turret;
- an old firing origin;
- a moving-target course used as the gun bearing;
- a correction measured from the wrong point.

### The bearing looks right but the impact distance is wrong

Check:

- final range;
- physical charge count;
- elevation;
- whether the card belongs to this target;
- whether the target moved after the solution was calculated.

### The firing card looks right but the gun behaves wrong

Check:

- shell type;
- left or right loader;
- selected gun;
- charge count;
- elevation on that side;
- armed state.

### The target was destroyed but the mission continues

Return to the teleprinter.

Do not automatically fire at the same coordinate again.

The mission may have:

- a second phase;
- optional targets;
- a reconnaissance task;
- a War Horn requirement;
- a live support request;
- a stand-down message you have not read yet.

This becomes especially important later in the campaign.

Rock of Gibraltar, Dead Reckoning, Fire on Call, High Tide, Final Harvest, and
White Shells all have points where **continuing to fire** and **finishing the
required objective** are no longer the same thing.

<h2 id="challenge-mode">
  Challenge Mode uses the same system, with less room for waste
</h2>

Challenge Mode still rewards the same firing discipline:

- locate before loading;
- keep active firing cards organized;
- avoid duplicate shots while an impact is unresolved;
- buy reconnaissance only when it removes useful uncertainty;
- use safe multi-target effects when they genuinely save time;
- stop refining decimals once the shell's safe effect area already solves the
  problem.

I am keeping Challenge Mode on the Hub for now.

I do not yet have enough verified scoring data, target-generation rules, and
repeatable high-score runs to claim a complete standalone Challenge Mode meta.

Until that changes, the campaign remains the best preparation: learn how to
build a correct firing solution quickly, then learn when **not** to spend
another thirty seconds perfecting one.
