<h2 id="iron-nest-guide">
  How to operate Iron Nest without guessing
</h2>

The gun usually does exactly what I tell it to do. The difficult part is making
sure I told it the right thing.

A miss commonly begins several stations before the firing lever:

- the teleprinter order was only partly read;
- Iron Nest was marked at an old position;
- a spotter bearing was copied into the turret;
- the final line began at the target or spotter instead of the Nest;
- the calculator used the wrong range;
- the loader contains a different charge count from the firing card;
- the left gun is loaded while the right gun is armed;
- a correction report arrived but the previous solution was reused.

I use one repeatable firing loop across the campaign.

The most important stopping point is between calculation and loading.

Do not buy or load a shell simply because a target will eventually need to be
fired upon. Confirm what the target is, where it is, and what the order requires
first.

<h2 id="calibration-fire">
  Calibration Fire: learn the complete firing cycle
</h2>

Calibration Fire remains in the Hub because it is the cleanest version of the
shared system.

The order gives a direct Iron Nest coordinate and target coordinate. There is
no triangulation, moving target, Counter-Battery Timer, reconnaissance search,
or correction chain.

My route is:

1. Read the complete order.
2. Mark Iron Nest.
3. Mark the calibration target.
4. Draw one final line from Iron Nest to the target.
5. Record the bearing and range.
6. Enter the range into the ballistic calculator.
7. Select a charge count that reaches the target.
8. Record the resulting elevation.
9. Load the required shell and the same number of charges.
10. Rotate the turret to the measured bearing.
11. Set the selected gun to the calculated elevation.
12. Arm the correct gun and fire.
13. Wait for the calibration confirmation before touching the map again.

Do not rush through Calibration Fire because the target is simple. It is the
best place to establish habits that become essential once later missions add
timers and multiple guns.

<h2 id="teleprinter-and-map">
  Read the teleprinter, then build the map
</h2>

I read the teleprinter before visiting the loader or requisition console.

A complete order can contain several different kinds of information:

- the current Iron Nest coordinate;
- direct target coordinates;
- spotter coordinates and bearings;
- distances from reference points;
- approximate search directions;
- a shell restriction;
- a timer and its actual trigger;
- a reconnaissance card;
- a movement or Position Report;
- a War Horn condition;
- a final stand-down condition.

<figure className="my-8">
  <Image
    src="/images/iron-nest/iron-nest-teleprinter-orders.webp"
    alt="Iron Nest teleprinter displaying mission objectives coordinates and firing restrictions"
    width={1600}
    height={900}
    className="rounded-xl"
  />
  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-500 dark:text-slate-400">
    Read every page of the order. The final paragraph often changes when the
    timer starts or what actually completes the mission.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

The order determines what kind of map problem I am solving.

### Direct coordinate

Mark the supplied target and measure from Iron Nest.

### Spotter intersection

Draw each bearing from the named spotter, place the target at the intersection,
then measure a separate final line from Iron Nest.

### Bearing and distance

Begin both measurements at the reference named in the report. Do not assume
that every distance begins at the Nest.

### Search sector

Words such as east, south, or southeast define an area to investigate. They are
not automatically precise firing bearings.

### Correction

Begin the correction at the previous impact point and move in the reported
direction by the reported distance.

### Position Report

A single report gives a possible line containing Iron Nest. Two reports provide
the intersection needed for a reliable new firing origin.

<h2 id="map-tools-and-cleanup">
  Use map tools for separate jobs
</h2>

The map becomes unreliable when every pencil line is treated as equally
important.

My normal map convention is:

- **red:** final Iron Nest-to-target firing line;
- **yellow:** spotter bearings, corrections, search work, and temporary support
  measurements;
- **white:** optional notes and planning references;
- **markers:** Iron Nest, confirmed targets, spotters, impacts, friendlies, and
  guesses;
- **Drafting Compass:** distance circles and radius-based construction;
- **Iris:** shell effect and danger-close checks.

I delete temporary construction lines after the target marker is confirmed.

Keep:

- the current Iron Nest marker;
- confirmed target markers;
- friendly and protected icons;
- the active final firing line;
- an impact marker still needed for correction.

Delete or relabel:

- rejected intersections;
- old firing origins;
- destroyed targets;
- expired waypoint solutions;
- pre-move firing lines;
- completed correction points;
- reconnaissance lines that no longer define the search area.

A cluttered map creates errors that still look mathematically valid.

<h2 id="bearing-range-charges-elevation">
  Keep bearing, range, charges, and elevation together
</h2>

A complete firing card needs four connected values:

- **Bearing:** measured from the current Iron Nest position to the final target
  point and entered into turret rotation.
- **Range:** measured on the same final line and entered into the calculator.
- **Powder charges:** selected as part of the calculator solution and physically
  loaded into the gun.
- **Elevation:** returned by the calculator for that exact range and charge
  count, then set on the selected gun.

<figure className="my-8">
  <Image
    src="/images/iron-nest/iron-nest-calculator-elevation.webp"
    alt="Iron Nest ballistic calculator converting range and powder charge count into gun elevation"
    width={1600}

  />

    Changing the charge count changes the elevation. The physical loader must
    match the calculator card.

I write the target number and gun side beside the values.

A firing card without a target label becomes dangerous when several solutions
have similar bearings. A firing card without a gun-side label becomes dangerous
once both elevations are prepared.

After Iron Nest moves, keep confirmed target markers but discard every old
bearing, range, charge, and elevation. The firing origin changed, so the
solution changed.

<h2 id="shells-and-target-choice">
  Choose the shell after confirming the target
</h2>

Shell choice is not a permanent tier list.

The correct shell depends on:

- what the order explicitly authorizes;
- target construction;
- target certainty;
- enemy spacing;
- friendly and protected positions;
- whether the goal is destruction, illumination, smoke, or correction.

The target role can matter as much as the target icon.

For example, AP can be correct for:

- an armored emplacement;
- an underground cache;
- a precise infantry position surrounded by friendlies.

The last case does not make the infantry armored. It makes a smaller, centered
impact safer than a larger H-shell effect.

<h2 id="requisition-recon-and-movement">
  Spend requisition after the current problem is known
</h2>

I use requisition to solve the active mission stage, not to fill every magazine.

Buy when:

- the target is confirmed;
- the shell requirement is known;
- the firing card is nearly ready;
- a reconnaissance gap genuinely blocks progress;
- an authorized movement or Position Report is required for recovery.

Wait when:

- the target marker is still a guess;
- an issued reconnaissance card has not resolved;
- a current shell is still in flight;
- an impact report may reveal additional enemies;
- the mission may change shell type in the next phase;
- the final stand-down message has already appeared.

A reconnaissance request should remove uncertainty that matters.

Good reconnaissance:

- crosses the named grid;
- covers a large unphotographed strip;
- searches a reported enemy sector;
- confirms a target type before AP or HCHE is purchased;
- reveals whether a shot is safe near friendlies.

Weak reconnaissance:

- overlaps a clear image;
- searches an old target position after command says it moved;
- is ordered before the current image develops;
- photographs a broad region without crossing the suspected target area.

Emergency Move follows a different rule. Use it when remaining in place is more
dangerous than rebuilding the firing solution.

After moving:

1. Delete the old Iron Nest marker.
2. Delete all final firing lines.
3. Keep target markers only when the targets themselves did not move.
4. Request enough position information to locate the new Nest.
5. Recalculate every shot from the new origin.

<h2 id="left-and-right-guns">
  Treat the left and right guns as separate firing states
</h2>

Once both guns are active, I stop thinking of Iron Nest as one cannon with two
levers.

Each side can have its own:

- shell;
- powder-charge count;
- elevation;
- loaded state;
- armed state;
- firing card.

<figure className="my-8">
  <Image
    src="/images/iron-nest/iron-nest-right-gun-link.webp"
    alt="Iron Nest left and right gun controls showing separate loading and elevation states"
    width={1600}
    height={900}
    className="rounded-xl"
  />
  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-500 dark:text-slate-400">
    Confirm the loaded side, displayed side, elevated side, and armed side before
    pulling the firing lever.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

Before every two-gun sequence, I label the cards `LEFT` and `RIGHT`.

Then I verify:

1. Left card matches the left loader.
2. Right card matches the right loader.
3. Both charge counts match their cards.
4. Both elevations are set on the correct sides.
5. The turret begins at the first planned bearing.
6. The second target bearing can be reached without confusing the cards.

Two preloaded guns are valuable in Counter-Battery and other timed missions,
but they also make a reversed firing card twice as expensive.

<h2 id="return-to-the-right-station">
  Return to the station that owns the error
</h2>

When a shot fails, I do not restart the entire process immediately.

I return to the part of the Nest that owns the missing information.

<GuideTable
  caption="Where to return when an Iron Nest shot or mission is stuck."
  columns={recoveryColumns}
  rows={recoveryRows}
/>

This separation prevents random correction.

When the shell lands on the correct bearing line but at the wrong distance, I
do not redraw every spotter report. I inspect the range, charges, elevation, and
impact correction.

When the shell lands in a completely different direction, I do not begin by
buying another shell. I inspect the final firing line, turret bearing, selected
gun, and whether an old card was used.

When the target is destroyed but the mission remains active, I do not fire at
the same coordinate again. I read the next teleprinter message.

<h2 id="challenge-mode">
  Challenge Mode stays a systems test
</h2>

Challenge Mode uses the same map and gun systems, but mistakes cost more time.

The useful habits are:

- solve the target before rushing the loader;
- keep only one active firing solution visible;
- assign left and right cards clearly;
- use one shot for multiple targets only when the effect area is safe;
- use reconnaissance when it removes more time than it costs;
- wait for confirmations instead of firing duplicate shells;
- stop refining decimals when the target effect already provides safe coverage.

This Hub does not claim a complete high-score route or permanent Challenge Mode
meta. A useful standalone Challenge guide needs verified scoring rules, target
generation, ending conditions, and repeatable high-score runs.

<h2 id="universal-mistakes">
  Universal mistakes to fix before blaming the gun
</h2>

### The final line begins at the wrong origin

Spotter reports locate the target. The firing line begins at the current Iron
Nest marker.

### An old Iron Nest marker remains after movement

Every bearing, range, charge count, and elevation must be recalculated from the
new position.

### The physical charge count does not match the calculator

A correct elevation belongs to one specific range-and-charge combination.

### The wrong gun side is armed

Check loaded side, elevated side, displayed side, and armed side.

### A search direction is treated as an exact target bearing

Use east, south, and southeast to define a sector. Confirm the target before
building the firing card.

### A correction is applied from Iron Nest

Observer corrections normally begin at the previous impact point.

### A new order is treated as part of the old shot

Clear completed lines and solve the new phase from its own instructions.

### The mission is assumed complete after the main explosion

Wait for the teleprinter's completion or stand-down message. Some missions add
new targets, support calls, reconnaissance stages, or optional cleanup after the
first objective.

### Coordinates from another run are copied as fixed answers

The worked values in this cluster show the process and provide a reality check.
Your operation's orders remain the source of truth.
