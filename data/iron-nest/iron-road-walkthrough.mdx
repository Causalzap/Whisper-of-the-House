<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
**Iron Road has two required phases.** First, read both orders, mark Iron Nest
at **B4 5:0** and Main Station at **J6 0:4**, then draw the railway east from
the station at **90°**. Mark the train's 6 km, 4 km, and 2 km waypoints with
their scheduled times.

Choose a waypoint that gives you enough time to calculate, load, rotate, and
account for shell flight. Your launch time is:

**waypoint arrival time − shell flight time**

After the train is destroyed, locate the two armored emplacements blocking the
blue allied route. Destroy both with AP and wait for both confirmations before
using the War Horn. Sounding it early causes mass casualties.

For the fastest clear, sound the horn as soon as both emplacements are gone.
Clear optional town targets first only when you are deliberately pursuing
kills, requisition, or additional medal conditions.

<h2 id="iron-road-route">
  Iron Road route at a glance
</h2>

Iron Road is the first mission where locating the target is not enough.

The train is moving. A perfect firing solution aimed at the train's current
position can still miss because the train continues along the railway while the
shell is in flight.

The safe route is:

1. Read the main operation order and the train schedule.
2. Mark Iron Nest at B4 5:0.
3. Mark Main Station at J6 0:4.
4. Draw the railway from the station at 90°.
5. Mark the 6 km, 4 km, and 2 km train waypoints.
6. Write each waypoint's scheduled arrival time beside its marker.
7. Choose a waypoint that still leaves enough preparation time.
8. Calculate the static firing solution to that waypoint.
9. Subtract shell flight time from the train's arrival time.
10. Fire at the calculated launch time.
11. Read the next order after the train is destroyed.
12. Locate both armored emplacements.
13. Destroy both with AP.
14. Decide whether to clear optional town targets.
15. Sound the War Horn only after both emplacement confirmations.
16. Stand down when command reports that the approach is clear.

The <Link href="/iron-nest">Iron Nest guide hub</Link> covers normal map
measurement, calculator use, ammunition, and gun controls. This page focuses on
moving-target timing and the War Horn decision unique to Iron Road.

<h2 id="read-both-iron-road-orders">
  Read both Iron Road orders
</h2>

The main teleprinter order gives:

- Iron Nest's current coordinate;
- Main Station's coordinate;
- the train's expected station-arrival time;
- an instruction to intercept it before arrival.

The secondary order gives:

- the railway direction;
- waypoint distances;
- the train's movement schedule;
- the train's approximate composition.

In one operation:

- Iron Nest: **B4 5:0**
- Main Station: **J6 0:4**
- station arrival: **T = 10:16:50**
- railway bearing: **90° east from Main Station**

The waypoint schedule was:

- **6.00 km from Main Station — 10:06:50**
- **4.00 km from Main Station — 10:10:10**
- **2.00 km from Main Station — 10:13:30**
- **Main Station — 10:16:50**

Do not order reconnaissance merely to discover the train's current position.
The schedule already defines its route and expected position at each listed
time.

The problem is to choose a future point, calculate a shot to that point, and
make the shell arrive when the train does.

<h2 id="mark-station-and-waypoints">
  Mark Main Station and the three train waypoints
</h2>

Place Iron Nest and Main Station first.

From Main Station, draw a straight line at **90°**. The train is approaching the
station along this railway, so the waypoint markers extend eastward from the
station.

Measure and mark:

1. 2 km from the station;
2. 4 km from the station;
3. 6 km from the station.

I label them by both distance and time:

- `6 km — 10:06:50`
- `4 km — 10:10:10`
- `2 km — 10:13:30`

Do not use the waypoint distance as the artillery range.

`6 km` means the train is six kilometres from Main Station. The actual firing
range is the distance from Iron Nest to that waypoint and can be much longer.

Keep the railway line and waypoint labels visible until the train is confirmed
destroyed.

<h2 id="choose-train-waypoint">
  Choose a waypoint that still gives you preparation time
</h2>

The earliest waypoint is not always the best waypoint.

Continue with a waypoint when there is enough time to:

- draw the Iron Nest-to-waypoint firing line;
- calculate range and elevation;
- procure or load the shell;
- load the correct powder charges;
- rotate the turret;
- set gun elevation;
- wait for the shell's flight time.

Move to the next waypoint when:

- the train is already too close to the current point;
- the required launch time has passed;
- the gun is not loaded;
- the firing solution is still incomplete;
- you are unsure which waypoint the card belongs to.

The 2 km marker is the final planned interception point before the station. It
provides more setup time than the earlier waypoints, but leaves little recovery
time before the train reaches its destination.

Do not keep adjusting a marker to an estimated live train position. The
schedule already gives three stable interception points that can be solved like
normal stationary targets.

<h2 id="subtract-shell-flight-time">
  Subtract shell flight time from the train's arrival time
</h2>

The waypoint time tells you when the train should reach the target point.

It does not tell you when to fire.

Use:

> **Launch time = waypoint arrival time − shell flight time**

For example, when:

- the train reaches a waypoint at `10:10:10`;
- the shell takes approximately 30 seconds to arrive;

the ideal launch time is approximately:

- `10:09:40`

Check the displayed flight time after entering the range and charge count. Do
not assume every Iron Road shot takes 30 seconds. Flight time changes with the
solution.

I keep the gun ready before the launch window:

- shell loaded;
- charges loaded;
- turret bearing set;
- elevation set;
- correct gun selected;
- firing control unlocked.

Then I wait at the clock rather than running between stations during the last
few seconds.

<h2 id="late-train-shot">
  What to do when the train shot is already late
</h2>

In the documented operation, the train was roughly **15 seconds** from the
selected waypoint while the shell needed around **30 seconds** to arrive.

The shell was therefore launched late.

It still destroyed the train, probably because the train had several cars and
the explosion covered enough of the railway behind the exact waypoint.

That is not a repeatable timing rule.

Do not conclude that firing 15 seconds late is better. A shorter train, smaller
effect area, slightly different waypoint, or different solution could miss.

When the launch time has passed but you have not fired:

1. Move to the next scheduled waypoint.
2. Draw a new final line.
3. Recalculate bearing, range, charges, elevation, and flight time.
4. Calculate the new launch time.
5. Prepare the gun before that window.

When a late shell is already in flight, wait for its result before buying and
firing a second shell. The first blast may still catch part of the train.

<h2 id="locate-two-emplacements">
  Locate both armored emplacements
</h2>

The emplacement reports use named spotters, bearings, and distances.

Use those reports to place separate markers for Emplacement 1 and Emplacement
2. The general triangulation method remains in the
<Link href="/iron-nest/fire-and-light-walkthrough">
  Fire and Light walkthrough
</Link>

For Iron Road, the important task-specific checks are:

- both markers lie near the route they are blocking;
- neither final firing line begins at a spotter;
- the blue allied route remains outside any optional destructive fire;
- both cards are labelled before the War Horn is touched.

When one emplacement marker has two plausible positions, fire at the more
certain target first. Its impact image can clarify the surviving emplacement
before the second AP shot.

Before firing, verify:

- AP is physically loaded;
- two charges are loaded for the example solution, or the count printed by your

- the turret is at the final Iron Nest-to-target bearing;
- the selected gun is set to the matching elevation.

After impact, read the updated reconnaissance before deleting every nearby
marker. The surviving emplacement may become easier to distinguish from
mechanized or logistical icons.

<h2 id="second-ap-emplacement">
  Recheck the second emplacement before firing
</h2>

The first impact clarified the second position in the documented operation.

The corrected second solution used:

- **79.1° bearing**
- **2 powder charges**
- **48.96° elevation**

The exact final range was not clearly preserved, so calculate it from your own
marker rather than copying an incomplete card.

In that run, the AP impact destroyed the second emplacement and a mechanized
unit located inside the same precise target area.

Do not deliberately shift the AP marker toward another icon unless both targets
still fall inside the confirmed impact area. The emplacement is the required
objective.

Wait until the teleprinter or reconnaissance confirms that both blocking
positions are destroyed.

Do not sound the War Horn based only on:

- seeing two explosions;
- spending two AP shells;
- destroying one emplacement and one mechanized unit;
- assuming the second marker was correct.

The horn is the commitment that the allied route is safe.

<h2 id="optional-targets-before-war-horn">
  Decide whether to clear optional targets before the War Horn
</h2>

After both emplacements are destroyed, there are two valid routes.

### Fast completion route

Sound the War Horn immediately after both destruction confirmations.

Use this route when your priority is:

- minimum ammunition;
- minimum time;
- minimum allied risk;
- straightforward mission completion.

### Optional destruction route

Before sounding the horn, clear selected enemy targets that are safely away
from the blue assault route.

The documented operation found that:

- an H shell cost approximately **10 requisition**;
- several smaller targets returned about **5 requisition** each;
- destroying the train station returned **75 requisition**.

The train station was therefore a strong optional target in that operation.
Ordinary five-point targets did not fully repay a ten-point H shell unless one
shot destroyed several units or helped a medal condition.

Do not fire optional shots merely because an enemy icon remains.

Continue only when:

- the target is clearly hostile;
- its effect area stays clear of the blue route;
- the shot destroys several units or a high-value installation;
- you are deliberately pursuing kills or medal conditions;
- enough ammunition remains for the required mission sequence.

Optional targets are safest **before** the War Horn. After the signal, allied
infantry begin advancing through the area.

<h2 id="sound-war-horn">
  Sound the War Horn only after both confirmations
</h2>

Before using the horn, confirm:

- the train is destroyed;
- Emplacement 1 is destroyed;
- Emplacement 2 is destroyed;
- both required AP reports have arrived;
- no optional shot is still in flight;
- the blue route is clear.

Now sound the War Horn.

The mission then reports that:

- the approach is clear;
- allied infantry are advancing;
- the primary objective is complete;
- remaining enemy elements in the town are no longer required targets;
- Iron Nest may stand down.

Do not order another reconnaissance flight or continue firing destructive
shells after the assault begins unless you have confirmed that the effect area

<h2 id="iron-road-medal-routes">
  Choose the medal route before optional fire
</h2>

The result screen from one operation rewarded performance tied to:

- minimum allied casualties;
- consecutive kills across multiple fire missions;
- a high kill-per-shot ratio across at least ten enemy kills;
- destroying a significant number of targets without requisitioning aerial
  reconnaissance.

That run cleared several optional targets before sounding the War Horn.

These conditions can conflict with a pure minimum-assets route.

For an efficient clear:

- use one train shell;
- use two AP shells;
- sound the horn after both emplacements;
- stop firing.

For a destruction-focused clear:

- group safe targets before the horn;
- prioritize the 75-requisition train station;
- avoid single five-requisition kills that cost a full ten-requisition shell;
- keep all optional blast areas away from the allied route.

The mission-completion achievement is **All Clear**. Earning Gold on every Iron
Road medal unlocks **Crowned in Brass**, but this page does not claim a fully
verified all-Gold route.

<h2 id="iron-road-stuck">
  Iron Road mistakes that waste the mission
</h2>

### Using the station-arrival time as the firing time

`10:16:50` is when the train reaches Main Station. The shell must arrive at a
waypoint before that.

### Measuring 6 km, 4 km, and 2 km from Iron Nest

Those distances begin at Main Station and define train positions along the
railway.

### Ignoring shell flight time

A correct waypoint solution still misses when the shell and train arrive at
different times.

### Treating the late hit as the ideal route

The documented shot was late and probably caught the train because of its
length and the explosion radius.

### Continuing with a waypoint after its launch time passes

Move to the next waypoint and recalculate every firing value.

### Sounding the War Horn after destroying only the train

The train is only the first phase. Two AP emplacements still block the allied
route.

### Loading H against the emplacements

The order explicitly requires AP.

### Confusing the blue route with an enemy line

The blue lines show where allied infantry will advance.

### Blowing the horn after only one AP confirmation

Two explosions do not prove that both required positions were destroyed.

### Firing optional shells after the horn

The remaining enemies are no longer required, and allied units are advancing.

### Chasing every five-requisition target

A ten-requisition shell does not pay for itself when it destroys only one
five-point unit.
