<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
**Fire and Light has one destructive phase followed by one illumination
phase.** Start by marking Iron Nest and both spotters. Draw the reported
bearings from the spotters, place the first hostile target at their
intersection, and then measure the final firing line from Iron Nest.

After the first H shell lands, reconnaissance reveals two additional hostile
elements. Center a second H shot between them only when both targets fall inside
the safe effect area.

Once Phase One is complete, switch to Star Shells. The first Star shot is an
observation shot. Read the yellow arrow and yellow distance from its impact
image, move **3.28 km from the impact point** in the shown direction, and
recalculate the entire firing solution.

In one operation, the corrected Star solution was
**326.1° / 5.64 km / 2 charges / 33.84°**. Stand down only after command reports
`Starburst confirmed` and `Fire mission complete`.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

<h2 id="fire-and-light-route">
  Fire and Light route at a glance
</h2>

Fire and Light is where I first learned that one mission can change its target,
shell type, and map logic halfway through.

The reliable route is:

1. Read the opening mission order.
2. Mark the current Iron Nest coordinate.
3. Mark Spotter 1 and Spotter 2.
4. Draw both reported spotter bearings.
5. Place the first hostile target at their intersection.
6. Draw a final firing line from Iron Nest to that target.
7. Fire the required H shell.
8. Inspect the new reconnaissance image.
9. Destroy the two additional hostile elements.
10. Clear the completed destructive lines from the map.
11. Read the Star Shell-only palace illumination order.
12. Plot the initial illumination point.
13. Fire one Star Shell.
14. Read the yellow correction arrow and distance.
15. Move from the Star Shell impact point, not from Iron Nest.
16. Recalculate bearing, range, charges, and elevation.
17. Fire the corrected Star Shell.
18. Stand down after the satisfactory-starburst confirmation.

The <Link href="/iron-nest">Iron Nest guide hub</Link> covers the general map,
calculator, loader, shell, and fire-control systems. This page focuses on the
mission-specific transition from spotter triangulation to Star Shell correction.

<h2 id="read-fire-and-light-orders">
  Separate the two Fire and Light phases
</h2>

The opening briefing describes two different operations:

- hostile anti-monarchist elements operating inside the city;
- a celebratory illumination over the royal palace.

Do not prepare both phases at the same time.

The first phase requires destructive H fire. The palace operation does not
become active until the hostile targets are destroyed, and its order explicitly
requires Star Shells.

I keep the phases separate on the map:

- Phase One uses numbered hostile target markers;
- Phase Two uses a fresh illumination marker;
- completed H-shell firing lines are removed before the Star order is plotted;
- the first Star impact receives its own temporary marker.

This prevents the most common Fire and Light mistake: copying a valid H-shell
bearing or target marker into the later illumination mission.

<h2 id="mark-iron-nest-and-spotters">
  Mark Iron Nest and both spotters
</h2>

In one operation, the opening coordinates were:

- Iron Nest: **H3 5:5**
- Spotter 1: **L9 8:6**
- Spotter 2: **L2 3:8**

The field reports then gave:

- **96° from Spotter 1**
- **53° from Spotter 2**

Your operation may print different coordinates or bearings, so treat these as a
worked example rather than fixed Fire and Light answers.

Place all three reference markers before drawing either bearing.

The two yellow bearing lines must begin at the spotters:

- the 96° line begins at Spotter 1;
- the 53° line begins at Spotter 2.

They do not begin at Iron Nest.

Extend both bearing lines far enough to create a clear intersection. A short
line can make the target look like a broad area rather than one usable point.

Place the hostile target marker at the crossing, then stop using the spotter
lines for the firing controls.

<h2 id="calculate-first-h-shot">
  Calculate the first H-shell solution from Iron Nest
</h2>

Once the intersection is marked, draw a new final line from Iron Nest to the
hostile target.

In the documented operation, that line produced:

- **65.7° bearing**
- **12.91 km range**
- **3 powder charges**
- **51.64° elevation**
- **H shell**

  <Image
    src="/images/iron-nest/iron-nest-fire-and-light-first-h-solution.webp"
    alt="Iron Nest Fire and Light first H-shell solution showing 65.7 degrees, 12.91 km, three charges, and 51.64 degrees elevation"
    width={1600}
    height={900}
    className="rounded-xl"
  />
  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">
    Do not copy either spotter bearing into the turret. The final turret bearing
    comes from the Iron Nest-to-target line.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

Before firing, verify:

- H is loaded;
- three charges are physically in the loader for this example solution;
- the turret is set to 65.7°;
- the selected gun is set to 51.64°;
- the fire-control card belongs to the first hostile target.

The operation is not under a Counter-Battery Timer, so there is no reason to
rush an uncertain intersection.

Return to the map when:

- the two spotter lines do not meet cleanly;
- the target marker sits on only one line;
- the final red line begins at a spotter;
- the range looks like a spotter-to-target measurement;
- the turret bearing matches one of the field reports instead of the final
  firing line.

Fire only after the intersection and final solution agree.

<h2 id="find-two-additional-hostiles">
  Use the first impact image to find two additional hostiles
</h2>

The first confirmed hit does not complete Phase One.

After impact, the teleprinter reports that reconnaissance has identified
**two additional hostile elements**. Both must be destroyed before the palace
illumination order becomes active.

In one operation, the two icons were close enough to place one H-shell target
marker between them.

The combined solution was approximately:

- **68.3° bearing**
- **12.97 km range**
- **3 powder charges**
- **51.88° elevation**

    width={1600}

    className="rounded-xl"
  />
  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">
    One centered H shot can clear both follow-up targets when both icons fall
    inside the effect area.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

Do not reuse the first target marker simply because the new bearing and range
are similar. The second shot belongs to a different point.

Before committing to one shell, confirm that:

- both new hostile icons are inside the intended impact area;
- the target center is between them rather than directly over one icon;
- no protected or friendly marker falls inside the same area;
- both targets are confirmed by the new reconnaissance image.

When the two targets are too far apart, prepare separate shots instead of
forcing a one-shell solution.

Wait for both destruction confirmations. The next phase begins only after the
teleprinter reports that Phase One is complete.

<h2 id="switch-to-star-shells">
  Clear the H-shell map and switch to Star Shells
</h2>

The next order changes the operation completely.

Command requests a starburst over the royal palace and states:

- **Star Shell only**
- non-combat operation;
- fire near the supplied position;
- a ground observer will issue corrections;
- repeat until the burst position is satisfactory.

<figure className="my-8">
  <Image
    src="/images/iron-nest/iron-nest-fire-and-light-star-shell-order.webp"
    alt="Iron Nest Fire and Light order requiring Star Shell illumination over the royal palace"
    width={1600}
    height={900}
    className="rounded-xl"
  />
  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">
    The first Star Shell is expected to generate a correction. It is not
    necessarily the final mission shot.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

Before plotting it:

1. Remove the completed hostile firing lines.
2. Keep the current Iron Nest marker.
3. Keep the spotter needed by the new palace report.
4. Load one Star Shell.
5. Keep another Star Shell available for the expected correction.
6. Do not preload a second elevation before the first impact report arrives.

The correction can change bearing, range, charge count, and elevation. Preparing
two identical Star Shell solutions in advance does not save useful time.

<h2 id="plot-first-star-shell">
  Plot the first palace illumination point
</h2>

In one operation, the initial palace estimate used:

- **248° from Spotter 1**
- **4.52 km from Spotter 1**

That places an approximate illumination marker.

From Iron Nest to that initial marker, the firing solution was:

- **1.4° bearing**
- **4.41 km range**
- **1 powder charge**
- **52.92° elevation**
- **Star Shell**

The value that goes into the turret is 1.4°, not the 248° spotter bearing.

Do not move the point simply because it does not look like the place where you
expected the palace to be. The observer system is designed to correct the
initial estimate after the first burst.

Fire one Star Shell and wait for the observer report.

<h2 id="read-star-shell-correction">
  Read the yellow Star Shell correction correctly
</h2>

The first Star Shell in the documented operation did not complete the mission.

The resulting image displayed:

- a yellow arrow;
- a yellow distance of **3.28 km**.

  <Image
    src="/images/iron-nest/iron-nest-fire-and-light-star-shell-correction.webp"
    alt="Iron Nest Fire and Light observer correction showing a yellow direction arrow and 3.28 km distance"
    width={1600}
    height={900}
    className="rounded-xl"
  />
  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">
    The yellow arrow points from the first Star Shell impact toward the desired
    burst position. The yellow number is the correction distance.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

The `3.28 km` value is not:

- the new range from Iron Nest;
- the distance from Spotter 1;
- an elevation value;
- the length of the arrow graphic;
- permission to repeat the same shot.

It means:

> Begin at the first Star Shell impact and move 3.28 km in the direction shown
> by the yellow arrow.

Place a marker at the first impact before drawing the correction. The impact,
not the original palace estimate, is the origin of this 3.28 km movement.

<h2 id="build-corrected-star-point">
  Build the corrected point from the first impact
</h2>

From the first impact marker:

1. Follow the yellow arrow's direction.
2. Measure 3.28 km.
3. Place a new illumination marker at the corrected point.
4. Delete or clearly label the old illumination marker.
5. Draw a new firing line from Iron Nest to the corrected marker.
6. Recalculate the full solution.

Do not draw 3.28 km from Iron Nest.

That mistake can still produce a valid-looking range and calculator card, but
it moves the target into the wrong part of the map.

Also do not add 3.28 km directly to the old 4.41 km firing range. The correction
changes direction as well as distance, so the new Iron Nest-to-target range
must be measured from the map.

<h2 id="fire-corrected-star-shell">
  Fire the corrected Star Shell
</h2>

The corrected marker in one operation produced:

- **326.1° bearing**
- **5.64 km range**
- **2 powder charges**
- **33.84° elevation**
- **Star Shell**

    elevation.

Notice how different the corrected shot is from the first Star Shell:

- bearing changes from 1.4° to 326.1°;
- range changes from 4.41 km to 5.64 km;
- charge count changes from one to two;
- elevation changes from 52.92° to 33.84°.

This is why repeating the first firing card cannot complete the correction.

Before firing, confirm:

- Star Shell loaded;
- two charges loaded for the example solution;
- corrected bearing set;
- corrected elevation set;
- gun side matches the active firing card;
- old H-shell or first-Star cards are no longer being read.

After impact, wait for the teleprinter.

Do not fire a third Star Shell while the observer report is still pending.

<h2 id="fire-and-light-completion">
  Stand down after Starburst Confirmed
</h2>

The required completion message is:

- `Starburst confirmed`
- burst position satisfactory;
- fire mission complete;
- stand down.

The mission does not end when:

- the first hostile target is destroyed;
- the two reconnaissance targets are destroyed;
- Phase One is complete;
- the first Star Shell lands;
- the correction marker is plotted.

It ends only after the corrected Star Shell receives the satisfactory-burst
confirmation.

The completion achievement is **Honour Salute**. Earning Gold on every Fire and
Light medal unlocks **Marksman's Honour**, but the available achievement list
does not provide the exact Gold thresholds.

<h2 id="fire-and-light-stuck">
  Fire and Light mistakes that block completion
</h2>

### Drawing both spotter bearings from Iron Nest

The 96° and 53° reports begin at their named spotters.

### Copying a spotter bearing into the turret

The turret uses the final Iron Nest-to-target bearing, which was 65.7° in the
worked example.

### Using 12.91 km as a spotter measurement

That is the final range from Iron Nest to the first target in the documented
operation.

### Standing down after the first H-shell hit

The impact reveals two additional hostile elements that must also be destroyed.

### Aiming the second H shell directly at only one icon

Center the point between both targets when one safe effect area can cover them.

### Keeping the Phase One lines on the map

The old hostile bearings can be confused with the palace illumination solution.

### Loading another H shell after the palace order

The second phase is Star Shell only and is explicitly non-combat.

### Expecting the first Star Shell to complete the mission

The first shot is used to generate observer correction data.

### Measuring 3.28 km from Iron Nest

The correction begins at the first Star Shell impact.

### Adding 3.28 directly to the old 4.41 km range

The yellow arrow changes direction too. Plot the corrected point, then measure a
new line from Iron Nest.

### Reusing the first Star Shell charge count

The documented first shot used one charge, while the corrected shot used two.

### Firing again before the observer responds

Wait for either another correction or the satisfactory-starburst confirmation.
