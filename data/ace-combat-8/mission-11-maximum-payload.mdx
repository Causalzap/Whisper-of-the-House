Mission 11 is an interception against aircraft large enough to look impossible to
lose in the clouds. A Podarge is roughly **267 meters long**, spans about **967
meters**, uses **8 turboprop engines**, and can carry up to **36,000 tons**, yet
thunderclouds and electronic warfare can reduce detection enough that the formation
disappears from your instruments surprisingly easily.

Those transports are carrying disassembled Land Battleship components toward Rocky
Island. Every surviving Podarge therefore matters. Joker Flight can spend half the
sortie winning dogfights and still lose the real race if one transport continues
toward the edge of the operation area.

<section id="prepare-for-interception" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Bring enough air-to-air ammunition for repeated passes

Maximum Payload is almost entirely an air battle, and the Podarges absorb far more
attention than ordinary aircraft.

The fleet is already short on missiles before launch, so the remaining air-to-air
supply is being committed to this interception. Build around endurance rather than
one spectacular attack. You need enough firepower to work through several large
transports while still being able to remove a defensive gun or fighter that blocks
an approach.

Anti-surface equipment contributes little here. A more useful setup gives you:

- enough standard missiles for repeated attacks;
- an air-to-air special weapon you can use more than once;
- an aircraft that remains comfortable during long turns and reacquisition passes.

There is no requirement to use one particular plane or special weapon. A familiar
aircraft with sufficient ammunition is more useful than an unfamiliar high-cost
option that leaves you struggling to stay lined up on the transport.

Do not empty the opening load into every fighter on radar. Escorts are part of the
pressure, but the ammunition spent clearing distant aircraft is ammunition no
longer available when another Podarge still has to be brought down.

</section>

<section id="find-the-podarges" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Follow the contrails when radar stops being useful

The first obstacle is locating the transports.

Thunderclouds cover the operation area while electronic-warfare aircraft shorten
radar and lock range. Searching by instruments alone can leave Joker Flight moving
through apparently empty sky while the transport formation continues toward Rocky
Island.

The Podarges leave a much better clue:

![Huge contrails reveal a Podarge through the Mission 11 jamming.](/images/ace-combat-8/mission-11-podarge-contrails.webp)

Look for the broad contrails cutting through the cloud layer and follow one toward
its source. As the distance closes, the transport becomes visible and the shorter
lock opportunities become usable again.

An EW aircraft is worth destroying when it is already close to the same attack
line and its interference is making the next pass difficult. Hunting a jammer far
away from the transport usually costs more than it saves. When a Podarge is already
in sight and moving toward the boundary, stay with it.

Queen Flight and other friendly aircraft are under pressure elsewhere during the
interception. That radio traffic can make breaking away sound urgent, but their
fight is also buying Joker Flight time to stop the shipment. Only leave the
transport for an escort that is actively forcing you defensive or repeatedly
breaking the next attack run.

</section>

<section id="bring-down-a-podarge" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Slow the Podarge, strip propulsion, then finish it

A Podarge exposes several targetable systems across its enormous airframe:
**engines, propellers, wing sections, and defensive weapons**.

Propulsion gives you the clearest way to control the interception. Engine damage
slows the aircraft, buying more time before it reaches the edge of the battle.
Destroying propellers removes more of its ability to keep flying.

![A Podarge propeller becomes a direct target during Mission 11.](/images/ace-combat-8/mission-11-podarge-propellers.webp)

Approach along one wing, damage the propulsion targets you can reach cleanly, then
extend far enough to turn without sitting directly behind the aircraft. Come back
on another section rather than trying to remain glued to the same rear angle.

That matters because the Podarge carries its own defenses. AA weapons can punish a
slow chase while escorts attack from outside your field of view.

A defensive gun deserves a missile when it is covering the wing you need for the
next pass. Once that lane is open, go back to engines and propellers. Dismantling
every gun on the aircraft is unnecessary when the transport itself is still
moving toward Rocky Island.

The most efficient pattern is usually:

1. remove a defensive weapon only when it blocks the approach;
2. damage an engine when slowing the Podarge will buy useful time;
3. continue through exposed propellers and propulsion components;
4. stay on that transport until its destruction is confirmed.

### Do not spread damage across several transports

Multiple Podarges can remain airborne at the same time, which makes switching
targets tempting.

That often creates the worst possible result: several damaged aircraft and no
confirmed kill.

A transport that is already slowed, missing propulsion components, and beginning
to fall away from formation is closer to being removed from the mission. Finish
it before committing to a fresh Podarge unless another transport is about to
escape immediately.

Once the operation confirms the kill, turn toward the next surviving aircraft.

### Smoke and engine fire do not mean the Podarge is finished

The size of the transport makes visual damage misleading.

A Podarge can be smoking, burning, or missing components and still remain airborne.
Do not leave because the aircraft looks doomed. Wait for the mission confirmation
and the transport count to change.

The interception is not complete until **every Podarge has been destroyed**.

When one finally goes down, break away from its path. The wreck is enormous, and
falling debris creates another hazard below the aircraft. There is no benefit in
flying underneath it while trying to acquire the next target.

</section>

<section id="finish-the-air-battle" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Keep the 55th Unit behind the transports in your priority order

The **55th Unit** adds a much stronger fighter threat later in the operation.

Its arrival changes how difficult the interception feels, but it does not change
why Joker Flight is there. While a Podarge remains airborne, a 55th Unit fighter
only moves ahead of the transport when it is directly preventing another attack.

Break its pressure, regain the transport, and continue the interception.

There is one exception for players returning for **Larus**: that named-aircraft
condition changes the required order between the Shadows and the Podarge. The
exact trigger belongs with the other mission-specific requirements in the
[Assault Records checklist](/ace-combat-8/assault-records).

For a normal clear, the priority flips only after the final Podarge is confirmed
destroyed. At that point no cargo aircraft remains to escape, so Joker Flight can
commit fully to the remaining critical air threat.

</section>

<section id="after-mission-11" className="scroll-mt-24">

## What happens after the last Podarge goes down?

Destroying every transport prevents the disassembled Land Battleship components
from reaching Rocky Island. Joker Flight then finishes the remaining air battle
and the interception ends.

The fleet's next problem is logistical rather than another transport chase. The
Endurance is preparing to move toward **Fort Grays** for badly needed fuel and
ammunition, but contact with the base has already been lost.

Continue with the
[Ace Combat 8 walkthrough](/ace-combat-8/walkthrough) for Mission 12 and the
defense of Fort Grays.
