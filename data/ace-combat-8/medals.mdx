Ace Combat 8 has **29 Campaign Medals**, but you do not need all 29 for
Meritorious Service. That achievement only asks for **10 medals**, which changes
the best cleanup route considerably.

The long-term kill medals and six full-campaign restrictions are useful when you
are going for everything. They are poor places to start when you only need a
few more medals. The mission-specific challenges give you a defined objective,
an immediate result, and usually require one sortie instead of an entire
campaign.

<section id="campaign-medals" className="scroll-mt-24">

## There are 29 Campaign Medals

The Campaign Medals screen tracks all 29 separately.

![All 29 Campaign Medals in Ace Combat 8.](/images/ace-combat-8/ace-combat-8-campaign-medals.webp)

They fall into four useful groups:

- cumulative destruction medals;
- S-Rank medals;
- one-mission challenge medals;
- full-campaign restriction medals.

Knowing which group you are working on matters because progress is handled very
differently. A Bronze Ace medal can grow naturally across many sorties, while
Proud Shoulders lives or dies entirely on what happens to the LCACs in Mission 5.

</section>

<section id="fast-ten-medals" className="scroll-mt-24">

## A practical route to 10 medals without long grinds

Before replaying anything, count the medals you already earned during the
campaign. A player who used the machine gun often, destroyed plenty of ships, or
earned several S ranks may already be much closer to 10 than the medal screen
first suggests.

When you still need a large block of medals, these ten mission challenges are a
good cleanup pool because none asks you to repeat thousands of kills or finish
another complete campaign under a restriction:

1. Mission 4 — navigate the narrow section in Operation Elder Hand.
2. Mission 5 — evacuate at least four LCACs.
3. Mission 9 — destroy all ten Land Aegis ships.
4. Mission 12 — destroy the Rapid Dragon before its cruise-missile launch.
5. Mission 14 — finish without being detected by radar.
6. Mission 17 — destroy every primary target with Joker Flight.
7. Mission 21 — let the escort target finish without taking damage.
8. Mission 23 — destroy every land ship.
9. Mission 25 — destroy all four Podarge.
10. Mission 27 — use a deck collapse to wipe out a large enemy group in one
    chain reaction.

This is not a fixed speedrun order. Skip anything you already have, and replace a
challenge that gives you trouble with another medal that is already close to
completion. Mission 30 is also a one-mission medal, but its perfect-defense
condition stretches across a long final sortie, so I would normally leave it
until after the shorter challenges.

</section>

## Leave the cumulative kill medals running in the background

Bronze, Silver, and Gold Ace ask for **1,000 / 2,000 / 3,000 total hostiles**.
The three Marksman medals instead count aircraft kills made with the machine
gun: **50 / 100 / 200**.

Raze the Roof requires **1,500 non-ship surface targets**, while Ship of Liberty
requires **100 ships**.

These are poor medals to force early unless one counter is already nearly done.
Normal campaign clears, S-Rank attempts, Assault Record cleanup, and MRP runs all
keep feeding the destruction totals. Grinding hundreds of extra targets for a
tenth medal is usually slower than completing another one-mission challenge.

Marksman is the exception when you already enjoy gun kills. Start finishing
damaged aircraft with the gun during ordinary replays rather than creating a
separate 200-kill grind at the end.

</section>

<section id="s-rank-medals" className="scroll-mt-24">

## The four S-Rank medals overlap

The wording matters here.

Pilot, Veteran, and Elite all say **that difficulty or higher**. Ace requires
Ace itself. That means an eventual all-mission Ace S-Rank run also satisfies the
difficulty requirement used by the three lower medals.

There is little reason to plan four completely separate S-Rank campaigns if Ace
S ranks are already your final goal. Work upward until you are comfortable with
the missions, then use the highest difficulty you actually intend to complete.

The time-versus-destruction balance changes by mission, so do not assume every S
rank is won by killing everything on the radar. Mission 29 is a good example:
a fast clear can earn S without destroying all four Escort UAVs.

</section>

<section id="mission-medals" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Mission medals: the conditions most likely to be missed

### Mission 4 — Tunnel Specter

The mission must include the **narrow-space route during Operation Elder Hand**.
Simply completing Mission 4 through the normal open path is not enough.

Treat the narrow section as the objective of the replay rather than something
to improvise after the rest of the fight is already finished.

### Mission 5 — Proud Shoulders

At least **four LCACs must evacuate** during Operation Long Shot.

The number matters more than your personal destruction score. When an enemy is
threatening the withdrawing landing craft, leave lower-priority targets and keep
those LCACs moving.

### Mission 9 — Aegis Penetrator

There are **10 Land Aegis ships** to destroy.

Do not end the run after breaking the Land Battleship and assume the surrounding
ships no longer matter. Count the Land Aegis kills before moving through the
last phase.

### Mission 12 — Rapid Dragon Slayer

The Rapid Dragon has to die **before it launches its cruise missiles**.

Once that aircraft becomes the relevant target, damage it immediately. A normal
Mission 12 clear after the launch does not satisfy the medal condition.

### Mission 14 — Homing Pigeon

This is a clean stealth condition: **zero radar detections**.

Do not trade cover for a shorter route. Staying low behind terrain and accepting
a slightly longer path is safer than crossing an exposed radar cone and losing
the medal near the end of the mission.

### Mission 17 — Broncobuster

Every primary target has to be destroyed by **Joker Flight**.

Allied kills are the danger here. When a marked primary target is already under
friendly attack, finish it before the allied unit can take the kill rather than
spreading damage across several targets.

### Mission 21 — Dawn Rising

The escort target must finish with **no damage taken**.

This is stricter than merely keeping it alive. Anything already on an attack run
against the escort moves ahead of convenient fighters elsewhere on the radar.

### Mission 23 — Land Piscivore

Destroy **every land ship** during Operation Rumbling.

Track the land ships separately from the rest of the ground battle. The mission
can keep moving even while one remains somewhere outside your current route.

</section>

<section id="mission-25-medal" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Mission 25: destroy the Podarge bodies, not just their weapons

Mission 25 asks for **all four Podarge**.

The first trap is the pair sitting around the bay. Stripping the weapons from
those transports is not the same as destroying the aircraft. The Podarge body
still has its own durability, so keep attacking until the transport itself is
actually destroyed.

Two more Podarge arrive later rather than sitting beside the first pair from the
start. Watch the later approach instead of ending the cleanup after the two
water-side transports.

For this medal, keep a simple count:

- first bay Podarge;
- second bay Podarge;
- first later-arriving Podarge;
- second later-arriving Podarge.

A run with four disarmed transports is not the same as a run with four destroyed
transports.

</section>

<section id="mission-27-medal" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Mission 27: leave enemies on the deck before collapsing it

Rootbreaker's medal is easiest to understand once you stop treating every red
target as something that should be killed individually.

Fatsia's float modules are held up by **Support Pillars**. Destroying those
supports can bring an entire deck section down at once.

![A Support Pillar collapse taking down a section of the Mission 27 Mega Float.](/images/ace-combat-8/mission-27-support-pillar-deck-collapse.webp)

For the medal attempt, choose a deck with a dense group of enemies and **leave
those targets alive while you work on the structure**. Destroy the supports and
let the collapse itself take the group with it.

Clearing the AA guns, parked targets, and other units first makes the structure
safer to attack, but it also removes the enemies you need for the chain
reaction. That is the main reason an otherwise successful Fatsia clear can miss
this medal.

Fuel and other explosive targets in the lower structure can also damage the
module, but the important result is the same: create one large structural
collapse while enough enemies are still sitting on the affected section.

Once the medal attempt is secured, return to the normal
[Mission 27 Fatsia route](/ace-combat-8/mission-27-fatsia-ocean-fortress) for the
surrender transition and central-reactor sequence.

</section>

<section id="mission-30-medal" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Mission 30: protect the Endurance for the entire sortie

Operation Showdown is longer than the other one-mission medal attempts because
the threat to the Endurance keeps changing.

At the opening, the submarine is still underwater and repeatedly fires
**anti-ship missiles**. Stay near enough to the carrier that a new launch does
not force a long return flight. The Endurance can shoot at some missiles itself,
but a medal attempt is not the time to rely on the ship absorbing a mistake.

The next critical targets are the **terminal-guidance UAVs**. They guide the
Tonitra Spear onto the moving carrier, and all of them need to be cleared before
the hostile laser solution remains active long enough to decide the strike.

![Laser-guidance UAVs threatening the Endurance during Mission 30.](/images/ace-combat-8/mission-30-laser-guidance-uavs.webp)

The same priority returns after the Rainband airship redirects the friendly
laser toward the submarine. Hostile guidance takes priority over that friendly
solution, so clear the entire hostile UAV group again rather than assuming the
Spear is already safely redirected.

Once the submarine surfaces, marked **active anti-ship weapons** remain the
important targets. Remove the launch systems while continuing to intercept
anything already heading for the Endurance.

Even the visible VLS launchers are not the end. A separate **underwater missile
launch tube** appears later and can continue putting anti-ship weapons into the
fight. Destroy it when the marker appears.

The safest medal run therefore follows the threat in this order:

1. Endurance-bound anti-ship missiles;
2. terminal-guidance UAVs;
3. another missile already close to impact before returning to other targets;
4. surfaced active anti-ship weapons and VLS systems;
5. the underwater missile launch tube;
6. the submarine itself when the final ramming attack begins.

The complete submarine phase, Rainband takeover, launch tube, and final ram are
covered in the
[Mission 30 Song of Wings route](/ace-combat-8/mission-30-song-of-wings).

</section>

<section id="campaign-restriction-medals" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Save the six full-campaign restriction medals for last

The final six medals are fundamentally different from a Mission 25 or Mission 27
cleanup. A mistake can invalidate much more than one short replay.

Four restrict your aircraft class:

- Attacker;
- Fighter;
- Multirole;
- Electronic Warfare.

Their wording applies to campaign missions **that allow aircraft selection**.
Forced-aircraft sections therefore do not require you to somehow match a class
you were never allowed to choose.

You also do not need a specific named aircraft for these medals. The requirement
is the class. If you already own a usable aircraft in the required category,
there is no reason to spend MRP on another one just for the medal. Buy or switch
only when a mission exposes a real weakness in range, ammunition, or weapon
coverage.

The last two restrictions are much harsher:

**No-Damage Campaign Medal** requires an entire campaign clear without taking
damage.

**Machine-Gun-Only Campaign Medal** requires a campaign clear using only the
machine gun.

Do the single-mission medals first. By the time you return for these runs, you
will already know which missions contain escort pressure, forced routes, long
boss phases, and places where ammunition or aircraft handling can turn a minor
mistake into a restart.

</section>

<section id="when-to-stop" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Stop at 10 if Meritorious Service is your only goal

Once the Campaign Medals screen shows **10 unlocked medals**, there is no benefit
to forcing a 3,000-kill counter or starting a no-damage campaign solely for
Meritorious Service.

Check the
[Ace Combat 8 trophy and achievement checklist](/ace-combat-8/trophies-achievements)
for whatever requirement is still incomplete.

Players going for all 29 should reverse the priority after the mission challenges
are finished: let the cumulative counters grow during S-Rank cleanup, then leave
the full-campaign restrictions for runs where they are the main objective rather
than an extra condition you are trying to remember halfway through the story.

</section>
