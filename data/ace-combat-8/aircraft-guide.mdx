<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
Ace Combat 8 has **34 aircraft** across Fighter, Multirole, Attacker, and Electronic
Warfare roles. For a first campaign, I would not spend every early MRP point rushing
toward a new plane. The Aircraft Tree also contains missile, mobility, weapon, and
support upgrades that improve every sortie. A practical progression route is to
build the early missile and handling upgrades first, use the F-16C area as a strong
early foundation, move toward a flexible mid-game multirole such as the **Rafale M**,
then commit to a late-game branch such as the **Su-57** or **F-22A** once the core
upgrades are already in place.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

The Aircraft Tree in Ace Combat 8 is not just a list of planes with increasingly
large price tags. Aircraft, special weapons, missile upgrades, handling parts, and
support systems are connected together, so the route you take determines how much
MRP you spend before reaching the aircraft you actually want.

That matters most during a first campaign. It is easy to see a new fighter several
nodes away and start buying directly toward it, but a route filled with useful
missile and handling upgrades can make the missions before that aircraft much easier.

If I were building for a smooth first clear rather than chasing a favorite real-world
airframe, I would prioritize a **useful path through the tree**, not simply the
highest-priced aircraft I can afford.

<section id="aircraft-tree" className="scroll-mt-24">

## How does the Aircraft Tree work?

Every aircraft sits inside a wider progression network. Reaching a new plane can
require moving through aircraft, weapon, or upgrade nodes first, while other branches
split toward different roles.

![An early Ace Combat 8 Aircraft Tree branch showing aircraft, upgrade nodes, and connected unlock paths.](/images/ace-combat-8/aircraft-tree-early-game.webp)

The important thing is that buying an intermediate node is not always wasted MRP.
Standard-missile reload, homing, payload, mobility, acceleration, and special-weapon
upgrades can remain useful long after the aircraft beside them has been replaced.

The tree also mixes several different kinds of purchases:

- **Aircraft** open new performance profiles and special-weapon sets.
- **Standard missile upgrades** improve the weapon you use in almost every mission.
- **Special-weapon upgrades** improve a narrower class of loadouts.
- **Body and mobility upgrades** change acceleration, speed, turning, and general handling.
- **Support nodes** can improve electronic warfare or other specialized systems.

![Weapon and upgrade nodes mixed into an Ace Combat 8 Aircraft Tree branch.](/images/ace-combat-8/aircraft-tree-parts-and-weapons.webp)

That is why I would not judge a route by the final aircraft alone. Two planes with
similar prices can require very different amounts of useful—or disposable—MRP to
reach.

</section>

<section id="best-progression-route" className="scroll-mt-24">

## A practical Aircraft Tree route for a first campaign

There is no single correct route if you already know which aircraft family you want.
A player who specifically wants Sukhoi aircraft should not be forced through an
American branch just because it is efficient.

For a first playthrough where the priority is simply making the campaign easier,
however, I would divide the tree into three stages.

### Early game: build the missile and handling foundation first

The early American side of the tree gives access to several upgrades that remain
useful across many later aircraft.

The standard-missile route is especially valuable. Improving reload time and homing
helps in almost every air battle, while nearby handling and weapon upgrades improve
the aircraft you are already flying instead of asking you to wait for one expensive
unlock.

The **F-16C Fighting Falcon** is a good point to build around in this stage. It is a
clear step above the earliest aircraft without requiring late-game money, and the
surrounding route contains upgrades that continue to matter after the F-16C itself
has been replaced.

My early-game rule is therefore:

**do not rush through every useful upgrade just to reach another aircraft icon.**

A slightly slower Aircraft Tree climb can produce a much stronger plane for every
mission along the way.

### Mid game: finish the core mobility and air-combat upgrades

Once the standard missile foundation is established, I would start filling the
mobility and speed side of the build.

Acceleration, top speed, broader maneuverability improvements, and useful
air-to-air special-weapon upgrades make a much larger difference once missions
begin mixing bombers, fast interceptors, escorts, and long multi-phase fights.

This is also the stage where Electronic Warfare support starts appearing as a real
branch rather than a curiosity.

![The EA-6B Prowler branch showing an Electronic Warfare aircraft, support weapons, and Aircraft Tree connections.](/images/ace-combat-8/aircraft-tree-ew-branch.webp)

You do not need to buy every branch before continuing. The point is to leave the
mid game with a functional base:

- reliable standard missiles;
- enough mobility to recover from poor attack angles;
- stronger air-to-air options;
- one or two aircraft that can handle more than a single mission type.

### Late game: buy versatility before chasing the final aircraft

For a campaign-focused route, **Rafale M** is one of the strongest value points in
the later tree.

Its appeal is not just raw aircraft performance. Its available weapon choices cover
very different mission problems: **HCAA** for air combat, **LACM** for long-range
surface and naval targets, and **LAAM** for long-range interception.

That makes one 350,000 MRP aircraft useful across several kinds of sorties instead
of forcing another purchase every time the campaign changes target type.

If MRP is tighter, the **F/A-18E Super Hornet** is a cheaper alternative on the
multirole side. I would still prefer the Rafale M when possible because it covers
more of the campaign with one purchase.

</section>

<section id="late-game-aircraft" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Su-57 or F-22A: which late-game route should you take?

The two obvious 550,000 MRP Fighter endpoints are the **F-22A Raptor** and
**Su-57 Felon**.

Their raw aircraft performance is close enough that I would let the Aircraft Tree
route and weapon access influence the decision more than the name on the airframe.

If I had followed the progression route above and cared only about reaching the end
of the campaign efficiently, I would lean toward the **Su-57 branch**.

The reason is the route around it. Once the earlier recommended upgrades are already
owned, fewer additional nodes are needed before reaching the aircraft, and its
ground-attack options also benefit from upgrades that were already useful earlier.

The F-22A remains an excellent late-game Fighter, especially for a player who prefers
its branch or wants to stay focused on air combat. It simply requires a different
investment path, including the F-15C side of the tree.

This is a progression recommendation, not a rule. If the F-22A is the aircraft you
actually want to fly, the difference is not large enough to justify avoiding it.

</section>

<section id="all-aircraft" className="scroll-mt-24">

## All 34 aircraft in Ace Combat 8

The base progression is divided into four broad roles: **Fighter, Multirole,
Attacker, and Electronic Warfare**.

Some aircraft come from normal MRP purchases, while others are story rewards,
post-game unlocks, preorder content, or seasonal rewards.

### Fighter aircraft

Fighters are the most air-combat-focused aircraft in the roster. They are the
natural choice when a mission is dominated by enemy aircraft, bombers, or long
interception phases.

<GuideTable columns={aircraftColumns} rows={fighterRows} />

### Multirole aircraft

Multiroles are the easiest class to recommend when you do not know what the next
mission will ask from you. They trade some specialization for the ability to switch
between air and surface targets without changing aircraft.

<GuideTable columns={aircraftColumns} rows={multiroleRows} />

The **F-35C** is a good example of why late-game multiroles stay useful. Its tree
node combines strong all-round aircraft statistics with **LACM, HCAA, and 8AGM**
options, allowing the same aircraft to move between interception and surface-heavy
missions.

![The F-35C Lightning II node showing its 550,000 MRP cost, aircraft statistics, and special-weapon options.](/images/ace-combat-8/aircraft-tree-f35c-late-game.webp)

### Attacker aircraft

Attackers make the most sense when the ground or naval target count is high enough
to justify giving up some air-combat flexibility.

<GuideTable columns={aircraftColumns} rows={attackerRows} />

For a straightforward ground-attack approach, the **A-10C** and **Su-34** are easier
to use than a more technical setup.

The **F-15E** becomes much more rewarding once you are comfortable with weapons such
as SFFS, but I would not make that my first recommendation to a new player who still
needs to learn how the campaign creates attack windows.

### Electronic Warfare aircraft

Electronic Warfare aircraft occupy a much narrower role than the other three
classes.

<GuideTable
  columns={aircraftColumns}
  rows={electronicWarfareRows}
/>

The **EA-6B Prowler** is the easiest early example of what this class does
differently. Its loadout includes electronic-support options alongside conventional
weapons, and the surrounding Aircraft Tree branch connects directly into the
support-upgrade side of progression.

I would treat EW aircraft as a specialized purchase rather than something every
first-playthrough build must rush.

</section>

<section id="post-game" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Which aircraft unlock after the campaign?

Three of the most expensive aircraft sit outside ordinary first-clear progression:

- **FA-36 Game** — 750,000 MRP
- **EA-36 Barricader** — 750,000 MRP
- **X-40 Nightmare** — 1,000,000 MRP

These are better treated as post-game goals than aircraft you need to save for
during the story.

The late Aircraft Tree makes that transition visible. The F-35C area continues
toward the FA-36 and EA-36 branches once normal campaign progression has already
reached its high-end aircraft.

![The late Aircraft Tree branch connecting the F-35C area to the FA-36 and EA-36 post-game aircraft.](/images/ace-combat-8/aircraft-tree-post-game-branch.webp)

The **F-14A Tomcat** and **ADFX-02 Morgan -PIXY-** are different again. They are
not normal Aircraft Tree purchases: the F-14A is tied to preorder content, while
the Morgan is a Season 1 Ace Pass reward.

Do not plan a normal Campaign Tree route around either one.

</section>

<section id="what-to-buy-first" className="scroll-mt-24">

## What should you buy first?

For a first campaign, I would spend MRP in roughly this order:

1. Build the early **standard-missile reload and homing** foundation.
2. Take useful nearby handling upgrades instead of skipping every part node.
3. Use the **F-16C** area as a strong early progression point.
4. Add mid-game speed, mobility, and air-combat upgrades.
5. Move toward a flexible aircraft such as the **Rafale M** rather than buying several narrowly specialized planes.
6. Commit to the **Su-57 or F-22A branch** once the underlying upgrades are already useful.
7. Leave the 750,000–1,000,000 MRP post-game aircraft until the campaign is complete.

That route is meant to minimize dead spending, not force one national aircraft line.

If you already know that you want an A-10C, F-22A, Su-57, or another specific plane,
building around that aircraft is perfectly reasonable. The campaign gives enough
opportunities to earn more MRP later; the real mistake is spending heavily on nodes
that do not improve either the aircraft you are flying now or the aircraft you
actually intend to reach.

</section>

<section id="mrp-and-completion" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Do you need to buy every aircraft?

Not for the first campaign.

Several achievements eventually reward aircraft and Aircraft Tree completion, but
there is no reason to force those purchases before the rest of your post-game
cleanup is ready.

The [trophy and achievement guide](/ace-combat-8/trophies-achievements) tracks the
5-aircraft, 20-aircraft, full-collection, weapon, and part requirements without
turning this page into a second trophy checklist.

Likewise, this guide focuses on **where to spend MRP**, not how to grind it. If the
Aircraft Tree becomes the last expensive part of your completion route, use the
[MRP farming route](/ace-combat-8/mrp-farm) after your normal campaign and replay
earnings are no longer enough.

For mission-specific aircraft decisions, the
[full campaign walkthrough](/ace-combat-8/walkthrough) is the better reference
because the useful role changes from sortie to sortie.

</section>
