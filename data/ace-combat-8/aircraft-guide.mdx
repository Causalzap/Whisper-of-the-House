The Aircraft Tree in Ace Combat 8 is not just a list of planes with increasingly
large price tags. Aircraft, special weapons, missile upgrades, handling parts, and
support systems are connected together, so the route you take determines how much
MRP you spend before reaching the aircraft you actually want.

That matters most during a first campaign. It is easy to see a new fighter several
nodes away and start buying directly toward it, but a route filled with useful
missile and handling upgrades can make the missions before that aircraft much easier.

If I were building for a smooth first clear rather than chasing a favorite real-world
airframe, I would prioritize a **useful path through the tree**, not simply the
highest-priced aircraft I can afford.

<section id="aircraft-tree" className="scroll-mt-24">

## How does the Aircraft Tree work?

Every aircraft sits inside a wider progression network. Reaching a new plane can
require moving through aircraft, weapon, or upgrade nodes first, while other branches
split toward different roles.

![An early Ace Combat 8 Aircraft Tree branch showing aircraft, upgrade nodes, and connected unlock paths.](/images/ace-combat-8/aircraft-tree-early-game.webp)

The important thing is that buying an intermediate node is not always wasted MRP.
Standard-missile reload, homing, payload, mobility, acceleration, and special-weapon
upgrades can remain useful long after the aircraft beside them has been replaced.

The tree also mixes several different kinds of purchases:

- **Aircraft** open new performance profiles and special-weapon sets.
- **Standard missile upgrades** improve the weapon you use in almost every mission.
- **Special-weapon upgrades** improve a narrower class of loadouts.
- **Body and mobility upgrades** change acceleration, speed, turning, and general handling.
- **Support nodes** can improve electronic warfare or other specialized systems.

![Weapon and upgrade nodes mixed into an Ace Combat 8 Aircraft Tree branch.](/images/ace-combat-8/aircraft-tree-parts-and-weapons.webp)

### The aircraft price is not the full unlock cost

The opening F/A-18C branch shows why I check the connected nodes before saving for another aircraft.

Three nearby upgrades have separate prices:

- **MSL.RELOAD+ — 5,000 MRP:** improves standard-missile reload.
- **MSL.AIR.POW+ — 10,000 MRP:** improves standard-missile power against air targets.
- **MSL.GND.POW+ — 10,000 MRP:** improves standard-missile power against ground targets.

These are different upgrades, and their value depends on the mission. Faster reloads help when I need repeated attacks, while a small damage improvement is less important if the same enemy still takes the same number of missiles to destroy.

The same issue becomes more expensive in the late Aircraft Tree. The **F-35C costs 550,000 MRP**, but reaching its node can also involve purchasing earlier aircraft and intermediate upgrades. Its displayed price should not be mistaken for the cost of starting that branch from scratch.

Before committing to a new aircraft, I zoom out, trace the connected nodes, and count what I still need to buy. Parts already owned do not need to be purchased again, so the best route also depends on where my current progression has reached.

That is why I would not judge a route by the final aircraft alone. Two planes with
similar prices can require very different amounts of useful—or disposable—MRP to
reach.

</section>

<section id="best-progression-route" className="scroll-mt-24">

## A practical Aircraft Tree route for a first campaign

There is no single correct route if you already know which aircraft family you want.
A player who specifically wants Sukhoi aircraft should not be forced through an
American branch just because it is efficient.

For a first playthrough where the priority is simply making the campaign easier,
however, I would divide the tree into three stages.

### Early game: improve the starting aircraft before replacing it

The free **F/A-18C Hornet** is enough to begin the campaign, so I would not rush into another aircraft simply because the first few tree nodes are affordable.

The first purchases should address whatever is slowing down the current missions. Standard-missile reload is useful when I am spending too much time waiting between attacks. Handling upgrades matter when I lose firing opportunities during tight turns. A weapon-capacity upgrade becomes more attractive when a long mission repeatedly runs out of special ammunition.

The **F-16C costs 180,000 MRP**, making it a reasonable early Fighter investment, but not a mandatory stop for every route. If the starting Hornet is still clearing missions reliably and I want a stronger multirole later, I would save rather than buy the F-16C just because it is available.

### Mid game: buy one aircraft that fits the missions ahead

The middle of the campaign is where buying a better aircraft can make more sense than continuing to add small upgrades to the starting plane.

The **F/A-18E and F/A-18F Super Hornets** each cost 240,000 MRP. They offer a mid-priced step into stronger multirole aircraft without immediately committing to a 350,000 or 550,000 MRP purchase.

The **Rafale M**, at 350,000 MRP, becomes more attractive when I need one aircraft for several different objectives. Its HCAA supports air combat, LACM provides long-range attacks against surface targets, and LAAM helps with distant aerial interception.

I would choose between the Super Hornet and Rafale based on weapon needs and the tree nodes already owned. Buying both is not automatically better than buying one and investing the remaining MRP in useful parts.

Electronic Warfare branches are a separate decision. They can provide specialized support, but I would not divert large amounts of MRP toward an EW aircraft unless I actually intend to use that role.

![The EA-6B Prowler branch showing an Electronic Warfare aircraft, support weapons, and Aircraft Tree connections.](/images/ace-combat-8/aircraft-tree-ew-branch.webp)

### Late game: commit to a high-end aircraft when its role matters

The **F-22A**, **Su-57**, and **F-35C** each have a 550,000 MRP aircraft price. That does not mean all three require the same total investment from an earlier point in the tree.

At this stage, I first check which branches I have already opened.

For missions dominated by enemy fighters and difficult interceptions, a high-end Fighter is a sensible upgrade. For a campaign that continues to mix air and surface targets, I also consider a multirole loadout before committing to another specialized aircraft.

I do not spend toward two late-game endpoints simultaneously unless I am already working on full aircraft collection. The immediate goal is to acquire one aircraft that solves the remaining missions, not to complete every visible branch.

</section>

<section id="late-game-aircraft" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Su-57 or F-22A: which late-game route should you take?

Both the **Su-57 Felon** and **F-22A Raptor** cost **550,000 MRP** at their aircraft nodes, but the displayed aircraft price is only part of the buying decision.

The more useful question is how much additional MRP I must spend from my current position in the Aircraft Tree.

If I have already invested in the branch leading toward the F-22A, changing direction to the Su-57 may require additional purchases that do not help my current aircraft. The reverse can also be true.

I check the remaining connected aircraft and parts before choosing either endpoint. An upgrade that improves missiles or handling may still be worthwhile, but I do not count every intermediate purchase as equally useful.

For normal campaign progression, I would choose the aircraft whose available weapons and performance fit the remaining missions, then stay on that route until it is unlocked.

Neither aircraft should be described as the universally cheaper option without comparing the actual unowned nodes in both branches.

</section>

<section id="all-aircraft" className="scroll-mt-24">

## All 34 aircraft in Ace Combat 8

The base progression is divided into four broad roles: **Fighter, Multirole,
Attacker, and Electronic Warfare**.

Some aircraft come from normal MRP purchases, while others are story rewards,
post-game unlocks, preorder content, or seasonal rewards.

### Fighter aircraft

Fighters are the most air-combat-focused aircraft in the roster. They are the
natural choice when a mission is dominated by enemy aircraft, bombers, or long
interception phases.

<GuideTable columns={aircraftColumns} rows={fighterRows} />

### Multirole aircraft

Multiroles are the easiest class to recommend when you do not know what the next
mission will ask from you. They trade some specialization for the ability to switch
between air and surface targets without changing aircraft.

<GuideTable columns={aircraftColumns} rows={multiroleRows} />

The **F-35C** is a good example of why late-game multiroles stay useful. Its tree
node combines strong all-round aircraft statistics with **LACM, HCAA, and 8AGM**
options, allowing the same aircraft to move between interception and surface-heavy
missions.

![The F-35C Lightning II node showing its 550,000 MRP cost, aircraft statistics, and special-weapon options.](/images/ace-combat-8/aircraft-tree-f35c-late-game.webp)

### Attacker aircraft

Attackers make the most sense when the ground or naval target count is high enough
to justify giving up some air-combat flexibility.

<GuideTable columns={aircraftColumns} rows={attackerRows} />

For a straightforward ground-attack approach, the **A-10C** and **Su-34** are easier
to use than a more technical setup.

The **F-15E** becomes much more rewarding once you are comfortable with weapons such
as SFFS, but I would not make that my first recommendation to a new player who still
needs to learn how the campaign creates attack windows.

### Electronic Warfare aircraft

Electronic Warfare aircraft occupy a much narrower role than the other three
classes.

<GuideTable
  columns={aircraftColumns}
  rows={electronicWarfareRows}
/>

The **EA-6B Prowler** is the easiest early example of what this class does
differently. Its loadout includes electronic-support options alongside conventional
weapons, and the surrounding Aircraft Tree branch connects directly into the
support-upgrade side of progression.

I would treat EW aircraft as a specialized purchase rather than something every
first-playthrough build must rush.

</section>

<section id="post-game" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Which aircraft unlock after the campaign?

Three of the most expensive aircraft sit outside ordinary first-clear progression:

- **FA-36 Game** — 750,000 MRP
- **EA-36 Barricader** — 750,000 MRP
- **X-40 Nightmare** — 1,000,000 MRP

These are better treated as post-game goals than aircraft you need to save for
during the story.

The late Aircraft Tree makes that transition visible. The F-35C area continues
toward the FA-36 and EA-36 branches once normal campaign progression has already
reached its high-end aircraft.

![The late Aircraft Tree branch connecting the F-35C area to the FA-36 and EA-36 post-game aircraft.](/images/ace-combat-8/aircraft-tree-post-game-branch.webp)

The **F-14A Tomcat** and **ADFX-02 Morgan -PIXY-** are different again. They are
not normal Aircraft Tree purchases: the F-14A is tied to preorder content, while
the Morgan is a Season 1 Ace Pass reward.

Do not plan a normal Campaign Tree route around either one.

</section>

<section id="what-to-buy-first" className="scroll-mt-24">

## What should you buy first?


My first Aircraft Tree purchases depend on whether I need more firing opportunities, better handling, or a larger special-weapon supply. Not every stat increase produces the same improvement in a mission.

### Upgrades I would prioritize

**Target ID Accelerator — standard-missile reload**

A roughly **22% reduction in standard-missile reload time** makes this particularly useful during missions with repeated fighter engagements. It improves how often I can attack, rather than simply increasing the damage of one missile.

**ECU Software Update — high-G handling and stall resistance**

This is one of the more valuable handling upgrades when tight turns cause frequent stalls.

The benefit is not just a small increase in turning performance. Better stall resistance can allow the aircraft to complete a high-G turn that would otherwise bleed too much speed.

It is especially attractive for difficult dogfights and aircraft that struggle to maintain control during aggressive maneuvers.

**Bypass Ratio Optimization System — speed and acceleration**

This upgrade provides approximately **5% more top speed**, with improved acceleration also observed in its performance comparison.

It is useful when a mission requires repeated interceptions or long-distance repositioning. For an escort mission, getting back into position can matter more than a small increase in missile damage.

**Takeoff Weight Augmentation — additional weapon capacity**

An increased special-weapon payload is valuable when ammunition is the limiting factor. I prioritize it for long missions where I cannot rely on frequent resupply.

The exact benefit depends on the equipped weapon, so I check the increased ammunition count before spending another slot on it.

### Useful upgrades for specific situations

**New High-Energy Propellant** increases standard-missile speed by approximately **13%**. It is worth considering against fast enemy aircraft, especially when I have enough ammunition but struggle to connect with moving targets.

**Long-Range Enhanced Shells** increase machine-gun effective range by up to **20%**. This is helpful for gun-focused setups and sustained attacks against large targets.

**Air Brake Auxiliary** improves deceleration by approximately **4%**. I would not prioritize it for ordinary combat, but it can be useful when precise speed control matters in narrow spaces.

**Special-weapon reload upgrades** depend on the weapon actually being used. Faster LACM or bomb reloads can help in surface-heavy missions, while the same investment provides little benefit when the sortie is almost entirely air combat.

### Upgrades I would not rush

A missile damage upgrade is not automatically efficient. If a normal enemy still requires two standard missiles after the upgrade, the higher damage may not change how quickly I clear that target.

The **Predictive Lock-On Algorithm** produced only a small improvement, approximately **2%**, in the cloud-related lock-on comparison. I would not prioritize it over upgrades that give a larger practical benefit.

The **Anti-Stealth Microwave Radar** did not increase lock-on range against the three stealth aircraft examined in the available comparison. I would not buy it solely to extend lock-on distance, although that does not rule out other detection-related benefits.

Likewise, increasing missile flight range does not necessarily extend the range at which I can acquire a target. When long-distance engagement is the problem, I check lock-on range separately.

### My buying rule

I first ask what is preventing me from completing the current mission.

If I cannot fire often enough, I improve reload. If I keep stalling during turns, I improve handling. If I run out of special ammunition, I increase capacity. If my aircraft cannot reach or intercept targets efficiently, I consider speed and lock-on improvements.

Only after those problems are addressed do I spend heavily on a new aircraft branch.

A 550,000 MRP fighter can be an excellent upgrade, but it will not automatically solve a loadout that lacks the ammunition, handling, or weapon behavior the mission requires.

</section>

<section id="mrp-and-completion" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Do you need to buy every aircraft?

Not for the first campaign.

Several achievements eventually reward aircraft and Aircraft Tree completion, but
there is no reason to force those purchases before the rest of your post-game
cleanup is ready.

The [trophy and achievement guide](/ace-combat-8/trophies-achievements) tracks the
5-aircraft, 20-aircraft, full-collection, weapon, and part requirements without
turning this page into a second trophy checklist.

Likewise, this guide focuses on **where to spend MRP**, not how to grind it. If the
Aircraft Tree becomes the last expensive part of your completion route, use the
[MRP farming route](/ace-combat-8/mrp-farm) after your normal campaign and replay
earnings are no longer enough.

For mission-specific aircraft decisions, the
[full campaign walkthrough](/ace-combat-8/walkthrough) is the better reference
because the useful role changes from sortie to sortie.

</section>
