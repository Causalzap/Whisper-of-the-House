Ace Combat 8: Wings of Theve changes the job inside the same sortie more often
than it first appears. An escort can turn into missile interception, a large
battle can become a score check, and a target that looked like the center of the
operation can stop mattering as soon as a new marker appears.

For the first campaign, that makes objective changes more important than simply
clearing every contact on the radar.

<section id="first-campaign" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Start with the full campaign route

The campaign runs through a Prologue and **30 numbered missions**. Early
operations establish fleet defense, escorts, mixed air-to-ground combat, and
target switching. The middle and late campaign increasingly expect you to react
to timers, identification checks, changing weak points, and threats that can end
the sortie before the wider battle is finished.

The [Ace Combat 8 walkthrough](/ace-combat-8/walkthrough) follows that sequence
from start to finish and is the best place to continue when you simply need to
know what the current mission expects next.

> **Campaign spoilers:** The mission notes below mention objectives from later operations.

A few ordinary missions are worth remembering because their failure condition
is easy to misread:

- **Mission 7:** reaching the required destruction score is enough; Faith Park
  does not need to be completely cleared.
- **Mission 12:** once the cruise missiles are airborne, intercepting them takes
  priority over the surrounding fighters.
- **Mission 16:** the disguised terrorist ships are identified by the **sun
  emblem and three cranes**, so attacking unconfirmed vessels is the wrong way
  to search.
- **Mission 18:** the rockets become the target after launch rather than while
  they are still on the ground.
- **Mission 20:** a large image-recognition missile heading for the shuttle
  transport matters more than the dogfight around it.
- **Mission 29:** the later air battle is tied to a laser-guidance threat, so
  simply trading shots with the enemy aircraft is not enough.

The pattern is consistent even when the missions are not: once the operation
changes the objective, the previous target can become a distraction.

</section>

## Four missions with multi-stage blockers

Most sorties can be cleared from the main campaign route, but four operations
place several different mechanics around the same major target. These are the
ones where knowing the next phase saves the most time.

### Mission 9 — The Land Battleship Blockade

The Land Battleship cannot be stopped by repeatedly attacking the hull. The
operation begins with bomb trucks that expose its mobility system, then moves
through the secondary treads and later containment attempts.

If the side armor will not open, the ship begins moving again after the treads
are destroyed, or the current movement target is unclear, the
[Mission 9 Land Battleship walkthrough](/ace-combat-8/mission-9-land-battleship-blockade)
covers the sequence from the first IED vehicle to the final stop.

### Mission 11 — Maximum Payload

Mission 11 hides enormous Podarge transports behind thunderstorms and
electronic warfare. Finding them is only the first problem: they still have to
be slowed and brought down before their Land Battleship cargo reaches Rocky
Island.

The most reliable approach is to follow the visible contrails, stay with one
transport instead of chasing every escort, and keep working its propulsion
system until the mission confirms the kill. The
[Mission 11 Maximum Payload walkthrough](/ace-combat-8/mission-11-maximum-payload)
covers the complete interception.

### Mission 27 — Fatsia, The Ocean Fortress

Fatsia begins as a huge exterior assault where structural targets matter more
than clearing every weapon on the Mega Float. Later, an explicit order changes
whether Joker Flight should continue attacking at all, and the operation
eventually moves away from the outer arms.

If destruction progress stalls, the hold-fire order is unclear, or you cannot
tell where the next objective has moved, continue with the
[Mission 27 Fatsia walkthrough](/ace-combat-8/mission-27-fatsia-ocean-fortress).

### Mission 30 — Song of Wings

The final operation repeatedly changes the immediate threat to the Endurance.
For much of the fight, protecting the carrier and dealing with guidance or
incoming weapons matters more than trying to force damage onto the submarine.

Once the submarine becomes the main target, its combat state continues changing,
so the correct target is not the same for the entire battle. The
[Mission 30 Song of Wings walkthrough](/ace-combat-8/mission-30-song-of-wings)
follows those transitions through the end of the operation.

</section>

## Keep the first clear focused on finishing the campaign

Thirty missions already ask you to learn escort priorities, destruction
thresholds, missile interception, visual identification, large target phases,
and several different ways a sortie can fail. The first run is much easier when
those mechanics are learned before replay requirements become the main concern.

There are still a few low-cost things worth doing when they naturally appear.
Complete takeoff and aerial-refueling sequences instead of skipping them, try
the wingman commands when allied aircraft already have useful targets, and learn
which special weapons actually help in different mission types.

### When should you buy another aircraft?

Do not treat every new Aircraft Tree unlock as an automatic upgrade. If your
current aircraft can handle the next mission with a different special weapon or
part setup, changing the loadout may solve the problem without spending MRP on
another airframe.

The reason to buy is a problem you have actually encountered: not enough
air-to-air pressure for a long interception, poor efficiency against dense
surface targets, or a special weapon that fits the next operation much better.

During the first campaign, keep enough MRP flexibility for those changes instead
of trying to clear the entire Aircraft Tree as soon as branches open.

Normal MRP, kills, aircraft purchases, and other progress will accumulate while
you learn the campaign. There is no need to turn an unfamiliar mission into a
grind before you know how its objective works.
