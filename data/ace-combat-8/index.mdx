Ace Combat 8: Wings of Theve becomes much easier once you stop treating every red
contact as equally important. A bomber approaching the fleet, a ground unit that
has stopped advancing, a missile already in flight, or a newly exposed component
can matter far more than the fighter directly in front of you.

This guide is the starting point for the campaign and post-game routes. Use the
full walkthrough when you need the next objective, switch to a mission-specific
guide when one operation has a unique blocker, and leave completion cleanup for
after you understand the campaign.

<section id="first-campaign" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Start with the 30-mission campaign

Ace Combat 8 has a Prologue followed by **30 numbered campaign missions**. Early
sorties introduce interception, fleet defense, ground support, escort work, and
target switching, while later missions combine several of those jobs inside the
same operation.

The
[complete Ace Combat 8 walkthrough](/ace-combat-8/walkthrough)
keeps all 30 missions in order and should be your main reference when the question
is simply:

- what happens next;
- which target matters first;
- when the objective changes;
- whether a mission is actually finished;
- when a return line or resupply window is worth using.

A first campaign does not need to become a perfect-rank run. Concentrate on
finishing the operations and learning where each mission changes state.

One thing is worth protecting from the start: complete every takeoff and aerial
refueling sequence instead of skipping it. That keeps **By the Book** available
without forcing another full campaign later.

</section>

<section id="mission-guides" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Use a mission-specific guide when the normal objective is not enough

Most missions do not need a separate walkthrough. The full campaign guide already
covers their main route.

A few operations have enough unusual phases, identification rules, or hidden
transitions that a focused guide is more useful.

### Mission 9 — Land Battleship Blockade

The Land Battleship does not stay disabled after one obvious attack. If the tread
sequence, bomb trucks, or final movement phase is the blocker, use the
[Mission 9 Land Battleship guide](/ace-combat-8/mission-9-land-battleship-blockade).

### Mission 11 — Maximum Payload

Thunderclouds and electronic warfare make the Podarge transports harder to track,
and simply finding one is not enough if it still escapes. The
[Mission 11 Maximum Payload guide](/ace-combat-8/mission-11-maximum-payload)
covers the full interception.

### Mission 16 — Singer

Singer mixes civilian ships with terrorist vessels, so identification matters
before firing. The
[Mission 16 Singer guide](/ace-combat-8/mission-16-singer)
covers the correct ships and the later UAV phase.

### Mission 18 — Moonlight and Shadows

The rockets are not supposed to be destroyed while they are still on the ground.
Use the
[Mission 18 Moonlight and Shadows guide](/ace-combat-8/mission-18-moonlight-and-shadows)
when the launch sequence or final debris phase is unclear.

### Mission 27 — Fatsia, The Ocean Fortress

Fatsia changes from exterior destruction into a completely different final phase.
The
[Mission 27 Fatsia guide](/ace-combat-8/mission-27-fatsia-ocean-fortress)
covers the structural targets, hold-fire transition, and interior sequence.

### Mission 30 — Song of Wings

The final mission repeatedly changes what threatens the Endurance and what can
actually be damaged. The
[Mission 30 Song of Wings guide](/ace-combat-8/mission-30-song-of-wings)
follows the submarine fight through its last phase.

The important pattern across all of them is the same: once the mission changes
priority, stop solving the previous problem.

</section>

<section id="aircraft-progression" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Build the Aircraft Tree around problems you actually need to solve

You do not need a new aircraft every time another node becomes available.

The Aircraft Tree mixes planes with standard-missile upgrades, special-weapon
upgrades, mobility improvements, parts, and electronic-warfare branches. Spending
MRP only on the next aircraft can leave useful upgrades behind and make the route
more expensive than it needs to be.

The
[Ace Combat 8 aircraft guide](/ace-combat-8/aircraft-guide)
covers all **34 aircraft**, their roles, MRP costs, unlock types, Aircraft Tree
progression, and a practical early-to-late purchase route.

For a first campaign, I would prioritize a route that improves the aircraft you are
already flying before rushing toward an expensive endgame plane. Flexible
multiroles become especially useful once missions begin switching between air,
ground, and naval targets.

The aircraft guide also separates normal Aircraft Tree purchases from starting
aircraft, story rewards, post-game aircraft, preorder content, and seasonal
rewards, so you can see which planes actually belong to normal campaign
progression.

</section>

<section id="first-clear" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Let the first clear teach you what later replays will need

The first campaign is most useful when you learn the parts of a mission that remain
important on harder runs:

- which enemy can actually fail an escort or defense objective;
- when a large target changes vulnerability;
- which missions have a worthwhile resupply window;
- where ammunition becomes a later-phase problem;
- which radio call means the old objective no longer matters.

Those lessons become much more valuable when you return for ACE difficulty, S
ranks, Assault Records, medals, MRP, or long kill counters.

You can also clear simple one-action requirements such as wingman commands when
they appear naturally, but there is little reason to interrupt a difficult first
clear just to force completion progress.

</section>

<section id="after-campaign" className="scroll-mt-24">

## After Mission 30, replay only for something you still need

Once the campaign is finished, random mission replays become inefficient because
the remaining goals require different kinds of runs.

Start with the
[Ace Combat 8 trophy and achievement guide](/ace-combat-8/trophies-achievements).
It tracks the 55 shared achievement requirements and separates the first-campaign
work from ACE difficulty, S ranks, Assault Records, medals, Aircraft Tree
completion, MRP totals, and long combat counters.

If a named aircraft is missing, use the
[Assault Records checklist](/ace-combat-8/assault-records)
and return to the mission tied to that specific appearance condition.

If aircraft purchases or the long MRP milestones remain after your other cleanup,
use the
[MRP farming guide](/ace-combat-8/mrp-farm)
for the Mission 28 farming route and resupply decisions.

If an S rank is still missing, return to that particular mission rather than
starting another general-purpose farming run. The
[all-missions walkthrough](/ace-combat-8/walkthrough)
remains useful for refreshing the phase order before an ACE rank attempt.

The best post-game sortie is the one that advances something still unchecked.

</section>
