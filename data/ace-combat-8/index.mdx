Ace Combat 8: Wings of Theve rarely asks Joker Flight to solve an entire sortie in
one way. A fleet-defense mission can suddenly become missile interception, a huge
target can stop being vulnerable until another unit acts, and destroying what
looks like the final objective can reveal one more phase.

The campaign becomes much easier once you stop asking, “What can I shoot?” and
start asking, **“What can end the mission right now?”** That might be a bomber
closing on a carrier, a ground unit that cannot advance, a countdown already
running, or a newly marked component on a target you have been fighting for several
minutes.

<section id="first-campaign" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Follow the campaign first, then worry about perfect replays

The campaign runs through a Prologue and **30 numbered missions**. The early
operations teach interception, fleet defense, escort work, air-to-ground attacks,
and target switching. Later missions expect you to recognize when those familiar
jobs have changed into something else.

The [full Ace Combat 8 walkthrough](/ace-combat-8/walkthrough) follows all 30
missions in order and is the best place to continue when the immediate question is
simply where to go or what to attack next.

> **Campaign spoilers start below.**

A first clear does not need to become an achievement grind. Learn the objective
changes, finish takeoff and aerial-refueling sequences instead of skipping them,
and try wingman commands when the situation is safe. MRP, aircraft kills, ranks,
and other long-term progress will accumulate naturally while you learn the
campaign.

Several missions are worth recognizing early because their failure condition is
easy to misread. Mission 7 only needs enough destruction to reach its required
score; Mission 12 turns C-130 cruise-missile launches into the immediate threat;
Mission 20 uses large image-recognition missiles that have to be intercepted
directly; and Mission 29 turns a difficult dogfight into a race against laser
guidance aimed at a much larger target.

Those missions do not all play alike, but they reward the same habit: when the
operation changes priority, change with it.

</section>

<section id="reading-the-battle" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Read movement, timers, and objective calls before the radar

A crowded radar can make every enemy look equally important. They rarely are.

During fleet defense, the aircraft actually moving toward the ships matter more
than a fighter trying to lure Joker Flight away. During ground support, an allied
unit that has stopped moving usually tells you where the real blocker is. When a
countdown or distance counter appears, that number can become more important than
everything else in the battle.

Mission 7 demonstrates this with Faith Park. Once the required destruction score
has been reached, continuing to hunt isolated structures across the entire valley
does not improve the main objective. Mission 12 does the opposite: once a cruise
missile is airborne, whatever fighter you were previously attacking becomes
secondary until the missile is stopped.

The same principle becomes even more important late in the campaign. Fatsia can
still show valid exterior targets after the operation no longer wants Joker Flight
firing at them. Mission 30 can put missiles, guidance drones, submarine weapons,
and the submarine itself into the same sortie, but those threats are not equally
important at the same moment.

Radio calls are therefore part of the fight, not background dialogue. When AWACS
announces a new objective, a distance warning, a target count, a hold-fire order,
or a change in what friendly forces are doing, use that information to decide
whether the target currently in front of you still matters.

</section>

## Use the mission-specific route when one fight keeps changing rules

A few operations contain enough separate phases that remembering only the opening
objective is not enough.

### Mission 9 — The Land Battleship keeps finding another way to move

The Land Battleship is not stopped by repeatedly attacking its hull. Ground units
first have to expose the mobility system, later containment becomes a race against
distance, and the final movement system is different again.

If the bomb trucks cannot expose the treads, the battleship starts moving after
you thought it was disabled, or the final target is unclear, follow the
[Mission 9 Land Battleship sequence](/ace-combat-8/mission-9-land-battleship-blockade).

### Mission 11 — You can see the Podarge and still fail the interception

Maximum Payload combines thunderclouds, electronic warfare, defensive weapons,
escorts, and several enormous transports moving toward Rocky Island.

The contrails are the easiest way to reacquire the Podarges when radar range
collapses, but locating them is only the beginning. Stay with one transport long
enough to bring it down instead of leaving several damaged aircraft still flying.

The [Mission 11 Maximum Payload route](/ace-combat-8/mission-11-maximum-payload)
covers the full transport interception.

### Mission 16 — Identify the ship before firing

Singer puts civilian vessels and terrorist ships in the same waters. The three
targets share a clear visual combination: **three cranes on deck and a sun emblem
on the hull**.

Firing first and identifying later is the wrong approach. If the search runs long
enough for the self-destruct UAVs to launch, the battle also changes from ship
identification into an altitude interception.

Use the
[Mission 16 Singer walkthrough](/ace-combat-8/mission-16-singer) for the ship
identification, launch timer, and UAV phase.

### Mission 18 — The rockets are not targets until after launch

Moonlight and Shadows first interrupts the route with Shadow Squadron, then sends
Joker Flight back toward the launch site. The rockets must be allowed to lift off
before they are destroyed, and the mission still has another danger after both are
gone.

The
[Mission 18 Moonlight and Shadows route](/ace-combat-8/mission-18-moonlight-and-shadows)
covers the Shadow fight, rocket interception, and falling debris.

### Mission 27 — Stop attacking when the battle tells you to stop

Fatsia starts as a structural destruction problem. Support pillars and explosive
industrial targets can remove large sections efficiently, but the operation later
orders Joker Flight to stop firing and eventually sends the fight inside the
central structure.

If exterior destruction stalls, the surrender pause is confusing, or the reactor
sequence is blocking the clear, use the
[Mission 27 Fatsia walkthrough](/ace-combat-8/mission-27-fatsia-ocean-fortress).

### Mission 30 — Protecting the Endurance matters before damaging the submarine

Song of Wings repeatedly changes what can kill the carrier next. Early on, the
submarine itself is not even a useful attack target; incoming missiles and terminal
guidance matter more.

The operation later moves through Tonitra Spear guidance, exposed submarine
weapons, a hidden underwater launch tube, and finally a direct hull-damage race.
The [Mission 30 Song of Wings route](/ace-combat-8/mission-30-song-of-wings)
follows that sequence through the end of the campaign.

</section>

<section id="aircraft-and-loadouts" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Buy another aircraft when it solves a problem you actually have

A new Aircraft Tree unlock is not automatically an upgrade for the next mission.

During the first campaign, changing special weapons or parts is often enough to
solve a weak loadout. A familiar multirole aircraft can be more useful than a new
airframe if you already understand its speed, turning behavior, weapon capacity,
and attack angles.

A purchase makes the most sense when the current aircraft has exposed a real
limitation. Long interceptions can justify more air-to-air endurance; dense surface
operations may benefit from a better ground-attack option; a mission built around
large or grouped targets can make a different special weapon much more valuable.

Mission 11 is a good example. The Podarges require repeated air-to-air passes, so
ammunition endurance matters more than buying a new aircraft simply because it
became available. Mission 27 pushes in the opposite direction: much of the exterior
battle rewards efficient structural and surface damage before the operation moves
inside.

Keep some MRP available while the campaign is still introducing new mission types.
Buying every newly available branch as soon as it appears can leave less room to
respond when the next operation exposes a loadout weakness you did not have before.

You also do not need to protect your MRP balance for the long-term earning
milestones. Those requirements track total MRP earned, so purchases that improve
the aircraft collection or help with current missions can still be worthwhile.

</section>

<section id="first-clear" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Use the first clear to learn where missions change state

The first campaign already contains enough information to absorb without adding
perfect ranks and collection cleanup to every sortie.

The most valuable things to learn are repeatable:

- which enemies can actually fail an escort or defense mission;
- when an objective changes after a large target appears to be disabled;
- where resupply is worth the time on a long operation;
- which missions punish wasting ammunition before their later phases;
- which radio calls mean the fight has moved on even though targets remain.

Completing takeoffs and aerial refueling is worth remembering from the first run,
because **By the Book** depends on successfully completing those sequences across
a finished campaign. Simple wingman and menu requirements can also be handled
when they appear naturally.

ACE difficulty, all-S-rank attempts, named aircraft, large kill counters, the
full aircraft collection, and the highest MRP total are better approached once
you already know the missions. Replaying a familiar sortie for a specific result
is much easier than learning the mission and trying to perfect it at the same
time.

</section>

<section id="after-the-ending" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Choose post-game missions by what is still unfinished

After Mission 30, random replaying becomes inefficient because the remaining goals
need different kinds of runs.

Start with the
[Ace Combat 8 trophy and achievement checklist](/ace-combat-8/trophies-achievements)
and check which category is actually incomplete.

If **A Record of Rivalry** is still open, use the
[Assault Records checklist](/ace-combat-8/assault-records) and return to the
specific mission containing the missing aircraft. Those appearances have their own
conditions, so a general farming run will not replace the correct trigger.

If ACE ranks and mission-specific work are already finished but the long currency
totals are still behind, the
[MRP farming route](/ace-combat-8/mrp-farm) uses Mission 28's sustained air battle
and available resupply to keep the remaining grind productive.

Aircraft kills and special-weapon counters can often be combined with those
replays instead of treated as completely separate jobs. Missing S ranks require
the opposite approach: return to the particular mission that still lacks the rank
and build the sortie around that result.

Once you know what remains, the next mission should have a reason to exist. That
keeps the final runs moving toward completion instead of repeating battles that
have already given you everything you need.

</section>
