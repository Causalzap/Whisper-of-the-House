Ace Combat 8 rewards target priority more than raw destruction. A fighter beside
you can wait when a bomber is already closing on the fleet, a missile is seconds
from impact, or a friendly ground unit has stopped moving because one weapon
system is blocking its route.

That becomes more important as the campaign goes on. Early sorties usually ask
for one clear job at a time. Later missions can switch from interception to
escort, from exterior destruction to an interior target, or from a boss that
appears defeated to another phase that still has to be finished.

<section id="first-campaign" className="scroll-mt-24">

## On your first campaign, follow the failure condition

The campaign runs from a Prologue through **30 numbered missions**. You do not
need to memorize every target type before starting; the more useful habit is
recognizing what the operation is currently protecting.

The
[complete campaign walkthrough](/ace-combat-8/walkthrough)
keeps the missions in order and is the fastest place to check when you are unsure
about:

- which target should die first;
- whether a new radio call changed the objective;
- when an escort or defense target is in immediate danger;
- whether the apparent boss kill actually ended the fight;
- when a return line is worth the time.

Do not turn the first clear into an S-Rank grind. Learn where each mission changes
state, finish the campaign, and save the stricter rank attempts for later.

One campaign-long condition is worth remembering from the beginning. Complete
takeoff and aerial-refueling sequences instead of skipping them if you want
**By the Book** without another full run. The rest of the achievement cleanup can
wait until the story is finished; the
[trophy and achievement checklist](/ace-combat-8/trophies-achievements)
covers those requirements when you are ready.

</section>

## When one mission is the blocker, solve that mission instead of replaying blindly

Most missions can be cleared from the campaign walkthrough alone. The operations
below are the ones where a single unusual mechanic or phase change can turn a
normal clear into repeated failures.

### Mission 9 — Land Battleship Blockade

The Land Battleship is not one large health bar. Its mobility and exposed
components change as the fight progresses, and attacking the wrong section can
waste an entire pass.

Use the
[Mission 9 Land Battleship route](/ace-combat-8/mission-9-land-battleship-blockade)
when bomb trucks, tread phases, or the final movement sequence are stopping the
run.

### Mission 11 — Maximum Payload

Thunderclouds, electronic warfare, and Podarge transports make target acquisition
the real difficulty. Finding a transport is only useful if you stay on it long
enough to stop the escape.

The
[Mission 11 Maximum Payload route](/ace-combat-8/mission-11-maximum-payload)
covers the interception from detection through the final kill.

### Mission 16 — Singer

Firing too early is the mistake here. Civilian ships are mixed with terrorist
vessels, so the correct hull markings and deck layout matter before you launch a
missile.

The
[Mission 16 Singer route](/ace-combat-8/mission-16-singer)
covers identification, the disguised ships, and the later explosive-UAV phase.

### Mission 18 — Moonlight and Shadows

The rockets are supposed to launch. Destroying them while they are still on the
ground solves the wrong problem.

Use
[Mission 18 Moonlight and Shadows](/ace-combat-8/mission-18-moonlight-and-shadows)
for the Shadow interruption, rocket ascent, altitude pressure, and falling-debris
finish.

### Mission 27 — Fatsia, The Ocean Fortress

Fatsia begins as a structural attack, then changes direction once the surrender
sequence starts. Continuing to fire at exterior targets after that transition
wastes time and ammunition because the decisive target moves inside the Mega
Float.

The
[Mission 27 Fatsia route](/ace-combat-8/mission-27-fatsia-ocean-fortress)
covers the support structures, hold-fire transition, closing shutter, and fusion
reactor.

### Mission 29 — Moon 11 / Selene

A brief missile lock on Moon 11 is not always a useful firing window. The X-40's
hard reversals, Escort UAV APS defense, and the phase after the first apparent
takedown are the parts most likely to stretch the fight.

The
[Mission 29 Moon 11 route](/ace-combat-8/mission-29-selene)
covers the attack windows, APS target priority, X-40 separation, and S-Rank
approach.

### Mission 30 — Song of Wings

The final operation repeatedly changes what can kill the Endurance. Anti-ship
missiles matter first, then guidance UAVs, surfaced submarine weapons, the hidden
underwater launch tube, and finally the ramming attack.

Use the
[Mission 30 Song of Wings route](/ace-combat-8/mission-30-song-of-wings)
when the current threat is unclear or the submarine seems to keep fighting after
its visible weapons are gone.

</section>

<section id="aircraft-progression" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Buy aircraft for the next problem, not because a new node appeared

The Aircraft Tree becomes expensive quickly if every new plane is treated as an
automatic purchase.

Aircraft, special weapons, missile upgrades, mobility parts, and electronic
warfare options all compete for the same MRP. A plane that is only slightly
better at the job you already handle comfortably may be less useful than an
upgrade that improves the aircraft you are actually flying.

The
[Ace Combat 8 aircraft guide](/ace-combat-8/aircraft-guide)
covers unlocks, roles, MRP costs, Aircraft Tree progression, and the difference
between normal purchases, story rewards, post-game aircraft, and other unlock
types.

During the first campaign, flexibility usually matters more than rushing toward
the most expensive aircraft available. Missions increasingly mix air, ground, and
naval targets, so a loadout that can survive an objective change often saves more
time than a specialized purchase that only dominates the opening phase.

Hold off on an expensive purchase when your current aircraft is still clearing
missions comfortably. Spend when a real limitation appears: not enough
air-to-air pressure, weak ground coverage, poor endurance in a long operation, or
a branch you need for later Aircraft Tree progress.

</section>

<section id="replay-goals" className="scroll-mt-24">

## After Mission 30, let the unfinished requirement choose the replay

Once the story is complete, different goals start asking for very different
sorties. Repeating a mission without knowing what you are advancing is the
quickest way to turn cleanup into a grind.

### You need Campaign Medals

**Meritorious Service requires 10 Campaign Medals**, not all 29.

Check the
[Ace Combat 8 medals guide](/ace-combat-8/medals)
before committing to a long cumulative grind. Several medals come from one
mission-specific condition, while others demand thousands of kills or an entire
campaign under a restriction. If you only need a few more medals, the short
mission challenges are usually the better place to start.

### You are missing named aircraft

Go to the
[Assault Records checklist](/ace-combat-8/assault-records).

Each missing aircraft is tied to a particular mission and appearance condition.
Replaying random sorties will not help when the aircraft only spawns after a
specific trigger.

### You want ACE completion, S ranks, or 100%

Use the
[trophy and achievement checklist](/ace-combat-8/trophies-achievements)
to separate what still needs a clean S-Rank attempt from what can continue
progressing during ordinary replays.

An S-Rank gap should send you back to that mission with a rank-focused plan.
There is no reason to restart the entire campaign simply because one result
screen was below S.

### MRP is now the bottleneck

Do your required replays first. Assault Records, rank cleanup, medals, and
aircraft progression all generate MRP while solving something else.

When currency is still the remaining obstacle after that work, move to the
[Mission 28 MRP farming route](/ace-combat-8/mrp-farm)
for a repeatable run built around target value, ammunition, and resupply timing.

</section>

<section id="replay-priority" className="scroll-mt-24">

## A useful replay should advance more than one thing when possible

Late-game cleanup gets much shorter when compatible goals are combined.

An ACE mission replay may also contribute to an S rank, cumulative kills, MRP,
aircraft usage, or a medal. An Assault Record attempt can still add destruction
and currency while you satisfy the spawn condition. A Mission 28 farming run can
build MRP and long combat counters at the same time.

The reverse is also true: some requirements are too specific to stack safely.
A no-damage campaign or machine-gun-only campaign should be treated as its own
run rather than something you casually add halfway through another objective.

Before launching, decide what has to be true when the result screen appears. That
single decision usually tells you whether the next sortie should be a rank
attempt, a medal challenge, an Assault Record run, or a farming repeat.

</section>
