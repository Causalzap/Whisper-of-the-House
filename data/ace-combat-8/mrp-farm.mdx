Ace Combat 8 has two long MRP milestones: **16,000,000 total MRP** for
**Need A Bigger Bank** and **25,000,000 total MRP** for **MRP Aplenty**.

Those are cumulative earning goals, so I would not sit on a large unused balance
just to protect trophy progress. Aircraft, special weapons, and upgrade parts still
need to be bought for other completion requirements. Spend MRP when a purchase
actually helps your aircraft collection or gives you a useful combat option; the
25-million target is about how much you earn over time, not having 25 million
sitting untouched at once.

The bigger decision is **when to stop progressing normally and start farming**.
Doing it too early creates extra work because ACE clears, S-rank attempts,
Assault Records, aircraft kills, and other replay goals all continue adding MRP.

<section id="when-to-farm-mrp" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Do not start a pure MRP grind after the first campaign

The first ending is too early to judge how much MRP you still need.

Several of the longest completion goals send you back into Campaign missions
anyway:

- clear the Campaign on ACE;
- earn the missing S ranks on ACE;
- finish all Assault Records;
- destroy **3,000 aircraft** in Campaign;
- complete the remaining aircraft, weapon, and part purchases.

Every one of those activities gives you another reason to fly before MRP becomes
the only unfinished counter.

I would finish the [Assault Records](/ace-combat-8/assault-records) before settling
into a repetitive money run. That collection alone sends you through many
different missions and includes special conditions that cannot be completed by
simply replaying Mission 28 over and over.

The same logic applies to ACE ranks. A replay that can give you a missing rank,
a named Ace, aircraft kills, and MRP is more valuable than a run whose only
purpose is moving the currency total.

Start farming when you check your remaining goals and the situation has become
simple: **the important mission-specific work is already done, but the 16M or
25M MRP milestone is still unfinished.**

</section>

## Mission 16 vs Mission 27 vs Mission 28: which should you farm?

There is no reason to use the same mission for every stage of MRP farming.

Mission 16 rewards fast target identification and clean execution. Mission 27 is a longer bombing route that becomes repeatable once the attack pattern is familiar. Mission 28 is better suited to players who also need to destroy large numbers of Campaign aircraft.

The Mission 16 and 27 examples work out to roughly **2.5 million MRP per hour of continuous mission time**. That is an idealized comparison, not a guaranteed hourly income. Menu navigation, restarting missions, missed targets, and failed runs reduce the actual rate.

Mission 28 is not included in that numerical comparison because I do not have an equivalent timed reward result for it.

If I am comfortable identifying the three ship patterns, Mission 16 offers very short runs. If those runs keep failing because I shoot the wrong ship or miss a UAV, Mission 27 can be the more reliable choice.

I would still use Mission 28 when I want its air-combat progress alongside MRP, rather than optimizing exclusively for money per minute.

<section id="mission-16-farm" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Mission 16 MRP farm: the three ship patterns

Mission 16, **Space Elevator Lighthouse**, revolves around identifying and destroying three disguised ships before dealing with the UAVs they launch.

The target ships have **three cranes and a red sun emblem**. I do not waste time attacking every vessel in the area. Instead, I inspect the first ship group near the Space Elevator and identify which of its three positions contains the target.

That first target determines the route through the remaining groups.

### Choose between a missile setup and a guns-only setup

For an easier clear, I use a multirole or Fighter equipped with multi-target air-to-air missiles. The F/A-18E with 6AAM-type weapons or Su-35S with suitable anti-air missiles is a useful option. Standard and special-missile speed or guidance upgrades help when the UAVs appear.

For the higher-reward guns-only approach, I use a **MiG-21bis Fishbed** or **F-4E Phantom II** with machine-gun pods and gun-enhancing parts.

The important difference is that a guns-only clear can provide an additional **30,000 MRP bonus**. I do not mix missile attacks into that attempt, because I am specifically trying to preserve the guns-only condition.

The reported MiG-21bis example earned approximately **129,000 MRP in three minutes**. I treat that as a repeatable-run target to work toward, not the starting expectation for someone learning the mission.

### Identify the first ship and follow its pattern

After the opening sequence, I approach the ship group near the Space Elevator and check which vessel matches the target description.

The three possible patterns can be followed using the Space Elevator as the center of a clock face:

**Pattern 1 — left/west starting ship**

Destroy the western target first. Continue toward the group around **11 o'clock**, where the next target is in the middle. Then move toward **9 o'clock** and attack the leftmost target in the next group.

This is the shortest of the three routes, so I pay attention to my speed to avoid flying past the final ship.

**Pattern 2 — middle starting ship**

Destroy the middle target, then head toward **12 o'clock**. Attack the leftmost ship in that group before continuing toward **8 o'clock**, where the final target is toward the front-right of the formation when approached from the north-northeast.

**Pattern 3 — right/east starting ship**

Destroy the eastern target, then head toward **10 o'clock** and attack the middle ship. Continue toward **7 o'clock** and locate the middle ship in the nearer row of the six-ship group.

These directions are relative to the Space Elevator and the approach used by the route. The exact target must still be confirmed before firing.

### Prepare for the UAVs before destroying the third ship

The third ship triggers the final UAV phase.

I do not want to finish that ship while flying away from the UAV approach with no room to turn.

For the missile setup, I begin repositioning around the last ship so that I can turn back toward the Space Elevator and meet the newly launched UAVs head-on. I allow my wingmen to use their special weapons when appropriate, then pick off any UAVs that escape the opening attack.

For a guns-only setup, alignment matters even more. Machine-gun pods can destroy the UAVs quickly when they fly into the firing path, but missing the opening group forces me to chase individual targets without missile assistance.

That is the main trade-off. The guns-only route has an additional reward, but an ordinary missile setup is easier when I am still learning which ships and UAVs to attack.

If I frequently fail the UAV phase or hit the wrong ships, I switch to a more forgiving setup rather than forcing a perfect three-minute clear.

</section>

<section id="mission-27-farm" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Mission 27 MRP farm: Su-34 bombing route

Mission 27, **Atusia**, is a strong alternative when I want a repeatable route without the disguised-ship identification of Mission 16.

The fast setup uses the **Su-34 Fullback** with large unguided bombs and 4AGM options. I equip useful bomb-enhancing parts and **Adaptive Armor Material** for extra protection during the repeated bombing passes.

For wingmen, I prefer aircraft equipped with **LACM**. I keep them grouped with special weapons enabled so their long-range attacks can help destroy the platforms and remaining surface targets.

### First pass: destroy the aircraft on the airfield

The first priority is the aircraft that have not taken off yet.

Bombing them while they are still on the ground removes enemies that would otherwise become airborne, distract my wingmen, or force additional dogfights later.

I deal with those parked aircraft before committing fully to the Mega Float's structural targets.

### Second pass: clear the outer support ring

Once the opening airfield targets are handled, I begin circling the Mega Float.

I attack the support pillars around its outer ring with bombs, keeping the aircraft moving rather than hovering over one platform under sustained anti-air fire.

Destroying the supports causes connected sections of the structure to collapse. That allows one bombing pass to accomplish more than individually chasing every small surface target.

As the outer platforms fall, the available anti-air fire decreases, making the following passes easier.

### Third pass: move inward and finish the remaining targets

After the outer ring, I move to the inner supports and any remaining required facilities.

I use bombs for clustered structures and 4AGMs or standard missiles to remove isolated targets left behind after the larger explosions.

The main objective is to reach the surrender sequence efficiently, not to spend extra time destroying every optional enemy that remains on the radar.

### Final phase: enter the core and escape

Once the mission shifts to the Mega Float's internal core, I move toward the southern side of the structure to prepare for the entry.

I watch for the marked entrance, fly into the opening, and attack the core. If another attack is required, I turn around for the follow-up pass before accelerating toward the return route.

Arriving near the entrance before this phase becomes active helps avoid an unnecessary long repositioning flight.

An optimized example of this route produced approximately **290,000 MRP in seven minutes**. That is around **2.5 million MRP per hour** before accounting for replay overhead.

Mission 27 becomes especially attractive if I can repeat its bombing passes consistently but keep losing Mission 16 attempts to missed UAVs or incorrect ship identification.

</section>

<section id="why-mission-28" className="scroll-mt-24">

## When Mission 28 is worth farming

Mission 28 is a useful choice when MRP is not the only thing I still need. Its sustained air-defense battle provides repeated opportunities to destroy Campaign aircraft, work toward combat-related achievements, and finish remaining mission objectives.

Unlike the short Mission 16 and 27 farming routes, I do not have a directly comparable timed MRP result for Mission 28. I choose it for the combined progression it provides, rather than assuming it earns more MRP per minute.

Mission 28 drops Joker Flight into a sustained defense of Sema Island against
what the operation describes as the remaining mass of the enemy air force.


That matters because the battle does not end after one small fighter group.
Reinforcements keep entering the airspace, bomber waves arrive during the fight,
and the sortie lasts long enough for ammunition management to become an actual
part of the route.

One ACE run can still show a HUD score of **33,960** deep into the fight while
another bomber wave is arriving:

![Bombers approaching Sema Island during a high-score Mission 28 run.](/images/ace-combat-8/mission-28-harmonius-tu160-bombers.webp)

That **33,960 is mission score, not an MRP balance**. I use it as a useful sign of
how much combat Mission 28 can pack into one clear, not as a promise that the
post-mission MRP reward will equal that number.

The mission also gives you something a long farm route badly needs: a way to
rearm without abandoning the sortie. That lets you keep converting new enemy
waves into progress instead of ending a good run because the first load of
missiles is gone.

</section>

<section id="mission-28-priority" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Farm aircraft without letting the bombers reach Sema Island

Mission 28 fills the radar quickly, but not every contact deserves the same
attention.

The critical distinction is between fighters occupying your attention and
aircraft that are actively pushing the attack toward Sema Island. Bomber calls
deserve an immediate response because the operation is still a defense mission,
not an endless free-flight score attack.

A useful rhythm is:

1. intercept an incoming bomber wave;
2. remove fighters that are already interfering with your attack;
3. work through nearby aircraft while the next wave forms;
4. turn back to bombers as soon as AWACS calls them out.

The battle explicitly brings in **TU-160 bombers**, and later communication
confirms multiple bomber waves. By the third wave, the fight is still feeding
new aircraft into the area.

Do not chase one fighter far away from the island merely because you already
damaged it. The valuable run is the one you finish. A single stubborn contact is
not worth allowing a bomber formation to cross the defensive space behind you.

This also makes Mission 28 useful while **Aircraft Annihilator** is still
unfinished. Its **3,000-aircraft Campaign requirement** and the MRP grind can
advance during the same replay instead of being treated as two separate jobs.

</section>

<section id="when-to-resupply" className="scroll-mt-24">

## When should you return to Sema Island to resupply?

Do not wait for every weapon counter to reach zero.

Partway through the battle, AWACS can explicitly warn that Joker One is
**burning through ammunition fast** and should resupply. By this stage the fight
has already continued through several formations, and bombers are still entering
the operation.

Later, the island confirms that the base is ready to resupply you.

![Sema Island is ready to resupply Joker One during Mission 28.](/images/ace-combat-8/mission-28-sema-island-resupply-ready.webp)

That is the useful return window.

I look at three things before turning back:

**Standard missiles.**  
A low standard-missile count makes the next large fighter group unnecessarily
slow even if a few special weapons remain.

**Your main air-to-air special weapon.**  
Mission 28 keeps producing clustered or high-value air targets. Returning while
you can still defend yourself is safer than waiting until the useful special
weapon is completely empty.

**The current bomber situation.**  
Do not start the landing cycle while an urgent bomber wave is already closing on
Sema Island. Clear the threat that can hurt the defense, then use the quieter gap
to rearm.

The mission itself demonstrates why the return matters: shortly after the
resupply warning, the run can have standard ammunition and special ammunition
nearing depletion while fresh enemies are still arriving. This is not a fight
designed around preserving one original loadout for the entire duration.

</section>

<section id="loadout-for-farming" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Pick a loadout for repeated air kills, not one perfect duel

Mission 28 is overwhelmingly about aircraft.

That makes a sustained air-to-air loadout more useful than building the whole
aircraft around one elite opponent. The farm has to work across ordinary
fighters, bombers, reinforcements, and later tougher aircraft without forcing a
return after every short wave.

Prioritize:

- enough standard missile capacity to keep clearing ordinary contacts;
- an air-to-air special weapon you can use repeatedly rather than save for one
  final target;
- an aircraft you already fly comfortably under heavy missile pressure.

There is no need to buy an unfamiliar aircraft solely because it looks stronger
on paper if it makes your repeated clears slower or less reliable. MRP farming is
a repetition problem. Consistency matters more than winning one spectacular
dogfight.

The exception is when a purchase is already required for your remaining Aircraft
Tree collection. In that case, buying the aircraft can serve two purposes:
progress the collection and give you another option to test during the next run.

</section>

<section id="spend-or-save-mrp" className="scroll-mt-24">

## You do not need to save 25,000,000 MRP in your balance

The two milestones are based on **total MRP earned**:

- **16,000,000 MRP** — Need A Bigger Bank
- **25,000,000 MRP** — MRP Aplenty

So the useful question is not “Can I spend this without ruining the 25M run?”
It is “Does this purchase advance something I still need?”

Buy aircraft, special weapons, and upgrade parts that contribute to the remaining
collection or make your next replay more effective. There is little value in
hoarding currency while leaving required purchases untouched.

On the other hand, once your collection goals are finished, spending MRP just
because it is available does not make the farming phase shorter. At that point,
keep flying and watch the cumulative milestone instead of inventing new shopping
goals.

</section>

<section id="combine-mrp-goals" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Stack MRP with the long counters before farming only money

The most efficient late-game run is one that removes more than one unfinished
requirement.

Before loading Mission 28 again, check whether you still need:

**3,000 Campaign aircraft kills.**  
Mission 28 is naturally useful because of its repeated air formations.

**Air-to-air special-weapon kills.**  
A fighter-heavy mission gives you plenty of opportunities to move that counter
while earning MRP.

**ACE completion or an S rank.**  
A rank attempt should be treated as a rank attempt first. Do not throw away the
mission result chasing one extra optional aircraft.

**Assault Records.**  
Use the mission-specific trigger instead of treating every replay as a generic
farm. Mission 28 itself contains Assault Record work, including Harmonius and,
after the Mission 18 prerequisite is handled, the Lux Lunae 22nd Unit.


Once those overlapping requirements disappear, I choose the route that I can repeat most reliably.

For pure MRP, I compare my Mission 16 guns-only clears against the Mission 27 Su-34 bombing route. Both have demonstrated high earnings in optimized examples, but failed runs can erase their time advantage.

If I still need Campaign aircraft kills, Mission 28 remains useful because each completed defensive sortie can advance that requirement alongside MRP. I return to Sema Island to resupply when necessary and prioritize the incoming bombers rather than chasing optional aircraft.


</section>

<section id="finish-25-million" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Stop farming as soon as the remaining MRP milestone is complete

The 16M achievement is only an intermediate checkpoint. If full completion is
your goal, the number that eventually matters is **25,000,000 total MRP**.

Check your progress after a finished sortie rather than trying to infer the
remaining currency from the HUD score during combat. Mission score tells you how
productive the battle has been, but it is not the same display as your cumulative
MRP total.

When the 25M milestone is complete, do not keep repeating the same MRP farming mission out of habit.

Return to the
[trophy and achievement checklist](/ace-combat-8/trophies-achievements) and see
what is actually still unfinished.

At that stage, another money run only makes sense when it also advances a
remaining kill or purchase requirement. Otherwise, move to the mission containing
the missing objective.

If Aircraft Tree purchases are the reason you are still farming, the
[aircraft guide](/ace-combat-8/aircraft-guide) shows all 34 aircraft, their
individual prices, unlock categories, and how to prioritize aircraft and parts
without wasting MRP on unnecessary branches.

</section>
