<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
Damage Fatsia efficiently instead of clearing every target: work one arm at a
time, use **support pillars** and explosive industrial targets for large
structural damage, and remove major guns only when they block your attack runs.
When command orders **hold fire**, stop shooting. After the Tonitra Spear threat
appears, enter the marked central opening and hit the **fusion reactor again**

</GuideQuickAnswer>

Fatsia is built on a scale that makes ordinary target-by-target clearing
inefficient. The Mega Float has a central terminal surrounded by **eight outer
modules**, each stretching roughly **six kilometers** from the center. Runways,
weapons, and air defenses occupy the upper decks, while docks, factories, fuel
stores, shipyards, and other industrial facilities sit below.

The operation does not require every object on those eight arms to disappear.
What matters is causing enough structural damage to force the defenders into the
next stage, recognizing the moment when firing has to stop, and then changing
completely from a wide ground-attack battle to a confined run inside the central
structure.

<section id="break-fatsia-exterior" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Break Fatsia without clearing every target

Fatsia's size makes travel time part of the fight. Crossing repeatedly from one
side of the Mega Float to another wastes long stretches of the mission, so it is
usually better to commit to an arm, damage the useful targets there, and only
move when the next section offers a better attack route.

The destruction percentage tells you how much damage the fortress has taken.
Calls such as **20%** and later **50% destruction** show that the assault is
progressing, but they are not thresholds you need to memorize. Keep attacking
until command explicitly changes the objective.

### Work one arm before crossing the fortress

A good pass should accomplish more than removing one isolated AA icon. If the
same approach gives you access to a support, an explosive facility, and a weapon
covering that structure, combine them into one route instead of circling the arm
several times.

Long, straight approaches are risky because the larger guns can cover wide
sections of the Mega Float. Make the run, take the useful targets already on your
line, then extend before turning back rather than remaining pointed at one arm
while searching for the last minor defense.

### Use support pillars to collapse the upper deck

The supports beneath the upper platforms are some of the most valuable targets
on Fatsia. Destroying them can bring an entire section of deck down along with
the facilities sitting above it.

![A support pillar beneath Fatsia's upper deck is targeted during Mission 27.](/images/ace-combat-8/mission-27-mega-float-support-pillars.webp)

The difficult part is the approach. Pillars sit underneath the platform, so the
attack naturally pulls you into a lower corridor where AA fire, missiles, and
the fortress's larger weapons have a cleaner shot.

Do not stay underneath the arm trying to line up several supports in one slow
pass. Hit the pillar you already have, continue through the opening, regain
space, and come back from a fresh angle.

When enough of the structure loses its support, the upper deck collapses and
takes multiple facilities with it. One well-positioned attack can therefore do
far more than shooting the same number of small targets on the surface.

### Use fuel and industrial targets when the underside is too dangerous

The lower levels also contain fuel and other explosive facilities. Their
secondary explosions can damage the surrounding structure, making them useful
when a pillar is awkward to reach or an underside approach is covered by too
much defensive fire.

This gives you two efficient ways to build damage:

- attack supports when the flight path underneath an arm is reasonably clear;
- attack clustered fuel or explosive facilities when they sit on an easier pass.

Neither requires clearing every weapon around the module first.

### Remove the big guns when they block your route

Main guns and rail-gun positions are worth destroying when they are preventing
you from making the structural attack you want.

The difference is whether the weapon is actually controlling your flight path.
A large gun already lining up on the arm you are attacking can make the next low
pass far more dangerous; removing it creates room to return to the pillars,
fuel, or other high-value structures.

Small AA positions are lower priority unless they are repeatedly forcing you
off the route. Fatsia has too many targets to dismantle every defensive system
before doing meaningful structural damage.

### Do you need to destroy all eight float modules?

No. Fatsia has **eight outer modules**, but Mission 27 does not require all eight
arms to be completely destroyed.

Continue attacking useful structural targets until command decides the fortress
has taken enough damage. When the next order arrives, the remaining intact
targets no longer matter.

## Stop attacking when the hold-fire order appears

Eventually the assault has done enough damage for command to demand Fatsia's
surrender.

![Mission 27 orders Joker Flight to hold fire while the surrender demand is transmitted.](/images/ace-combat-8/mission-27-hold-fire-surrender.webp)

When you hear **hold fire**, stop shooting.

The HUD can still show weapons, buildings, and other valid-looking targets, but
the operation has moved into a negotiation. Continuing to attack because
something remains targetable is the wrong response.

This pause feels unusually quiet after several minutes of continuous bombing,
especially when large parts of Fatsia are still intact. Wait for the radio
exchange to finish rather than trying to squeeze in more destruction.

### Why does the battle continue after the surrender demand?

The surrender sequence reveals that the larger threat has not disappeared.
Laser targeting appears on the allied fleet and another **Tonitra Spear** attack
becomes part of the operation.

At that point, destroying another exterior arm is no longer the solution. The
important system is inside the **central section** of Fatsia, and Joker One is
given a new route toward it.

The battle has stopped being a wide destruction operation. From here on, the
correct target is no longer somewhere among hundreds of exterior icons.

</section>

<section id="central-entry" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Enter Fatsia through the central opening

Once the Tonitra Spear sequence begins, leave the outer arms and look for the
marked entry point on the central structure.

Approach it with enough distance to straighten the aircraft before crossing the
opening. There is little room for a large correction after entry, so forcing a
bad angle is much harder than extending once and coming back aligned.

Your wingmen remain outside. **Joker One enters alone.**

The target inside is the **fusion reactor**.

### Do not rush the entry angle

The temptation is to dive toward the opening as soon as the marker appears, but
the interior gives you much less space than the exterior battle. A poor entry can
leave the aircraft offset from the reactor with too little room to set up the
follow-up attack.

Use the approach to solve the alignment before entering:

- center the aircraft on the opening;
- avoid excessive speed that makes the interior correction harder;
- keep enough control authority to turn back toward the reactor after the first
  strike.

There is no useful reason to explore other parts of the interior. The reactor is
the objective.

</section>

<section id="fusion-reactor" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Destroy the fusion reactor before the shutter closes

Reaching the reactor does not mean one hit will immediately finish the sequence.

The first successful attack damages its outer protection, and the mission can
still report **no change in the heat signature** afterward. At the same time, a
large shutter begins closing across the interior route.

![The fusion reactor is attacked while Fatsia's internal shutter begins closing.](/images/ace-combat-8/mission-27-fusion-reactor-shutter.webp)

Do not interpret that first heat-signature call as proof that you attacked the
wrong target. The reactor has taken damage, but another successful strike is
still required.

### Turn back for the second reactor attack immediately

The first hit leaves very little reason to continue deeper into the structure.
Get Joker One pointed back toward the reactor as quickly as the interior space
allows and prepare another attack while the shutter is moving.

The important sequence is:

1. enter the central structure;
2. hit the fusion reactor;
3. use the remaining interior space to turn back;
4. attack the reactor again before the shutter blocks the route;
5. wait for the **high heat-signature** confirmation.

The closing shutter is part of this sequence. It does not mean you entered the
wrong passage or missed a hidden switch somewhere else in Fatsia.

### What if the shutter is already closing?

Keep moving toward the reactor.

Do not abandon the target to search for another route, and do not turn the
interior into a loop looking for something else to destroy. The reactor remains
the target while the available attack window shrinks.

A clean initial entry makes this much easier because the first pass determines
how much room you have left for the return attack.

### Why does the reactor matter to the Tonitra Spear attack?

Fatsia's central system is connected to the continuing orbital-weapon threat.
Once the reactor is destroyed, the Mega Float loses the power and control needed
to continue operating that sequence normally.

Projectiles that have already been released do not simply disappear, but the
planned strike is disrupted after the central system goes down. That is why the
mission eventually stops caring about the remaining exterior structures and
reduces the entire fight to one internal target.

</section>

<section id="after-fatsia" className="scroll-mt-24">

## What happens after Fatsia falls?

Destroying the reactor ends Fatsia's role as the enemy's major floating military
and production complex and removes its central system from the continuing
Tonitra Spear operation.

The campaign is not finished. The remaining orbital-weapon threat and the people
behind it still have to be dealt with.

Continue with the
[Ace Combat 8 walkthrough](/ace-combat-8/walkthrough) for Mission 28 and the
final stretch of the campaign.
