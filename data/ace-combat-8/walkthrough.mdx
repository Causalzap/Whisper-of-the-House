Ace Combat 8's campaign runs through a Prologue and **30 numbered missions**.
Early sorties teach interception, carrier defense, and close air support, but
later operations change objectives much more aggressively. A mission can begin
as an escort and end with missile interception, visual identification,
structural demolition, or a multi-stage fight where the correct target changes
several times.

A useful rule throughout the campaign is simple: **ask what can make the mission
fail right now**. A crowded radar does not necessarily need to be cleared. If a
bomber is approaching the fleet, a cruise missile is already airborne, or a
friendly assault group has stopped moving, that problem takes priority over an
enemy fighter that happens to be closer.

## Missions 1–5: Learning target priorities

### Mission 1 — Jokers Wild

Mission 1 establishes a rule that remains useful for the rest of the campaign:
**the nearest enemy is not always the enemy that matters**. The opening UAVs give
you room to learn lock range, missile timing, target switching, and radar
reading, but the more important test begins when reconnaissance aircraft arrive
with fighter cover.

Those recon planes are the objective. Escorts can interrupt a missile lock or
force an evasive turn, but following one into a long dogfight gives the
reconnaissance aircraft more time to escape. Close until the lock is stable,
destroy the recon targets, and only engage escorts that are directly preventing
the interception.

Once the reconnaissance aircraft are gone, the sortie moves toward recovery
rather than hiding another major combat phase.

### Mission 2 — Nomad

The Endurance comes under attack before the opening sequence has fully settled,
so Mission 2 quickly becomes carrier defense. Keep the fight close enough to the
fleet that you can turn back when bombers or low-flying attackers break through.

Ordinary fighters become dangerous mainly when they pull Joker Flight away from
the ship while heavier aircraft continue toward it. When bomber formations
appear, abandon long pursuits and intercept them before they reach the carrier.

Special weapons are more useful against grouped attackers approaching the same
airspace than against one isolated fighter. Later attacks can also come in low,
making radar awareness particularly important around the fleet.

### Mission 3 — Purple Magic Takes Flight

White Valley begins with **three radar sites** around the port that need to
remain operational while successive enemy formations attack the base.

Stay close enough to intercept aircraft before they settle onto those
installations. Bombers deserve priority over ordinary fighters because they can
continue damaging the radar network while a dogfight drifts somewhere else.

Purple Magic joins as the battle expands, so Joker Flight does not need to erase
every contact personally. Watch where the pressure is moving and keep stronger
ammunition for heavier formations later in the sortie.

### Mission 4 — The Fall of the Numbered Fleets

Mission 4 sends Joker Flight against **two expeditionary fleets at separate
ports on Newfield Island**. The Third Fleet is at Beltaport in the northwest,
while the Fifth Fleet operates from Anderson Port in the east.

An anti-surface loadout fits the operation well. Ships, shore defenses, aircraft,
and the defected 262nd — the Sharks — all compete for attention, but the naval
force remains the main objective.

A clean attack rhythm is:

- finish ships or defensive targets already close to destruction;
- remove AA or radar positions blocking the next attack run;
- clear only the fighters directly interfering with the strike;
- move to the second fleet once the first one collapses.

The Sharks can pull Joker Flight into a prolonged dogfight if you follow them too
far. If allied ships are taking losses, return to the surface battle instead of
chasing an aircraft across the island.

### Mission 5 — Retaking of Theve

The landing craft are the center of Mission 5. Aircraft, ships, tanks, howitzers,
and helicopters matter because they can stop those troops from reaching shore
or advancing inland.

During the approach, remove anything firing directly on the landing force. Once
the troops are ashore, follow their progress and attack whatever blocks the next
stretch of ground. A vehicle or artillery position stopping the advance matters
more than a fighter already leaving the operation area.

The mission begins teaching you to alternate between air and surface threats
instead of completely clearing one category before touching the other.

## Missions 6–10: Drinwich, Faith Park, and the first Land Battleship

### Mission 6 — Attack on the Drinwich Airfield

Mission 6 begins with aerial refueling, then becomes a combined assault on
Drinwich Airfield. Friendly helicopters and mechanized units approach along
different routes while SAMs, fighters, and armored defenders try to stop them.

The important constraints are easy to lose track of once the airfield becomes
busy:

- support whichever allied route is currently under pressure;
- do not let enemy aircraft pull you far away from the advancing units;
- remove armor the ground force cannot handle by itself;
- avoid destroying fuel and ammunition facilities inside the airfield.

The FCU wants the base and its supplies intact. Chain explosions can destroy the
resources the operation is trying to capture, so precise attacks are more useful
than flattening everything near a hostile vehicle.

### Mission 7 — A Lifeline in the Desert

**24,000 points is the number that matters in Faith Park Valley.** The objective
is to cripple the supply network supporting Land Battleship 1, not to destroy
every target on the map.

Faith Park has four major attack areas:

- **North:** solar power plant;
- **West:** airfield and enemy fighters;
- **East:** ammunition storage;
- **South:** refinery with heavier ground defenses.

The sectors can be attacked in any order, so choose based on distance, remaining
ammunition, and the targets already lined up for your next pass.

At the northern plant, many lighter solar structures are fragile enough for
guns, letting you save missiles for tougher facilities elsewhere. Once a sector
has lost most of its valuable targets, moving on is usually better than circling
for one final small structure.

The eastern ammunition area contains dense targets and large secondary
explosions, but the **tunnel depot cannot be destroyed from above**. Drop low,
find the entrance cut into the hillside, and attack through the opening.

Mission 7 also provides a **return line** for resupply and loadout changes. Use
it when several substantial ground sectors remain and your current weapons no
longer suit them. Returning when you are already close to 24,000 usually costs
more time than finishing the remaining score with what you have.

Once the requirement is reached, the HUD makes the change clear:

![Ace Combat 8 Mission 7 bonus time after reaching the required score.](/images/ace-combat-8/mission-7-faith-park-bonus-time.webp)

The display can show **25,470 / 24,000** while AWACS tells you to continue
destroying targets until the timer expires. That is bonus time. The supply
network has already taken enough damage; there is no requirement to reach 100%
destruction.

Use the remaining time on nearby facility clusters, ammo dumps, or aircraft
already in position instead of crossing the entire valley for isolated targets.

### Mission 8 — Retaking of Theve II

Mission 8 returns to close air support with a broader mixture of naval, ground,
and air threats. Bombers deserve immediate attention because escorts can occupy
Joker Flight while the aircraft carrying the real payload continue toward
friendly forces.

Follow the allied advance below you. When one group reports being blocked,
destroy the vehicles or weapons directly in front of it, then move with the line.
Enemies already sitting behind the advance are less important unless they begin
another attack.

As the operation approaches enemy headquarters, the radar becomes crowded
enough that total target count stops being useful. Allied movement is the better
indicator: if the troops are advancing again, the immediate blocker has been
removed.

### Mission 9 — The Land Battleship Blockade

The Land Battleship cannot be stopped by treating its hull like one large health
bar. Ground forces first use bomb trucks to expose the mobility system, so wait
for the armor around the treads to open and attack the newly vulnerable targets
instead of spending missiles on protected sections.

Immobilizing the battleship does not finish the mission. The operation later
moves through containment and another propulsion problem, with the correct
target changing each time the ship finds a new way to keep moving. The
[Mission 9 Land Battleship walkthrough](/ace-combat-8/mission-9-land-battleship-blockade)
covers the complete sequence when the bomb trucks cannot expose the treads, the
battleship begins moving again after being disabled, or the final propulsion
targets are still unclear.

### Mission 10 — Over the Rainband

Do not search the clouds at random. **Wait for the targeting lasers.**

Heavy cloud cover interferes with the Tonitra satellite's ability to lock moving
targets, so airships inside the Rainband provide laser guidance for incoming
Spear strikes. Their jamming also makes radar a poor way to locate them before
they reveal themselves.

When the attack window begins:

- keep the fleet alive against nearby fighters;
- watch the Spear countdown;
- wait for the visible targeting laser;
- follow the beam toward the guidance airship.

![Ace Combat 8 Mission 10 laser guidance leading toward an airship.](/images/ace-combat-8/mission-10-airship-laser.webp)

Later, **two airships can guide at the same time**. Both must be destroyed before
the strike reaches the carrier. Drone screens can interfere with locking, so
clear enough of the shield to expose one airship, destroy it, and move directly
to the second guidance source rather than cleaning every drone from the area.

Once the active guidance airships are gone, the interference clears and the
fleet can continue through the Rainband.

## Missions 11–15: Heavy transports, Fort Grays, and escape

### Mission 11 — Maximum Payload

Thunderclouds and jamming reduce radar usefulness, so use **contrails and visual
contact** to locate the large transports carrying Land Battleship components.
Keep those aircraft ahead of surrounding fighters and unrelated surface targets
in your priority order.

Their exposed propulsion components are the practical way to slow and eventually
bring them down. Once a transport has already lost part of its propulsion, stay
on that aircraft long enough to finish the job instead of spreading damage
across several large targets.

Jamming, contrail tracking, propulsion damage, AA weapons, and later fighter
pressure overlap once the battle becomes crowded. The
[Mission 11 Maximum Payload walkthrough](/ace-combat-8/mission-11-maximum-payload)
explains how those systems fit together when you can find the transports but
still cannot bring them down cleanly.

### Mission 12

Fort Grays begins as a conventional base defense, but the important threat
changes once C-130s start dropping cruise-missile pallets.

There are two ways to stop each attack:

- destroy the pallet before launch;
- intercept the missiles once they are airborne.

Destroying the pallet is easier. Once missiles are already moving, however,
everything else waits. The range calls can fall from **15,000** to **10,000**
and then **5,000** from the base while you are still closing, so finishing an
unrelated fighter during that sequence can cost the interception.

More C-130s arrive from different directions, making it important to remain near
Fort Grays after the first wave instead of drifting far from the base.

### Mission 13 — The People of the Lost Century

The operation begins as reconnaissance and turns into an escort once Joker
Flight commits to helping the resistance. From that point onward, **the
transport is the objective**.

Move slightly ahead of its route and remove the next AA position, vehicle group,
or portable launcher before the transport reaches it, then return toward the
escort instead of continuing deeper into hostile territory. Threats can appear
behind or above the route, so remaining permanently in front creates another
kind of vulnerability.

If the transport is destroyed, the mission fails, so do not trade its safety
for extra kills away from the escort route.

A fuel leak adds pressure later in the mission. Clear the landing area before
the transport arrives and stay close enough to react to another portable
launcher during the final approach.

### Mission 14 — Two on the Run

Mission 14 removes the usual option of fighting your way out. The escape
aircraft is **unarmed**, while radar and quadcopter coverage makes open sky
dangerous.

Stay low and use the canyon. Detection does not immediately end the mission, but
once fighters identify you, stealth becomes an evasion problem and fuel makes
wide detours increasingly expensive.

Later, the **mushroom cloud** becomes an escape tool. Diving through it disrupts
visibility and missile tracking long enough to shake immediate pursuit. After
coming out the other side, return to the canyon and use terrain to prevent the
search network from rebuilding a clean track.

### Mission 15 — Wings of Wax

Heavy bombers are the main danger to the allied fleet. Fighters can fill the
radar and force evasive movement, but repeated bomber runs can cost ships much
more quickly.

Stay between the bomber formations and the fleet, especially once the operation
warns that another lost vessel could force an abort. When a bomber exposes
engines, weapon bays, or other vulnerable components, attack those parts rather
than scattering missiles across nearby escorts.

Some bomber weapons create dangerous blast zones, so break away after a
successful pass rather than sitting directly behind a damaged aircraft waiting
for another lock.

## Missions 16–20: Identification, rockets, and the Space Elevator

### Mission 16

Mission 16 is an identification problem before it becomes an interception
mission. There are **three disguised terrorist container ships**, and the safe
way to identify them is by the same pair of visual markers:

- **three cranes on the deck**;
- **a sun emblem on the hull**.

![A disguised terrorist ship in Mission 16 identified by the sun emblem on its hull and three cranes on deck.](/images/ace-combat-8/mission-16-disguised-ship-sun-emblem-three-cranes.webp)

Civilian traffic remains in the same waters, and attacking the wrong vessel is
not acceptable. Close enough to confirm both markers before firing. Unmarked
ships have already been identified as safe.

The terrorists need less than ten minutes to prepare their self-destruct UAVs,
which makes long inspection loops expensive. Fighters can appear during the
search, but unless one is actively preventing identification, keep looking for
the ships.

Sinking all three does not necessarily end the danger. UAVs can launch while the
last ship is going down, and the objective then changes immediately. The drones
climb toward the Space Elevator and can be called at **6,000 m**, later reaching
**9,000 m** on the way toward the **12,000 m** target altitude, so stop
inspecting surface traffic and intercept them before they climb out of reach.

### Mission 17 — Land Battleship No. 3

Prepare the attack corridor before the friendly bombers arrive. SAMs, radar
sites, and anti-air weapons capable of threatening their approach are more
important than unrelated vehicles, and trying to damage the Land Battleship
early does little to help the planned strike.

The rail gun remains dangerous even after much of the local air defense is gone,
so avoid long, straight attack runs while waiting for the bomber formation.
When the bombers arrive, protect their attack window.

After the strike lands, follow the newly marked weapon systems instead of
returning to a general sweep of the battlefield.

### Mission 18 — Moonlight and Shadows

Mission 18 has an unusual restriction: **the rockets must be destroyed after
launch, not while they are still on the ground or at low altitude**.

Before that phase begins, Queen Flight is under pressure from Shadow aircraft.
Help with the enemies threatening Queen One and Queen Three rather than following
unrelated bandits across the map.

Once the operation switches to the launch site:

- leave retreating Shadows alone;
- wait for both rockets to lift off;
- commit to one rocket and destroy it;
- turn immediately toward the second;
- after both rockets are gone, destroy the large falling debris.

The rockets will eventually move beyond your effective ceiling, so spreading
damage between both of them wastes closing time.

### Mission 19 — Clearing the Lighthouse

The second Land Battleship is positioned beside the Space Elevator, which makes
collateral damage part of the fight. The target is enormous, but the structure
next to it is something the operation is trying to preserve.

Clear the anti-air defenses threatening the friendly bomber and give it a clean
approach. Its strike is part of the plan for opening the battleship.

As new components become vulnerable, follow the marked targets rather than
firing through the Space Elevator area at whichever section of the hull is
easiest to reach. Main weapons and launch systems threatening the elevator or
allied forces remain the important targets.

### Mission 20 — Escort Orbiter

The shuttle transport attracts enough fighters that Mission 20 initially looks
like a conventional escort, but the critical threat is the **large missile**.

![Ace Combat 8 Mission 20 large missile approaching the shuttle transport.](/images/ace-combat-8/mission-20-escort-orbiter-large-missile.webp)

These missiles use **image recognition** to identify the transport, so chaff and
flares do not solve the problem.

When a large-missile warning appears:

- break away from the current dogfight;
- turn directly toward the missile;
- destroy it before returning to nearby fighters.

Stay close enough to the shuttle that another launch does not leave you several
kilometers out of position. Damage can slow the transport, making later waves
even more dangerous.

Later, other aircraft take over shuttle protection while Joker One is left to
deal with the remaining Shadow pressure. Once that handoff occurs, focus on the
air battle instead of continuing to orbit the transport.

## Missions 21–25: Launch defense and the Rocky Island offensive

### Mission 21 — The Star-Bound Bird

The shuttle countdown is the main clock in Mission 21. Aircraft and naval
targets converge on the launch area, but the important enemy is whichever one
can interrupt the shuttle or its control facilities next.

When Magnolia arrives, **ESM support** improves the air battle inside its
coverage. Use that area against the heavier formations appearing later in the
countdown, and avoid exhausting all special ammunition on the opening wave.

As long as the launch facilities remain intact and the shuttle keeps moving
through its countdown, the defense is still working.

### Mission 22 — Pursuing the HSCA

The HSCA is trying to reach Fatsia's defensive zone, so stay on the aircraft
unless another enemy is directly preventing the pursuit. When propulsion
components become available, attack them to reduce its ability to escape instead
of spreading damage across unrelated weapons.

Long-range fire from Fatsia becomes more dangerous as the chase approaches the
Mega Float. Keep enough lateral movement to avoid turning the pursuit into one
predictable straight line.

Once the HSCA is neutralized, leave the defensive area rather than remaining
under Fatsia's weapons unnecessarily.

### Mission 23 — The Destruction of Expo City

Expo City is too large to clear in geographical order. Follow the friendly
advance instead.

Land destroyers, rail guns, artillery, and other heavy ground units can stop the
assault columns outright. When an allied group reports that it cannot move,
destroy the unit blocking it before spending time on aircraft elsewhere.

The battle later shifts toward naval fire from the bay. Silence the ships
holding the advance back, then return inland as the airfield and headquarters
become the next resistance points.

A return line is available during the wider operation. Use it when ammunition is
actually limiting the next heavy phase rather than simply because the option is
there.

### Mission 24 — The Battle of Rocky Island, Day 1

Three assault groups are trying to establish beachheads, and their pressure
changes independently.

During the opening:

- destroy coastal guns firing into the landing craft;
- stabilize the assault group currently taking the most pressure;
- move when Alpha, Bravo, or Charlie reports a new blocker.

After the beachheads are secure, follow the ground advance inland toward the
Turntable and geothermal plant. The Turntable becomes the final pocket of
resistance, and clearing its defenders gives the landing force the foothold
needed for the second day.

### Mission 25 — The Battle of Rocky Island, Day 2

Day 2 divides the ground pressure between eastern and western routes, and neither
side remains safe permanently. Watch which force is moving and which has
stopped; a route that was dangerous a minute ago may no longer need Joker Flight,
while the opposite side can suddenly be pinned by vehicles, SAMs, or
reinforcements.

The return line is useful if the long operation has already drained the weapons
needed for later ground phases. Otherwise, use guns on lighter targets and save
missiles for heavier positions.

As the assault reaches the main defenses, Land Battleship weapons begin blocking
the advance. Destroy the marked systems and stay with the final push instead of
peeling away toward isolated aircraft.

## Missions 26–30: Fleet battle, Fatsia, Selene, and the final sortie

### Mission 26 — Crossing Fleets

Mission 26 mixes air and naval threats densely enough that the closest contact
is often the wrong target.

The battle repeatedly changes what matters most:

- bombers and anti-ship attacks take priority while the fleet is exposed;
- radar and AA sites matter when friendly bombers need a clear approach;
- the return line becomes useful if the next heavy phase is approaching and your
  anti-surface weapons are nearly empty.

Listening to fleet reports is more reliable than trying to clear the entire radar
in one direction.

### Mission 27 — Fatsia, The Ocean Fortress

Fatsia is a multi-stage Mega Float rather than a collection of unrelated
buildings. During the exterior assault, structural and high-value targets matter
most, with support pillars offering particularly efficient damage because
collapsing them can bring larger deck sections down.

Three state changes are especially important:

- attack the exterior until the required destruction is reached;
- **stop firing** when the surrender order arrives;
- once Joker One enters the central structure, shift from exterior damage to the
  fusion-reactor sequence.

The exterior modules, structural collapse, surrender pause, central entry,
shutter timing, and reactor attack are all connected parts of the same fight.
The [Mission 27 Fatsia walkthrough](/ace-combat-8/mission-27-fatsia-ocean-fortress)
covers those transitions when the outside targets stop making sense, the
hold-fire order appears, or the reactor phase begins inside the fortress.

### Mission 28 — A Thousand Points of Light

Sema Island becomes the center of a long air-defense battle, making early
ammunition discipline important. Bombers and slower heavy aircraft should be
removed before ordinary fighters when they are moving toward the island because
they can continue threatening the defense while dogfights drift elsewhere.

Several waves arrive, including reinforcements on both sides. Electronic support
later creates more favorable conditions, so use that coverage when the heavier
late formations enter instead of spending the whole sortie fighting in neutral
airspace.

The final portion brings higher-value enemy pilots into the engagement. Once
they appear, support the fight deciding the wave instead of trying to remove
every remaining contact.

### Mission 29 — Selene

Moon 11 is not only trying to win a duel. The **targeting laser on her aircraft
is guiding a Tonitra Spear toward the disabled Land Battleship near Theve**,
turning the dogfight into a race to stop the guidance attack.

The Land Battleship is nuclear-powered, so a direct orbital strike could destroy
the reactor and spread radioactive contamination.

Moon 11 can sustain extremely hard turns, so firing every time the lock briefly
appears wastes missiles. Keep enough speed to remain inside the fight, allow the
high-G maneuver to develop, and attack when the flight path becomes more
predictable.

Wingman pressure can create those openings by forcing her to react to more than
one attack angle. Cloud cover can also break weapon lock, so reposition and
reacquire rather than continuing to fire into a disappearing solution.

Keep following the mission state after heavy damage. The fight is not finished
until the guidance threat is actually stopped.

### Mission 30 — Song of Wings

Mission 30 begins with the Endurance under attack while the enemy submarine
remains underwater, so searching for the submarine itself is a distraction.
Intercept the incoming anti-ship missiles first and stay close enough to the
carrier that another volley cannot arrive while you are several kilometers away.

The middle of the fight is easier to follow as a sequence:

- destroy the first terminal-guidance UAVs;
- follow the Endurance toward the Rainband;
- keep intercepting missiles while the airship laser is redirected;
- destroy the second wave of guidance UAVs so the friendly laser remains the
  only active guidance source;
- attack the submarine once it finally surfaces.

During the surface phase, remove active weapon systems and add hull damage
whenever you have a clean opportunity. After every visible VLS has been
neutralized, an **underwater missile launch tube** becomes another target.

Destroying that tube removes the remaining missile capability, but it does not
end the mission. The submarine turns toward the Endurance on a collision course,
and the final phase becomes a direct hull-damage race.

The [Mission 30 Song of Wings walkthrough](/ace-combat-8/mission-30-song-of-wings)
covers the full Rainband guidance sequence, repeated surface and dive cycles,
VLS cleanup, underwater launch tube, and final ramming phase when one of those
transitions is stopping the clear.
