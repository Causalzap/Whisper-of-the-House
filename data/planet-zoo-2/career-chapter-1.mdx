**Chapter 1: Wild At Heart** takes you through two locations: **Earning Your Stripes** at Great Rift Valley Reserve and **Spreading Your Wings** at East Africa. It starts with animal photography and ends with your first conservation release.

The chapter's main complication is that the work is split between a Wildlife Reserve and a zoo. After obtaining Bronze at Great Rift Valley, you leave for East Africa, prepare animals there, and return to the reserve for Silver.

You also begin with two different kinds of animal enclosures: an Aardvark Habitat and a covered Shoebill Aviary. Both need to be accessible to staff, but their construction requirements are different.

<section id="great-rift-valley-bronze" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Great Rift Valley: Find the Animals and Complete Bronze

The first location begins at the Great Rift Valley Wildlife Reserve. Follow the compass toward the Research Outpost, then enter the Research Vehicle.

The vehicle is your main way of covering ground between animal groups. The compass points toward the current destination, so there is no need to search the entire reserve on foot.

Your first stops are the Common Hippopotamuses, followed by African Savannah Elephants and Plains Zebras.

### Photograph the Hippos and Elephants

Drive toward the Hippos near the water and press **F** to enter Photo Mode.

The opening task accepts a Research Photo containing a Common Hippopotamus. You do not need an exceptionally high photo score to proceed through this introductory sequence.

After the Hippo objective is completed, the route shifts toward the Elephants.

There is water between the animal groups. The safer approach is to drive through the shallower sections instead of cutting through deep water.

Follow the compass as you cross and photograph an African Savannah Elephant once the animal is clearly in view.

Photo scoring considers several elements, including the number of animals in the frame, framing quality, distance, and animal behavior. For these opening tasks, getting the required species into the picture matters more than spending a long time trying to maximize the score.

### Why Won't the Zebra Photo Complete?

The Zebra objective is different because the animals are easily startled.

Drive toward their location, but stop the Research Vehicle before getting close. Approach the animals on foot and use a crouched position if necessary.

The task requires a Research Photo containing **two Plains Zebras**.

![The Great Rift Valley photography objective requiring two Plains Zebras in a research photo.](/images/planet-zoo-2/career-chapter-1-zebra-photo-objective.webp)

A photograph of one Zebra may look perfectly acceptable but still fail to satisfy the objective. Before taking the picture, check that two animals are visible within the same frame.

When the required photograph is accepted, **Earning Your Stripes earns Bronze** and East Africa becomes available.

You can now leave the reserve. There is no reason to continue searching for animals simply to finish the introductory stage.

## East Africa: What to Do When You First Arrive

Open the Planet Hub and travel to **East Africa**, where Alice Jones introduces zoo management.

This scenario begins with **$20,000** in starting funds and an existing zoo. You are not building an entire zoo from an empty map.

Before constructing anything, the opening tasks ask you to inspect facilities already present.

Locate the Warthog Habitat near the Giraffes, select an existing Exhibit, and choose a Lion to enter Animal Camera.

These interactions familiarize you with the different ways animals are housed and inspected. You do not need to buy a Warthog, Lion, or Exhibit animal to complete them.

The first substantial building project begins after these inspection tasks.

</section>

<section id="aardvark-habitat" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Build the Aardvark Habitat Without Blocking Staff Access

Alice directs you to a highlighted construction area where the new Aardvark Habitat belongs.

The most important part of this task is completing the enclosure and its service access before moving the animals in.

Start by placing the **Habitat Staff Gate**. Then extend the Habitat Barrier until it forms one continuous enclosed boundary.

Connect the staff entrance to the existing path network. A closed barrier is not enough if the staff cannot reach the gate.

### Add the viewing windows and basic facilities

Select the appropriate barrier sections and use the Window controls to provide guest viewing.

The scenario asks for **five windows**.

After that, provide the required basic facilities:

1. Place the **Food Bowl - Small**.
2. Add water to the depression already present in the habitat terrain.
3. Place a **Water Pump** and connect the water body to a valid Freshwater Filtration Network.
4. Open Animal Trading and locate the two Aardvarks waiting in the Trade Centre.
5. Move both Aardvarks into the completed habitat.

The natural depression saves you from having to sculpt an entirely new pond. Use the Add Water tool to fill it, and check that the animals can reach the water without an excessively steep bank.

The Water Pump maintains the water body. Placing it beside the pond does not remove the need for a valid filtration connection.

### Why are the Aardvarks not being delivered?

Animal transfers require a functioning Trade Centre connected to the zoo's path network.

An available Caretaker or Veterinarian transports an animal from the Trade Centre to its destination. Staff move the Aardvarks individually, so the two deliveries do not necessarily happen at the same moment.

If the objective is still waiting for delivery, check the Trade Centre's accessibility and the new habitat's staff gate before purchasing more animals.

The scenario already provides the required pair. Additional Aardvark purchases are unnecessary for this task.

## Fix Aardvark Shelter, Terrain, and Enrichment Welfare

Once the pair arrives, construction is not quite finished. Alice asks you to inspect their welfare and improve the parts of the habitat that are still unsuitable.

The first correction is shelter.

Place an **Animal Burrow** inside the Aardvark Habitat. This provides the covered space the animals need and improves Shelter Welfare.

Next, select one of the Aardvarks and open its **Terrain Welfare** information.

### Aardvark Terrain Welfare: More Grass, Less Soil

The initial terrain balance is not ideal.

In the completed opening sequence, the animals need **more Grass and less Soil**. Use the terrain-painting controls to adjust those categories, watching the animal's Terrain Welfare rather than relying on the enclosure's appearance.

The objective is to bring Terrain Welfare to **100%**.

![The Aardvark's Terrain Welfare panel showing the grass and soil requirements that must be adjusted.](/images/planet-zoo-2/career-chapter-1-aardvark-terrain-welfare.webp)

Different visual styles of grass can contribute to the Grass category. You do not need to cover the habitat in a single identical texture merely to satisfy the requirement.

The game evaluates the terrain balance, so stop painting when the welfare condition has been met.

### Increase Enrichment Welfare to 66%

After terrain comes enrichment.

The scenario requires **66% Enrichment Welfare** for the Aardvarks.

Food and toy enrichment are both available. The opening sequence demonstrates placing a **Melon Feeder** and a toy to improve the animals' environment.

These serve different purposes from the ordinary Food Bowl. Enrichment provides activities that stimulate the animals rather than merely giving them a place to receive food.

Additional enrichment choices become available through research, but the immediate objective can be handled with the facilities available during the tutorial.

Once Shelter, Terrain, and Enrichment Welfare have been improved, the scenario moves to the Aviary project.

</section>

## Build the Shoebill Aviary and Place the First Bird

The Shoebill construction area is already marked, but it is obstructed by construction materials.

Before working on the enclosure, use the **Demolish Brush** to clear the marked area.

The objective requires removing **42 construction objects**.

Use the Demolish Brush's filters to target the unwanted construction materials. The tool can also remove scenery, paths, and facilities, so selecting the correct categories prevents unnecessary damage to nearby structures.

### Install the Nest Before Finishing the Roof

Once the site is clear, the next task is to provide a suitable nesting location.

The Shoebill sequence uses a **Water Platform** nest.

Place the nest within the intended bird enclosure, then move on to the roof.

Unlike the Aardvarks, the Shoebill needs a covered space that prevents it from flying away.

### Raise the Roof With Supports

The roof is not just a flat decorative piece.

The bird needs enough usable flying space, and the tutorial introduces **Roof Supports** to raise the roof above the enclosure.

Place the supports first, then select the habitat and use the Roof tool to finish enclosing the flying area.

![The completed Shoebill aviary before adoption, with the objective requiring one Shoebill to be adopted and delivered.](/images/planet-zoo-2/career-chapter-1-shoebill-aviary-roof.webp)

The game explains that an adult Shoebill can reach a wingspan of approximately **2.5 metres**. Providing enough height and flying room is therefore more important than making the roof visually elaborate during this first task.

### Adopt the Shoebill and Complete the Staff Tasks

With the Aviary ready, open Animal Trading and adopt the required **one Shoebill**.

Send it to the new habitat and wait for staff to deliver it.

The animal will remain in the Trade Centre until a destination has been selected, so adopting it without assigning the Aviary does not finish the delivery objective.

After the Shoebill arrives, hire **one additional Keeper** and place **one Keeper Hut** near the new animal facilities.

The Keeper Hut is where Keepers prepare animal food. Positioning it conveniently near the habitats avoids creating an unnecessarily long service route.

You do not need to decorate the entire Aviary or expand the animal collection before continuing. The next objective concerns animals already present elsewhere in East Africa.

</section>

## Prepare the Masai Giraffes and Return to Great Rift Valley

With the Aardvark and Shoebill tasks finished, Alice turns your attention back to the Giraffe breeding programme.

These animals are intended for the Great Rift Valley Wildlife Reserve.

Find the existing Masai Giraffes at the East Africa zoo and prepare the required Release Group through animal Storage.

The important distinction is that **sending an animal to Storage is not the same as completing its Release Group**.

Once the group is ready and finalized, the animals become available for transfer to the reserve.

Animals committed to a Release Group cannot return to the zoo, so review the group before finalizing it. The complete preparation and release process is explained in the [Wildlife Reserves guide](/planet-zoo-2/wildlife-reserves).

After preparing the Masai Giraffes, return through the Planet Hub to **Great Rift Valley Reserve**.

This is the point where the first chapter changes from zoo construction back to wildlife restoration.

</section>

## Great Rift Valley Silver: Restore the Dense Forest and Release Giraffes

The Giraffes are ready, but the receiving area is not.

Back at Great Rift Valley, the next task identifies invasive plants within the **Dense Forest Ecosystem**.

Remove the marked invasive Bramble Bushes, then improve the affected Ecosystem until its status becomes **Established**.

The required restoration involves rocky cover and suitable tree categories. Do not attempt to release the group while the Ecosystem still displays Not Established.

The Wildlife Reserves guide covers the individual environmental requirements and why apparently complete ecosystems can still fail their validation.

Once the Dense Forest is Established, complete the Masai Giraffe release into the reserve.

The animals are now present in their restored environment rather than simply being removed from your zoo.

The scenario then introduces the reserve's Biodiversity Rating, which reflects both environmental restoration and research activity.

Completing this stage earns **Silver at Great Rift Valley**.

### Why is the next Career location still locked?

Career progression considers the medals you have obtained and the requirements of the next location. It is not always a simple matter of finishing one task and immediately receiving every subsequent unlock.

Check the Planet Hub's Career Accreditation and medal progress when the next destination is unavailable.

At this stage, the sensible goal is to finish the currently accessible Bronze and Silver work and continue into the newly available scenarios.

There is no need to obtain every Gold medal before learning the systems introduced in the following chapter.

</section>

## When Should You Return for Chapter 1 Gold?

The first chapter has more to complete after its introductory route.

Great Rift Valley eventually asks for further wildlife reintroduction and higher reserve development, while East Africa's later tasks place greater demands on animal diversity, research, breeding, and zoo performance.

Those are much broader goals than building the first Aardvark Habitat and Aviary.

After the initial release, continue through the available Career locations rather than remaining in East Africa and purchasing a large collection of animals immediately.

The later East Africa objectives benefit from a developed zoo, expanded animal access, and additional research progress. Its Gold requirements include substantial species and breeding work rather than another small tutorial construction.

Likewise, Great Rift Valley's later objectives involve more than releasing the first Giraffe group. You will eventually need to revisit the reserve with additional wildlife and improve its overall rating.

Returning after unlocking more of the Career network lets you work with the animals and facilities those later requirements expect.

The two locations do not need to be abandoned permanently after their initial medals. They remain part of your Career progression and can be revisited.

</section>

<section id="chapter-one-next" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Where to Go After Chapter 1

Once the early Great Rift Valley and East Africa objectives are complete, the next stage of Career introduces more complicated forms of animal care and zoo management.

**Nordic Fjords** begins the next chapter and introduces aquatic animals, Aquarium construction, water filtration, and temperature management.

If the Aquarium's water conditions become the obstacle, the [Aquarium guide](/planet-zoo-2/aquariums) explains the Staff Gate, Filtration Network, power, and water-temperature requirements.

The practical transition is to leave East Africa with its opening animal facilities functioning and the Giraffe group released at Great Rift Valley.

You can then move forward without confusing unfinished long-term Gold objectives with a failure to complete the introductory chapter.
