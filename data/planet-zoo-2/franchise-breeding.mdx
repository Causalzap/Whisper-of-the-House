The first successful breeding season can create more work than building the original Habitat. A pair of Toucans produces several offspring, the Black Wildebeest herd keeps growing, and suddenly the animals that were comfortable last season have less space and unsuitable social groups.

There are two separate issues to solve. **Contraception prevents further unwanted breeding**, while **population management deals with the animals already living in the Habitat**. Treating only one of these problems leaves the other untouched.

This matters particularly in Franchise Mode, where animals can be retained for future breeding, traded, or prepared for conservation work. Keeping every offspring is rarely necessary, and replacing a breeding adult without checking family relationships can restart the same inbreeding problem.

<section id="stop-inbreeding" className="scroll-mt-24">

## How to Prevent Inbreeding in Franchise Mode

Start with the animal management list rather than selecting random animals around the enclosure.

Open **Zoo Management** and locate the animals in the affected Habitat. Review which are adults, which are juveniles, and which can currently breed. Use the **Contraception** control for animals you do not want reproducing.

You do not need to stop the entire zoo from breeding. The useful approach is to decide which animals should form the next breeding group and prevent close relatives or surplus animals from producing additional offspring.

A growing Black Wildebeest herd provides a good example. After several births, young animals were approaching maturity while other females were already pregnant. Contraception was applied to the offspring, but one unwanted pregnancy had already occurred.

![The Animals panel showing age, sex, and Contraception controls for Red Deer and other animals in a Franchise zoo.](/images/planet-zoo-2/franchise-breeding-contraception.webp)

Once the animals have been reviewed, check their family relationships before allowing any additional breeding.

### Contraception Does Not Undo an Existing Pregnancy

An animal that is already pregnant still needs to be managed through the birth.

Enabling contraception afterward is not a way to cancel that pregnancy. It is a measure to prevent subsequent breeding.

This explains why you can apply contraception across a group and still see another offspring arrive. The breeding may have occurred before you changed the settings.

Check the animal's reproductive status first. If the pregnancy is already underway, prepare for the additional animal and reassess the available space before it becomes a welfare problem.

### Do All Animals Need Contraception?

No. A successful breeding program needs at least one suitable breeding combination, while the other animals can remain in the social group without necessarily reproducing.

For each species, consider three questions:

- Which adults are intended to produce the next generation?
- Are their potential partners closely related?
- How many additional animals can the Habitat support?

The answers depend on the species.

A compatible pair of Toucans does not have the same preferred social structure as a herd of Wildebeests or a pack of Grey Wolves. Open the Zoopedia and inspect the species' group requirements instead of applying one breeding arrangement to every Habitat.

## Too Many Toucans: What to Do With the Offspring

An early Franchise Aviary began with a breeding pair and quickly filled with young Toucans.

The first clutch produced **3 offspring**. A later clutch produced **1 more**. With the original adults still present, the player was soon dealing with a crowded group and more potential breeding animals than the Aviary needed.

The Zoopedia lists a **preferred group range of 1–2 Toco Toucans**, with a maximum of **one male and one female** in the breeding group. Their mating system is **Monogamous**. That makes the original pair a reasonable long-term arrangement, but keeping every offspring in the same Aviary creates a population problem as the young birds mature.

![Toco Toucan social requirements showing a preferred group of 1–2, one animal per sex, and a monogamous mating system.](/images/planet-zoo-2/franchise-breeding-toucan-offspring.webp)


Two young male Toucans earned **Bronze ratings**, while the original breeding adults had none. One of those males could improve the next breeding generation, but he would need an unrelated female rather than breeding with his mother.

### Choose the Next Breeding Pair

Review the juveniles' sex, ratings, and individual attributes before selecting a replacement. Keep the most suitable candidate and use Contraception to prevent unwanted breeding while the group is being reorganized.

Search Animal Trading for an unrelated partner of the opposite sex. Suitable listings may take time to appear, so there is no need to purchase an incompatible animal simply to resume breeding immediately.

Once the new pair is ready, check their social requirements and remove Contraception from the animals intended to reproduce.

## Why Animal Welfare Falls After Breeding

A Habitat that once met every requirement can begin failing welfare checks after its population increases.

One Black Wildebeest herd had fallen to just **36% Overall Welfare**. Its Terrain and Toy Enrichment were already at **100%**, and Food-Based Enrichment was at **82%**. Yet the animals still had only **42% Space** and **35% Social Group** welfare.

![Black Wildebeest welfare at 36%, with Space at 42% and Social Group at 35% despite fully satisfied Terrain and Toy Enrichment.](/images/planet-zoo-2/franchise-breeding-wildebeest-space.webp)

More toys would not address the two weakest categories. The herd needed changes to its available space and social composition, including reviewing which offspring to keep as they matured.

Contraception helps stop the herd growing further, but it does not reduce the number of animals already present.

The immediate task is to inspect the current welfare categories and decide what needs changing.

### Check Space and Social Group Size Separately

The total number of animals does not tell you everything.

An enclosure can be physically large enough but still have an unsuitable social composition. Another Habitat may have an acceptable group structure while lacking the area needed for the current population.

Open the Habitat and the affected species' Zoopedia entry. Compare the current group against the preferred requirements.

Look for:

- **Usable space:** has the growing population exceeded the available area?
- **Social composition:** are there too many adults of one sex or too many breeding animals?
- **Shelter and environmental needs:** are all animals still able to use the existing facilities?
- **Food and enrichment:** can the current group access enough resources?

The game evaluates the animals' actual needs, not whether the enclosure looks spacious from above.


### Should You Expand the Habitat or Remove Animals?

Expanding is sensible when the species will remain an important part of the zoo and the additional population is intentional.

Removing surplus animals is often more practical when the recent births were unexpected.

For example, a breeding herd may have produced several offspring you do not plan to retain. Expanding the enclosure permanently for those temporary residents can leave you with unnecessary construction and maintenance costs.

First identify which animals you actually want in the long-term group.

If the current adults and selected offspring fit the existing space once the surplus is removed, expanding may not be necessary.

## Managing Grey Wolf and Red Deer Offspring

Breeding problems can appear in several Habitats during the same season. One Franchise zoo had young Grey Wolves, a growing Red Deer herd, and pregnant adult deer at the same time.

The immediate concern was keeping the next generation from breeding with close relatives. The player reviewed the juveniles in Zoo Management and applied Contraception before they could create more unwanted pregnancies.

For Red Deer, check which male is breeding with the herd and which females are his offspring. In a Grey Wolf family, review the parents and maturing pups before deciding which animals should remain available for breeding.

An unrelated partner may take time to find. During the same Franchise run, the player repeatedly checked Animal Trading for a suitable male Grey Wolf while listings changed. Continue managing the existing group's welfare while waiting for the right animal, rather than choosing a breeding partner solely because it is available.

## What Should You Do With Surplus Animals?

After selecting the animals you want to keep, decide where the others should go. Their available options depend on age, health, and eligibility.

- **Keep a replacement breeder:** Retain an animal with desirable attributes while searching for an unrelated partner. Check its Contraception setting before allowing the new pair to breed.
- **Use Animal Storage or another zoo:** Move eligible animals when they no longer fit the current Habitat. Check Storage capacity and whether each animal can be transferred.
- **Trade eligible animals:** Selling a healthy surplus animal may be more useful than maintaining additional enclosure space for it. Market availability and demand can change.
- **Prepare a conservation release:** Animals suitable for a Wildlife Reserve must meet the required Release Group conditions. For group preparation and ecosystem requirements, see the [Wildlife Reserves guide](/planet-zoo-2/wildlife-reserves).

A juvenile that cannot yet be moved still needs enough space and appropriate care. Use Contraception to prevent further breeding while managing the animals already present.

</section>
