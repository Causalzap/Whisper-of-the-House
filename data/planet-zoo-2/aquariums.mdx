An Aquarium in **Planet Zoo 2** can look finished long before it is ready for fish or sea turtles. Water filling depends on the enclosure and its staff entrance, while water quality depends on a separate filtration system. The order of construction also matters: installing the Aquarium Staff Gate too early can leave you repeatedly removing water whenever you need to change a barrier or add an underwater tunnel.

The **Nordic Fjords** career scenario introduces the essentials with a partly built Aquarium. You install its missing staff entrance, restore filtration and power, set the water conditions, and bring in Golden Trevally and Hawksbill Sea Turtles. Building one from an empty plot gives you more freedom, but it also makes the barrier layout, water depth, and staff access your responsibility.

## How to Build an Aquarium in Planet Zoo 2

For a new Aquarium, I would finish the tank's shape and any difficult construction before installing its Staff Gate. The entrance can trigger automatic water filling, which is convenient when you are ready for the water but frustrating while you are still moving barriers and digging out terrain.

Use this construction order:

1. Open **Habitat Barriers** and select the Aquarium construction options. Draw the enclosure with Aquarium Barriers, leaving space for staff access.
2. Adjust the barrier shape and height. If you want a shallow viewing area, a deeper centre, or an underwater viewing tunnel, prepare the required terrain now.
3. Add any **Aquarium Guest Gates** and connect the underwater tunnels before the enclosure is filled.
4. Finish terrain adjustments and structural scenery that would be difficult to change underwater.
5. Install the **Aquarium Staff Gate** in the barrier and connect it to the staff path network.
6. Check that the Aquarium fills with water and that its actual water level leaves the Staff Gate usable.

The fifth step is the useful shortcut. You can work on the empty enclosure without repeatedly triggering the water fill. Once the barrier layout, guest entrances, and terrain are ready, installing the staff entrance allows the Aquarium to fill.

An **Aquarium Staff Gate** is different from an ordinary Habitat Staff Gate. Aquatic keepers use the Aquarium entrance to reach animals inside the water. Using the wrong entrance can leave an otherwise completed Aquarium empty.

![An Aquarium Staff Gate installed in the enclosure barrier, allowing the aquarium to fill with water.](/images/planet-zoo-2/aquarium-staff-gate-water-fill.webp)

### Why is my Aquarium not filling with water?

If the barrier is complete but the tank remains empty, check the entrance before buying additional water facilities. A Water Filter maintains water conditions; it does not replace the Aquarium Staff Gate.

Make sure you have used the Aquarium-specific entrance and that the enclosure is properly closed. If you deliberately left the Staff Gate out while building, install it now. If the Aquarium has already been filled and drained during construction, select the Aquarium and use its water control to refill it when the layout is complete.

Water filling and staff accessibility are separate checks. A tank may contain water while its keepers still cannot reach the entrance, so confirm the path connection before bringing animals in.

## How to Add Underwater Tunnels or Edit a Filled Aquarium

Underwater tunnels are easiest to build while the Aquarium is empty. They use **Aquarium Guest Gates**, not the entrance used by keepers, and the path between the gates must be created separately.

Open **Habitat Barriers**, select the Aquarium options, and place a Guest Gate where the tunnel should enter the tank. Add another at the intended exit, then choose **Path Tunnels** and draw the connection through the Aquarium.

![The Path Tunnels tool connecting underwater viewing entrances across an empty Aquarium before filling it with water.](/images/planet-zoo-2/aquarium-underwater-tunnel-guest-gates.webp)

For a new build, complete this connection before installing the Aquarium Staff Gate. If the tank fills during tunnel construction, remove the water before continuing to edit the barriers.

You can also change an existing Aquarium without rebuilding it. If animals are already inside, box them and move them into storage before draining the water or making major structural changes. Once the enclosure is empty, remove the water and adjust the barriers, terrain, or tunnel connections.

- **To edit the barrier or lower its height:** Select the water and press Delete to drain it, then open the barrier editor.
- **To add another underwater passage:** Drain the Aquarium, install the Guest Gates, and connect them with Path Tunnels.
- **To stop automatic refilling during a larger redesign:** Remove the Aquarium Staff Gate while you finish the structural changes, then reinstall it.
- **If the Aquarium stays empty afterward:** Select the Aquarium and use its water control to add the water again.

After refilling the Aquarium, check the tunnel entrances, Staff Gate, filtration connection, power, salinity, and temperature. A change to the barrier or surrounding terrain can leave an entrance or service connection unusable. Return the animals only after the Aquarium is accessible and its water conditions meet their requirements.

The same principle applies to large indoor Aquariums. Establish the service area, viewing tunnels, and tank boundaries before enclosing them in decorative walls and ceilings. It is much easier to move an exposed barrier than one already surrounded by a finished building.

## Aquarium Gate Above Water Level: What Should You Check?

An Aquarium Staff Gate does not need to sit on the terrain at the very bottom of the tank. Staff use an aquatic entrance, so placing the gate above ground level can be perfectly reasonable. The important difference is whether the **water actually reaches the gate**.

A shallow Aquarium can reveal the problem. The viewing area may have enough water to look like a pond, while the staff entrance is too high relative to the waterline for keepers to use it.

If the gate reports a water-level problem or staff cannot enter, inspect the following:

1. **Actual water height:** Compare the visible waterline with the Staff Gate, not just with the surrounding terrain.
2. **Gate position:** If the water is below the entrance, adjust the gate's position or provide a deeper area around it.
3. **Barrier height and terrain:** Changing the barrier can alter the usable water level. Test the filled result before covering the structure with scenery.
4. **Staff access:** If the gate is sufficiently submerged but keepers still cannot enter, inspect the path connection and any accessibility warnings separately.

Aquarium water does not necessarily rise smoothly with every small barrier adjustment. It can settle at particular height levels, leaving a visible gap between the water surface and the top of the enclosure. A tiny increase in barrier height may produce no visible change in water level.

For a shallow pond, it can be easier to retain a deeper section around the Staff Gate and keep the public-facing portion shallow. This preserves a usable aquatic entrance without forcing the whole viewing area to be a deep tank.

Do not confuse this with the automatic filling problem. Installing the Staff Gate last helps avoid repeated draining during construction; it does not by itself correct a gate positioned above the waterline. If the tank is full but the same warning persists, examine its depth and gate placement before rebuilding the entire enclosure.

## How to Connect Water Filters to an Aquarium

After the Aquarium contains water, it still needs a working **Filtration Network**. Placing a Water Filter beside the enclosure is not enough. The Aquarium must belong to the intended network, and the filter needs electricity before it can maintain the water.

Open **Facilities**, place a Water Filter, and select **Edit Filtration Network**. Connect the filter's pipe node to the connection node on the **Aquarium Staff Gate**, not to an ordinary barrier panel. The Staff Gate serves as the Aquarium's filtration connection point.

Once connected, compare the network's **Filtration Rate** with its current **Demand**. The filtration rate must meet or exceed demand to maintain Water Cleanliness. Demand rises as you increase the aquarium's water volume or add more animals, so a filter that worked for the original tank may become insufficient after an expansion.

If the Aquarium reports **Insufficient Water Filtration**, check the pipe connection, filter power, and network capacity before placing another filter. Add connected filtration capacity when the existing network cannot meet demand.

In Nordic Fjords, an existing filtration system is already at capacity. The additional Aquarium needs another filter, followed by a working power connection, before the scenario's water-quality requirements can be completed.

![The Aquarium filtration network showing the Water Filter connection and water salinity controls.](/images/planet-zoo-2/aquarium-filtration-network.webp)

### Freshwater, brackish water, and saltwater

A Filtration Network can use one of three salinity settings:

- **Freshwater**
- **Brackish water**
- **Saltwater**

The selected water type must suit the animals living in the connected Aquariums. A filled, filtered tank is not automatically suitable for marine animals if its network is still configured for Freshwater.

Nordic Fjords requires **Saltwater** for its Golden Trevally and Hawksbill Sea Turtle setup. Changing the filter's salinity is a separate action from filling the enclosure.

For a zoo containing both marine and freshwater Aquariums, plan separate filtration networks where different salinity settings are needed. For example, a marine tank and a freshwater fish display should not depend on one shared network set to the wrong water type for either enclosure.

Positioning the filters in a backstage service area keeps them accessible without forcing you to squeeze equipment between guest viewing paths. Before adding another tank to an existing network, check its salinity, connections, and available filtration capacity.

## Why Is My Aquarium Water Filter Not Working?

A correctly assigned Filtration Network can still fail because its Water Filter is not powered. In Nordic Fjords, this is an important distinction: changing the network to Saltwater will not complete the filtration requirement while the equipment remains without electricity.

When the Aquarium reports a filtration problem, inspect the equipment in this order:

1. Confirm that the Aquarium is connected to the intended Filtration Network.
2. Check whether the network has enough filtration capacity for the enclosures assigned to it.
3. Select the Water Filter and make sure a working Power Generator covers the facility.
4. Check the network's salinity against the animals' requirements.

If the filter has power but the Aquarium is connected to another network, adding more electricity will not correct the assignment. Likewise, a properly connected filter cannot operate without power.

### Standard power or renewable power?

Conventional generators can be a practical way to get an early Aquarium operational. Wind and solar facilities provide Sustainability benefits and avoid the running costs associated with conventional generators, although their initial construction cost is higher.

Choose a power source your zoo can afford, then confirm the filter is actually powered before spending more money on additional facilities.

### How to set Aquarium water temperature

Temperature is controlled separately from filtration salinity. The **Aquarium Staff Gate** needs an active power supply for the Aquarium's temperature controls to work. Powering the Water Filter alone does not guarantee that the gate is powered.

In Nordic Fjords, the required water temperature is **28°C**. Select the Aquarium, open **Environment**, and adjust the temperature until it reaches that value. If the temperature controls are unavailable, check whether the Staff Gate is within the coverage of a working power source.

![The Aquarium Environment controls showing the 28°C water temperature required in Nordic Fjords.](/images/planet-zoo-2/aquarium-water-temperature.webp)

That setting belongs to the Nordic Fjords task; it is not a universal temperature for all aquatic animals. Check the intended species' requirements before copying the same value into another Aquarium.

An Aquarium is ready for stocking only when its water conditions suit the animals, its filtration is operating, and staff can reach the enclosure.

## How to Add Aquatic Animals and Check Compatibility

Once the water conditions are stable, use the animal management controls to send aquatic animals to the Aquarium. Check the enclosure type first: **Moon Jellies** are kept in Exhibits, while fully aquatic Habitat species such as **Golden Trevally** and **Hawksbill Sea Turtles** use Aquariums.

Fish may be handled in **shoals**, rather than as individually managed animals. Make sure the group you are transferring matches what you intend to add, and wait for staff to finish the delivery before checking the final animal or shoal count.

### Can different aquatic species share one Aquarium?

Sharing a water type does not automatically make two species compatible. Check their Zoopedia requirements before purchasing the second species, particularly when mixing large aquatic animals with fish or animals that need different swimming conditions.

For a mixed-species Aquarium, compare:

- **Salinity and temperature:** Both species must be able to live in the same water conditions.
- **Compatibility and social requirements:** Check whether the selected species can share the enclosure and whether their groups meet their own social needs.
- **Usable swimming space:** Large animals and different-sized fish need enough accessible water volume, not just a visually impressive tank.
- **Environment and welfare:** Inspect the requirements shown for each resident after the animals arrive.

A large tank is not automatically a suitable mixed-species habitat. If the proposed combination cannot meet every animal's needs, use separate Aquariums rather than trying to solve an incompatibility by adding more decorative space.

Golden Trevally and Hawksbill Sea Turtles share the prepared Saltwater Aquarium in Nordic Fjords. The [Chapter 2 walkthrough](/planet-zoo-2/career-chapter-2) follows the scenario's deliveries, medal requirements, and veterinary objective.

Later, **Maluku Islands** introduces more demanding aquatic welfare and shoal targets, including Ocean Sunfish. The [Chapter 4 walkthrough](/planet-zoo-2/career-chapter-4) covers those objectives alongside the Maluku research and progression requirements.

## Can You Build a Shallow Aquarium or Koi Pond?

You can make a shallow water display by adjusting Aquarium Barriers and sculpting the terrain inside them, but a functioning Aquarium still has to satisfy its staff access and animal movement requirements.

One workable layout uses a shallow public-facing pond connected to a deeper section around the Staff Gate. The deeper portion can sit behind rocks, walls, or other scenery, while the viewing area remains low enough for guests to see fish near the surface.

![An Aquarium Staff Gate positioned in a recessed section beside the surrounding terrain.](/images/planet-zoo-2/aquarium-shallow-pond-deep-staff-gate.webp)

Keep the Staff Gate beside the deeper section, where the water can reach its entrance. If staff cannot use it, check the [waterline and gate position](#gate-water-level) before changing the pond's overall shape.

Swimming space is the other constraint. In shallow Koi Pond builds, rocks, low decorative structures, and narrow passages can prevent fish from reaching the areas intended for viewing.

If the animals are not using the viewing area, examine their available swimming space before adding more fish. Try opening the passage, removing obstructive scenery, or deepening part of the pond. Some cramped layouts can cause animals to be boxed and returned to another part of the Aquarium rather than navigating normally.

Different aquatic species may also react differently to the same shallow layout. Do not assume that an arrangement suitable for small fish will also work for large rays, sharks, or sea turtles.

The safest time to test this is before the surrounding building is finished. Get the water, gate access, and animal movement working first, then conceal the deeper service area with scenery.

## Why Are My Aquarium Animals Unhappy or Sick?

When an aquatic animal's welfare drops, open its welfare and health information before changing the Aquarium. Poor water conditions, limited swimming space, incompatible species, and illness can all lower welfare, but they require different fixes.

Start with **Environment**. Compare the Aquarium's actual salinity and temperature with the species' requirements, then check whether the Filtration Network is connected and powered. Also inspect the usable swimming space. A tank may appear large enough while rocks, shallow terrain, or narrow passages prevent animals from reaching much of it.

If **Social** or species compatibility is the problem, changing the water will not help. Check group requirements and whether the animals can share the enclosure. Separate species when their needs cannot be met together.

### Why Is My Aquarium Still Dirty When the Filter Is Working?

**Water Cleanliness** and **Tank Cleanliness** are separate conditions. A powered Water Filter can maintain clean water without keeping the Aquarium itself clean.

- **Water Cleanliness:** Maintained by the Filtration Network. If it falls, check the pipe connection, power supply, and whether the network's **Filtration Rate** meets its current **Demand**. Poor water quality can also cause Tank Cleanliness to deteriorate faster.
- **Tank Cleanliness:** Maintained by Keepers entering and cleaning the Aquarium. If Water Cleanliness is good but the tank remains dirty, check whether Keepers can reach the Aquarium Staff Gate and have enough time to complete their work. Another Water Filter will not replace cleaning.
- **Barrier Condition:** Maintained by Mechanics. Damaged Aquarium Barriers can eventually develop cracks and leaks. Check the barrier's condition and make sure a Mechanic can reach it when repairs are needed.

These conditions are worth checking separately. Adding filtration capacity will not fix a dirty tank that Keepers cannot access, while assigning more Keepers will not solve a Filtration Network operating below its required capacity.

### What If an Aquatic Animal Is Sick?

Illness and injury require veterinary care, not just changes to the water settings. In Nordic Fjords, a **Manatee** needs treatment even after the Aquarium has been prepared for aquatic animals.

Request veterinary care for a sick or injured animal, and check its environment for conditions that may be contributing to poor health. Treating the animal does not correct contaminated water or unsuitable habitat conditions.

Once the animal has recovered or the underlying problem has been corrected, inspect its welfare again. Make sure the Aquarium has suitable water conditions, accessible staff facilities, enough swimming space, and the correct social environment before moving on.
