<GuideQuickAnswer title="How to Release Animals into a Wildlife Reserve">
Send the selected animals to **Storage**, prepare their **Release Group**, and mark it **Ready for Transport**. At the Wildlife Reserve, confirm that every required Ecosystem is **Established**, then complete the release.

In **Planet Zoo 2**, moving animals from a zoo to a Wildlife Reserve is only part of the conservation process. The animals also need a suitable ecosystem, and the reserve needs research facilities to monitor its wildlife.

The Great Rift Valley introduces this system with a Masai Giraffe release. Scottish Highlands makes it more demanding by requiring a suitable home for Eurasian Lynx, while Sumatra adds environmental restoration and water-dependent ecosystems.

These locations expose three different problems: a Release Group that has not been prepared correctly, an Ecosystem that cannot support its animals, and a Reserve whose Biodiversity Rating still needs improvement after the first release.

<section id="release-groups" className="scroll-mt-24">

## How to Create a Release Group and Send Animals to the Wild

The first release begins in **East Africa**, where the zoo has giraffes available for the Great Rift Valley Wildlife Reserve.

You cannot complete the transfer simply by selecting a giraffe in its habitat and expecting it to appear in the reserve.

Open the animal's information panel and send the selected animal to **Storage**. From there, open the Release Groups controls and assign the animal to the appropriate group.

For the Great Rift Valley task, the prepared group is a male Masai Giraffe group. The group interface also displays information related to its members, including genetic diversity, immunity, and longevity.

The important sequence is:

1. Select the giraffes in the East Africa zoo and send the required animals to Storage.
2. Open **Release Groups** and assign the stored animals to the prepared group.
3. Check that the group meets its requirements.
4. Mark it **Ready for Transport**.
5. Return to Great Rift Valley to complete the release into the reserve.

![Four Masai Giraffes in animal Storage, with the Release Groups tab available at the top.](/images/planet-zoo-2/wildlife-reserve-giraffe-storage.webp)

### Can you cancel a Release Group?

Check the members carefully before committing them.

Finalizing a Release Group is an irreversible decision. Once the group is finalized for release, its animals cannot be transferred back into your zoo.

Review the selected animals before confirming the group, particularly when they have valuable breeding attributes or are still needed for another zoo objective. Keep those animals in your zoo until you are ready to commit them to conservation.

### Why can't I find the animals I need to release?

First check which part of the animal management system the task is using.

Animals that are still housed in zoo habitats must be sent to Storage before being assigned to their Release Group. Animals supplied specifically for a conservation project may already be available through the **Gifts** section instead.

Scottish Highlands provides an example of the second situation. The required Eurasian Lynx are gifted for the reserve, so repeatedly searching the current zoo for animals to purchase will not solve the problem.

Open the relevant animal storage and Gifts controls, locate the supplied group, and use the Release Groups interface to prepare it for the reserve.

Do not purchase replacement animals until you have checked whether the task has already supplied the required group.

## How to Establish an Ecosystem in a Wildlife Reserve

Open **Ecosystems** at the Wildlife Reserve and select the area you want to restore or create.

Each Ecosystem has its own combination of area, terrain, vegetation, and other environmental requirements. The selected region must satisfy those requirements before its status changes from **Invalid** to **Established**.

This is not determined by how many trees you can see on the screen. The Ecosystem checks specific kinds of environmental coverage.

### Great Rift Valley: Establish the Dense Forest

When the giraffes are ready for release, Great Rift Valley still has an environmental problem: invasive flora occupies part of the area needed for the animals.

Remove the invasive plants before rebuilding that portion of the reserve as a Dense Forest.

The restoration requires several distinct environmental elements, including:

- **Dense Crown Trees**
- **Spreading Crown Trees**
- **Medium Rocky Cover**

Use the Nature Brush to add suitable vegetation. The brush can work with individual plants or combinations of plants, and you can configure it for different types of coverage.

Rocks need attention as well. Planting trees alone does not complete the Dense Forest if its rocky-cover requirement remains below the necessary level.

![Dense Forest is not established because Medium Rocky Cover, Spreading Crown Trees, and Dense Crown Trees are below their requirements.](/images/planet-zoo-2/wildlife-reserve-dense-forest-requirements.webp)

Work through the remaining requirements shown in the Ecosystem panel until the status becomes Established.

At that point, the giraffe release can proceed.

### Scottish Highlands: Why is Montane Forest still Invalid?

The Montane Forest in Scottish Highlands demonstrates a more complicated version of the same system.

The reserve needs a suitable area for Eurasian Lynx. When you first designate the Montane Forest, the game can report that the region is **Too Small**.

Make the Ecosystem boundary large enough before spending time adding vegetation. The lynx require room to roam and hunt, and environmental coverage alone cannot compensate for an undersized area.

After the boundary is accepted, additional requirements become visible.

The Montane Forest construction sequence involves adjustments to:

- Short Grass
- Moss
- Forest Floor
- Understory Plants
- Cold-hardy Trees

The requirements do not necessarily become satisfied together. You can finish placing one kind of vegetation and still have the Ecosystem marked Invalid because another category is short.

The practical approach is to work from the current requirements panel rather than continuously painting the whole area with the same brush.

Start by checking the boundary size. Then add only the categories that are still insufficient, checking the Ecosystem status as you go.

## Why Won't My Animals Release into the Wildlife Reserve?

An Ecosystem that was Established earlier may no longer meet its requirements when you attempt the release.

This happens during the Scottish Highlands lynx project.

The Montane Forest reaches Established, but when the Release Group is examined again, the Ecosystem has fallen below its requirements because it no longer has enough **Short Grass**.

The missing amount is small, but the release still depends on the Ecosystem satisfying the required conditions.

Do not rebuild the whole forest immediately.

Open the Ecosystem again, identify the category that has dropped below its requirement, and restore that coverage. Once the status is valid again, return to the Release Group.

### Check every required Ecosystem

The Eurasian Lynx release also shows why establishing one forest does not automatically satisfy the complete animal requirement.

The Release Group identifies **Mixed Lowland Forest** and **Montane Forest** as required ecosystems.

A completed Montane Forest is therefore only part of the environmental preparation. Check the full list of required habitats instead of assuming the most recently created forest is enough.

This is particularly important for animals introduced into reserves containing several different ecosystems.

The game evaluates the habitat requirements of the Release Group, not just whether the reserve contains an established patch of vegetation somewhere on the map.

A similar problem occurs in **Poland Reserve**, where releasing Eurasian Bitterns can be blocked by missing Wetland Engineers and Terrestrial Foragers. The [Chapter 4 walkthrough](/planet-zoo-2/career-chapter-4) explains the Fen Wetland restoration and how the Poland and Swiss Alps assignments connect.

### What happens after a successful release?

Once the requirements are satisfied, the animals can enter the reserve.

In Scottish Highlands, the Eurasian Lynx group is successfully released and the conservation project advances. The animals can be observed in the reserve rather than simply disappearing from the zoo management system.

The lynx are introduced to help control an excessive deer population.

That ecological role matters because simply increasing the number of animals in the reserve is not always the right objective. The conservation project can require a balance between predators and prey.

After introducing a predator, check which species are still needed for the surrounding ecosystems before committing more release groups.

## How to Build a Research Network in Wildlife Reserves

Wildlife Reserves are not managed through the same staff and habitat facilities used in zoos.

Instead, research installations help monitor animal populations and improve the reserve's research capabilities.

In Scottish Highlands, the task begins with a deer overpopulation problem. Before releasing the lynx, you establish the research infrastructure needed to study the reserve.

First build the **Research Outpost**, then place **three Observation Platforms**.

The platforms need to form a useful monitoring network across the area.

### Why shouldn't Observation Platform coverage overlap?

The Scottish Highlands instructions specifically ask you to position the Observation Platforms so their coverage does not overlap.

The reason is to reduce the impact of monitoring facilities on wildlife while still creating the required research coverage.

Spread the three platforms around the reserve rather than clustering them in one place.

![Research Network requirements showing zero of three Observation Platforms, Camera Traps at 0%, and Acoustic Monitors locked at Accreditation Tier 3.](/images/planet-zoo-2/wildlife-reserve-research-network-requirements.webp)

Once the Observation Platforms are placed, additional monitoring options become relevant.

**Camera Traps** provide another method of monitoring wildlife. During the Scottish Highlands sequence, three camera traps are placed while the research coverage is being expanded.

**Acoustic Monitors** are another research network facility, but the player encounters a progression restriction when trying to use them.

At that point in Career Mode, the relevant research network option is locked behind **Accreditation Tier 3**.

That restriction should not be confused with a failed Ecosystem. A Reserve can have established habitats and released wildlife even when a particular research facility is not yet unlocked.

Continue with the monitoring methods available at your current Career tier rather than trying to force a locked facility into the reserve.

## How to Increase Wildlife Reserve Biodiversity Rating

The **Biodiversity Rating** measures more than the number of animals you have released.

Open the reserve's Biodiversity Rating panel to see two separate components:

**Ecosystem Rating** and **Research Rating**.

They measure different parts of your restoration work.

### Ecosystem Rating

This reflects how successfully the reserve's environment has been restored and how well it supports wildlife.

Ways to improve it include:

- Creating and establishing the required ecosystems.
- Improving ecosystems beyond their initial established state.
- Removing invasive flora and environmental pollution.
- Increasing species diversity by introducing suitable animals.

The difference between merely establishing an ecosystem and developing it further is important. Reaching Established is enough for certain release requirements, but it is not necessarily the end of the restoration process.

### Research Rating

This measures the reserve's ability to monitor and study its wildlife.

Expand research coverage with the facilities available to you, including Observation Platforms, Camera Traps, and later research networks.

A reserve can have suitable habitats and successfully released animals while still having an incomplete research setup.

![The Great Rift Valley Biodiversity Rating panel showing Ecosystem Rating and Research Rating.](/images/planet-zoo-2/wildlife-reserve-biodiversity-rating.webp)

### What should you improve first?

Look at which of the two ratings is holding the reserve back.

When Ecosystem Rating is low, concentrate on invalid ecosystems, environmental cleanup, and suitable species diversity.

When Research Rating is behind, investigate the monitoring network rather than releasing more animals without addressing research coverage.

This distinction prevents an expensive cycle of preparing additional release groups when the real bottleneck is missing research infrastructure.

Reaching a high overall rating requires progress in both categories, not just completing the first successful animal release.

## Why Is the Sumatra River Ecosystem Still Invalid?

The River restoration in Sumatra can remain Invalid even when the designated area appears large enough. Its requirements include both the Ecosystem's area and the amount of water contained within it.

During the restoration, expanding the Ecosystem boundary alone did not satisfy the water requirement. The terrain had to be lowered to create more room for water before the remaining environmental conditions could be completed.

### Fix the Water and Terrain Requirements

Open the River Ecosystem panel and check which condition is still below its required level.

1. **Check the designated area.** Make sure the River Ecosystem meets its minimum area requirement.
2. **Increase the water area or volume.** Lower the terrain where necessary and add water until the Ecosystem's water condition is satisfied.
3. **Complete the remaining terrain coverage.** Check Soil, Scree, and Freshwater Bed Plants against the requirements displayed in the Ecosystem panel.
4. **Confirm Established.** Recheck the Ecosystem after modifying the terrain or water, especially if the changes affect a neighbouring Ecosystem.

Adding more vegetation will not correct an insufficient water requirement. Likewise, widening the designated boundary is ineffective when the missing condition is the amount of water inside it.

Be careful when modifying an established neighbouring Ecosystem. Terrain changes or overlapping boundaries can cause an area that was previously valid to fall below its requirements.

Once the River and Peatland environments are ready, the Sumatra conservation work can continue with the rescued otters. The complete sequence of animal releases and the three-otter Research Photo objective is covered in the [Chapter 3 walkthrough](/planet-zoo-2/career-chapter-3).
