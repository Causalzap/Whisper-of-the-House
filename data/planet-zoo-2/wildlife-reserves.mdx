In **Planet Zoo 2**, a Wildlife Reserve is more than a destination for animals leaving your zoo. Each reserve has eligible species, environmental requirements, and research facilities that affect its conservation progress. You can prepare a perfectly valid Release Group and still be unable to release it because the destination lacks a suitable Ecosystem, a required prey species, or a nesting structure.

Career Mode introduces these problems across several locations. Great Rift Valley begins with Masai Giraffes and a damaged Dense Forest, Scottish Highlands adds predator-prey relationships and Montane Forest restoration, and Sumatra requires substantial terrain and water adjustments. Franchise Mode uses the same conservation systems, but reaching higher reserve ratings calls for a more deliberate choice of species and research coverage.

## How to Create a Release Group and Send Animals to a Wildlife Reserve

A Release Group must be prepared before its animals can enter the selected Wildlife Reserve. In the Great Rift Valley Career assignment, the process begins at the East Africa zoo, where Masai Giraffes are available for the conservation project.

Select the animals you intend to release and send them to **Storage**. Open **Release Groups**, assign the appropriate animals, and inspect the group's requirements before marking it ready.

For the Great Rift Valley task, the required group consists of male Masai Giraffes. The group interface also shows information about the selected animals, including genetic diversity, immunity, and longevity.

The transfer follows this sequence:

1. Select the required giraffes at East Africa and send them to Storage.
2. Open **Release Groups** and assign the animals to the intended group.
3. Review the group requirements and selected members.
4. Mark the group **Ready for Transport**.
5. Travel to Great Rift Valley, establish the required environment, and complete the release.

![Four Masai Giraffes in animal Storage, with the Release Groups tab available at the top.](/images/planet-zoo-2/wildlife-reserve-giraffe-storage.webp)

### Why can't I find the animals for my Release Group?

An animal must be available through the appropriate management controls before you can assign it. Check Storage first, including whether the required animals are still living in one of your zoo habitats.

Some Career assignments supply animals through **Gifts** rather than asking you to breed or purchase them. Scottish Highlands provides Eurasian Lynx for its conservation project, so the missing animals may already be waiting in the supplied inventory.

In Franchise Mode, inspect the group eligibility information if an animal you expected to use does not appear. Animals acquired from the market should not automatically be assumed to qualify for every release requirement. Captive-bred animals provide another option, including some very young animals that can already be accepted into release groups.

### How to find animals eligible for a specific Wildlife Reserve

Not every animal can be released into every Wildlife Reserve. The destination has its own list of eligible species, and choosing an animal that belongs in another region will not make it valid for the reserve currently selected.

In the Release Groups interface, use the **Valid Wildlife Ecosystem** filter and select the destination reserve. This narrows the selection to species that can be released into that reserve.

![The Create Release Group filter menu with Sumatra Reserve selected as the target Wildlife Reserve.](/images/planet-zoo-2/wildlife-reserve-valid-ecosystem-filter.webp)

For example, selecting Sumatra excludes species that cannot be released there. If **Release to Wildlife Reserve** is unavailable for the selected group, inspect its destination eligibility before changing the surrounding terrain.

The **Quick Release** option is different. It does not place animals into your selected Wildlife Reserve or establish a population there. Use the Wildlife Reserve release option when you need the animals to contribute to that reserve.

### Can you cancel a Release Group?

Check the animals before finalizing their release. Once the group has been committed and released, its members cannot be transferred back into the zoo.

This matters when the selected animals have valuable breeding characteristics or are still needed for another objective. Keep essential breeding animals in your zoo and release the animals you can afford to remove permanently.

## How to Establish an Ecosystem in a Wildlife Reserve

Open **Ecosystems** at the Wildlife Reserve and select the area you want to restore or create. The game checks the designated area's size and environmental conditions before changing its status from **Invalid** to **Established**.

An area can contain plenty of trees and still fail its requirements. Different Ecosystems need particular combinations of vegetation, terrain, rocky cover, or water, and satisfying one category does not compensate for another that remains insufficient.

### Great Rift Valley: How to Establish the Dense Forest

The first Great Rift Valley restoration involves removing invasive flora from the designated area before rebuilding a Dense Forest suitable for the conservation project.

The Ecosystem requires several different environmental elements:

- **Dense Crown Trees**
- **Spreading Crown Trees**
- **Medium Rocky Cover**

Use the Nature Brush to restore the missing vegetation, checking the Ecosystem panel after each adjustment. The brush can place individual plant types or mixtures, but repeatedly adding the same kind of tree will not complete a different requirement.

Rocky cover is particularly easy to overlook. Even after the surrounding forest looks sufficiently dense, the Ecosystem can remain Invalid because it still lacks enough Medium Rocky Cover.

![Dense Forest is not established because Medium Rocky Cover, Spreading Crown Trees, and Dense Crown Trees are below their requirements.](/images/planet-zoo-2/wildlife-reserve-dense-forest-requirements.webp)

Finish the categories still below their required levels and confirm that the Ecosystem becomes **Established** before attempting the giraffe release.

### Scottish Highlands: Why is Montane Forest still Invalid?

The Eurasian Lynx assignment in Scottish Highlands introduces another constraint: the designated Montane Forest can initially be **Too Small**.

Expand the Ecosystem boundary until it satisfies the area requirement before filling it with vegetation. If the boundary is insufficient, planting more trees and grass will not solve the underlying problem.

Once the area is large enough, check the remaining environmental requirements:

- Short Grass
- Moss
- Forest Floor
- Understory Plants
- Cold-hardy Trees

These categories may become sufficient at different times. Work through the values displayed in the Ecosystem panel rather than painting the entire area repeatedly.

## Why Won't My Animals Release into the Wildlife Reserve?

If a prepared Release Group cannot be released, inspect the exact missing condition before rebuilding the destination.

### An established Ecosystem has become Invalid

In Scottish Highlands, Montane Forest can reach Established and then fall below its requirements again. During the lynx project, a shortage of **Short Grass** prevents an otherwise prepared release.

Return to the Ecosystem panel and restore the missing coverage. There is no reason to rebuild the entire forest when only one category has dropped below its required level.

A similar situation can happen after terrain adjustments or changes to neighbouring Ecosystems. The Eurasian Lynx release requires both **Montane Forest** and **Mixed Lowland Forest**, so completing one does not satisfy the whole group. Confirm that every required environment is still Established when you attempt the release.

### Predators need suitable prey

A predator's release can depend on the presence of an appropriate food source. In Franchise reserves, establishing a deer population before introducing wolves provides the prey needed for the predator's role.

Scottish Highlands uses Eurasian Lynx to help manage an excessive deer population. The release is part of restoring a functioning ecosystem, not simply adding another species to increase the animal count.

If a predator cannot be released, check the required species and Ecosystem conditions rather than repeatedly creating new groups of the same predator.

### Some birds require nesting structures

Bird releases can have additional requirements even when the destination is otherwise suitable.

For example, a Great Hornbill release in Sumatra can require a **Cavity Nest Large**. Place the required nest in the reserve, then return to the Release Group and try again.

The same kind of nesting restriction can affect other birds, including vultures and shoebills. Use the specific nesting requirement shown for the selected species rather than assuming every bird needs the same structure.

### Research facilities or species roles are missing

An unavailable Release Groups control is different from a release being rejected for environmental reasons.

In a newly created Sandbox Wildlife Reserve, the control can initially be inactive until a **Research Outpost** is placed. The outpost and the reserve's wider monitoring facilities are covered below.

Other releases depend on ecological roles that the reserve has not yet established. In Poland Reserve, for instance, the Eurasian Bittern assignment can be blocked by missing Wetland Engineers and Terrestrial Foragers. The [Chapter 4 walkthrough](/planet-zoo-2/career-chapter-4) follows the Fen Wetland restoration and its accompanying objectives.

### What happens after a successful release?

Released animals live in the Wildlife Reserve rather than simply disappearing from the management system. They can move through their established environments, reproduce, and interact with other species.

That also means the reserve continues changing after the first release. Predators may reduce prey populations, new offspring can increase species numbers, and an ecosystem that was initially balanced may need attention later.

Check the population and Ecosystem status after allowing time to pass, especially before adding another predator group.

## How to Build a Research Network in Wildlife Reserves

Wildlife Reserves use research facilities to monitor wild populations and develop their Research Rating. They do not operate through the same keeper facilities and enclosed habitats used in zoos.

The **Research Outpost** is the starting point. In a new Sandbox Reserve, placing an outpost can activate the previously unavailable Release Groups control.

For example, in a tropical Sumatra Reserve, place the Tropical Research Outpost from the appropriate Blueprint options. Once the outpost is active, the Release Groups control becomes available.

Sandbox settings also affect the starting conditions. Choosing an established setup and disabling Ecosystem requirements simplifies initial wildlife releases, whereas Career and Franchise objectives must be completed under their applicable conditions.

### Scottish Highlands: Research Outpost and Observation Platforms

Scottish Highlands requires more than a single outpost. The Career assignment calls for a **Research Outpost** followed by **three Observation Platforms**.

The Observation Platforms must be distributed across the reserve without overlapping their coverage. Place them in separate locations rather than clustering them around the outpost.

![Research Network requirements showing zero of three Observation Platforms, Camera Traps at 0%, and Acoustic Monitors locked at Accreditation Tier 3.](/images/planet-zoo-2/wildlife-reserve-research-network-requirements.webp)

After establishing the platforms, continue building the research network with the available monitoring equipment. The Scottish Highlands sequence uses **three Camera Traps** while expanding research coverage.

**Acoustic Monitors** have an additional progression requirement. In this Career assignment, the option is locked behind **Accreditation Tier 3**.

A locked research facility does not mean that your Ecosystems or existing animal releases are invalid. Continue with the available monitoring equipment until the required Accreditation Tier is reached.

Career and Franchise have separate Accreditation progression. In Franchise Mode, Acoustic Monitors also unlock at Tier 3, but the Tier requirements depend on qualifying zoos and Wildlife Reserves rather than Career medals. Check [Franchise Accreditation](/planet-zoo-2/franchise-accreditation) for those unlock conditions.

## How to Increase Wildlife Reserve Biodiversity Rating

Releasing more animals is not always the fastest way to improve a Wildlife Reserve's rating. The **Biodiversity Rating** panel separates progress into **Ecosystem Rating** and **Research Rating**, and each requires different work.

A reserve may already contain several animal populations while its research network remains incomplete. Alternatively, the observation equipment may be in place while the ecosystems lack the species roles or environmental conditions needed to develop further.

![The Great Rift Valley Biodiversity Rating panel showing Ecosystem Rating and Research Rating.](/images/planet-zoo-2/wildlife-reserve-biodiversity-rating.webp)

### Ecosystem Rating: Restore habitats and fill missing species roles

An Established Ecosystem has met its initial environmental requirements, but its condition can improve further. Inspect the Ecosystem's health and species information to see what is still missing before sending another Release Group.

Different animals contribute different ecological roles. Wolves provide a predator role, while deer can contribute prey and seed-dispersal roles. Wild Boar and other species may provide further functions needed by the environment.

![Wild Boar species roles in a Wildlife Reserve, including Soil Rooters, Medium Prey, Terrestrial Spore Dispersers, and Grazers.](/images/planet-zoo-2/wildlife-reserve-species-roles.webp)

The useful question is not simply how many animals have been released, but whether the reserve has the roles and species required for a healthy ecosystem.

Open Zoopedia and the selected Ecosystem's information to compare the requirements with the animals already living there. If a role is missing, choose a suitable species that can fill it rather than releasing additional animals with roles already represented.

A flourishing ecosystem can also depend on maintaining its required populations over time. Revisit its status after predators have begun hunting or after a newly introduced species has started reproducing.

### Research Rating: Expand the monitoring network

Research Rating develops through the reserve's research facilities and monitoring coverage.

When it is lagging behind Ecosystem Rating, investigate the Research Network instead of automatically releasing more animals. Check the Research Outpost, Observation Platforms, Camera Traps, and any additional monitoring equipment available at your current progression level.

Conversely, adding another Observation Platform will not repair a damaged Ecosystem or replace a missing animal role.

### How to Raise a Wildlife Reserve to 3 Stars and Beyond

A Wildlife Reserve with **3 Biodiversity Stars** can count toward Franchise Accreditation, while reaching **5 Stars** requires further development. The number of qualifying reserves needed for each Tier is shown under [Franchise Accreditation](/planet-zoo-2/franchise-accreditation).

One practical approach is to develop reserves that accept overlapping species. **Scottish Highlands and Sweden** can share part of the same breeding supply, allowing you to prepare eligible animals for more than one destination once both reserves are available.

A workable progression is:

1. **Establish the required ecosystems** in the first reserve and check which species can be released there.
2. **Build a supply of suitable animals** in your zoo. Red Deer, Wild Boar, and other relatively fast-breeding species can provide repeated release opportunities.
3. **Open another reserve with overlapping eligible species**, such as Sweden after Scottish Highlands, and use the destination filter to confirm which groups can go there.
4. **Fill missing ecological roles.** Introduce suitable prey before predators when required, and check the Ecosystem's species information for roles that remain absent.
5. **Allow populations time to develop.** Reproduction and ecosystem changes do not necessarily update the rating immediately after an animal is released.
6. **Improve Research Rating alongside the ecosystems.** Add the monitoring facilities available at your current Tier, then return to the Biodiversity Rating panel to identify what is still holding progress back.

The strategy works best when you avoid treating every release as an isolated purchase. A zoo with an established breeding population can prepare animals for several reserves without rebuilding the same breeding operation each time.

For example, Red Deer can produce offspring for future releases while existing reserve populations continue developing. Some newborn animals may already be eligible for a Release Group, so check the group interface rather than assuming that every animal must reach adulthood first.

Do not empty your breeding population simply to accelerate the next release. Keep enough suitable animals in the zoo to maintain future supply. If reproduction is slowing or inbreeding is becoming a problem, the [Franchise Breeding guide](/planet-zoo-2/franchise-breeding) covers those zoo management decisions.

There is no need to keep releasing the same species when the reserve's limiting factor is elsewhere. Once the animals are established, compare Ecosystem Rating and Research Rating again and focus on whichever still needs work.

## Why Is the Sumatra River Ecosystem Still Invalid?

Sumatra's River Ecosystem can remain **Invalid** even when the designated area looks large enough. Unlike a forest that primarily needs vegetation and terrain coverage, this environment also depends on having enough water within its boundaries.

Expanding the Ecosystem boundary alone does not necessarily satisfy the water requirement. The River restoration can require lowering the terrain to create room for more water before the remaining environmental conditions can be completed.

### Fix the water and terrain requirements

Open the River Ecosystem panel and identify which condition is still insufficient.

1. **Check the designated area.** Expand the River boundary if it has not met its minimum area requirement.
2. **Increase the available water.** Lower the terrain where necessary, then add water until the Ecosystem's water requirement is satisfied.
3. **Complete the remaining ground coverage.** Compare Soil, Scree, and Freshwater Bed Plants with the values displayed in the Ecosystem panel.
4. **Confirm Established.** Recheck the Ecosystem after changing its terrain or water, particularly where it borders another established environment.

Adding more plants cannot make up for insufficient water. Similarly, increasing the designated area will not help if the remaining problem is the amount of water contained inside it.

Be careful around neighbouring Ecosystems. Excavation, terrain painting, and overlapping boundaries can change the environmental coverage of areas you have already restored.

Once the River and Peatland environments are ready, the Sumatra conservation work continues with the rescued otters. The [Chapter 3 walkthrough](/planet-zoo-2/career-chapter-3) covers the animal transfers and the three-otter Research Photo objective.
