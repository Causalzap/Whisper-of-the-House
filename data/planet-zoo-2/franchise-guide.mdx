A new Franchise can look promising long before it can afford to grow. Visitors are arriving, the first animals have settled in, and the paths are taking shape. Then an animal refuses to leave the Trade Center, a keeper fails to refill a feeder, or the zoo keeps losing money despite attracting guests.

These are often separate problems. Animal delivery depends on usable paths and staff access, while profit depends on actual revenue, operating costs, and whether guests have reasons to spend money. A larger enclosure will not fix either one.

The first useful milestone is a small zoo that operates reliably: animals receive care, visitors can see them, facilities earn enough to support their running costs, and there is money left for the next habitat.

## Start Your First Franchise Zoo

Choose **Franchise** from the main menu, create your Franchise, and select a location for your first zoo.

Franchise connects your zoos and Wildlife Reserves through the Planet Hub. Unlike Sandbox, it limits available animals and facilities through progression, and you must manage the finances of your own locations.

Franchise uses online services, so a connection problem can prevent you from accessing your zoo. Check that Franchise has loaded successfully before starting construction.

A Challenge Sandbox zoo does not contribute to your Franchise Accreditation. Create your location through Franchise and confirm that it appears in the correct Planet Hub.

### Choose a Map You Can Afford to Build On

A sculpted map may look much more interesting than flat terrain, but steep slopes complicate the first staff area, habitat entrances, and path connections.

One early Franchise attempt began on a tropical map with extensive water and very little convenient building space. The player abandoned that location and started again on a more manageable temperate map.

There is nothing wrong with choosing an ambitious landscape later. For your first zoo, look for:

- Enough reasonably level ground near the entrance for your initial staff facilities.
- A straightforward route from the Trade Center to the first habitat.
- Space for a guest viewing path and a small group of shops or services.
- Room to expand without rebuilding the entire entrance.

The aim is to minimize construction problems while money and animal choices are still limited.

### Build the Staff Area Before Adopting Animals

Start with the buildings needed to receive and care for animals.

The first practical setup consists of a **Trade Center**, **Keeper Hut**, **Staff Room**, and the paths connecting them. Add the necessary staff and other facilities as your first animals require them.

A Research Center is useful once you begin unlocking enrichment and education options, but placing every available facility immediately adds construction costs before the zoo generates income.

A compact staff area works well because the first keepers do not have to travel far between animal habitats and food-preparation facilities.

Build the first Habitat Staff Gate where it can connect directly to this area. Test the path network before spending time on decorative plazas, complicated bridges, or extensive landscaping.

## Why Won't My Animals Leave the Trade Center?

Animal transport can fail even when both the Trade Center and the destination Habitat appear to have paths.

During one first-day Franchise setup, an animal delivery produced a **"No Path to Destination"** warning. A staff member could be seen walking nearby, yet the animal still could not be transported.

The issue persisted through several attempts to adjust the existing path.

![Habitat 1 showing a No path to destination warning despite a nearby path, preventing animal delivery.](/images/planet-zoo-2/franchise-guide-trade-center-path.webp)

Before replacing buildings, inspect the full route:

1. Select the destination Habitat and verify that it has a valid **Staff Gate**.
2. Follow the path back toward the Trade Center, checking for disconnected segments or elevation changes.
3. Confirm that the Trade Center itself is attached to the usable path network.
4. Allow the game to run briefly after changing paths, then retry the transfer.

A path that looks connected from above may not be accepted as a functional route.

In the affected zoo, the player eventually placed another Trade Center closer to the Habitat and connected it using a short, direct path. The animal transfer then worked.

This is a useful fallback when repeated edits to the original route are getting nowhere. You do not need to rebuild the animal enclosure merely because the original Trade Center cannot be reached.

### Finish One Working Habitat Before Building Three

Once transport works, place the first animals and inspect their welfare.

A Black Wildebeest habitat was one early Franchise choice. Another zoo began with species such as Aardvarks and Galápagos Giant Tortoises before expanding to Camels.

The specific animal is less important than choosing one you can afford and support with the buildings already available.

Before adding the next species, check that your first Habitat has:

- A working Staff Gate and keeper access.
- Appropriate food, water, shelter, and terrain.
- Suitable environmental conditions and enrichment.
- A guest viewing area that does not leave the animals excessively stressed.

A functioning first Habitat provides a predictable place to test your staff and facility network. Several unfinished habitats create more problems to diagnose simultaneously.

## How to Make Money in Franchise Mode

A zoo can attract hundreds of visitors and still have almost no money left for expansion. The Finance panel is the first place to look before buying another animal or building a large Habitat.

One developing Franchise Zoo earned **$9,141.94** in a season: **$1,638** from tickets, **$3,268.20** from shops, and **$4,235.74** from donations. Its total expenses reached **$8,467.68**, leaving just **$674.26 in profit**.

![A Franchise zoo's seasonal finances showing $9,141.94 income and $8,467.68 expenses, leaving $674.26 in profit.](/images/planet-zoo-2/franchise-guide-income-expenses.webp)

The previous season also included a **$47,500 loan**. That money increased the zoo's available cash, but it was not revenue earned from guests. Loan repayments then became another expense competing with animal care, staff wages, and future construction.

At this stage, donations brought in more money than ticket sales, while the overall profit margin remained narrow. The next investment needs to address an existing weakness or generate enough additional revenue to justify its ongoing cost.

Check the Finance panel over several seasons before adding another expensive species. A positive bank balance immediately after receiving a loan can hide an operation that is still struggling to support itself.

### Check Whether You Are Charging Admission

This sounds obvious, but it caused a real problem in an early Franchise.

After building the first habitat and welcoming guests, the player discovered that visitors were entering without paying. The zoo had started operating, but **ticket sales were contributing nothing**.

Open the zoo's management controls and check the adult and child admission prices.

Choose an affordable starting price and examine the next visitors' reactions. Guest feedback is more useful than guessing how much the zoo should charge.

One established Franchise Zoo was charging **$15 for adults and $10 for children**, and an arriving guest described the ticket price as "pretty good." The management panel also showed **84% Guest Happiness** at the time, although that rating reflects the wider visitor experience.

![Zoo Management showing $15 adult admission, $10 child admission, and positive guest feedback on ticket prices.](/images/planet-zoo-2/franchise-guide-ticket-price-feedback.webp)

The prices were later increased to **$17 for adults and $12 for children**, with guests still describing them as fairly reasonable.

Use those figures as an example of testing prices, not as fixed rates for every zoo. Adjust admission in small increments and check new visitor comments after each change.

### Put Donation Bins Where Guests Actually Stop

Donation income becomes useful as soon as visitors have animals to watch.

Place Donation Bins beside your first Habitat's viewing areas, then check whether they begin collecting money. One early Franchise zoo showed individual donation bins bringing in approximately **$2,000 and $1,000** at different locations.

This does not mean every bin will generate those amounts. The viewing opportunity, guest traffic, and zoo conditions influence the result.

A Donation Bin placed along an empty path is unlikely to be as useful as one near a popular enclosure.

Add ordinary Trash Bins near these areas as well. Litter becomes another recurring management issue as guest traffic increases.

### Start With a Small Shop Area

Food, drinks, and merchandise provide additional income, but shops have expenses.

An early zoo received a **"Shop is losing money"** warning while its visitor numbers were still low. Another developing Franchise relied on relatively modest food facilities rather than constructing a large restaurant district immediately.

Start with one or two suitable outlets near the main visitor route. Watch whether guests use them and whether the outlets earn enough to justify their running costs.

Vending Machines can be a useful low-complexity choice while the zoo is still small. Add seating and bins where guests gather, but avoid spending heavily on decorative food courts before there is consistent demand.

The relevant question is whether the next shop serves an unmet need or simply increases maintenance and staffing costs.

## Which Animals Should You Buy First?

Franchise does not offer every species immediately, and the animals currently listed in Trading can vary.

Early on, the sensible choice is usually an affordable animal that is available at your current Accreditation Tier and can live comfortably in a straightforward Habitat.

Animal Trading provides more information than just the purchase price. Check the species, age, sex, characteristics, and asking price before adopting.

### Check the Available Sexes Before Planning a Breeding Pair

One developing Franchise wanted Bactrian Camels as its next attraction.

The market offered female Camels, but no suitable male at that moment. The player adopted two females to begin the Habitat and planned to check later for a male.

That can be a reasonable decision when you want the animal as an attraction immediately. It is less useful when your entire plan depends on breeding from the start.

Before buying the first animal of a species, decide whether the immediate goal is:

- **Guest appeal:** an animal can serve as an attraction while you wait for a suitable mate.
- **Breeding:** check for a compatible male and female before committing to the setup.
- **Conservation releases:** consider the species and Release Group requirements before raising animals for transport.

The market may contain the species you want without offering the sex or quality you need.

A different early Franchise struggled to obtain a male Zebra despite repeatedly checking Trading. The player eventually found suitable animals later, but the delay illustrates why you should not leave an expensive completed Habitat empty while relying on one uncertain listing.

Keep alternative species in mind until your trading options improve.

### Budget for the Whole Habitat, Not Just the Animal

The adoption price is only one part of the decision.

A relatively affordable animal can still need more shelter, heating, staff facilities, or enrichment than you anticipated.

One Galápagos Tortoise setup improved its welfare after shelter was added, but another check found the zoo at approximately **13°C** while a tortoise's displayed tolerable range was **18–33°C**.

Adding heating introduced another requirement: the heater needed power.

This is why the species' environment and the zoo's utilities should be checked before completing a purchase. An enclosure can have food and water while still exposing its residents to unsuitable temperatures.

Research also becomes important when animals need enrichment or improvements not yet available in the construction browser.

Use the research facilities to unlock the items needed by your current species rather than spending your entire early budget researching unrelated animals.

## Why Aren't Keepers Feeding the Animals?

One Franchise Habitat displayed **"No Keeper to Refill Feeders"** and **"Keeper Urgently Requested"**, even though the zoo already employed a keeper.

The first attempt to fix it was to create a Work Zone and assign the relevant staff.

That revealed another issue: the Keeper Hut was not properly accessible.

After the path connection was rebuilt, the keeper began moving toward the Habitat.

The important lesson is that hiring another employee does not automatically resolve a problem with access or assignment.

When animals are waiting for care, check the following:

- **Work Zones:** confirm the Habitat and relevant staff facilities are included, with appropriate keepers assigned.
- **Path access:** verify that the Habitat Staff Gate and Keeper Hut can actually be reached.
- **Power:** investigate any unpowered facilities involved in the work.
- **Staff workload:** check whether the current employee has too many jobs or is unavailable.

A second keeper will add ongoing wages. Fixing a disconnected path may cost considerably less.

### Keep a Reserve for Veterinary Problems

Another early zoo delayed constructing Quarantine to save money.

Later, a Black Wildebeest became ill, forcing the player to build Quarantine when the cash balance was already low. The new facility used much of the remaining money and also needed power.

The animal's treatment then required veterinary facilities and properly functioning transport.

That sequence is a reason to retain some cash after finishing your first Habitat. You may not need every medical building on the first day, but a completely exhausted construction budget makes an unexpected animal-care problem harder to handle.

Do not treat a veterinary emergency as an opportunity to expand. Stabilize the sick animal and the staff route before moving on to another species.

## What If Your Habitats Become Overcrowded?

A successful breeding season can leave a previously healthy Habitat short of space. As juveniles grow, check whether the existing enclosure can support the larger group before spending money on another species.

Some animals may need Contraception, while eligible surplus animals can be moved elsewhere. For the breeding controls, social group requirements, and practical examples, see [how to stop inbreeding and overcrowding](/planet-zoo-2/franchise-breeding).

## When Should You Expand Your Franchise?

A first zoo is ready for meaningful expansion when its essential services work reliably and it can absorb the expense of another Habitat without immediately requiring another loan.

Before making the investment, check:

1. Is income consistently covering operating expenses?
2. Are the current animals healthy, with enough room for their populations?
3. Can staff reach their assigned Habitats and facilities?
4. Is there enough cash for the next animal's full enclosure and ongoing care?
5. Is the species available at the Franchise's current Accreditation Tier?

Once the first location is stable, there are two different ways to progress.

You can expand the current zoo with new species and guest facilities, improving its attraction and Reputation. Or you can begin developing another Franchise location when the broader network requirements make that worthwhile.

The first major milestone is reaching **3 Zoo Reputation Stars**, which unlocks Franchise Accreditation Tier 2 and another 33 species.

The exact unlock requirements, later tiers, and reasons animals remain locked are covered in the [Franchise Accreditation guide](/planet-zoo-2/franchise-accreditation).

Higher tiers also require Wildlife Reserves. These locations have their own Biodiversity Rating, and the animals you prepare in your zoos can be released into suitable ecosystems. The [Wildlife Reserves guide](/planet-zoo-2/wildlife-reserves) explains those release and research systems.

A second zoo is much easier to manage when the first one is no longer losing money or struggling with inaccessible facilities. Expand because your existing operation can support the next step, not simply because there is another attractive animal on the market.
