Complete Career progression while building up breeding, research, and conservation progress. Develop one zoo for the species-collection achievements, then finish the specialized Aquarium, Wildlife Reserve, and Gold Medal goals using the tracker below.

**Planet Zoo 2 has 39 Steam achievements**, ranging from adopting your first animal to earning Gold in every Career Scenario. The difficult part of reaching 100% is not remembering 39 separate tasks. It is recognizing which achievements can share the same zoo, which require one specific facility, and which will take time regardless of how efficiently you build.

A large aquarium, a successful breeding zoo, and a five-star Wildlife Reserve can each contribute to several achievements. The advantage disappears when the wrong species are grouped together, animals are transferred before you finish breeding them, or progress is spread across facilities that must meet a requirement individually.

<section id="roadmap" className="scroll-mt-24">

## A Practical Route to 100% Completion

Four goals can progress alongside one another: Career medals, zoo breeding and species collections, reserve conservation, and specialized construction.

The first is normal Career progression. The second is a long-term zoo for breeding and species collections. The third is conservation and research progress. The fourth is specialized construction and final cleanup.

You do not need to abandon one project whenever the next becomes available. In fact, keeping several of them progressing together reduces the amount of repeated building later.

### 1. Let Career introduce the systems before committing to expensive builds

The opening Career scenarios introduce animal adoption, breeding, habitat construction, aviaries, aquariums, and Wildlife Reserves.

These activities naturally put several early achievements within reach. **Let's Get Started** comes from adopting an animal, while **Earth, Wind and Water** needs one adoption from each of the Aquatic, Avian, and Terrestrial categories.

The first successful birth contributes **The Future is Bright**, and finishing research on a species earns **Student**.

There is little reason to construct an enormous aquarium or an elaborate multi-species habitat while these systems are still being introduced. Complete the immediate Career objectives, learn how the facilities operate, and avoid spending heavily on a design that may need to be rebuilt.

Career also introduces Bronze, Silver, and Gold medals. Progress through the available locations first, keeping track of scenarios that still lack Gold. You can return to those after unlocking the facilities and animals needed for more demanding objectives.

### 2. Keep one developed zoo for the species and breeding achievements

Several achievements become much easier to organize when one zoo remains your main collection and breeding location.

That zoo can work toward:

- **Birds of a Feather** — five Avian species.
- **Jungle Beat** — three Primate species.
- **Bear-Able** — three Bear species.
- **Cats and Dogs** — two Canine and two Feline species.
- **First Steps** — ten critically endangered Terrestrial or Aquatic animals born in that zoo.

You do not need to purchase all these animals immediately. Expand the collection as suitable habitats, staff capacity, and available funds allow.

For **First Steps**, the important decision is where to establish the breeding population. The achievement explicitly requires the births to occur in a single zoo. Spreading breeding pairs across several zoos creates more facilities to maintain without helping you satisfy that specific location requirement.

Keep the zoo's breeding and long-term welfare goals in view as you expand it. **They Grow Up So Fast** requires an animal born in your zoo to reach 15 years of age, so a long-lived zoo is more useful than repeatedly abandoning developed facilities.

### 3. Build conservation progress alongside the normal game

Wildlife Reserve restoration provides an opportunity to work on several cumulative achievements without running separate projects solely for them.

During reserve restoration, let **Green Thumb**, **Naturalist**, and **Job Well Done** progress together. Keep the Release Group requirements in mind before committing valuable breeding animals, especially when working toward **They Do Move In Herds** or **Worth Its Weight in Gold**.

Use these conservation tasks to build progress naturally. Once the reserve work becomes more demanding, concentrate on improving the environment and research network rather than releasing extra animals without a clear purpose.

Two objectives deserve more deliberate planning: **They Do Move In Herds**, which requires one group containing more than ten animals, and **Worth Its Weight in Gold**, which checks the genetics of an animal being released.

The [Wildlife Reserves walkthrough](/planet-zoo-2/wildlife-reserves) explains how to prepare Release Groups and why animals should not be committed until you are ready to release them.

### 4. Reserve special builds and complete collections for later

Some achievements are best attempted when the zoo has sufficient room, money, and access to the required animals.

The most obvious examples are the 25-million-litre Aquarium, the five-species Aquarium, the five-species Thriving habitat, and the Ocean Sunfish welfare requirement.

They are not ordinary construction milestones. Each has a specific condition that the final setup must satisfy.

Once your general progression and long-term zoo are in good shape, use the unchecked entries to decide which specialized project to tackle next.

That is also a sensible time to return to the remaining Career Gold medals and rating-related achievements.

## Which Achievements Can Share the Same Zoo?

Some of the biggest time savings come from keeping compatible goals together.

A zoo built for a varied animal collection can contribute to Birds of a Feather, Jungle Beat, Bear-Able, and Cats and Dogs while also supporting education, research, and long-term animal care.

Those conditions count species within a zoo, so a sufficiently developed collection can address several of them without requiring separate locations.

There are limits to this approach.

**First Steps** requires actual births from critically endangered animals. Purchasing ten qualifying animals does not fulfill a requirement for ten animals to be born in the zoo.

**They Grow Up So Fast** also depends on an animal that was born in your zoo, not merely one that has reached 15 years of age after being adopted.

These differences matter when choosing between maintaining breeding animals and trading or releasing them. An animal may be useful to your zoo for longer than its immediate conservation value suggests.

A successful breeding zoo can also support the research work needed for **Professor**, although that achievement has its own requirement: fully research 80 different species. Do not mistake research progress on a few familiar animals for progress toward 80 distinct completed species.

### Combine education objectives where possible

Your established zoo is also a reasonable place to work on **Lessons Learned**, **Did You Know?**, and **Follow Me!**.

These involve different outcomes: a five-star Education Rating, all Education Topics for a species, and setting up an Educational Tour.

Work on education facilities and species research as the zoo expands. However, track those three conditions separately. Completing an Educational Tour does not automatically mean every topic for a species is available, and neither action guarantees a five-star Education Rating.

</section>

<section id="thriving" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Plan the Thriving Achievements Separately

**Shared Accommodation** and **Unbothered. Focused. Thriving.** sound similar, but they should not be treated as interchangeable.

The easier arrangement to plan first is having three species Thriving simultaneously within a zoo. Those species are not required to occupy one Terrestrial Habitat.

Shared Accommodation has a much more specific requirement: five different species in one Terrestrial Habitat, all sharing a Thriving relationship.

That means building five separate successful habitats will not satisfy Shared Accommodation. Nor should you combine five animals simply because their environmental needs appear similar.

Before redesigning an existing habitat, check the relationship information for the exact species you intend to combine.

There is no advantage in dismantling several healthy enclosures merely to attempt an unconfirmed five-species combination.

A useful distinction while planning is:

- **Three Thriving species:** concentrate on maintaining simultaneous suitable conditions within the zoo.
- **Five-species Shared Accommodation:** treat the compatibility relationship and single-habitat requirement as the central challenge.

The second can remain a late cleanup goal while you continue improving the broader zoo.

</section>

## Can One Aquarium Help With Multiple Achievements?

Potentially, yes, but the achievements check different properties.

**A Few More Hoses** concerns the volume of one Aquarium Habitat. **Full Tank** concerns the number of different species living in one Aquarium. **Sieve The Day** concerns the filtration rate of one network.

One sufficiently large Aquarium could contribute to the first two goals if it also provides suitable conditions for five compatible species.

The filtration requirement is different. Reaching 5,000 across several independent networks is not the same as reaching that value in a single network.

For that reason, do not build several disconnected small aquariums and assume their combined water volume or filtration capacity will satisfy the single-facility achievements.

Before attempting a major Aquarium project, establish which of these conditions it is intended to fulfill.

A 25-million-litre enclosure consumes a great deal of space. Five-species housing also requires careful consideration of salinity, temperature, compatibility, and animal welfare.

Plan the enclosure's footprint before making expensive decorative changes, then check its actual volume and the species requirements before stocking it.

For the construction process, including Staff Gates, filtration, water types, and temperature controls, use the [Aquarium setup walkthrough](/planet-zoo-2/aquariums).

### Ocean Sunfish is a separate welfare target

**Mola Mola Likes You** requires an Ocean Sunfish to reach 100% welfare.

Completing a massive Aquarium does not guarantee that result. The animal's own welfare status is the condition that matters.

It makes sense to pursue this achievement after you are comfortable maintaining aquatic habitats rather than using it as your first Aquarium experiment.

</section>

## Do Not Confuse Release Groups, Ecosystems, and Reserve Ratings

Wildlife Reserve achievements fall into several different kinds of progress.

For **Job Well Done**, the number of completed releases matters: 20 Release Groups.

For **They Do Move In Herds**, the size of one group matters. More than ten means at least eleven animals in a single Release Group.

For **Naturalist**, the goal is to establish ten Ecosystems. The number of animals released does not replace establishing the required environments.

Then there is **Paradise**, which requires a five-star Wildlife Reserve rating.

A reserve's Biodiversity Rating is made up of Ecosystem Rating and Research Rating. Restoring vegetation and releasing suitable species helps the environmental side, while observation and monitoring facilities contribute to research.

The important distinction is that a reserve can contain released animals and established ecosystems without having reached its maximum rating.

You may therefore complete several conservation achievements while Paradise remains unchecked. Continue improving the rating category that is behind rather than releasing more animals without examining the reserve's status.

### Leave high-scoring photography as its own task

**Snap!** needs a photograph scoring 10,000 in a Wildlife Reserve.

That is a single-photo target. Multiple ordinary research photographs do not combine into one score.

The normal Career photography activities provide opportunities to learn the camera controls, but finishing those early objectives is not proof that you have reached the achievement's required score.

Keep Snap! separate in your cleanup list until you can produce a photograph that actually meets the target.

</section>

<section id="career-gold" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Career Bronze, All Gold, and Maximum Accreditation

The Career-related achievements should be tracked as three different completion checks.

**Good Start…** rewards Bronze in every Career Scenario.

**Excellent Finish** requires Gold in every Career Scenario.

**Credit Where It's Due** requires the highest Accreditation level in a non-Sandbox hub.

Bronze is useful for advancing through Career and reaching more scenarios, while Gold represents a higher level of completion.

Accreditation is another progression measure. Reaching the highest tier is not the same objective as earning every Career Gold medal.

This distinction matters when deciding whether to remain in a scenario or move to a newly unlocked location.

Finishing the currently available objectives and opening more of the game is a reasonable first priority. After more species, facilities, and research options become available, revisit incomplete Gold scenarios as needed.

For **Excellent Finish**, the final requirement is all Career Scenarios at Gold. An incomplete Gold medal cannot be replaced by reaching a high Accreditation tier elsewhere.

The six scenarios in **Chapter 4** include Maluku Islands, Channel Islands, Singapore, Poland Reserve, Swiss Alps, and Tanzania Reserve. If any of their Gold medals remain unfinished, use the [Chapter 4 walkthrough](/planet-zoo-2/career-chapter-4) to check the remaining objectives.

Credit Where It's Due also specifies a non-Sandbox hub, so do not assume that reaching a similar progression state in Sandbox satisfies the condition.

</section>

## Five Long-Term Goals Worth Keeping in View

Not every remaining achievement needs a special strategy. Some require large totals that are easier to accumulate during normal play.

**Professor — 80 fully researched species.** Research a variety of species as your collection develops. This goal is about distinct species reaching full research completion, not the total amount of time spent studying one animal.

**Green Thumb — 1,000 trees.** Wildlife Reserve restoration already requires environmental work, so planting trees while establishing and improving ecosystems can contribute naturally.

**Job Well Done — 20 Release Groups.** Plan releases as you continue conservation work. A release should still use suitable animals and environments rather than sacrificing breeding plans just to increase the count.

**Long Distance Builder — 25 km of barrier.** Ordinary zoo expansion contributes to this construction goal. It is unlikely to deserve a large, purposeless enclosure solely for the counter until you know how much distance remains.

**Where Am I? — 10 km in the Research Vehicle.** Reserve exploration can contribute to this distance requirement, making it suitable for background progress.

These goals are good reasons to keep developing useful facilities rather than creating disposable construction projects at the beginning.

### Some goals depend on a specific action, not a long waiting period

**Show-Pony** requires starting Gold Tier (Level 3) Marketing for an animal.

The stated condition is to start the qualifying marketing activity. Do not confuse it with a requirement to maintain Gold-tier Marketing indefinitely.

**Ultimate Conservationist** requires at least one Conservation Project of every type, whereas Budding Conservationist only requires completing a project.

The efficient way to approach that difference is to keep track of which project types you have already finished. Repeating a familiar type is not a substitute for completing one that is still missing.

</section>

<section id="final-cleanup" className="scroll-mt-24">

## What to Do When Only a Few Achievements Remain

Late in a 100% run, the remaining achievements usually tell you which kind of project is unfinished.

A missing Career medal calls for returning to the relevant scenario. A missing zoo collection achievement may require acquiring a particular animal family. A filtration achievement means checking a network rather than expanding an unrelated habitat.

Before starting another expensive build, read the exact requirement in the checklist and identify what the game is counting.

Pay special attention to wording such as:

- **In a single zoo:** the relevant animals or births must be located together.
- **In a single habitat or Aquarium:** separate enclosures do not combine into one result.
- **In one filtration network:** independently configured networks cannot be treated as a single system.
- **At the same time:** the required states must coexist.
- **More than 10:** the target starts at 11.
- **All Career Scenarios:** completing a subset is not sufficient.
- **One of every type:** repetition does not replace variety.

Those distinctions are more useful than a rigid universal order for the last few achievements.

Use the checklist to hide completed entries, identify the requirement that is still missing, and finish that specific project without dismantling the zoo or reserve systems already working well.

</section>
