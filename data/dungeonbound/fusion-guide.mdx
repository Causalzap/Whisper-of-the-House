## Dungeonbound Fusion vs. Merge: What's the Difference?

Merging and fusing both improve equipment, but they solve different problems.

### When Should You Merge Duplicates?

Suppose your primary weapon already benefits from a rune, works with your hero's item types, and sits in a useful position on the equipment grid.

Finding another copy of that weapon is an opportunity to improve the existing piece without dismantling the arrangement around it.

Place two matching items of the same level together to merge them. Item levels can reach **Level 10**, and duplicates are especially valuable when they improve a weapon or support piece you expect to keep using.

Do not automatically sell a duplicate just because your equipped grid is full. Check whether it can upgrade an existing item first.

However, duplicates are not all equally valuable. Saving another copy of equipment you plan to replace can waste inventory space that would be better used for healing, a useful item type, or a future fusion.

Keep duplicates for pieces that are central to your current build, rather than collecting every matching item.

## How Do You Use the Fusion Forge in Dungeonbound?

The Fusion Forge appears as a dungeon encounter. It allows you to combine compatible equipment into a new item.

Fusion Forges are not guaranteed during a run. Keep your equipped items functional rather than saving a weak combination in the hope of finding one. For floor-by-floor equipment decisions during your first climb, follow the <Link href="/dungeonbound/act-1">Climb Act 1 walkthrough</Link>.

When you reach one:

1. **Select two compatible items** from your equipment or inventory.
2. Place them into the Forge's input slots.
3. Examine the resulting item preview.
4. Switch between the available variants when the Forge offers more than one result.
5. Compare the finished item's shape, stats, abilities, tags, and other relevant properties.
6. Confirm the fusion only when the result improves your equipped build.

The Forge combines the selected items, so the original pieces are no longer available separately afterward.

### Which Item Should You Use as the Base?

The base affects the resulting equipment's identity and shape. When the Forge offers two variants, each can preserve a different ingredient as the foundation of the new item.

Consider combining a large weapon with a smaller accessory.

A weapon-shaped result might keep the role you need for attacking. An accessory-shaped result might preserve valuable effects while freeing space occupied by the original weapon.

The smaller result is not automatically better. If it loses a trigger or changes the equipment types your build relies on, the freed cells may not compensate for the lost effect.

Compare both variants before making the decision.

### Why Does the Forge Offer Two Variants?

Different variants can change what you retain from the original equipment.

The most important comparison is often not the total Damage or Block. It is whether the finished piece still performs its intended job.

For example, combining two items that provide attack-related effects might create a useful hybrid. But choosing a different variant could remove the Bleed application, change the item's shape, or make the result less compatible with the rest of your loadout.

The Dawnhammer Earring preview above shows what happens when Citrine Earring is selected as the base and combined with Dawnhammer. The proposed result lists Damage, Charge, and Vulnerable effects, but only one Gem slot. Compare that with the Dawnhammer-base variant before deciding which shape and effects better suit your equipped items.

That is a useful way to approach the Forge: **try the candidate combinations, compare the results, and reject the ones that do not solve a real problem.**

You do not have to fuse simply because a Forge appears.

## What Does a Fused Item Keep or Lose?

A fused item can inherit properties from its ingredients, but it should not be treated as a perfect copy of both.

Inspect these parts of the preview before confirming.

### 1. Shape and Equipment Type

The new item's footprint determines whether it still fits the board.

A large item may carry useful stats, but those benefits are less attractive if equipping it requires removing several smaller pieces.

When choosing a variant, try fitting the expected shape into your board before committing.

Pay particular attention to:

- Important items that must remain adjacent.
- Equipment positioned around a rune.
- Pieces that contribute to your hero's type bonuses.
- Your remaining space for healing and defensive equipment.

Saving several cells is valuable only when you can use the freed space productively.

### 2. Stats and Abilities

Read the actual finished item's values rather than assuming every stat and ability from both ingredients transfers unchanged.

Check both the numerical result and the effects listed below it.

An item providing slightly less Damage might still be an improvement if it carries a valuable healing effect and creates room for another offensive piece.

Conversely, an item with higher raw stats may be worse if the fusion removes the trigger that previously activated your damage combination.

The preview is especially important when an ingredient has multiple effects. Do not assume all of them will survive.

### 3. Item Tags and Hero Affinity

An item can be useful because of the types it contributes to a build, even if its personal stats are unimpressive.

This matters for heroes with specific equipment affinities.

For example, a Valkyrie can benefit from Holy and Lightning equipment. A fused item that retains the relevant types may support several other pieces at once.

Check the finished item's tags before replacing the originals.

If the fusion removes an important type bonus, calculate the trade against the *whole build*. You might gain Damage on one item while losing bonuses elsewhere.

### 4. Curses and Other Drawbacks

Fusing a cursed item is not a reliable way to remove its penalty.

Curses can carry into the resulting equipment, and combining two cursed pieces can leave the new item with drawbacks from both.

Before using a cursed ingredient, inspect the finished item's negative properties.

A compact hybrid is not worth much if it gains a harmful penalty that undermines the rest of your loadout.

Instead of relying on two separate equipped items with their own activation behavior, the finished piece uses one displayed interval for its timed effects. That can concentrate several abilities into one activation, but it also changes when those effects contribute to a fight.

### What If One Ingredient Activated Sooner?

Compare the original items' activation intervals with the new timer before confirming.

If an important defensive or interrupting effect previously activated earlier, moving it onto a slower shared timer can leave your character exposed. The result may have better stats while producing its most useful effect too late.

Keep the ingredients separate when the original timing is more important than the space or stats gained through fusion.

## How Do Gems and Runes Work With Fused Items?

Gems and runes influence equipment differently.

A **gem** provides an additional effect or stat modification through the item's gem system. A **rune** occupies part of the loadout grid and benefits the equipped item that covers the relevant area.

Both can change the value of a fusion.

### What Happens to Gems When You Fuse Equipment?

Gems belonging to the parent items are carried into the fusion result, but **the finished item has its own gem slot capacity**.

Do not assume that combining two socketed pieces creates an item with the total number of both parents' slots.

Examine the result's available sockets and gem effects before confirming.

This is particularly important in Climb mode, where socket availability can limit your future equipment choices.

In Incremental mode, gem application works differently and does not use the same slot restrictions. Keep that distinction in mind when applying advice from one mode to the other.

### Why Is My Rune Powering the Wrong Item?

Runes occupy **2×2 areas** of the loadout grid. The item covering most of a rune's area receives its effect.

Merely touching the rune is not enough.

This becomes a problem when a fusion changes equipment shapes.

Imagine that your main weapon originally covered most of a rune. After replacing another piece with a larger fused item, you rearrange the board and accidentally move the weapon away.

The rune remains in place, but its effect may now apply to a different item.

The weapon still looks equipped, and the board may appear full and efficient, yet your strongest piece has lost an important bonus.

After every significant fusion:

1. Reopen your equipment grid.
2. Check which item covers each important rune.
3. Move or rotate equipment if the wrong piece receives the effect.
4. Recheck adjacency and type bonuses after repositioning everything.

A fused item should be judged in its final equipped position, not only in the Forge preview.

## How Does the Mega Forge Work in Dungeonbound?

The **Mega Forge** combines **two to four touching items** into one piece of equipment.

Unlike a regular Fusion Forge, it is designed for consolidating several items at once.

The result takes a shape based on the largest ingredient and can carry a combination of useful properties from the selected pieces.

### When Is the Mega Forge Worth Using?

The strongest reason to use it is to reduce the amount of space occupied by pieces that are already working together.

For example, a defensive build might contain several items contributing Block, Health, and a useful equipment effect.

Combining them could create room for another piece that helps the build survive longer or deal more damage.

### What Should You Check Before Confirming a Mega Forge?

The Mega Forge preview indicates what the combination will lose.

That warning matters because a multi-item combination may not retain every benefit that each ingredient currently provides.

Before confirming, check:

- Which abilities and stats remain on the result.
- Whether important equipment effects will disappear.
- Whether the resulting shape fits your intended layout.
- Whether you lose useful type bonuses or rune coverage.
- Whether the additional free cells can actually hold useful equipment.

Do not feed all your strongest items into the Mega Forge just because the resulting number is large.

A Mega Forge can be excellent for turning several awkward pieces into one usable item. It can also remove the separate effects that made those pieces valuable.

If the preview shows a major loss to your primary damage or survival setup, keeping the existing equipment may be better.

## Fusion Forge, Affinity Forge, Upgrade Altar, and Dismantling Bench

Not every equipment encounter should be used for fusion.

Each station solves a different problem.

### Upgrade Altar — Improve an Existing Item

Use an Upgrade Altar when you want to strengthen a piece without replacing it or finding a matching duplicate.

It is especially valuable for an item that already fits your grid and contributes an important effect.

Some upgraded items become difficult to improve through duplicates alone, particularly after multiple fusions have changed their identity.

In that situation, an Upgrade Altar offers a straightforward way to keep investing in the same equipment.

### Affinity Forge — Change an Item's Type

The Affinity Forge changes one of an item's types to one belonging to your hero.

This is useful when an otherwise strong item does not contribute to your hero's preferred equipment types.

Before using it, check the types already present in your build.

If your hero benefits from Lightning equipment, changing a suitable piece to Lightning may improve more than the selected item alone.

However, replacing an existing type can also interfere with another bonus. Compare your currently active combinations before making the change.

### Dismantling Bench — Turn Equipment Into Stats

The Dismantling Bench allows you to sacrifice an item and gain stats from it.

This is a different decision from Fusion.

Instead of combining the item with another piece of equipment, you give up the item itself in exchange for additional character stats.

It is useful when an item has strong numerical value but no longer has a practical place in your equipped build.

A large, awkward piece with useful stats may be worth dismantling if keeping it would prevent you from equipping several smaller items with better effects.

Do not dismantle equipment that still provides a crucial trigger or type bonus just because its shape is inconvenient.

### Sanctifying Altar — Add a Blessing

A Sanctifying Altar can improve an item through a blessing.

Choose equipment that you expect to keep rather than investing in a temporary piece that will soon be replaced.

When deciding between several items, prioritize one whose improved property supports your current build.

These stations are not interchangeable. The correct choice depends on whether you need **more power on an existing item, a new equipment type, additional permanent stats, or fewer items occupying your grid**.

## Can You Fuse Potions Into Weapons or Other Items?

Yes. Potions can be combined with other equipment through the Forge.

Potions no longer disappear permanently after being used. Their Ammo refills between battles, allowing a useful potion to contribute repeatedly across a run.

Fusing a potion into another item can transfer its effect while freeing the cells the potion previously occupied.

This is particularly useful when your equipment grid is crowded but your build still needs healing or another survival effect.

### When Should You Fuse a Potion?

Consider potion fusion when:

- Your build repeatedly loses health before its damage effects become effective.
- A useful potion occupies cells needed for another item.
- The finished piece preserves the potion effect you need.
- You can keep the resulting item equipped without breaking stronger combinations.

Inspect the finished item's Ammo and activation behavior rather than assuming the potion will activate independently exactly as it did before.

### When Should You Keep the Potion Separate?

Keep it separate if the fusion would remove important equipment effects, introduce an unfavorable shape, or make the combined item less useful.

A free inventory slot is not always worth sacrificing a reliable source of healing.

## Why Is My Build Weaker After Fusing Items?

If a fusion makes your build weaker, compare the finished item with the equipment it replaced before changing anything else.

### Check What the Fusion Removed

Start with the original ingredients and the finished item's tooltip.

- **Missing abilities:** Check whether Bleed, healing, Charge, or another important effect survived.
- **Changed timing:** Compare the new timer with the activation intervals of the original pieces.
- **Lost item types:** Check whether the result still contributes to the bonuses your loadout uses.
- **Reduced Gem capacity:** Inspect the finished item's sockets and retained Gem effects.

If an essential effect is missing, adding more raw Damage may not restore what the original combination provided.

### Check What Changed on the Grid

A fused item can work correctly while weakening the surrounding equipment.

Reopen your loadout and check whether:

1. An important item lost its adjacency bonus.
2. A different piece now covers most of a rune.
3. The new shape forced a useful item out of the equipped grid.
4. The finished item still benefits from the types and effects around it.

Compare the complete loadout rather than judging the new item's stats alone.

### Check Whether the New Item Solves the Original Problem

If the goal was to save space, confirm that the freed cells can hold something useful.

If the goal was stronger offense, confirm that the finished item still activates the effects responsible for your damage.

If the goal was better survival, check whether the fusion retained the healing, Block, or cleansing effects you needed.

A fusion is only an improvement when the final equipped setup performs its intended job better than the original combination.

If your equipment still appears to function correctly but you keep losing to rivals, use the <Link href="/dungeonbound/act-1">Climb Act 1 walkthrough</Link> for fight-specific survival and opponent analysis.

## Which Items Should You Fuse First?

Start with a combination whose result you can evaluate against your next fight.

For example, combining Rowan Cloak with Wooden Buckler can consolidate useful defensive properties. The result is worth keeping when its shape fits and the remaining equipment still provides enough damage and recovery.

If your primary weapon is already working, check for a matching duplicate or an Upgrade Altar before sacrificing it at a Fusion Forge. Improving its level may be sufficient without changing the ability that carries your damage.

When a Forge offers a more complicated result, compare what the finished equipment retains with the items you would remove. If both variants weaken an essential trigger or no longer fit the grid, leave the ingredients separate.

## What Should You Check Before Every Fusion?

Before confirming the result, make these checks:

1. **Purpose:** Does this fusion improve damage, survival, or equipment space?
2. **Variant:** Have you compared both possible results?
3. **Abilities:** Does the new item retain the effects you actually use?
4. **Timing:** Are timer-based effects still activating when you need them?
5. **Shape:** Can the finished item fit without removing more valuable equipment?
6. **Types:** Will the result preserve useful Affinity or set bonuses?
7. **Gems and Curses:** Are there transferred effects or penalties that change the decision?
8. **Runes:** Will the equipped result still allow the right item to receive its rune bonus?

Once the item is fused, rearrange the board and inspect your stats.

After forging, equip the finished item and confirm that its expected effects still activate. A preview can show stronger stats, but the actual grid position, shared timer, and rune recipient determine whether the result works as intended.
