## How Do You Start Climb Act 1?

Select **The Climb** from the game's modes, choose Act 1, and pick an available hero.

This is different from the Incremental campaign. Your Climb equipment belongs to the current run, so you cannot rely on permanent campaign upgrades to make up for poor choices.

Your hero determines your starting equipment, affinities, and other advantages, but the items you find during the run still matter.

### Which Hero Should You Choose?

The successful Act 1 clear used the **Paladin**. After earlier attempts with Haru failed, the player switched heroes and built around immediate damage, Regen, and several status effects.

That run eventually combined Burn, Poison, Freeze, and defensive effects rather than relying on damage alone.

For your first clear, choose a hero whose starting equipment gives you a practical way to survive the opening fights. Prioritize enough damage to defeat early opponents, then add recovery before stronger rivals begin overwhelming your HP.

Haru can still complete Act 1, but several earlier attempts struggled against Potion Merchants and other fast-damaging opponents. If your build takes too long to become effective, changing your defensive equipment may matter more than increasing its eventual damage.

You do not need to unlock the Paladin before attempting Act 1. The important goal is to build around the strengths of whichever hero you can currently use.

### What Should You Look for Before the First Fight?

Inspect your hero's starting item and determine what is missing.

If your starting equipment already provides healing, an early damage increase may be more valuable than another small source of recovery.

If you begin with decent damage but little protection, prioritize a reliable defensive item before the difficult rivals begin appearing.

Avoid filling the equipment grid with unrelated pieces simply because there is room for them.

Keep enough flexibility to reposition items as your build develops.

## How Do Hearts Work in Dungeonbound Act 1?

You begin a normal Climb with **10 Hearts**.

Losing a fight costs Hearts, and the penalty increases on deeper floors.

This changes how you should approach risk.

An early defeat is frustrating, but it usually leaves room to recover. Losing on Floor 5 or later costs three Hearts, so a few mistakes can end the run quickly.

### Should You Restart After Losing an Early Fight?

Not automatically.

First check what caused the defeat.

If your build is nearly functional and the next shop or upgrade can correct a specific weakness, continuing may still be worthwhile.

If your equipment cannot deal enough damage, you have no useful recovery, and the same problem persists after several encounters, continuing without making changes is unlikely to help.

Before deciding, inspect:

1. How many Hearts remain.
2. Whether the last fight exposed a specific weakness.
3. Whether you have Gold or equipment that can address it.
4. Whether your next available encounters offer a realistic opportunity to improve.

The important question is not simply whether you lost. It is whether the next version of your build can avoid losing for the same reason.

## Floors 1–2: What Should You Buy and Upgrade First?

The opening floors are where you establish the build's direction.

At this stage, do not sacrifice reliable damage to collect several effects that only become useful much later.

### Get Enough Damage to Finish Early Fights

In a successful Act 1 run, the opening equipment decisions favored Crit, a shield, and a lantern, but early damage remained a problem.

When offered a choice between more Regen and additional damage, the immediate damage increase was selected because enemies were taking too long to defeat.

That is a useful decision rule.

If your character is surviving but barely damaging opponents, another defensive item may extend a losing battle without solving it.

Look for equipment that directly improves your main attack, provides a useful On Attack effect, or helps your current weapon trigger more effectively.

### Add Recovery Before Damage Starts Accumulating

Do not interpret damage priority as a reason to ignore healing.

The same successful run soon added Regen after early fights exposed how quickly Poison and other effects could threaten HP.

A build needs both:

- Enough offense to avoid giving enemies unlimited time to attack.
- Enough recovery or protection to survive while its offense works.

If your first few fights are already draining HP rapidly, take an accessible healing option instead of waiting for a rare defensive item.

### Do Not Spend All Your Gold on Random Equipment

A shop can offer several attractive items that have no relationship to your current build.

Before buying, decide what you need:

- More damage from your primary weapon.
- A useful item with your hero's preferred type.
- A Duplicate that upgrades an existing piece.
- Healing, Block, or protection against a recurring status.

If none of the shop items addresses that need, keeping the Gold is a valid choice.

Shop rerolls become more expensive each time. Avoid repeatedly rolling for an ideal item while leaving yourself unable to purchase a useful alternative.

## Floors 3–4: How Do You Make Your Build Work Together?

By the middle of the first Act, the challenge shifts from finding useful items to making them operate as one build.

You may already have enough equipment to fill most of the grid. That does not mean every piece is helping.

### Rearrange Your Loadout After Important Pickups

During the successful Paladin run, new equipment repeatedly forced changes to the loadout. Temporary pieces were replaced, while useful damage and recovery effects were kept in the equipped grid.

Use the same approach when your inventory begins filling up:

1. Keep the items currently providing your main damage and recovery.
2. Check whether a new pickup offers a useful effect that your build lacks.
3. Remove equipment that no longer contributes enough to justify its space.
4. Rearrange the remaining pieces before entering the next fight.

Do not replace a working item simply because a new one has higher rarity. The useful comparison is what your equipped loadout gains or loses.

For the full rules governing equipment shapes, adjacency, and runes, see the <Link href="/dungeonbound/fusion-guide">Dungeonbound Fusion Guide</Link>.


### Start Strengthening a Few Core Items

Use available upgrades on pieces you are likely to keep.

In the successful run, upgrades were used while the equipment arrangement gradually shifted toward stronger recovery and status effects.

If an item is responsible for most of your damage or protection, improving it can be more useful than buying another unrelated piece.

Duplicates are worth checking before selling, especially when they can upgrade something central to the loadout.

For the differences between Merge, Fusion Forge, and item upgrades, use the <Link href="/dungeonbound/fusion-guide">Dungeonbound Fusion Guide</Link>.

### Which Encounter Should You Take on Floors 3–4?

By Floor 3, another defeat costs two Hearts. The next encounter should help correct a weakness before those losses become even more expensive on Floor 5.

If your main weapon already performs well, an upgrade opportunity may be more valuable than buying another unrelated item. If the current loadout lacks healing or sufficient damage, a shop gives you a chance to find the missing effect. A Forge is useful when two existing pieces can be combined without sacrificing an essential ability.

Compare the Gold offered by each route with the opportunity you would pass up. Taking a larger payout is less valuable if it leaves the same equipment problem unresolved before the next rival.

## Floors 5–7: How Do You Survive Stronger Rivals?

From Floor 5 onward, a defeat costs three Hearts.

You now have much less room for an equipment combination that fails to perform.

The most dangerous mistake is assuming that stronger-looking items automatically produce a stronger build.

### Build Damage That Can Actually Finish Opponents

A successful Act 1 run developed overlapping **Poison, Burn, and Freeze** effects while continuing to improve its immediate damage.

These effects served different purposes.

Poison and Burn provided additional ways to wear down opponents, while Freeze helped interfere with enemy attacks.

The important part was not collecting every status effect. It was making sure the equipped items could apply the chosen effects consistently.

A weak, slow item that occasionally applies Poison is not necessarily better than a stronger piece that contributes immediately.

Check whether an item's trigger actually activates often enough to help.

### Use the Mega Forge to Strengthen Your Current Build

During the successful Paladin run, a Mega Forge was used to create **Colossal Stormplate Cuirass (Lv 3)**.

The finished armor provided:

- **+26 Max HP**
- **+66 Damage**
- **+15 Block**
- **+3 Speed**

It also had a 25% chance to inflict Stun on an attacker when hit.

Its offensive effects applied Burn on attack. Against a burning enemy, the armor provided additional Damage during the battle, with chances to inflict Poison and gain Regen.

### Watch for Potion-Based Rivals

Potion Merchants were a repeated source of failed runs.

One dangerous attempt developed substantial Crit and Bleed damage, but the opponent's consumable effects overwhelmed the available recovery.

The lesson is not that every Potion Merchant must be avoided.

It is that their board needs inspection before you assume a strong damage number will win the fight.

Check whether the opponent has:

- Repeated healing effects.
- Poison or other damage-over-time effects.
- Enough protection to survive your opening attacks.
- Control effects that interfere with your primary items.

If your damage develops slowly, a rival with strong healing and status pressure may outlast your build.

Preserve enough survival to reach the point where your main effects become effective.

## Floor 8: How Do You Beat the Act 1 Boss?

Floor 8 is the final goal for your first Act 1 clear.

The most important preparation is to arrive with a build that already works.

Do not use the final floor to redesign your equipment around a completely new strategy unless the current setup has an obvious, fixable problem.

### What Should You Check Before the Final Fight?

**First, check your remaining Hearts.**

A late defeat is expensive. If you have very few Hearts left, avoid gambling on a combination you have not evaluated.

**Second, check your damage timing.**

Make sure your primary weapon and status effects activate reliably.

If most of your damage requires a long buildup, consider whether your defensive equipment can keep you alive long enough for that buildup to matter.

**Third, check your recovery.**

Inspect healing, Regen, Block, and any effects that help you survive repeated attacks.

A large Block value is not a complete answer to every threat. Status effects that pressure HP or interrupt equipment can still be dangerous.

**Fourth, check your equipment layout.**

An upgrade or fusion performed near the end of the run can accidentally disrupt a useful arrangement.

Look for misplaced equipment, lost adjacency benefits, and important effects that no longer activate.

### What Does a Successful Act 1 Clear Look Like?

After winning the Floor 8 encounter, the result screen confirms **ACT CLEARED — The Forest**.

It also shows that **Act II: The Castle** is now open and that its items can appear in future Climbs.

## Why Do You Keep Losing in Dungeonbound Act 1?

If you repeatedly fail on the same floors, identify which part of your build stops working.

### You Die Before Your Strong Items Activate

A loadout may have impressive damage effects that activate too slowly.

Check your primary item's trigger timing and the amount of damage you take before it begins contributing.

Possible adjustments include replacing an unnecessary late-activating item, adding earlier damage, or preserving more opening defense.

### Your Damage Is High, but Rivals Keep Healing

A high damage stat is not enough if an opponent restores HP faster than you deal effective damage.

Inspect whether your attacks and status effects are actually reducing the opponent's health over time.

If the fight repeatedly resets in the rival's favor, your build may need stronger sustained pressure rather than another small increase in raw damage.

### Poison Keeps Killing You Through Block

A defense-heavy build can still fail when Poison repeatedly damages HP.

Inspect the enemy's effects and the damage breakdown rather than assuming that increasing Block will fix everything.

## How Do You Check What Killed You?

After a failed Climb, the results screen provides three options: **BATTLE LOG**, **DAMAGE**, and **WATCH REPLAY**.

Use them to investigate the fight before replacing your equipment.

### Check the Damage Breakdown

Open **DAMAGE** to examine which attacks and effects contributed to the loss.

If Poison caused the defeat, determine whether you need more recovery or protection against that effect rather than automatically buying additional Block.

If ordinary attacks caused most of the damage, consider whether your current defensive equipment is sufficient.

### Review the Battle Log

Open **BATTLE LOG** and inspect the sequence of attacks and triggered effects.

Look for items that contributed very little, effects that activated too late, and moments when healing or protection failed to keep up with incoming damage.

This helps distinguish a weak item from an otherwise useful item that did not activate at the right time.

### Watch the Replay

Use **WATCH REPLAY** to review how the opponent's build performed.

Pay attention to whether the rival gained healing, applied status effects, or dealt substantial damage before your own equipment became effective.

Then make one targeted change to your loadout before the next encounter.

A failed fight is most useful when it tells you what to change, rather than simply encouraging you to buy stronger-looking items.

## What Should You Do After Beating Act 1?

### Claim Your Completed Quests

If you won with the Paladin, the **Holy Fire** quest is complete. Open the Quest menu and claim the reward.

Check for other completed objectives before starting your next climb.

For the complete achievement requirements, use the <Link href="/dungeonbound/achievements">Dungeonbound achievements checklist</Link>.

### Continue to Endless or Start Another Act?

Choose **Continue to Endless** if you want to keep improving the current build and test it against stronger encounters.

If your goal was to unlock the next Act, you have already achieved it. You can stop the current run and return to the new content.

The later Endless floors are not required to earn your first Act 1 clear.

If you want to play the separate persistent campaign, follow the <Link href="/dungeonbound/walkthrough">Dungeonbound Story walkthrough</Link>.
