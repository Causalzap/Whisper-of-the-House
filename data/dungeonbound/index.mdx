In Dungeonbound, a sword sitting in your bag does not contribute to combat. Move it into the upper Equipment grid, start the battle, and watch your character attack automatically. Item placement matters from the very first fight, long before you find complicated weapons or powerful fusions.

Your choice of mode also changes what happens after a defeat. Joshua keeps developing in The Long Haul, while a Climb uses a temporary build and limited Hearts. Choose the mode that matches the kind of progression you want before starting your first run.

## Which Dungeonbound Game Mode Should You Start With?

Dungeonbound offers different ways to progress. They use similar equipment and combat systems, but what happens after a defeat is different.

### Should You Start With Incremental or Roguelite?

Choose **The Long Haul** if you want to develop one character over multiple attempts.

This is the Incremental campaign. You begin in The Forest, keep building your equipment and permanent stats, and eventually progress through five Story dungeons.

You are expected to lose some fights while your character is weak. A defeat sends you back to improve the character rather than forcing you to abandon everything you earned.

Choose **The Climb** if you prefer building a character for one run.

You begin with a new loadout, collect and buy equipment, fight rival builds, and attempt to reach the Act's final floor.

The Climb also has Hearts. Losing encounters consumes them, and running out ends the attempt.

A completed Act 1 Climb requires beating Floor 8.

### When Should You Try Co-op?

Co-op Climbs support two to four players.

Use this mode when you want to coordinate equipment choices and survive encounters as a team.

Do not assume that a strong solo build automatically guarantees a successful Co-op run. Your teammates' survival matters too.

If you are still learning which equipment belongs on the active grid and when to replace an item, a few solo attempts can make those decisions easier to understand.

### What About Ranked, Daily, and Versus?

Dungeonbound also includes competitive and challenge-oriented modes.

These offer different objectives from the persistent Story campaign.

For your first session, choose a normal Story or Climb run so you can learn equipment placement, item rewards, and the basic progression loop before adding further restrictions.

## How Do You Start Your First Dungeonbound Run?

A straightforward introduction is to begin The Long Haul with **Joshua**.

Joshua starts with a sword and a shield, giving you both an attack and a basic defense before you acquire any additional equipment.

### Step 1 — Select Joshua and Start Run

Open the Incremental campaign, select Joshua, and check the character screen.

You will see his stats on the left, the Equipment grid on the right, and the Inventory below it.

The initial screen also shows the upgrades and Start Run controls.

Check that Joshua's starting equipment is in the active Equipment grid.

Then choose **Start Run**.

The opening Story sequence leads into The Forest, where Joshua begins searching for answers after his village is destroyed.

### Step 2 — Start the First Battle

The battle does not require you to manually swing your weapon.

Use **Start Battle**, then watch Joshua and the enemy attack automatically.

His equipped items determine the stats and abilities available during combat.

Watch the enemy's HP and your own HP and Block while the fight progresses.

If Joshua wins, inspect the rewards before continuing.

### Step 3 — Collect Useful Loot

After a battle, you may receive equipment or another reward.

Do not automatically sell a new item because its rarity is low.

For example, an early cloak that adds HP or protection may be more useful than another weapon you cannot currently equip.

Check what the item does and whether you have space for it.

Move the item into the Equipment grid if you want its equipped effects to help in battle.

Otherwise, store it in your bag until you decide what to do with it.

### Step 4 — Continue Toward the Next Encounter

After handling the loot, continue to the next event or battle.

At route choices, look for rewards that solve a problem with Joshua's current build.

If he survives comfortably but kills enemies slowly, you need more effective damage.

If he deals enough damage but repeatedly dies, recovery and protection become more important.

You do not need to visit every possible shop or Forge to make progress.

Choose the option that will help you survive the next fight.

### Step 5 — Return After a Defeat

Your first run may end quickly.

A chest in the opening Forest route can turn out to be a Mimic, ending Joshua's attempt before he has earned enough levels to survive it.

After the defeat, check the equipment and resources Joshua retained. Buy an available upgrade if you can afford one; otherwise, begin another Forest attempt with the progress you already have. You do not need to reset the character.

## How Do Equipment and Inventory Work in Dungeonbound?

The most important early distinction is between an item you own and an item that is actively equipped.

Dungeonbound uses a grid where equipment occupies different shapes and sizes.

### Equipment vs. Inventory: What's the Difference?

**Equipment** is the active grid.

Items placed there contribute their equipped effects during combat.

**Inventory** is your storage area.

An item in storage is available to keep, sell, or use later, but it does not provide its normal equipped effects.

This matters when you find a useful ring, shield, or healing item.

If the item remains in your bag, Joshua will not suddenly gain its abilities just because you picked it up.

Move it onto the Equipment grid before starting the next fight.

### How Do You Rotate an Item?

Some equipment occupies multiple grid cells.

While carrying an item, use the displayed rotate control to change its orientation.

On mouse controls, right-clicking while carrying an item rotates it.

Rotation can help a sword, shield, or irregularly shaped piece fit into a space that initially looks too small.

However, rotating one item may prevent another from fitting.

Before removing equipment, check which effects you would lose.

An empty space is not worth creating if it requires removing the only healing item keeping your character alive.

### Why Can't You Equip Another Weapon?

There are two possible restrictions.

First, your active grid may not have enough empty cells for the new item.

Second, your character may have reached the current limit on equipped weapons.

These are different problems.

Expanding your Equipment grid provides more physical space.

Upgrades that increase weapon capacity address the number of weapons your character can equip.

Do not spend Gold expanding storage when the real problem is an equipped-weapon limit.

### How Do You Get More Equipment Space?

The Equipment grid has unlockable rows.

At Joshua's starting progression state, the first additional row is displayed at **800 Gold**.

Unlocking a row adds active Equipment space.

This is different from **Bigger Bag**, which expands storage for spare items.

If you are holding a useful piece of armor but cannot fit it onto the active grid, an Equipment row may be more valuable than additional Inventory space.

If you do not have a piece worth equipping immediately, spending Gold on damage or survival upgrades can be the better early decision.

### What Happens to Loot You Leave Behind?

Read the controls on the loot screen before continuing.

Items left in the reward area can be sold automatically when you move on.

Move anything you intend to keep into your Equipment grid or bag.

Before using a bulk sale option, review the items it will affect. Move any equipment you intend to keep into the appropriate inventory area before confirming.

## How Does Combat Work in Dungeonbound?

Dungeonbound's battles are automatic, but they are not independent of your decisions.

Your character attacks according to its current stats and equipped effects.

That means a small change to the board can completely change the outcome of a fight.

### What Do Damage and Speed Do?

**Damage** influences how hard your attacks hit.

**Speed** affects how quickly your character attacks.

A weapon with high Damage may still perform poorly if it activates too slowly or leaves your character without enough protection.

Likewise, a fast build with very low damage may struggle to finish stronger opponents.

Look at how long each fight lasts.

If Joshua survives but cannot defeat an enemy, improve damage or the effects that contribute to it.

If the enemy defeats Joshua before his equipment has time to work, focus on surviving the opening attacks.

### How Are HP, Block, and Healing Different?

**HP** is your health.

When it reaches zero, the character loses the battle.

**Block** provides another layer of protection, but it does not protect against every effect in the same way.

Healing and **Regen** can restore HP during a fight when their activation conditions are met.

For example, an item that grants Regen on attack depends on that effect actually activating.

If your character dies before the item contributes enough recovery, simply having the item equipped will not solve the problem.

You may need earlier healing, more HP, better protection, or enough damage to shorten the fight.

### Why Are Burn and Poison Dangerous?

Status effects can change which defensive stats matter.

**Burn** deals damage over time and can affect both Block and HP.

**Poison** ignores Block.

This is why a character with a strong shield can still lose a fight against an opponent that repeatedly applies Poison.

When you lose, check whether the enemy defeated you through direct attacks or through a status effect.

The response should be different.

Against repeated direct attacks, Block and HP can be valuable.

Against Poison, recovery and ending the fight sooner may be more useful than stacking additional Block alone.

### How Do You Find Out What an Enemy Does?

Inspect the enemy's displayed stats and abilities.

Some enemies show status effects or attack properties that explain why their damage is different from the previous fight.

The battle interface also provides combat information that can help you identify when effects activated.

In The Climb, the defeat screen can offer **Battle Log**, **Damage**, and **Watch Replay**.

Use those options to identify whether your main weapon, recovery effect, or defensive equipment actually performed as expected.

## What Should You Upgrade First in Dungeonbound?

After your first defeat in The Long Haul, return to Joshua's character screen and check the available permanent upgrades.

Your first purchase should address the problem that ended the previous attempt.

### Which Early Upgrade Should You Choose?

Three useful options are:

- **Sharpened Blade:** Increases base damage. Choose this when enemies survive too long and keep attacking Joshua.
- **Iron Constitution:** Increases maximum HP. Choose this when Joshua loses health too quickly.
- **Reinforced Plate:** Adds Block. Choose this when repeated direct attacks overwhelm his defenses.

Damage is a useful early priority when Joshua survives long enough to attack but cannot finish enemies.

However, if Poison is killing him despite having Block, increasing HP or recovery may be more helpful than buying another defensive upgrade.

### Should You Upgrade Stats or Unlock More Equipment Space?

Check whether you actually have another useful item ready to equip.

If your grid is full and a stronger weapon or defensive item is waiting in storage, unlocking another Equipment row may provide an immediate improvement.

If you have no useful item to fill that space, spend your Gold on the stat that will help your next fight.

As Joshua levels up, additional upgrades requiring Skill Points become available.

You do not need to unlock every option before returning to The Forest.

## What Should You Buy, Sell, or Keep During a Run?

When you find new equipment, compare it with what your character already has equipped.

A stronger-looking item is not always a better choice if it takes too much space or removes an important effect.

### Which Item Should You Choose?

Use three checks before keeping or buying equipment:

1. **Does it solve a problem?** Choose damage when enemies take too long to defeat, or recovery when your character is losing HP too quickly.
2. **Can you equip it?** Check the item's shape and whether your current grid has enough room.
3. **What would you lose?** Replacing a useful shield or healing item may weaken the complete loadout.

For example, a small ring that grants Regen can be more useful than another large weapon when your existing damage is already sufficient.

### When Should You Sell Spare Equipment?

Keep an extra item when you have a specific use for it, such as replacing weaker equipment or using it at a Forge.

Otherwise, selling an item you cannot use may be more valuable than keeping it in storage indefinitely.

Do not sell equipment that is currently responsible for an important effect without checking what will replace it.

### Should You Reroll the Shop?

Decide what you need before rerolling.

If the shop does not offer anything useful, compare the reroll cost with your remaining Gold.

Stop when continued rerolls would leave you unable to afford an important purchase.

For floor-by-floor shop decisions and Gold management during your first Climb, use the <Link href="/dungeonbound/act-1">Dungeonbound Climb Act 1 guide</Link>.

## What's the Difference Between Merge and Fusion in Dungeonbound?

Found a second copy of an item you already use? Matching equipment of the required level can be **Merged** into a higher-level piece.

A **Fusion Forge** offers a different choice: combine two compatible items to create one hybrid. The finished equipment may save space, but its abilities and shape can change.

Use Merge when you want to strengthen an existing piece. Consider Fusion when combining two items could preserve useful effects while making more room for other equipment.

### Should You Use the First Fusion Forge You Find?

Not automatically.

Before confirming a Fusion, inspect the result and check whether it still provides the abilities your character needs.

For example, combining two defensive items is only worthwhile if the finished equipment leaves you with enough protection and recovery for the next fight.

If the result removes your only reliable healing effect or will not fit your current grid, keep the original items.

For Fusion variants, Mega Forge, equipment shapes, runes, Gems, and inherited effects, see the <Link href="/dungeonbound/fusion-guide">Dungeonbound Fusion Guide</Link>.

## What Happens After You Die or Clear an Act?

The result depends on whether you are playing The Long Haul or The Climb.

### Does Dying Erase Your Progress?

In **The Long Haul**, Joshua keeps developing through repeated attempts. Return to the character screen, improve his equipment or permanent stats, and try again.

In **The Climb**, losing all your Hearts ends the current run. Your next attempt starts with a new run-specific build.

For the persistent campaign, follow the <Link href="/dungeonbound/walkthrough">five-dungeon Story progression</Link>.

### What Does Beating Act 1 Unlock?

Defeat Floor 8 in The Climb to complete **Act I: The Forest**.

The completion screen confirms **ACT CLEARED** and opens **Act II: The Castle**.

Items from The Castle can then appear in future Climbs.

You can also choose **Continue to Endless** if you want to keep developing the current build beyond the initial Act clear.

For Hearts, rival encounters, and the Floor 8 victory, see the <Link href="/dungeonbound/act-1">Climb Act 1 walkthrough</Link>.

## How Do You Unlock More Heroes in Dungeonbound?

In The Climb, many heroes become available after completing specific Quests.

Some require combat milestones, shop actions, or reaching a certain floor. Others are tied to later Acts and named bosses.

### Where Do You Claim a Hero Unlock?

Open **QUESTS** and check the reward for your completed objective.

For example, **Quick Paws** unlocks Snuffy after reaching 20 Speed in a Climb.

Once the requirement is complete, select **CLAIM**, then return to the hero selection screen.

If the character remains locked, check whether the Quest is still unclaimed and whether its reward is actually a hero.

Not every Quest rewards a character. Some provide equipment, name tags, dialogue, or other unlocks.

For the complete character requirements, including Story classes and later-Act heroes, use the <Link href="/dungeonbound/hero-unlocks">Dungeonbound Hero Unlocks guide</Link>.

### What Should You Do After Claiming a New Hero?

Return to character selection and choose the newly available hero before starting a Climb.

A later Floor 8 victory with that character may satisfy a separate achievement. Claiming the character itself does not complete its victory challenge.

You can check which character victories remain unfinished in the <Link href="/dungeonbound/achievements">Dungeonbound achievements checklist</Link>.

## Why Are You Still Losing in Dungeonbound?

If the next fight keeps ending the same way, check the specific problem before replacing your entire build.

- **An item is not activating:** Confirm that it is in the Equipment grid, not Inventory, and inspect its activation requirement.
- **A stronger item made the build weaker:** Check whether you removed healing, Block, or an effect that supported your main weapon.
- **You cannot equip a new item:** Check both the available grid space and the equipped-weapon limit. Bigger Bag only increases storage.
- **Poison keeps killing you:** Poison ignores Block. Consider better recovery or enough damage to shorten the fight.
- **Your first Forest attempts end quickly:** Return to Joshua's character screen and improve damage or survival before trying again.

Avoid changing several important items at once. If you change one weakness at a time, the next fight gives you a clearer indication of whether the adjustment helped.

For specific rival losses and Floor 8 preparation, use the <Link href="/dungeonbound/act-1">Climb Act 1 guide</Link>.

For problems caused by combining equipment, see the <Link href="/dungeonbound/fusion-guide">Fusion Guide</Link>.
