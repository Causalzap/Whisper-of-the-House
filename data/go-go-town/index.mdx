My **Go-Go Town 1.0** route follows one rule:

**I finish one working loop before expanding into the next system.**

The game introduces four industries, shops, Townies, tourist desires, EGO Tech,
Couriers, Town Rankings, Import, Treasure Hunting, map expansion, and late-game
facilities very quickly.

The difficult part is rarely finding something to build. It is knowing which
system should receive my Coins, EGO, workers, and attention next.

I use this route:

**Forestry and Mining → Town Hall → Train Station → first working shop →
housing and Townies → Town Goals → stable production → automation → Import and
Treasure Hunting → Challenge 10 → final Agents**

<h2 id="first-town-route">
  My First Town Route
</h2>

The TownCo. tutorial teaches more than controls. It introduces the production
loop used throughout the rest of the game:

**collect → refine → store → create demand → deliver → sell**

It also introduces four industries:

- **Forestry**
- **Mining**
- **Fishing**
- **Farming**

    processed construction materials needed for my opening route.

I begin with Forestry and Mining.

My opening route is:

1. inspect the starting Forestry and Mining Zones;
2. open EGO Tech;
3. obtain the opening industry package;
4. place the Chainsaw and Table Saw in Forestry;
5. place the Drill and Foundry in Mining;
6. produce Planks and Bricks;
7. deliver the required materials to the Town Hall;
8. finish the structure and cut the ribbon;
9. prepare the first shop;
10. open the Train Station.

I keep each tool close to its processor and Storage.

During the opening, I care more about seeing the complete material route than
creating a perfect industrial district. When raw materials, processors, and
finished construction supplies are close together, I can identify a missing
step in a few seconds.

Fishing and Farming become more important when Town Goals begin requesting fish,
crops, food recipes, or additional tourist services.

I do not fully automate all four industries simply because the tutorial
introduced them.

<h3 id="town-hall-and-station">
  Finish the Town Hall Before Expanding
</h3>

The Town Hall opens the Top Town Tournament structure that drives the main
progression route.

I spend my opening EGO on the production needed to finish it rather than buying
several optional tourist packages.

Before encouraging more tourists to enter, I prepare one basic service loop:

- one completed shop;
- one selected recipe;
- enough stock for the first customers;
- a counter I can operate manually;
- a usable entrance;
- and a place to collect the first garbage.

When tourists are arriving faster than I can serve them, I stop adding shops
and attractions.

An empty second shop does not help. I restock the working shop, clean the main
route, and stabilize the current visitors first.

<h2 id="shops-housing-townies">
  Shops, Housing, and My First Townies
</h2>

A shop shell is not a working business.

I still need to:

1. finish construction;
2. cut the ribbon;
3. select the correct fit-out;
4. choose a recipe;
5. place the ingredients in shop Storage;
6. operate the counter or assign a worker;
7. confirm that tourists can reach the entrance.

Before I have enough housing, I run the first shop myself.

That keeps Coins and EGO moving while I complete invitation requests and
prepare a place for the first Townie. It is more useful than constructing
several businesses with no workers, recipes, or stock.

I add the next shop when the first one is:

- consistently stocked;
- serving tourists;
- generating Coins;
- reachable from the main visitor route;
- and no longer consuming most of my day.

I wait when the existing shop is empty, unreachable, dirty, or missing a
worker. Those are setup problems, not evidence that the town needs another
store.

<h3 id="housing-before-jobs">
  Housing Comes Before Permanent Job Assignments
</h3>

Inviting a Townie, housing them, and assigning their job are separate actions.

My order is:

1. complete the invitation request;
2. invite the Townie;
3. provide a free housing slot;
4. assign the Townie to a workplace;
5. reopen the workplace to confirm the assignment;
6. watch one complete work cycle.

I recruit for a known job instead of inviting everyone immediately.

My first useful roles are usually:

- the first shop worker;
- one Forestry or Mining worker;
- the first Courier;
- and Maintenance once garbage becomes persistent.

I do not build a large apartment district while most workplaces are unfinished
or have no active demand.

More residents increase the town's capacity only when I have real jobs and
working systems for them.

<h2 id="progression-systems">
  How Go-Go Town Progression Actually Works
</h2>

Several resources appear connected but control different parts of the town.

### Coins

Tourists spend Coins on products and services.

I use Coins for construction, land, Townie invitations, Import purchases,
additional copies, and physical expansion.

### EGO

Satisfied tourists generate EGO.

I spend it in the EGO Tech App to unlock buildings, systems, production
options, housing, tourist services, and other packages.

### Thumbs-up

Happy departing tourists provide thumbs-up progress.

This contributes to broader town progression and some tournament requirements.

### Town Rankings

The Town Rankings App shows the exact goals for the active Top Town Tournament
bracket.

I complete the named requirements and then return to the App to claim the
reward.

### EGO Tech and Rebuy

EGO Tech handles the original unlock.

Rebuy handles another copy after the original node has already been purchased.

A later Town Rank does not automatically purchase every earlier EGO Tech node.
When a building appears to be missing, I scan backward through older tiers
before earning more EGO.

My EGO Tech priority is:

1. the node named by the current Town Goal;
2. production required for current construction;
3. housing for a Townie I already need;
4. transport or Storage that removes a measured bottleneck;
5. a service that fills a weak tourist desire;
6. optional shops and decoration.

I do not buy every node because the EGO meter is full.

I buy a node when I know which current problem it solves.

<h3 id="claim-rewards">
  Claim Rewards Before Waiting for the Next Goals
</h3>

Completing every visible goal does not automatically begin the next bracket.

I return to Town Rankings and check for **Claim Rewards**.

My sequence is:

1. finish every listed goal;
2. reopen Town Rankings;
3. claim the completed reward;
4. let the full TownCo. conversation end;
5. close and reopen the App;
6. confirm that the next goal set appeared.

I do not build another copy of the goal object while a reward is still waiting
to be claimed.

<h3 id="tourist-tracker">
  Use Tourist Tracker Before Buying Another Service
</h3>

Tourists have six broad desire categories:

- Shopping
- Hunger
- Exploration
- Entertainment
- Rest
- Health

I open the **Tourist Tracker App** before buying another desire package.

The App helps me identify which part of the visitor experience is producing the
weakest result. I then travel to that district and inspect the actual service.

Before placing a duplicate service, I check whether the existing one is:

- open;
- staffed;
- stocked;
- connected to a usable path;
- close to the route tourists actually use;
- surrounded by clean ground;
- and able to handle the current number of visitors.

An attraction does not satisfy tourists merely because it exists somewhere on
the map.

When the service works but its capacity is clearly too low, I add another one
near the same visitor route.

<h2 id="automation">
  When I Start Automating the Town
</h2>

I automate a route only after I can complete the same route manually.

My first test chain is:

**Forestry worker → Storage → Courier → shop Storage → active recipe**

Before purchasing the first Courier, I confirm that:

- the source produces the correct item;
- the worker has real demand;
- the item reaches usable Storage;
- the shop has an active recipe;
- the destination has free space;
- and the Courier Building connects to a road.

I buy the first Courier when carrying materials myself is taking time away from
shops, goals, invitations, and construction.

I buy another Courier only when the first one is already moving almost
continuously and a functioning destination still runs out of stock. That is a
capacity problem.

A Courier that never leaves is not a capacity problem.

<h2 id="import-treasures-expansion">
  Midgame Import, Treasures, and Expansion
</h2>

By the middle tournament brackets, the main danger is no longer a lack of
systems.

It is having too many unfinished systems at the same time.

I finish one required project before placing the next large shell. An unfinished
building occupies valuable space but produces no materials, Coins, EGO, or
tourist satisfaction.

<h3 id="when-to-import">
  When I Use Import
</h3>

The Import App lets me order Items and Vehicles from the TownCo. Catalogue with
Coins.

Some catalogue entries only appear after I discover the corresponding item or
vehicle elsewhere. When something is missing from the list, I consider whether
I have actually found it before treating the App as broken.

I import when:

- a small quantity is blocking a required Town Goal;
- the item has already been discovered;
- rebuilding the entire production route would delay the bracket;
- or I need one vehicle or object for a specific current task.

I do not use Import as a permanent substitute for basic production.

When a shop consumes the same ingredient every day, I repair or automate the
source instead of repeatedly paying for imports.

My decision is:

- **one short construction shortage:** import it;
- **repeating shop or processor demand:** stabilize the production chain;
- **an item is missing from the Catalogue:** explore and discover it first;
- **optional decoration:** wait until the current challenge is funded.

Import is strongest when it removes one immediate blocker. It becomes expensive
and inconvenient when I use it to hide a production route that never worked.

<h3 id="treasure-hunting">
  What I Do With Treasures and Duplicate Finds
</h3>

Treasure Hunting gives me collectibles across the Town Centre and outer zones.

I use the Collections App to check whether a find is new before deciding what
to do with it.

A newly discovered Treasure can contribute to the collection and may be useful
for a display inside the Town Hall or elsewhere in town.

My order is:

1. collect the Treasure;
2. check whether it is a new discovery;
3. decide whether I want it in a display;
4. keep at least one copy when it fills a collection or display gap;
5. sell extra copies when I need Coins.

I do not immediately sell every Treasure.

Selling the first copy can remove an item I later want for a themed display,
while keeping every duplicate fills space without helping the town.

I also do not treat Treasure selling as my primary economy.

Treasures provide useful extra Coins, but stable shops and reliable production
remain the repeatable income route.

Treasure collection becomes especially important after reaching number one,
because Agent Peppermint asks me to collect **50 Treasures**.

I do not wait until the final Train Station sequence to begin exploring. I pick
up Treasures naturally while opening new regions, checking resource nodes, and
completing other Townie requests.

<h3 id="when-to-expand">
  When I Buy More Land
</h3>

I expand the map when:

- the active Town Goal requires a building that no longer fits;
- the plot opens an exploration or resource route;
- a working production chain genuinely needs another district;
- a new region provides materials or Treasures I cannot reach elsewhere;
- or my tourist route has reached practical capacity.

I wait when:

- existing shops are empty or unstaffed;
- I am moving the same broken chain farther away;
- Courier and Maintenance routes are already too long;
- the new plot has no immediate function;
- or the current goal needs the same Coins.

More land does not automatically create more progress.

Early production, Storage, housing, and shops work better when I can move
between them quickly. I separate districts after the town has enough workers
and transport to support the distance.

<h2 id="creative-mode">
  Creative Mode or Normal Progression?
</h2>

I use **Creative Mode** when I want to test construction, terrain, roads, and
district layouts without normal economic pressure.

It is useful for checking:

- building footprints;
- Courier road layouts;
- Industry Zone spacing;
- bridges and terrain changes;
- and large tourist districts.

I stay in normal progression when I want to complete Town Goals, unlock EGO
Tech, reach number one, or continue the final Agent route.

Creative Mode is a planning and free-building option. It is not the fix for a
missing reward, recipe, worker, or Town Goal in my progression town.

<h3 id="final-agents">
  The Three Final Agent Requirements
</h3>

The final Agents appear during the same endgame window, but their invitation
requirements are separate.

- **Agent Toffee:** pay 30,000 Coins.
- **Agent Peppermint:** collect 50 Treasures.
- **Agent Peanut:** receive the Safe code, discover the TownCo. Bunker Door Key,
  and return to Peanut.

Completing Toffee's payment does not provide Peanut's code.

Collecting Peppermint's Treasures does not open the Town Co. Safe.

I speak to every visible Agent and allow each conversation to finish, but I
track their objectives separately.

<h2 id="faq">
  Go-Go Town 1.0 FAQ
</h2>

### Can I move buildings or Industry Zone signs after placing them?

Yes. I use the pencil option in the Build Menu to reposition existing Props,
including buildings and Zone signs. This is useful when an early layout blocks
a road or leaves a processor outside the correct Industry Zone.

### Why can I no longer import another vehicle?

Vehicle imports can temporarily reach a purchase limit before the Catalogue
restocks. I wait for the restock instead of assuming the vehicle was permanently
removed or buying another vehicle type I do not need.

### Do the three final Agents only appear at a particular time of day?

No. Once I meet the number-one ranking and Challenge 10 requirements, I check
the Train Station without waiting for a specific morning, afternoon, or night
window. Pending dialogue and arrival sequences matter more than the clock.

### What should I do before my Coin or EGO wallet reaches its cap?

I spend the currency on a planned unlock, required construction, or scheduled
challenge before continuing to earn more. Leaving a wallet capped can waste
income or EGO that the town would otherwise generate.

### Does unlocking the Jailbreak App disable Story Mode achievements?

No. Unlocking the hidden Jailbreak App does not disable Story Mode achievement
progress. Individual codes may still provide specific items, currency, or
Townies, so I keep those rewards separate from the normal progression route.

### Is the Import App available in Creative Mode?

Yes. The Import App can also be used in Creative Mode. I use it there when I
need a specific discovered item or vehicle for a layout test rather than waiting
for a normal Story Mode production chain.
