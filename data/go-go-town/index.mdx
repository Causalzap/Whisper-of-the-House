My **Go-Go Town 1.0** route follows one rule:

**I finish one working loop before expanding into the next system.**

The game introduces four industries, shops, Townies, tourist desires, EGO Tech,
Couriers, Town Rankings, Import and Export, map expansion, and late-game
facilities very quickly.

The difficult part is rarely finding something to build. It is knowing which
system should receive my Coins, EGO, workers, and attention next.

I use this route:

**Forestry and Mining → Town Hall → Train Station → first working shop →
housing and Townies → Town Goals → stable production → automation → midgame
expansion → Challenge 10**

<h2 id="first-town-route">
  My First Town Route
</h2>

The TownCo. tutorial teaches more than controls. It explains the production
loop used throughout the rest of the game:

**collect → refine → store → create demand → deliver → sell**

It also introduces four industries:

- **Forestry**
- **Mining**
- **Fishing**
- **Farming**

    processed construction materials needed for my opening route.

I begin with Forestry and Mining.

My first route is:

1. inspect the starting Forestry and Mining Zones;
2. open EGO Tech;
3. obtain the opening industry package;
4. place the Chainsaw and Table Saw in Forestry;
5. place the Drill and Foundry in Mining;
6. produce Planks and Bricks;
7. deliver the required materials to the Town Hall;
8. complete the structure and cut the ribbon;
9. open the Train Station;
10. prepare the first shop for arriving tourists.

I keep each tool close to its processor and Storage.

During the opening, I care more about seeing the full material route than
creating a perfect industrial district. When raw materials, processed
materials, and finished construction supplies are close together, I can find a
missing step in a few seconds.

Fishing and Farming become more important when Town Goals begin requesting fish,
crops, food recipes, or additional tourist services. I do not automate all four
industries simply because the tutorial introduced them.

<h3 id="town-hall-and-station">
  Finish the Town Hall Before Expanding
</h3>

The Town Hall opens the Top Town Tournament structure that drives the main
progression route.

I spend my opening EGO on the production needed to finish it rather than buying
several optional tourist packs.

Before encouraging more tourists to enter, I prepare one basic service loop:

- one completed shop;
- one selected recipe;
- enough stock for the first customers;
- a counter I can operate manually;
- and a place to collect the first garbage.

When tourists are already arriving faster than I can serve them, I stop adding
new shops and attractions.

An empty second shop does not help. I restock the working shop, clear the main
route, and stabilize the current visitors first.

<h2 id="shops-housing-townies">
  Shops, Housing, and My First Townies
</h2>

A shop shell is not a working business.

I still need to:

1. finish construction;
2. select the correct fit-out;
3. choose a recipe;
4. place the ingredients in shop Storage;
5. operate the counter or assign a worker;
6. confirm that tourists can reach the entrance.

Before I have enough housing, I run the first shop myself.

That keeps Coins and EGO moving while I complete invitation requests and
prepare housing. It is more useful than constructing several businesses with no
workers, recipes, or stock.

I add the next shop when the first one is:

- consistently stocked;
- serving tourists;
- generating Coins;
- and no longer consuming most of my day.

I wait when the existing shop is empty, unreachable, or missing a worker. Those
are setup problems, not evidence that the town needs another store.

<h3 id="housing-before-jobs">
  Housing Comes Before Permanent Job Assignments
</h3>

Inviting a Townie, housing them, and assigning their job are separate actions.

My order is:

1. complete the invitation request;
2. invite the Townie;
3. provide a free housing slot;
4. assign the Townie to a workplace;
5. reopen the workplace to confirm the assignment;
6. watch one complete work cycle.

I recruit for a known job instead of inviting everyone immediately.

My first useful roles are usually:

- the first shop worker;
- one Forestry or Mining worker;
- the first Courier;
- and Maintenance once garbage becomes persistent.

I do not build a large apartment district while most workplaces are still
unfinished or have no demand.

More residents increase the town's capacity only when I have real jobs for
them.

<h2 id="progression-systems">
  How Go-Go Town Progression Actually Works
</h2>

Several resources appear connected but control different parts of the town.

### Coins

Tourists spend Coins on products and services.

I use Coins for construction, land, invitations, additional copies, and
physical expansion.

### EGO

Satisfied tourists generate EGO.

I spend EGO in the EGO Tech App to unlock buildings, systems, production
options, housing, tourist services, and other packages.

### Thumbs-up

Happy departing tourists provide thumbs-up progress.

This matters for broader town progression and some tournament requirements.

### Town Rankings

The Town Rankings App shows the exact goals for the active Top Town Tournament
bracket.

I complete the named requirements and then return to the App to claim the
reward.

### EGO Tech and Rebuy

EGO Tech handles the original unlock.

Rebuy handles another copy after the original node has already been purchased.

A later Town Rank does not automatically purchase every earlier EGO Tech node.
When a building appears to be missing, I scan backward through older tiers
before earning more EGO.

My EGO Tech priority is:

1. the node named by the current Town Goal;
2. production required for current construction;
3. housing for a Townie I already need;
4. transport or Storage that removes a measured bottleneck;
5. a service that fills a weak tourist desire;
6. optional shops and decoration.

I do not buy every node because the meter is full.

I buy a node when I know which current problem it solves.

<h3 id="claim-rewards">
  Claim Rewards Before Waiting for the Next Goals
</h3>

Completing every visible goal does not automatically begin the next bracket.

I return to Town Rankings and check for **Claim Rewards**.

My sequence is:

1. finish every listed goal;
2. reopen Town Rankings;
3. claim the completed reward;
4. let the full TownCo. conversation end;
5. reopen the App;
6. confirm that the next goal set appeared.

<h3 id="tourist-tracker">
  Use Tourist Tracker Before Buying Another Service
</h3>

Tourists have six broad desire categories:

- Shopping
- Hunger
- Exploration
- Entertainment
- Rest
- Health

I open the **Tourist Tracker App** before buying another desire package.

The App helps me identify which part of the visitor experience is producing the
weakest result. I then travel to that district and inspect the actual service.

Before placing a duplicate service, I check whether the existing one is:

- open;
- staffed;
- stocked;
- connected to a usable path;
- close to the route tourists actually use;
- and free of garbage around its entrance.

An attraction does not satisfy tourists merely because it exists somewhere on
the map.

When the service works but its capacity is clearly too low, I add another one
near the same visitor route.

<h2 id="automation">
  When I Start Automating the Town
</h2>

I automate a route only after I can complete the same route manually.

My first test chain is:

**Forestry worker → Storage → Courier → shop Storage → active recipe**

Before purchasing the first Courier, I confirm that:

- the source produces the correct item;
- the item reaches a usable Storage;
- the shop has an active recipe;
- the destination has free space;
- and the Courier Building can connect to a road.

I buy the first Courier when carrying materials myself is taking time away from
shops, goals, invitations, and construction.

I buy another Courier only when the first one is already moving almost
continuously and a working shop still runs out of stock. That is a capacity
problem.

A Courier that never leaves is not a capacity problem.

<h2 id="import-export-expansion">
  Import, Export, and Midgame Expansion
</h2>

By the middle tournament brackets, the main danger is no longer a lack of
systems.

It is having too many unfinished systems at the same time.

I finish one required project before placing the next large shell. An unfinished
building occupies valuable space but produces no materials, Coins, EGO, or
tourist satisfaction.

<h3 id="when-to-import">
  When I Use Import
</h3>

The Import App is most useful when one missing item is blocking a required
building or production upgrade.

I import when:

- the material has already been discovered;
- I need a small quantity to finish an active Town Goal;
- rebuilding the entire production chain would delay the bracket;
- or I need a discovered vehicle or object for a specific current purpose.

I do not use Import as a permanent substitute for basic production.

When a shop consumes the same ingredient every day, I repair or automate the
source instead of repeatedly importing it.

My decision is:

- **one short construction shortage:** import can save time;
- **repeating shop or processor demand:** build a stable supply chain;
- **optional decoration:** wait until the current challenge is funded.

<h3 id="when-to-export">
  When I Use Export
</h3>

Export gives surplus resources a purpose.

I export items when:

- Storage is filling with stock I do not currently need;
- the production chain can replace the exported amount;
- and no active Town Goal or construction project uses the same material.

I do not export the last stack of a processed material merely because its raw
resource is easy to collect.

Planks, Bricks, Fiber Boards, Metal, and later construction materials can become
the exact item that stops the next required building.

I keep a deliberate construction reserve and export the excess.

<h3 id="when-to-expand">
  When I Buy More Land
</h3>

I expand the map when:

- the active Town Goal requires a building that no longer fits;
- the plot opens an exploration or resource route;
- a working production chain genuinely needs another district;
- or my tourist route has reached practical capacity.

I wait when:

- existing shops are empty or unstaffed;
- I am moving the same broken chain farther away;
- Courier and Maintenance routes are already too long;
- or the current goal needs the same Coins.

More land does not automatically create more progress.

Early production, Storage, housing, and shops work better when I can move
between them quickly. I separate districts after the town has enough workers
and transport to support the distance.

<h2 id="creative-mode">
  Creative Mode or Normal Progression?
</h2>

I use **Creative Mode** when my real goal is experimenting with construction,
terrain, and district layouts without normal economic pressure.

It is useful for testing:

- a large tourism district;
- shop footprints;
- Courier road layouts;
- Industry Zone spacing;
- bridges and terrain changes;
- and decorative plans before rebuilding a progression town.

I do not treat Creative Mode as a repair button for blocked Town Goals.

When I want to continue the same progression route, I still need to solve the
missing reward, EGO Tech node, building setup, recipe, worker, or tourist
requirement in that town.

My choice is simple:

- **I want to win the Top Town Tournament:** stay in normal progression.
- **I only want to test a layout or build freely:** use Creative Mode.
- **I am stuck but still want the same save to progress:** diagnose the actual
  blocked system.

<h2 id="faq">
  Go-Go Town 1.0 FAQ
</h2>

### When should I import a material instead of producing it?

I import when a small quantity of one discovered material is blocking a
required construction project. When a shop or processor needs the same item
repeatedly, I build or repair a permanent production chain instead.

### What should I export first?

I export renewable surplus that is not reserved for a Town Goal, construction
project, recipe, or Tech purchase. I keep processed building materials until I
know what the next bracket requires.

### Should I expand the map before buying my first Courier?

Usually not. I keep the first industries, Storage, shop, and housing close
together. I expand after the current district has a real space problem or the
new plot opens a required route.

### Why is EGO still slow when every tourist desire has a building?

The building may be empty, unstaffed, inaccessible, dirty, or too far from the
main visitor route. I use Tourist Tracker to identify the weakest result, then
inspect the corresponding service in person.

### Can Creative Mode complete my normal Town Goals?

I use Creative Mode for construction and layout experiments, not for repairing
a blocked tournament route. Normal progression still requires its own goals,
rewards, Tech purchases, buildings, recipes, and workers.

### When is a second Courier worth buying?

I add one when the first Courier is correctly assigned, has a direct road, moves
almost constantly, and still cannot keep a functioning destination stocked. A
stationary first Courier needs diagnosis rather than duplication.
