<h2 id="system-not-unlocked">
  Check Whether the Automation System Is Actually Unlocked
</h2>

A missing building and a broken building are different problems.

If I cannot find Courier, Maintenance, a processor, or another automation
option in the Build menu, I stop searching the map and open the Phone.

I check:

1. the current Town Goals;
2. whether a completed bracket still has a reward waiting to be claimed;
3. the newest available EGO Tech tier;
4. older EGO Tech tiers I may have skipped;
5. the Rebuy section after confirming that the first unlock was purchased.

Town Rank and EGO Tech do not purchase each other automatically.

I can advance the tournament while leaving an older Tech node unpurchased. When
a supposedly available system is missing, I scan backward through the tiers
before assuming the save is broken.

EGO comes from happy tourists. When I am short of the EGO needed for an
automation unlock, I inspect the tourist route rather than waiting beside the
Build menu.

I check which desire tourists are repeatedly missing, whether the matching
service is open and stocked, and whether garbage is lowering the quality of the
area.

I do not buy random decoration solely to generate EGO. I fix the specific
Shopping, Hunger, Exploration, Entertainment, Rest, or Health gap that visitors
are actually showing.

For missing goals, unclaimed rewards, EGO Tech tiers, Rebuy, or buildings that
never appeared, I use the{" "}

<h2 id="townie-requirements">
  Get a Townie Into the Job Before Testing the Chain
</h2>

Inviting a Townie, housing a Townie, and assigning a Townie are three separate
steps.

When a Townie arrives, they usually give me an invitation request. Completing
that request makes them available to join, but it does not automatically place
them in a home or workplace.

My route is:

1. complete the Townie's request;
2. invite them into town;
3. open a tent, house, or apartment with a free slot;
4. assign the Townie to that housing;
5. walk to the workplace;
6. manage the Time Clock, shop, Courier Building, or Maintenance Office;
7. select the same Townie as its worker;
8. close and reopen the workplace to confirm the assignment.

    I give the Townie a housing slot first, then return to the workplace and
    make the job assignment separately.
  </figcaption>

When the worker selection is empty, I do not build another workplace.

I check whether:

- every available Townie is already assigned;
- the new Townie is still waiting for an invitation request;
- the Townie has joined but has no home;
- the housing building is full;
- or I accidentally assigned the Townie to another shop or service.

I invite another Townie when I already have a specific open job and an available
housing slot.

I wait when I have neither. A larger population does not repair a production
chain that still has no recipe or demand.

<h2 id="workers-standing-still">
  Why My Workers Are Standing Still
</h2>

The first time I assigned a worker to Forestry, the worker stood beside the Time
Clock without collecting anything.

The worker was housed. The assignment was correct. The missing link was demand.

Workers do not collect every available resource automatically. I create demand
in one of two ways:

1. select a shop or processor recipe that consumes the resource;
2. lock that resource in Storage so workers maintain a stockpile.

I check the destination first.

When I want wood for a shop, I walk to that shop, manage it, and confirm that
its selected recipe actually uses wood. I then return to Forestry and watch the
worker.

The result tells me what to do next:

- **The worker starts moving:** the problem was missing demand.
- **The worker stays at the Time Clock:** I check housing, assignment, tools,
  Storage, and the zone.
- **The worker gathers resources but the shop remains empty:** I move to
  Courier and destination checks.
- **The worker stops after filling Storage:** the requested demand may already
  be satisfied.

Workers normally collect enough to cover current demand. They do not need to
move continuously when every requested inventory is already full.

<h3 id="tools-processors">
  Check the Tool and Processor Separately
</h3>

An Industry Zone is not a single production building.

A working raw-material chain can require:

- the correct Industry Zone;
- a Time Clock;
- an assigned Townie;
- a gathering tool;
- raw-material Storage;
- a processor;
- an active processor recipe;
- and output space.

When raw materials appear but the refined product does not, I stop checking the
worker.

I walk to the processor and verify:

1. the correct recipe is selected;
2. the input item is present;
3. the finished item has somewhere to go;
4. the output is not already blocking production.

This distinction matters with materials such as Fiber Boards. I look in the
Forestry production chain rather than searching Farming simply because the word
“fiber” sounds agricultural.

<h2 id="storage-locks">
  Use Storage Locks Without Blocking Other Items
</h2>

Each Storage can lock up to **four unique item types**.

Once I lock an item, that Storage only accepts its selected locked items, and
workers continue gathering them while space remains.

That is useful for:

- wood needed across several construction projects;
- stone or Metal required by repeated upgrades;
- an ingredient used by several shops;
- or a processor that needs a reliable input buffer.

It can also create a hidden bottleneck.

When I lock four low-priority resources in one general Storage, there may be no
valid slot for the material needed by the current recipe.

My early layout is simpler:

- one Storage beside the Industry Zone for raw materials;
- one beside the processors for refined materials;
- shop Storage reserved for the selected recipe;
- one or two deliberate locks instead of four automatic locks.

I change or remove a lock when:

- the Storage remains full of an item I am no longer using;
- the processor cannot unload;
- a Courier arrives but cannot deposit anything;
- or another required item has no valid destination.

I do not add another Storage until I know whether the current one is full
because of useful stock or because of the wrong locks.

<h2 id="industry-zone-connection">
  Connect Mining and Forestry to Their Entrances
</h2>

Mining and Forestry workers need the Industry Zone itself to reach the matching
resource entrance.

For Forestry, I extend the zone to the **Deep Forest Entrance**.

For Mining, I extend it to the **Mine Entrance**.

I open zone editing and follow the colored area from the Time Clock toward the
entrance.

A small visual gap can stop workers from traveling into the Mine or Deep Forest.

I diagnose the result like this:

- **Worker never enters the resource area:** check the zone connection.
- **Worker enters but returns with nothing:** check the tool, demand, and
  Storage.
- **Raw resources arrive but nothing is refined:** check the processor.
- **Refined materials exist but never reach the shop:** check the Courier.

<h2 id="courier-unlock">
  Courier Building Missing From the Build Menu
</h2>

Courier is not useful to troubleshoot until the system is unlocked.

When I cannot find the Courier Building, I check Town Rankings and EGO Tech
instead of searching every Build category.

I also confirm that I have:

- claimed the latest completed bracket reward;
- purchased the relevant EGO Tech option;
- enough materials to finish the building;
- a Townie who can be housed and assigned.

In my early 1.0 route, the Courier Building itself displayed a price of **150
Coins**. Construction then required **2 Metal and 3 Fiber Boards**.

I gather those materials before placing the building. An unfinished shell
cannot make deliveries, even when it looks as though the Courier service has
already been placed.

I do not write a fixed Town Rank into my diagnosis. The safer check is whether
the unlock is visible in my current EGO Tech and whether the preceding bracket
reward has been claimed.

<h2 id="courier-not-working">
  Why My Courier Is Not Working
</h2>

My first Courier failure happened before the first delivery: the building did
not have proper road access.

    I finish the road connection, construction materials, housing, and worker
    assignment before judging Courier speed.

</figure>

I start at the Courier Building and trace a continuous road back into town.

Then I manage the building and confirm that a housed Townie is assigned.

Once the Courier begins moving, I follow one complete trip:

1. Which source does the Courier visit?
2. Does that inventory contain the requested item?
3. Is the item compatible with the destination?
4. Does the destination have free space?
5. Is its recipe still requesting the item?
6. Does the Courier successfully deposit it?

A walking Courier is not automatically a useful Courier.

When the worker repeatedly walks to an empty Storage, the route is valid but the
source chain is broken.

When the worker reaches the shop but cannot deposit, the destination may be full
or may no longer request that item.

<h3 id="courier-too-slow">
  Shorten the Route Before Adding Another Courier
</h3>

At later Town Ranks, my Courier could be working correctly but spend most of the
day crossing the map.

I first improve the layout:

- move frequently used Storage closer to the shops;
- group shops with similar ingredient needs;
- keep processors close to raw-material Storage;
- remove unnecessary road detours;
- avoid making one Courier serve distant districts on opposite sides of town.

I add a second Courier only when:

- the first worker is assigned;
- the road is continuous;
- source and destination inventories both work;
- the route is already reasonably direct;
- and the Courier is moving almost constantly but demand still exceeds delivery
  speed.

That is the one place where another Courier solves the problem: the chain works,
but its capacity is too low.

<h2 id="storage-full">
  Fix “No Space” and Processors That Stop
</h2>

`No Space` does not always refer to my backpack.

I check where the message appeared:

- **On my character:** clear a backpack slot or use a vehicle.
- **At raw-material Storage:** remove surplus stock or adjust its locks.
- **At a processor:** remove the finished output.
- **At shop Storage:** sell or remove current stock.
- **At a Courier destination:** free a valid slot for the delivered item.

A processor can contain all required ingredients and still appear idle when its
finished product has nowhere to go.

I remove one output item and watch the next cycle.

If production resumes, I have confirmed an output bottleneck. I then create
dedicated refined-material Storage or shorten the route to the next destination.

When the processor still does nothing, I recheck its selected recipe and input
items before rebuilding it.

<h2 id="garbage-automation">
  Fix Garbage, Dirty Ground, and Maintenance
</h2>

Garbage cleanup has separate parts:

1. **Garbage Bins** collect trash from busy areas.
2. **Garbage Disposal** receives the collected trash.
3. **Maintenance workers** move trash through the service.
4. The **Bubble Gun** cleans dirty or smelly ground.

    bins, disposal capacity, and a practical cleanup route.

When garbage piles up, I walk the full cleanup route:

1. start at the busiest shops or attractions;
2. inspect nearby bins;
3. check whether they are full;
4. follow the path toward Garbage Disposal;
5. confirm that Maintenance has an assigned worker;
6. watch whether the worker can complete one pickup;
7. use the Bubble Gun on dirty ground left behind.

Trash and dirty ground are not the same state.

A clean bin route does not remove stains automatically. The Bubble Gun does not
replace Garbage Disposal.

I place more bins when tourists generate garbage faster than the current bins
can hold it.

I add another Maintenance worker only after the current worker, disposal, and
walking route are confirmed to work.

<h2 id="garbirds">
  How I Get Rid of Garbirds
</h2>

Garbirds begin appearing when the town has too much garbage.

They make the problem worse by running around and flicking trash out of bins, so
I handle the immediate creature and the underlying garbage backlog separately.

<figure className="my-8">
  <Image
    src="/images/go-go-town/go-go-town-garbird-infestation.webp"

  />
  <figcaption className="mx-auto mt-3 max-w-3xl text-sm text-slate-400">
    Driving a Garbird away stops the immediate disruption. It does not fix the
    full bins and missing cleanup capacity that allowed it to appear.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

To scare a Garbird away, I use one of these:

- a vehicle horn;
- the vehicle itself;
- an Industry tool such as the Blower;
- a thrown item;
- or a high five.

The vehicle horn was the easiest option when my truck or bike was already
nearby. I drive toward the bird, use the horn, and keep pressure on it until it
leaves.

I do not stop after the warning disappears.

I immediately:

1. empty the nearest full bins;
2. clear loose garbage;
3. check Garbage Disposal capacity;
4. confirm the Maintenance assignment;
5. add bins near the tourist hotspot if needed;
6. clean the dirty ground with the Bubble Gun.

When Garbirds return quickly, I treat that as evidence that the garbage system
is still undersized or too slow.

It is not proof that the Garbird removal action failed.

<h2 id="reset-checklist">
  My Least-Destructive Reset Checklist
</h2>

When every visible part looks correct but the chain still does not update, I use
this order:

1. Save the game.
2. Confirm that the system is unlocked in EGO Tech.
3. Reopen housing and verify the Townie still lives there.
4. Unassign and reassign the affected workplace.
5. Confirm the final destination recipe.
6. Remove and reapply the relevant Storage lock.
7. Clear one slot at both the source and destination.
8. Recheck the Industry Zone entrance connection.
9. Follow one complete Courier or Maintenance trip.
10. Reload the save only after recording the current assignments and recipes.

I do not delete the entire district first.

Removing the Time Clock, Storage, processor, road, and destination at the same
time destroys the evidence that tells me which link was failing.

<h2 id="faq">
  Go-Go Town Automation FAQ
</h2>

### Why is the Courier Building missing from my Build menu?

I treat that as a progression problem rather than a Courier route problem. I
check the current Town Goals, claim any completed bracket reward, and inspect
both current and older EGO Tech tiers. Rebuy only helps after the original
unlock has been purchased.

### Why can I invite a Townie but not assign them to a job?

Inviting does not automatically provide housing. I place the Townie in a tent,
house, or apartment with a free slot, then manage the workplace and assign the
same Townie separately. I also check that they are not already working
somewhere else.

### Can I operate a shop while I wait for another Townie?

Yes. I stand behind the counter and run the shop manually. I use this to keep
Coins and EGO moving while I complete a Townie request or build more housing,
but I still need the correct recipe and ingredients.

### Why does a worker stop after collecting resources successfully?

The current demand may already be satisfied. Workers collect enough to cover
active demand unless I create continuing stockpile demand with a Storage lock.
I check whether the destination and locked Storage are already full before
changing the worker.

### Why does `No Space` remain after I empty my backpack?

The blocked inventory may belong to Storage, a processor, shop stock, vehicle,
or Courier destination. I reproduce the message and note which object I am
interacting with before clearing more items.

### Does a Garbird mean my Maintenance Worker is broken?

Not necessarily. Garbirds appear when garbage has built up and can scatter more
trash from bins. I scare the Garbird away, then check whether bin capacity,
Garbage Disposal, walking distance, or Maintenance staffing caused the original
backlog.

### Why is my EGO full but an automation building is still unavailable?

A full EGO meter does not purchase an unlock automatically. I open EGO Tech,
find the required node, and buy it. When the node is unavailable, I return to
Town Rankings and check the active goals and unclaimed rewards.
