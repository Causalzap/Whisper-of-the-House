## Opening and Wellness Check: How Do You Get Past the First Consoles?

After the Antarctic opening, both Explorers wake inside a Norček Industries facility. Director Kathleen Bowe explains that they have been frozen beneath the ice for several years and directs them through rehabilitation.

Follow the accessible route, climb across the ledges, and reach the first mechanism requiring both players. Each Explorer must hold the corresponding interaction. Once it activates, move into the separate testing rooms.

### Complete the three-symbol console

One Explorer sees three unfamiliar symbols on a display. The other has the corresponding physical controls.

The symbols are difficult to describe precisely, especially when two designs share the same general shape. Decide on a distinct name for each before anyone presses a button, then enter them in the displayed order.

1. Read all three symbols from the current display.
2. Find the matching controls in the opposite room.
3. Activate the controls in sequence.
4. Check the acceptance indicators before proceeding.

A rejected attempt can change the symbols. Read the new display instead of immediately repeating the previous combination.

### Match the machinery pictures to the room controls

The next instruction display depicts physical mechanisms rather than abstract symbols. The partner must identify which picture represents which piece of equipment.

Available interactions include a lever, a button-operated device, and a pressure-related mechanism. Follow their connected wires when two objects look similar.

The pictures do not need to appear in the same physical order as the devices. Match the object first, then perform the requested sequence.

Once the console registers the completed sequence, use the opened passage rather than continuing to operate the earlier controls.

## Canister Transfer: How Do You Send the Container to Your Partner?

The next rooms contain a canister, connected door controls, and a transfer mechanism that carries items between the Explorers.

The complication is that opening one doorway can close another. A player may reach the container but lose the route back unless the partner changes the door configuration.

Have the Explorer near the switches establish access to the canister room. The second Explorer should retrieve the container and bring it to the transfer apparatus.

Complete the transfer in this order:

1. Open the doorway to the container.
2. Retrieve the container and place it in the transfer holder.
3. Operate the control that sends it through the connected tube.
4. Open the receiving-side passage as necessary.
5. Collect the container and install it where the receiving equipment requires it.

The transfer control and the doorway control perform different jobs. Sending the container does not automatically open its receiving room.

If the apparatus shows only one active indicator, inspect the receiving side before assuming that pulling both controls at once is the entire solution. Once the container is installed and the required mechanisms have registered, operate the paired controls to continue.

The route leads deeper into Norček's research facilities.

If the miniature Explorer cannot leave the chamber, have the normal-sized player reopen the exit. Once outside, stay on the reachable work surfaces and follow the small gaps toward the circuit assembly; falling from the edge can return you to an earlier position.

## Circuit Matching: Which Six Components Go in the Sockets?

The miniature circuit assembly uses illustrated components, colored fasteners, and a reference display visible to the other Explorer.

Each component is associated with three symbols. The sockets have colored fastener markings that correspond to the same symbol set.

Matching one color is insufficient. Several components share two of their three symbols, so the complete combination matters.

### Color and symbol key

The reference establishes these correspondences:

- **Green:** Square
- **Gray:** Pentagon
- **Red:** Triangle
- **Purple:** Star
- **Blue:** Y-shaped symbol
- **Yellow:** Plus sign

![The six circuit component designs and their matching symbol combinations.](/images/we-were-here-tomorrow/circuit-component-matching.webp)

### Match all six illustrated components

On the reference display, the combinations are:

- **Top left:** Triangle + Y + Star
- **Top right:** Triangle + Pentagon + Plus
- **Middle left:** Triangle + Pentagon + Star
- **Middle right:** Square + Pentagon + Plus
- **Bottom left:** Square + Y + Star
- **Bottom right:** Square + Y + Plus

These are the positions of the illustrations on the reference display, not the physical locations of the sockets.

Have the miniature Explorer examine the socket fasteners and compare them with the three symbols assigned to each illustrated component.

Where two sockets look possible, temporarily leave that component aside. Place the component with fewer possible locations first, then use the remaining socket combinations to resolve the ambiguity.

After all six are installed, activate the circuit.

A correctly assembled circuit powers the connected equipment and allows the laboratory sequence to continue.

## 3D Resin Mixing: What Is the Correct Chemical Order?

The miniature Explorer must operate the container while the normal-sized Explorer controls the dispensing equipment and reads the resin recipe.

The recipe calls for equal portions of Acrylonitrile and Butadiene, stabilized by adding Styrene before and after them.

### Set the nozzles and mix the resin

The dispenser has five positions: **1 and 5 = Butadiene**, **2 and 4 = Styrene**, and **3 = Acrylonitrile**.

![The numbered resin nozzles used to dispense Styrene, Acrylonitrile, and Butadiene.](/images/we-were-here-tomorrow/resin-mixing-controls.webp)

Use **4 → 3 → 1 → 4**, dispensing once at each position:

1. **Nozzle 4 — Styrene**
2. **Nozzle 3 — Acrylonitrile**
3. **Nozzle 1 — Butadiene**
4. **Nozzle 4 — Styrene**

The middle two chemicals receive equal portions. Styrene goes both before and after them.

Position the container directly beneath each selected nozzle before dispensing. After the fourth ingredient, complete the processing interaction to activate the next part of the laboratory.

### When the resin will not process

Two mistakes are easy to confuse.

If the ingredients were added in the wrong order, starting with the middle chemicals or omitting the final Styrene does not satisfy the recipe. Restart with the complete four-part sequence.

If the nozzle is correctly selected but no liquid enters the container, reposition the container beneath its outlet before activating the dispenser again.

The successful mixture allows the miniature route to continue into the computer machinery.

## Floppy Disk Puzzle: How Do You Open Doors 1 and 2?

The miniature Explorer encounters a passage blocked by two oversized floppy disk doors marked **1** and **2**.

The controls outside and the direction indicators inside must be operated together. The two barriers use similar inputs, but the second introduces diagonal directions.

### Door 1: Four 90-degree indicators

The first set contains four rotating indicators, each moving in **90-degree increments**.

The available directions are up, right, down, and left.

![The direction controls used to coordinate the floppy disk door puzzle.](/images/we-were-here-tomorrow/floppy-direction-dials.webp)

Have the Explorer who can read the required orientations call them out in order. The partner operating the indicators stops each one at the corresponding direction.

Watch the light associated with each indicator. A correctly registered position turns blue; a failed position can reset the attempt.

The indicators may continue to move briefly after a control is released, so account for that movement when timing the stop.

Once all four directions register correctly, **Floppy Disk Door 1** opens.

### Door 2: Four 45-degree indicators

Proceed through the first barrier to reach the second mechanism.

This set rotates in **45-degree increments**. It therefore includes the four diagonal directions:

- Upper-left
- Upper-right
- Lower-left
- Lower-right

The partner must distinguish these from the ordinary cardinal directions.

Read the second set of target orientations and stop the indicators individually. The first door's combination is not a substitute for the second door's current display.

After the successful sequence, the remaining floppy disk barrier opens, allowing the miniature Explorer to reach the 3D printer controls.

## 3D Printer Grid: How Do You Get the Green Key Card?

Beyond the floppy disk barriers, the miniature Explorer reaches a 3D printer control system.

One player operates directional inputs and a central button. The other can observe a **4×4 grid of lights** and guide the active position.

The important distinction is between the current cursor and the pattern being created.

### Move the blinking cursor

The blinking indicator represents the currently selected grid position. The directional controls move this active position around the grid.

The two players may view the mechanism from opposing orientations. A movement that appears to go down on one screen can seem to go up from the other perspective.

Use one test movement to establish the direction mapping before trying to complete the pattern.

### Reproduce the required grid pattern

Move through the illuminated target positions:

1. Identify a grid position that needs to change.
2. Move the blinking cursor onto it.
3. Press the central button to change that light's state.
4. Check the result before moving elsewhere.

Moving the cursor alone does not activate the target. The central button performs the change.

If a correct light turns off again, it was likely toggled a second time. Move away from completed positions rather than pressing the same control repeatedly.

Once the required grid pattern is registered, the printer mechanism activates.

### Collect the Green Key Card

The completed machinery produces the **Green Key Card** needed for the earlier laboratory access door.

Collect the card and return through the size-changing equipment. Have the other Explorer restore the miniature player to normal size.

Rejoin your partner, return to the corresponding access panel, and use the card to progress toward the prototype equipment.

This is the earlier laboratory's Green Key Card. A different green access-card objective appears later at the Cargo Elevator.

## Hook Gun and Goop Gun: Which Tool Opens Each Door?

After the shrinking laboratory, both Explorers gain access to Norček's prototype equipment.

The tools have distinct purposes:

**Hook Gun:** Connects to compatible targets, allowing an Explorer to pull mechanisms, interact with objects at a distance, and reach certain elevated positions.

**Goop Gun:** Creates solid cubes that can support movement, activate pressure-based devices, and increase a partner's effective weight.

The Goop Gun initially has a limited deployed-cube capacity. Recall the existing cube when the next placement is blocked. A later upgrade allows **two cubes** to remain deployed.

### Follow the powered doors' cable connections

Several prototype testing rooms contain doors with multiple colored cables.

Follow each cable to the device it controls. A hook target and a cube-operated switch may both contribute to the same doorway, but they must be activated with the appropriate tools.

Complete the individual requirements until their indicators register. A partly powered door often means one connected device remains inactive, not that an earlier button sequence needs repeating.

A free-standing cube works on an ordinary pressure plate. Later, a two-person plate has a different solution; the exact positioning and Goop Gun action are covered at the [Magnetic Stabilizer](#magnetic-stabilizer).

Continue through the prototype testing rooms toward Exotic Matter research.

## Cargo Elevator: Where Are the Fuse and Access Cards?

The Cargo Elevator contains several requirements that can appear connected even when they operate separate mechanisms.

The area includes purple and green access-card slots, a missing Fuse, anti-gravity equipment, and door controls on opposite sides of the machinery.

Completing one requirement does not automatically finish the elevator.

### Retrieve the purple access card

Explore the side passages surrounding the anti-gravity workspace.

Have one Explorer operate the access controls while the other moves through the opened route and retrieves the **purple access card**.

Bring it back toward the two-card access point.

### Lower the suspended Fuse

The Fuse is mounted on a **red mechanical arm** above the machinery. It is visible from below but initially beyond interaction range.

First, locate the fan near the upper machinery and use the Goop Gun to stop its movement.

The other Explorer should then reach the elevated control and operate the lever connected to the suspended arm.

The arm lowers toward the player beneath it.

![The lowered red mechanical arm with the hanging Fuse ready to collect.](/images/we-were-here-tomorrow/cargo-elevator-hanging-fuse.webp)

Take the Fuse from the lowered arm and insert it into the corresponding socket.

This completes the Fuse-powered requirement. The normal progression route still needs the green access card.

### Find the green access card

Return to the anti-gravity workspace and inspect the lower area visible through the side opening.

The green card is easy to miss because it is visible from the room but not positioned on the obvious upper-platform route.

Have your partner operate the door control, then approach the accessible edge of the opening and interact with the card.

You do not need to climb fully into the opening to retrieve it.

Return to the access panel and insert both required cards.

### If the main door stays locked

For the **Fuse-powered route** described above, check the three separate requirements:

- **Purple access card:** Returned from the side passage.
- **Green access card:** Retrieved at the anti-gravity room opening.
- **Fuse mechanism:** Powered after lowering the red arm and inserting the Fuse.

The Fuse-assisted route above is sufficient for normal progression. If you're attempting **Many Ways**, the elevator must instead be activated without using a Fuse. See the [Many Ways achievement requirements](/we-were-here-tomorrow/achievements#many-ways) before completing this section.

For the standard route, inspect whichever access indicator remains inactive rather than repeating a device that has already registered.

Proceed through the opened elevator passage toward the Particle Accelerator.

Have the reading Explorer describe one symbol at a time, using details that distinguish similar designs:

- Number and direction of arrows
- Hollow or filled center
- Lines crossing the middle
- Curved or horn-shaped elements
- The direction and spacing of surrounding marks

After the symbol stage, the machinery introduces three dials with positions numbered **1–5**.

## Magnetic Stabilizer: How Do You Activate the Two-Person Plate?

The Magnetic Stabilizer contains a pressure mechanism that requires more weight than one unmodified Explorer provides.

The second Explorer cannot simply walk over to share the plate because the routes are separated.

### Make one Explorer heavy enough

Position one player on the pressure plate.

The partner with the **Goop Gun** should apply a cube effect directly to that Explorer, increasing their effective weight.

![The Magnetic Stabilizer's pressure indicators showing the mechanism's activation state.](/images/we-were-here-tomorrow/magnetic-stabilizer-pressure-plate.webp)

The pressure system then registers the heavier load, allowing the connected machinery to activate.

This is different from using a separate cube on an ordinary floor switch. The Explorer on the plate is the object being weighted.

After the mechanism has registered, remove the effect when it is no longer needed so movement can resume normally.

### Move through the rotating platforms

The route beyond the pressure system uses moving sections and changing gravity.

One player controls the machinery while the other crosses the platform.

Bring the moving section into an accessible orientation, wait until the crossing player is safely positioned, then change its orientation to connect with the next passage.

Some targets remain covered until a flap or glass barrier is moved by the other Explorer.

Open the cover before trying to hit the exposed target. Repeatedly firing while the target remains obstructed will not advance the mechanism.

Continue through the connected laboratory route toward Laser Calibration.

## Laser Calibration: Which Crystals and Beam Route Are Correct?

The Laser Calibration puzzle requires more than directing a visible beam into the receiving machinery.

The beam must pass through the correct crystal specimens in the correct sequence. Several crystals have similar colors and silhouettes, so a beam can reach the receiver and still trigger **Wrong Crystal Configuration**.

### Select the crystals in the required order

The beam must pass through these three specimens, in this sequence:

1. **Pingosius Crystalis** — Mirror / Piercing
2. **Rupium** — Serene / Stabilizing
3. **Cattonimus** — Fiery / Heating

![The six crystal specimens used to distinguish the required materials from their similar-looking alternatives.](/images/we-were-here-tomorrow/laser-crystal-selection.webp)

The catalogue also contains **Cattonimo**, **Cattonium**, and **Pingosso Crystallo**. A matching color is not enough: check the shape before installing a crystal.

### Tell Pingosius Crystalis from Pingosso Crystallo

The required **Pingosius Crystalis** has additional rounded growths oriented toward the upper part of its crystal structure.

**Pingosso Crystallo** looks similar at a glance, but its smaller growths are arranged differently.

When viewing the specimens from the opposite side of the chamber, compare these details instead of choosing whichever blue crystal seems closest.

The other crystal groups also contain similar-looking specimens. Check the catalogue's shape descriptions for Rupium and Cattonimus before assuming the correct color guarantees the correct material.

### Route the beam through the rooms

The successful configuration sends the beam through the **yellow room before the turquoise room**, followed by the final crystal stage.

Start from the emitter and identify the reflector that can carry the beam into the first required crystal.

Then follow the available connections into the stabilizing stage and onward to the heating crystal.

Finally, direct the processed beam into the receiving apparatus.

Not every reflector can rotate through every direction. Trace the connection from the receiving apparatus backward when the forward route appears to terminate against a wall.

### Check the obscured triangular reflector

One easy-to-miss part of the route involves the green beam.

Before assuming that a required crystal is unavailable, inspect the space behind the surrounding machinery for a **partly obscured triangular optical component**.

It is possible to see the more obvious reflectors while overlooking this connection, leaving the green beam without a usable route into the next stage.

Once that connection is established, continue following the available reflectors toward the receiver.

### Fix "Wrong Crystal Configuration"

A beam reaching the receiver with this message means that reaching the endpoint was not enough.

Check these conditions in order:

1. **Correct specimen:** Confirm Pingosius Crystalis rather than the similar Pingosso Crystallo.
2. **Correct remaining crystals:** Compare Rupium and Cattonimus with the catalogue, not only their colors.
3. **Correct order:** The beam must encounter the three required effects in sequence.
4. **Complete path:** Confirm the green-beam connection and any reflector concealed behind machinery.

A successful setup activates the receiving equipment and opens the route onward.

The next corridor contains six numbered rings with closely related circular designs.

**4 → 1 → 5**

## Gravity Rooms: How Do You Reach the Blue Area and Final Lever?

The Gravitonics section uses inverted rooms, rotating floors, colored corridors, and mechanisms positioned on different sides of the same structure.

The main challenge is remembering which control opens which route while gravity changes the direction that appears to be up.

### Rotate the red L-shaped floor

Locate the control for the large **red L-shaped platform**.

One Explorer operates the rotation mechanism while the partner uses the changed orientation to reach another corridor.

The useful position is the one that connects the player's current platform to a reachable exit, not simply a position where the red floor looks level.

Allow the crossing player to reach the destination before rotating the floor again.

### Use the colored passages as landmarks

The main connected areas use **red, purple, and blue** surfaces.

These colors are more reliable than ordinary left/right directions because the same corridor may appear inverted from another position.

After crossing the red platform, find the purple passage and follow the connected route toward the blue-area controls.

Some doors must be held open or operated from another room while the partner passes through.

### Reach the elevated Beta door control

The blue **Beta** passage initially appears inaccessible.

Follow the connected cable toward the elevated part of the purple section.

Look for the **lift or moving platform** that brings one Explorer high enough to reach the upper controls. The objective is not to force a direct jump from below.

Use the lift to reach the upper section, operate the required lever, and have the other Explorer check whether the Beta entrance has become accessible.

The area may require one Explorer to remain at the mechanism while the partner takes the newly opened path.

### Coordinate the blue platform and wall target

Inside the connected blue route, one player reaches a platform while the other can operate a distant mechanism.

The target may be protected by a glass cover or movable flap.

Complete the interactions in this order:

1. Open the required blue passage.
2. Move the Explorer who needs to cross onto the accessible platform.
3. Operate the control that exposes the next target.
4. Use the appropriate prototype tool on the exposed mechanism.
5. Confirm that the associated indicator activates.

An unopened protective cover is not solved by firing at it repeatedly.

### Return through the red area when required

After the blue mechanism activates, the next operation may lie in an earlier red section rather than directly ahead.

One Explorer should maintain the necessary access while the partner returns through the connected passage.

Use the newly available route to reach the remaining controls instead of repeatedly rotating the original red platform.

### Find the final lever at the purple T-junction

Return to the large **purple T-junction** near the place where an Explorer was previously flipped onto a purple panel.

Do not repeat the earlier route onto that panel.

**Turn left at the junction** and follow the connected passage. Your partner may need to hold the relevant door open.

Continue until you reach the wall-mounted lever mechanism.

Place the lever into its position and operate it to activate the remaining indicator.

Return through the now-accessible passage and rejoin your partner.

Reaching another familiar purple room does not necessarily mean a puzzle reset. These corridors reconnect in loops until the needed mechanisms are activated.

The next major section begins with the connected power machinery.

## R&D Power Network: How Do You Connect the Battery Circuits?

After Gravitonics, the Explorers reach a larger electrical network involving numbered terminals, differently colored cables, and doors that depend on the available power route.

The two players do not see one complete diagram. Each has to describe the connections available in their own control area.

The important principle is that **a powered terminal is not necessarily a useful next terminal**. Activating a branch can leave the system without a valid route to the next required device.

### Identify the numbered control areas

The battery terminals are identified by **Greek-letter prefixes and numbers**—for example, the illuminated units marked **Ψ02, Λ03, and Θ03** below. Those identifiers distinguish different terminals; a plain number such as "3" is not enough to identify one.

![Charged battery terminals with Greek-letter identifiers and connected cables in the R&D power network.](/images/we-were-here-tomorrow/rd-power-network-wiring.webp)

When communicating a connection, call out:

- **Complete terminal ID:** Letter prefix and number.
- **Cable:** Its color or physical path.
- **Next destination:** Which door or terminal receives the connection.

Before activating a branch, have your partner confirm where its outgoing cable leads.

### Complete R&D battery wiring sequence

Start from the GEN terminal and follow the connected modules in order. Each terminal is identified by a Greek letter and a number, so check both markings before interacting.

**Short route (17 terminals):**

**GEN → Ψ01 → Ψ04 → Ψ02 → Ψ03 → Ψ06 → Σ04 → Σ03 → Σ01 → Σ02 → Ψ08 → Θ03 → Δ02 → Δ03 → Λ02 → Λ03 → Λ01 → Ψ07**

**Alternative route (20 terminals):**

**GEN → Ψ01 → Ψ04 → Ψ02 → Ψ03 → Ψ06 → Σ04 → Σ03 → Σ01 → Σ02 → Ψ08 → Θ03 → Θ02 → Θ04 → Δ05 → Δ04 → Δ03 → Λ02 → Λ03 → Λ01 → Ψ07**

Both routes reach the final Ψ07 terminal, but they take different paths through the Theta and Delta sections.

The important checkpoints are:

- **Σ03:** Opens the red access route.
- **Σ02:** Opens the green access route.
- **Δ03:** Opens the blue access route.
- **Ψ07:** Final terminal used to complete the powered connection.

### Why the battery circuit stops working

Each terminal must be reached through the currently connected electrical path. A powered indicator does not mean you can skip directly to any numbered module.

If your next terminal cannot be activated, check whether you took the short route or the alternative route. Do not combine their different Theta and Delta sequences.

Follow the chosen sequence from its last confirmed connection. Once the final Ψ07 interaction succeeds, the connected battery terminals become powered and the machinery registers the completed network.

## Temporal Research Centre: How Do You Restore Access and Power?

The Temporal Research Centre introduces a different kind of separation.

Instead of merely standing in different rooms, the Explorers can interact with different versions of the facility. An accessible object or pathway in one version may be blocked, absent, or incomplete in the other.

The main tasks involve ventilation routes, electrical cubes, temporary bridges, time-exchange devices, and Fuse panels.

### Follow the labeled ventilation routes

Near the entrance, one Explorer reaches ventilation passages while the partner operates the corresponding hatch controls.

The ducts have identifiers such as **H36B** and **H34E**.

Read the full identifier from the duct before asking the partner to open anything. A hatch with a similar number but a different letter is not necessarily connected to the same passage.

The controller may only be able to keep **one hatch open at a time**.

Once the correct hatch opens, the traveling Explorer moves through it and reports the next room, symbol, or device reached.

### Use the time-exchange spheres

The facility contains spherical devices that let the two Explorers exchange their positions between versions of the area.

Before exchanging, check whether your current version contains something your partner still needs:

- A reachable Fuse
- An electrical charging station
- A closed or open access door
- A bridge position
- A lever or socket available only from one side

Do not assume that an item collected in one version will automatically appear in the other version in a usable place.

At certain points, both Explorers must reach their corresponding sides of the sphere before the exchange can occur.

### Open routes with charged cubes

Some doors and lifts require electrical power rather than ordinary cube weight.

Locate the charging device, prepare an electrified cube, and place it on the required target or transfer it through an available opening.

If a door remains unpowered, determine whether the cube is reaching the electrical contact rather than merely landing somewhere close to the mechanism.

A weighted pressure plate and an electrical connection are different requirements. Adding weight to the wrong surface will not replace a missing charge.

### Use the temporary bridge controls

The research centre also contains bridge mechanisms connected to numbered positions and distinctive symbols.

The Explorer who controls the bridge should first learn which placement creates a usable route for the partner.

Moving a bridge may help one player while blocking the other from returning.

Before changing the placement again, confirm that the traveling Explorer has crossed to stable ground or has another route available.

## Temporal Research Centre Fuse Puzzle: What Is the Eight-Slot Arrangement?

The later Fuse mechanism uses **eight slots arranged in two rows of four**.

The puzzle requires the correct combination of Fuse states and empty positions. Filling every slot is not the solution.

The two Explorers may see Fuse colors or states differently depending on the equipment and their current side of the facility.

### Use the eight-slot warning diagram

The nearby diagram explicitly says **"danger: avoid this fuse placement."** It illustrates a hazardous configuration, not the normal safe setting. However, reproducing that arrangement in the completed route causes the machine to react and opens access to the next crystal devices.

![The warning diagram showing the eight-slot Fuse placement used to trigger the machinery response.](/images/we-were-here-tomorrow/trc-fuse-arrangement.webp)

Read the slots **left to right**:

- **Top row:** Orange → Empty → Green → Purple
- **Bottom row:** Green → Purple → Orange → Empty

Leave both **Empty** positions unfilled. Make sure the partner reading the diagram distinguishes an empty slot from a pale-colored Fuse; they are not interchangeable.

### After the machinery reacts

When the arrangement registers, check the newly available crystal mechanisms. Both Explorers must reach the corresponding crystal controls before advancing to the next part of the facility.

Some earlier Fuse panels instead use flashing slots that indicate the current position to fill. Those are separate interactions: follow their active indicators first, then use the two-row warning arrangement at the eight-slot panel.

## Xenobotany: How Do You Grow the Right Plant in the Future?

Xenobotany changes how the Explorers affect each other's environment.

One Explorer works with seeds, cultivation equipment, and conditions in an earlier version of the laboratory. The partner sees the resulting growth in the later version.

**Planting a seed is only the beginning.** A plant must also receive the correct conditions and grow into the form required by the obstacle ahead.

A plant that appears in the future is not necessarily the right specimen or the right growth state.

### Determine what the future room requires

Before preparing a seed, have the Explorer in the future describe the obstacle.

Ask the Explorer in the future what needs to change **before** choosing a seed:

- **Height or platforms:** Grow a specimen that provides a reachable climbing route.
- **Passage blocked by growth:** Change the relevant plant's state rather than planting another random specimen.
- **Equipment needs power:** Note the connected plant or condition; the later power-chain puzzle requires more than simply growing a plant.

The earlier Explorer can then match the seed and environment to that specific objective.

### Match the seed colors and environment

Cultivation can involve both color combinations and a condition such as sunlight or water.

Two distinct combinations encountered during the route include:

- **Blue + Purple + sunlight:** One of the sun-grown specimens used in the greenhouse route.
- **Blue + Red + water:** The purple growth encountered later in the laboratory.

These are separate plant requirements, not two stages of one universal recipe.

Use the corresponding seed and environmental setting for the plant that the future Explorer actually needs.

A specimen that stays small or develops incorrectly may indicate that its cultivation conditions are not satisfied.

### Use the earlier and later versions of the same room

The two players should identify matching landmarks across their versions of the laboratory.

A pot, door, machine, or flooded area may occupy a related position while having a different state in the other time period.

Once the earlier Explorer completes the planting conditions, the future Explorer checks the resulting growth.

Confirm the result in the future version: a climbing route should become reachable, an obstruction should change, or the linked device should respond. If nothing changes, recheck the seed symbols and environmental requirement in the past version before creating more plants.

When a new seed becomes available, bring it to the relevant cultivation equipment before leaving the room.

The later purple growth requires the **water-grown Blue + Red** combination. Prepare it in the earlier version, then return to its corresponding location in the future and inspect the resulting passage or device before moving on.

## Time Loop Puzzle: How Do You Replay Actions to Open the Route?

The time-rewind machinery can **replay earlier actions**, letting the Explorers divide a timed route across multiple cycles. Cubes, plates, moving platforms, and levers are not all meant to be handled during the same uninterrupted run.

### Record the setup, then use the replay

1. **Establish a starting action.** Place the needed cube or activate the control that prepares the first section.
2. **Build the next connection.** Reach the plate or lever that makes a platform or shortcut accessible.
3. **Use the next cycle.** Let the earlier action replay, then take the route it opens rather than repeating the entire setup.
4. **Cross during the active window.** Have the partner confirm that the platform or door is available before committing to the jump or moving on.

If the timed crossing still fails, identify **which interaction happens too late**. A late pressure-plate activation may require changing the earlier action order; a long detour may mean an available shortcut was missed. Repeating the same run faster will not fix an incorrect dependency.

Once the final interaction registers, continue through the opened passage.

## Xenobotany Power Network: Why Won't the Plants Activate the Machinery?

When the route returns to the plant-related machinery, the objective changes from **growing a route** to **completing a conditional power chain**. Each condition must make the next device available; one visibly healthy plant is not enough.

### Start with the eye sensor and electrical plant

Read the condition shown on the connected control panel. In one linked setup, the **eye sensor detecting an Explorer** activates an electrical plant; the powered plant then changes the visibility of the next plant.

Check each transition on the other Explorer's side before changing a second condition. An inactive downstream plant may simply be missing its upstream trigger.

### Change cactus and algae states to unblock the route

The later machinery also uses cactus-like growth and algae-like growth that can become **non-solid or otherwise stop obstructing a passage**. Watch what changes when an associated condition activates; do not assume every plant has to be removed or regrown.

Where a powered cube or electrical plant is part of the chain, place it at the active contact and verify that the next device responds. Moving the cube without restoring its required trigger may interrupt the chain again.

### Activate the final connected sequence

1. Identify the first condition that can be activated from your current position.
2. Verify which device or plant it powers in the partner's area.
3. Change the next plant's state or electrical connection as indicated.
4. Repeat until the last mechanism registers and the exit opens.

If the final device stays inactive, follow the conditional connections backward to the earliest missing trigger rather than repeatedly operating the last switch.

## Rift Zero: How Do You Reach the Final Choice?

The final section leads the Explorers toward the machinery controlling **Rift Zero**.

The route continues to use both players' equipment, but the goal shifts from opening ordinary research doors to activating the components needed for the rift controls.

### Restore access to the final apparatus

Follow the connected mechanisms into the final control area.

Both Explorers may need access cards to operate their respective sides of the machinery. Inspect the nearby containers and accessible equipment when a panel reports that its card is missing.

Insert the required cards and operate the corresponding controls to bring the final apparatus online.

Do not confuse an individual control panel becoming active with the complete rift sequence being finished.

### Close or Stabilize Rift Zero?

The final consoles display two alternatives:

- **Close Rift**
- **Stabilize Rift**

![The final Rift Zero controls showing Close Rift and Stabilize Rift as alternative choices.](/images/we-were-here-tomorrow/rift-zero-ending-choice.webp)

These are separate endings, not two steps of one procedure. Agree on your choice before both Explorers commit to the final interaction.

Once the chosen operation is activated, follow the final interaction and resulting sequence.

### Can you see the other ending?

Yes. After finishing one ending, the chapter-selection system can be used to return to the final stage and attempt the other choice.

This avoids replaying the entire laboratory route simply to view the alternative conclusion.

Your Rift Zero choice is independent of the earlier laboratory puzzles. If you're aiming to unlock both ending achievements, check the [Dawn of Tomorrow and Tomorrow May Never Come requirements](/we-were-here-tomorrow/achievements#rift-zero-endings) before selecting the remaining outcome.
