**We Were Here Tomorrow** uses two explorers who often see different parts of the same puzzle. One player may have the instructions while the other has the controls, and the correct solution usually depends on connecting those two sets of information.

After the Antarctic opening, both explorers awaken inside a Norček Industries research facility. The first major obstacles involve console symbols and equipment transfers, followed by a laboratory where one explorer is shrunk to work inside machinery. Later sections combine the Hook Gun, Goop Gun, access cards, lasers, and rotating rooms.

This walkthrough follows the main route through the opening facilities, Exotic Matter Laboratory, Particle Accelerator, and the connected gravity puzzles. The later Temporal Research Centre and Rift Zero expedition are not covered here.

A few puzzles display symbols or control states that change between attempts. Where that happens, use the method for matching the current display rather than copying a previous combination.

## Opening and Wellness Check: Reach the First Console

After the opening sequence, both explorers wake inside the Norček facility. Director Kathleen Bowe explains that the pair have been frozen beneath Antarctica for several years and directs them through a rehabilitation area.

The first outdoor stretch is straightforward. Follow the illuminated route, climb across the accessible ledges, and reach the mechanism that both players must operate together.

Hold the interaction on both sides to activate it. A single player cannot complete this interaction alone.

Beyond the rehabilitation sequence, the explorers are separated into rooms connected by communication equipment. The first proper console puzzle starts here.

### How to use the three-symbol console

One explorer sees **three symbols on a display**. The partner has physical buttons and controls corresponding to those symbols.

The player reading the display should describe each symbol in its displayed order. The other player activates the matching controls.

1. Examine all three symbols before beginning.
2. Identify the corresponding buttons on the opposite side.
3. Activate them in the requested sequence.
4. Watch the console for the accepted input before continuing.

The symbols can change when the sequence resets, so memorizing one set of pictures is less useful than recognizing the correct controls.

### Match the machinery symbols to the physical controls

The next display uses pictures of mechanical equipment rather than abstract symbols.

The corresponding controls include a lever, a button-like mechanism, and a pressure-operated device. One player sees the sequence while the other can interact with the equipment.

Follow the wires to distinguish the available controls. The pictures are not necessarily arranged in the same order as the objects in the room.

Once the correct sequence registers, the console confirms success and the door unlocks.

Do not continue pressing controls after the successful sequence. The next interaction takes place beyond the newly opened passage.

## Canister Transfer: Open the Doors and Send the Container

The following rooms contain several interconnected doors and a canister transfer mechanism.

One explorer operates the controls that switch which doorway is accessible. The other searches the newly accessible rooms and retrieves the container.

A door control can open one passage while closing another. This makes it easy to strand your partner temporarily on the wrong side of a doorway.

### Retrieve and transfer the canister

Start by having the player near the door controls open the route into the room containing the canister.

The other explorer should:

1. Enter the accessible room and locate the canister.
2. Return to the transfer apparatus.
3. Place the canister in the available holder.
4. Ask the partner to operate the transfer mechanism.
5. Collect the canister from the receiving side.

The players must also switch the doors as needed to reach the correct side of the apparatus.

You do not need to hold every control simultaneously. The important distinction is between a door-switching control and the mechanism that actually transfers the container.

If the receiving player cannot reach the transferred item, check the door configuration first. Moving the canister does not automatically open every connecting passage.

The successful transfer allows the pair to move deeper into the facility and reach the experimental prototype equipment.

## Shrinking Laboratory: Get Inside the Circuit Machine

The next major laboratory introduces equipment that changes an explorer's size.

A player-sized experimental chamber connects to a miniature work area. The explorer operating the outside machinery remains normal-sized, while the other player can shrink and enter areas that would otherwise be inaccessible.

Begin by exploring the laboratory and locating the access cards, machinery controls, and the chamber used for shrinking.

One explorer enters the chamber. The partner activates the machinery and then opens the exit leading into the miniature workspace.

### How to release the shrunken explorer

Shrinking does not automatically give the player a usable exit.

The normal-sized explorer must operate the chamber controls and open the appropriate passage. Once released, the smaller player can traverse the laboratory equipment and enter the circuit assembly.

Avoid jumping from the edge of the miniature work area before identifying a reachable landing. Falling can return the player to an earlier position instead of advancing through the machinery.

The next puzzle requires the small explorer to manipulate components while the partner reads the information necessary to place them correctly.

## Circuit Matching Puzzle: Which Components Go in Each Socket?

The circuit assembly contains multiple components and sockets marked with colored fasteners.

One player can inspect the components and their visual designs. The other has access to the reference information that associates each design with a set of symbols.

The circuit cannot be solved reliably by matching the most obvious color on a component to the nearest socket.

Each component has a group of acceptable symbols, while the sockets display their own combinations. The goal is to place each component in a compatible location.

### Match the six circuit components

The component reference shows six illustrated designs, each marked by three symbols. The symbols correspond to the colored markings on the sockets.

The color key is:

- Green — Square
- Gray — Pentagon
- Red — Triangle
- Purple — Star
- Blue — Y-shaped symbol
- Yellow — Plus sign

![The six component designs and their matching socket symbols.](/images/we-were-here-tomorrow/circuit-component-matching.webp)

Read the six component combinations by their position on the reference display:

- **Top left**: Triangle + Y + Star
- **Top right**: Triangle + Pentagon + Plus
- **Middle left**: Triangle + Pentagon + Star
- **Middle right**: Square + Pentagon + Plus
- **Bottom left**: Square + Y + Star
- **Bottom right**: Square + Y + Plus

These positions identify the component illustrations on the reference display, not the physical socket locations.

The explorer inside the machine must compare each component's three symbols with the markings around an available socket.

Some sockets share two symbols, so matching only two colors can place a component incorrectly. Use all three conditions. When more than one socket seems possible, place the component with the fewest matching locations first.

Once all six components are correctly placed, activate the circuit and continue through the powered passage.

## 3D Resin Mixing Puzzle: Correct Chemical Order

The resin mixing station is another cooperative puzzle inside the laboratory.

The small explorer works with the container and dispensing apparatus, while the other player reads the recipe and operates the surrounding equipment.

The recipe calls for equal portions of Acrylonitrile and Butadiene, with Styrene added before and after them.

### Correct 3D Resin Recipe

The mixing machine has five numbered nozzle positions. Some dispense the same chemical:

- **No. 1 — Butadiene**
- **No. 2 — Styrene**
- **No. 3 — Acrylonitrile**
- **No. 4 — Styrene**
- **No. 5 — Butadiene**

![The numbered resin nozzles and chemical labels.](/images/we-were-here-tomorrow/resin-mixing-controls.webp)

For the completed mixture, use this sequence:

1. **No. 4 — Styrene**
2. **No. 3 — Acrylonitrile**
3. **No. 1 — Butadiene**
4. **No. 4 — Styrene**

The two middle chemicals need equal portions. Styrene must come both before and after them.

Align the container beneath each selected nozzle before dispensing. After the final Styrene portion, activate the processing mechanism.

The successful mixture powers the next laboratory mechanism and opens the way forward.

### Why the resin mixture fails

A combination containing the correct middle ingredients can still fail if Styrene is missing from the beginning or end.

The machine can also fail to dispense correctly when the container is not aligned under the outlet.

If the expected liquid does not enter the container, adjust its position and operate the control again rather than assuming the ingredient has already been added.

## Floppy Disk Puzzle: Open Both Numbered Doors

The miniature explorer eventually reaches a computer-like passage blocked by two oversized floppy disk doors marked **1** and **2**.

The other player can inspect the equipment outside, but the two doors will not open until the direction-matching controls are completed.

If the miniature route appears to end at an inaccessible pit or a closed floppy disk, return toward the shrinking chamber and coordinate with the partner at the outside controls. There is no need to force a jump through the blocked passage.

### First direction puzzle: Four 90-degree dials

The first control has **four rotating direction indicators**. Each changes direction in 90-degree increments.

One explorer can see the required directions, while the partner operates the rotating indicators.

The target directions use:

- Up
- Right
- Down
- Left

![The four direction indicators used to unlock the first floppy disk door.](/images/we-were-here-tomorrow/floppy-direction-dials.webp)

The player reading the target display should call out the four directions in order.

The player operating the rotating mechanism must release the control to stop each indicator at its required direction.

Allow for the delay between releasing the control and the indicator actually stopping. Releasing exactly when the pointer appears aligned can cause it to advance one more step.

An incorrect position turns the corresponding indicator red and can reset the sequence.

The target directions may change after a reset, so read the display again before retrying.

When all four indicators register correctly, they turn blue and **Floppy Disk Door 1 opens**.

### Second direction puzzle: Four 45-degree dials

Enter the newly accessible section and move toward the next set of controls.

This time, the indicators rotate in **45-degree increments**, introducing diagonal directions.

In addition to the four cardinal directions, you must distinguish:

- Upper-left
- Upper-right
- Lower-left
- Lower-right

Read all four target orientations from the partner's display, then stop the rotating indicators one at a time.

Do not reuse the first puzzle's directions. The second mechanism requires its own four matches.

When the second sequence registers successfully, the remaining floppy disk barrier opens.

Continue through the miniature passage toward the **3D printer**.

## 3D Printer Grid Puzzle: Get the Green Key Card

After passing the second floppy disk barrier, the miniature explorer reaches the 3D printer controls.

One player has directional controls and a central activation button. The other can observe a **4×4 grid of lights**.

The players must reproduce the required illuminated pattern by moving the active position and changing individual lights.

### How the 4×4 light grid works

The grid contains two important visual states:

- **Solid lights:** Positions used to identify the required pattern.
- **Blinking red indicator:** The current position being controlled.

The blinking indicator is not another fixed target. It shows which position the player can currently modify.

The explorer operating the controls moves between grid positions using the directional inputs.

The partner watching the grid should describe where the blinking indicator moves after each input.

This also helps establish the correct orientation of the controls, which can be confusing when the two players view the machinery from different angles.

### Match the illuminated pattern

Once both players understand which direction moves the active position, work through the grid:

1. Identify the next position that needs to be changed.
2. Guide the blinking indicator to that position.
3. Press the **central button** to toggle its light.
4. Confirm the result before moving to the next position.

The central button changes the state of the selected position. Moving the indicator alone does not complete the pattern.

Continue until the required lights match.

Avoid repeatedly pressing the central button while troubleshooting. Toggling a position again can undo a previously correct light.

### Collect the Green Key Card

The completed grid activates the printer mechanism.

The resulting item is the **Green Key Card** needed for the earlier laboratory access door.

Collect the card, return to the size-changing chamber, and have your partner restore you to normal size.

Rejoin your partner and return to the access panel that was missing the green card.

Completing that earlier door requirement allows both explorers to proceed toward the prototype equipment.

</section>

## Hook Gun and Goop Gun: How to Open the Powered Doors

After clearing the shrinking laboratory and obtaining the Green Key Card, the explorers gain access to Norček's prototype equipment.

**The Hook Gun** attaches to compatible targets, allowing you to pull objects and manipulate mechanisms beyond normal reach.

**The Goop Gun** creates solid cubes that can serve as platforms, hold down pressure plates, or add weight to your partner.

Only one deployed cube may be available at a time. Recall it before placing another when the previous cube is still active.

### Activate the door mechanisms

The laboratory route includes doors connected to colored cables and switches.

Trace each cable from the locked door back toward its associated device.

Some devices require a Cube to hold a mechanism active. Others must be manipulated with the Hook Gun.

When several colored indicators are connected to one door, each required mechanism must be activated before the door opens.

Do not spend time trying to force open a door that has no active power. Follow the cable to its control and complete that interaction first.

### Use cubes on pressure plates

A Cube can sometimes replace one player's weight on an ordinary pressure plate.

This frees both explorers to move away while the mechanism remains active.

However, later parts of the game distinguish ordinary pressure requirements from heavier loads. A single Cube or player will not solve every pressure-based puzzle.

After opening the powered route, continue through the prototype testing rooms and toward the Exotic Matter Laboratory.

## Cargo Elevator: Fuse and Green Key Card Locations

The Cargo Elevator section contains several overlapping objectives.

The explorers encounter:

- A doorway requiring colored access cards.
- A Fuse missing from the machinery.
- A nearby anti-gravity workspace.
- Doors controlled from different sides.
- Elevated equipment accessible through Hook and Cube interactions.

One card slot is purple and the other is green.

Opening an individual side door does not mean the entire elevator puzzle is finished.

### Retrieve the purple access card

Explore the rooms around the anti-gravity machinery.

One explorer operates the door controls while the other enters the newly accessible space and retrieves the purple access card.

The purple card is associated with the area reached through the movable machinery and side passage.

After collecting it, return toward the two-card access point.

### How to get the hanging Fuse

The missing Fuse is attached to a red mechanical arm suspended above the machinery. You can see it from below, but it is initially out of reach.

First, look for the rotating fan near the upper machinery.

Use the Goop Gun on the fan mechanism to stop its movement. This makes the next interaction possible.

The other explorer should reach the elevated control area and operate the lever connected to the suspended arm.

The red arm lowers toward the player waiting underneath.

![The lowered red mechanical arm holding the Fuse.](/images/we-were-here-tomorrow/cargo-elevator-hanging-fuse.webp)

Once the arm is down, collect the Fuse and insert it into the empty socket near the access controls.

The corresponding door opens, but the main route still requires the green access card.

### Where to find the green access card

Even with the Fuse inserted and the purple card obtained, the main route still requires the green access card.

Return to the anti-gravity workspace.

Look toward the area visible through the side opening. The green card is positioned within sight of this room, but it is easy to overlook while searching the higher platforms.

Have the partner open the relevant anti-gravity room door.

Approach the opening and interact with the card from the accessible edge. You do not need to fully climb inside the opening to collect it.

Once the card is obtained, return to the access panel and complete the two-card requirement.

### The door opens, but you still cannot proceed

This usually means one of the separate requirements remains incomplete.

Check the purple card, the green card, and the Fuse-powered mechanism individually.

The nearby switches and access controls do not all operate the same doorway.

If one section opens while the main passage stays locked, return to the incomplete mechanism rather than repeating a puzzle that has already registered successfully.

The route continues into the Particle Accelerator machinery.

## Particle Accelerator: Match the 4×4 Symbols and Three Dials

The Particle Accelerator sequence separates the explorers again.

One player has a **4×4 grid of symbol buttons**, while the partner receives a sequence of symbols that must be communicated.

The player operating the grid should not simply activate every symbol they recognize. The input must match the currently displayed sequence.

### Enter the symbol sequence

Have the player viewing the symbols describe them one at a time.

Useful identifying details include:

- Whether a symbol has a filled or hollow center.
- The number and direction of its arrows.
- Whether it resembles a sun with multiple spokes.
- Whether lines or shapes cross through its center.
- The direction of curved or horn-shaped elements.

Activate the matching buttons on the 4×4 grid in the communicated order.

A wrong button can reset the sequence and produce a new set of symbols.

When that happens, begin again using the new display. Repeating the old symbols will not correct the failed attempt.

### How to solve the three-dial control

After the symbol sequence, the machinery presents three dials.

The dials use positions numbered from **1 to 5**, while the partner can see the information needed to select the correct positions.

Read the currently active indicator for each dial and communicate the corresponding numbered selection.

Complete all three.

Do not use a fixed number combination from a different attempt. The correct procedure is to read the current display and match the associated dial positions.

After successful activation, the machinery opens the next passage.

## Magnetic Stabilizer: Make One Player Count as Two

The following section includes a particularly deceptive pressure-plate puzzle.

One explorer reaches a platform or plate requiring the equivalent of two people. The partner cannot simply walk over and stand beside them because the route is separated by machinery and access barriers.

The solution uses the Goop Gun.

### Increase the player's effective weight

Position one explorer on the required plate.

The player holding the Goop Gun should shoot their partner with a cube, making them heavy enough to activate the pressure plate.

The affected player becomes heavy enough for the pressure plate to register the required load.

![The red and blue pressure indicators used to check the Magnetic Stabilizer mechanism.](/images/we-were-here-tomorrow/magnetic-stabilizer-pressure-plate.webp)

This is different from placing a separate Cube on an ordinary floor switch.

The player themselves carries the additional effective weight, allowing the mechanism to recognize the required pressure.

Once the plate registers successfully, the next part of the Magnetic Stabilizer machinery becomes accessible.

Remove the effect when it is no longer needed so the affected player can move normally again.

### Cross the moving platform

The route beyond the stabilizer uses moving platforms and rotating sections.

One explorer controls the machinery while the other moves through it.

Operate the control to bring the platform into a usable position. Wait until the moving explorer has safely reached the platform before changing its orientation again.

The orientation of the environment can change while the player is standing on the moving section.

Do not assume that an apparently vertical or overhead platform is inaccessible. The game can rotate the local gravity direction together with the platform.

Continue opening the flaps and access points required by the partner. Some of these expose additional Cube targets that cannot be reached until the corresponding cover is moved.

</section>

## Laser Calibration Puzzle: Correct Crystal Order and Beam Route

The large Laser Calibration puzzle combines several different tasks:

- Finding usable crystal material.
- Recognizing which crystal is required.
- Routing the beam through the correct rooms.
- Using the required crystal order.
- Directing the finished beam into the final machinery.

A beam reaching the correct-looking destination does not necessarily mean the configuration is valid.

### Step 1: Identify the emitter and receiving path

Begin at the main laser emitter and identify the direction of the beam.

Follow the available reflectors, crystal holders, and openings between rooms.

Some reflectors can rotate while others have fixed orientations. This limits which connections are possible.

Rather than starting by turning every reflector, trace the beam backward from the final receiving point.

Determine which reflector can deliver the beam into that receiver, then identify the preceding connection needed to feed that reflector.

The two explorers will describe directions differently when their rooms are inverted or on opposite sides of the structure.

Use recognizable landmarks and the actual laser connections instead of relying only on "left," "right," "up," and "down."

### Choose the correct crystals and beam order

The laser must pass through three different crystal effects before reaching the final apparatus.

The calibration instructions describe the required sequence:

1. **Mirror / Piercing** — the initial crystal effect.
2. **Serene / Stabilizing** — the second crystal effect.
3. **Fiery / Heating** — the final crystal effect.

The crystal catalogue contains six specimens:

- Cattonimo
- Rupium
- Cattonimus
- Cattonium
- Pingosso Crystallo
- Pingosius Crystalis

![The six crystal specimens and their shapes.](/images/we-were-here-tomorrow/laser-crystal-selection.webp)

The first important distinction is between **Pingosso Crystallo** and **Pingosius Crystalis**.

They have similar names and angular shapes, but the required Pingosius Crystalis has additional small growths, including rounded shapes near the upper spikes.

Pingosso Crystallo lacks that same combination of features.

### Route the beam through the correct rooms

The successful route sends the beam through the **yellow room before the turquoise room**, followed by the final crystal stage.

This order matters. A beam can reach the receiver and still be rejected if it passes through the crystal effects in the wrong sequence.

Start by selecting the correct crystal in the yellow section. Direct the beam through it, then use the available reflectors to pass the beam toward the turquoise section.

Continue through the stabilizing stage before sending the beam through the final heating crystal.

Use the final reflector to direct the processed beam into the central receiving apparatus.

A successful configuration activates the machinery and opens the next passage.

### Fix "Wrong Crystal Configuration"

If the laser reaches the central apparatus but the display still reports **Wrong Crystal Configuration**, the receiving path alone is not the issue.

Check the crystal sequence and, in particular, the two similarly named Pingosso specimens.

The required Pingosius Crystalis has the additional small rounded growths near the top. Selecting the wrong specimen can produce a valid-looking beam that fails the calibration.

After correcting the selection, restore the yellow-to-turquoise order and direct the beam into the final receiver again.

## Six Rings Puzzle: Correct Symbol Order

The ring corridor contains six numbered rings with similar circular symbols. One explorer sees the required symbol sequence, while the partner must identify and activate the corresponding numbered rings.

For the following target symbols, the successful input is:

**Ring 4 → Ring 1 → Ring 5**

### Match each symbol to its ring

**First — Ring 4**

Look for eight small hollow circles arranged around one larger central circle.

**Second — Ring 1**

Find the crosshair symbol with arrows pointing outward from its center.

The outward-facing arrows matter. Do not confuse it with the similar design whose arrows point inward.

**Third — Ring 5**

Find two overlapping circles. One consists of horizontal lines, while the other is a hollow ring.

Look closely at the lined circle: the matching version does not have an additional outer circular border.

### Activate the rings

Have the explorer at the rings activate the matching controls in the requested sequence.

For the three symbols described above, use **4, then 1, then 5**.

When the final ring registers correctly, the barriers open and the explorers can advance through the corridor.

If your displayed target symbols differ, match the symbols currently shown rather than repeating these numbers.

## Gravity Puzzle: Rotate the Red Floor and Reach the Blue Room

The next major environment contains upside-down rooms, colored floors, rotating platforms, and passageways that connect in unexpected directions.

This is one of the most disorienting parts of the route because the room's apparent floor changes as the machinery moves.

The explorers must coordinate their positions before activating the controls.

### Step 1: Rotate the red L-shaped platform

Find the mechanism controlling the large **red L-shaped floor**.

One explorer operates the rotation control while the other uses the new orientation to reach previously inaccessible paths.

Pay attention to where the moving platform will connect after rotating.

Simply turning it to a new angle does not guarantee that your partner can reach the intended exit.

The correct orientation creates access to another corridor or staircase.

Once the player has crossed, the mechanism can be moved again to establish the next connection.

### Step 2: Open the colored passageways

The route contains purple, red, and blue sections.

These colors are useful navigation landmarks because the same physical space can appear upside down from the other explorer's perspective.

After using the red floor, locate the purple section and follow the available passage toward the controls connected to the blue area.

Some doors are controlled by mechanisms located on the other side of the room.

The explorer operating those controls must maintain access while the partner passes through.

### Step 3: Follow the yellow cable toward the blue route

The blue passage initially appears inaccessible.

Look for the yellow cable connected to its mechanism and follow it toward the elevated portion of the purple area.

There is a route leading upward from the purple section.

The player who reaches the relevant control can open the blue passage for the partner.

Once the blue route is available, the other explorer should move into position rather than remaining at the earlier red platform.

### Step 4: Coordinate the blue platform and wall target

A later interaction requires one player to stand at the correct blue platform while the other activates a target.

The platform and nearby controls are connected through colored wiring.

Position the player at the required mechanism before attempting the shot.

Some targets cannot be activated until another control opens the glass or access flap in front of them.

The sequence therefore depends on both players being in the appropriate positions:

1. Open the passage into the blue area.
2. Move the required explorer onto the blue platform.
3. Operate the control exposing the next target.
4. Have the partner activate the available mechanism.
5. Confirm that the corresponding indicator has registered.

Do not repeatedly shoot the target while its protective cover remains closed.

### Find the final lever from the purple T-junction

After activating the blue mechanism, return toward the purple passages.

Look for the large open **purple T-junction** near the area where one explorer was previously flipped onto a purple panel.

This time, do not return to that panel.

**Turn left at the junction** and continue through the connecting passage.

Your partner may need to keep the door open while you move through the route.

Follow the passage until you reach the wall-mounted lever position. Place the lever into the mechanism and operate it.

This activates the remaining indicator.

Return toward your partner using the opened passage, then continue into the next separated-room section.

If you reach the same rotating purple panel again, you have taken the earlier route instead of the left-hand passage at the junction.

### Why do the doors keep leading back to the same room?

The colored pathways can form loops until their connected mechanisms are activated.

A door that opens into an already visited area is not necessarily the progression exit. It may simply create a connection that allows the other explorer to reach the correct control.

Track the completed indicators and identify which mechanism remains inactive.

The reliable way through this area is to use the wire connections, platform positions, and colored room landmarks together.

Moving randomly between red, blue, and purple passages makes it very easy to return to the same junction without activating anything new.
