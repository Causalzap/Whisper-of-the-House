You can experience Ideologies without choosing a new Origin or Civic. Start by deciding whether your empire will emphasize leaders, exploration, economic development, or ideological influence. Stellaris: Willpower changes what it means to build a powerful empire. Your Ethics are no longer limited to domestic policies and faction approval. With enough support, an Ethic can become an **Ideology** that reaches foreign systems, influences populations, unlocks empire-wide Tenets, and shapes diplomacy.

The biggest early mistake is treating ideological expansion like ordinary territorial expansion. Claiming another system does not automatically strengthen your beliefs, and covering foreign territory with Ideological Reach does not mean the neighboring empire has adopted your Ideology.

Your first decision is whether to use one of Willpower's new Origins or an existing empire setup. Golden Generation emphasizes leader development, Lost Makers adds a Precursor investigation, and Great Mosaic provides an extra Civic slot. Whichever start you choose, keep your economy stable while developing the faction support needed for your Ideology.

<section id="willpower-content">

## What Does Stellaris Willpower Add, and What Is Free in 4.6?

The **Willpower expansion** launched alongside the free **Stellaris 4.6 Corona Borealis** update, but they introduce different content. Willpower adds the major Ideology systems, three Origins, five Fanatic Civics, Galactic Halls, and the Force of Will Ambition. The accompanying free update changes and fixes existing gameplay systems, including leaders and empire development.

### What is included in the expansion?

The major additions are:

- **Ideologies and Gestalt Directives:** turn powerful beliefs into a shared political and galactic force.
- **Fanatic Factions and Absolute Ethics:** strengthen your commitment to one Ethic.
- **Tenets:** choose bonuses that affect every empire following an Ideology.
- **Galactic Halls:** construct a relocatable megastructure that supports your Ideology.
- **Force of Will:** pursue victory through galactic ideological conversion.
- **Three Origins:** Golden Generation, Lost Makers, and Great Mosaic.
- **Five new Fanatic Civics:** unlock different economic, diplomatic, spiritual, or military playstyles.
- **Ideological Diplomacy and Espionage:** influence adoption, interfere with rival Ideologies, and impose beliefs through war.

The expansion also includes ideological events, Paragons, buildings, and additional cosmetic content.

### Are Scenarios included in Willpower or Stellaris 4.6?

**No.** Scenarios were not released as part of Stellaris 4.6.

They are planned for the later 4.7 Capricornus update.

If you are looking for a Scenario option while setting up a Willpower campaign, its absence is not a missing unlock in this expansion.

### Can you turn Ideologies off?

Yes. Ideologies have a game setup option, allowing you to disable the system if you want to play without it.

If you intend to try Willpower's main mechanics, leave Ideologies enabled when creating the galaxy.

For an initial campaign, keeping the default Ideological Reach settings also gives you a more straightforward way to judge how the system behaves.

## Which Stellaris Willpower Origin Should You Choose?

Willpower adds **Golden Generation**, **Lost Makers**, and **Great Mosaic**.

These Origins lead to different opening experiences, so the best choice depends on what you want your first campaign to accomplish.

### Golden Generation: Build Powerful Leaders

**Golden Generation** is the choice for a campaign centered on developing exceptional leaders.

Your empire begins with a **Golden Academy**, where promising students are trained through a sequence of events and decisions.

The choices you make during their education influence the leaders they become.

You can focus their development around different leader classes, including Officials, Commanders, and Scientists.

#### How Do You Train Your First Golden Academy Graduates?

Check the Golden Academy and follow its event chain while continuing normal empire development.

Do not neglect expansion and research while waiting for the students to graduate.

The first cohort takes approximately **20 in-game years** to complete its training. Once the students graduate, you can recruit the resulting leaders and benefit from their special traits.

A Golden Generation leader can receive substantial effective skill bonuses without consuming normal Leader Capacity.

These leaders can become especially valuable when assigned to positions that already benefit from high effective skill.

#### Can you train unlimited Golden Generations?

No.

The Origin provides **one Golden Generation cohort**, rather than a repeating cycle that continuously produces new groups of exceptional leaders.

Your Academy can continue providing benefits afterward, and later events can involve graduates, but the initial student-training sequence is not an endlessly repeatable recruitment system.

This changes how you should approach the early events.

Treat major educational decisions as investments in a small group of important leaders, not as disposable choices you can repeat for the next generation.

### Lost Makers: Find the Creators of Your Machine Empire

**Lost Makers** is for Machine Intelligences.

Instead of beginning with a complete understanding of their own creation, your machines must investigate their lost origins.

The story connects your empire to one of the galaxy's existing **Precursors**.

#### Where Does the Lost Makers Investigation Begin?

At game setup, make sure an eligible Precursor is available.

Your campaign can use a selected Precursor or allow the game to choose from the enabled possibilities.

After starting:

1. Locate the archaeological site connected to your Origin.
2. Assign a suitable Scientist and begin excavation.
3. Continue ordinary surveying and economic development.
4. Follow the special events and decisions tied to your discoveries.
5. Apply the rewards to your current research and Machine Intelligence economy.

The Origin's opening archaeological progress is especially important because its Precursor storyline depends on that investigation.

Do not leave the introductory site unattended while expecting the rest of the story to develop automatically.

#### Does Lost Makers always have the same outcome?

No.

Different Precursors can produce different story content and rewards.

These can include research bonuses, special modifiers, new choices, or unique ideological effects associated with the Precursor.

That makes Lost Makers appealing if you want to explore a new campaign mystery instead of following the same predetermined rewards on every run.

### Great Mosaic: Start With an Extra Civic

**Great Mosaic** is the most direct of the three Origins.

It gives your empire an additional Civic slot from the beginning, letting you combine more bonuses and mechanics during empire creation.

Unlike Golden Generation and Lost Makers, it does not center on an extended Origin-specific narrative.

Your population also follows a more diverse collection of Ethics.

#### How Should You Use the Extra Civic Slot?

Build your empire around the additional Civic rather than treating it as a bonus to an otherwise ordinary setup.

Choose Civics whose effects complement one another.

For example, if you want a diplomatic and immigration-focused campaign, use your additional slot to reinforce that direction. If you want an industrial economy, combine bonuses that actually improve the production chain you intend to build.

The tradeoff is ideological diversity.

Because Great Mosaic supports attraction toward multiple Ethics, it may be harder to concentrate your population behind a single Fanatic Faction.

If your long-term goal is a powerful Ideology, monitor your factions before assuming that the additional Civic alone will make ideological growth easier.

## Which New Fanatic Civic Should You Pick?

Willpower introduces five new Fanatic Civics, each associated with a specific Ethic.

These Civics are not merely small starting bonuses. Several change how you should handle war, migration, development, or internal progression.

<GuideTable
  caption="The five new Willpower Fanatic Civics and their main gameplay mechanics."
  columns={willpowerCivicColumns}
  rows={willpowerCivicRows}
/>

### Warmongers: Fight Worthy Opponents

**Warmongers** rewards conflict with comparable or stronger enemies.

Its military economy benefits from challenging opponents, but remaining at peace or fighting substantially weaker enemies brings penalties.

This means the Civic does not reward every war equally.

Before starting a conflict, compare your relative strength with the target. A quick war against a weak opponent may not provide the bonuses you expected.

The early challenge is keeping your economy stable between worthwhile wars.

Do not build around constant military aggression unless you can support the upkeep that comes with it.

### Social Concord: Pressure Other Empires to Make Peace

**Social Concord** provides a diplomatic way to pressure empires involved in aggressive wars.

Its **Sow Peace** action can increase pressure on an aggressor, encouraging the conflict to end.

This is useful for players who want to influence the diplomatic situation without personally invading every opponent.

However, choosing a Pacifist-focused strategy does not remove the need to maintain defenses.

You still need enough economic and military security to survive neighboring empires that refuse peaceful outcomes.

### Open Stars Sovereignty: Encourage Immigration

**Open Stars Sovereignty** rewards immigration and species diversity.

It includes access to an **Immigration Bureau** Starbase building and bonuses associated with first contact, migration, and a diverse population.

Explore early, meet neighboring empires, and develop useful diplomatic relationships.

The Civic becomes more interesting as you gain access to additional species and immigration opportunities.

If you isolate yourself from other empires, you will miss many of the interactions that make this Civic valuable.

### Path of the Self: Develop Spiritual Cultivation

**Path of the Self** introduces **Spirit Cultivation**, allowing a Spiritualist empire to develop a collection of bonuses through its cultivation decisions.

These can improve areas such as Unity, Stability, leadership, and other aspects of empire development.

Check the active cultivation situation and the costs of accelerating it.

The best choice depends on whether your empire needs immediate development or can afford a longer-term investment in stronger modifiers.

### Megacity Architects: Build an Urban Economy

**Megacity Architects** emphasizes urban construction, district flexibility, and advanced development.

It provides the **Urban Expansion Framework** decision and an early connection to Ecumenopolis-related progression.

Its value is not limited to the late game.

The additional construction flexibility can support a growing empire long before its most expensive industrial projects are complete.

Plan your district choices around the actual Jobs and resources you need rather than maximizing buildings without a clear production purpose.

## What Should You Do First in Stellaris Willpower?

Your first priority is still to establish a working Stellaris economy.

Willpower adds a new set of objectives, but it does not remove the need for exploration, research, resource production, or defensible borders.

The strongest opening is one where your Ideology grows alongside your existing empire rather than competing with every other investment.

### What Should You Prioritize Before Your First Ideology?

**If you have not met another empire:** Keep surveying, complete First Contact, and establish a working economy. You do not need to delay normal expansion while waiting for an Ideology.

**If you have First Contact but no eligible Fanatic Faction:** Open the Factions interface and check which Ethic has sufficient support. Your next goal is to develop an eligible faction rather than spending resources on ideological infrastructure you cannot use yet.

**If Advocate Faction is available:** Check the resource cost, your economy, and the faction's approval before establishing your Ideology.

Follow the [Ideologies Guide](/stellaris-willpower/ideologies) for the complete founding process.

### What Should You Prioritize After Founding an Ideology?

Continue expanding your economy while monitoring Ideological Strength and your domestic followers.

If Strength is growing, work toward the next Tenet unlock or Galactic Hall technology.

If Strength is falling, inspect its modifiers before investing in additional expansion.

If your economy is struggling, stabilize basic resources and Unity production before committing to expensive ideological actions.

Your next goal should solve the problem currently limiting your empire, not simply follow the next available unlock.

</section>

<section id="ideological-reach">

## What Should You Do When Your Ideology Stops Spreading?

First, determine whether you are losing support inside your empire or failing to influence neighboring systems.

**If domestic Ideological Strength is falling:** Inspect the Strength tooltip, faction approval, and penalties before spending more resources on foreign expansion.

**If your Ideology is stable but cannot cross a border:** Check the reach of your nearest colonies and whether a rival Ideology is applying stronger pressure. Additional infrastructure near the frontier may help more than improving a distant capital.

**If foreign systems show your Ideology but neighboring empires have not adopted it:** Reach and formal adoption are separate. Expanding the colored area of the map does not automatically change another empire's Official Ideology.

Use the [Ideologies Guide](/stellaris-willpower/ideologies) to diagnose Reach, Strength, Momentum, Adoption, and rival ideological pressure.

</section>

<section id="tenets-progression">

## When Should You Prioritize Tenets in Stellaris Willpower?

Your first three Tenet slots unlock at **250, 500, and 750 Ideological Strength**, corresponding to Intensity I, II, and III.

Before spending Influence on a selection, consider whether the available bonus addresses a current problem.

A Stability or Unity bonus may help an empire struggling with domestic development, while a production bonus becomes more valuable when the corresponding Jobs already form a significant part of your economy.

If your Ideology is approaching another Strength threshold, maintaining its growth may be more important than pursuing an unrelated expansion.

If the available Tenets are unsuitable, check the selection and reformulation options before committing resources.

For individual effects, eligibility conditions, special empire variants, and replacement costs, see the [Tenets Guide](/stellaris-willpower/tenets).

</section>

<section id="galactic-hall">

## When Is a Galactic Hall Worth Building?

A Galactic Hall is most valuable when its location solves a problem your existing colonies cannot handle efficiently.

A developed colony is the better candidate when you want to strengthen its local production. A system near a competing Ideology becomes more attractive when your immediate goal is to extend Ideological Reach beyond the current frontier.

Do not relocate an existing Hall solely because another system looks slightly better. The move costs Energy and starts a 10-year relocation cooldown, so the new position needs to justify the interruption.

If your main problem is Ideological Strength rather than Reach, examine your other ideological infrastructure before committing to another expensive construction project.

See [Galactic Hall construction, Chambers, and ideological infrastructure](/stellaris-willpower/ideologies) for the available upgrades and their requirements.

</section>

<section id="absolute-ethics">

## Is an Absolute Ethic Worth Adopting?

An Absolute Ethic strengthens your commitment to one governing Ethic but removes the others. Whether that helps depends on how much of your empire still relies on its previous Ethic combination.

Before changing, examine the Civics, policies, and bonuses that could become unavailable. A stronger primary Ethic is less attractive if the transformation disrupts an economic setup that is already working.

For **Force of Will**, the decision is more direct: adopting an Absolute Ethic is required before Stage 2. Prepare the Unity and complete the necessary transformation before attempting to advance the Ambition.

Without Force of Will, you can compare the new bonuses against the flexibility of your current government instead of making the change immediately.

The [Absolute Ethic requirements and Force of Will progression](/stellaris-willpower/force-of-will) explain the additional steps when pursuing that Ambition.

</section>

<section id="late-game">

## Should You Pursue Force of Will?

**Force of Will** offers a galactic victory through ideological conversion instead of requiring direct conquest of every rival empire.

It requires three previously selected Ascension Perks and an eligible Ideology under your Authority. Militarist, Authoritarian, and Xenophobe Ideologies cannot begin this Ambition.

Before committing, examine two weaknesses in your empire: whether your Ideology has enough support to keep growing, and whether your economy can support the diplomatic and military commitments ahead.

The eventual victory requires at least **50% of the galaxy's population** to follow your Ideology, along with resolution of the required Fallen Empire conflicts.

If your government is still struggling with declining Strength or resource shortages, address those problems before committing to the Ambition.

For the five stages, Willpower rewards, Cultural Envoys, and Utopia requirements, see [Force of Will progression and victory](/stellaris-willpower/force-of-will).

</section>

## Why Is My Stellaris Willpower Progress Stuck?

Most early problems come from confusing a requirement for one system with progress in another.

Use the exact interface associated with the blocked action instead of assuming every issue is caused by insufficient Ideological Strength.

If multiple problems appear at once, resolve the earliest blocker first. An unavailable Advocate Faction must be addressed before Tenet selection matters, while a collapsing economy should be stabilized before pursuing more diplomatic or military commitments.

For founding, ideological expansion, and Strength problems, use the [Ideologies Guide](/stellaris-willpower/ideologies). For unavailable Tenet choices, check the [Tenets Guide](/stellaris-willpower/tenets).

<section id="next-goal">

## Where Should You Go After Your First Major Unlock?

Your next objective depends on which system is preventing the empire from developing further.

An empire with no established Ideology needs to resolve its faction or diplomatic requirements first. Once the Ideology is active, falling Strength or hostile ideological pressure can be diagnosed through the [Ideologies interface and its modifiers](/stellaris-willpower/ideologies).

A newly unlocked Tenet slot creates a different decision: whether an available bonus improves your current Jobs, resources, or government. Compare the actual choices in the [Tenets reference and selection rules](/stellaris-willpower/tenets).

For an empire that already has a strong ideological position and wants a galactic victory, examine the [Force of Will requirements](/stellaris-willpower/force-of-will) before spending an Ascension Perk.

Continue developing your Origin and Civic mechanics alongside these choices. Golden Academy training, Lost Makers excavation, and your selected Fanatic Civic can remain important long after your first Ideology is established.
