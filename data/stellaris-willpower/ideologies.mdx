<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
To create an Ideology in **Stellaris: Willpower**, form a **Fanatic Faction**, establish contact with another empire, and use **Advocate Faction** to establish its Ideology. Afterward, spread it through colonies, Unity production, diplomacy, and Ideological Buildings. Keep **Ideological Strength** growing to avoid negative **Momentum** and the risk of Ideological Revolts.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

An Ideology can extend across several neighboring empires without any of them formally adopting it. The colored area on the galactic map shows **Ideological Reach**, but it does not tell you how many foreign populations have converted or whether another empire has agreed to follow your Ideology.

Three values matter throughout Willpower:

- **Ideological Reach** determines where your Ideology projects influence.
- **Ideological Strength** measures how firmly your Ideology is established and supported.
- **Ideological Momentum** tracks whether that Strength has improved or deteriorated over the previous ten years.

<section id="create-ideology">

## How Do You Create an Ideology in Stellaris Willpower?

A regular empire needs a **Fanatic Faction** and contact with another empire before it can establish its first Ideology.

Starting with a Fanatic Ethic makes the process easier. A compatible Fanatic Faction can appear when your factions form, particularly if your empire uses a Civic associated with that Fanatic Ethic.

If your empire does not have a suitable Fanatic Faction, you must first develop one.

### Step 1: Find or develop a Fanatic Faction

Open the Factions interface and inspect your available factions.

If a faction already represents your chosen Fanatic Ethic, check whether its Ideology can be advocated.

Otherwise, work toward stronger support for the Ethic you want to promote. You can use faction promotion and, when available, **Embrace Faction** to move your government toward a Fanatic Ethic.

Factions can also become Fanatic through sustained support and approval.

Pay attention to faction demands. A Fanatic Faction may offer stronger resource output, but unmet demands can also produce more severe disapproval.

Creating a Fanatic Faction that immediately dislikes your government can leave you with a weak foundation for your new Ideology.

### Step 2: Establish First Contact

Your empire must have established contact with another regular empire before using the normal Advocate Faction action.

If the action is unavailable early in the game, check whether you have completed First Contact rather than spending additional resources trying to improve an already Fanatic Faction.

### Step 3: Use Advocate Faction

Once the requirements are met:

1. Open the Factions interface.
2. Select the Fanatic Faction representing your preferred Ethic.
3. Choose **Advocate Faction**.
4. Confirm the resource cost.
5. Establish the new Ideology and open the Ideologies interface.

In Stellaris 4.6, the recorded Advocate Faction cost is **100 Influence and 1,000 Unity**.

You must not already have an adopted Ideology to establish another through the normal founding action.

![The Advocate Faction action used to establish a new Ideology from a Fanatic Faction in Stellaris Willpower.](/images/stellaris-willpower/willpower-advocate-faction.webp)

An Ideology may also emerge through a faction event. If you receive such an event before manually advocating the faction, inspect its available choices instead of assuming you must pay for the normal action.

### What should you do immediately after founding an Ideology?

Open **Government → Ideologies** and check your new Official Ideology.

Look at its current Strength, Momentum, follower distribution, and the colonies contributing to its Reach.

Your next priority is not necessarily to declare an Ideological War. Establish a reliable domestic following first, then begin projecting your Ideology into neighboring systems.

<section id="ideological-reach">

## How Do You Increase Ideological Reach?

**Ideological Reach spreads outward from the colonies and other sources projecting your Ideology.** It is influenced by economic activity, population support, buildings, and Ideology-specific modifiers.

Unity production is particularly useful, but simply concentrating every Unity-producing job on your capital is not always the best strategy for reaching other empires.

### Put Unity production where you need influence

Suppose a neighboring empire follows a competing Ideology.

Building additional Unity production on a distant capital may strengthen your empire without creating enough pressure at the contested border.

A colony closer to that border can provide a more useful location from which to project Reach.

For a frontier focused on ideological expansion:

1. Find the contested systems using Ideology Map Mode.
2. Identify your colonies closest to that frontier.
3. Check their existing Reach and Unity-producing jobs.
4. Improve Unity production and other relevant local sources.
5. Add suitable ideological infrastructure when it becomes available.
6. Recheck which Ideology controls the neighboring systems.

The objective is to increase pressure in the systems you actually want to influence.

A dedicated Unity world remains useful for Unity production. However, when your goal is territorial ideological pressure, distributing some Unity-producing infrastructure among strategically placed colonies can be more effective than relying on one distant source.

![Ideology Map Mode showing the spread of Ideological Reach across systems and neighboring empires.](/images/stellaris-willpower/willpower-ideological-reach.webp)

### What else contributes to Reach?

The main contributors include:

- Unity production on your colonies.
- Trade and Amenities.
- Populations already following your Ideology.
- Certain jobs associated with the Ideology's Ethic.
- Galactic Halls and Ideological Buildings.
- Ideological Beacons, Push modifiers, and other relevant bonuses.

Different Ethics can benefit from different jobs. A Materialist Ideology, for example, can receive additional Reach contributions from research-related activity.

Inspect the actual colony modifiers rather than assuming the same building combination is equally effective for every Ideology.

### What happens when two Ideologies overlap?

When multiple Ideologies project Reach into the same system, they compete through **Ideological Clashing**.

The stronger Ideology controls that system's visible ideological coverage.

Clashing can also contribute to the Strength of the competing Ideologies. You can formally designate an Ideological Rival to intensify the competition.

A system may change ideological control as the relative strength of nearby colonies, buildings, and other modifiers changes.

However, winning the Reach competition does not immediately change the governing Ethic of the foreign empire occupying that system.

</section>

<section id="strength">

## How Do You Increase Ideological Strength?

Ideological Strength determines how firmly your Ideology is established. It also influences access to stronger ideological systems.

Unlike Reach, Strength is not simply a measure of the distance your Ideology covers on the galaxy map.

You can inspect its components by hovering over the Ideological Strength value.

### Main sources of Strength

<GuideTable
  caption="Main sources of Ideological Strength."
  columns={ideologyStrengthColumns}
  rows={ideologyStrengthRows}
/>

Strength can also face penalties from Empire Size, competing ideological populations, and the balancing effects applied to high existing Strength.

### Why is my Ideological Strength decreasing?

Start with the tooltip.

A common cause is **Empire Size**. In one campaign, the player noticed Ideological Strength falling shortly after the empire exceeded the size threshold associated with a new penalty.

Further expansion can therefore increase your population and economic output while simultaneously reducing the net Strength gained from your Ideology.

Other possible causes include:

- More domestic Pops following competing Ideologies.
- Weak faction approval.
- Insufficient Strength-producing infrastructure.
- Changes in ideological follower distribution.
- Larger penalties as your existing Strength rises.

![The Ideological Strength tooltip showing positive modifiers, Empire Size penalties, and other sources of Strength loss.](/images/stellaris-willpower/willpower-ideological-strength.webp)

### What should you fix first?

**If Empire Size is the main penalty**, avoid assuming that another round of territorial expansion will solve it. Improve the ideological output of existing colonies and strengthen your domestic following.

**If foreign Ideologies are gaining followers inside your borders**, inspect which colonies are exposed to competing Reach. Improve domestic ideological pressure in those systems.

**If faction approval is low**, inspect the faction's demands and identify which of your policies are working against it.

**If Strength is already high**, remember that additional Strength becomes increasingly difficult to accumulate. Check the actual tooltip before expecting a new building to create the same improvement it provided earlier.

Assigning an appropriate Official to advocate for the Ideology can also help, provided your empire type supports Advocates.

</section>

<section id="momentum">

## How Does Ideological Momentum Work?

**Momentum tracks the change in Ideological Strength over the previous ten years.**

That makes it different from the current Strength value.

An Ideology can still be powerful while its Momentum has turned negative, because its Strength has been decreasing relative to its earlier position.

### Positive vs Negative Momentum

<GuideTable
  caption="Ideological Momentum states and how to respond."
  columns={ideologyMomentumColumns}
  rows={ideologyMomentumRows}
/>

Positive Momentum can provide Unity-related benefits and favorable ideological events.

Negative Momentum can create additional Unity costs and Stability penalties. Severe problems may lead to an **Ideological Revolt**.

Unlike an ordinary local revolt, an Ideological Revolt can create a rival Ideology or involve multiple colonies attempting to separate.

### How do you stop negative Momentum?

Use the following order rather than trying to change several systems at once.

1. **Open the Strength tooltip.** Identify whether the decline comes from Empire Size, rival followers, faction problems, or another modifier.
2. **Check your domestic ideological distribution.** Find colonies where foreign Ideologies are becoming influential.
3. **Improve support for your Official Ideology.** Address faction demands and relevant ideological infrastructure.
4. **Reduce foreign pressure where possible.** Strengthen local Reach, use Ideological Push, or increase Resistance.
5. **Monitor the trend.** A single improvement to monthly Strength does not immediately erase a negative ten-year trend.

If the situation is already progressing toward Revolt, prioritize the affected colonies instead of focusing only on additional foreign expansion.

### Can Momentum fall temporarily without causing a revolt?

Yes. A short decline in Strength is not the same as a completed ten-year downward trend.

In a Pacifist campaign, Ideological Strength briefly decreased after Empire Size increased, then began climbing again.

The useful response was to examine the source of the loss and keep monitoring the trend.

Don't abandon an otherwise successful Ideology because of one negative monthly change. Act when the underlying cause remains unresolved and Momentum continues deteriorating.

</section>

<section id="ideology-adoption">

## How Do You Get Other Empires to Adopt Your Ideology?

There are two separate objectives:

- Increase the number of foreign Pops following your Ideology.
- Convince the foreign empire itself to **adopt** that Ideology.

Expanding Reach helps with the first objective. It does not guarantee the second.

### Why is my Ideology spreading but nobody is adopting it?

In one Militarist campaign, Warmongers and an Absolute Militarist leader pushed Ideological Reach across much of the galaxy. Yet most foreign empires still had not formally adopted the Ideology.

The map showed ideological coverage, not political allegiance. Even with extensive Reach, those empires remained outside the Ideology until they adopted it through an available method.

The practical distinction is:

<GuideTable
  caption="The difference between Ideological Reach, population followers, and formal adoption."
  columns={ideologyAdoptionColumns}
  rows={ideologyAdoptionRows}
/>

If the map is almost entirely your color but foreign adoption remains low, stop treating additional Reach as the only solution.

Inspect the target empire's followers, governing Ethics, diplomatic relationships, and adoption options.

### Invite another empire to adopt your Ideology

For a peaceful approach:

1. Open diplomacy with a foreign empire.
2. Check whether its governing Ethics are compatible with your Ideology.
3. Look for the option to invite it to adopt your Ideology.
4. Inspect the acceptance modifiers and any restrictions.
5. Improve the relevant conditions or select another target if the action is unavailable.

Empires with compatible Ethics are more promising candidates than those firmly committed to opposing beliefs.

A foreign empire's existing Ideology, overlord restrictions, or unfavorable diplomatic conditions may prevent acceptance.

High Reach alone does not override every diplomatic restriction.

### Can you adopt somebody else's Ideology?

Yes. You do not have to establish your own.

If a foreign Ideology fits your empire's direction, you can choose to adopt it rather than starting from nothing.

After selecting the adoption option, check your Situation Log for the associated adoption project and its Society Research requirement.

Joining an existing Ideology does not automatically make you its Ideological Authority.

That matters because the Authority controls its Tenets.

</section>

<section id="ideological-authority">

## How Do You Become the Ideological Authority?

When an empire establishes a new Ideology, it begins as that Ideology's Authority.

Other empires can later adopt the same Ideology, creating a competition for leadership.

The Authority is determined by **Ideological Strength and Diplomatic Weight**.

To replace the current Authority, your empire must outperform it in both relevant measures and satisfy the leadership-change requirement.

This creates an important decision when adopting a foreign Ideology.

Joining a successful one can give your empire access to an established galactic movement, but you may initially have no control over which Tenets are selected.

### How can you protect your leadership?

Focus on the measures that support both sides of the comparison:

- Maintain high Ideological Strength.
- Keep your faction approval and domestic follower base healthy.
- Improve your Diplomatic Weight.
- Use relevant ideological infrastructure and leader assignments.
- Check the current Authority's values when another member becomes competitive.

If another empire has joined your Ideology but is gaining on you in both Strength and Diplomatic Weight, expanding Reach without improving your own position may not be enough to remain in control.

The [Tenets Guide](/stellaris-willpower/tenets) explains what the Authority can choose and how to compare the available bonuses.

</section>

<section id="ideological-wars">

## How Do Ideological Wars Work?

A full victory with **Impose Ideology** forces the defeated empire to adopt your Official Ideology. It does not automatically make that empire your subject.

**Overthrow Government** changes the target's government instead. If your goal is to establish a Doctrinal Proxy through war, Force of Will provides the separate **Impose Ideological Subjugation** option after the required Ambition progression.

The choice depends on whether you want ideological adoption, a different government, or a subject relationship.

### Impose Ideology vs Overthrow Government

<GuideTable
  caption="Ideological wars and subject agreements compared."
  columns={ideologyWarColumns}
  rows={ideologyWarRows}
/>

The important distinction is diplomatic.

Imposing an Ideology does not necessarily give the defeated empire the same positive opinion modifiers associated with a government liberation.

In one campaign, the player preferred changing a target government first and then inviting it into the Ideology, because the resulting relationship was easier to manage.

That does not make Overthrow Government universally superior. It depends on whether you want direct adoption, a friendly government, or a new subject.

### Does Status Quo force adoption?

Do not assume a Status Quo settlement gives the same result as winning the war.

In an early Willpower campaign, a Status Quo settlement did not produce the expected adoption by the new empire created from the settlement.

If formal ideological adoption is the objective, inspect the war goal and surrender terms before accepting peace.

For a dependable full imposition, plan around achieving the actual **Impose Ideology victory condition**, not merely occupying some territory.

</section>

<section id="ideological-espionage">

## How Do You Spread Ideology Through Espionage?

Espionage provides another way to apply pressure when direct diplomacy fails or when you want to weaken an opposing Ideology without immediately declaring war.

Three ideology-related operations serve different purposes.

<GuideTable
  caption="Ideology-related Espionage operations and their uses."
  columns={ideologyEspionageColumns}
  rows={ideologyEspionageRows}
/>

Information Manipulation can help extend ideological pressure into a foreign empire when your existing Reach struggles to cross its borders.

Incite Ideological Revolt has a different requirement: your Ideology must already have a substantial following inside the target empire. Check the operation's available conditions and affected colonies before committing resources.

### When is Espionage worth using?

Espionage is most relevant when your Ideology has already entered another empire but cannot convert enough Pops or secure political adoption.

For example, a foreign planet may have some followers of your Ideology while the target's own Ideology continues to dominate.

An operation that changes ideological resistance or attraction can help you compete without committing a fleet.

However, operations consume resources and time. Compare their cost with alternatives such as diplomacy, colonizing near the border, or building stronger ideological infrastructure.

</section>

<section id="galactic-halls">

## How Do You Unlock and Use Galactic Halls?

**Galactic Halls are relocatable megastructures that project Ideological Reach and provide additional bonuses.**

To unlock one, first adopt an Ideology and raise its Ideological Strength until the corresponding **Galactic Hall technology** becomes available.

Research that technology before attempting to construct the structure.

### Where should you build your Galactic Hall?

There are two useful approaches.

**Above a developed colony**

This is attractive when you want the Hall's colony bonuses. Some effects depend on the Ethic associated with your Ideology.

A large, productive colony can make good use of those benefits.

**Near a contested border**

This is useful when your main objective is to push Ideological Reach toward another empire.

A Galactic Hall can orbit a celestial body in a system you own, even if you are not placing it directly over a populated colony.

Choose the location based on whether you currently need stronger domestic output or more pressure along an ideological frontier.

![A completed Galactic Hall showing its colony bonuses, Ideological Reach modifier, and Relocate Megastructure option.](/images/stellaris-willpower/willpower-galactic-hall.webp)

### Can you move a Galactic Hall?

Yes.

Relocating a Galactic Hall costs Energy and is subject to a **ten-year cooldown**.

That makes placement a meaningful decision.

If you currently need the economic benefits above your capital but expect an important ideological confrontation on another frontier soon, consider the relocation delay before moving it.

### What are Galactic Hall Chambers?

Galactic Halls begin with a Chamber slot that allows you to add an additional bonus.

Available Chambers include different economic, technological, military, and social effects.

Your governing Ethic restricts incompatible opposing-Ethic Chambers.

Rather than installing a Chamber solely because it provides the largest-looking modifier, compare it with the needs of your empire.

A Research bonus is valuable when laboratories are a major part of your economy. A defensive or Unity-oriented option may be more useful for an empire trying to stabilize its Ideology while managing hostile neighbors.

Additional technologies can improve Galactic Hall capabilities.

### When do Ideological Buildings and Beacons unlock?

Galactic Hall-related technology also provides access to additional ideological infrastructure.

**Ethical Buildings** can increase Ideological Strength and provide other effects on colonies. Later Ideological technologies allow further upgrades.

**Ideological Beacons** are Starbase buildings that provide Unity and increase Ideological Push.

Push helps extend the effective influence of your existing Reach. Resistance works against incoming ideological pressure.

If you are struggling to project influence from a colony near a rival empire, inspect both the colony's ideological output and the Starbases supporting that frontier.

A Galactic Hall is useful, but it is not the only source of ideological projection.

</section>

<section id="gestalt-nomads">

## How Do Ideologies Work for Gestalt and Nomadic Empires?

Gestalt and Nomadic empires can participate in the Ideology system, but they do not use exactly the same mechanisms as ordinary settled empires.

### Gestalt Consciousness

Hive Minds and Machine Intelligences do not begin by managing ordinary individual political factions in the same way as regular empires.

After encountering other empires and discovering ideological concepts, they can establish their own ideological direction.

Hive Minds can develop **Instincts**, while Machine Intelligences use **Directives**.

The available process can involve an event, an Agenda, or a relevant project.

Gestalt empires cannot simply assign a normal Ideology Advocate in the same way as regular empires.

Foreign ideological pressure also has a different consequence.

Because their drones share a collective consciousness, they do not gradually convert as independent political individuals.

Instead, foreign ideological influence can produce **Deviancy**, weakening the empire's stability and Ideological Strength.

When playing Gestalt, inspect Deviancy and foreign ideological pressure rather than looking only for ordinary Pop faction conversions.

The [Tenets Guide](/stellaris-willpower/tenets) covers the separate Gestalt versions of effects that normally apply to individual Pops.

### Nomadic Empires

Nomadic empires project ideological influence primarily through **Waystations**, with their capabilities tied to their Arkships.

Their Arkships do not function like settled colonies that automatically spread Reach through surrounding systems.

To extend a Nomadic Ideology:

1. Develop the ideological contribution of your Arkships.
2. Use suitable Waystations to project Reach into nearby systems.
3. Inspect available ideological modules and arrays.
4. Check whether foreign Waystations are projecting competing Ideologies within your territory.

Nomadic Galactic Halls are implemented as modules rather than the standalone structures used by settled empires.

A foreign Nomadic empire may therefore exert pressure from Waystations inside another empire's territory if its agreements allow access.

</section>

Supporting the wrong Ideology may give your rivals a stronger position than your own.

<section id="absolute-ethics">

## How Do You Unlock Absolute Ethics?

An Absolute Ethic becomes available when your empire meets the relevant Ideological Strength and faction requirements. Depending on the circumstances, the transformation can be initiated through an eligible faction action or a corresponding event.

The change requires Unity and commits your government to one Ethic, removing the other governing Ethics.

### What Changes When You Become Absolute?

The resulting bonuses depend on the chosen Ethic. Absolute Militarism emphasizes military strength, while Absolute Xenophilia provides a different set of diplomatic and population-related advantages.

The loss of your other Ethics can also affect Civic eligibility, policies, and modifiers. Inspect these dependencies before completing the change, particularly if your economy relies on a combination of Ethics.

For empires pursuing **Force of Will**, the Absolute Ethic is a prerequisite for advancing to Stage 2. The required Strength thresholds, Unity payment, and project are explained in the [Force of Will Ambition](/stellaris-willpower/force-of-will).

</section>

<section id="next-goal">

## What Can You Do With a Stable Ideology?

An Ideology with sustained domestic support gives you several ways to influence neighboring empires.

A compatible government may accept an invitation to adopt your Ideology. A rival with competing Reach may require stronger frontier infrastructure, while a hostile government could make Espionage or an Ideological War more relevant.

If you have reached another Strength threshold, you can also improve the shared Ideology through a new [Tenet selection](/stellaris-willpower/tenets).

A government seeking galactic ideological victory can investigate [Force of Will](/stellaris-willpower/force-of-will), provided it meets the Ascension Perk and Authority requirements.

For your next campaign, compare the [Willpower Origins and Fanatic Civics](/stellaris-willpower) before creating a new empire. Your Origin is fixed at game start, so a different starting strategy requires a new campaign.
