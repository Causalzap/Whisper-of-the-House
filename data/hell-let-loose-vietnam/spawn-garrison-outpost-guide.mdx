<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
I treat **Outposts and Garrisons as two different jobs**.

An **Outpost** is my squad's fast forward spawn. It costs **no supplies**, has
the shortest redeploy wave of the normal spawn structures, and is the one I use
to keep my own squad attached to the fight.

A **Garrison** is team infrastructure:

- **50 supplies** in friendly territory;
- **100 supplies** in unlocked enemy territory;
- at least **200 meters** from another Garrison;
- unavailable in locked enemy territory.

My normal order is:

**safe Garrison network → useful forward OP → supplies → next Garrison.**

When the front collapses, I do not automatically choose the closest remaining
spawn and start another long run.

I first check whether the Squad Leader can restore the OP, whether a useful
Garrison still exists, and whether one temporary Specialist supply run can
rebuild the team's route.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

<h2 id="outpost-vs-garrison">
  Outpost and Garrison solve different spawn problems
</h2>

The biggest improvement I made as Squad Leader was deciding **who needs the
spawn** before pulling out the Field Pad.

If my squad needs to get back to me quickly, I want an **Outpost**.

If several squads need another route into the sector, I want a **Garrison**.

Those are different problems.

I have played with a good forward OP where my squad felt mobile while the rest
of the team still had a terrible spawn network.

I have also seen several Garrisons on the map while our own squad had no OP,
which meant every death still started farther back than it needed to.

So I no longer ask:

**What spawn can I place here?**

I ask:

**Who am I trying to move?**

That normally tells me which structure matters.

<h2 id="outposts">
  I use the Outpost to keep my squad attached to the front
</h2>

An Outpost is specific to my squad.

It has a shorter redeploy wave than the other normal spawn structures, which
makes it my fastest way to keep the unit together after deaths.

It also costs:

**0 supplies.**

And there is another important territory advantage:

**the Outpost is the normal spawn structure I can place in neutral territory.**

That makes the OP much more flexible during a moving fight.

I do not wait until five squad members are dead and asking where the OP is.

As soon as we:

- cross a dangerous open area;
- establish a useful flank;
- move through difficult jungle;
- or reach cover I do not want to repeat from the rear,

I start looking for the next stable OP position.

My target is not:

**as close to the objective as possible.**

It is:

**close enough to remove wasted travel, but far enough from obvious enemy
movement that one push does not erase it immediately.**

I judge the position from three things:

- where the squad is actually fighting;
- which direction enemy pressure is arriving from;
- whether the spawn has cover from the most obvious approach.

If the current OP already gives me a short and relatively safe route, I do not
move it just to gain another thirty or forty meters.

A stable OP that survives several waves is often better than an aggressive OP
that disappears immediately.

<h2 id="garrisons">
  I build a Garrison when the entire team needs another route
</h2>

A Garrison is different because **the entire team can deploy on it**.

That turns it from a squad convenience into map infrastructure.

In friendly territory, a Garrison costs:

**50 supplies.**

Supplies can come from several logistics sources.

For my decision as Squad Leader, the important distinction is simply:

**we need another Garrison**

versus:

**we have enough supplies to actually build one.**

A perfect location with no supplies is not yet a Garrison position.

If I reach the location first, I stop moving farther forward and solve the
logistics problem before the squad drifts away from the place we actually need
to build.

<h2 id="garrison-territory-rules">
  Garrison cost and legality change with territory
</h2>

The territory state changes the plan.

The two costs I use are:

**friendly territory → 50 supplies**

**unlocked enemy territory → 100 supplies**

The aggressive Garrison costs twice as much.

That means a supply amount that was enough for the defensive Garrison behind me
may not be enough once I cross into enemy territory.

There is also a harder boundary:

**locked enemy territory does not allow normal spawn structures to be placed.**

So if I have the Field Pad out and every position keeps failing, I stop walking
around looking for a slightly flatter patch of dirt.

I open the Tactical Map and check the sector state.

Sometimes the problem is not terrain.

Sometimes the structure is simply illegal in that territory.

<h2 id="garrison-200-meter-rule">
  I check 200-meter spacing before blaming the placement
</h2>

The other hard rule I check constantly is:

**Garrisons must be at least 200 meters apart.**

If I have enough supplies and a location still refuses to work, I check the
nearest existing Garrison before doing anything else.

I have had positions that looked perfect:

good cover,

correct side of the fight,

supplies available,

but no valid placement.

The useful question was not:

**Is the ground bad?**

It was:

**How close is the nearest Garrison?**

That is also why I check spacing **before** asking somebody to carry supplies
across the sector.

If the planned location is inside another Garrison's 200-meter radius, the
supply box will not fix the problem.

I move the planned spawn instead.

<h2 id="territory-flip">
  When territory flips, I immediately re-check every forward spawn
</h2>

A spawn network that worked thirty seconds ago can stop being the network I have
after the sector changes hands.

In training, losing the territory removed the forward spawn structures that had
been placed during that sequence.

The live-match version of that lesson hurts even more.

We can have:

**a working OP → a useful push → the sector changes → the OP is gone → the next
spawn is suddenly far behind us.**

My old reaction was:

**pick the closest remaining spawn → start running.**

Now I pause and check:

- Is my Squad Leader still alive near the front?
- Can another OP go down?
- Does an existing Garrison still support the current objective?
- Did the battle shift back toward the previous point?
- Are we attacking from a network that no longer exists?

That last question matters.

In one bad collapse, we had no useful forward OP, no useful nearby Garrison, and
no supplies where we needed them.

The position being discussed was roughly **745 meters away**.

At that point, running harder was not the solution.

The spawn network itself had to be rebuilt.

<h2 id="rebuild-after-collapse">
  I rebuild the network from the rear forward
</h2>

When the spawn situation collapses, I do not try to fix every missing structure
at once.

My order is:

### 1. Preserve the last useful team spawn

If we still have a Garrison behind the fight that can support defense, I do not
abandon it just because everybody wants to continue attacking.

### 2. Restore a useful squad OP

Because the OP needs no supplies, it is often the fastest way to stop my own
squad from spawning at HQ.

The Squad Leader gets forward safely enough to place it.

Now the squad has a temporary working route.

### 3. Bring supplies to the next legal Garrison position

That converts the squad's temporary route into infrastructure the entire team
can use.

### 4. Extend again only after the route behind us works

I do not build the exciting forward Garrison first and then discover there is
nothing between it and HQ.

My rebuild direction is:

**rear stability → squad mobility → team mobility → offensive extension.**

That sequence is much more reliable than trying to recreate the old front line
in one jump.

<h2 id="specialist-supply-workflow">
  One temporary Specialist life can fix the entire squad's route
</h2>

One of the most useful squad workflows is also one of the simplest.

If we need a friendly-territory Garrison and nobody has supplies, one player can
temporarily redeploy as **Specialist**.

The sequence is:

**Squad Leader places or restores the OP**

→ **one player redeploys as Specialist**

→ **Specialist spawns on the OP**

→ **Specialist brings the supply box**

→ **Squad Leader builds the Garrison**

→ **the player returns to the role the squad actually needs.**

I do not need to become a permanent logistics player.

I only need to recognize when one short role swap saves several teammates
minutes of travel on every future death.

That is an extremely good trade.

<h2 id="backup-before-offense">
  I want a backup Garrison before gambling on the aggressive one
</h2>

Aggressive Garrisons are tempting because the value is obvious.

A good one near the next objective can give the team an entirely new attack
angle.

The problem appears when everybody uses that Garrison while the route behind it
is weak.

If the current sector falls, the team can suddenly have no clean way back.

So my rule is:

**backup first, pressure second.**

Before committing supplies to a risky forward Garrison, I ask:

**Where do we spawn if this one disappears or the attack fails?**

If the answer is:

**HQ**

I am probably extending too aggressively.

This does not mean every spawn needs to be far behind the fight.

It means the network needs layers.

A forward Garrison becomes much safer to use aggressively when losing it does
not destroy the team's entire route.

<h2 id="garrison-coverage">
  I build routes, not a pile of Garrison icons
</h2>

More Garrisons do not automatically mean a better spawn network.

Three Garrisons can still be poor if all three create nearly the same route.

What I want is **coverage**.

One Garrison might:

**anchor the defense.**

Another might:

**open a side approach.**

Another might:

**become the forward route when the next sector changes hands.**

The 200-meter spacing rule makes careless placement expensive because each
Garrison uses part of the map that another one cannot use.

So before building, I ask:

**What new route does this Garrison create?**

If the answer is:

**basically the same route we already have**

I would rather reposition and keep the supplies.

A Garrison should solve a movement problem.

It should not exist only to increase the number of icons on the spawn screen.

<h2 id="destroy-enemy-spawns">
  When I reach an enemy Garrison, I usually remove the spawn first
</h2>

The same logic applies in reverse.

If enemies keep arriving from the same direction, killing one player does not
necessarily solve the problem.

There may be a Garrison feeding the entire route.

If I reach it safely, dismantling the spawn can remove far more pressure than
chasing the last infantryman who ran away from it.

Instead of fighting another wave from the same direction, we gain time to:

- clear the surrounding area;
- move onto the objective;
- establish our own OP;
- or start building another team spawn.

<h2 id="spawn-network-check">
  The spawn checks I make before starting another long run
</h2>

Whenever I die and the spawn screen suddenly looks terrible, I use the same
order.

### Do we still have the forward OP?

If not, I check whether the Squad Leader can restore it before I commit to a long
run.

### Do we have a Garrison that actually supports this objective?

A Garrison existing somewhere on the map does not automatically make it useful
for the current fight.

### Do we have enough supplies for another Garrison?

I need:

**50 in friendly territory**

or:

**100 in unlocked enemy territory.**

### Is the planned Garrison at least 200 meters from the nearest one?

If not, more supplies will not solve the placement.

### Is the territory legal?

Locked enemy territory is not fixed by walking farther forward with the Field
Pad.

### If the attack fails, where do we spawn next?

If I have no answer, I fix the backup route before gambling everything on the
forward spawn.

Once I started treating these questions as part of the battle rather than
something I dealt with after dying, the game changed completely.

The spawn network is not just how I return to the battlefield.

**The spawn network is part of the battlefield.**
