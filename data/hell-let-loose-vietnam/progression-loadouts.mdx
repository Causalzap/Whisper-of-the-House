<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
Hell Let Loose: Vietnam progression is tied to the **role I actually play**.

As that role levels up, its loadout can gain:

- more weight capacity;
- new weapons and secondaries;
- lethal and utility slots;
- ammunition customisation;
- role equipment;
- and appearance options.

I do not treat every unlock as an automatic upgrade.

My rule is:

**check the next role level → decide what new job it enables → check the weight
cost → only grind it if that change matters to the way I want to play.**

A good example is NVA Machine Gunner. At **Role Level 2**, I gained anti-tank
grenades and a **Type 67 secondary**, but the **Wrench** I needed for the AA Gun
was still locked until **Level 3**.

That also explains one of the strangest early progression problems: a Tutorial
can hand me equipment before my normal live-match version of that role has
actually unlocked it.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

<h2 id="how-role-progression-works">
  I level the role that owns the equipment I want
</h2>

The biggest progression mistake I made early was looking at my overall account
level and treating it like one universal unlock track.

That is not how I plan my equipment.

I look at the **specific role**.

If I spend the match as Medic, Medic progression is the track I care about when
I return to the Barracks.

If I want a Machine Gunner tool, I check Machine Gunner.

If I want something on Squad Leader, I check that role's own progression instead
of assuming another infantry role has unlocked it for everyone.

The progression screen can expand several parts of a loadout:

- primary and secondary weapons;
- lethal equipment;
- utilities;
- role equipment;
- ammunition options;
- available slots;
- loadout weight;
- appearance.

That means I decide what I am trying to unlock **before** deliberately grinding
a role.

If I switch between five roles every match, I can still make useful account
progress, but I am spreading role progression across five different tracks.

If one particular unlock changes what I want to do next, I stay on that role
long enough to reach it.

<h2 id="medic-progression">
  US Medic shows how the whole loadout expands from Level 1 to Level 10
</h2>

The clearest overview I have is the US Medic progression tree.

It starts at **6 lb of loadout capacity at Level 1**.

Then the role expands step by step:

**Level 2 — 7 lb**

A Secondary Slot and Lethal Slot become available.

**Level 3 — 8 lb**

I get Ammo Customisation, **2 Utility Slots**, and the M18 x2 option.

**Level 4 — 9 lb**

The **M16A1 Bayonet** becomes available.

**Level 5 — 10 lb**

The tree expands to **3 Utility Slots**, with additional M61 and M18 options.

Later levels continue increasing the weight budget until it reaches
**15 lb at Level 10**.

Level 7 expands the role to **4 Utility Slots**, while Level 9 adds
**2 Lethal Slots**.

This changed the way I think about leveling.

I am not only asking:

**What weapon do I get next?**

I am also asking:

**What new combination becomes possible?**

Level 3 is a good example.

The important part is not only one piece of equipment.

I now have:

**8 lb + Ammo Customisation + 2 Utility Slots.**

That can change the whole Medic setup.

By Level 4, another pound of capacity and the M16A1 Bayonet create another
decision.

Progression is therefore changing both:

**what I can equip**

and:

**how much of it I can carry at once.**

<h2 id="loadout-weight">
  I treat weight as a budget, not empty space I have to fill
</h2>

Once I understood the weight system, I stopped building every loadout by
selecting everything that was available.

A secondary weapon has a cost.

Another magazine has a cost.

A grenade has a cost.

Utility equipment has a cost.

So the question is not:

**Can this fit?**

It is:

**Is this worth the weight compared with what I would have to leave behind?**

I build from the problem backward.

If I regularly survive long enough to run out of ammunition, more magazines
have obvious value.

If I die with ammunition still available but keep reaching fights where I need
smoke or another utility, ammunition is not the problem I should spend more
weight solving.

If a secondary sits unused for an entire match, simply having a secondary slot
does not make carrying the weapon free.

An unlocked slot gives me another option.

It does not tell me which option is best.

<h2 id="machine-gunner-progression">
  Machine Gunner Level 2 and Level 3 changed two different jobs
</h2>

NVA Machine Gunner gave me the best early example of why I check the next level
instead of blindly chasing progression.

At **Role Level 2**, I gained access to:

- anti-tank grenades;
- a **Type 67 secondary**.

Those were useful additions, but they did not solve the problem I was actually
trying to solve.

I wanted to use the role's **Wrench**.

That required **Level 3**.

The difference mattered because those levels were unlocking two different kinds
of value.

Level 2 made my normal combat loadout more flexible.

Level 3 opened a new battlefield job.

So if my goal was simply:

**mount the MG and suppress infantry**

I did not urgently need to grind another level.

But if my goal was:

**build the NVA AA Gun**

then Level 3 directly changed what I could do.

That is the kind of unlock I will deliberately work toward.

<h2 id="tutorial-equipment">
  Tutorial equipment is not proof that my live loadout has unlocked it
</h2>

This was the progression blocker that confused me most.

Machine Gunner Training handed me a **Wrench** and taught me to place an AA Gun.

I used it normally.

Then I entered a real match as Machine Gunner and wanted to do the same thing.

No Wrench.

My first reaction was to troubleshoot controls.

I checked the equipment selection.

I wondered whether I had missed a keybind.

I looked for the tool again.

The real problem was simpler:

**the live role had not reached the required progression level.**

For the NVA Machine Gunner progression I was using:

**Level 2 → anti-tank grenades + Type 67 secondary**

**Level 3 → Wrench available**

Now whenever a Tutorial gives me equipment that disappears in a real match, my
order is:

**1. Open the role loadout.**

**2. Open Role Progression.**

**3. Find the missing item.**

**4. Check the required level.**

Only after that do I start assuming something is wrong with the controls or
loadout.

The Tutorial is showing me what the role can do.

It is not necessarily showing me the exact starter build I have already
unlocked.

<h2 id="medic-k50m">
  The NVA Medic K50M shows why one pound of weight can matter
</h2>

The NVA Medic gave me another useful loadout example.

The **K50M weighs 3 lb**.

The other primary I was comparing against weighed **4 lb**.

That one-pound saving sounds small until I use it for something else.

With the lighter K50M setup, I had enough space to add a **fifth magazine**.

Then I checked the magazine itself.

The K50M held **35 rounds per magazine**.

So that loadout choice was effectively:

**save 1 lb → fit another magazine → carry another 35 rounds.**

That is a real trade rather than a vague stat difference.

But I still do not automatically take the fifth magazine.

Medic is a perfect role for asking:

**What am I actually spending during a life?**

If I stay around my squad, revive people, use smoke, and die with ammunition
left over, another 35 rounds are not solving my biggest problem.

If I am surviving long enough to burn through the K50M repeatedly, the extra
magazine becomes much more attractive.

The weight saving creates the option.

My match behavior decides whether I use it.

<h2 id="role-trees-are-specific">
  I do not assume an item unlocks at the same level on every role
</h2>

Another trap is seeing an item on one progression tree and treating that as a
game-wide unlock rule.

The NVA Squad Leader tree is a good example.

Its progression includes the **K50M at Level 3**, together with RDG-1 x2, Ammo
Customisation, a second Utility Slot, and another Weight Point.

That does not mean:

**K50M always unlocks at Level 3.**

It means:

**this NVA Squad Leader progression places the K50M there.**

This also keeps me from confusing faction equipment.

If I built an NVA loadout and then switch to the US side, I check the faction
before assuming the game deleted my setup.

The same role name does not mean both factions use the same progression tree or
equipment.

<h2 id="when-to-equip-unlock">
  I only equip a new unlock when it fixes something I actually noticed
</h2>

I use the same decision for secondaries, ammunition, grenades, and utilities.

### Secondary weapon

I add one when my primary has a weakness I actually encounter.

For Machine Gunner, a compact secondary can be useful when I have to leave the
mounted position and fight through tight buildings or vegetation.

If it remains holstered for entire lives, I reconsider spending the weight on
it.

### Extra ammunition

I add magazines when I actually start running dry.

If I usually die with several magazines left, carrying another magazine is not
improving the loadout.

### Grenades and utility

I keep them when they are part of the way I play.

If I repeatedly reach cover and wish I had smoke, the utility is earning its
weight.

If I equip a grenade every match and never remember to throw it, I either remove
it or deliberately start building situations where I use it.

I do not fill slots simply because progression made them available.

**An unused unlock still costs weight.**

<h2 id="when-to-level-role">
  I grind a role when the next level changes my next match
</h2>

Not every progression level needs to become a project.

Before committing another match to one role, I ask:

**What exactly unlocks next?**

Then:

**Will it change what I can actually do?**

Machine Gunner gives me the easiest example.

**Level 3 → Wrench**

That opens the AA construction job I already wanted.

That is a meaningful target.

Medic capacity gives me another.

If I keep hitting the weight ceiling and already know which support item I want
to add, the next Weight Point has immediate value.

A cosmetic unlock is different.

A secondary I probably will not use is different.

I do not keep playing one role for hours simply because another progression box
exists.

My rule is:

**Will this unlock change my next match?**

If yes, I stay on the role.

If not, I am comfortable switching to whatever the squad needs.

<h2 id="role-level-vs-training-lock">
  Role Progression and Tutorial requirements are separate lock systems
</h2>

This distinction prevents a lot of wasted grinding.

A piece of equipment can be unavailable because my **Role Level is too low**.

A leadership role can also be unavailable because I have not completed the
required **Tutorials**.

Those are not the same problem.

If Squad Leader says:

**Requirements Not Met**

I do not grind Career XP or another infantry role hoping the message disappears.

I check the exact prerequisites in the
<Link href="/hell-let-loose-vietnam/unlock-squad-leader-commander">
  {" "}Squad Leader and Commander unlock guide
</Link>
.

Once the role itself is available, its equipment progression becomes a separate
question.

So I diagnose the word **locked** in this order:

**Can I select the role at all?**

If no, I check Tutorial requirements.

**Can I select the role but not equip a specific item?**

Then I check Role Progression.

That keeps two different progression systems from looking like one bug.

<h2 id="progression-check">
  The four questions I check before deliberately leveling a role
</h2>

Before I spend another match grinding one progression track, I use four
questions.

### What exactly unlocks next?

I look at the progression screen instead of relying on memory.

### Does it change the job I want to perform?

A Wrench that opens AA construction matters more than a secondary I do not plan
to use.

### What does the new setup cost?

More slots do not remove the loadout weight limit.

I still have to fit everything together.

### What am I willing to remove?

This is the question that stops me from equipping everything automatically.

If I cannot name what the new equipment replaces or why I need it, I probably
do not need it in this loadout yet.

That is how I use progression now.

I am not trying to make every role carry the maximum number of unlocked items.

I am trying to make each role better at the specific job I need it to do.
