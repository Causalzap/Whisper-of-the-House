A growing bank balance does not necessarily mean you are getting closer to the center of the egg in **CRACK**. You can spend an entire shift extracting valuable material, only to discover that Hatch Corp still wants a specific assignment completed before it will unlock the equipment you need.

Progress comes from managing three things together: **Work Orders, equipment, and the specimen's reactions**. The egg becomes harder to excavate as you move inward, but the real delays often come from full Buckets being mishandled, money spent before a required purchase, or an agitation event interrupting your only usable tunnel.

You can complete the main assignment without stripping every layer from the specimen. A workable excavation route, reliable deposits, and the right equipment upgrades are more useful than removing the largest possible amount of material.

<section id="getting-started">

## What Should You Do First in CRACK?

Read the Work Order in the upper-left corner and identify what it actually requires: a material deposit, an equipment upgrade, an arcade score, construction, or another interaction. Your cash balance alone does not complete an assignment.

During the opening shifts, focus on Shell deposits and obtaining the tool needed for Membrane. When a later objective names a material or facility you cannot access, complete its [unlock requirement](/crack/walkthrough) before spending money on optional equipment.

### How to decide what matters on your current shift

Before leaving the Resource Depot, identify which of these situations applies:

- **A required material is still inaccessible:** Spend money on the excavation tool capable of reaching it.
- **You can extract the material, but transportation takes too long:** Consider a larger Bucket or a more efficient way to move loads.
- **A facility remains locked despite having enough money:** Check the exact Work Order rather than buying unrelated equipment.
- **The specimen is approaching Critical Agitation:** Finish immediate collection, prepare for a reaction, or attempt Sedation before digging deeper.
- **The assignment is complete but you have not advanced:** Check whether it unlocked a catalog, a new area, or a different interaction elsewhere in the laboratory.

These decisions prevent one of the most expensive habits in CRACK: using the wrong activity to solve a progression problem.

## What Should You Buy First? Tools, Buckets, Vehicles, or Workbots?

A higher-tier tool is not automatically your best purchase. Its value depends on what is preventing the current Work Order from advancing.

### Buy excavation capability before carrying capacity

When the next material cannot be broken by your existing equipment, a larger Bucket will not help you reach it.

Prioritize the appropriate Pickaxe, Chisel, Shovel, or later excavation tool. The stronger equipment allows you to open the next layer, exposing material that would otherwise remain inaccessible.

The first major example comes when the Intermediate Catalog opens: obtaining a Shovel is more important than spending the same early earnings on optional conveniences because Membrane requires a different excavation action from the Shell.

Later, buying more powerful equipment makes sense when your current tools cannot damage the exposed material or wear out before you can finish an important excavation.

Avoid replacing a functional tool solely because a more expensive model has become available. An upgrade that provides no immediate access to new material may contribute less than the additional Bucket you need to complete the current shift.

### Upgrade transport when trips become the limiting factor

Carrying resources between the egg and the Resource Depot becomes more expensive in time as your excavation moves inward.

A small Bucket can be perfectly adequate beside the outer Shell. It becomes frustrating when every load requires a long journey through a deep passage.

A capacity upgrade has the greatest value when:

- Your current tool already excavates the required material efficiently.
- Loose resources accumulate faster than you can transport them.
- You repeatedly reach the Depot with full Buckets while leaving substantial material behind.
- The return trip consumes a significant part of the remaining shift.

Buying more carrying capacity does not remove the need to complete the actual deposit. A Bucket delivered to the Depot must still be processed correctly.

The Golf Cart helps once there are enough loads to justify driving it. For a short trip from an exposed Shell patch to the Depot, retrieving and positioning a vehicle may take longer than carrying the Bucket yourself.

That changes as the egg opens up and the amount of material increases. Compare the time spent driving, loading, and unloading against the number of individual carrying trips you are avoiding.

### Don't confuse automation with excavation

Commission the first Workbot when the assignment requires it, but postpone buying additional robots until carrying and collecting material become a consistent bottleneck.

An idle robot is not automatically a reason to expand the fleet. Check its [collection and delivery conditions](/crack/workbots) before spending money on another unit.

### Scanner upgrades should solve an actual search problem

The Deposit Scanner is valuable because it can locate material hidden inside the specimen. However, detecting a resource and being able to excavate it are different things.

Decide what is limiting your current search before buying another scanner upgrade.

A greater scanning depth is useful when the material you need lies beyond your current search range. Sensitivity matters when you are trying to identify deposits that the current scanner cannot detect. Longer-lasting markers become more useful when you regularly lose track of deposits before reaching them.

Do not repeatedly spend money on scanner upgrades when the real problem is a wall your excavation equipment cannot break.

Similarly, a detected deposit does not justify digging in every direction. Use the marker to choose an approach that preserves a practical route back to the laboratory.

## How Does Egg Agitation Work, and When Should You Sedate It?

The egg reacts to excavation. Continued disturbance increases its **Agitation**, particularly when you keep working in the same area.

The laboratory's monitoring equipment shows the specimen's condition and warns when the current level is becoming dangerous.

As Agitation rises, the game can interrupt normal work with reactions that affect the specimen or the laboratory. These can include flooding, tentacles, beetles, pressure-line problems, and power failures.

The immediate challenge is deciding whether another few strikes are worth the risk of interrupting your current task.

### Spread out excavation before the specimen becomes critical

Repeatedly attacking one narrow section can increase local agitation while leaving you with little room to escape a reaction.

When you have several accessible surfaces, move to another workable area instead of concentrating every swing on the same point.

You still need a route toward the center, so spreading out does not mean removing the entire exterior. A wider entrance and an alternative working position can be enough to reduce your reliance on a single exposed section.

Be especially careful inside a deep tunnel. A reaction that changes the material around your entrance can leave you with a much longer return route than expected.

### Sedation delays reactions; it does not eliminate them

Hatch Corp's containment instructions allow **three Sedation doses per work cycle**.

Sedation is therefore a limited way to postpone trouble, not a permanent method of preventing the egg from reacting.

Use the labCONTAINMENT terminal when the specimen's condition calls for intervention. The terminal may present a labCAPTCHA task, such as rotating pipe sections to connect two arrows or completing another displayed matching challenge.

![The labCONTAINMENT signal-matching puzzle showing the Phase slider and yellow and green waveforms.](/images/crack/crack-agitation-sedation.webp)

*Adjust Phase, Amplitude, and Frequency until the yellow waveform matches the green one. Successful Sedation lowers specimen Agitation to 0%.*

Follow the instruction shown on the terminal rather than assuming every challenge uses the same controls.

A successful Sedation attempt can lower Agitation and give you more working time. It does not make the next excavation permanently safe.

After the available doses are exhausted, or when the specimen reports that it is unresponsive to Sedation, plan around the possibility of another reaction instead of waiting for a guaranteed reset.

This is an important time-management decision: one additional stretch of mining may produce valuable material, but it can also trigger a problem that consumes the remainder of your shift.

### What to do when a reaction interrupts work

Different reactions require different immediate responses.

**Flooding:** Stop treating the event like an ordinary mining task. Identify the affected area and the resources needed for the repair. Keeping some accessible Shell material can be useful when the laboratory demands crushed Shell for emergency work.

**Pressure Line Rupture:** The containment instructions call for packing leaks with crushed Shell and tightening the affected connections. Locate every required leak instead of assuming the first repaired section ends the event.

**Tentacles or Appendages:** Move away from the active openings. Continuing to excavate beside a tentacle exposes you to being grabbed or thrown.

**Beetles:** Decide whether you need to deal with the creatures immediately or preserve your current task. If you are pursuing Pest Control, these events also offer opportunities to accumulate the required kills.

**Power Failure:** Check the laboratory's status before assuming that a disabled terminal is permanently broken. Wait for power restoration where the event indicates that service will return.

An event is not always solved by buying stronger excavation equipment. The first step is identifying whether the problem is damaged infrastructure, a temporary shutdown, or a hazard created by the specimen itself.

During emergency repairs, watch where your Buckets are going. Automatic delivery can interfere with plans to keep a filled Bucket nearby for immediate use.

## How Do Shifts, Sleep, and Dream Bonus Affect Your Money?

The laboratory operates on daily work cycles. When a shift approaches its end, you must choose between extracting one more load and returning to Employee Quarters.

The company warns employees to return to their assigned quarters when the work cycle ends. Remaining on the floor can result in penalties, and the sleep-aid sequence can remove your ability to finish an unfinished task normally.

A small amount of extra Shell is rarely worth losing a productive end-of-shift routine.

### The Dream Bonus depends on what you earned that shift

At the end of a work cycle, the game calculates a **Dream Bonus** against the earnings associated with that day's work.

The important distinction is between the bonus percentage and the money that percentage is applied to.

A high Dream Bonus percentage does not automatically produce a large payment. One shift can report a substantial bonus percentage while paying essentially nothing because no money was earned during the cycle.

![CRACK Day 19 shift results showing $549 earnings, a 36% Dream Bonus, and $746 total income.](/images/crack/crack-dream-bonus.webp)

*This shift earned $549 before the Dream Bonus. The 36% bonus added $197, bringing the final payout to $746.*

That makes the final part of a shift an economic decision.

When you have valuable material ready to deposit, completing the sale before returning to bed can make more sense than beginning a new excavation you cannot finish.

On the other hand, a mostly empty Bucket of low-value Shell does not automatically justify ignoring the work-cycle warning.

Consider what you can realistically complete before the cycle closes:

- **A filled Bucket is near the Depot:** Finish the deposit if there is sufficient time.
- **An expensive deposit is still deep inside the egg:** Avoid getting trapped trying to bring it out at the last moment.
- **The Work Order is nearly complete:** Finish it only when you can safely register the result before returning.
- **A new tool is available but not needed tonight:** Leave the purchase until the next shift rather than rushing through the shop.

The goal is not to maximize the number of seconds spent excavating. It is to finish productive work without losing the value of the cycle.

### Sleeping normally is different from being forced to stop

Return to Employee Quarters and use the bed when the work cycle allows it.

Do not assume that being knocked unconscious by the laboratory's sleep gas counts as sleeping normally.

This distinction becomes particularly important for **Well Rested**, which requires sleeping in your quarters every night throughout a completed run. The specific completion requirements and other shift-related achievements are included in the [CRACK achievements checklist](/crack/achievements).

For ordinary progression, the safer routine is to stop starting long excavation jobs as the shift runs out, complete any convenient deposits, and return to your quarters.

## Are Pills, Gambling, and Extra Equipment Worth the Money?

CRACK provides several ways to spend money that do not directly increase your ability to break the egg.

Their usefulness depends on what is already unlocked and whether your main assignment is waiting for something else.

### Morale Supplements can help, but the effect is not guaranteed

The Morale Supplements machine sells pills for **$20**.

Pills can provide different temporary effects, including improved gambling odds and faster tool swings. Some have no useful effect at all.

The **Feeling Lucky** effect improves gambling odds, making it more attractive when you already intend to use the slot machines.

That does not mean you should spend your limited opening cash repeatedly buying pills while a required Shovel or other critical tool remains unaffordable.

A potentially useful temporary buff is still less reliable than a purchase that permanently unlocks access to the next material.

### Treat gambling as a specific objective, not steady income

The Break Room's slot machines can produce significant payouts, but they should not be treated as a predictable replacement for selling resources.

One Work Order requires a single qualifying slot-machine win to unlock an equipment catalog. Completing that assignment is a reason to use the machines even when you would normally avoid gambling.

Outside that requirement, decide how much money you can afford to lose before starting.

A large payout is helpful; repeated unsuccessful attempts can delay your next essential tool purchase.

The same reasoning applies to arcade activities. Complete the required score when a Work Order demands it, but do not assume that every cabinet must be mastered to reach the specimen.

Higher arcade scores and the remaining gambling achievements have separate conditions in the achievements checklist.

## What Happens When You Reach the Center of the Egg?

Reaching the innermost containment area changes the nature of the assignment.

Early CRACK is about materials and equipment unlocks. At the center, you must deal with the Containment Plating and release the specimen.

Once the specimen escapes its containment sack, the company stops asking you to excavate deeper and instructs you to retrieve the creature.

The specimen is a chicken. Its destination determines the ending: deliver it to the Holding Chamber for company processing, or prepare the [hidden escape route](/crack/endings) before carrying it away from the egg.

The laboratory also contains secrets unrelated to the final specimen delivery. In particular, the Redacted security room has a randomized six-digit password revealed through UV markings elsewhere in the facility.

You can investigate that puzzle independently of the ending. Find the necessary flashlight and three clues using the [Redacted door code instructions](/crack/redacted-door-code).

After finishing the assignment, there may still be substantial work left for a complete Steam achievement run. Not every goal is earned through normal excavation: some require another player, rare reactions, unusual shift conditions, or demanding arcade performance.
