A Workbot standing beside the egg with **100% Charge**, **100% Condition**, and the status **Looking for Work** can seem like an expensive decoration. The robot is operational, but it may have no suitable Bucket or collection job available. Buying another robot does not necessarily fix that.

CRACK has two types of Workbots: **Collector Bots** gather loose resources into Buckets, while **Delivery Drones** transport filled Buckets toward the Resource Depot. Neither type excavates new material from the egg. You still need to break the layers, expose the resources, and give the robots something they can handle.

The system becomes useful once you understand the difference between a robot being idle, a Bucket waiting for collection, and a delivery that still needs attention at the Depot.

## How to Unlock and Buy Workbots in CRACK

Complete the Work Order to deposit **$70 worth of Tissue** to unlock Workbot Storage. Enter the newly opened area and interact with the Workbot commissioning terminals.

![The Workbot Station where Collector Bots and Delivery Drones are commissioned.](/images/crack/workbot-station.webp)

*Commission your first Workbot at the storage terminal after completing the Tissue deposit requirement.*

Commission and activate one Collector Bot to complete the next Work Order and unlock the **Specialty Catalog**. You do not need a Delivery Drone or Chalazite upgrade for this assignment.

### Workbot purchase costs

The price increases as you commission additional robots:

| Workbot purchase | Cost |
|---|---:|
| First | $100 |
| Second | $150 |
| Third | $200 |

Later purchases continue becoming more expensive. Check the terminal's displayed price before adding another unit.

This makes the first purchase a straightforward progression requirement, while the next few are an actual investment decision.

A second Collector Bot can speed up collection when several accessible deposits are producing loose material. But two Collectors do not replace a Delivery Drone: full Buckets can accumulate while the robots wait for somewhere to put their next load.

Likewise, buying Delivery Drones before you have available Buckets and loose material gives them little useful work to perform.

For early automation, get the required first robot operating, observe what is limiting it, and purchase the complementary type when there is enough material for both to stay busy.

## Collector Bot vs. Delivery Drone: How the Work Actually Moves

The two robots perform different stages of the same job.

**Collector Bots** look for a Bucket they can use, find accessible loose resources, and load those resources into it. They do not dig new passages or break intact Tissue, Bone, or other egg layers.

**Delivery Drones** handle the transport stage. They collect filled Buckets and carry them toward the Resource Depot.

The working cycle looks like this:

1. Excavate part of the egg with your normal tools.
2. Leave accessible loose resources and an available Bucket for a Collector Bot.
3. Let the Collector fill the Bucket.
4. Have a Delivery Drone transport the filled Bucket to the Depot.
5. Check the Depot and complete any required deposit interaction before counting the material toward your Work Order.

![A Delivery Drone carrying a Bucket through the laboratory.](/images/crack/workbot-bucket-delivery.webp)

*Delivery Drones transport filled Buckets toward the Resource Depot, reducing the need to carry each load manually.*

You may see a Collector working with a partially filled Bucket. That is not a problem: it can continue filling an available container rather than requiring every Bucket to start completely empty.

A key limitation is that a robot delivering a Bucket does not mean every pending Work Order has been completed. Check whether the material actually registered at the Resource Depot. If a filled Bucket is waiting there, finish the normal terminal interaction as needed.

The robots reduce the carrying and gathering work. They do not replace your employee Badge, equipment unlocks, or the game's assignment conditions.

### Can Workbots collect material above the floor?

Collectors are small and may have trouble accessing elevated excavation areas on their own.

Delivery Drones can assist the automated movement around higher work areas, and robots may spend time repositioning before beginning another task. However, do not rely on automation to solve an inaccessible excavation.

Opening a workable passage, leaving resources within reach, and providing Buckets is much more reliable than buying several additional robots while all the material remains trapped inside a narrow tunnel.

This matters particularly once you begin using Scaffolding. A large elevated pile can appear ready for automation, but the robots still need an accessible collection and transport cycle.

## Why Is My Workbot Looking for Work or Not Moving?

A Workbot displaying **Looking for Work** with 100% Charge and 100% Condition is not necessarily broken.

Check whether it has an available Bucket and accessible work before using a Wrench or purchasing another robot. Newly commissioned Workbots may also need a short period before they begin moving.

### Collector Bot has no usable Bucket

Start with the Bucket supply.

A Collector needs a container before it can complete its collection job. When Buckets are already full, being transported, or unavailable, the Collector may wait instead of moving toward the resource pile.

Try this:

1. Bring an empty or partly filled Bucket to an accessible collection area.
2. Leave loose material nearby by excavating a small section of the egg.
3. Give the Collector time to select the available work.
4. Check whether its status changes from searching to collecting.

The relationship becomes particularly noticeable with several Collectors and too few Buckets. You can have enough loose material for multiple loads but only one container circulating through the system.

Adding another Collector in that situation increases the number of robots waiting for the same limited resource.

### Delivery Drone is waiting for a full Bucket

A Delivery Drone needs something ready to transport.

If your Collectors are still filling containers, or no full Bucket is available, the Drone may have nothing to collect yet.

Let the current Bucket finish filling, then observe whether the Drone begins fetching it.

This is different from a broken robot. Purchasing another Drone will not make a partially filled container reach capacity sooner.

### The robots are active, but the Resource Depot has not credited the materials

Follow the Bucket rather than assuming that a moving Drone equals a completed deposit.

A robot may place a filled Bucket where it still needs to be processed. Check the Depot, use your employee Badge where required, and confirm that the Work Order counter actually increases.

This is especially important with deposit assignments. You can spend an entire shift digging more material while a useful load is already waiting near the terminal.

### The robot has no accessible loose material

Bots collect what you have already excavated; they cannot break a new layer to create their own assignment.

Expose a workable pocket of material and clear enough room for the collection process. Loose debris beyond an obstructed entrance may not turn into a practical task for a ground-level Collector.

If the robot begins working after a Bucket or reachable material becomes available, there was no reason to repair or replace it.

## How to Repair a Broken Workbot

Workbots have separate **Charge** and **Condition** indicators.

Condition deteriorates while robots work. Eventually, a Workbot can break down and stop contributing to the collection cycle.

Use a **Wrench** to repair a damaged Workbot.

Approach the affected robot with the Wrench equipped and strike it to perform the repair. Inspect its condition afterward rather than assuming that one repair attempt necessarily resolves every issue.

A few checks help distinguish damage from ordinary inactivity:

- **Low or depleted Condition:** The robot needs maintenance. Use the Wrench.
- **Looking for Work with healthy Condition:** Check for accessible resources and usable Buckets first.
- **Low Charge:** Inspect the robot's power state; repairing Condition is not the same as restoring Charge.
- **A Delivery Drone stopped while carrying a Bucket:** Check both the robot and its load before purchasing another Drone.

Broken Delivery Drones also deserve attention around busy work areas because the Bucket they were handling may no longer be where you expected it.

### Repairing robots and IT Specialist

The **IT Specialist** achievement requires **10 Workbot repairs**.

Those repairs fit naturally into an automation-heavy run as Condition declines. For the achievement's completion tracking and other cleanup requirements, use the [CRACK achievements checklist](/crack/achievements).

For normal work, repair malfunctioning robots before increasing the size of the fleet. Restoring an already purchased Collector or Drone may solve the collection bottleneck without another expensive commission.

## How to Upgrade Workbots With Chalazite

Workbots do not use the same cash-upgrade process as the Golf Cart.

Their levels increase through **Chalazite crystals** found inside the egg. You can locate these crystals using the Deposit Scanner, excavate toward the deposit, and carry the crystal to a robot.

Read the crystal's description before using or selling it. Different Chalazite crystals provide different level increases.

| Chalazite crystal | Workbot upgrade |
|---|---:|
| Green Chalazite | +1 level |
| Crystal marked "+2 Levels" | +2 levels |
| Purple Chalazite | +3 levels |

The maximum Workbot level is **Level 5**. Upgrading a Workbot to that level also unlocks the **Overclocked** achievement.

![Purple Chalazite showing that it can upgrade a Workbot by three levels.](/images/crack/workbot-chalazite-upgrade.webp)

*Purple Chalazite grants +3 Workbot levels. Recover the crystal and bring it to a robot that can still be upgraded.*

To apply an upgrade:

1. Find a Chalazite crystal and pick it up.
2. Approach a Collector Bot or Delivery Drone that is below Level 5.
3. Aim at the robot until the upgrade interaction appears.
4. Use the displayed interaction to apply the crystal.
5. Check the robot's new level.

A +2 crystal applied to a Level 1 Workbot raises it to Level 3. A +3 crystal on that same Level 1 robot takes it to Level 4.

Increased levels make the robots more effective at moving through their work. Faster movement is especially noticeable when several Buckets are being collected or delivered around the laboratory.

### Which Workbot should get the upgrade first?

Upgrade the part of the system that is actually slowing you down.

A Collector Bot deserves the crystal when several loose resource piles are accessible, Buckets are available, but the collection work is progressing slowly.

A Delivery Drone benefits more when full Buckets are waiting to be transported while your Collectors are already keeping pace with excavation.

There is little benefit in giving every Collector the maximum level while your only Delivery Drone cannot keep up with the filled Buckets.

Likewise, a faster Drone provides limited improvement if no Collector has anything ready for pickup.

The +3 crystal is particularly valuable early because it can move a Level 1 robot straight to Level 4. Consider using it on an underleveled unit rather than one already close to the cap.

Do not automatically send a newly discovered Chalazite crystal through the Shredder or sell it as ordinary material. Its upgrade value may be greater than the immediate payment.

### The Work Order that requires a Workbot upgrade

A later Work Order asks you to **upgrade a Workbot by one level** to unlock the Prograde Catalog.

You need a Chalazite crystal and a robot that can still receive an upgrade. Apply the crystal and confirm that the assignment advances.

The assignment does not require every robot to reach Level 5.

Once Prograde is unlocked, you can return to the main excavation route. The upgraded bot continues contributing to resource collection while you work toward the deeper layers.

## Why Delivery Drones Take Buckets You Wanted to Keep

A Delivery Drone may collect a full Bucket that you intended to save for a later task. Once the Bucket is available for automated transport, the Drone can treat it as another delivery.

This becomes inconvenient when you have prepared materials for an emergency, such as repairing a flooded area. The robot is carrying out its normal job, but the material is no longer where you planned to use it.

Workbots operate autonomously, so there is no individual task-assignment control for telling a Drone to leave one particular available Bucket alone.

To avoid losing immediate access to the materials you need:

- Finish an urgent repair before expanding automated delivery.
- Keep a required Bucket under direct control when you need to use it immediately.
- Check where the Drone has moved a load before excavating replacement materials.
- Avoid buying additional Delivery Drones solely to speed up work during an event that requires material to remain nearby.

Do not assume that moving a reserved Bucket a short distance will reliably protect it. The important decision is whether that container can safely enter the automated transport cycle at all.

After the immediate repair or other urgent interaction is complete, normal collection and delivery can resume without competing for those supplies.

## When Are More Workbots Worth Buying?

The most expensive Workbot fleet is not necessarily the most productive one.

Before commissioning another unit, look at what your existing robots are doing.

**Several Collectors waiting for Buckets:** Supply more usable Buckets and check whether deliveries are keeping up before buying another Collector.

**Full Buckets accumulating near the egg:** A Delivery Drone is a better next purchase than another ground Collector.

**Both robot types moving consistently:** Additional units or Chalazite upgrades can reduce time spent carrying resources, particularly during prolonged Tissue, Calcite, and other deposit assignments.

**Most robots waiting with healthy Condition:** The bottleneck is likely available work, not robot count. Excavate more accessible material, resolve the Bucket shortage, or finish pending Depot deliveries.

**A robot repeatedly breaking down:** Repair it and consider improving the existing fleet before paying the next rising purchase price.

Collector Bots and Delivery Drones are most useful during the labor-heavy middle of CRACK, when your tools can expose material faster than you can personally gather and transport it.

Add another robot only when the existing collection and delivery jobs are consistently occupied. An idle fleet gains little from additional units, while a balanced group of Collectors and Delivery Drones can keep materials moving as you excavate deeper.
