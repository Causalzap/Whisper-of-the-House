## How to Build and Expand a Safe Zone

Start by identifying the civilians you can protect without enclosing more ground than your budget can support.

Press **B** to select the Safe Zone tool, then draw the zone around the area you want to protect. A compact group of buildings can provide a useful starting point, especially when you can defend its perimeter without stretching your resources across several distant streets.

The zone's walls provide a barrier against zombies. However, they also cost money, and that cost increases as the boundary becomes larger.

When drawing the initial zone:

1. Look for an area with civilians close enough to protect together.
2. Keep the boundary around the useful buildings and streets rather than enclosing large amounts of empty ground.
3. Leave room in the budget for Gates and defenses.
4. Check the zone's coverage and cost before starting the outbreak.
5. Identify where civilians outside the boundary will need to enter.

The first zone does not have to contain everyone you intend to save. Civilians can move through Gates after the outbreak begins, allowing the protected population to increase.

### How Do You Make an Existing Safe Zone Bigger?

Select the Safe Zone and adjust its boundary corners to include additional ground. You can reshape the perimeter as the available city area expands between scenarios.

Before extending a wall around another neighborhood, compare the new cost with the benefit of protecting that population. A long extension around a few isolated buildings may consume money that would be more useful elsewhere.

You can also select the zone and use **Upgrade** to strengthen it. Upgrading improves its durability; expanding changes the area it encloses. These are different decisions, and neither replaces the need for a defended entrance.

## How Do Gates Let Civilians Enter a Safe Zone?

A Safe Zone wall protects the people behind it, but civilians outside need a usable opening.

Place a **Gate** on the boundary where people can reasonably approach from surrounding streets. The Gate creates an entrance through which civilians can pass into the protected area.

Press **G** to place a Gate, then choose the part of the wall where you want the entrance.

## How Does Broadcast Bring Civilians Into a Safe Zone?

A building assigned to **Broadcast** can attract civilians from the surrounding area toward a Safe Zone. This is especially useful when people are scattered across nearby streets rather than already standing close to the walls.

When configuring a suitable building, check its selected function. A building intended to attract civilians needs to be set to Broadcast rather than another available function such as Repair or Budget.

Broadcast and Gates solve different problems. Broadcast draws people toward the protected area, but civilians still need an accessible entrance through the boundary.

If a zone is not receiving the population you expected, inspect both the building's selected function and the Gate positions. In one evacuation attempt, civilians began moving toward the zone after the player noticed Broadcast had not been enabled.

On larger maps, several smaller Broadcast-supported Safe Zones can offer nearby destinations for separated groups. However, each additional zone still needs enough protection to survive the outbreak.

## What Does Hold Do in Zombies Aren't Real?

**Hold gathers civilians into a designated area and keeps them there.**

The Hold tool uses two concentric circles:

- The **outer circle** attracts nearby civilians toward the center.
- The **inner circle** holds civilians after they arrive.

This makes Hold useful when you want people concentrated in a particular location rather than continuing to move through the surrounding streets.

Hold does not create a defensive wall. It controls civilian movement, so the location still needs to be suitable for people to remain there.

### How to Use Hold

Press **E** to select Hold, then drag from the center toward the edge to define the area where civilians should gather.

Check both circles rather than looking only at the central point. The outer circle determines which nearby people the Hold area can influence, while the inner circle identifies where they are meant to remain.

A practical use is to gather scattered civilians into one area before directing them onward with an Evac Corridor.

You can also use Hold to keep people within an area you have already prepared to protect.

However, keeping civilians in place can be dangerous if the surrounding streets are about to be overrun. A Hold area is not a substitute for a protected destination.

### Can Civilians Leave a Hold Zone?

Yes. An **Evac Corridor can move civilians out of Hold's inner circle**. Draw the corridor from the gathering area toward an accessible Gate, then watch whether the civilians begin leaving the Hold area.

If they remain stationary, check whether the corridor covers the waiting group and points in the intended direction. Adding another Hold area is unlikely to help when the civilians have already gathered but have not been given a useful route onward.

## How to Use an Evac Corridor

**Evac Corridor creates a directional route for civilian movement.**

Press **C** to select Corridor, then click and drag from the intended starting area toward the destination.

The game displays a shaded corridor with a direction indicator. The arrow shows where the corridor is trying to send civilians.

To guide people toward a Safe Zone:

1. Locate the civilians you want to evacuate.
2. Choose the Gate they should approach.
3. Select the Evac Corridor tool.
4. Drag from the civilian area toward the Safe Zone entrance.
5. Check that the corridor covers the civilians you intend to move.
6. Confirm that the arrow points toward the destination, not away from it.
7. Start the outbreak and observe whether civilians enter the route and reach the Gate.

The direction indicator is an important part of the setup. A corridor pointing away from the entrance will not serve the same purpose as one leading toward it.

### Does the Corridor Have to Touch the Gate?

If civilians gather near a Gate without entering, try redrawing the final section of the Evac Corridor closer to that entrance. In one evacuation attempt, civilians began moving after the route was adjusted.

This does not mean every corridor must intersect a Gate at an exact point. Watch whether the affected civilians can reach the opening. If they stop short or move elsewhere, check the corridor's direction and the Gate position together.

### Does Evac Corridor Protect Civilians?

No. Its purpose is to direct civilian movement, not create a fortified passage.

People using the route can still be exposed to zombies before they reach safety.

A long evacuation path across an exposed street or bridge may fail because the infection reaches the civilians before they complete the journey.

Where possible, shorten the distance to safety and avoid routing large groups through areas that are already being overrun.

</section>

## How to Combine Hold and Evac Corridor

On a map with civilians scattered across several side streets, place Hold where its outer circle can attract the largest nearby group. Then draw an Evac Corridor from the gathering area toward the Safe Zone's accessible entrance. The intended movement is **scattered streets → Hold → Corridor → Gate**.

Start the outbreak and watch whether civilians leave the inner circle and enter the route. If they remain gathered, check the corridor's starting position and direction before adding another Hold area. If they enter the route but never reach safety, inspect the approach to the Gate and whether zombies have already reached that street.

You do not need both commands in every situation. Civilians already moving along a useful route may only need a Corridor to correct their direction, while Hold alone can keep a group at a suitable gathering point. Neither command provides protection against zombies, so a long route through an exposed district can still fail.

</section>

## One Large Safe Zone or Multiple Smaller Safe Zones?

There is no universal rule that one large Safe Zone is always better than two smaller ones.

The choice depends on the civilian distribution, the amount of boundary you must build, the available defensive positions, and the routes by which people can reach safety.

<GuideTable
  columns={safeZoneSizeColumns}
  rows={safeZoneSizeRows}
/>

A large Safe Zone can work well when its boundary follows a defensible area and protects a substantial population.

It becomes less attractive when a long stretch of wall encloses empty streets or parks while leaving too little money to protect the perimeter.

### Why Can a Huge Safe Zone Be a Bad Idea?

In one Scenario debrief, the game warned that Safe Zone walls consumed **53% of the available budget** and recommended shrinking the protected area to free money for weapons.

## Why Are Civilians Not Entering the Safe Zone?

Start with the route into the Safe Zone rather than immediately assuming the defensive wall or the entire zone is broken.

### 1. There Is No Accessible Gate

A wall without a useful opening prevents civilians outside it from reaching the interior through that section of the boundary.

Inspect the side facing the stranded population. If the only Gate is on the opposite side, consider moving or adding an entrance where civilians can approach it.

### 2. Broadcast Is Not Enabled

If you expect a special building to attract civilians into the Safe Zone, check whether its selected function is actually set to **Broadcast**.

Without the intended Broadcast effect, nearby civilians may not move toward the zone as expected. Activate Broadcast, confirm that a usable Gate exists, and observe where people move once the outbreak begins.

### 3. The Evac Corridor Points the Wrong Way

Check the corridor's direction arrow.

If the route points away from the Safe Zone, redraw it toward the intended entrance. Also check whether the corridor actually reaches the population you want to influence.

### 4. Civilians Gather Near the Gate but Do Not Enter

Inspect the last part of the route.

Try adjusting the corridor so its destination approaches the Gate more directly. Observe whether the civilians begin passing through after the change.

A nearby Gate does not guarantee that every civilian will immediately choose the desired path.

### 5. Hold Is Keeping People in Another Location

If the affected civilians are within a Hold area, remember that the inner circle is intended to keep them there.

Check whether an Evac Corridor is positioned to move them out of that area. Otherwise, Hold may be producing the gathering behavior you requested rather than the evacuation you intended.

### 6. The Route Is Too Exposed

A corridor cannot save people who are overwhelmed before reaching the destination.

If the infection spreads across a bridge or major road faster than the civilians can travel, consider moving the protected destination closer, changing the route, or defending the approach.

### 7. Civilians Still Behave Unexpectedly

Civilian movement is one of the systems being refined during Early Access.

You may see people flee in unexpected directions, fail to use a nearby entrance, or respond differently from what the route initially suggests.

Before treating this as a fixed rule, retry with a simpler arrangement: one nearby Gate, a short corridor, and a clearly visible group of civilians.

Change one part of the layout at a time so that you can identify which adjustment actually affects their movement.

</section>

## What to Check Before Starting the Outbreak

A good evacuation plan has a protected destination, a usable way to enter it, and a reason for civilians to move in the desired direction.

Before beginning the next Scenario, check these points:

- **Safe Zone:** Does the boundary protect a useful concentration of civilians without consuming too much of the budget?
- **Gates:** Can people outside the walls reach an entrance?
- **Broadcast:** Are buildings intended to attract nearby civilians assigned to Broadcast?
- **Hold:** Are the gathering areas placed where you actually want people to stop?
- **Evac Corridor:** Does each arrow point toward the intended destination?
- **Travel distance:** Can civilians plausibly reach safety before the infection spreads through their route?
- **Defense:** Are the exposed Gates and important walls protected?

Once the outbreak begins, watch where civilians actually move rather than judging the plan only by the shapes drawn during preparation.

If people fail to enter, adjust the entrance and corridor arrangement on the next attempt. If they enter but the zone collapses, investigate the defense instead.

Players attempting special objectives such as winning without placing a Safe Zone should check the different restrictions in the <Link href="/zombies-arent-real/achievements">Zombies Aren't Real achievements guide</Link>.
