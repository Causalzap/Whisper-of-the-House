<GuideQuickAnswer title="What Should You Build First in Zombies Aren't Real?">
Start with a compact **Safe Zone**, place **Gates** for civilians, and deploy **Garrisons** in buildings that can fire toward its perimeter. Check your starting civilian coverage against the Scenario's required survival percentage before pressing **Space**. After unlocking Mortars and battle abilities, use elevated firing positions and emergency support to stop concentrated zombie groups. Spend your budget on a defensible perimeter rather than enclosing more streets than you can protect.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

Your first few minutes in **Zombies Aren't Real** are spent planning a defense before the outbreak begins. The city is full of white civilian dots, and your job is to keep enough people alive while your weapons eliminate the zombies.

The challenge is not simply covering the largest possible area. Your walls need defenders, your defenders need useful firing positions, and civilians outside the protected area need somewhere to go.

As you clear scenarios, the available city area grows and new units, abilities, and special building functions become available. The same simple Garrison setup that works on an early Boston map may need substantial changes when you reach larger cities.

<section id="first-scenario" className="scroll-mt-24">

## How Do You Start Your First Scenario?

The preparation phase gives you time to examine the map and place defenses before the infection begins.

On an early Boston scenario, the game required **4% of the population to survive**. That percentage was the objective for that particular battle, not a fixed requirement for every map.

The top of the screen shows information such as your current protected population and the coverage available from your Safe Zones. Read those values before deciding how much ground to enclose.

### Build Your First Safe Zone

Press **B** and draw a Safe Zone around a group of buildings containing civilians.

A useful starting position has several buildings near the boundary where soldiers can defend the walls. Avoid extending the perimeter across a large park or empty district unless you can actually protect the added wall length.

Once the boundary is in place, press **G** to add Gates.

Civilians outside your initial Safe Zone can enter through those openings after the outbreak starts. Your starting protected percentage therefore does not necessarily have to equal the Scenario's final survival requirement.

In one early Boston battle, the player began with approximately 2% of civilians protected against a 4% survival objective. Additional civilians entered through Gates, and the Scenario ended successfully.

For Gate placement, Broadcast, Hold, and Evac Corridor controls, see the <Link href="/zombies-arent-real/safe-zones">Safe Zones and evacuation guide</Link>.

### Place Garrisons Where They Can Actually Shoot

Press **1** to select a Garrison, then choose buildings with a useful view of the Safe Zone boundary.

You do not need to fill every building with soldiers. Begin with the perimeter sections most likely to be attacked, then inspect the remaining gaps.

Select a deployed Garrison to examine its firing range.

Pay particular attention to walls that extend far beyond the surrounding buildings. A wall can be within your Safe Zone yet still be difficult for the nearest Garrison to defend.

### Check the Defense Before Starting

Before pressing **Space**, make sure the layout answers three questions:

1. Can enough civilians reach or remain inside the protected area?
2. Can the Garrisons cover the important wall approaches?
3. Is there enough money left to correct an obvious weakness?

When the battle begins, the zombie outbreaks appear and the plan is tested. You can deploy available battle abilities during combat, but the initial building and wall layout must be prepared beforehand.

## Why Are Your Garrisons Not Shooting the Zombies?

A common early mistake is building a large defensive perimeter without checking whether the nearest soldiers can actually fire at the zombies attacking it.

In one Boston setup, a wall crossed a park well ahead of the Garrison buildings. The soldiers were positioned inside the Safe Zone, but the wall's location left them unable to cover part of the attack.

The next attempt moved the wall closer to the defended buildings and reconsidered the placement of soldiers.

### Look for Dead Zones Along the Perimeter

Select your Garrisons and compare their firing ranges against the walls.

If a long section has no useful coverage, consider whether you can move the wall closer to the buildings instead of buying another unit.

Sometimes an additional Garrison is the answer. In other cases, a smaller boundary solves the problem without increasing your defense cost.

Buildings themselves can also interfere with useful lines of fire. Do not judge a position only by the distance between the unit and the zombies.

### Concentrate Fire Where Zombies Will Arrive

A Garrison deep inside the protected area may survive longer, but it may contribute little while zombies are attacking a distant section of wall.

During preparation, examine streets, bridges, and open approaches that lead toward the boundary. Positions covering the same important approach can be more useful than spreading every weapon evenly across the entire city.

Do not leave the opposite side completely undefended, however. Later scenarios can produce threats from several directions.

If the wall has already failed, see the <Link href="/zombies-arent-real/safe-zones">Safe Zones guide</Link> for breach outcomes, exposed entrances, and boundary adjustments.

</section>

## Which Units Should You Use First: Garrisons, Mortars, Snipers, or Turrets?

Different defenses solve different problems. The strongest early setup is usually a combination of weapons rather than placing the same unit on every available building.

### Mortars Handle Groups of Zombies

Mortars become available through progression. Their attacks have a low firing rate but deal area damage, making them useful against groups of zombies.

The game also encourages using higher rooftops for Mortars.

Press **V** to inspect building heights, then compare elevated positions with the areas you need to cover. The tallest building is not necessarily the best choice if it leaves the important approach outside the weapon's useful range.

Mortars can also hit friendly units or civilians. In an early battle, mortar fire struck civilians gathered near a Gate.

Avoid treating every crowded street as a safe target simply because zombies are present.

### When Should You Use Snipers or Turrets?

Snipers become useful when a distant approach needs additional coverage. Check their actual range before placing them behind tall buildings or in locations where their view may be restricted.

Turrets provide another way to concentrate fire around an important position. They are especially worth evaluating when you already have the wider approaches covered but a nearby section still lacks damage.

Neither weapon should be added automatically. Compare the cost with the part of the battlefield it will actually influence.

## How Should You Spend Money and Manage Ammunition?

Your preparation budget must cover the protected area and the weapons that keep it standing. Expanding the perimeter before funding its defenses can leave you with more walls than your Garrisons and Mortars can protect.

Spend enough to establish a workable Safe Zone, then inspect the perimeter for gaps in firing coverage. If the remaining money cannot defend those approaches, reduce the boundary or reconsider which buildings need weapons.

For the cost and coverage tradeoffs between larger and smaller protected areas, see the <Link href="/zombies-arent-real/safe-zones">Safe Zone size and budget comparison</Link>.

### Do Not Garrison Every Building

If a building cannot cover an exposed wall or useful street, leave it empty until you identify a reason to place a weapon there.

When funds are limited, a single well-positioned unit may contribute more than several soldiers covering quiet streets far behind the perimeter.

Some unit costs also increase as more copies are deployed. For example, an early Mortar tutorial explains that each additional Mortar increases the price of the next one.

Watch the current price shown on the deployment control instead of assuming every copy costs the same amount.

### How Does Reloading Work Inside a Safe Zone?

Building-based defenses benefit from operating inside an intact Safe Zone.

Early gameplay demonstrates that Garrisons inside protected areas can continue reloading instead of exhausting a fixed ammunition supply. Units placed outside the zone do not necessarily receive the same benefit.

Later battles also show ammunition problems after parts of a Safe Zone are breached, although those observations do not establish every rule governing reloading after a breach.

For a sustained defense, check whether a weapon is actually inside the protected area and whether that area is likely to remain intact.

### Should You Spend Every Dollar?

Do not buy another weapon simply because money remains.

Check whether an exposed approach still needs protection, a useful special building needs an assignment, or a unit could be moved to a better position.

A small amount of unspent money is less important than a layout that can stop the expected attacks.

## What Do Special Buildings Do in Zombies Aren't Real?

Special buildings give you additional ways to improve the defense, influence civilian behavior, or evacuate people.

The available functions depend on the building. Civic buildings, trade buildings, and rescue-related locations do not all provide the same choices.

  rows={specialBuildingRows}
/>

### Choose the Function That Solves Your Immediate Problem

A **Repair** building near the outer wall can help sustain the perimeter while zombies attack it.

A **Budget** building is more useful when several planned defenses fall within its effective area. The savings are less valuable if there are few eligible weapons nearby.

A **Radio Tower** can improve the reach of building-based weapons, making it useful when multiple firing positions cover an important district.

**Police** can provide additional officers, while **Militia** changes some civilians into combatants. Since converted civilians no longer count as saved, choosing Militia involves a tradeoff between additional firepower and your civilian total.

### Broadcast and Evacuation Buildings

Broadcast attracts nearby civilians, while Airlift offers a way to evacuate people through suitable rescue buildings. Both can improve a Scenario where saving enough civilians is harder than holding the defensive perimeter.

Before relying on either function, check whether the building is positioned to help the population you want to protect. For Gate access, Broadcast setup, and civilian movement problems, see the <Link href="/zombies-arent-real/safe-zones">Safe Zones and evacuation guide</Link>.

### Avoid Choosing Every Function the Same Way

A building that repairs nearby walls has little immediate value when no relevant walls fall inside its effective area.

Likewise, making every available building a Broadcast location may draw people toward zones that cannot defend them.

Look at the building's actual area of influence and the part of the city you need to protect before selecting a function.

<section id="battle-abilities" className="scroll-mt-24">

## How Do You Use Paratroopers, Bombers, and Helicopters?

As scenarios are completed, additional battle abilities become available.

Press **Tab** to open the Loadout. Spend your available skill points on the support options you intend to use during combat.

The preparation budget and your battle-ability allocation serve different purposes. A strong Garrison arrangement does not automatically provide helicopters or airstrikes; those abilities need to be available in the Loadout.

### Paratroopers

Paratroopers can be deployed during battle to reinforce a threatened location or attack a group of zombies away from the main defensive line.

They can be particularly useful when an outbreak starts far from the Safe Zone and you need to slow its spread.

The ability includes different orders, including Guard and Seek and Destroy. Choose an order that matches the purpose of the deployment.

Dropping soldiers directly into a very large zombie group may waste the reinforcement before it can accomplish anything useful.

### Bomber Airstrikes

Bombers are useful against concentrated zombie groups and exposed approaches.

A single targeted strike can be preferable when you want to preserve additional bombing opportunities for later threats. Dragging a target strip can commit multiple strikes across an area, depending on the selected ability and available uses.

Bombing bridges can also change the routes available to zombies, potentially concentrating them at crossings that remain intact.

That can help create a chokepoint, but it may also make the surviving crossings much more dangerous.

### Helicopters

Helicopters provide mobile fire support after being called into battle.

They can respond to a large group of zombies without requiring you to place another permanent defensive building during preparation.

A helicopter does not remain available indefinitely. It can expend its ammunition and eventually leave the battlefield.

Do not plan an entire defense around one helicopter arriving at exactly the right location or remaining in action for the rest of the Scenario.

### When Should You Call Support?

Wait until you can identify a useful target rather than automatically spending everything as soon as combat begins.

Early support can prevent one outbreak from spreading into several districts. Later support may be needed to stop a concentrated attack that has already reached an important wall.

Watch the cooldowns, the remaining number of uses, and the location of civilians before deploying explosives or reinforcements.

## How Does Campaign Progression Work?

**Zombies Aren't Real** includes Campaign and Free Play modes.

In the Early Access Campaign shown, Boston and Miami are available as initial city choices. Additional cities and scenarios become accessible through progression.

A Campaign is divided into successive stages. Early stages can cover only part of a city, with later scenarios extending into larger portions of the map.

Your previous defensive layout provides a starting point for reconsidering the next Scenario, but a layout that handled an earlier outbreak may be poorly suited to the newly exposed area.

### What Should You Change After Winning a Scenario?

Start by looking at the result rather than immediately adding more units.

If the wall survived and civilian losses were the main problem, the population may have been too far from safety or unable to reach an entrance.

If plenty of people reached the Safe Zone but the walls broke, your next priority is usually the defensive perimeter and weapon positions.

When a new unit or special building becomes available, decide which weakness it addresses. Mortars can improve area damage, while additional support abilities give you more options during battle.

Do not replace a successful defense simply because a newly unlocked unit appears stronger.

### Why Are Later City Maps Harder?

Later scenarios expose larger portions of the city, increasing the distance between civilian populations and the positions you can defend.

A layout that survived an earlier stage may no longer cover the most important approaches. Recheck building heights, weapon ranges, available special buildings, and the routes zombies can use before starting the next battle.

Larger maps can also make it harder for civilians to reach safety in time. If evacuation distance becomes the main obstacle, compare the alternatives in the <Link href="/zombies-arent-real/safe-zones">Safe Zone size and cost guide</Link> before committing your next preparation budget.

</section>

<section id="why-losing" className="scroll-mt-24">

## Why Do You Keep Losing Scenarios?

Repeated failures usually reveal something specific about the layout. Use the location and timing of the failure to decide what to change.

### Zombies Reach a Wall That Nobody Can Defend

Inspect the buildings near the failed section.

Check their firing ranges and whether the wall is too far away. A smaller wall layout or a different Garrison position may be more effective than adding soldiers elsewhere.

### You Run Out of Money Before Deploying Enough Weapons

Look at the total length of your Safe Zone boundaries.

Remove unnecessary extensions around empty ground, then spend the recovered budget on exposed approaches.

A Budget special building may reduce the cost of qualifying defenses when enough of them fall within its area.

### Civilians Remain Outside Your Safe Zone

If your walls remain intact but the protected population barely increases, more firepower will not solve the immediate problem. Watch where civilians stop: those gathering outside the perimeter may be unable to reach an entrance, while people farther away may never be drawn toward the zone.

Check the <Link href="/zombies-arent-real/safe-zones">Gate, Broadcast, and evacuation route troubleshooting steps</Link> before changing the defensive buildings.

### Mortars Are Killing Civilians

Area damage can hit more than zombies.

When friendly casualties occur near a Gate or evacuation path, reconsider where your Mortars are firing. A weapon that destroys large zombie groups may still reduce your result if its attacks land among the people you are supposed to protect.

### One Outbreak Escapes Your Initial Defenses

After the battle begins, watch where the first infected groups emerge.

A fast response with Paratroopers, a helicopter, or an airstrike may prevent a group from spreading through the surrounding streets.

Once multiple outbreaks merge into a much larger horde, stopping them can require considerably more firepower.

## Do You Need Diamond to Progress in Zombies Aren't Real?

No. You can clear a Scenario and continue progressing without earning its highest badge.

After each victory, the results screen shows the awarded rating alongside information about the battle. Use those results to identify whether your current setup is strong enough to move forward or worth improving before the next stage.

Gold and Diamond ratings become more important when attempting special Campaign-wide challenges. If you want to improve your grades or complete those objectives, see the <Link href="/zombies-arent-real/achievements">Zombies Aren't Real achievements guide</Link> for all 14 requirements and the difference between individual Scenario badges and full-Campaign conditions.

## What Should You Focus On After Your First Win?

After a victory, inspect which wall took the most damage and whether the protected civilian population increased once the outbreak began. A collapsing perimeter calls for different weapon positions or a shorter exposed wall; a secure perimeter with few survivors points toward an evacuation problem instead.

When a new unit or battle ability becomes available, use the next Scenario to test how it addresses the weakness you just encountered. A Mortar on a high rooftop can help against clustered zombies, while Paratroopers or a helicopter give you another way to respond when an outbreak develops away from the main defenses.

Two areas deserve particular attention as you progress:

- <Link href="/zombies-arent-real/safe-zones">Safe Zones, Gates, Broadcast, Hold, and Evac Corridors</Link> — for expanding protected areas, directing civilians, and fixing evacuation problems.
- <Link href="/zombies-arent-real/achievements">All 14 Zombies Aren't Real Achievements</Link> — for individual Scenario challenges, Campaign badges, and special restrictions.

A successful early defense does not have to save every civilian or achieve Diamond. It needs to protect enough people, eliminate the zombie threat, and leave you with a clearer idea of what to change when the next part of the city opens.
