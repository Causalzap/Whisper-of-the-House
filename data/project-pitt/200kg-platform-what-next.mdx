<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
If I am standing at the **0 / 200 kg** platform, I do this:

**put enough stable physical objects on the plate to go past 200 kg**

→ the upper bulkhead retracts

→ climb up to the device showing **0%**

→ if Anomalies are not unlocked yet, leave and continue normal progression

→ once I can make Anomalies, bring a couple back upstairs

→ feed them into the 0% device

→ the meter reaches **100%** and the hidden gate opens

I do **not** aim for exactly 200 kg. One working run went:

**118 → 146 → 172 → 206 kg**

and **206 kg** opened the route immediately.

I also do not keep feeding normal products into the 0% device. If Anomalies are still locked, I leave and come back later.

<h2 id="stable-weight">
  Stable weight matters more than a bigger messy pile
</h2>

The frustrating part is not usually finding enough mass.

It is keeping that mass on the plate.

A loose pile can shift when I bump it, climb over it, or knock one object into another.

So I build the weight in a stable way:

**larger objects first**

→ **keep them near the center**

→ **avoid sweeping or kicking around the plate**

→ **stop rearranging once the route opens**

If the number drops after I add something, I restack what is already there before I bring more objects.

A stable **172 kg** is more useful than a pile that briefly reaches 200 and immediately slides away.

<h2 id="what-opens">
  The bulkhead opening is the confirmation I care about
</h2>

When the threshold is met, I watch the environment.

The useful signal is:

**the upper bulkhead retracts**

That tells me the weight puzzle is done.

Once that happens, I do not keep adjusting the platform.

I leave the weight where it is and climb into the newly opened section.

The next blocker is separate.

<h2 id="if-anomalies-are-locked">
  If Anomalies are still locked, I leave
</h2>

This is the most important timing decision on the page.

I can reach the 0% device **before Anomalies are available**.

If that happens, I stop.

I do not build a Cannon.

I do not make another conveyor.

I do not feed increasingly expensive normal products.

I go back downstairs and keep normal progression moving.

Once Anomalies unlock, I return.

That is much faster than treating 0% like a physics puzzle that needs a clever machine.

<h2 id="when-to-return">
  I return as soon as I can make Anomalies
</h2>

When Anomalies become available, I prepare a few before going back upstairs.

If I still need help producing them, I use the <Link href="/project-pitt/anomaly-guide">Anomaly Guide</Link>.

I do not send every Anomaly straight into the Pit.

I keep a couple accessible for the hidden route.

That prevents the annoying situation where I reach the 0% device, remember what it wants, and have to climb all the way back down again.

<h2 id="feed-anomalies">
  Anomalies are what move the 0% device to 100%
</h2>

When I return, I feed Anomalies into the device.

In a completed route, **a couple of Anomalies** were enough to take the meter from:

**0%**

to:

**100%**

and open the hidden gate.

For progression, carrying the required products up is enough.

<h2 id="if-the-meter-does-not-move">
  If an Anomaly does not change the meter, I check the intake before making more
</h2>

Anomalies are valuable enough that I do not throw ten of them at the room blindly.

If one appears to do nothing, I first check whether it actually entered the device.

An object sitting beside the machine is not the same thing as the machine accepting it.

So I move closer, line the Anomaly up with the intake, and watch the percentage.

Once I see the meter move, I repeat the same placement.

I only start questioning the progression state if the correct product is clearly accepted and the percentage still refuses to change.
