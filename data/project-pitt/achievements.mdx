<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
Project P.I.T.T. has **23 achievements**.

I do not grind them all from the start.

My cleanup order is:

**normal progression**

→ **upper hidden route**

→ **Secret Ending route**

→ **collectibles**

→ **late factory grind**

→ **Main Ending / IGTAP only after I have a verified route**

The achievements that need dedicated solutions send me to their Owner Pages instead of turning this checklist into a second walkthrough.

I also treat the unlock percentages below as a **launch-window snapshot**, not permanent difficulty ratings.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

<h2 id="all-achievements">
  All 23 Project P.I.T.T. achievements
</h2>

I keep one master checklist and let normal progression remove as many entries as possible before I start grinding.

The percentages are useful for context.

They show which achievements most players reach through normal progression and which sit behind late systems.

I do not use them as a promise that one achievement is objectively easier than another.

<h2 id="normal-progression">
  I let normal progression unlock the obvious achievements
</h2>

I do not stop the run to grind:

**QWACK!**

**KACHING!**

**FIESTA!**

**WEIRD!**

These are product milestones.

If I follow the <Link href="/project-pitt/progression-guide">Progression Guide</Link>, they should happen as I move through the product chain.

`STUFFED!` is similar even though it takes much longer.

It needs **15,000 products fed**.

I do not grind that number with a weak early factory.

Later automation increases the counter much faster, so I let it accumulate in the background while I work on other goals.

My rule for cumulative achievements is:

**if a later machine makes the counter rise faster, I wait for the later machine.**

<h2 id="combo-achievements">
  I treat x100 and x200 as progression, then save x300 for cleanup
</h2>

The combo achievements are:

**MORE! — x100**

**EXTRA! — x200**

**MEGA! — x300**

I do not build three separate combo farms.

A continuous Duck line is useful because it fills gaps between larger products.

Cash Registers and Piñatas add bigger bursts.

Late Anomaly and crate setups add much more volume.

One late-game screenshot I use as a reference is at **combo 196**, already very close to the x200 threshold.

For `MEGA!`, I wait until the entire factory is available.

The jump to **x300** is large enough that I want late production, reliable routing, and multiple streams before I commit time to it.

If the combo repeatedly dies in one gap, I fix that gap.

I do not rebuild every product line at once.

<h2 id="loose-products">
  I save MESS! and LAG! for a run where cleanup is easy
</h2>

`MESS!` needs **500 loose products**.

`LAG!` needs **1,000 loose products**.

These achievements intentionally ask me to create the opposite of a healthy factory.

Normally, a growing loose pile means I should fix throughput.

For these two, I deliberately let the room get messy.

I wait until I already have stronger cleanup options and enough money that temporarily disrupting a good factory does not matter.

I do not chase 1,000 loose products while I am still trying to understand my first Duck line.

Once the achievement is done, I clean the room before I return to automation work.

<h2 id="gambling-crates">
  I collect Gambling Crates on the way and finish the set later
</h2>

`GAMBLING!` requires **all 10 Gambling Crates**.

I collect the obvious ones while I am already moving through the factory and upper areas.

I do not abandon progression every time I see one in the distance.

One run was already at **5 / 10** before I turned the remaining crates into a dedicated completion task.

<h2 id="hidden-route">
  I group the hidden-route achievements into one progression chain
</h2>

Several achievements belong to the same broad hidden route:

**BAD? — destroy a camera**

→ **UP? — place all fuses**

→ **HEAVY? — open the weight route**

→ **OPEN? — open the hidden gate**

→ **INVISIBLE? — resolve the mining minigame**

→ **SECRET? — reveal the keypad**

→ **END? — reach the Secret Ending**

I do not solve those as seven unrelated mysteries.

I use the correct Owner Page when I reach each blocker.

For the stage order and camera / fuse branch, I use the <Link href="/project-pitt/progression-guide">Progression Guide</Link>.

For `HEAVY?` and `OPEN?`, I use the <Link href="/project-pitt/200kg-platform-what-next">200 kg Platform and 0% guide</Link>.

For the route after the gate opens, including mining, UV, keypad access, and the Secret Ending, I use the <Link href="/project-pitt/secret-ending">Secret Ending guide</Link>.

This achievements page only tells me **which badge belongs to which stage**.

<h2 id="endgame-grind">
  I save RICH!, FAST!, MEGA!, and CONSUMERISM! for the fully unlocked factory
</h2>

These are my true factory cleanup goals:

**RICH! — $1 trillion**

**FAST! — 400 products/min**

**MEGA! — combo x300**

**CONSUMERISM! — unlock and buy everything**

I do not rebuild Phase 1 around these numbers.

For `RICH!`, late Anomaly production and Anomaly crates give me much stronger money opportunities than early Ducks.

If money is the goal, I improve the <Link href="/project-pitt/anomaly-guide">Anomaly setup</Link> instead of polishing an old low-value line.

For `FAST!`, I wait until the major sources are unlocked.

The achievement asks for **400 products/min**, but I do not have a verified minimum layout that I would trust as an exact recipe.

So I treat it as a late throughput target rather than inventing a “guaranteed” build.

For `CONSUMERISM!`, I wait until the product and tool trees are actually open.

Buying everything before the later tiers exist is not a useful cleanup strategy.

<h2 id="main-ending-and-igtap">
  I keep END! and LOOP? on the checklist until the routes are verified
</h2>

`END!` says:

**Main ending**

`LOOP?` says:

**Resolve the IGTAP minigame**

The achievement list confirms that both goals exist.

I do not currently have a verified step-by-step route for either one that I am willing to publish as a solution.

So I keep them visible on the checklist instead of inventing directions.

Once either route is fully verified, that is when it deserves its own solution or a dedicated Owner Page.

<h2 id="achievement-not-unlocking">
  If a hidden achievement stays locked, I separate the route from the badge state
</h2>

I have seen a completed Secret Ending while some related hidden achievements still appeared locked afterward.

That means one missing badge does not automatically prove the route itself failed.

My first checks are:

**Did the environmental progression actually happen?**

**Did the ending or gate complete?**

**Did the achievement list refresh after returning to the menu?**

I do not replay the entire hidden route immediately.

For the full “progression worked but the badge stayed locked” diagnosis, I use the <Link href="/project-pitt/troubleshooting">Troubleshooting guide</Link>.

<h2 id="my-100-percent-order">
  My 100% achievement cleanup order
</h2>

This is the order I would use again:

**1. Normal progression**

QWACK!, KACHING!, FIESTA!, WEIRD!, plus whatever combo and product-count milestones happen naturally.

**2. Upper hidden route**

BAD?, UP?, HEAVY?, OPEN?.

**3. Secret route**

INVISIBLE?, SECRET?, END?.

**4. Collectibles**

GAMBLING!.

**5. Factory cleanup**

MESS!, LAG!, STUFFED!, MORE!, EXTRA!, MEGA!, RICH!, FAST!, CONSUMERISM!.

**6. Remaining verified endgame content**

END! and LOOP? once I have a route I can actually stand behind.

That order keeps me from grinding a weak early factory for achievements that become much easier after the whole product chain is unlocked.
