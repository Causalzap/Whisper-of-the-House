<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
My Piñata route has one rule before anything else:

**keep the Piñata intact during transport, then break it where the Candy is already contained.**

My basic route is:

**Piñata source → Cushion transport → controlled airflow → hard break point near the Pit → side walls / roof → Candy falls in**

I do not start with Slick transport because the first problem is not friction.

It is accidental impact.

In one run, Candy was worth about **$125** and an intact Piñata about **$10,000** before later upgrades and combo bonuses changed the numbers.

The automatic Piñata machine cost **$10 million**, so I only buy it after the manual route already works.

Once Piñatas become my main payout, I still keep a cheap Duck line running because its steady feed helps hold combos between Piñata bursts.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

That is my signal to stop trying to rebuild the old Cash Register factory exactly as it was.

Piñatas create a different logistics problem from Ducks or Cash Registers.

The product itself is valuable.

The Candy inside it is also valuable.

That means the goal is not simply “keep the object safe all the way into the Pit.”

I want to control **where the object breaks**.

In one run, I saw Candy at about **$125** each and an intact Piñata around **$10,000** before later upgrades changed those values.

I do not treat those as permanent prices.

I use them to understand why a controlled burst is worth building around.

<h2 id="first-route">
  I keep the transport soft until the exact break point
</h2>

My first mistake was treating Piñatas like another Cash Register.

A Slick route sounds attractive because it moves products easily.

But the first Piñata problem is not friction.

It is impact.

If the product hits a hard floor, exposed edge, or bad corner too early, it can burst before the Candy is anywhere near the Pit.

So I start with Cushion through the travel section.

    I keep the travel path padded until the Piñata is already inside the area where I want it to burst.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

This is one of the few places where I am comfortable using several Cushion surfaces in sequence.

With Ducks or Cash Registers, one soft impact point may be enough.

With Piñatas, one exposed hard contact in the middle of the route can become an accidental break point.

My goal is a continuous safe path from the source to the final chamber.

<h2 id="break-point">
  I create one intentional hard break point near the Pit
</h2>

Once the Piñata can survive the approach, I give it one place where breaking is intentional.

My preferred sequence is:

**padded transport**

→ **hard wall or hard final surface**

→ **Piñata bursts**

→ **Candy stays inside the chamber**

→ **Candy falls toward the Pit**

  <figcaption className="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-400">
    I want one deliberate burst near the Pit instead of several accidental bursts along the transport lane.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

That gives the route a clean division:

**soft before the break**

**hard at the break**

**contained after the break**

If the Piñata breaks too early, I fix the approach.

If it reaches the final area intact every time and the Candy never becomes part of the flow, I adjust the intentional impact point.

The important decision is not whether Piñatas should ever break.

It is whether they break **where I planned for the Candy to go**.

<h2 id="candy-containment">
  The break chamber needs more containment than the travel lane
</h2>

A route can contain the large Piñata perfectly and still fail after the burst.

Candy is smaller and much easier to throw over a wall.

So I treat the final chamber as a separate structure.

I start with side walls around the impact point.

If Candy is still escaping upward, I add a roof.

I do not automatically roof the whole Piñata lane.

The expensive containment belongs where one large product suddenly becomes many small moving pieces.

If Candy only escapes from the final two Panels, those are the Panels I enclose.

<h2 id="airflow">
  I use Fans to deliver the Piñata, not to create random break points
</h2>

I keep Piñata airflow simple.

The Fan only needs to move the product through the padded route and into the intentional break point.

More force is not automatically better.

If the Piñata ricochets around the chamber or bursts one or two Panels early, I reduce or move the final push before I add more walls.

The Piñata-specific rule is:

**transport softly → break hard once**

For general overshoot, undershoot, jam, and airflow diagnosis, I use the <Link href="/project-pitt/automation-layouts">Automation Layout Helper</Link>.

<h2 id="manual-first">
  I prove the manual route before I buy automation
</h2>

The manual Piñata source is enough to answer every important design question.

Before I spend on automation, I test:

- Does the Piñata survive the padded approach?
- Does it reach the intended hard break point?
- Does the Candy stay inside the chamber?
- Does the Candy move toward the Pit?
- Does the chamber clear before the next Piñata arrives?

If any answer is no, faster production does not help.

It only creates more failed bursts.

That is why I test several manual Piñatas before I buy the expensive source.

My buy condition is:

**the Piñata leaves the source cleanly**

**the padded route does not create early breaks**

**the intentional break point works consistently**

**most Candy stays inside the chamber**

**the Pit side clears without a growing pile**

When those are true, the automatic machine upgrades throughput.

When they are false, I keep the money.

The automatic machine does not fix the route.

It only feeds the route faster.

Two sources create a new risk.

If two Piñatas collide before the planned break point, I have created an accidental break point by adding throughput.

So I do not force both feeds through one tight entrance.

I give them enough room to merge without hitting each other.

If the second source destabilizes the route, I turn it back into an optional bonus instead of rebuilding the entire lane around it.

<h2 id="production-speed">
  I upgrade Piñata production when the route is waiting on the next Piñata
</h2>

I do not upgrade Piñata speed simply because I have the money.

The signal I want is:

**the route is clear**

**the break chamber is ready**

**Candy has already cleared**

**the combo is waiting because the next Piñata has not arrived**

That is when production has become the bottleneck.

In one later run, once the route itself was stable, the gaps between Piñatas became the obvious problem.

After production improved, Piñatas could arrive more consistently and combo chains climbed much more aggressively.

I saw chains around **15–20** while the payout moved into the **10+ million** range during that run.

I do not treat that payout as a fixed Piñata value because upgrades and combo bonuses change it heavily.

I use it as evidence for the buying order:

**route first**

→ **containment second**

→ **production speed after both are already working**

<h2 id="early-breaks">
  If Piñatas break early, I remove the hard contact first
</h2>

When a Piñata bursts halfway down the route, I inspect the exact contact point.

I look for:

**normal hard floor**

**an exposed edge**

**a sharp transition**

**too much final Fan force**

I replace the hard contact with Cushion first.

I do not automatically pad the entire room.

The goal is one continuous safe path from the source to the planned break point.

If that path is fully padded and the Piñata still breaks early, then I look at force.

<h2 id="candy-escapes">
  If Candy escapes, I fix the break chamber instead of rebuilding the approach
</h2>

Candy escape is the failure that tells me the Piñata transport may already be working.

If the Piñata arrives intact and bursts in the correct place, I leave the approach alone.

I change the chamber.

My order is:

**confirm where the Piñata bursts**

→ **move the burst closer to the Pit if needed**

→ **add side containment**

→ **add a roof if Candy still escapes upward**

→ **only then retune the final force**

I do not rebuild the padded travel lane to solve a failure that happens after the break.

<h2 id="when-to-stop">
  I stop optimizing when the burst happens where I already want it
</h2>

I do not need every Piñata to behave identically.

I need the route to be reliable enough that I am not chasing Candy around the warehouse.

I consider the Piñata line finished when:

**Piñatas usually survive the transport section**

**the main break point is near the Pit**

**Candy mostly stays inside the chamber**

**the source does not build a growing pile**

**the route can accept the current production rate**

**the Duck support line can help keep combos alive**

At that point I stop adding Panels.

I stop trying to make every burst look perfect.

I save the next investment for the next product system.

When Anomalies become the next objective, I leave the Piñata line earning in the background and move to the <Link href="/project-pitt/anomaly-guide">Anomaly Guide</Link>.
