<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
When Project P.I.T.T. looks broken, I first decide whether I am seeing a **layout problem**, a **progression requirement**, or an actual **state / unlock problem**.

My fastest checks are:

**Factory breaks exactly when Phase 3 starts** → I treat that as progression, not save corruption.

**The hidden device stays at 0%** → I stop testing normal products and use the <Link href="/project-pitt/200kg-platform-what-next">200 kg / 0% solution</Link>.

**The Anomaly workbench does nothing** → I check whether all three required products are present before I blame the machine.

**A hidden achievement stays locked after I completed the action** → I check the achievement state before replaying the whole route.

**The game only hitches while recording or streaming** → I test capture load before I tear down a working factory.

**Ducks or Cash Registers jam, overshoot, lose momentum, or escape** → that is an automation-layout problem, so I use the <Link href="/project-pitt/automation-layouts">Automation Layout Helper</Link>.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

<h2 id="bug-or-progression">
  I first decide whether the game is broken or waiting for a different condition
</h2>

The most expensive troubleshooting mistake is restarting a long run because something **looks** wrong.

Project P.I.T.T. has several moments where the correct progression state looks like a failure:

- part of the factory can be damaged when Phase 3 begins;
- the hidden 0% device can ignore normal products completely;
- the Anomaly workbench can appear inactive when one ingredient is missing;
- hidden achievements can fail to update even after the route itself has progressed.

So I do not start with:

**“Is my save broken?”**

I start with:

**“What exact action was supposed to change the game, and did the environment change even if the UI did not?”**

That distinction saves me from replaying hours of factory progression for something that was either expected or tied to a different trigger.

<h2 id="automation-or-system">
  I send normal physics problems to the Layout Helper
</h2>

If the problem is:

**products piling up**

**products flying past the Pit**

**products stopping halfway down a lane**

**Panels or Fans moving**

**Cash Registers catching a corner**

**Ducks escaping a chute**

I do not troubleshoot those here.

Those are automation-layout problems.

I use the <Link href="/project-pitt/automation-layouts">Automation Layout Helper</Link>, because that page owns airflow, geometry, containment, friction, catch zones, Sticky Tools, and the Duck / Cash Register differences.

This page starts where ordinary layout tuning stops:

**the factory or progression state looks wrong even though the route itself may be fine.**

<h2 id="phase-three-destruction">
  If the factory breaks when Phase 3 starts, I do not restart the save
</h2>

One of the easiest moments to misread is the transition into Phase 3.

I had automatic Duck and Cash Register systems running when the phase change triggered, and the facility damage tore through the existing build.

That looks awful if I just spent a long time making the factory neat.

It is still not the signal I use for save corruption.

My decision is:

**damage happens at the phase transition**

→ **new progression becomes available**

→ **I continue**

I only become suspicious if the factory disappears or becomes unusable **outside** the expected phase transition and there is no new progression state to explain it.

For what comes next after the transition, I return to the <Link href="/project-pitt/progression-guide">Progression Guide</Link>.

<h2 id="zero-percent">
  If the hidden device stays at 0%, I stop treating it like a broken machine
</h2>

The hidden 0% device is one of the clearest examples of a progression blocker that looks like a malfunction.

Normal products can go in and do nothing useful.

A recycled object can go in and still leave the display at 0%.

That does not mean I need a better conveyor.

It does not mean I need a Cannon.

It does not mean the device is bugged.

It means I am testing the wrong requirement.

So once I recognize the 0% device, I stop experimenting with normal factory output and use the <Link href="/project-pitt/200kg-platform-what-next">200 kg Platform / 0% guide</Link>.

That page owns the actual solution.

The troubleshooting rule here is only:

**0% does not move with ordinary products → do not keep escalating automation.**

<h2 id="anomaly-workbench">
  If the Anomaly workbench does nothing, I verify the full recipe first
</h2>

The Anomaly workbench can look inactive even when the machine itself is fine.

Before I change Fans, move the output lane, or assume the workbench has stopped responding, I check whether all three earlier products are actually available:

**Duck**

**Cash Register**

**Piñata**

If one is missing, the machine is not failing.

The supply chain is incomplete.

That gives me a clean troubleshooting order:

**check Duck**

→ **check Cash Register**

→ **check Piñata**

→ **confirm the workbench is receiving all three**

→ only then investigate the output

I keep the full recipe, Intern Substitute behavior, input staging, and late-game Anomaly setup in the <Link href="/project-pitt/anomaly-guide">Anomaly Guide</Link>.

<h2 id="achievement-not-unlocking">
  If a hidden achievement stays locked, I do not replay the entire route immediately
</h2>

I have seen the Secret Ending complete while earlier hidden achievements still appeared locked afterward.

The missing ones included progression tied to the hidden weight route, gate, and keypad sequence.

That is important because it means:

**ending completed** does not always equal **every related achievement updated correctly**.

My first response is not a new save.

I check:

**Did the ending or environmental change actually complete?**

**Did the achievement list update after returning to the menu?**

**Is only one earlier badge still missing while later progression clearly worked?**

If the route itself completed, I treat the missing badge as an unlock-state problem before I treat it as a failed route.

For the full checklist and which achievement belongs to which trigger, I use the <Link href="/project-pitt/achievements">Achievements Guide</Link>.

<h2 id="secret-ending-complete-but-achievements-locked">
  A completed Secret Ending is stronger evidence than one stale badge
</h2>

The hidden route gives me several environmental confirmations that are more useful than one achievement notification.

A gate opens.

A minigame resolves.

A new tool is dispensed.

The elevator override is accepted.

The Secret Ending completes.

If those later states all happened, I do not assume the whole route failed because one earlier achievement did not register.

I separate two questions:

**Did progression work?**

and:

**Did the achievement system record every trigger?**

Those are not always the same problem.

This prevents me from replaying the clone room, mining section, UV search, and elevator route just to chase one stale unlock.

<h2 id="when-i-reload">
  I reload state before I restart progression
</h2>

When the environment and the UI disagree, I use the least destructive check first.

My order is:

**finish the current interaction**

→ **return to a normal game state**

→ **open the achievement or progression screen again**

→ **return to the menu if needed**

→ **check whether the state refreshes**

I do not immediately rebuild the factory.

I do not immediately replay the hidden route.

I do not immediately create a new save.

A menu/state refresh costs almost nothing compared with repeating a late-game progression chain.

<h2 id="recording-hitching">
  If the game only hitches while recording or streaming, I test capture load separately
</h2>

I have seen light hitching while recording, and another session had OBS struggle badly enough that lowering graphics and disabling Dynamic FOV appeared to help.

That is useful as a troubleshooting clue.

It is **not** enough for me to claim a universal Project P.I.T.T. FPS fix.

If the game feels normal when I am not recording, my first test is:

**lower graphics one step**

→ **disable Dynamic FOV**

→ **close other heavy applications**

→ **test again with the same factory**

I do not tear down automation to solve a problem that may be coming from the capture workload.

I also do not use this as evidence for Steam Deck settings, controller performance, or a general low-FPS guide.

<h2 id="when-i-suspect-a-real-problem">
  I only suspect a real save or state problem after expected progression checks fail
</h2>

I become more suspicious when all of these are true:

**I performed the exact known trigger**

**the expected environmental change did not happen**

**the correct product or item was used**

**the relevant progression page confirms I am at the right step**

**a menu or state refresh does not change anything**

At that point, I stop changing the factory.

A layout rebuild cannot fix a progression flag.

I also avoid turning an uncertain state into a made-up solution.

If I cannot verify a recovery step, I would rather keep the diagnosis narrow than tell myself to delete or replay a save without evidence.

<h2 id="my-troubleshooting-order">
  The troubleshooting order I use before I redo anything expensive
</h2>

When Project P.I.T.T. appears stuck, I use this order:

**1. Identify the exact system.**

Is this automation, progression, an achievement, or performance?

**2. If products are physically moving badly, leave this page.**

Use the <Link href="/project-pitt/automation-layouts">Automation Layout Helper</Link>.

**3. Check whether the “failure” is expected progression.**

Phase 3 damage is the obvious example.

**4. Check the actual requirement.**

The 0% device and Anomaly workbench both punish guessing.

**5. Trust environmental progression before one stale UI badge.**

If the route clearly advanced, I do not replay everything because one achievement stayed locked.

**6. Refresh state before restarting content.**

Menu first. Replay later.

**7. Separate capture hitching from factory performance.**

If the issue only exists while recording, I test the recording load.

**8. Restart or replay only after the cheap checks fail.**

That is the point of this page.

I use it to avoid destroying a working run while trying to fix something that was never an automation problem in the first place.
