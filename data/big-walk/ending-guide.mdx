*Major ending spoilers follow.*

Big Walk has two completion states that are easy to confuse. **Big Goodbye** is the normal ending reached after the main tower progression and the final black sphere. **Big Game** is the 100% ending, unlocked only after returning to the island, completing every remaining puzzle, collecting the final key, and entering the hidden room near the beginning.

The normal ending closes the main journey. The true ending explains what that journey was really about: House House built the island, but the people walking through it created the experience for one another.

<h2 id="how-to-get-the-big-goodbye-ending">How to get the Big Goodbye ending</h2>

<AdUnit placement="article_after_first_h2" />

I start the ending route only after the main tower progression is finished. The earlier routes remain in the [Red Tower and Map Room guide](/big-walk/how-to-unlock-map), [Green Tower and Chairlift guide](/big-walk/green-tower-walkthrough), [Yellow Tower and Tunnel guide](/big-walk/yellow-tower-tunnel), and [Blue Tower and Train guide](/big-walk/blue-tower-train).

Before attempting Big Goodbye, I make sure the group has:

1. completed the Red, Green, Yellow, and Blue Tower progression;
2. completed the Black Tower;
3. used the Black Tower reward to open the route toward the large black sphere;
4. finished the final cooperative puzzles inside the sphere.

The **Black Tower** and the **large black sphere** are separate locations. The Black Tower is an earlier late-game progression structure. The sphere is the enclosed final main-route area that leads directly toward Big Goodbye.

### Press the final button inside the black sphere

Near the end of the sphere is a dark red room containing a suspended orb-like mechanism and a nearby button. This is the **final mechanism inside the black sphere**, not another tower.

<h2 id="how-to-get-the-big-game-true-ending">How to get the Big Game true ending</h2>

Big Game requires complete puzzle cleanup after the first ending.

I use this order:

1. finish Big Goodbye and return to the same save;
2. complete all seven purple post-game puzzles;
3. check the map for older normal puzzles that remain unfinished;
4. deliver every remaining completion piece to its matching room;
5. return to the final-key position near the beginning;
6. collect the key once it rises;
7. carry it back toward the starting area;
8. use it on the door that remained locked during the main game;
9. enter the hidden room and stay for the complete reading;
10. walk through the white opening that appears behind the group.

I do not search for the hidden room first. The key is the useful completion test. When it has not appeared, I return to puzzle cleanup instead of pressing doors around the beginning area.

### Where the final key appears

The final key does not appear just because the seven purple puzzles are finished. It is tied to complete puzzle progression across the entire map.

<h2 id="big-goodbye-vs-big-game">Big Goodbye vs. Big Game</h2>

The difference is progression depth, not a permanent story choice.

### Big Goodbye requires

- the main tower progression;
- the Black Tower step;
- the final black sphere puzzles;
- the hanging-mechanism button;
- the route across the water;
- the Wave Goodbye entrance.

This is the normal campaign ending.

### Big Game requires

- Big Goodbye already completed;
- all seven purple post-game puzzles;
- every skipped normal puzzle;
- every required completion delivery;
- the final key;
- the hidden 100% room;
- the final reading and white-exit sequence.

This is the complete ending.

I stop at Big Goodbye when the group only wants to finish the main journey. I continue to Big Game when the goal is every puzzle, every completion check, and the final message.

<h2 id="big-walk-true-ending-explained">Big Walk true ending explained</h2>

The true ending is not built around a hidden villain, a final boss, or a large story twist. Its focus is the group.

Throughout Big Walk, I repeatedly have to:

- find players after the group separates;
- communicate without perfect information;
- pass objects between different roles;
- trust another player to finish a step I cannot see;
- turn failed attempts into a shared solution.

The final reading makes that design goal explicit. House House created the island and its challenges, but the players created the experience through the way they communicated, struggled, joked, waited, and solved problems together.

That is why the ending asks for a memory instead of presenting another puzzle. The full-completion reward is a pause to think about the people who made the walk memorable.

The bright exit reads as a departure from the completed journey, but the final screen does not frame that departure as permanent. It welcomes the group back to walk and talk again even after every formal objective is done.

I therefore read the two endings this way:

- **Big Goodbye** ends the walk through the game.
- **Big Game** asks the group to remember who walked it with them.

This is an interpretation of the ending presentation, not a separate lore reveal stated by the game.

<h2 id="why-the-final-key-is-not-appearing">Why the final key is not appearing</h2>

When the final key does not rise, I assume the completion check is still missing something.

### One or more purple puzzles are unfinished

Big Goodbye adds seven purple puzzles. I recount the complete set instead of stopping after the most visible ones.

### A normal puzzle was skipped before the first ending

The post-game purple set does not erase earlier unfinished markers. I return to the Map Room and check the complete island again.

### A puzzle was started but not fully completed

I confirm that the challenge produced its actual completion result. Reaching a room or partially operating its mechanism is not the same as clearing it.

### A required completion object was left behind

When a solved challenge produces a physical object, I check the challenge area, temporary cache, Backpack, and completion room. I do not assume the object moved automatically.

### The key position is being checked too early

Big Goodbye alone does not produce the final key. I finish the cleanup route before repeatedly returning to the starting area.

My final-key checklist is:

1. Big Goodbye completed;
2. all seven purple puzzles completed;
3. no normal markers left unresolved;
4. every required completion object delivered;
5. final-key position checked again.

<h2 id="does-big-walk-have-multiple-endings">Does Big Walk have multiple endings?</h2>

Yes. Big Walk has a main completion ending and a full-completion ending.

### Do I need 100% completion for Big Goodbye?

No. Big Goodbye finishes the main route. Full puzzle completion is required only for Big Game.

### Can I continue after Big Goodbye?

Yes. I return to the same world, where the sphere remains open and the purple post-game puzzles become part of the 100% route.

### Is Big Game a separate branching choice?

No. It follows Big Goodbye on the same save. I do not choose one ending at the expense of the other.

### What is the building across the water called?

The available in-game material does not give it a clear proper name. I call it the **ending building** or **building across the water** rather than inventing an official title.

### Can I keep playing after Big Game?

Yes. The completion screen includes **Continue** and welcomes the group back even after everything has been seen.

<h2 id="is-the-black-tower-the-same-as-the-black-sphere">Is the Black Tower the same as the black sphere?</h2>

No. I keep four late-game landmarks separate:

- **Black Tower:** a late main-progression tower completed before the final sphere route.
- **Large black sphere:** the enclosed final main-route puzzle area.
- **Final sphere mechanism:** the hanging-orb room and button that open the sphere.
- **Ending building:** the structure across the water where I use Wave Goodbye.

The common route mistake is saying that pressing the hanging-mechanism button opens the Black Tower. At that point, the structure opening is the **black sphere**.

This distinction makes the final instructions easier to follow. A player who has only reached the Black Tower is not yet standing at the Wave Goodbye entrance.

<h2 id="final-takeaway">Finish the main route first, then decide whether to clear everything</h2>

I use one simple stopping rule:

- stop after **Big Goodbye** when the goal is to finish the campaign;
- continue after the seven purple puzzles appear when the goal is **Big Game**.

For the first ending, I finish the sphere, press the final mechanism button, cross the water route, use Wave Goodbye, and enter the building.

For the complete ending, I return to the Map Room, clear every purple and normal puzzle, deliver the remaining completion pieces, collect the final key, enter the hidden room, and finish the reading and completion sequence.

The first ending reaches the end of the walk. The second explains why the walk mattered.
