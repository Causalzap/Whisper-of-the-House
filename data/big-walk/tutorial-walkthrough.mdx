The opening route ends when I return **four Heads** to the beach receiver, shape
the blank key through **four cutters**, and use the finished key at the red
drawbridge.

This walkthrough follows the **two-player world version**. The four-Head
progression remains the same in larger groups, but individual rooms can add
buttons, roles, or simultaneous interactions based on the player-count version
chosen by the Host.

<h2 id="find-head-receiver">
  Find the four-slot receiver before following the red stairs
</h2>

From the starting rooms, I follow the lit route downhill toward the water. The
first progression landmark is a beach structure with **four yellow Head slots**.
The locked red stairs nearby are not the first objective; their mechanism needs
the finished key earned at the end of this loop.

I place the loose starter Head into the receiver immediately. This leaves three
empty slots and establishes the opening rule:

- each completed challenge releases one Head;
- every Head must be physically returned to this receiver;
- and the blank key appears only when all four slots are filled.

I deposit a Head whenever the beach is already on my route. A solved challenge
does not count if its reward is still sitting beside the building where it
appeared.

<h2 id="glass-head">
  Keep one player at the remote control for the glass-enclosure Head
</h2>

From the beach receiver, I turn away from the red stairs and follow the coastal
route past the paint area and flare equipment. The next clear landmark is a
raised structure with a Head sealed behind glass.

The control that opens the enclosure is not beside the Head. It sits back along
the route, which means both players should not walk to the glass together.

My two-player solution is:

1. leave one player at the remote control;
2. send the second player to the glass enclosure;
3. activate and hold the mechanism;
4. keep it active until the collector confirms the Head is in hand;
5. reunite at the beach receiver.

The common failure is releasing the control when the collector first reaches the
glass. I keep the mechanism active until the reward has been removed from the
enclosure.

At night, the collector carries the light because that player has the longer
route. The control player remains beside the mechanism rather than trying to
follow from a distance.

<h2 id="symbol-room">
  Name the blue symbol-room shapes before starting the sequence
</h2>

After returning the glass-enclosure Head, I look uphill for the **blue,
funnel-shaped building**. Its silhouette is easier to follow than the narrow
rocky paths around it.

Inside, one player enters the enclosed side and starts the sequence. The other
player remains with the removable symbol pieces. The door blocks direct visual
comparison, so the solution depends on clear shape names.

The order can change after the room resets. I do not copy an earlier sequence or
use only left-and-right positions. Before starting, I agree on simple names such
as:

- lightning bolt;
- slash;
- three circles;
- split percentage sign;
- large swirl.

The enclosed player calls one shape at a time. The outside player places the
matching piece before asking for the next call. When two symbols look similar, I
add one distinguishing feature rather than a position—for example, “large
swirl” instead of “the one on the right.”

Opening or resetting the room can produce a new order. When the callouts become
confused, I finish or reset the current attempt, agree on new names, and start a
fresh sequence.

<h2 id="green-switches">
  Put one player on each green-building switch
</h2>

From the blue symbol room, I continue toward the nearby green structure instead
of returning to the starting rooms. In the two-player version, the controls sit
on separate sides of the building.

I place one player at each switch and wait until both players confirm that the
interaction prompt is available. Then I use one countdown:

`Three — two — one — press.`

Both switches must activate together. If one player presses early, I wait for
both controls to reset before trying again. Repeatedly pressing one switch while
the other player runs into position is less reliable than a clean synchronized
attempt.

The completed interaction releases the fourth Head. I collect it and return
directly to the beach receiver.

<h2 id="return-four-heads">
  Return all four Heads before touching the blank key
</h2>

After collecting the green-building Head, I stop exploring the opening area and
return to the receiver.

I check the four indicators rather than counting the challenges I remember
finishing. A reward left at the glass enclosure, symbol room, or green building
does not activate its slot.

The key that appears after all four Heads are inserted already has a handle and
fits inside the machines, but it is still a **blank**. The receiver supplies the
key material; the cutter route gives it the shape required by the drawbridge.

For the return:

- one player carries the newest Head;
- the second player carries the light when needed;
- both confirm all four receiver indicators;
- and one player keeps the blank key until the bridge opens.

<h2 id="cut-blank-key">
  Follow all four cutter arrows before returning to the bridge
</h2>

I carry the blank key from the beach receiver to the first cutter on the rocky
route above the drawbridge. Each box removes another section of the blank and
points toward the next station.

The four cutters function as one continuous route:

1. insert the key into the cutter;
2. wait for the shaping action to finish;
3. remove the same key;
4. follow the direction arrow;
5. repeat until the final cutter no longer sends the group onward.

I do not return to the drawbridge after the first or second cut. A partly shaped
key can still fit into the mechanism, but the bridge will not move.

At night, I separate the jobs: one player carries the key and the other leads
with the light. The key obstructs part of the carrier's view, so that player
should not also be responsible for finding every climb and arrow.

<h2 id="open-drawbridge">
  Insert the completed key and wait for the drawbridge
</h2>

After the fourth cutter, I return to the red drawbridge mechanism and insert the
finished key.

The mechanism can look inactive for a moment. I leave the key in place and wait
for the bridge animation instead of immediately removing and reinserting it.
Once the extension completes, the bridge becomes usable from both sides.

Crossing it triggers the **Big Walk** achievement and opens the route to the main
island.

<h2 id="stuck">
  Tutorial and drawbridge fixes
</h2>

### I returned a Head, but the key did not appear

Check the four receiver indicators. The blank key appears only when all four
Heads have been inserted. A reward still sitting beside a challenge does not
count.

### The glass enclosure closes before the Head is collected

Keep one player at the remote control and hold the mechanism until the collector
confirms the Head is in hand.

### The symbol sequence changed

A reset can produce another order. Stop using the earlier sequence, agree on
short shape names, and call the new symbols one at a time.

### Both green switches activate, but the Head does not appear

Wait for both switches to reset. Put one player at each control, confirm both
interaction prompts, and press on one countdown.

### The key fits the drawbridge, but nothing happens

The key is still partly shaped. Return to the latest cutter, follow its arrow,
and continue until all four cutter boxes have been used.

### The key is complete, but the bridge still looks inactive

Leave the key inserted for a moment. The drawbridge animation does not always
begin immediately.

<h2 id="faq">
  Big Walk tutorial FAQ
</h2>

### Why does my tutorial have more buttons or roles?

The Host selected a three-player or four-plus world version. The receiver still
needs four Heads, but the individual challenges can assign more active roles.

### Can I carry several tutorial Heads before returning?

Yes, but there is no early Backpack in this area. I deposit a Head whenever the
beach is already on the route instead of carrying every loose reward until the
end.

### Can I skip a tutorial challenge?

Challenge skipping can be used when an interaction is inaccessible or the group
cannot complete it. It should not be used to compensate for choosing a
player-count version that does not match the group.
