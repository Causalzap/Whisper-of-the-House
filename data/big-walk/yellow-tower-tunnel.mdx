The Yellow Tower route is where I turn the Green Chairlift into a practical
cross-island route, collect five Heads around the Yellow coastline, shape the
Yellow key through five cutters, and open the underground Tunnel.

The real time loss is not the receiver itself. It is taking the wrong Chairlift route
direction, carrying too much gear down to the beach, pressing random buttons in
the separated-room puzzles, searching above a structure when the missing piece is
underneath it, and dropping the key while following cutter arrows across steep
ground.

At the Chairlift station, I look toward the tallest mountain and take the

I do not carry the portable map, Compass, Laser Pointer, two radios, and multiple

<h2 id="set-up-yellow-tower">
  Use Yellow Tower as the deposit point before going to the beach
</h2>

The Yellow receiver needs five Heads. I visit it first, fire one Flare when the
party is still scattered, and choose the tower as the only permanent deposit
point.

From Yellow Tower, the useful collection direction is down toward the beach.
After leaving the tower, I move left past the red arches and continue downhill.
Several unfinished challenges sit along that side of the region.

The group can easily split here because the tower, Chairlift, beach, and ridge remain
visible from different elevations. I use one rule:

> Anyone who loses the party returns to Yellow Tower instead of guessing which
> beach structure the group entered.

A Flare marks the tower for new arrivals, but I stop firing once a communication
puzzle begins. Extra explosions and radio chatter make audio and movement calls
harder to hear.

<h2 id="return-yellow-heads">
  Return the first two Heads before collecting the last three
</h2>

I do not wait until all five rewards are loose on the coastline.

My first Yellow loop ends after two Heads. I bring both back to the receiver,
remove unneeded equipment from the carriers, and then start the longer beach
loop.

This creates a useful checkpoint:

- two Heads are protected at the receiver;
- the group knows the tower return path;
- the next successful puzzle brings the receiver to three;
- and only two final rewards remain when the group commits to the far side.

After three Heads are inserted, I stop revisiting already completed structures. I
use the Map Room flags or visible puzzle lights to choose two unfinished
challenges closer to the beach.

A challenge completion is not a receiver deposit. Before leaving any structure,
I find the physical Head, name its carrier, and confirm that the carrier has not
replaced it with Binoculars, a radio, or a spare light.

<h2 id="choose-yellow-puzzles">
  Choose five practical Heads instead of clearing every Yellow puzzle
</h2>

The Yellow receiver does not require one fixed puzzle list. I use nearby
challenges because they shorten the return, but another completed Head can replace
a difficult Yellow-area puzzle.

My preferred set includes:

- the separated button-wall challenge;
- the hidden-piece collection area;
- the seven-position hand-signal puzzle;
- the five-sound matching house;
- and one additional nearby reward that the current group can finish quickly.

I leave a challenge when the required communication tool is missing, the player
count is wrong for the current room, or the group has already spent several
minutes guessing without agreeing on a shared coordinate system.

<h2 id="yellow-button-wall">
  Number the separated button walls before anyone presses
</h2>

This puzzle places the two teams in separate rooms. One side sees a highlighted
button. The other side has to press the matching physical position.

The real failure condition is uncontrolled input. When someone presses a random
button, the highlighted target can change, making a correct direction sound
wrong by the time the other team reaches it.

Before starting, I assign every wall a name:

- entrance wall;
- left wall;
- back wall;
- right wall.

Within each wall, I number positions from left to right and top to bottom. When a
shape is easier to recognize, I combine both systems:

- bottom-left beside the switch;
- top of the three-button L;
- middle button in the right column;
- second position from the far-left floor row.

My role split is:

- one caller stays with the highlighted target;
- one presser stays in the answer room;
- one Megaphone or radio carries the instruction;
- and every other player keeps their hands off the wall.

After the caller gives one location, the presser repeats it before moving. The
caller waits until the button is accepted before describing the next target.

<h2 id="yellow-hidden-piece">
  Search underneath the structures before building a player stack
</h2>

The hidden-piece area encourages the wrong search direction.

When one item appears missing, the tall structure makes the roof look like the
obvious answer. A group can spend a long time stacking players, climbing, and
checking trees while the final piece remains underneath a low platform or nearby
support.

I use this search order:

1. walk the entire ground boundary;
2. crouch and check underneath platforms;
3. inspect the back of ramps and low supports;
4. look behind movable objects;
5. only then build a player stack for the roof.

I also stop carrying the entire equipment pile during this search. One light
stays with the ground checker while the remaining players spread around the
perimeter.

When I find the last piece, I show its exact location to the group before carrying
the Head away. That prevents the same false roof search when another player
returns later.

<h2 id="yellow-seven-light-signal">
  Use arm raises for the seven-position signal puzzle
</h2>

This challenge separates the player who can see the required position from the
players standing near seven buttons or seven lights.

The first communication method I avoid is crouch-counting. Fast crouches are
difficult to distinguish, and the receiving side can lose track of whether a
movement was part of the number or the end signal.

My cleaner protocol is:

- buttons are numbered **1 through 7 from left to right**;
- every arm raise counts as one;
- the caller repeats the number twice;
- the caller then crouches and stays down to mark the end;
- the receiving side repeats the count with arm raises;
- and only one player presses the button.

For button 5, for example, I raise both arms five clear times, pause, repeat five,
then remain crouched. I do not mix left-arm and right-arm meanings into the same
sequence.

The final confirmation matters more than speed. A wrong input creates another
round, while repeating a five-count takes only a few seconds.

<h2 id="yellow-sound-puzzle">
  Match the five sounds one at a time
</h2>

The sound house uses five audio prompts. One player controls or hears the target
side, while the other players test sound objects and place the matching choice in
the hallway or answer position.

I keep one caller in the target room for the entire sequence. The caller plays
only one prompt and describes three properties:

- instrument or voice;
- whether the pitch rises, falls, or stays level;
- and whether the sound is short, repeated, or sustained.

In my run, the five prompts were easiest to remember as:

1. a soft cradle-like or xylophone sound;
2. a xylophone phrase rising up the scale;
3. a ringtone-like xylophone phrase;
4. a low male humming or harmonizing voice;
5. a computer or robot shutting down.

I do not treat those descriptions as object names. The receiving side still plays
the candidates and compares them. The first two are especially easy to confuse
because both use a xylophone-like tone.

After a match is placed, the caller waits for confirmation before playing the next
prompt. Playing several sounds back-to-back makes the team argue about which
description belongs to which slot.

<h2 id="finish-yellow-heads">
  Stop exploring when the fifth Yellow Head is ready
</h2>

Once the group has five physical rewards, I end the puzzle loop.

I recount the submitted and carried Heads:

- Heads already inside the receiver;
- Heads currently held by players;
- any Head left beside the most recent challenge;
- and any reward placed near the beach or Chairlift during a regroup.

I do not start another puzzle merely because it is visible on the route back. The
blank Yellow key becomes the priority, and every unnecessary detour adds another
chance to lose a Head or separate the key carrier from the light.

When all five slots are filled, the receiver releases the blank Yellow key.

<h2 id="yellow-key-cutters">
  Keep one Yellow key carrier through all five cutters
</h2>

The Yellow key must pass through **five cutter stations**.

Each completed cutter displays an arrow toward the next station. I assign the
key to one permanent carrier and the light to a second player. The rest of the
group follows behind rather than running ahead with every useful tool.

My cutter rules are:

- wait until the cutting action fully finishes;
- read the arrow before removing the key;
- keep the light aimed at the ground and nearby slopes;
- do not kick or throw the key;
- and stop after any fall to confirm who still has it.

The route crosses uneven ground. A player can survive the drop while the key
continues sliding below the path. When the carrier falls, I recover the key before
following the next arrow.

<h2 id="open-yellow-tunnel">
  Insert the finished key and enter the Yellow Tunnel
</h2>

After the fifth cutter, I follow the final direction to the Yellow lock. The
finished key opens the large door into the underground route.

I step fully through the doorway before checking the achievement list. The
progression point is entering the Tunnel, not merely completing the fifth cutter
or standing beside the closed door.

The Tunnel is a shortcut under the island. It removes the repeated upper-land
walk between the Yellow side and later regions.

<h2 id="yellow-stuck">
  Fix common Yellow Tower and Tunnel problems
</h2>

### The Tram took me away from Yellow

Return to the Green Chairlift station. Face toward the tallest mountain and use
the left-side direction. I confirm the destination before the seat leaves because
the ride is long.

### The highlighted button keeps changing

Someone on the answer side is pressing extra buttons. Stop all input, let the
caller identify the new target, and allow only one designated presser to touch
the wall.

### We cannot find the final hidden piece

Search below the platforms and low supports. I check the ground perimeter before
building a player stack or climbing the roof.

### The seven-position puzzle keeps receiving the wrong number

Use arm raises as the count and a held crouch as the end marker. Do not use rapid
crouches for both counting and confirmation.

### The two xylophone sounds seem identical

Listen for direction. One prompt rises through the scale; the other behaves more
like a short ringtone phrase.

### The Yellow key disappeared after a fall

Stop the route and search the slope directly below the carrier before continuing
to the next cutter. The key can slide farther than the player.

### The Tunnel is open, but the Blue route is still blocked

The Yellow Tunnel is transportation. It does not fill the Blue receiver or shape
the Blue key. Exit toward Blue, find the beacon, and begin the five-Head route.

<h2 id="yellow-faq">
  Yellow Tower and Tunnel FAQ
</h2>

### Do I have to complete Yellow before Blue?

No fixed colored-tower order is required after the early progression opens up.
Yellow first is useful because the Tunnel shortens later travel toward Blue.

### Should I carry the portable map through every Yellow puzzle?

I leave it at Yellow Tower once the beach route is understood. A light and
communication tool are usually more useful inside the separated rooms.

### What should I do when the Host reconnects after a puzzle completion?

I check the Map Room flag and return to the reward point before replaying the
whole challenge. A completed challenge can remain complete even when the Head was
not deposited before the disconnect.

### Can I solve the sound puzzle by copying fixed names?

The descriptions are memory aids, not guaranteed object names. I compare pitch,
rhythm, and duration in the current world instead of selecting solely from a
written label.

### When should I return to Yellow Tower?

I make the first return with two Heads, then finish the remaining three. I also
return immediately when the group loses its light, long-range communication, or a
clear route back from the beach.
