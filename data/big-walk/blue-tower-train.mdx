The Blue Tower route is the point where I stop treating the rail line as scenery
and turn it into a working island shortcut. I locate the five-slot Blue receiver,
collect five practical Head rewards around the Blue region, protect the blank
key through all five cutters, use the finished key at the Train system, and board
a moving carriage to unlock **Big Ride**.

This walkthrough focuses on route order, puzzle selection, Head returns, the
easy-to-miss cutter near the rocks, Train activation, and what the rail loop does
after it starts. The exact puzzle arrangement can change with the Host's selected
player-count version, but the Blue progression remains **five Heads, one blank
key, five cutters, and the final Train lock**.

<h2 id="check-blue-receiver">
  Check the five-slot Blue receiver and choose a return point
</h2>

I visit the Blue receiver before starting the collection loop. It needs exactly
**five Heads**, and the tower becomes the safest place to protect rewards that
have already been carried across the region.

The five rewards do not have to come from one fixed set of Blue-colored puzzles.
A completed Head can be used at the active receiver even when the challenge sits
closer to another landmark. I still favor nearby Blue-region challenges because
they reduce the time spent carrying loose progression objects through fog,
tunnels, and steep ground.

I use the tower itself as the final deposit point. When a challenge is far from
the receiver, I choose one named intermediate cache such as a station entrance or
tunnel exit, but I do not scatter Heads across several unnamed rocks.

<h2 id="choose-five-blue-puzzles">
  Choose five Blue-area puzzles that fit the group
</h2>

I do not force every challenge around the Blue Tower into one run. I use the Map
Room flags and choose five rewards that match the current group size, available
communication tools, and remaining daylight.

My practical Blue set is:

1. the separated symbol relay rooms;
2. the five-shape transfer challenge;
3. the pose-matching room;
4. the pot and height-code room;
5. the blindfold obstacle course.

When another nearby Head has already been completed, I substitute it instead of
replaying a longer communication puzzle. The receiver checks the physical reward,
not the name of the challenge that produced it.

<h2 id="blue-symbol-relay">
  Solve the separated symbol relay without losing the sequence
</h2>

This challenge divides the group between rooms. One side can inspect the target
symbols, while the other side must select or arrange matching pieces. The main
difficulty is not recognizing the symbols; it is preserving their order while
the rooms interrupt normal communication.

Before starting, I give every strange mark a short name. Useful descriptions
include:

- arrow with a curled mouth;
- broken pretzel;
- capital B with an extra loop;
- scissor handles;
- and bent line with a hook.

I avoid changing a name halfway through the sequence. When one player calls the
second symbol a broken pretzel, everyone continues using that label until the
answer is submitted.

My role split is:

- one player stays with the target sequence;
- one player stays with the selectable symbols;
- one runner uses the rear opening or outside wall to relay one symbol at a time;
- and nobody advances to the next symbol until the receiving side repeats the
  current description.

Some layouts allow the two groups to hear each other only after stepping outside
the rear opening. I test that communication point before memorizing the full
sequence. If the receiver cannot repeat the first symbol, I fix the relay route
before touching the rest.

<h2 id="five-shape-transfer">
  Assign one player to each shape in the transfer challenge
</h2>

The shape-transfer challenge becomes much easier when the group stops treating
the pieces as a shared pile.

I first inspect the target side and identify the five required pieces. Then I
assign one shape to each available carrier. Common descriptions are enough:
star, diagonal line, three circles, E shape, and stick with a ball.

My rules are simple:

- one player owns one shape;
- carriers keep their piece until it reaches the target area;
- pieces are passed or placed rather than kicked down the slope;
- and the group recounts all five before leaving the source side.

With fewer players, I move the pieces in two trips. With a larger group, everyone
takes one and travels together. The challenge wastes time only when several
players grab the same object or a required shape is thrown out of the route.

<h2 id="blue-pose-room">
  Use confirmation signals in the pose-matching room
</h2>

The pose room separates the players who can see the required body position from
the players selecting or placing the matching answer.

I do not copy a fixed pose sequence because the arrangement can change. Instead,
I use a short vocabulary:

- crouch;
- neutral stand;
- both arms raised;
- left arm extended;
- right arm extended;
- and one arm raised.

The observer demonstrates one pose, then waits. The matching side brings its
chosen answer to the window or visible confirmation point. The observer nods or
jumps only when the match is correct. That extra confirmation prevents the group
from placing several answers quickly and losing track of which pose caused the
reset.

When the room has two teams, I keep one person in each side as the permanent
observer. Players who rotate between rooms tend to reverse left and right or
forget which pose has already been accepted.

<h2 id="blue-pot-code">
  Translate the pot heights into top, middle, and bottom rows
</h2>

The pot room uses one-way windows. One side can see the target arrangement, while
the answer side receives only a set of pots, stands, or marked positions.

Before closing the doors, I make both sides agree on the same layout:

- each display group gets a name or color;
- every stand is read as **top, middle, or bottom**;
- a pot placed on the floor represents the lowest level;
- and an empty group means that no marker belongs in that section.

One player on the target side records the pattern. One player on the answer side
places the markers. Everyone else stays out of the room while the doors change
sides; extra bodies can stop the door cycle or hide the floor positions.

The important step is mapping the structure, not guessing individual dots. I
translate one group completely, confirm its top-to-bottom order, and only then
move to the next group. If the answer fails, I recheck the floor-level pot first.
It is easy to treat that pot as unused even though it represents the bottom row.

<h2 id="blue-blindfold-course">
  Walk the blindfold route before putting the mask on
</h2>

The blindfold challenge is easier after the runner sees the obstacle course once.

Before equipping the mask, I walk the route with the lights on and identify:

- the opening turn;
- the crouch passage;
- the first jump;
- the short staircase;
- the narrow approach;
- and the final running jump.

Then I divide the course between two guides. The first guide handles the entrance
and low obstacles. The second waits near the far side and takes over when the
runner reaches the later platforms.

I use only short movement calls:

- forward;
- stop;
- turn left or right;
- crouch;
- stand;
- jump;
- and run, then jump.

For small corrections, I use clock directions or a rough angle. I never stack
several commands into one sentence.

The final running jump is the main failure point. Before starting the countdown,
I center the runner on the platform and ask for one small forward check. A good
jump can still fail when the runner drifts slightly left or right.

I also keep radios, Flares, and unrelated conversation quiet while the runner is
moving. The guide's voice must be the loudest instruction in the area.

<h2 id="return-blue-heads">
  Return after two or three Heads instead of carrying all five loose
</h2>

I make an early Blue Tower return after two or three rewards.

The Blue region includes steep drops, fog, underground passages, and several
objects that require free hands. Carrying five Heads together with the portable
map, Flashlight, Walkie-talkies, Flare gun, and puzzle equipment creates more risk
than it saves.

My return rule is:

- deposit immediately when the tower lies on the way to the next challenge;
- otherwise return after the third reward;
- leave inserted Heads in the receiver;
- and take only the equipment needed for the final two challenges.

When the last rewards are far from the tower, I stage them together at one named
landmark and move them as a group. I do not leave one Head at a tunnel exit,
another beside a rock, and another at a station platform.

Before leaving the final challenge, I count the actual rewards and assign every
one to a carrier. A completed flag on the map does not help when the physical Head
is still sitting at the challenge.

<h2 id="get-blue-key">
  Insert five Heads and protect the blank Blue key
</h2>

Once all five slots are filled, the receiver releases the blank Blue key.

I assign one player as the key carrier until the Train starts. The second player
handles the light and checks the cutter arrow. In a larger group, the remaining
players move ahead only far enough to locate the next station; they do not take
the key or throw it between carriers.

Five Heads do not activate the Train directly. They only produce the blank key.
The key must still pass through the complete five-cutter chain.

<h2 id="blue-key-cutters">
  Follow all five Blue cutter arrows and scan below the route
</h2>

The Blue key passes through **five cutter stations**.

After each cutter finishes, I look at the arrow on top and follow that direction.
I wait for the full cutting action before removing the key. A partly processed
key can fit into another station without being ready for the final lock.

The clean role split is:

- one player carries the key;
- one player keeps the route lit;
- one player watches the arrow and nearby ledges;
- and the rest of the group stays behind the key carrier.

The arrow is a direction marker, not a promise that the next cutter sits at eye
level. On sloped terrain, it may appear to point far past the correct location.
Before walking downhill or across the whole ridge, I search:

- behind the nearest rock;
- below the edge of the path;
- beside the previous challenge structure;
- and at the base of any short drop.

<h2 id="easy-miss-blue-cutter">
  Find the easy-to-miss cutter beside the rock
</h2>

The most common route mistake is walking too far from the tower because the arrow
appears to point across open terrain.

One cutter sits much closer to the previous route than it first appears. It is
partly hidden by a rock and lower ground, so a group following only the horizon
can pass directly over or beside it.

When I cannot see the next station within a short walk, I stop the key carrier and
send the light player back toward the last arrow. I scan downward rather than
continuing forward. This is faster than carrying the key through the whole Blue
region and later discovering that the missing cut was near the starting point.

<h2 id="activate-train">
  Use the finished Blue key at the Train system
</h2>

After the fifth cut, I take the finished key to the Blue Train lock.

I insert the key and wait for the activation to finish before running onto the
track. The Train system starts only after the completed key reaches this final
lock. An inactive station, a parked carriage, or a control that does nothing means
the Blue route is not finished yet.

Three Train carriages begin circulating on the rail loop. I let the first moving
carriage pass if the group is still scattered, then board together from a clear
station edge. Players can fall or be pushed from the exterior, so I sit or move
away from the outer edge once aboard.

**Big Ride** appears after I board a Train that is already moving.

Reaching the station is not the trigger. Standing beside an inactive carriage is
not the trigger. I complete the Blue key route, wait for the Train to circulate,
and step or jump onto the moving carriage.

Once aboard, I remain on the carriage for a moment instead of immediately jumping
off at the next landmark. The achievement normally appears during the first ride.

<h2 id="train-loop">
  Use the Train loop for regrouping and later puzzle cleanup
</h2>

The Train is deliberately slow, but it removes several long cross-island walks.
The loop passes major parts of the starting and Red Tower side of the island, and
the carriages can be used to reconnect players who have split between regions.

The Train also has communication equipment that helps riders talk while spread
along the carriage. I still use short calls before stopping or jumping off because
several players operating the controls at once can create confusion.

My Train rules are:

- agree on the next landmark before boarding;
- keep one player near the control;
- hold the stop control long enough when recovering someone who fell;
- avoid standing on the outside edge;
- and do not jump off until the route leader calls the landmark.

After the first ride, I return to the Map Room and inspect the remaining empty
flags. Activating the Train does not mean every tower or ending requirement is
complete. It only gives the group a faster way to reach unfinished regions.

<h2 id="blue-train-stuck">
  Fix common Blue Tower and Train problems
</h2>

### The Train controls do nothing

The Train is not activated merely by finding a station. I confirm that:

1. five Heads are inserted at the Blue receiver;
2. the blank key has passed through all five cutters;
3. the key is fully shaped;
4. and the finished key has been used at the Train lock.

When one of those steps is missing, I return to the last completed cutter instead
of pressing every Train control.

### I completed five puzzles but one Blue slot is empty

I return to the most recent challenge and look for the physical Head. A completed
challenge flag does not automatically move the reward to the tower.

I also check the last named cache, tunnel exit, station platform, and the hands of
players who carried optional equipment.

### The cutter arrow points into empty terrain

I search below the route before moving farther away. The easy-to-miss station can
sit beside a rock or below a ledge even when the arrow appears to point across the
ridge.

### The Blue key was dropped or kicked away

I first search the slope below the last known position and the ground around the
most recent cutter. After reconnecting, I inspect the Blue receiver and the last
completed cutter before replaying any Head puzzle. The key can return to a recent
progression point while the completed tower remains intact.

### Big Ride did not unlock

I wait until the Train is moving and board a circulating carriage. Standing on a
station, touching an inactive Train, or reaching the rail line before activation
does not complete Big Ride.

### I exited the Yellow Tunnel but cannot find the Blue Tower

I leave the tunnel, recover high ground, and look for the blue beacon above the
ridge. I do not keep following underground corridors after the route has already
entered the Blue region.

<h2 id="blue-train-faq">Blue Tower and Train FAQ</h2>

### How many Heads does the Blue Tower need?

The Blue receiver needs **five Heads**.

### Do I have to complete five specific Blue puzzles?

No. I use five practical nearby challenges, but another completed Head can replace
one of them. The receiver checks the reward, not a mandatory puzzle list.

### Is the Yellow Tunnel required to reach the Blue Tower?

No. It is a useful shortcut after it opens, but the Blue region can also be
reached from the starting and Map Room side of the island.

### Does inserting five Heads start the Train?

No. Five Heads release the blank Blue key. The key still needs all five cuts and
must be used at the final Train lock.

### How many Blue key cutters are there?

There are **five cutter stations** in the Blue key chain.

### Why does the Train still look inactive after I found it?

Finding the station is not enough. Finish the Blue receiver, cutter chain, and
final key insertion first.

### How do I unlock Big Ride?

Finish the Blue Train route, wait for a carriage to move, and board it.

### What should I do after activating the Train?

Ride the Train loop once, regroup the party, then return to the Map Room and
check which tower, puzzle, or late-game objective is still unfinished.
