Big Walk becomes easier when I treat the first session as a route-management
problem instead of trying to carry every object and clear every visible
challenge.

My beginner route is:

**choose the correct player-count version → regroup and test communication →
cross the tutorial drawbridge → use the Train Station as a return point → take a
Backpack → deposit three Red Tower Heads → collect two more → finish the Map
Room route.**

The dedicated walkthroughs handle exact puzzle inputs. This page focuses on the
decisions that keep the group together, protect progression rewards, and prevent
the first island loop from turning into several lost piles of equipment.

<h2 id="choose-player-count">
  Choose the player-count version for the group that will finish the route
</h2>

<AdUnit placement="article_after_first_h2" />

The Host chooses between **two players**, **three players**, and **four-plus
players** before the session begins. I match that choice to the people who will
actually play, not to friends who may join later.

The selection changes puzzle layouts and required roles. It cannot be changed in
the middle of that world, so choosing a larger version “just in case” can leave
the current group facing interactions built for players who are not present.

I use this rule:

- choose **two players** when two people will complete the session;
- choose **three players** when the same three people can stay for the route;
- choose **four-plus players** only when at least four players are present;
- use challenge skipping for accessibility or a blocked interaction, not as a
  replacement for the correct player-count choice.

When a puzzle contains more controls or roles than expected, I check this setting
before assuming that the room is broken.

<h2 id="regroup-first">
  Regroup and test communication before leaving the starting building
</h2>

Players can begin in separate colored rooms. I do not start searching for the
exit until everyone reaches the shared space and confirms that proximity voice
works.

My opening checks are practical:

1. adjust mouse sensitivity before narrow paths become frustrating;
2. set the field of view before long movement causes discomfort;
3. test pointing, grabbing, raising an item, kicking, crouching, and sprinting;
4. walk apart until proximity voice fades, then regroup;
5. agree on one short callout that means “stop and come back.”

This prevents the first communication puzzle from becoming the moment the group
discovers that one player's microphone, volume, or controls are not ready.

I also check the **Lost and Found**. When the Host ends the session, equipment
being held or worn returns there at the next start. I therefore stop at a sensible
checkpoint instead of carrying every loose tool back to the opening building.

<h2 id="multiplayer-roles">
  Organize larger groups around fixed roles, Flares, and one return point
</h2>

With four or more players, the main problem is usually not puzzle difficulty. It
is several people giving different directions, useful tools moving between
players, and late arrivals chasing a group that has already left the marked
location.

I assign four responsibilities before the first long split:

- **route leader:** carries the portable map when it is needed and names the next
  tower, station, or beach;
- **progression carrier:** wears the Backpack and keeps Heads and keys out of
  other players' hands;
- **light carrier:** stays beside the progression carrier at night and during
  cutter routes;
- **communication lead:** carries the Walkie-talkie, Megaphone, or Flare gun and
  gives the final instruction when several players are talking.

A group with more than four players does not need more leaders. Extra players
become puzzle runners, object carriers, or tower returners. I keep the key with
one named carrier and never pass it around simply because another player is
closer to the next cutter.

### Use a Flare to mark a landmark, not a moving party

I fire a Flare from a stable place such as a tower, Train Station, or puzzle
entrance. New arrivals can travel toward that point even when the active group
moves briefly inside a nearby structure.

I do not fire several colors in different directions. One location gets one
agreed color, and the communication lead repeats it only when a joining player
asks for another marker.

I stop using the Flare gun during:

- sound-matching puzzles;
- blindfold guidance;
- symbol relays;
- and any room where one short voice instruction controls the next input.

The explosion, light, and surrounding chatter can cover the call that matters.

### Recover the route before replaying a puzzle after a disconnect

After reconnecting, I do not assume that every completed action has reset.

I regroup at the starting building, Train Station, or the last named tower and
check the route in this order:

1. inspect Lost and Found for equipment that was held or worn;
2. check the active receiver for Heads already inserted;
3. check whether the key is at the receiver, latest cutter, or destination lock;
4. use the Map Room flag when it is available;
5. return to the challenge reward location before replaying the full puzzle.

A challenge can remain completed even when its physical Head was not deposited
before the disconnect. I replay it only when the completion marker is absent and
the reward is not available at the challenge.

This recovery order protects completed progress and avoids walking a large group
back through a communication puzzle that has already paid out.

<h2 id="carry-priority">
  Carry the next solution, not every useful object
</h2>

Hands are the first real resource in Big Walk. An item can be useful and still be
the wrong thing to carry when it blocks the progression reward that completes
the current tower.

I do not carry a music radio simply because it can be picked up. Before leaving a
landmark, I ask:

1. does the next challenge split the group?
2. will darkness make the route unreadable?
3. is a player already carrying a Head?
4. does this tool solve a known interaction, or are we only guessing?

A Walkie-talkie is worth a hand before a closed-room or remote-control puzzle. A
light is worth a hand when the return landmark disappears at night. An optional
tool is not worth carrying when both players already need to transport
progression rewards.

<h2 id="station-staging-point">
  Use the inactive Train Station as a named staging point
</h2>

After crossing the drawbridge, I follow the main route through the outdoor music
area until I reach the inactive Train Station.

The station is useful before the Train works because it gives the group three
stable references:

- the **platform** is a recognizable place for spare equipment;
- the **train line** helps with orientation;
- the **red blinking beacon** shows the direction of the Red Tower.

I do not hide Heads or important tools beside unnamed trees, rocks, or beaches.
When I must free a hand, I use the platform or another structure both players
can name.

My branch rule is:

1. leave the station toward one nearby challenge;
2. collect its Head;
3. move the reward back toward the station or tower;
4. reorganize equipment at the known landmark;
5. then start the next branch.

This keeps five useful objects from becoming five separate recovery missions.

<h2 id="backpack-first">
  Equip the first Backpack before a longer collection loop
</h2>

The Backpack changes the route because it stores one Head or another loose item
without occupying a hand.

I equip the first Backpack as soon as I find it. I do not leave it hanging because
the current reward is already close to the tower. Its value appears on the next
branch, when the carrier can protect a Head while still climbing, pointing, or
holding a light.

In a two-player group, one Backpack can make it physically possible to move three
Heads at once by using:

- one Backpack slot;
- one player's free hands;
- and the second player's hands.

That is the maximum carrying setup, not the default plan. When the next challenge
needs a Walkie-talkie, light, or timed object, I return with fewer Heads rather
than filling every hand.

<h2 id="return-wait-skip">
  Decide whether to return, wait for daylight, or leave a puzzle
</h2>

### I return to the tower when

- the group has two or three Heads and no useful hand remains;
- the station or red beacon is still visible;
- a challenge is complete but the next branch is uncertain;
- or the group is about to split without working Walkie-talkies.

### I wait for daylight when

- the solution depends on distant colors, platforms, or an open-terrain path;
- the group is trying to recover several loose objects;
- or darkness has removed the landmarks needed for a safe return.

I do not wait for daylight inside a small room where every required object is
already visible.

### I leave a puzzle for later when

- it depends on coordinate equipment that the group has not identified yet;
- it appears connected to inactive transport;
- a completed Head is ready to be deposited;
- or every attempt depends on guessing what an unidentified object might do.

<h2 id="end-session">
  End the session after completing the current objective
</h2>

I do not need to return every held item to the starting building before the Host
ends the session. Held and worn equipment returns through Lost and Found.

I still avoid stopping halfway through a multi-step puzzle. Before leaving, I
prefer one of these checkpoints:

- a Head has been inserted into a tower receiver;
- the group has returned to the Train Station;
- a key has completed its cutter route and opened the destination;
- or the current challenge has paid out its reward.

A clear checkpoint removes the need to reconstruct which switch, timed object,
or loose reward was still in progress when the group returns.

<h2 id="stuck">
  First-session fixes
</h2>

### We have a Head but cannot carry the next tool

Move the Head to the Train Station platform or another named structure. Do not
hide it beside an unnamed tree. Return after clearing a hand or equipping a
Backpack.

### We deposited three Heads, but the Red Tower did not open

The Red Tower needs five Heads. The three inserted rewards are a safe first
deposit, not the completion requirement.

### We keep returning to the tutorial coastline

Stop following the coast. Find the inactive Train Station, face the red blinking
beacon, and restart the route from that fixed landmark.

### A Backpack or held item is missing after restarting

Check Lost and Found in the starting building. Equipment that was held or worn
when the Host ended the session returns there.

### Our puzzle has more controls than expected

Check the player-count version selected by the Host. A three-player or four-plus
world can assign more roles than the two-player layout.

<h2 id="faq">
  Big Walk beginner FAQ
</h2>

### Do I need to complete every visible puzzle before the Map Room?

No. The Red Tower needs five Head rewards. Once its five slots are full, I stop
clearing unrelated branches and finish the key route.

### Does the Train need to work before I can reach the Map Room?

No. During the first Red Tower loop, I use the inactive Train Station and rail
line as landmarks and complete the route on foot.

### Who should wear the first Backpack?

I give it to the player responsible for carrying Heads and other progression
objects, not automatically to the Host.

### Should I carry a Walkie-talkie all the time?

No. I take one before a split route, closed room, remote control, or timed
handoff. When both players stay together and need free hands, I leave it at a
named staging point.

### Can the group stop away from the starting building?

Yes. I finish the current objective, let the Host end the session, and recover
held or worn equipment from Lost and Found at the next start.
