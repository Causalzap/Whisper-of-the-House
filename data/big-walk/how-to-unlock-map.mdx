I unlock the map after crossing the tutorial drawbridge, returning **five Heads**
to the Red Tower, shaping the second blank key through **five cutters**, and
using the finished key at the upper red entrance.

The numbered `4166 / 1899` coordinate challenge is not required. I leave it for
later and use five shorter Head challenges around the Red Tower and train line.

This route follows the **two-player world version**. The Red Tower still uses a
five-slot receiver in larger groups, but individual puzzle roles can change with
the Host's selected player count.

<h2 id="five-head-receiver">
  Check the five-slot receiver before choosing puzzles
</h2>

I inspect the receiver before leaving the tower. It needs exactly **five Heads**.
That tells me when to stop exploring and prevents me from treating every nearby
device as mandatory Map Room progression.

The numbered coordinate board, inactive Train, music radios, and loose equipment
are separate systems. None of them replaces the five physical Head rewards.

Several puzzles around this part of the island can supply those Heads. My
two-player route used:

- the green communication room;
- the timed maze handoff;
- the basketball challenge;
- the timed projectile relay;
- and the five-pose platforms.

I use this as a practical route, not a fixed required list. When another nearby
puzzle is easier for the current group, I take that Head instead. The full

<h2 id="three-head-return">
  Return after three Heads instead of carrying all five at once
</h2>

My first return happens after three rewards.

One Backpack can protect a Head while keeping a player's hands free, but a
two-player group still needs space for a light and communication equipment.
Trying to carry five loose progression objects while also moving radios,
binoculars, the Map, or other tools creates more risk than it saves.

At three Heads, I stop scouting and go back to the Red Tower because:

- the tower is already a known landmark;
- inserted Heads cannot be lost in the dark;
- the remaining two challenges benefit from Walkie-talkies;
- and the final route returns toward the Train Station anyway.

<h2 id="red-tower-key">
  Insert five Heads and keep the blank key with one player
</h2>

When all five slots are filled, the Red Tower releases a new blank key.

I assign that key to one player until the Map Room opens. I do not pass it back
and forth near cliffs, throw it down slopes, or leave it beside the Train Station.
The route is easier when the second player handles the light and points out the
next cutter.

Five Heads do **not** open the Map Room directly. They only produce the blank key.
The key still needs to be shaped through the complete cutter chain.

<h2 id="map-key-cutters">
  Follow all five cutter arrows instead of returning to the tower
</h2>

In my route, the Red Tower key passes through **five cutter stations**.

Each cutter changes the key and points toward the next station. After the cutting
animation finishes, I follow the arrow from that cutter rather than returning to
the Red Tower or retracing the direction I arrived from.

The clean two-player setup is:

- one player carries the key;
- one player carries the light;
- both wait until each cutter finishes;
- and the key carrier stays on the marked route until the upper red entrance is
  reached.

Parts of the chain cross familiar ground, which can make the route feel circular.
A familiar landmark does not mean the key is finished. I continue until the
shaped key reaches the red lock above the Map Room.

<h2 id="enter-map-room">
  Use the finished key at the upper red entrance
</h2>

After the last cutter, I climb to the upper red entrance and insert the finished
key. The door opens onto a large physical model of the island rather than a
traditional menu map.

I look over the model before picking up any portable tool. This is where the
island starts making sense as a connected progression system:

- towers show the major Head-return objectives;
- rail lines and other transport routes show how the regions connect;
- challenge sites can be checked for completion;
- Backpack and radio locations can be identified;
- and large structures such as the central wall become easier to place relative
  to the starting area.

<h2 id="map-flags">
  Use flags and empty flagpoles to avoid repeating completed sites
</h2>

The model is most useful as a completion check.

A challenge site with a **blue flag** has already been completed. A site with an
**empty flagpole** is still available. I scan for empty poles near the next tower
instead of walking back to every structure I remember seeing.

That distinction also helps when the group has solved a puzzle but cannot
remember whether the Head was collected. If the model shows the completed flag,
I stop treating that location as a missing challenge and check the receiver,
Backpack, or last equipment stop for the physical reward.

<h2 id="portable-map">
  The portable map does not show your live position
</h2>

The fold-out map is a physical item, not a GPS. It does **not** place a live
player marker on the page.

This is the point where players often think the map is not working. I locate
myself by combining three pieces of information:

1. the nearby tower, rail line, coast, or large building;
2. the map section shown in the corner of the portable map;
3. the direction from the Compass or a known coordinate board.

The map can zoom into different island sections and is useful for coordinate-based
puzzles, but I still need to identify the local landmark first. When I cannot
match the surrounding terrain to the page, I return to a clear anchor such as the
Train Station, a colored tower, or the coast before choosing a direction.

<h2 id="map-compass-lasers">
  Choose one navigation tool for the next trip
</h2>

The portable map, Compass, and Laser Pointers solve different problems. I do not
take all of them just because they are available.

I take the **portable map** when the next route crosses an unfamiliar region,
uses coordinates, or combines several objectives.

I take the **Compass** when I already know the destination but need to hold one
direction across open terrain.

I take a **Laser Pointer** when one player can see a distant platform, tower, or
route that the other player cannot identify.

I leave the navigation tools behind when both players need Walkie-talkies, a
light is essential, or the next objective follows a visible beacon. Carrying
capacity matters more than having every tool.

<h2 id="stuck">
  Map Room unlock fixes
</h2>

### I inserted five Heads, but the Map Room is still closed

Take the blank key and complete all five cutters. The receiver awards the key;
it does not open the upper red entrance.

### We completed five puzzles, but the receiver still has an empty slot

Check the reward area at the final puzzle and the last place where equipment was
reorganized. Completing a challenge and inserting its physical Head are separate
steps.

### The cutter route keeps bringing us back to familiar landmarks

Wait for the current cut to finish and follow the arrow on that station. The
route can cross familiar ground before the final key shape is complete.

### The portable map is open, but I cannot find our marker

There is no live player marker. Match a nearby tower, coast, rail line, or large
building to the current map section, then use the Compass or coordinates to set
the direction.

<h2 id="faq">
  Big Walk Map Room FAQ
</h2>

### Can I use a different set of five puzzles?

Yes. The Red Tower needs five Head rewards, not one fixed list of five challenge
sites. Choose the nearby puzzles that fit the current player-count version and
available equipment.

### Do I need a Backpack before unlocking the Map Room?

No. A Backpack makes Head transport safer, but it is not a Map Room requirement.
Returning after three Heads reduces the carrying problem even without one.

### Is the 4166 and 1899 coordinate puzzle required?

No. The Red Tower can be completed with five other nearby Heads. The coordinate
challenge is easier to revisit after the portable map becomes available.

### Does the Map Room activate the Train or Chairlift?

No. The Map Room reveals the island layout and provides navigation tools. The
Train and Chairlift use separate tower keys and progression routes.
