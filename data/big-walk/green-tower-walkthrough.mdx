The Green Tower route is a five-Head collection loop built around the Train
Station. I identify the green receiver first, use the station as a cache, protect
the first three rewards, finish two longer communication challenges, consolidate
four Heads into one two-player load, recover the fifth Head at the station, and
return all five to receive the blank Green Key.

This guide covers the route from the opened Map Room through the **five completed
Green slots and Green Key reward**. Individual puzzle mechanics stay in the
dedicated solutions page so this walkthrough can focus on route order, carrying
capacity, return timing, and the final receiver run.

<h2 id="use-train-station">
  Use the Train Station as the Green-route cache
</h2>

The Train Station is the best staging point in this region even before the Train
is active. It is easier to relocate than an unnamed puzzle building, and several
Green-route challenges can be connected back to it.

I store only items that both players can identify later:

- completed Heads waiting for the final carry;
- the portable map after the route is known;
- spare tools that do not help the next challenge;
- and coordinate equipment that belongs to a separate objective.

I do not mix progression rewards with every loose object found nearby. Heads go
together in one visible area. Navigation tools use another part of the platform.
That makes it much harder to leave with four rewards while assuming a radio,
panel, or coordinate device is the fifth.

The station is a cache, not a reason to interrupt the route and investigate the
Train. During this loop, its value is organization.

<h2 id="choose-five-heads">
  Choose five nearby Head rewards instead of forcing one fixed puzzle order
</h2>

The Green receiver needs five physical Heads, but it does not require one exact
set of five challenge sites.

My completed route included rewards already staged near the Train Station plus
the longer Green Path, Counting and Symbol, and Blindfold challenges. Another
group can replace one of these with a different nearby unfinished challenge and
still complete the receiver.

I choose based on the current conditions:

- use a short nearby challenge while both players have free hands;
- wait for daylight before a route depends on distant landmarks;
- keep Walkie-talkies available before players separate;
- avoid entering a gear-removal challenge while carrying existing Heads;
- and return a completed reward before starting an unrelated transport problem.

The `4166 / 1899` coordinate equipment is not required for the Green Key route.
I leave that device at the Train Station rather than giving up a hand that should
carry a Head or communication tool.

<h2 id="protect-three-heads">
  Protect the first three Heads before the longer split-role challenges
</h2>

Three Heads is my first consolidation point.

Once the group has three physical rewards, I stop leaving them at separate
challenge buildings. I move them toward the Train Station or another established
part of the return route before beginning the final two challenges.

This return is worth the time because:

- three rewards are enough progress to protect;
- the remaining challenges can occupy both players for a long period;
- communication gear may need both available hands;
- and remembering three separate reward locations is harder than returning to one
  named station.

I count the physical Heads rather than the puzzles I remember completing. A
challenge can be solved while its reward is still sitting beside the final panel,
socket, or platform.

Before leaving the station again, I keep only what the next challenge needs. The
portable map can stay behind when the route is already known. Walkie-talkies stay
with the players until the final role assignment is complete.

<h2 id="finish-last-two">
  Finish the last two challenges without carrying the existing rewards inside
</h2>

The final challenges often require the group to separate, count symbols, guide a
player through a structure, or handle objects that already occupy both hands.

I do not bring the cached Heads into those buildings. They remain at the Train
Station or another agreed staging point.

For the Counting room, both players agree on the number language before the lower
door closes. The Walkie-talkie can stop helping once the room blocks
communication, but it remains useful while assigning roles and recovering the
route afterward.

For the Blindfold building, I leave the current Heads, portable map, and extra
equipment outside. Putting on the large face removes the player's vision and can
drop worn or carried gear, so the puzzle begins only after the progression load
is safe.

This page does not repeat the panel shapes or movement calls. The important
Green-route rule is that the final challenge should add one Head to the collection
without scattering the four rewards already earned.

<h2 id="carry-four-heads">
  Consolidate four Heads into one two-player load
</h2>

After the final challenge pays out, I stop exploring and organize the return.

A two-player group can move **four Heads at once** with this setup:

- one Head inside the Backpack;
- one Head carried by the Backpack wearer;
- two Heads carried by the second player.

Both players are full, so this is not the moment to keep the portable map,
coordinate device, spare radio, or another optional tool.

I keep the group together during the carry. Splitting up saves little time when
both players are already responsible for multiple progression objects. One fall
or wrong turn can turn a clean return into a search for a Head on an unnamed
slope.

The destination for this load is the Train Station, where the fifth Head is
already waiting.

<h2 id="recover-fifth-head">
  Recover the fifth Head at the Train Station and recount everything
</h2>

At the station, I place the four arriving Heads beside the cached reward and
count the physical objects again.

My final station checklist is:

1. one Head is secured in the Backpack;
2. all four loose Heads are visible;
3. no puzzle panel or coordinate device is being counted as a reward;
4. both players know the route to the Green receiver;
5. no optional challenge will be started before submission.

Once all five are together, I do not leave one behind to make carrying more
comfortable. The station has already done its job: it turned several separate
collection trips into one final receiver run.

<h2 id="return-to-green">
  Leave the Train Station together and go directly to Green
</h2>

The final journey is not another exploration loop.

Both players leave the station together, follow the known route to the Green
receiver, and ignore unrelated structures along the way. With every hand occupied,
a side trip creates more risk than value.

At the receiver, I insert the five Heads one at a time and watch each slot
activate. If the fifth slot remains empty, I check the ground and Backpack before
walking back toward any puzzle.

I do not remove or rearrange accepted Heads after they enter the receiver. Once
all five indicators are active, I wait for the reward sequence.

<h2 id="get-green-key">
  Insert five Heads and take the blank Green Key
</h2>

Completing the five-slot receiver produces the blank Green Key.

By this point, the submitted progression total is:

- `4` Heads for the opening drawbridge;
- `5` Heads for the Red Tower and Map Room;
- `5` Heads for Green;
- `14` Heads in total.

The new key is a progression reward, but it is still blank. I treat its appearance
as the completion point for the Green Tower collection route.

<h2 id="session-checkpoint">
  Use the Green Key as a clean session checkpoint
</h2>

I make sure the key has fully appeared before ending the session. Stopping while
the fifth Head is still being carried or while the receiver animation is
incomplete leaves the group uncertain about the actual saved state.

The Green Key reward is a cleaner stopping point because:

- all five Heads are already protected by the receiver;
- the reward has visibly appeared;
- the collection route is complete;
- and the next session can begin with one clear progression object instead of
  several scattered puzzle rewards.

Held or worn equipment returns through Lost and Found after the Host ends the
session, but I still finish the receiver sequence before leaving.

<h2 id="stuck">
  Green Tower and Green Key fixes
</h2>

### We completed five puzzles, but one Green slot is empty

Count the physical Heads at the Train Station and final challenge buildings.
Challenge completion does not automatically submit the reward.

### We have five Heads but cannot carry all of them

Move four in one two-player load and use the Train Station as the fifth-Head
cache. Keep all rewards at named landmarks rather than dropping one on the route.

### The cable-car controls still do nothing

The Green receiver awards a blank key. Filling the five Head slots does not by
itself complete the key-shaping route.

### We lost track of which receiver needs these Heads

Return to the Map Room or the previously visited five-slot Green receiver. Do not
choose a destination only from the color of the nearest puzzle building.

### We reached the station but can only find four Heads

Check the Backpack first, then the exact place where the previous load was set
down. Keep puzzle panels and coordinate equipment in a separate part of the
station so they cannot hide the cached reward.

<h2 id="faq">
  Big Walk Green Tower FAQ
</h2>

### How many Heads does the Green Tower need?

The Green receiver needs five physical Heads.

### Do I need the 4166 / 1899 coordinate puzzle for the Green Key?

No. Five other nearby challenge rewards can complete the receiver.

### Why use the Train Station if the Train is inactive?

The platform is a stable, recognizable cache near the Green-route challenge
cluster. It reduces the number of rewards left at unnamed buildings and gives the
group one place to recount all five Heads.

### How many Heads can two players carry at once?

With one Backpack, two players can move four Heads in one load: one in the
Backpack, one in that player's hands, and two with the second player.

### When should I stop the session?

The blank Green Key is a clean checkpoint. I wait until the receiver has accepted
all five Heads and the key is fully visible.
