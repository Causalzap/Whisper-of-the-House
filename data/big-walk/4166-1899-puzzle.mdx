The **4166 1899 puzzle in Big Walk** looks like a code puzzle, but the two numbers are not a password for the structure in front of you.

They are **map coordinates**.

I lost the most time by treating `4166` and `1899` as something I needed to enter nearby. The useful interpretation is **41.66 and 18.99**. Once I read the board that way, the puzzle changed from a code hunt into a navigation problem.

There is one more catch: the solution is split across the island. **One player needs to stay at the glass box while another player travels to the hidden button.**

My most important rule here is **do not send the whole group to the coordinates**. The distant button and the reward box are separate parts of the same puzzle.

<h2 id="how-to-find-4166-1899-location">How to find the 4166 1899 location</h2>

Once I know roughly where 41.66 / 18.99 sits, I stop staring at the map and navigate landmark to landmark.

That is much more reliable than trying to watch the coordinate grid while moving.

My route is:

1. leave the original 4166 / 1899 structure;
2. move **left** toward the train-track area;
3. pass the train station / puzzle area;
4. continue behind the mountain;
5. keep moving left, then climb upward;
6. reach the area with the **green structure**;
7. move right and upward around it;
8. continue toward the train;
9. keep the green structure as the main reference;
10. move behind it toward the rocks.

<h2 id="where-is-4166-hidden-button">Where the hidden 4166 1899 button is</h2>

The final approach is where I would expect most players to get stuck.

Reaching approximately 42 / 19 does **not** put a giant button in the open.

I go behind the green structure and look for:

1. a **large rock**;
2. the smaller rocks behind or beside it;
3. a small **camouflage-colored box** tucked into that area.

The button is mounted on that box.

<h2 id="how-two-players-solve-4166-1899">How two players complete the 4166 1899 puzzle</h2>

Finding the button is only half the solution.

The other half is back at the original structure.

Before anyone begins the long walk, I assign two roles.

### Player 1: stay at the glass box

I leave one player beside the original locked glass box containing the **red piece**.

That player does not need to follow the coordinate route.

I keep a hand free near the end. If I am holding the map or another loose object while waiting, I put it down before trying to take the reward.

### Player 2: go to the hidden button

The second player takes the coordinate route:

**4166 structure → train tracks → behind the mountain → green structure → rocks → camouflage box.**

Once that player reaches the button, I do not press it immediately if the other player is still travelling or distracted.

I confirm that the waiting player is standing beside the glass box first.

Then I press the button.

<h2 id="4166-puzzle-common-mistakes">The mistakes that wasted the most time</h2>

### Treating 4166 and 1899 as a passcode

This is the biggest conceptual trap.

I do not search for a keypad. I insert the decimal mentally and read the numbers as map coordinates.

### Sending both players to 41.66 / 18.99

I avoid this completely.

The remote button is far from the glass box, so I leave one player at each end of the interaction.

### Walking past the green structure

The train and mountain make good large landmarks, but the **green structure is the important final one**.

Once I have reached it, I tighten the search radius instead of continuing across the island.

### Searching the obvious side of the rocks

The button is attached to a small camouflage-colored box.

I deliberately walk around the rock cluster and check the back side. From the wrong angle it blends into the terrain.

### Trying to solve the Train route first

The Train is useful for island movement, but I do not turn this one puzzle into a complete Blue Tower detour unless I actually want the Train unlocked for broader progression.

For the Blue Tower route, key cutters, Train activation, and Big Ride, I use the dedicated [Blue Tower and Train guide](/big-walk/blue-tower-train).

<h2 id="4166-1899-faq">Big Walk 4166 1899 FAQ</h2>

### Can I find the hidden button without carrying the map all the way there?

Yes. I use the map to understand that the destination is around **41.66 / 18.99**, then switch to landmarks. Once I know the route, the train tracks, mountain, green structure, and rocks are enough to find the button again.

### Why did pressing the hidden button seem to do nothing?

The visible result happens at the **other end of the puzzle**. Have another player watch the original glass box while the remote button is pressed.

### Should I bring every player to the coordinate location?

No. I keep at least one player at the glass box. The puzzle is easier when the group treats it as two simultaneous stations rather than a shared expedition to the same destination.

<h2 id="4166-1899-takeaway">Use the numbers to find the area, then trust the landmarks</h2>

My clean route for this puzzle is:

**4166 / 1899 → 41.66 / 18.99 → lower-right map region → train tracks → mountain → green structure → rocks → camouflage box → remote button.**

The numbers solve the navigation problem, but they do not finish the puzzle.

The real completion condition is cooperation across distance: one player reaches the hidden button while another stays beside the glass box and collects the red piece when it opens.

Once I understood those as two separate jobs, the 4166 / 1899 puzzle stopped feeling like a cryptic number puzzle and became one of the clearest navigation-and-coordination challenges on the island.
