Big Walk gives you a large island, more puzzle rewards than each tower needs, several transport systems, and very little pressure to follow one exact route. The problem is that this freedom makes it easy to spend an hour solving the wrong thing for your current objective.

For a clean first completion, this is the **Big Walk walkthrough route I use**:

**Tutorial → Red Tower and Map Room → Green Tower and Chairlift → Yellow Tower and Tunnel → Blue Tower and Train → Black Tower and Wall → Black Sphere → Big Goodbye → seven Purple Puzzles and remaining cleanup → Big Game.**

The colored towers do not have to be completed in exactly that order. I prefer **Green → Yellow → Blue** after Red because each transport unlock makes the next long trip easier.

<h2 id="progression">Understand the progression loop before choosing puzzles</h2>

The first four colored routes use the same broad progression pattern:

1. solve useful challenges;
2. recover their physical Head rewards;
3. return enough Heads to the active receiver;
4. take the blank key;
5. finish every cutter in that key chain;
6. use the finished key on the route's major unlock.

The published route through Blue uses:

- **4 tutorial Heads** before the drawbridge;
- **5 Red Heads** for the Red progression;
- **5 Green Heads** for the Green progression;
- **5 Yellow Heads** for the Yellow progression;
- **5 Blue Heads** for the Blue progression.

The important detail is that finishing a puzzle is not enough by itself.

I still need to recover the physical reward and get it back to the receiver. If the receiver count did not change after a successful puzzle, I check the reward location before replaying anything.

Heads also are not locked to the color of the nearest tower.

That changes how I route the game. If one challenge beside Green is taking too long, I do not keep failing it just because I am working on Green. I use another available Head and return to the difficult puzzle later.

<h2 id="opening-route">Finish the tutorial before exploring the full island</h2>

The opening route has one useful objective:

**fill the four-slot receiver → finish the opening key → cross the drawbridge.**

I do not spend much time collecting optional equipment before that bridge opens.

The island becomes easier to route after the tutorial, while early detours mostly create loose equipment that someone later has to remember or recover.

If the group has:

- fewer than four returned Heads;
- a blank or partly cut key;
- a key that enters the drawbridge but does nothing;

I stay on the opening route.

The [Tutorial and Drawbridge Walkthrough](/big-walk/tutorial-walkthrough) owns the four challenge solutions and exact cutter sequence.

<h2 id="red-tower-route">Make Red Tower the first major destination</h2>

After crossing the drawbridge, I use the inactive Train Station as an easy regrouping point and move toward the Red side.

The reason I prioritize Red is not just tower completion.

It unlocks the **Map Room**, which changes how I handle every later stage.

My Hub-level checkpoint is simple:

**Red is finished when I can physically enter and use the Map Room.**

If I am still carrying a partly shaped Red key, I do not start routing toward Green yet.

The [Red Tower and Map Room Guide](/big-walk/how-to-unlock-map) owns the five-Head route, Red key cutters, Map Room door, flags, portable map, Compass, and Laser Pointer.

<h2 id="map-room">Return to the Map Room whenever the route becomes unclear</h2>

I use the Map Room as a decision point, not as another puzzle destination.

It is most useful when:

- the group no longer remembers which challenges were finished;
- several players are carrying loose rewards;
- I need to choose between two distant tower routes;
- I am looking for cleanup targets later in the game.

I do not carry every navigation tool at once.

For a long route, I normally choose the one that solves the current problem:

- a portable map when crossing multiple regions;
- the Compass when maintaining direction matters;
- the Laser Pointer when I need to mark something visible in the distance.

The rest stays at a named location.

That matters because a free hand for a light, Walkie-Talkie, key, or puzzle object is often more valuable than carrying three navigation tools.

<h2 id="green-route">Do Green next when you want the best early transport upgrade</h2>

After Red, I prefer Green.

The reason is the **Chairlift**.

Once active, it gives the group an elevated transport route and reduces the amount of repeated walking needed to reach later regions.

The Hub checkpoint is:

**Green receiver finished → Green key finished → Chairlift activated → board a moving seat.**

I do not repeat the individual Green puzzles here. The [Green Tower and Chairlift Guide](/big-walk/green-tower-walkthrough) owns the complete Head route, reward staging, key cutters, Chairlift station, and Big Sit trigger.

<h2 id="yellow-route">Use the Chairlift to reduce the trip toward Yellow</h2>

After Green, I use the higher transport route to move toward Yellow instead of retracing the entire ground route.

Yellow's progression reward is the **underground Tunnel network**.

The route is not finished merely because I reach a tunnel entrance.

My checkpoint is:

**Yellow key completed → Tunnel opened → group can actually enter the underground route.**

That distinction matters because the underground system later becomes useful for both travel and the hidden Shiny route.

The [Yellow Tower and Tunnel Guide](/big-walk/yellow-tower-tunnel) owns the receiver, Yellow puzzles, key cutters, and Tunnel unlock itself.

<h2 id="blue-route">Move from Yellow toward Blue and activate the Train</h2>

The Tunnel can shorten the trip toward Blue, but it does not substitute for Blue progression.

Once I reach the Blue side, I go back to the normal tower loop:

**Heads → receiver → key → cutters → Train activation.**

The Blue route is complete when the Train is actually moving and the group can board a carriage.

If I have reached the Train but it is inactive, I do not start pressing random railway controls. I return to the Blue key progression.

The [Blue Tower and Train Guide](/big-walk/blue-tower-train) owns the five-Head route, Blue puzzle sequence, hidden cutter, Train activation, and Big Ride.

<h2 id="black-route">Treat the Black Tower, Wall, and Black Sphere as separate stages</h2>

This is the late-game distinction that saves the most confusion.

There are three separate landmarks:

**Black Tower → Wall → Black Sphere.**

The Black Tower is where I enter the Black progression and eventually obtain the **Black Key**.

The Black Key belongs at the **Wall / large gate**.

The Black Sphere comes after that gate.

I do not describe the synchronized Black Tower trigger, vision, current completion requirement, Black Key location, or Wall interaction here because those are now owned by the [Black Tower and Black Key Guide](/big-walk/black-tower).

My Hub checkpoint is simply:

**if I have not opened the Wall yet, I am still on the Black Tower route.**

Once the Wall is open, I switch to the ending route.

<h2 id="ending-route">Finish the Black Sphere before worrying about post-game cleanup</h2>

After the Wall opens, I move into the Black Sphere sequence.

This is where the main-route ending begins.

The progression handoff is:

**Wall opens → Black Sphere → final sphere mechanism → route across the water → Wave Goodbye → Big Goodbye.**

I do not mix post-game Purple Puzzle instructions into this Hub anymore.

The [Big Walk Endings Guide](/big-walk/ending-guide) owns the Black Sphere sequence, the building across the water, Wave Goodbye, Big Goodbye, the final key, hidden completion room, and Big Game ending.

When **Big Goodbye** appears, I consider the normal campaign complete.

If I want 100% completion, I continue the same save.

<h2 id="post-game-route">After Big Goodbye, clear the seven Purple Puzzles and anything you skipped</h2>

Continuing the same save adds **seven Purple Puzzles** to the cleanup route.

Those seven challenges are not the only possible remaining work.

Any normal puzzle that I skipped earlier can still matter to full completion.

My post-game decision order is:

1. confirm all seven Purple Puzzles are available;
2. clear the Purple set;
3. return to the Map Room;
4. check for unresolved normal puzzles;
5. recover and deliver any physical rewards still left behind;
6. only then check the final-key progression.

For the actual seven solutions, I use the [All 7 Purple Puzzles Guide](/big-walk/purple-puzzles).

That page owns the 30-minute timer, Golf Course heavy-object route, light communication room, music puzzle, nine-object matching, symbol room, and Purple Flagpole.

The Ending Guide owns what happens **after** that cleanup is complete.

<h2 id="achievement-route">Use achievements to confirm progress instead of changing routes for them</h2>

Most achievements are more useful as checkpoints than as reasons to abandon the active objective.

During the normal progression route I can naturally confirm:

- **Big Walk** after the opening drawbridge;
- **Big View** around Map Room progression;
- **Big Sit** from the moving Chairlift;
- **Big Tunnel** after entering the opened Tunnel;
- **Big Ride** from the moving Train;
- **Big Goodbye** after the main ending;
- **Big Game** after complete cleanup and the hidden ending route.

**Big Makeover** is different enough to justify its own side route because Shiny is hidden underground rather than sitting at the normal paint station.

For the complete twelve-achievement checklist, I use the [Big Walk Achievements Guide](/big-walk/achievements).

For Shiny specifically, I use [How to Get Shiny](/big-walk/how-to-get-shiny) instead of duplicating the tunnel route inside the achievement checklist.

<h2 id="multiplayer">Keep multiplayer roles simple on long routes</h2>

Big Walk supports **2–12 players**, but I do not give every player a separate leadership job.

Before a long transfer between regions, I normally identify:

- one route leader;
- one player responsible for the important Head or key;
- one light carrier when travelling at night;
- one person responsible for communication when the group separates.

Everyone else can help with puzzle roles, carrying, scouting, and catching physical objects.

The important part is knowing **who has the progression object**.

Losing five minutes because the group forgot who carried a key is much easier to prevent than recovering that key after everyone has boarded different transport.

For Host choice, Flares, Lost and Found, reconnect recovery, equipment planning, and first-session roles, the [Beginner Guide](/big-walk/beginner-guide) remains the Owner Page.

<h2 id="stuck">What I check when I no longer know what to do next</h2>

I use a short progression diagnosis instead of replaying random puzzles.

### I have a partly shaped key

I stay on the current tower route.

A partly shaped key means at least one cutter in that route is still unfinished.

### I solved a puzzle but the receiver count did not change

I check for the physical reward.

The puzzle can be finished while its Head is still sitting at the challenge, in someone's hands, in a Backpack, or at a temporary cache.

### We keep failing the puzzle nearest the tower

I leave it.

Heads are not restricted to the nearest colored region, so another finished challenge may provide the progression reward I need.

### I have no idea which challenges remain

I return to the Map Room.

That is the moment when the extra walk is worth it.

### The Train is active and I do not know what comes next

I move into late progression:

**remaining cleanup → Black Tower → Black Key → Wall.**

The [Black Tower Guide](/big-walk/black-tower) takes over from there.

### The Wall is already open

I stop looking for more Black Tower steps.

The route has moved into the Black Sphere and endings, so I switch to the [Ending Guide](/big-walk/ending-guide).

### Big Goodbye is complete but I still have objectives

That is expected.

I continue on the same save, clear the seven [Purple Puzzles](/big-walk/purple-puzzles), then use the Map Room to locate older unfinished work.

<h2 id="faq">Big Walk walkthrough FAQ</h2>

### What is the best tower order for a first playthrough?

I use **Red → Green → Yellow → Blue → Black**.

Red gives me the Map Room first, while Green, Yellow, and Blue progressively add Chairlift, Tunnel, and Train travel. It is a routing preference rather than a mandatory color order.

### When should I return to the Map Room?

I return when I no longer know which puzzle is worth doing next, after a major transport unlock if the group has become scattered, and again during 100% cleanup.

I do not return after every individual puzzle.

### Should I complete every side puzzle before entering the Black Tower?

Not necessarily.

For a normal Big Goodbye run, I prioritize whatever progression the Black route currently requires. Full normal-and-purple cleanup matters when I continue toward Big Game.

### Is Shiny required for the main ending?

I treat Shiny as an optional achievement route rather than a required step in the normal tower-to-Big-Goodbye progression. The dedicated [Shiny Guide](/big-walk/how-to-get-shiny) owns that route.

### Are the seven Purple Puzzles the final seven puzzles in the game?

Not necessarily.

They are the seven new post-game challenges, but normal puzzles skipped earlier can still remain unfinished.

### Should I start a new save after Big Goodbye?

No. I continue the same active save when pursuing the Purple Puzzles, remaining cleanup, final key, and Big Game.

<h2 id="complete-route">The full Big Walk route I use</h2>

For a normal first run, I keep the whole game in four phases.

**Phase 1 — Open the island**

Tutorial → four Heads → opening key → drawbridge.

**Phase 2 — Build the transport network**

Red / Map Room → Green / Chairlift → Yellow / Tunnel → Blue / Train.

**Phase 3 — Finish the main journey**

Black Tower → Black Key → Wall → Black Sphere → Big Goodbye.

**Phase 4 — Complete everything**

Seven Purple Puzzles → remaining normal puzzles → final key → hidden completion route → Big Game.

Whenever I become unsure what to do next, I first ask which of those four phases the save is currently in.

That is more reliable than following whatever puzzle happens to be closest.
