Big Walk is a cooperative adventure built around navigation, physical puzzle
rewards, shared equipment, and communication. The route I now use for a clean
first progression run is:

**four tutorial Heads → Red Tower and Map Room → Green Tower and Chairlift
→ Yellow Tower and Tunnel → Blue Tower and Train.**

This is not the only valid colored-tower order. I use Yellow before Blue because
the underground Tunnel creates a useful route toward the Blue side. The island
also contains more challenges than each receiver requires, and Head rewards are
not locked to the color of the nearest tower.

<h2 id="progression">
  Use receiver counts and physical Heads as the progression tracker
</h2>

Each major route repeats the same structure:

1. complete practical nearby challenges;
2. collect the physical Head rewards;
3. return the required number to the active receiver;
4. protect the blank key;
5. complete every cutter in its chain;
6. use the finished key at the next major system.

The current published route covers:

- **4 tutorial Heads** for the drawbridge;
- **5 Red Heads** for the Map Room route;
- **5 Green Heads** for the Green receiver;
- **5 Yellow Heads** for the Tunnel route;
- **5 Blue Heads** for the Train route.

That is **24 submitted Heads** through Train activation.

A completed puzzle does not automatically increase the tower count. I still need
to locate the physical reward, name its carrier, return it, and insert it into the
receiver.

Heads are also not color-locked. The colored regions are route anchors, not item
restrictions. When one nearby challenge is blocking the group, I use a different
completed Head instead of forcing that puzzle for the current receiver.

<h2 id="opening-route">
  Finish the four-Head opening route before chasing island equipment
</h2>

The tutorial has one objective: fill the four-slot beach receiver, shape the first
blank key through four cutters, and open the drawbridge.

I return each Head as soon as the route passes the receiver. I do not carry all
four while searching for an exit or collect optional equipment before the bridge
is open.

<h2 id="red-tower-route">
  Use the Train Station and red beacon for the Red Tower route
</h2>

After the drawbridge, I use two landmarks:

- the inactive Train Station as a staging and regrouping point;
- the red blinking beacon as the direction toward the Red Tower.

The Red receiver needs five Heads. In a two-player session, I return after three
instead of trying to hold all five loose rewards. The last two then become a short
cleanup loop.

The Red key opens the Map Room only after all five cutters are complete. Reaching
the upper red door with a partly shaped key is the main reason the key appears to
fit but does nothing.

<h2 id="map-room">
  Use the Map Room to plan, not to carry every navigation tool
</h2>

The large island model shows tower relationships, transport routes, completed
challenge flags, and unfinished locations without flags.

I choose one navigation tool for the next route:

- portable map for multi-region travel;
- Compass when the destination has a stable direction;
- Laser Pointer when the group needs to mark a distant structure.

I leave unused tools at a named staging point. A light or communication item is
often more valuable than a second navigation tool inside a separated-room puzzle.

<h2 id="green-route">
  Complete Green Tower and activate the Chairlift
</h2>

I use the inactive Train Station as the Green Head cache, protect the first three
rewards, and return five physical Heads to the Green receiver.

A two-player group with one Backpack can move four Heads in one load. The fifth
waits at the Train Station until the final receiver run.

The blank Green Key is not the end of the route. I assign it to one carrier,
finish all five cutter stations, and take the completed key to the Chairlift
station beside Green Tower rather than returning to the Train Station.

Inserting the finished key starts the Chairlift. Boarding a moving seat unlocks
Big Sit and opens the upper transport route used before traveling toward Yellow.

<h2 id="yellow-route">
  Ride the active Green Chairlift toward Yellow
</h2>

After Green progression activates the Chairlift, I return to the station beside
Green Tower and take the route toward the tallest mountain.

My first Yellow return is after two Heads. That protects early progress and leaves
three rewards for the longer beach loop.

The main Yellow card points are:

- stop random inputs in the separated button rooms;
- check underneath low structures before stacking players upward;
- use arm raises for the seven-position signal puzzle;
- describe one of the five sound prompts at a time;
- keep one permanent Yellow key carrier through all five cutters.

The finished key opens the underground Tunnel. I cross the threshold before
treating the route as complete.

<h2 id="blue-route">
  Use the Tunnel or Map Room route to finish Blue and activate the Train
</h2>

The Yellow Tunnel shortens travel toward Blue, but it does not complete the Blue
receiver. I leave the underground route, recover high ground, and locate the blue
beacon.

The Blue receiver needs five Heads. I choose challenges that fit the current
group instead of forcing one fixed list. The published route includes symbol
relay rooms, shape transfer, pose matching, pot-height coding, and the blindfold
course.

After all five Heads are inserted, I keep the blank Blue key with one carrier.
The key passes through five cutters. One cutter is easy to miss because it sits
close to the route beside or below a rock; when an arrow appears to point too far
away, I search the nearest lower ground before continuing.

The final key activates the Train loop. **Big Ride triggers after I board a moving
carriage**, not when I merely reach the station or stand beside an inactive Train.

<h2 id="multiplayer">
  Assign fixed multiplayer roles and use Flares only at stable landmarks
</h2>

Big Walk supports groups of **2–12 players**, but larger groups need fewer
leaders, not more.

Before a long route, I assign:

- one route leader;
- one Head and key carrier;
- one light carrier;
- one communication lead.

Extra players become puzzle runners and object carriers.

I fire a Flare from a stable landmark such as a tower, station, or puzzle
entrance. I stop using it during blindfold, sound, and symbol communication
because the noise and surrounding chatter can cover the next instruction.

After a disconnect, I check Lost and Found, receiver progress, the latest key
location, Map Room flags, and the challenge reward point before replaying an
entire puzzle.

<h2 id="stuck">
  Big Walk progression fixes
</h2>

### The key fits but the door or transport does nothing

The key is still blank or partly shaped. Return to the latest cutter and follow
its arrow to the missing station.

### A solved puzzle did not increase the receiver count

Find the physical Head reward and insert it into the active receiver.

### We are forcing one puzzle because it is beside the tower

Leave it and use another completed Head. Rewards are not restricted to the nearest
tower color.

### The Chairlift route keeps taking us away from Yellow

Return to the Green Chairlift station, face toward the tallest mountain, and use
the left-side route toward Yellow.

### The Yellow Tunnel is open, but Blue is still blocked

The Tunnel is transportation. Exit toward Blue, find the beacon, and complete the
five-Head Blue route.

### The Train controls do nothing

Confirm five Blue Heads, five completed Blue key cuts, and final key insertion.
Finding the station alone does not activate the Train.

### We disconnected after finishing a puzzle

Check the completion flag and reward location before replaying the full challenge.
The puzzle may remain complete while its Head still needs to be recovered.

<h2 id="faq">
  Big Walk guide FAQ
</h2>

### Is there one fixed colored-tower order?

No. This hub uses Green → Yellow → Blue because the Yellow Tunnel shortens the
route toward Blue.

### Are Heads locked to a tower color?

No. I use any completed physical Head at the active receiver.

### Do I need to solve every puzzle near a tower?

No. Each receiver requires a fixed number of Heads while additional challenge
sites remain available.

### Does the Map Room activate the Chairlift, Tunnel, or Train?

No. The Map Room provides route and completion information. Each transport system
has its own tower and key progression route.

### Can the group continue without the original Host?

The Host owns the save and world progress. The same Host needs to start that save
again.

### Can Big Walk be played solo?

The selectable world versions begin at two players, and the route is built around
cooperative roles.
