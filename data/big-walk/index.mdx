Big Walk is a cooperative adventure built around navigation, physical puzzles,
shared objects, and communication. The clean early progression route is:

**four tutorial Heads → first key and drawbridge → five Red Tower Heads → Map
Room → five Green Heads → blank Green Key.**

The island contains more challenges than each receiver requires. I therefore use
the active receiver, the Train Station, and the Map Room as route anchors instead
of trying to clear every structure in sight.

<h2 id="progression">
  Use the receiver count as the main progression tracker
</h2>

The opening island teaches one repeatable structure:

1. complete nearby challenges;
2. collect the physical Head rewards;
3. return the required number to the active receiver;
4. take the blank key;
5. follow the complete cutter chain;
6. use the finished key at the next major route.

The early receiver counts are:

- **4 tutorial Heads** for the first key and drawbridge;
- **5 Red Tower Heads** for the Map Room key;
- **5 Green Heads** for the blank Green Key.

That produces **14 submitted Heads** through the Green route.

A solved challenge does not automatically increase receiver progress. I still
need to find the Head reward, protect it during the return, and insert it into the
correct structure.

I also do not assume that every visible challenge belongs to the current tower.
When the receiver needs one more Head and another nearby puzzle is easier for the
current group, I leave the blocked challenge and collect a different reward.

<h2 id="opening-route">
  Finish the four-Head opening route before chasing island equipment
</h2>

The tutorial area has one clear objective: fill the four-slot beach receiver and
open the drawbridge.

The four opening challenges teach the main puzzle language:

- one player may control an enclosure for another;
- a closed room can force visual communication;
- separated switches may need one countdown;
- and a blank key must pass through every cutter before it works.

I return each Head to the beach receiver instead of carrying all four while
searching for an exit. Once the complete key opens the drawbridge, I cross to the
far side and treat the opening route as finished.

<h2 id="red-tower-route">
  Use the Train Station and red beacon to organize the Red Tower route
</h2>

After the drawbridge, the island presents several colored structures, loose
equipment, inactive transport, and branching paths. I avoid navigating only by
memory.

The first two stable landmarks are:

- the **Train Station**, used as a staging and regrouping point;
- the **red blinking beacon**, used to identify the Red Tower direction.

The Red receiver needs five Heads. In a two-player session, I make the first
return after three rewards instead of trying to hold all five at once.

That early deposit:

- protects the first three Heads;
- frees hands for Walkie-talkies and lights;
- establishes the tower as a known return point;
- and reduces the number of rewards left beside unrelated buildings.

After the final two Heads are inserted, the receiver awards the second blank key.
The Map Room opens after that key completes all five cutters and reaches the upper
red entrance.

<h2 id="map-room">
  Use the Map Room to choose a route, not to carry every tool
</h2>

The Map Room changes the island from an open search into a planning problem.

The large model shows:

- tower and transport relationships;
- blue flags at completed challenge sites;
- unfinished locations without completion flags;
- and the broader shape of the island.

The room also contains a portable map, Compass, and Laser Pointers. I choose one
tool for the next problem instead of carrying all of them.

I take the portable map when the route crosses several regions or when I have not
learned the return path. I leave it at the Train Station when a tower beacon,
known rail line, or nearby structure already provides enough orientation.

The Map Room does not replace communication equipment. A Walkie-talkie or light
can be more valuable than the map when the next puzzle separates the group or
continues after dark.

<h2 id="green-route">
  Build the Green route around five physical Heads and one safe cache
</h2>

For the Green receiver, I return to the Train Station and use it as the central
cache.

The route does not require one rigid set of five challenges. I choose nearby
unfinished puzzles that fit the current group, equipment, and daylight.

The dedicated Puzzle Solutions page covers the longer mechanics, including:

- preserving a confirmed prefix in the Green Path order puzzle;
- agreeing on a number language before the counting-room door closes;
- leaving Heads and equipment outside the blindfold building;
- and assigning final positions before timed handoffs begin.

I protect the first three rewards before committing to the last two longer
challenges. Once five physical Heads are together, I stop exploring.

A two-player group with one Backpack can move four Heads in one load:

- one inside the Backpack;
- one in the same player's hands;
- two with the second player.

The fifth waits at the Train Station. I recover it, recount all five, and go
directly to the Green receiver.

Completing the receiver produces the blank Green Key and gives the group a clean
session checkpoint.

<h2 id="multiplayer">
  Multiplayer, Host saves, player-count versions, and communication
</h2>

Big Walk supports groups of **2–12 players**. The campaign depends on a Host save,
so I choose the Host based on who can reliably return for later sessions.

The Host also chooses a world version for:

- two players;
- three players;
- or four-plus players.

That selection changes challenge roles and controls. I choose the version for the
group that will actually complete the session, not for people who may join later.

The game uses proximity voice as part of its puzzle design:

- voices fade with distance;
- enclosed rooms can block normal communication;
- Walkie-talkies reconnect separated players;
- gestures and visible symbols matter when speech is unavailable.

I test communication before leaving the starting building. When the group stops
for the day, I finish the current receiver or wait for the new key to appear
before the Host ends the session.

Held and worn equipment returns through Lost and Found on the next start, so I do
not spend a long final trip carrying every tool back to the opening rooms.

<h2 id="stuck">
  Big Walk progression fixes
</h2>

### The drawbridge key fits but does nothing

The key is still blank or only partly shaped. Return to the latest cutter and
follow its arrow to the remaining station.

### We solved a puzzle, but the tower count did not change

Find the physical Head reward and carry it to the active receiver. Puzzle
completion and Head submission are separate actions.

### We keep returning to the tutorial coastline

Find the Train Station, face the red blinking beacon, and restart the Red Tower
route from those fixed landmarks.

### We have five Green Heads but cannot carry all of them

Move four in one two-player load and use the Train Station as the fifth-Head
cache.

### A held item disappeared after the Host ended the session

Check Lost and Found in the starting building. Held and worn equipment returns
there on the next start.

### Our puzzle has more controls than the guide describes

Check the world version selected by the Host. Three-player and four-plus worlds
can add roles or controls while keeping the same central mechanic.

<h2 id="faq">
  Big Walk guide FAQ
</h2>

### Is there one fixed tower order?

The opening drawbridge and Red Tower provide the clearest early route. After the
Map Room opens, the island offers more choice. This hub uses Green as the next
collection route.

### Do I need to solve every puzzle near a tower?

No. The Red and Green receivers each need five Heads while additional challenge
sites remain available.

### Can I play Big Walk solo?

The progression is built for cooperative roles. The selectable world versions
begin at two players.

### Can the group continue without the original Host?

The Host owns the save and world progress. The same Host needs to start that save
again.

### Does entering the Map Room activate the Train or Chairlift?

No. The room provides navigation and completion information. Transport systems
have their own progression routes.

### What should I do immediately after crossing the drawbridge?

Use the Train Station as the first regrouping point, locate the red blinking
beacon, and begin collecting five Heads for the Red Tower.
