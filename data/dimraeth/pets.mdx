<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
Companions become useful after **Myrll** joins the Sanctum, but each pet can still have its own unlock condition. The first route I could reproduce was **repair the broken bridge → reach the Beast-Occupied Ruins → defeat the Alpha Wolf → return to Myrll for the Fangra Cub**. After that, pet choice depends on the character: **Moon Cat** restores Concentration, **Carrion Parrot** adds Bleeding to enemies that are already bleeding, **Ember Hound** brings Fire Damage and Burning, and **Fangra Cub** mixes Strike Damage, Bleeding, and Chill. Pets also level, carry items, and can use Courier to send loot to Sanctum chests.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

<h2 id="unlock-pets">
  How to unlock companions through Myrll
</h2>

Dimraeth does not hand over a combat pet at the beginning of the game. My companion system opened through **Myrll**: after meeting her during story progression, finishing the request tied to her, and inviting her back to the Sanctum, her companion options became available.

That only opens the system. It does not automatically unlock every creature she can eventually offer.

The first individual pet unlock I could trace cleanly came later in the main route. After repairing the **broken bridge**, I reached the **Beast-Occupied Ruins** and killed the **Alpha Wolf** there. The game immediately gave me the pet unlock, and when I returned to Myrll the **Fangra Cub** was available through her companion trade.

The route was:

1. progress far enough to meet **Myrll**;
2. finish her request and invite her to the Sanctum;
3. continue the story until the **broken bridge** is repaired;
4. enter the **Beast-Occupied Ruins**;
5. defeat the **Alpha Wolf**;
6. return to Myrll and check the companion trade.

Ordinary wolves did not substitute for that Alpha Wolf encounter in the run I followed. If Myrll is already in the Sanctum but Fangra Cub is still missing, the Beast-Occupied Ruins boss is the first place I would check.

General recruitment, followers, and construction stay with the <Link href="/dimraeth/sanctum">Sanctum guide</Link>; Myrll matters here because she is the gateway into the companion system.

<h2 id="fangra-cub">
  Fangra Cub is an early combat pet with three damage effects
</h2>

The Fangra Cub immediately made it clear that Dimraeth pets are not cosmetic followers.

Its ability, **Savage Leap**, sends the cub through the nearest enemy and combines **Strike Damage**, **Bleeding**, and **Chill**. Myrll's trade showed the Fangra Cub at **50 G** when I obtained it.

That makes it a flexible first pet. It does not require a finished status build to contribute, yet Bleed or Chill characters can still get extra value from the effects it already applies.

I would not read the custom name shown on a pet as its species. The Fangra Cub I later used was named **Nero**, but searches, unlocks, and companion comparisons should still be based on **Fangra Cub**.

<h2 id="choose-pet">
  Pet abilities matter more once the character has a clear build
</h2>

Early on, almost any combat companion is an upgrade over having none. That changes after the build starts revolving around a particular resource or status.

The pets I tested filled noticeably different jobs:

- **Moon Cat** supports repeated spellcasting by restoring Concentration.
- **Carrion Parrot** extends Bleed pressure when enemies are already bleeding.
- **Ember Hound** adds Fire Damage and Burning.
- **Fangra Cub** supplies a broader mix of Strike Damage, Bleeding, and Chill.

There was also a defensive companion option whose effect granted a damage-absorbing barrier for roughly **three seconds** after the player took damage. I would value that kind of pet very differently from an offensive companion before a boss where surviving one bad exchange matters more than adding another small damage source.

The result is that there is no useful universal answer to “best pet” yet. A pet can be excellent on one character and replaceable on another because the ability is interacting with a different problem.

<h2 id="moon-cat">
  Moon Cat can become part of a caster's resource economy
</h2>

The **Moon Cat** was the cleanest match for my Shadow caster because its contribution was not another damage type.

**Astral Purr** remains near the player and restores **Concentration over two seconds**. The companion screen I captured for Gizmo showed **Concentration 20**, and the pet started at **Level 1 with 0/100 XP**.

That fitted a Shadow already spending Concentration on Poison, Decay, and repeated Corruption casts. A small amount of extra companion damage would not have changed the rotation very much; another source of Concentration did.

The effect also scaled as the Moon Cat gained levels. When **Gizmo** reached a higher level, the amount restored by Astral Purr increased. That gives pet levels real combat value rather than making XP a cosmetic progression bar.

<h2 id="carrion-parrot">
  Carrion Parrot is the Bleed companion for the Brawler
</h2>

The bird I used on the Brawler is a **Carrion Parrot**. My companion's custom name
was **Beansprout**, but for searches, unlocks, and companion comparisons, the name
that matters is the species name: **Carrion Parrot**.

Its ability is **Carrion Sweep**.

When it sees a **Bleeding** enemy, the effect adds **+1 Bleeding** to that target and **+2 Bleeding** to one nearby enemy. The Level 1 companion screen I captured showed **17/100 XP** and a **Bleeding 2** stat.

That trigger is why it stayed with my Minotaur Brawler after the build moved toward dedicated Bleed. The character was already creating the condition Carrion Sweep wanted, so the pet strengthened an existing loop instead of asking for another damage package.

Against groups, the nearby-enemy portion is particularly useful because the parrot can spread extra pressure without forcing the Brawler to abandon the primary target.

The companion is not a substitute for the Brawler's own Bleed engine. It works because that engine already exists. The full Physique, Agility, Frenzied Slashes, and status progression belongs in the <Link href="/dimraeth/builds#brawler-transition">Bleed Brawler section</Link>.

<h2 id="ember-hound">
  Ember Hound gives Fire builds a much more direct offensive pet
</h2>

**Ember Hound** takes the opposite approach. Instead of waiting for a status the character has already applied, it brings an aggressive Fire attack of its own.

The companion dashes through enemies in a short line and leaves a **Fire pit** at the destination, dealing **Fire Damage** and applying **Burning**. Myrll's trade showed it at **50 G**.

It was immediately tempting because the attack is visually and mechanically obvious. I still kept the Carrion Parrot on the Bleed Brawler because the bird was amplifying the status the rest of the character already cared about.

On a Fire-focused character, that decision could reverse. Ember Hound becomes more interesting when Burning already interacts with the skills, equipment, or passives being used elsewhere in the build.

<h2 id="pet-levels">
  Companion levels can improve the ability, and Level 4 showed Max Level on Fangra Cub
</h2>

Pet XP has a practical effect. Moon Cat's Concentration restoration improved as Gizmo levelled, so replacing a developed support pet with a fresh Level 1 companion is not always an upgrade even when the new ability looks more dramatic.

The clearest cap indicator I captured came from the Fangra Cub. **Nero was Level 4 and the interface displayed `XP: Max Level`.**

I would treat that as an observed current-build result, not as proof that every companion will permanently share the same cap throughout Early Access. Balance and pet progression can still change.

What is already useful is the immediate consequence: if a companion is central to the build, repeatedly swapping it out slows the development of the pet that is actually contributing every fight.

<h2 id="pet-inventory">
  Courier can extend an exploration run without teleporting home
</h2>

Combat is only half of what made pets useful.

Companions have their **own inventory**, and the pet interface also exposes **Courier**. That combination matters once Wildwood starts filling the character inventory with equipment, crafting materials, and boss drops.

With Nero selected, pressing **Courier** opened the Sanctum chest list rather than simply deleting the items from the field. The captured capacities were:

- **ALDER35 — 14/18**
- **BRAMBLE2 — 18/18**
- **FENNEL87 — 6/18**
- **RUSTY50 — 18/18**

That tells me exactly where the limitation sits. Courier can prevent a nearly full character inventory from ending the route, but it cannot create storage space that does not exist. A destination already showing `18/18` is full, so the practical choice is one of the chests that still has room.

This also keeps the ownership boundary clean between the two systems: the pet owns **Courier and its carried inventory**; storage construction and chest management belong to the <Link href="/dimraeth/sanctum#storage">Sanctum</Link>.

  Pet Pen stores companions; passive XP is not confirmed
</h2>

Until the XP behavior is repeatable, I equip a pet when I specifically want that companion to gain combat progress rather than assuming time spent in the Pen will do the same job.

<h2 id="which-pet">
  Which pet should you use?
</h2>

The choice becomes easier when I start with the problem rather than the pet list.

A Shadow running out of Concentration has a direct reason to take **Moon Cat**. A Brawler already stacking Bleed gets an immediate interaction from **Carrion Parrot**. A Fire setup can make better use of **Ember Hound** than a build with no Burning support, while **Fangra Cub** remains a flexible option when Strike Damage, Bleeding, and Chill can all contribute.

A defensive barrier companion becomes more attractive when a boss is exposing survivability rather than damage.

Once the active companion is levelled and already fixing the weakness that matters, another pet is mostly a new option rather than an urgent upgrade. Myrll can keep offering companions without every stock change becoming a reason to abandon the one that is already working.

If I am collecting pets for completion rather than for the current build, the goal changes. Pet unlocks also feed into the game's broader completion milestones, so I track those requirements in the <Link href="/dimraeth/achievements">Dimraeth achievements guide</Link> instead of choosing companions around them here.
