<h2 id="current-classes">
  How do classes and races work in Dimraeth?
</h2>

Character creation gives you three current class choices — **Magician, Brawler, and Shadow** — and three races — **Human, Minotaur, and Elf**. You may also see Magician called **Mage** in older Early Access wording or player discussions; they are referring to the same class, so there is not a separate fourth Mage class.

The more important choice is the combination. Race is not cosmetic: pairing a class with Human, Minotaur, or Elf can change the resulting archetype, starting weapon setup, abilities, stat interactions, and the direction available to that character.

For example, choosing Magician does not always produce the same caster. **Human + Magician becomes Elemental Ranger**, while **Minotaur + Magician becomes Earthshaker**. One starts from sword-and-crossbow flexibility, while the other combines magic with a battle axe.

I would choose from the **race + class pair**, not from the class name by itself. Race can change the archetype, weapon setup, stat interactions, and the Skill Tree surrounding that class. A Human Magician carrying a sword and crossbow is not simply a different-looking version of a Minotaur Magician carrying a battle axe; they begin with different tools and develop toward different combat loops.

<h2 id="same-class-different-race">
  The same class can become a completely different character
</h2>

Magician gives the clearest example.

The **Human Magician / Elemental Ranger** creation screen shows a **sword and crossbow** setup with **Fireball, Blizzard, Weapon Swap, and Aimed Shot**. Its listed combat themes are **Hunter, Blade, Fire, and Frost**, and Adventure contributes additional Magic Damage.

That gives the Human version a practical ranged escape route. I can use the crossbow when standing inside melee range is unnecessary, then move back into sword or spell pressure when the situation allows it.

The Minotaur version changes much more than the character model.

This is why I would not pick “Magician” first and assume the race can be decided later. The race is already changing what that Magician is built to do.

<h2 id="best-starting-class">
  What is the best starting class in Dimraeth?
</h2>

For a first character, the most forgiving option I have found is **Minotaur Brawler / Horned Vanguard**.

The Level 1 character sheet is useful here because it shows exactly what the build starts with before training or equipment starts changing the numbers:

- **Memory 4**
- **Intelligence 4**
- **Physical 8**
- **Strength 10**
- **Adventure 6**
- **Charisma 4**
- **Agility 4**
- **Energy 8**

The starting sheet already lines up with what Horned Vanguard needs: **10 Strength, 8 Physical, and 8 Energy**, backed by a large axe that hits hard, reaches across multiple nearby enemies, and creates useful stagger pressure. That combination made ordinary early packs much easier for me to handle without waiting for a particular spellbook, equipment set, or status interaction to make the character function.

That matters more on a first run than having the highest theoretical damage setup. Early progression repeatedly puts normal enemy groups between quests, gathering routes, and new objectives, and wide melee coverage lets Horned Vanguard deal with those fights before specialized gear arrives.

Brawler can then become more specialized instead of needing its final setup from Level 1. Physical damage carries the opening, while Rage and Bleed interactions become more important as the right skills arrive. <h2 id="human">
  When should you choose Human?
</h2>

Human becomes much more attractive when I want to control distance instead of relying on the Minotaur's heavier melee coverage.

The Elemental Ranger shows the clearest reason: **Weapon Swap** gives the Human Magician access to both sword and crossbow play. That means an enemy does not have to be inside my melee range before I can keep applying pressure.

I value that more against enemies whose close-range attacks force repeated disengagement. Instead of waiting through the entire dangerous window, the crossbow gives me something productive to do while repositioning.

The tradeoff is visible when several weaker enemies collapse onto the character at once. A large Minotaur swing can solve that situation with raw coverage; the Human route asks me to use spacing, ranged attacks, and abilities more deliberately.

So I would choose Human when **range and flexibility are part of the plan**, not because it is automatically the safer version of every class.

<h2 id="minotaur">
  When should you choose Minotaur?
</h2>

Minotaur makes the most sense when I already know that I want heavier attacks and a Strength-oriented start.

Horned Vanguard begins with **10 Strength**, and Earthshaker demonstrates that Strength can remain relevant even outside a pure physical Brawler: its archetype passive converts Strength into additional Magic Damage.

That makes the race more flexible than “pick Minotaur only for melee” suggests.

What stays consistent is the heavier feel. The Minotaur combinations I used are more comfortable when I want to stand my ground, hit several nearby enemies, and get value from the large weapon rather than constantly creating distance.

For a new character where I do not yet know which drops or spellbooks I will find, that dependable basic combat is why Horned Vanguard remains my preferred starting point.

<h2 id="elf">
  When should you choose Elf?
</h2>

Elf moves in the opposite direction. The weapon feel is faster, and the combinations I saw lean much more naturally toward mobility, repeated effects, and status interactions.

**Elf Shadow / Night's Whisper** is a good example because the character screen already tells you what kind of fights it wants.

Its listed themes are **Twin Daggers, Time Magic, Shadow Arts, and Plague Magic**. The character begins with tools including **Vanishing Strike, Backstep Strike, and Chrono Spike**, while its racial interaction gives **Agility an additional 2.5 Stamina**.

That creates a very different early rhythm from Horned Vanguard. I am moving more, using shorter attack windows, and getting more value from abilities that reposition the character or continue affecting an enemy after the initial hit.

The downside appears in crowded fights. Fast daggers do not automatically reproduce the wide area covered by a Minotaur axe, so positioning matters more when several enemies reach me at once.

Elf is the race I would choose when that faster, more active combat style is the reason I am making the character in the first place.

<h2 id="class-differences">
  Which class should you pair with your race?
</h2>

Once the race is chosen, the three classes provide the second half of the character's direction.

**Brawler** is the easiest class for me to recommend when the goal is dependable direct combat. It fits particularly well with Horned Vanguard because the Minotaur's starting stats and weapon already support the kind of fights Brawler wants to take.

**Magician** has the largest identity shift between the examples I checked. Elemental Ranger uses sword, crossbow, Fire, and Frost, while Earthshaker combines a battle axe with Earth, Fire, and Frost. I would therefore decide which Magician I want from the resulting archetype rather than from the word “Magician.”

**Shadow** makes more sense when I want movement and status interactions to be central to combat. Night's Whisper demonstrates that clearly through Vanishing Strike, Backstep Strike, Chrono Spike, twin daggers, and plague-oriented magic.

The class still matters, but it is only half of the choice.

<h2 id="skill-tree-impact">
  Does race change the Skill Tree?
</h2>

Yes. Race and class both affect the tree you receive, so two characters using the same class can still have different routes available around them.

That is important when copying someone else's build, because “Brawler” or “Shadow” by itself is not enough information to guarantee that your board matches theirs.

Before spending heavily, I check that the other character is using the same race/class combination. The actual node types, pathing, Skill Point progression, search function, and respec process are covered in the <Link href="/dimraeth/skill-tree">Dimraeth Skill Tree guide</Link> rather than repeating the whole system here.

<h2 id="which-class">
  Which Dimraeth class should you choose?
</h2>

For my first progression character, I would still choose **Minotaur Brawler / Horned Vanguard**. Starting at **10 Strength, 8 Physical, and 8 Energy**, it already has the stat profile and weapon style needed to make ordinary early combat comfortable before specialized gear arrives.

I would move to **Human** when ranged pressure is something I actively want to use, especially with Elemental Ranger's sword-and-crossbow setup.

I would choose **Elf** when fast attacks, mobility, and status-heavy abilities sound more appealing than wide melee coverage. Night's Whisper is a much better fit for that player than trying to force Horned Vanguard into the same rhythm.

After choosing the character, I would stop rerolling and actually move into progression. The opening route in the <Link href="/dimraeth/walkthrough">Dimraeth walkthrough</Link> gives the character enough real fights, XP, skills, and equipment to show whether the combination is working before I judge it from the creation screen alone.
