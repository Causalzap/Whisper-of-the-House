<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
Build the Sanctum around jobs that save time outside it. **Storage and a Water Well** pay off early, recruited NPCs open more of the settlement, and the **Facility Center** matters more than finishing the house. Once production facilities become available, assign followers instead of assuming every finished structure runs on its own. Before a difficult push, come home once to unload, collect production, rest, spend upgrades that are already within reach, and leave again. **Home Uplift is not a prerequisite for advancing the current story.**
</GuideQuickAnswer>

<h2 id="what-to-build-first">
  Build around the trips you no longer want to make
</h2>

The Sanctum can consume a lot of time without improving the next hour of play. New foundations, walls, furniture, crafting stations, storage, and production options arrive close enough together that treating every unlock as an immediate project turns the base into its own grind.

The better early question is practical: **what am I repeatedly leaving the field to fix?**

If the answer is a full backpack, add storage. If water keeps becoming another thing to collect manually, build the well. If a resource is needed in batches and a follower can produce it while I am away, that production chain is worth materials that would otherwise disappear into the house.

Early on, I want the Sanctum to handle four jobs reliably:

* hold materials and equipment that do not need to stay on the character;
* restore the character and organize supplies before a difficult route;
* produce frequently used resources in the background;
* turn recruited NPCs and followers into useful progression rather than extra bodies standing around the base.

Once those jobs are covered, a story objective usually gives me more immediate value than another round of construction.

<h2 id="storage">
  Add storage before encumbrance starts deciding when you have to go home
</h2>

Inventory pressure shows up well before the Sanctum feels finished. Wildwood alone can fill the character with equipment sets, crafting materials, food, spell-related items, and boss drops quickly enough that carrying everything “just in case” becomes its own progression problem.

A materials chest solved more than simple clutter. Building can draw from stored resources, so planks, stone, and other construction supplies do not all need to sit in the character inventory while I work around the Sanctum. That makes storage part of the crafting loop rather than a place for forgotten items.

I eventually added a **second materials chest** because one general pile had stopped being convenient. Gear that might become useful after a build change could stay separate from the raw materials I was constantly consuming, while construction could still use what had already been deposited.

That is also the point where I stop trying to solve encumbrance by constantly deciding what to destroy in the field. If the backpack is forcing bad loot decisions before the route is finished, the Sanctum is due for a visit.

<h2 id="water-well">
  A Water Well removes one small chore from every later trip
</h2>

The Water Well does exactly what its description promises: it **periodically produces water for collection**.

Nothing about that sounds dramatic, which is precisely why it is useful. Water is a recurring requirement rather than a one-time unlock, so passive supply keeps paying back the materials long after the structure is placed.

I build it before decorative expansion. The well can keep producing while I am progressing quests, fighting bosses, or gathering something more valuable; another wall cannot.

That same test becomes useful for later facilities. Once construction starts asking for larger batches of planks, stone, nails, ingredients, and upgrade materials, background production becomes increasingly valuable because it overlaps with time I was going to spend outside anyway.

<h2 id="recruit-npcs">
  Recruit offers are worth interrupting a route for
</h2>

The Sanctum changes character once NPCs begin moving in.

The first recruitment opportunity appeared after progress around the Lost Child section. An NPC offered to join, and the game updated to **Recruit your first NPC to the Sanctum**.

The useful part is not assuming that one particular quest always produces the same recruitment timing. What matters is recognizing the prompt when it appears. Once somebody can move into the Sanctum, I accept before heading back into another long objective chain.

Later recruits behaved similarly: finish the relevant relationship or quest step, accept the move, then check the Sanctum again. A new resident can matter to crafting, production, quests, facilities, or follower management, so this is one of the few base events that can justify changing plans immediately.

<h2 id="facility-center">
  The Facility Center matters more than finishing the house
</h2>

The **Facility Center** was the unlock that made me stop treating every construction option as roughly equal.

Its description identifies it as the **central hub for upgrading and repairing facilities in your base**. That places it in a completely different category from another piece of flooring or an unfinished section of wall: it expands what the working Sanctum can do.

Materials can delay it. **Stone Nails** were one early bottleneck, while later construction steps began asking for planks I had not stockpiled. That changed how I handled raw resources: instead of converting or spending everything as soon as it entered storage, I started keeping an eye on the next functional upgrade.

If one component is missing, there is little value in standing around the Sanctum rearranging other pieces. I note the missing material, go back outside, and gather it while advancing something else.

<h2 id="followers">
  A production building can be finished and still produce nothing
</h2>

Facilities and automation are not the same thing. Some Sanctum production needs a **follower assigned to the job**, and that is easy to overlook when the structure itself already appears complete.

I first noticed this after checking a facility that looked ready but was not producing anything useful. The missing step was the worker, not another copy of the building.

Before spending more materials, I check whether the facility actually has somebody assigned to run it.

Follower management becomes more valuable as the base grows because several ordinary resources can compete for the same workforce. Spreading workers evenly is not always the best answer.

If pine keeps delaying planks, pine production deserves attention. If stone is blocking the next facility, the quarry has more value. I move followers toward whichever ordinary material is currently interrupting progression most often.

That keeps the Sanctum focused on removing repetitive gathering rather than producing a little of everything.

<h2 id="passive-resources">
  Use passive production for materials you consume over and over
</h2>

Once the Sanctum began generating resources such as **Pine** and opened the path toward a **Stone Quarry**, returning home stopped being only an inventory reset.

Construction repeatedly consumes basic materials in quantities large enough to make manual gathering noticeable. Producing some of that supply while completing quests changes the cost of future upgrades: the resource arrives without replacing a story route or boss attempt.

The best candidates share the same pattern:

* they are consumed frequently rather than once;
* gathering them manually pulls me away from something more valuable;
* the Sanctum can generate them while I am already occupied elsewhere.

That distinction matters. Passive production is excellent at shrinking repetitive construction chores; it does not replace targeted farming for rare drops or boss materials.

<h2 id="home-uplift">
  Home Uplift does not need to hold the story hostage
</h2>

**Home Uplift** looks important enough to tempt a completionist into finishing every construction step before moving on.

The story does not require that approach.

I still had **Build the Pinewood Foundation in the Sanctum** unfinished when the active route had already advanced to **Clear the Corrupt Zone to Open the Way**. Clearing the corruption opened the next progression path despite the incomplete house.

That separates two kinds of progress that happen to coexist in the same quest log. The house can continue growing, but its unfinished foundation does not automatically mean the campaign is blocked.

When the Sanctum already provides storage, preparation, and the production I currently need, I follow the story instead of forcing Home Uplift to completion. The wider campaign order is covered in the <Link href="/dimraeth/walkthrough">main progression walkthrough</Link>.

<h2 id="when-to-return">
  Come back when one trip can fix several problems
</h2>

Returning after every quest wastes almost as much time as refusing to return at all. The useful middle ground is to let several reasons accumulate, then handle them in one visit.

I normally head back when two or more of these are happening at once:

* inventory weight is starting to affect what I can carry;
* a newly recruited NPC has moved to the Sanctum;
* stored materials now cover a meaningful facility or production upgrade;
* a serious fight is next and my supplies need a reset;
* passive production should have finished enough material to unblock construction.

That turns the Sanctum into a pit stop instead of a detour.

<h2 id="pre-boss-reset">
  Before a major boss, make one clean reset instead of farming at random
</h2>

A difficult fight is the clearest reason to use the Sanctum deliberately. I do not come home to rebuild the character from scratch; I come home to make sure resources already earned are actually helping.

A good reset is compact. Unload the backpack, collect production, store equipment I may want later, rest to restore the supplies that refill through rest, spend training or upgrades that are already affordable, and leave with enough free inventory space for the route and its rewards.

What I avoid is turning this into an hour of speculative farming. If the character already handles normal enemies on the approach, finishing one immediately available upgrade is usually more useful than chasing an incomplete set several drops away.

Fight-specific preparation still belongs with the fight itself. A boss that pressures Poison, Bleed, adds, or another particular mechanic may justify a different consumable or loadout. The Sanctum's job is to send the character back out in a clean state; the encounter determines what that state needs to contain.

<h2 id="multiplayer-sanctum">
  In co-op, build the expensive version of the Sanctum in the world you actually use
</h2>

Sanctum investment changes meaning in multiplayer because buildings belong to the **world**, not to the travelling character.

A regular group therefore benefits from deciding where its shared infrastructure will live before pouring resources into several separate settlements. If one player normally hosts the campaign, concentrating the Facility Center, production, followers, and recruited NPCs there creates a useful home much faster than reproducing partial versions across multiple worlds.

Characters can still bring their own progression into another world, but the world itself retains its story state and built environment. The full split between those two types of progress is covered in the <Link href="/dimraeth/co-op-progression">co-op progression guide</Link>.

For the Sanctum, the practical rule is simple:

**Put expensive infrastructure in the world the group expects to keep advancing.**
