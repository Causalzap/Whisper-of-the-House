<GuideQuickAnswer title="What Should I Build First?">
Get the **Workbench** running first, then put down a **Pinewood Chest** so materials stop filling your backpack. After that, prioritize recurring utility such as the **Water Well**, useful crafting stations, and production facilities. Do not hold up the main story just to finish the Pinewood house.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

<h2 id="what-to-build-first">
  What should you build first in the Dimraeth Sanctum?
</h2>

My early Sanctum order is driven by what removes the next bottleneck rather than by how complete the settlement looks.

The first important chain is:

**Workbench → storage → recurring utility → production**

The Workbench matters first because it breaks an awkward material loop that can otherwise make the opening base quest look impossible. Storage comes immediately after because carrying wood, stone, planks, nails, equipment, food, and quest loot back and forth wastes both time and carry weight. Once those two pieces are working, a Water Well and additional crafting or production facilities begin saving time while I am away from the Sanctum.

I leave walls, flooring, and larger house projects behind those practical upgrades unless a quest specifically needs them.

<h2 id="pinewood-planks">
  How do you get Pinewood Planks before you have a Workbench?
</h2>

This is the first real Sanctum trap.

The Workbench itself requires **2 Pinewood Planks**, but the Workbench is also what lets you turn Pine into Pinewood Planks afterward. If you arrive at the building menu with no planks, it can look like a circular recipe with no way forward.

Before the Workbench exists, I get the first planks from exploration loot. I found them among ordinary world pickups such as **chests, bodies, and searchable spots in logs**. If I have been collecting the highlighted loot during the opening route, I check the town stash before going back out to search again.

Once the Workbench is down, the problem changes completely. Pine can now be processed into Pinewood Planks, so I no longer depend on random world pickups every time another structure asks for planks.

That is why I resist spending the first few Pinewood Planks on anything decorative. Until the Workbench exists, those planks are the key that unlocks repeatable production.

<h2 id="storage">
  Build a Pinewood Chest before encumbrance starts controlling the route
</h2>

After the Workbench, I move straight to storage.

The early game starts throwing several types of items into the same limited inventory: equipment, crafting materials, food, spell-related items, boss drops, wood, stone, and building components. Carrying everything back into the field eventually means leaving useful loot behind simply because the backpack is full.

A **Pinewood Chest** is the first fix I want. If Nails are the missing component, they can be crafted from Stone rather than waiting for another lucky pickup.

The useful part is not just having somewhere to dump items. Materials stored at the Sanctum can be used while building, so I do not need to load every plank, stone, and construction component back onto the character before placing another structure.

Once one chest becomes messy, I split storage by job. My early setup usually ends up with separate space for:

- frequently used crafting materials;
- food and consumables;
- equipment or set pieces I may actually use later.

I do not keep every unusual drop indefinitely. Storage buys time to make better decisions; it should not become a second inventory filled with items that have no planned use.

<h2 id="water-well">
  The Water Well is valuable because it keeps working while you are gone
</h2>

The **Water Well periodically produces Water for collection**.

That sounds less exciting than another crafting station, but it solves exactly the kind of chore I want the Sanctum to absorb. Water is something I can need repeatedly, while the well keeps generating it during quests, exploration, and combat.

I use the same standard for later facilities: a structure deserves materials sooner when it removes a repeated gathering trip or produces something I consume regularly.

A wall or floor improves the settlement visually. A working production structure can save time on every later construction project.

<h2 id="crafting-stations">
  Build the stations that solve the next problem, not every station at once
</h2>

After the Workbench and storage are stable, crafting expands quickly.

The **Alchemy Table** is one of the first stations that gave me an immediate combat reason to invest further. Better healing preparation matters before difficult bosses, so this is a useful place to spend materials when the next progression step is combat-heavy.

The **Woodfire Stove** becomes part of the same working crafting area rather than something I scatter far across the base. I keep stations close enough that unloading materials, crafting, and leaving again takes one short circuit through the Sanctum.

There is a practical limit to packing them together, though. If several interactable objects overlap too tightly, selecting the station I actually want becomes annoying. I leave enough space to target each one cleanly.

The important decision is timing. When I am still missing the materials for a meaningful station, I go back out instead of trying to make the Sanctum feel finished with unrelated construction.

<h2 id="recruit-npcs">
  Accept Sanctum recruits when the option appears
</h2>

NPC recruitment changes the base more than another piece of furniture.

The first time I reached a recruitment opportunity, the objective updated to **Recruit your first NPC to the Sanctum**. Later recruits followed the same general pattern: progress their quest or interaction far enough, accept the move, then check what changed back home.

I do not assume that every new resident will immediately solve the same type of problem. Some matter more for crafting, some for quests or services, and others connect to production or follower management.

What matters during progression is noticing the recruitment prompt instead of leaving the NPC behind and then wondering why the settlement still feels empty several quests later.

The longer recruitment count is tracked in the <Link href="/dimraeth/achievements">Dimraeth achievements guide</Link>; here I care about what each new resident actually adds to the working Sanctum.

<h2 id="facility-center">
  Prioritize the Facility Center over finishing the house
</h2>

The **Facility Center** changed how I valued later construction.

Its role is to support the **upgrading and repairing of facilities**, which means it improves the systems already doing useful work rather than simply expanding the footprint of the house.

This is also where careless material spending begins to hurt. **Stone Nails** can become a bottleneck, and later structures demand enough planks and other basics that using everything as soon as it enters storage leaves the next important project one component short.

When that happens, I stop building. I note what is missing and return to quests or gathering until I can finish the upgrade in one visit.

<h2 id="followers">
  A finished production building may still need a follower
</h2>

One of the easiest mistakes is assuming that construction equals production.

Some facilities need a **follower assigned to the job** before they begin producing useful resources. I first noticed this when a completed structure sat there doing nothing; the missing step was a worker, not another building.

As more production opens, I do not spread followers evenly just because every facility has an empty slot. I assign them around the resource currently slowing me down.

When Pine is repeatedly delaying planks, Pine production gets priority. When Stone is holding up the next facility, the Stone side becomes more valuable. The best assignment can therefore change as the next construction target changes.

That is much more useful than producing small amounts of several materials I am not currently short on.

<h2 id="passive-resources">
  Let the Sanctum produce the materials that keep interrupting progression
</h2>

Once Pine production and facilities such as the **Stone Quarry** enter the settlement, returning home starts doing more than clearing inventory.

The materials I care most about automating have three things in common: I use them often, gathering them manually repeatedly pulls me off the current route, and the settlement can produce them while I am doing something else.

Pine, Stone, and Water fit that pattern much better than rare boss drops.

This changes how I look at construction costs. A large batch of basic materials is less painful when some of it accumulates during a quest chain instead of requiring a separate gathering session every time.

Rare or highly specific items still need targeted farming. The Sanctum is most effective at removing the repetitive part of construction, not replacing every resource hunt in the game.

<h2 id="home-uplift">
  Do you need to finish Home Uplift before continuing the story?
</h2>

No.

I still had **Build the Pinewood Foundation in the Sanctum** unfinished when my active progression had already reached **Clear the Corrupt Zone to Open the Way**. Clearing that corruption continued the route even though the house objective remained incomplete.

That was the point where I stopped treating every Sanctum quest as a gate on the campaign.

The Pinewood house can continue growing when I have spare resources, but I do not delay a working character just because a foundation objective is still sitting in the quest log. Once storage, crafting, and the production I currently need are functioning, I go back to the <Link href="/dimraeth/walkthrough">main Dimraeth progression route</Link>.

<h2 id="when-to-return">
  Return when the same trip can solve several problems
</h2>

Going home after every small objective creates too much dead travel. Staying outside until the character is overloaded and underprepared causes the opposite problem.

I usually return when several needs line up at once: my carry weight is beginning to force loot decisions, a recruited NPC should now be at the Sanctum, passive production has had time to accumulate, stored materials can finish a useful facility, or the next route ends in a difficult encounter.

That turns one visit into a proper reset.

I unload materials and equipment first, collect whatever production has finished, check whether a worker needs moving, complete any upgrade that is already affordable, and then leave enough empty inventory for the next area's drops.

Before a boss, I also make sure the crafting side of the settlement has already done what it can. The Sanctum should prepare the character for the encounter; the exact boss mechanics and loadout decisions still depend on the fight itself.

<h2 id="multiplayer-sanctum">
  Where should you build the Sanctum in co-op?
</h2>

In multiplayer, expensive Sanctum infrastructure is most useful in the **world your group actually keeps progressing**.

Buildings and the settlement belong to the world rather than following a travelling character into every host's save. If one player normally hosts the campaign, duplicating a Facility Center, production chain, followers, and recruited residents across several worlds spreads the same resources across multiple incomplete settlements.

For a regular group, I concentrate the expensive setup in the world we expect to keep using.

Character progress and world progress do not behave identically in co-op, so I keep the larger save and hosting rules in the <Link href="/dimraeth/co-op-progression">Dimraeth co-op progression guide</Link> rather than treating the Sanctum as the answer to every multiplayer progression question.
