<GuideQuickAnswer title="Which World Should We Use for Co-op?">
Use the world belonging to the player whose **story and Sanctum you want the group to advance**. Characters can bring their level, equipment, inventory, spells, and personal progression into another player's world, while story state, recruited NPCs, and buildings stay with the world being hosted. Dimraeth supports up to **8 players**.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

<h2 id="how-multiplayer-works">
  How does multiplayer progression work in Dimraeth?
</h2>

The easiest way to understand Dimraeth co-op is to separate the things attached to your **character** from the things attached to the **world**.

Your character can continue gaining levels, collecting equipment, carrying inventory, and learning spells regardless of which friend's world you enter. The campaign state is different. Story progress, recruited residents, and the Sanctum belong to the world where those things happened.

That distinction matters almost immediately for a regular group. Joining a friend's campaign with a developed character does not copy your own settlement or story state into their save, and completing several hours of construction in their world does not build the same Sanctum in yours.

For a one-night session, that difference is easy to ignore. For a group planning to keep playing together, I decide early which world is going to carry the shared campaign.

<h2 id="hosting">
  Who should host a long-term Dimraeth co-op game?
</h2>

I use the world of the player who is most likely to keep hosting the group.

Dimraeth supports as many as **8 players in the same hosted world**, but the important resource is not the available player count. It is the accumulated world progress: quests completed there, NPCs recruited there, and buildings constructed there.

If four players spend an evening expanding one Sanctum, recruiting NPCs, and clearing story objectives, the result is one much more developed world. Switching to another player's untouched world next session means returning to that world's earlier campaign and settlement state.

That is not a problem when the group intentionally wants separate saves. It becomes frustrating only when everyone assumed the same world progress would appear everywhere.

For the group I would rather have one clearly established campaign world than several half-built copies competing for the same playtime.

<h2 id="what-carries-over">
  What does your character keep when joining another world?
</h2>

Character-bound progress is the part I am comfortable investing in between group sessions.

The character can bring:

- its **level**;
- equipped and stored-on-character **gear**;
- **inventory**;
- learned **spells**;
- other progression attached directly to that character.

That means a character I have already spent hours developing does not need to be rebuilt simply because a friend is hosting.

A Shadow character can arrive with its current spell setup and equipment already in place. A Brawler can keep the gear and abilities it has been developing instead of beginning again at Level 1.

Items being carried by the character also remain useful when moving between worlds. That is different from spending those materials on a structure, because the completed structure belongs to the world where it was built.

The detailed attribute, equipment, and combat choices remain the same ones described in the <Link href="/dimraeth/builds">Dimraeth builds guide</Link>; multiplayer does not require rebuilding a working character from scratch.

<h2 id="what-stays-in-world">
  What progress stays with the host's world?
</h2>

Three things are especially important to remember:

**story progress, recruited NPCs, and buildings.**

If the group opens a major story route in one player's world, that changes that world's campaign state. If an NPC is recruited there, the recruit becomes part of that settlement. If everyone contributes materials toward the Sanctum, those completed facilities remain in that world.

The Sanctum is where this becomes expensive enough to notice. A Facility Center, production buildings, followers, storage, and recruited residents take enough time and materials that reproducing the same setup across several hosts can slow the group down.

For that reason, I concentrate the expensive settlement work in the world we actually intend to keep advancing. The building order itself is covered in the <Link href="/dimraeth/sanctum">Dimraeth Sanctum guide</Link>.

<h2 id="existing-character">
  Can you use an existing character in a friend's new world?
</h2>

Yes. A developed character can enter a world that is still much earlier in the campaign.

The host's world does not automatically jump forward to match that character. I can arrive with stronger equipment and more abilities while the story is still dealing with early objectives.

That is useful when I am helping a friend who started later. I do not need to abandon the character I already enjoy simply because their campaign is behind mine.

There is one tradeoff: an established character can overpower the opening sections.

When I want to help someone move quickly, that is useful. When the goal is for both of us to experience the early difficulty and learn encounters together, bringing an advanced character can flatten too much of the combat.

Starting fresh is therefore a playstyle choice, not a requirement for joining an early world.

<h2 id="host-offline">
  What happens when the host goes offline?
</h2>

The hosted world depends on its host being online.

When the host leaves the multiplayer session, the other players do not keep that world running as a permanently available shared server. The session closes with the host.

That makes availability worth considering before choosing the group's main world. A player who can only host occasionally may not be the best place for every piece of shared infrastructure if the rest of the group wants to play much more often.

Two schedules work well for different groups.

When everyone mostly plays together, one person keeps the main campaign world and hosts whenever the group meets. When schedules vary heavily, each player can continue developing their own character and world between sessions, then bring those characters together when the regular host is available.

Because character progression travels, solo play between group sessions does not mean throwing away those hours.

<h2 id="sanctum-coop">
  Which Sanctum should a co-op group invest in?
</h2>

I put the expensive version in the world the group expects to keep using.

Splitting early materials across several versions of the same settlement can leave everybody with partial storage, incomplete production, fewer upgraded facilities, and repeated recruitment work. One developed home gives the regular group a more useful place to return between long routes.

That does not prevent anybody from building their own Sanctum for solo play. It simply means I do not assume that contributing to a friend's base is also progressing mine.

Once the host is chosen, I treat Sanctum materials as world investment. Character gear and inventory remain portable; buildings do not.

<h2 id="support-builds">
  Co-op makes healing and defensive spells much more useful
</h2>

A larger party does not need every player filling another damage slot.

I found support-oriented spells that become much more interesting once allies are standing beside me. **Combat Medic** provides a healing-oriented option, while **Barrier** can place a protective shield on another character.

<h2 id="deeds-coop">
  When is adding more players especially useful?
</h2>

Normal campaign progress can be played solo, so I do not treat multiplayer as a requirement for getting through the story.

The repeatable Deed fights are where another player starts solving a more obvious combat problem. A tougher version of a familiar encounter lasts longer and gives dangerous attacks more chances to land. Another character can split pressure, add a different status effect, provide defensive support, or keep the run alive after one mistake.

I still fix obvious character weaknesses before using party size as the answer. If an early Deed already consumes every healing resource and the build struggles against ordinary enemies on the way there, adding more players can hide that weakness without fixing it.

The actual tiers, rewards, boss progression, and decisions about how far to push are covered in the <Link href="/dimraeth/deeds-bounties">Dimraeth Deeds & Bounties guide</Link>.

<h2 id="solo-and-coop">
  How do I combine solo progress with a regular co-op campaign?
</h2>

I keep developing my character when the group is offline and preserve one stable world for the shared campaign.

Those two activities work together because they are advancing different things. Solo time can improve levels, spells, inventory, and equipment. When everyone meets again, that same character can enter the group's world without requiring another full character grind.

The shared world then continues handling the things that need consistency: story progression, recruited residents, and the developed Sanctum.

For my group, that creates a simple rhythm. Personal character progress can happen wherever I am playing; campaign and settlement progress happens in the world we agreed to keep using.

That avoids the two multiplayer mistakes that cost the most time: repeatedly rebuilding the same settlement under different hosts, and avoiding useful solo progression because everyone assumes the group must always advance at exactly the same pace.
