<h2
  id="story-leveling"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Let Quests and Level Milestones Stack Together
</h2>

A large part of the list clears while I am simply moving forward.

**A New Start** comes from The First Steps, **Help a Lost Elf** from Time Tardy
Scout, **Village Hero** from Village in Crisis, and **Timber!** from The
Corrupted Treant. Later progression continues through **The Broken Bridge**,
**Bread and Blood**, **Hoard King's Fall**, **Opening the Throne Path**, and
**Crown of Thorns**.

The level achievements rise alongside those quests:

- **Novice** — reach Level 5
- **Adept** — reach Level 10
- **Veteran** — reach Level 15
- **Chaptered** — reach Level 20
- **Seasoned Veteran** — reach Level 25

I keep moving through quests, map exploration, side objectives, and combat
instead of stopping at one of those milestones to farm XP in place. The same
activity can advance the level counter, uncover another quest, expose a
mini-boss, or move a Deed at the same time.

**Light Reading** also belongs in this early flow. It only requires the first
ruined spellbook. Once that unlock fires, later spellbooks should be chosen
because they help the character rather than because the achievement needs more
of them.

When the route itself is the problem, I return to the
<Link href="/dimraeth/walkthrough"> Dimraeth walkthrough</Link> and keep the
achievement list in the background.

<h2
  id="sanctum-building"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Build Toward Workshop Before Spending Materials on Extras
</h2>

Several achievements start with the Sanctum, but they do not all ask for the
same amount of investment.

**MacGyvered It** only needs one crafted item. **Tools of the Trade** needs the
first crafting station. Both arrive early enough that I do not spend valuable
materials trying to force them.

The first meaningful building checkpoint is **Workshop**, which specifically
requires:

- a **Workbench**;
- an **Alchemy Table**;
- a **Woodfire Stove**.

The Workbench is the important first investment because it changes the early
material loop. Once it is running, Pinewood can be processed into the planks
needed for further construction. That is more useful than spending the same
early materials on decoration or structures that do not move crafting forward.

The long building target is **Industrialist**, which requires **7 different
production buildings**. Repeating one useful building does not solve a
requirement that explicitly asks for different types.

Recruitment grows alongside the same progression. **A New Ally** fires after
the first NPC joins the Sanctum, while **Sanctum Society** needs **5 NPCs**.
I keep completing the quests that actually bring people home instead of trying
to treat recruitment as an isolated counter.

**Wayfarer's Gate** only requires one built Waypoint. I place that Waypoint
where it improves travel rather than dropping it somewhere inconvenient simply
to trigger the achievement.

The full Pinewood Plank, storage, crafting, production, and recruitment order is
in the <Link href="/dimraeth/sanctum">Dimraeth Sanctum guide</Link>.

<h2
  id="upgrades"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Save Maximum Upgrades for Equipment You Actually Intend to Keep
</h2>

Three achievements can burn resources unnecessarily if I chase them as soon as
they appear.

**Tinkerer** needs one combat-item upgrade.

**Permanent Edge** needs a combat item upgraded to its maximum level.

**Patron** needs a facility upgraded to its maximum level.

The first upgrade is cheap progress. The two maximum-level goals are where I
become selective.

A temporary weapon can earn Tinkerer without much commitment. I would not keep
feeding that same item materials just to reach Permanent Edge if I already know
it will be replaced.

The facility choice follows the same logic. I wait until there is a facility I
am repeatedly using, then let Patron reward an upgrade that was already helping
the save.

That prevents the achievement list from turning upgrade materials into a
separate tax.

  One Pet Starts the Chain; Nine Lives Needs the Collection to Reach Nine
</h2>

Pet achievements have a short target and a much longer one:

**Faithful Companion** — unlock **1 pet**.

**Nine Lives** — unlock **9 pets**.

Once the first pet is unlocked, I stop treating every companion as an urgent
achievement target. I continue normal exploration and keep track of which
unlocks are still missing.

That distinction matters because a character may already have the pet it wants
for combat long before Nine Lives is finished. My Bleed setup, for example,
can make practical use of Carrion Parrot, but the achievement only cares about
the total number unlocked.

The actual pet unlocks, functions, leveling, and combat choices are in the

**Cutting Teeth** asks for one Tiered Deed completed at **Tier 1**.

That is only the beginning of the system. **Tier Pusher** requires a Tiered
Deed to be pushed to **Tier 3 and completed**.

The late combat counter is **Bounty Hunter**:

**Defeat 10 different mini-bosses.**

The word **different** is the important part. Repeating a convenient mini-boss
may still be useful for loot, but it does not replace another unique kill on
this counter.

When I am down to the last few Deeds or mini-bosses, I use the
<Link href="/dimraeth/deeds-bounties">Dimraeth Deeds & Bounties guide</Link>
to check the progression instead of repeatedly clearing the same target.

<h2
  id="side-quests"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Local Help Is Easier When Side Quests Travel With the Main Route
</h2>

**First Favor** asks for one side quest.

**Local Help** raises that requirement to **25 side quests**.

Twenty-five is large enough that leaving every optional quest until the end
creates unnecessary travel. When I enter a town or return after a major quest,
I check NPCs before leaving again.

That does not mean I immediately chase every marker across the map. I accept
what is available, finish the objectives already close to the route I am
taking, and leave distant ones until progression carries me back toward them.

This also keeps the Level 20 and Level 25 milestones moving while I am doing
something more useful than farming one enemy group for XP.

<h2
  id="bosses"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  Boss Achievements Only Need the Kill
</h2>

Several named enemies have their own achievements:

**Blob Slayer** — kill the monstrous droop.

**Long Live the King** — defeat the Goblin King.

**The Hart of the Forest** — defeat Aelwynor.

**Uprooted** — defeat Woldroot the Path Guardian.

I treat those as combat checkpoints. There is no reason to repeat a complete
boss strategy here when the achievement itself only needs the kill.

If the Goblin King is stopping progression, I use the
<Link href="/dimraeth/goblin-king">Goblin King guide</Link>. For Aelwynor, I
use the <Link href="/dimraeth/forest-king">Forest King guide</Link>.

Once the required boss is dead, I move on. Any repeat kills after that are for
loot, progression, or build testing rather than another achievement count.

<h2
  id="cleanup-order"
  className="scroll-mt-24"
>
  My Cleanup Order for 37 / 37
</h2>

By the time the main progression slows down, I want the remaining list to be
mostly long counters rather than unfinished early systems.

I clean them up in this order:

1. Finish any remaining quest-related achievements while continuing toward
   **Level 25**.
2. Complete enough side quests to bring **Local Help** to **25**.
3. Finish the Sanctum targets: **5 recruited NPCs**, Workshop's three required
   stations, one maxed facility, a Waypoint, and **7 different production
   buildings**.
4. Max a combat item I actually intend to keep for **Permanent Edge**.
5. Bring the pet collection to **9**.
6. Push one Tiered Deed through **Tier 3** and complete it.
7. Check which unique mini-bosses are still missing and reach **10 different**
   kills for Bounty Hunter.

That order leaves the most resource-heavy goals until I understand which
equipment, facilities, pets, and activities are still useful to the character.
It also prevents me from maxing temporary gear or constructing unnecessary
buildings simply because one unfinished achievement is visible.
