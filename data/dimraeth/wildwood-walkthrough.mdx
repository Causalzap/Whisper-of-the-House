<GuideQuickAnswer title="Quick Answer">
Wildwood progression follows a much narrower route than the quest log suggests. The main sequence is **Northern Crossing → Goblin storyline → Goblin King → Clear the Way / Keys of Bark and Stone / Light the Wayfinder Line → Crown of Thorns → Forest Path → Forest King**. If a marker looks close but terrain blocks every direct approach, look for the route into that section instead of clearing nearby events. After the Goblin King, keep following the forest story; the Bounty Board is useful, but it does not replace the remaining campaign.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

<h2 id="wildwood-route">
  The quest log is bigger than the route that actually moves Wildwood forward
</h2>

Wildwood is the first stretch of Dimraeth where following whichever objective happens to be nearest can waste a surprising amount of time. Town-board jobs, dynamic events, bounties, NPC requests, and the region's story quests can all remain active together, yet only a few of them control access to the next part of the forest.

The route becomes much easier to read if I track its major transitions instead of every icon:

**Northern Crossing → Goblin King → Opening the Throne Path → Crown of Thorns / Forest King.**

Everything else can still be worth doing for XP, materials, relationships, or gear, but it stops being my default answer when a story gate refuses to move. When progression stalls, I first ask which of those transitions has not happened yet.

That distinction becomes increasingly important later in the region. Wildwood has enough optional combat to keep a character busy long after the next required objective is already available, and after the Goblin King the repeatable systems become tempting at exactly the wrong moment for anyone trying to finish the current story first.

<h2 id="lost-child">
  Lost Child and Northern Crossing are the same navigation problem
</h2>

**Find the Lost Child of the Forest** looks simple on the map because the objective marker appears close. The terrain tells a different story.

On my run, walking toward the child put me on the wrong side of the surrounding land. I doubled back, circled the area, and spent almost **three minutes** finding the approach that actually led inside. The useful clue was the other active objective: **Search for the Northern Crossing**.

I stopped treating those as separate tasks. The child is the destination; Northern Crossing explains how to reach that destination.

That gives this objective a reliable failure check. If the marker sits nearby but a wall, ridge, or closed piece of terrain keeps forcing me away, I do not start looking for a key or assume an enemy encounter failed to trigger. I am approaching the area from the wrong side.

Once the correct entrance is found, the confusing part is finished. I speak to the Lost Boy, complete the local interaction, and continue with the forest rather than combing the pocket for an additional progression trigger.

<h2 id="wildwood-quest-log">
  Optional Wildwood activity can be useful without being part of the story gate
</h2>

The crowded quest log matters because Wildwood does not visually separate “good thing to do” from “thing required to open the next route” as strongly as I would like.

An **Alpha Wolf Bounty II** can solve a material problem. A dynamic event can be worthwhile when the character is close to an attribute or skill breakpoint. NPC quests can improve relationships and eventually feed other systems. None of those facts make them automatic prerequisites for the forest story.

I therefore stop using completion count as a measure of progress. If a named story objective is available, I follow that objective until the route changes. Optional work comes back into consideration when it solves a concrete need rather than simply because it is visible.

That approach also keeps underpowered and lost from becoming the same problem. If normal enemies are manageable but I cannot find the destination, more farming is unlikely to help. If I know exactly where to go and ordinary packs are exhausting most of my healing, then the route is solved and the character needs attention instead. The <Link href="/dimraeth/builds">builds guide</Link> covers those damage, attribute, and equipment decisions in more depth.

<h2 id="goblin-hideout">
  Finding the Goblin Hideout is a route problem before it becomes a combat problem
</h2>

The goblin section can stall before the King ever enters the picture. **Find the Goblin Hideout** sends the search through a part of Wildwood where nearby caves, walls, and paths make it easy to believe the objective must be reached from the wrong direction.

The important change is the quest state. While the objective still says **Find the Goblin Hideout**, I am solving navigation. Once it becomes **Clear the Raid**, the location has already been found and wandering farther around the map will not move the quest.

The complete landmark sequence and wrong turns are covered in the <Link href="/dimraeth/goblin-hideout">Goblin Hideout route</Link>. From there, the goblin chain continues toward the explosives objective and the King himself.

<h2 id="goblin-king">
  Killing the Goblin King opens the next phase; it does not close Wildwood
</h2>

The Goblin King has the scale of a regional finale, and the game immediately offers enough other activity afterward to reinforce that impression. It is still only a major transition inside the larger Wildwood chain.

The goblin progression eventually reaches **collect the explosives from the camp**, followed by the King encounter. Once that fight is won, I return to the forward progression and forest objectives instead of treating the victory as a signal to settle into repeatable content.

The dedicated <Link href="/dimraeth/goblin-king">Goblin King guide</Link> covers the fight itself, including the attack rhythm, adds, healing pressure, and what to do if the boss is available but the character cannot finish the encounter.

For the regional route, the important result is simpler: **the forest story continues after the King dies**.

This is where the Bounty and Deed systems can pull attention sideways. They become valuable for targeted materials and repeat encounters, but while the campaign still points toward the blocked forest route, I treat them as optional power-building rather than the new main path.

<h2 id="opening-throne-path">
  Opening the Throne Path comes down to three named quests
</h2>

The next major Wildwood gate becomes much less ambiguous once **Opening the Throne Path** appears. Instead of requiring a vague percentage of regional completion, its tracker identifies three specific objectives:

1. **Clear the Way**
2. **Keys of Bark and Stone**
3. **Light the Wayfinder Line**

From this point, the quest tracker is more useful than the density of icons around it. If one of the three remains incomplete, that is where I put my attention. Clearing a bounty, another pack of enemies, or an unrelated NPC task does not substitute for the missing requirement.

If all three requirements are finished and the path still has not visibly advanced, I go back through the corresponding quest handoff rather than assuming the character needs another level. At that stage, progression depends on finishing the chain cleanly, not on increasing combat power.

This part of Wildwood felt much less confusing once I stopped asking “what else is there to do here?” and started asking “which of these three named requirements has not resolved?”

<h2 id="crown-of-thorns">
  Crown of Thorns marks the moment the route stops being a checklist
</h2>

After the throne requirements are dealt with, **Crown of Thorns** provides the clearest confirmation that the region has entered its final story stretch.

The objective changes to:

**Confront the Forest King**

with the immediate task:

**Unlock the Door — Unlock the path to the Forest King.**

This is the point where I stop checking whether another village job, bounty, or dynamic event might secretly be mandatory. The destination is now explicit.

I also avoid starting another long farming cycle simply because one more upgrade would be possible. A single preparation stop makes sense before the Forest Path; open-ended farming does not unless the character is already struggling badly against ordinary enemies.

<h2 id="before-forest-path">
  Leave the Sanctum with the build you already earned, then test it on the approach
</h2>

Before the Forest Path, I made one deliberate return to the Sanctum. The purpose was not to rebuild the character or finish every facility. I used what was already available: rest, stored materials, training, equipment upgrades, and enough inventory space to make the next run clean.

On the Brawler I had been developing, **Agility reached 20** before this push. That number belonged to that build and that run; it is not a universal Forest King requirement. A Shadow, another race, or a differently constructed Brawler can arrive with different priorities.

The useful part is converting resources that are already sitting unused before committing to the final route. The <Link href="/dimraeth/sanctum#pre-boss-reset">Sanctum preparation section</Link> covers the broader reset routine, so there is no reason to recreate a full base checklist here.

I leave when the character is rested, the obvious affordable upgrades are finished, and the next destination is still the Forest Path. If the plan has turned into “farm indefinitely until the build feels perfect,” I have usually gone further than the story requires.

<h2 id="forest-path">
  Forest Path is both the route to the boss and the last practical readiness test
</h2>

The structure of Wildwood changes again after entering the **Forest Path**. Earlier objectives encouraged moving between scattered places; this stretch is much more linear and combat-focused.

I pay more attention to resource loss here than to a recommended character level. If the normal enemies on the approach are taking only a reasonable amount of healing, I keep moving. If several ordinary encounters have already drained the character before the arena is close, that tells me more than an arbitrary level threshold would.

Dying has a real cost to momentum. The character returns to the nearest campsite, enemies repopulate, and consumed charges need another rest before they are restored. Repeating the approach with the same weakness therefore wastes more than the boss attempt itself.

That makes an early retreat reasonable when the run is clearly collapsing. Arriving at the Forest King with most resources available is a better sign of readiness than reaching the door simply because I refused to turn around.

<h2 id="forest-king">
  Forest King ends the current Wildwood story chain
</h2>

Once the Forest King encounter is available, the regional navigation problem is finished. The remaining challenge is the fight.

The <Link href="/dimraeth/forest-king">Forest King guide</Link> covers the encounter in detail, including Poison management, extra enemies, recovery after a bad opening, and the post-fight rewards. For the Wildwood route, the important change happens after the victory: the game no longer supplies another obvious mandatory forest objective.

That is the end of the current linear Wildwood story progression, not the end of the character.

Side quests, NPC relationships, Sanctum development, build refinement, and repeatable encounters can all continue. If the goal shifts toward replaying bosses, raising difficulty, or farming specific materials, the <Link href="/dimraeth/deeds-bounties">Deeds and Bounties guide</Link> picks up from there.

If the goal is simply to know whether another hidden Wildwood entrance still needs to be found after the Forest King, the answer is no. The region's current main-story chain has already delivered its final destination.
