<h2 id="main-progression">
  The story is easier to follow when every activity is not treated as equal
</h2>

Dimraeth starts filling the map with useful distractions long before the main route becomes difficult. Town-board jobs can provide XP and materials, dynamic events feed other progression systems, NPC requests unlock relationships and recruits, and the Sanctum can absorb as much time as I am willing to give it.

None of those systems reliably tells me where the campaign goes next.

For a first clear, I keep one progression spine in mind:

1. Progress the main objectives around **Earlwood**.
2. Follow the story into **Wildwood**.
3. Resolve the **Lost Child / Northern Crossing** route.
4. Move through the goblin storyline and locate the **Goblin Hideout**.
5. Reach **Hoard King's Fall** and defeat the **Goblin King**.
6. Return to the forest storyline and open the blocked route toward the throne.
7. Finish the requirements leading into **Crown of Thorns**.
8. Enter the **Forest Path**.
9. Defeat **Aelwynor the Wildroot Father**, the Forest King.
10. Shift into Deeds, Bounties, side quests, NPC progression, Sanctum work, and build farming.

The important part is not completing every icon between those stages. I take optional content when it solves a current problem; otherwise, I leave it available for later.

<h2 id="when-to-leave-route">
  Three different problems can stop progression, and they need different fixes
</h2>

When the campaign slows down, I first work out what kind of problem I actually have.

**If the destination is unclear**, more levels usually will not help. A marker sitting nearby behind impassable terrain, an objective that refuses to update, or several plausible paths all point toward a navigation problem.

**If the destination is obvious but ordinary enemies are consuming most of my healing**, the route is probably fine and the character needs attention. That is when an attribute breakpoint, spell upgrade, equipment change, or cleaner damage loop becomes worthwhile. The <Link href="/dimraeth/builds">builds guide</Link> owns those decisions.

**If inventory weight, empty consumables, or stored upgrade materials are becoming the limiting factor**, I make one useful Sanctum visit rather than returning after every quest. Storage, facilities, followers, production, and the broader reset routine stay in the <Link href="/dimraeth/sanctum">Sanctum guide</Link>.

Separating those three cases prevents a lot of wasted farming. Being lost can feel like being underpowered when both result in standing in the same part of Wildwood for too long, but the solutions are completely different.

<h2 id="earlwood-to-wildwood">
  Stay on the main objective until Wildwood turns the map into part of the puzzle
</h2>

The early Earlwood stretch introduces enough systems that it is tempting to stop whenever something new becomes available. I keep moving as long as the active story objective is clear and normal combat still feels comfortable.

Wildwood is where that approach first needs adjustment.

The **Lost Child** objective looks geographically close before the correct route is obvious. The paired **Northern Crossing** objective is the clue that matters: if the child marker appears nearby but the terrain keeps blocking a direct approach, the problem is the entrance rather than a missing key, hidden interaction, or combat trigger.

I only need that conclusion here. The actual approach, the wrong side of the terrain, and the route into the enclosed section belong to the <Link href="/dimraeth/wildwood-walkthrough#lost-child">Wildwood route</Link>.

Once that section is resolved, I continue following the named forest objectives instead of cleaning up every remaining local activity.

<h2 id="goblin-story">
  The goblin chain has two separate checkpoints: finding the Hideout and reaching the King
</h2>

The goblin storyline can feel longer than expected because the Goblin King is discussed before the game is ready to send me directly to him.

The first genuine route problem is **Find the Goblin Hideout**. If that text is still active, I am not at the boss stage yet. The search around Wildwood Center has its own misleading branch through Wildwood Caverns, so I use the <Link href="/dimraeth/goblin-hideout">Goblin Hideout route</Link> rather than treating every cave full of goblins as story progress.

The objective changing to **Clear the Raid** is the useful confirmation that the navigation problem has been solved. From there, the goblin chain continues rather than immediately dropping the player into the King arena.

Later, **Hoard King's Fall** provides the much stronger boss cue. Its objective sends me to **collect Tamsin's explosive from the camp**, which tells me the storyline has moved into the final setup for the Goblin King encounter.

At that point I stop detouring into optional events unless there is a concrete reason to do so. A material I need right now, an affordable upgrade, or a character that is clearly struggling on the approach can justify a break; an event being nearby is not enough by itself.

<h2 id="goblin-king">
  Goblin King is a campaign milestone, not the Early Access ending
</h2>

The Goblin King is the first encounter that can make the current campaign feel as though it has reached a finale. The story presentation is bigger, the preparation matters more, and repeatable systems start becoming more visible around the same stretch of progression.

For the main route, only two things matter here: arrive in a reasonable resource state, then defeat the boss.

The exact attack timing, add pressure, parry decisions, healing diagnosis, and Bleed examples belong to the <Link href="/dimraeth/goblin-king">Goblin King guide</Link>. I do not rebuild those mechanics inside the campaign route.

After the victory, I report back to **Corin** and continue with the forward forest objectives.

This is where the **Bounty / Deed Board** can create a false sense of handoff. Repeat fights are now useful, especially when I need a specific boss material or want to test a stronger version of the character, but the appearance of repeatable content does not mean the remaining Wildwood story has disappeared.

If the goal is still the first main-story clear, I keep moving.

<h2 id="blocked-forest">
  After the Goblin King, progression shifts from goblins to the Forest Throne
</h2>

The next section feels less direct because several systems are competing for attention at once. NPC conversations have changed, Deeds can be available, new build options may be within reach, and the forest itself is pushing toward a more corrupted area.

I follow the objectives tied to the **blocked forest path** and the **Forest Throne**.

Eventually, **Opening the Throne Path** makes the gate much easier to read by naming the three objectives that matter:

**Clear the Way**, **Keys of Bark and Stone**, and **Light the Wayfinder Line**.

That list is the point where I stop using general regional completion as a guide. If one of those three is unresolved, unrelated bounties and board quests are not substitutes for it.

The <Link href="/dimraeth/wildwood-walkthrough#opening-throne-path">Wildwood walkthrough</Link> keeps the full route, handoffs, and regional checkpoints together. For the main campaign, completing those requirements is what matters.

<h2 id="crown-of-thorns">
  Crown of Thorns is the signal to stop looking for another regional prerequisite
</h2>

Once the throne route is open, the campaign becomes much more explicit. **Crown of Thorns** advances toward **Confront the Forest King**, and the remaining path stops behaving like another scattered Wildwood checklist.

This is where I commit to the final push rather than returning to clear whichever side objective happens to be closest.

The transition into the **Forest Path** is also the best practical readiness check in this part of the game. I pay attention to what the approach is costing me. If ordinary enemies are still going down cleanly and most healing remains intact, I continue. If several normal encounters have already consumed the resources I expected to have for the boss, I turn around before turning one weak approach into several weak boss attempts.

That tells me more than chasing an arbitrary level target.

<h2 id="forest-king">
  Aelwynor is the final mandatory destination in the current story chain
</h2>

At the end of the route, **Aelwynor the Wildroot Father** is the Forest King the campaign has been building toward.

Once the encounter is active, Wildwood navigation is finished. Whatever happens next is a boss problem rather than another map problem.

Poison, the extra Fey, ground pressure, defensive timing, and the decision to preserve or reset an attempt all belong to the <Link href="/dimraeth/forest-king">Forest King guide</Link>. The campaign-level question is simpler: defeating Aelwynor completes the current linear Early Access story.

There is no additional mandatory forest entrance to discover after that victory.

<h2 id="after-story">
  After the Forest King, progression stops being one straight line
</h2>

The character remains fully playable after Aelwynor falls, but the game no longer hands me another obvious main-story destination.

That changes how I choose goals.

Repeatable bosses become more useful because I am no longer delaying a mandatory story route by farming them. NPC quests and relationships can be finished without wondering whether they are pulling me away from the next chapter. Sanctum upgrades have more room to become projects of their own, while unfinished builds can chase spellbooks, equipment, attributes, pets, and boss materials without competing with another immediate campaign objective.

For repeat bosses, targeted material farming, and higher difficulty, I move into the <Link href="/dimraeth/deeds-bounties">Deeds and Bounties guide</Link>.

If I have already defeated the Forest King and the question is simply **“Where is the next main quest?”**, there is no hidden continuation to search for in the current Early Access build. The campaign has reached its present endpoint.
