<GuideQuickAnswer title="What Should I Do Next?">
On a first run, keep the main route simple: **Earlwood → Wildwood → Goblin King → Forest Path → Aelwynor**. If you are lost, follow the <Link href="/dimraeth/walkthrough">Dimraeth walkthrough</Link>. If the route is clear but combat is failing, fix the character before pushing farther. After Aelwynor, shift your attention to Deeds, Bounties, pets, the Sanctum, achievements, and stronger builds.
</GuideQuickAnswer>

<h2 id="where-to-start">
  What should you do next in Dimraeth?
</h2>

Dimraeth opens several systems before the story is finished, so the quest log can make progression look less linear than it really is. A Deed can be available while a required story objective is still waiting. A Sanctum upgrade can look important without blocking the next region. A side quest can be worth doing even though it does not move the campaign forward.

When I am unsure what to do, I identify the thing that is actually stopping the run.

If I cannot tell **where to go**, I return to the <Link href="/dimraeth/walkthrough">main progression route</Link>. If the objective is somewhere inside Wildwood and the map is no longer enough, I narrow the problem to the <Link href="/dimraeth/wildwood-walkthrough">Wildwood route</Link>. If I already know the destination but ordinary enemies are exhausting healing and resources, the issue is probably the character rather than navigation.

That one distinction prevents a lot of wasted time. I do not clear every available activity just because it appeared; I solve the problem that is blocking the next useful step.

<h2 id="main-progression">
  What is the main story order?
</h2>

The current Early Access campaign has a clear backbone.

It begins around **Earlwood**, moves into **Wildwood**, follows the goblin storyline through the **Goblin King**, continues into **Forest Path**, and reaches its current major endpoint at **Aelwynor the Wildroot Father**.

The <Link href="/dimraeth/walkthrough">Dimraeth walkthrough</Link> keeps the full quest order together, including the points where an optional objective can be ignored for the moment and the places where a particular quest really does open the next route.

Wildwood is the section where that sequence becomes easiest to lose. Several objectives can coexist there, and they do not all move the campaign by the same amount.

<h2 id="wildwood">
  What if you are stuck in Wildwood?
</h2>

First check the wording of the active objective.

When the problem is the overall sequence through Northern Crossing, Lost Child, the later corrupted forest, and the path toward the throne, use the <Link href="/dimraeth/wildwood-walkthrough">Wildwood walkthrough</Link>.

When the objective specifically says **Find the Goblin Hideout**, the useful answer is much narrower. The <Link href="/dimraeth/goblin-hideout">Goblin Hideout route</Link> starts from Wildwood Center and gets past the misleading turn that can send you toward the wrong cave.

Once the Hideout is found and the raid stage begins, retracing that route no longer helps. The next problem is now farther along the quest chain.

<h2 id="bosses">
  Which bosses matter for the current story?
</h2>

Two fights mark the biggest campaign transitions.

The **Goblin King** closes the goblin portion of Wildwood. Reaching **Hoard King's Fall** and obtaining **Tamsin's explosive** are the signals that the search-and-raid portion has moved into the boss stage. The <Link href="/dimraeth/goblin-king">Goblin King fight</Link> covers the encounter and what opens afterward.

That is not the end of the story.

The forest route continues to **Aelwynor the Wildroot Father**. When that fight becomes the blocker, the <Link href="/dimraeth/forest-king">Forest King fight</Link> covers the mechanics and preparation without requiring another detour through unrelated progression systems.

Dimraeth also has other bosses and repeatable encounters, but Goblin King and Aelwynor are different because defeating them changes where the main campaign goes next.

<h2 id="classes">
  Which class and race should you start with?
</h2>

Dimraeth currently combines **three classes with three races**, and the resulting character is more specific than the class name alone suggests.

A Human Magician does not start the same way as a Minotaur Magician, and a Minotaur Brawler has a very different opening rhythm from an Elf Shadow. I choose the combination according to the weapon feel and combat loop I actually want rather than picking “Magician,” “Brawler,” or “Shadow” in isolation.

The <Link href="/dimraeth/classes">Dimraeth classes comparison</Link> shows the confirmed archetypes, starting stats, weapons, abilities, and the differences between Human, Minotaur, and Elf combinations.

<h2 id="difficulty">
  Which difficulty should you choose?
</h2>

World difficulty is a choice I would make carefully when creating the world. I have not found a way to change that setting afterward, so I would not choose Hard simply because I expect better progression rewards later.

For a first character, **Normal is the safer starting point** while learning bosses, attributes, Spellbooks, and the Skill Tree. Hard is mainly useful when I specifically want tougher combat; I have not seen a clear extra loot or XP reward that makes it the better progression choice by default.

This is separate from **Deed difficulty**. Deeds have their own repeatable difficulty levels and reward structure, which become much more relevant later; the <Link href="/dimraeth/deeds-bounties">Deeds & Bounties guide</Link> covers that system.


<h2 id="skill-tree">
  Why do you have XP but no Skill Points?
</h2>

More kills are not always the answer.

Dimraeth separates earning XP from turning that XP into character levels and Skill Points. If the XP total is rising while the Skill Tree still shows no points to spend, return to a place where the character can train.

The <Link href="/dimraeth/skill-tree">Dimraeth Skill Tree guide</Link> explains the training step, connected paths, tree Search, race and class differences, and how to respec a route that no longer fits the character.

<h2 id="spells">
  Why did you unlock a spell but still cannot use it?
</h2>

Not every spell comes from the Skill Tree. Some appear through Spellbook recipes, exploration, NPC crafting options, and boss progression, and a newly unlocked recipe can still require materials or specific attributes before the spell is usable.

If you found a Spellbook recipe but nothing appeared on the ability bar, or a book is still blocked by its requirements, use the <Link href="/dimraeth/spells">Dimraeth spells and Spellbooks guide</Link> for the crafting sequence, attribute gates, world finds, vendor options, and boss spell unlocks.

<h2 id="builds">
  When should you stop following the story and fix your build?
</h2>

I pay attention to how much each ordinary fight is costing me.

A character that still clears normal packs cleanly does not need to abandon progression every time a new item drops. A character that reaches every boss with empty healing, poor resource recovery, or several abilities pulling in unrelated directions probably does.

The <Link href="/dimraeth/builds">Dimraeth builds guide</Link> follows complete Shadow and Minotaur Brawler examples through the point where attributes, skills, equipment, statuses, and resource recovery begin reinforcing the same combat loop.

For a first run, that is more useful than changing the entire character every time one new skill or set piece appears. If the build direction is clear but the spell itself is still locked, use the <Link href="/dimraeth/spells">spells and Spellbooks guide</Link> before changing the rest of the character.

<h2 id="pets">
  Are pets worth using?
</h2>

Yes. Pets can affect combat directly, and some also reduce how often inventory pressure forces a return home.

Different companions can add damage, support a status effect, help with resources, or solve a different weakness. The <Link href="/dimraeth/pets">Dimraeth pets guide</Link> covers their unlocks and abilities, including companions such as Fangra Cub, Moon Cat, Carrion Parrot, and Ember Hound.

I choose a pet according to what the character already does rather than treating one companion as the answer for every build.

<h2 id="sanctum">
  What should you build first in the Sanctum?
</h2>

The early priority is practical: get the **Workbench** working, add storage, then spend materials on facilities that reduce repeated gathering or preparation.

The first Pinewood Planks can be awkward because the Workbench itself is what makes later plank production easy. Once that hurdle is cleared, the Sanctum becomes much more useful as a place to process materials, unload gear, collect passive resources, and prepare for the next route.

The <Link href="/dimraeth/sanctum">Dimraeth Sanctum guide</Link> covers the Workbench, Pinewood Planks, storage, Water Well, crafting stations, NPC recruitment, followers, and Home Uplift.

Finishing the house is not something I use as a reason to stop a working story run.

<h2 id="coop">
  How does co-op progression work?
</h2>

Dimraeth supports up to **8 players**, but character progress and world progress do not travel in the same way.

Your character can bring its level, equipment, inventory, spells, and personal progression into another player's world. Story state, recruited NPCs, and buildings stay with the world where that progress happened.

For a regular group, that makes host choice important before everyone spends heavily on one Sanctum. The <Link href="/dimraeth/co-op-progression">Dimraeth co-op progression guide</Link> explains what carries over, what stays with the host world, what happens when the host goes offline, and how an established character behaves in an earlier campaign.

<h2 id="deeds">
  When should you start pushing Deeds?
</h2>

The Deed Board becomes useful before the story is over, but I do not automatically replace the campaign with repeat boss farming as soon as it appears.

Before Aelwynor, I run a Deed when there is a concrete reason for it: a useful reward, a needed boss material, or a character that genuinely needs another power step.

After the current story ends, that tradeoff changes. There is no longer another mandatory story destination competing for the same time, so pushing Deeds and clearing Bounties becomes much more natural.

The <Link href="/dimraeth/deeds-bounties">Dimraeth Deeds & Bounties guide</Link> covers the difficulty tiers, repeat fights, rewards, materials, and when a higher tier stops being worth forcing.

<h2 id="achievements">
  Should you worry about achievements on the first run?
</h2>

Not much.

Dimraeth currently has **37 achievements**, and a large part of the list advances through normal quests, leveling, crafting, boss kills, the first Deed, pets, and Sanctum progression.

I let those happen alongside the campaign rather than changing the opening route around them. The longer counters become much easier to clean up once more regions, NPCs, facilities, pets, and repeatable activities are already available.

The <Link href="/dimraeth/achievements">Dimraeth achievement tracker</Link> shows all 37 requirements and makes it easier to see what is actually left instead of repeating activities that are already complete.

<h2 id="map-locations">
  Do you need a full Dimraeth map?
</h2>

For the current campaign, I have not needed one to solve the main progression blockers.

The locations that matter most are usually attached to a specific question: how to cross into the next section, where the Goblin Hideout actually is, how to move through Wildwood, or which route reaches Forest Path.

When I am simply lost on the campaign, the <Link href="/dimraeth/walkthrough">main walkthrough</Link> is faster than scanning the entire world for the next marker. Inside Wildwood, the <Link href="/dimraeth/wildwood-walkthrough">Wildwood route</Link> narrows that further.

A complete map becomes more useful for broad collection and location work than for solving the current story sequence.

<h2 id="all-bosses">
  Are Goblin King and Forest King the only bosses?
</h2>

No.

They are the two bosses most closely tied to the current main-story transitions, but other bosses and mini-bosses appear through exploration and repeatable activities.

That difference matters when deciding what to do next. Defeating Goblin King or Aelwynor changes campaign progression. Repeating another boss may instead be about materials, a Deed, a Bounty, or improving the character.

For repeat fights and mini-boss progression, I use the <Link href="/dimraeth/deeds-bounties">Deeds & Bounties guide</Link> rather than treating every boss kill as another story checkpoint.

<h2 id="after-story">
  What should you do after Aelwynor?
</h2>

Once **Aelwynor the Wildroot Father** is dead, I stop searching Forest Path for another hidden mandatory marker.

From that point, the best next activity depends on what the character still lacks.

A build that is only half assembled can keep farming equipment or finish its Skill Tree direction. A player who wants harder combat can push Deeds and Bounties. A collector can finish pets and achievements. The Sanctum can be expanded into a stronger production base, unfinished NPC quests can be cleaned up, and the same character can continue into regular co-op sessions.

There is still plenty to improve after the current story ends; the difference is that one campaign objective is no longer deciding the order for you.
