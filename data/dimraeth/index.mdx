<h2 id="where-to-start">
  What should you do next in Dimraeth?
</h2>

Dimraeth puts several useful systems in front of the player at the same time, which makes the game look less linear than the current story actually is. A town-board quest can be worth doing without advancing the campaign. A Deed can be available while there is still main story left. A newly unlocked facility may help later without being necessary before the next boss.

I sort those situations by the thing that is stopping progress.

If the problem is **direction**, I follow the <Link href="/dimraeth/walkthrough">main story route</Link>. If the problem is a specific Wildwood gate, the regional route usually matters more than character level. If normal enemies or bosses are already draining every potion, I look at attributes, skills, and equipment instead of continuing to wander. When the linear story is finished, the priority changes again and repeatable systems become much more valuable.

That separation is more useful than trying to clear every icon as soon as it appears.

<h2 id="main-progression">
  The current story has a clear spine even when the quest log gets crowded
</h2>

The Early Access campaign moves from the opening **Earlwood** objectives into **Wildwood**, through the goblin storyline, past the **Goblin King**, and then deeper into the corrupted forest before ending at **Aelwynor the Wildroot Father**, the Forest King.

The <Link href="/dimraeth/walkthrough">Dimraeth walkthrough</Link> keeps that full sequence together and answers the question that matters most on a first run:

**Where do I go next?**

That page is also where I check whether an optional detour is solving a real problem or simply delaying the next required objective.

<h2 id="wildwood">
  Wildwood has two different kinds of route problem
</h2>

The first is broad: the region has several objectives active at once, and it is not always obvious which one opens the next story gate. The <Link href="/dimraeth/wildwood-walkthrough">Wildwood walkthrough</Link> covers that progression from **Lost Child / Northern Crossing** through the later forest route toward the throne.

The second is much narrower. When the quest specifically says **Find the Goblin Hideout**, the useful answer is a route rather than another regional overview. The <Link href="/dimraeth/goblin-hideout">Goblin Hideout guide</Link> starts from Wildwood Center, shows the misleading Wildwood Caverns turn, and follows the objective until the raid stage takes over.

If the Hideout is already found, there is no reason to keep retracing that route. At that point the problem has moved forward with the quest chain.

<h2 id="bosses">
  Goblin King and Forest King mark two different transitions
</h2>

The **Goblin King** sits at the end of the goblin portion of Wildwood. **Hoard King's Fall** and **Tamsin's explosive** are the useful signals that the story has finally moved from searching and raiding into the boss stage. The <Link href="/dimraeth/goblin-king">Goblin King guide</Link> covers the fight itself and the handoff that follows it.

Defeating him does not finish the current campaign.

The forest storyline continues until **Aelwynor the Wildroot Father**. The <Link href="/dimraeth/forest-king">Forest King guide</Link> is where Poison, arena hazards, the Fey add, defensive timing, Bleed pressure, failed attempts, and the final kill belong.

After Aelwynor, the structure changes more dramatically: there is still plenty of progression, but there is no longer another obvious mandatory story destination.

<h2 id="builds">
  A build becomes easier to manage once every upgrade serves the same fight
</h2>

Race, class, attributes, spellbooks, skill trees, equipment sets, status effects, and pets can all pull a character in different directions.

The <Link href="/dimraeth/builds">build guide</Link> follows two tested examples far enough to show how those systems connect rather than listing isolated stats.

The Shadow route uses **Poison, Decay, Concentration recovery, Memory, Intelligence, and set bonuses** to keep a casting loop alive. The Minotaur Brawler gradually moves from mixed damage into a dedicated **Bleed** setup once skills such as **Frenzied Slashes** make that commitment worthwhile.

The same principle works outside those two builds: an upgrade is most useful when it strengthens something the character is already doing well.

<h2 id="pets">
  Pets can change combat and how long you stay in the field
</h2>

Companions are more than a cosmetic system. Their abilities can restore resources, add status effects, supply direct damage, or cover a defensive weakness.

The <Link href="/dimraeth/pets">pets guide</Link> starts with **Myrll** and the Alpha Wolf route that unlocks **Fangra Cub**, then compares companions with clearly different jobs. **Moon Cat** supports Concentration-heavy casters, **Carrion Parrot** builds on Bleeding enemies, while **Ember Hound** brings Fire Damage and Burning.

Pets also carry items. **Courier** can move those items into available Sanctum chests without ending the exploration run, which becomes increasingly useful once equipment and materials start filling the character inventory.

<h2 id="sanctum">
  The Sanctum is most valuable when it removes friction from the next trip
</h2>

I do not wait for a perfectly developed base before returning to the campaign.

The <Link href="/dimraeth/sanctum">Sanctum guide</Link> focuses on the facilities that change normal play: storage, **Water Well**, recruited NPCs, the **Facility Center**, followers, and passive resource production.

A useful Sanctum should make the next field run easier. Extra construction can continue later when it has a purpose instead of becoming a requirement before every story objective.

<h2 id="coop">
  Co-op progress is split between the character and the world
</h2>

Dimraeth supports up to **8 players**, but the important multiplayer question is not simply how many people can join.

Characters and worlds keep different kinds of progress.

Your character can carry its level, equipment, inventory, spells, and personal progression into another world. The world's story progress, recruited allies, buildings, and Sanctum development stay with that world.

That makes the <Link href="/dimraeth/co-op-progression">co-op progression guide</Link> especially useful before a regular group invests heavily in one campaign. Choosing whose world the group is developing can matter much more than deciding who joins first on a single evening.

<h2 id="deeds">
  Deeds are useful before the ending, but they matter much more after it
</h2>

The Deed Board becomes available while the Wildwood story is still moving, so repeat bosses should not automatically replace the campaign the moment they appear.

Before Aelwynor, I use Deeds when I can name the reason for the detour: a boss material, spell component, useful reward, or a character that genuinely needs another power step.

After the current story ends, those repeats no longer compete with another mandatory destination. The <Link href="/dimraeth/deeds-bounties">Deeds and Bounties guide</Link> covers **Deed Levels 1–10**, boss-material farming, reward differences, efficient difficulty ceilings, harder solo clears, and when adding players becomes more useful than forcing the next tier alone.

<h2 id="classes-races">
  Which race and class should you choose?
</h2>

The current Early Access build has **three races and three classes**, and the same class does not behave identically on every race. Race and class together affect the skill-tree direction and the way the character develops, so I would not choose purely from the class name.

For now, the most useful choice is the one that matches the combat loop you actually want to play.

A player who wants persistent status pressure can already see strong directions through Shadow DoT or Brawler Bleed. Someone who cares more about support, direct physical pressure, or another spell school should use the same test: look for the race-class combination whose early tree and available spells reinforce that style instead of trying to predict a final tier list before enough builds have been tested.

The <Link href="/dimraeth/builds">build guide</Link> has the two combinations I have enough real data to break down in detail.

<h2 id="map-locations">
  Is there a full Dimraeth map?
</h2>

The current guides cover the locations where navigation has actually become a progression problem, rather than pretending every landmark needs the same level of explanation.

That includes **Northern Crossing**, the route into the **Goblin Hideout**, Wildwood's later throne path, and the transition into **Forest Path**.

If the question is “where do I go next?”, start with the <Link href="/dimraeth/walkthrough">main walkthrough</Link>. If the problem is inside Wildwood, the <Link href="/dimraeth/wildwood-walkthrough">regional walkthrough</Link> has the tighter route.

A full map only becomes useful when it can answer more than those progression routes.

<h2 id="achievements">
  What about achievements and missables?
</h2>

I would not change a first playthrough route around achievements yet.

The current story can be completed while still leaving side quests, NPC relationships, repeat bosses, Sanctum work, pets, and other progression available afterward. Nothing in the current route gives me a reason to abandon the main campaign simply because an optional system has appeared.

That makes the safer first-run priority straightforward: finish the story path, keep obviously important side rewards when they are nearby, and leave completion cleanup for after the Forest King unless a specific objective proves time-sensitive.

<h2 id="all-bosses">
  Are Goblin King and Forest King the only bosses?
</h2>

No. Dimraeth has other bosses and repeatable boss encounters, and the Deed system gives several of them a longer life after the original kill.

What makes **Goblin King** and **Forest King** different is their role in the current main-story progression. They are the two fights where a separate boss answer also helps explain where the campaign goes next.

For repeatable fights, materials, and higher difficulty, the <Link href="/dimraeth/deeds-bounties">Deeds and Bounties guide</Link> is more useful than treating every boss as another main-story milestone.

<h2 id="after-story">
  What should you do after the Forest King?
</h2>

Once **Aelwynor the Wildroot Father** is defeated, I stop searching the forest for another hidden mandatory quest.

The next goal depends on what the character is missing.

I can finish a build, farm boss materials through Deeds, push a higher difficulty, level or change a pet, improve the Sanctum, return to unfinished NPC quests, or keep developing the same character for co-op.

The game still has progression left; it has simply stopped telling me that one yellow marker is more important than everything else.
