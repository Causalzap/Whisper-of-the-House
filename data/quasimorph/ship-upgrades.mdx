My biggest early Magnum mistake is treating every branch as equally urgent.

They are not.

The Conveyor, Monitoring, Scanner and Capsule solve different failures:

- the Conveyor replaces basic supplies;
- Monitoring improves access to useful work;
- the Scanner reduces time spent wandering;
- the Capsule protects progress that should not die with the operator.

I buy the branch that removes the cost I keep paying. A fixed order is useful as
a starting point, but the last failed or inefficient mission decides which node
comes next.

My default early route is:

> Conveyor → Monitoring or Scanner → the other core branch → Capsule →
> Scavengers and Recycling → Clone Rank → sustainable Engineering.

I change that order when the campaign gives me a different bottleneck.

> Tested in Quasimorph 1.0 across fresh and developed campaigns. Faction control,
> available components and station barter vary between saves. The numeric values
> below come from the 1.0 upgrade UI used for this guide.

<h2 id="conveyor-foundation">Use the Conveyor as a Foundation, Not the Whole Plan</h2>

The Conveyor is often the first practical upgrade because the opening route can
provide the Welder required for its first node.

The installed base Conveyor shows:

- **one production slot**
- **one queue slot**

That is enough to start producing licensed basic supplies instead of rebuilding
every loadout from random floor drops.

    The first Conveyor gives me a basic production loop. Once one useful supply
    can be replaced aboard the Magnum, I stop deepening Supply automatically and
    move to the branch that fixes the next mission problem.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

I move on when:

- at least one useful medical, ammunition or combat supply can be produced;
- another branch will improve contracts, navigation or item recovery;
- the next Supply node would consume a component needed by a core system.

I continue deeper when:

- one production slot is creating a real queue bottleneck;
- the licensed item is used on nearly every mission;
- repeated supply shortages are still ending runs;
- the required components are not blocking Monitoring, Scanner or Cloning.

The Conveyor stabilizes the first loop. It does not make every additional Supply
node the next best purchase.

<h2 id="monitoring">Buy Monitoring When the Contract Board Is the Bottleneck</h2>

Monitoring changes the campaign before I select an operator.

The base module displayed:

- **three charges**
- **504 hours Reload**, equal to **21 days**

Its verified upgrade nodes also showed:

- **Diplomatic Protocol:** `+3 Reputation points`
- **Data Decoders:** `+200 Reward points`
- **Compromise Management:** `+400 Reward points`
- **Pirated Software:** `Reload -144h`
- **Modern Processors:** `Reload -72h`
- **Battery Array:** `+1 charge`
- **Battery Plant:** `+2 charges`

    when another random local contract would actually help.
  </figcaption>

### I buy Monitoring early when

- every local job supports the wrong faction;
- the available stations cannot provide the component I need;
- travelling would take longer than generating another local opportunity;
- faction control is removing useful contracts from the system;
- the campaign repeatedly stalls while waiting for work.

### I use a charge when

- the Magnum will remain in the current system;
- another random contract has real value;
- the current board contains no useful station type;
- one more local mission would complete a component or reputation goal.

### I save a charge when

- I am about to travel;
- the board already contains a short useful contract;
- the roster is too injured or under-equipped for another job;
- I need a specific story trigger that random work cannot guarantee.

Monitoring improves opportunity. It does not guarantee the faction, objective or
station I want.

<h2 id="scanner">Buy the Scanner When Blind Navigation Is the Bottleneck</h2>

The base Scanner in the tested 1.0 branch displayed **one floor**.

I do not describe that as full-station knowledge. The verified starting value is
a one-floor scan, and deeper coverage should only be stated when the relevant UI
node is visible.

<figure className="my-8">
  <Image
    src="/images/quasimorph/quasimorph-floor-scanner-upgrade-tree.webp"
    alt="Quasimorph Scanner upgrade tree showing a base scan of one floor"
    width={1280}
    height={720}
    className="mx-auto h-auto w-full max-w-4xl rounded-xl"
  />
  <figcaption className="mx-auto mt-3 max-w-4xl text-sm text-slate-400">
    Even one revealed floor can stop a blind sweep. I use the map to identify the
    elevator route first and take detours only for a named medical, loot or
    objective target.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

The Scanner saves more than movement.

Every blind room can cost:

- Calories;
- ammunition;
- wound-treatment supplies;
- Quasimorphosis margin;
- time before the contract objective;
- inventory space spent on supplies for unnecessary combat.

My route becomes:

1. open the revealed map;
2. identify the elevator or target direction;
3. note rooms that match a current need;
4. move toward the objective;
5. take short detours only for a named component, medicine or ammunition;
6. return for optional loot only when the extraction margin remains safe.

### I buy the Scanner before Monitoring when

- the contract board is already useful;
- most failed runs come from elevator searches;
- food and medicine disappear before the objective floor;
- multi-floor missions are the current source of progress;
- Quasimorphosis rises because I spend too long exploring irrelevant rooms.

### I delay it when

- the campaign cannot generate worthwhile work;
- most contracts are short and easy to route;
- the required component is also the only item blocking Monitoring;
- navigation has not yet caused a real loss.

Monitoring solves **what job I can take**. The Scanner solves **how efficiently I
can finish it**.

  </figcaption>

- rare Magnum components;
- a story item when the mission allows it;
- a weapon or armour piece that creates a new practical build;
- an item I travelled specifically to obtain.

### I buy the Capsule when

- valuable items are found before the final floor;
- losing one item would force several contracts to be repeated;
- I am willing to return to the extraction point and use the system;
- Monitoring and Scanner are no longer blocked by the same component;
- the operator is entering longer or less predictable missions.

### I delay it when

- it consumes the only AI Chip needed by a more urgent branch;
- missions are short enough that immediate extraction is already safe;
- the real problem is finding valuable items rather than protecting them;
- my play style never returns to use the Capsule.

The Capsule is powerful only when I use it.

<h2 id="scavengers-recycling">Use Scavengers and Recycling to Remove Routine Shortages</h2>

Scavengers are less dramatic than the Scanner or Capsule, but they change what I
need to carry out of every mission.

The verified branch displayed these category values:

- **Resources: 1**
- **Food / Medicine: 2**
- **Ammo: 2**
- **Weapons / Armor: 1**

<figure className="my-8">
  <Image
    src="/images/quasimorph/quasimorph-scavenger-supply-upgrades.webp"
    alt="Quasimorph Scavenger supply upgrades showing Resources 1, Food and Medicine 2, Ammo 2, and Weapons and Armor 1"
    width={1280}
    height={720}
    className="mx-auto h-auto w-full max-w-4xl rounded-xl"
  />
  <figcaption className="mx-auto mt-3 max-w-4xl text-sm text-slate-400">
    The branch supports routine supply categories. Once one category stops being
    scarce, I stop sacrificing rare loot slots to bring home excessive copies of
    the same ordinary resource.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

I do not assume Scavengers replace targeted farming.

I still extract:

- specialty ammunition;
- rare medical items;
- licensed chips;
- components for the next Magnum node;
- equipment that creates a clear loadout upgrade.

What changes is my threshold for common supplies.

My economy loop is:

1. build a Scavenger category that matches the recurring shortage;
2. observe which supply becomes stable;
3. stop over-collecting that common category;
4. use the freed backpack space for progression items;
5. recycle duplicate equipment that has no loadout or barter purpose.

Recycling is useful after I unload ammunition and keep the best-condition copy.
It is not a reason to dismantle every weapon immediately or assume one damaged
gun will produce a specific scarce component.

- the roster still lacks a dependable baseline operator;
- another branch fixes a campaign-wide problem;
- the class plan is not defined;
- the components would block Monitoring, Scanner or the Capsule.

<h2 id="engineering">Delay Engineering Until Projects Are Sustainable</h2>

Engineering is tempting because the result is visible on a weapon or armour
piece. It is also an easy place to consume rare components before the Magnum can
replace them.

I work toward Cost Optimization and project-capacity improvements before
starting a broad upgrade programme.

<figure className="my-8">
  <Image
    src="/images/quasimorph/quasimorph-engineering-cost-optimization.webp"

    width={1280}
    height={720}
    className="mx-auto h-auto w-full max-w-4xl rounded-xl"
  />
  <figcaption className="mx-auto mt-3 max-w-4xl text-sm text-slate-400">
    The visible branch confirms Cost Optimization as part of the Engineering
    route, but I do not quote an exact reduction without a readable tooltip. I
    wait until the ship economy can support repeated projects.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

I start a project when:

- the weapon or armour pattern is already proven;
- I expect to keep using it across several missions;
- project costs no longer block the core ship branches;
- Scavengers and Recycling can help replace ordinary materials;
- the project solves a specific damage, resistance, range or durability problem.

I wait when:

- the item is likely to be replaced by the next faction Technology increase;
- the project consumes a chip needed by a campaign-wide system;
- the upgrade exists only because the button is available;
- losing the operator would make the project impossible to rebuild;
- I have not tested the item against the enemies it is meant to solve.

The right moment is not when Engineering becomes visible. It is when repeating
the project no longer stops the rest of the Magnum.

<h2 id="late-branches">Leave Specialized Branches Until the Campaign Uses Them</h2>

Several branches become powerful later. They are not weak upgrades; they are
often early purchases with no immediate job.

### Travel Engine

I delay long-range travel while useful contracts and missing components remain
near the current area. A faster trip does not help when the operator cannot
finish the destination mission.

### Augmentation

I wait until implants can be replaced and extraction is reliable. Installing the
only valuable implant on an operator who cannot be recovered is not a stable
economy.

### Pacts and Quasimorph systems

I do not invest before a build, mission or faction route uses them. The full

<h2 id="buy-wait-turn-back">My Buy, Wait or Turn Back Rules</h2>

### I buy when

- the contract board repeatedly offers no useful work;
- blind navigation consumes food, medicine or Q margin;
- rare progression items keep dying with the operator;
- common supplies consume too much extraction space;
- one reliable operator needs more Health and efficiency;
- a proven equipment line is limited by sustainable project cost.

### I wait when

- the branch needs my only rare chip;
- the previous level has not been used enough to prove the next one matters;
- barter or one targeted contract can solve the shortage more cheaply;
- the upgrade serves a late system the current campaign does not use;
- buying it would leave no components for a more frequent failure.

### I turn back from the purchase when

- it blocks Monitoring or Scanner without solving a larger problem;
- I am expanding Capsule capacity but rarely use the base charges;
- I am upgrading a temporary weapon before Engineering is sustainable;
- I am investing in travel while local missions still provide what I need;
- the node does not change the next contract, extraction or survival decision.

The Magnum branches become powerful when they support one another.

Monitoring creates opportunities. The Scanner shortens the route. The Capsule
protects rare progress. Scavengers and Recycling stabilize supplies. Clone Rank
and Engineering become efficient after that foundation works.

That is when the ship stops being a collection of upgrades and becomes a
repeatable campaign economy.
