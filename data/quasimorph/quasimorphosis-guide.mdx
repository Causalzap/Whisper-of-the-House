Quasimorphosis is the mission clock I cannot pause.

A contract can still look under control while the meter is climbing, the first
Ecolapse has already changed the floor and I am carrying only one pack of
Cigarettes. The dangerous moment is usually not the first Quasimorph. It is the
moment I keep opening optional rooms after the objective is complete because the
operator still has Health and ammunition.

I treat the meter as a route decision. I ask how many floors remain, what is
raising it, how much reduction I can still spend and whether I actually have a
weapon that can finish a Baron. When those answers are bad, I stop trying to
clear the station and start protecting the exit.

This guide covers the verified Quasimorphosis survival loop in Quasimorph 1.0:
reading the meter, reacting to Ecolapse, using reduction items, deciding whether
to fight a Baron and surviving the floor after the Baron dies. It does not try to
list every Baron, every pact or every planet-specific resistance.

> Tested in Quasimorph 1.0 across Normal-difficulty and experienced campaign
> runs. Station layouts, events, available reduction items and the local
> Quasimorph faction vary between missions. Exact item values should be read from
> the current in-game tooltip.

<h2 id="read-meter-as-clock">Read the Quasimorphosis Meter as a Mission Clock</h2>

I do not wait for the Baron before taking the meter seriously.

In one early run, the first floor had already reached **152 Grasp** while the
operator was still searching rooms. The floor was large, enemies were still
active and the main objective was not yet secured.

  </figcaption>

The practical problem is cumulative exposure:

- another floor means more enemies, movement and food use;
- a long elevator search keeps the operator inside the station;
- flesh can solve Calories while worsening the meter;
- an Ecolapse can turn a controlled human fight into a second enemy problem;
- a limited reduction item may be needed later for the return route.

In the tested early campaign, a mission longer than three or four floors was
already treated as probable Baron territory because the crew had almost no
reliable reduction supply. I do not use that as a universal formula. A fast
Scanner route, a different event or a better reduction loadout changes the real
risk.

I use it as a warning:

> A low-difficulty contract can still become a Quasimorphosis problem when it is
> long.

Before accepting, I combine the meter risk with the contract card:

1. read Floors;
2. read the objective;
3. check whether I must return with an item;
4. check available Cigarettes or other reduction tools;
5. decide whether the operator can kill a Baron or must avoid one;
6. leave enough equipment in the Magnum that one failed run will not end the
   campaign.

<h2 id="what-raises-meter">Do Not Use Flesh as Free Food on an Active Q-Meter Mission</h2>

Flesh solves one resource while spending another.

In the tested run, raw meat immediately made Quasimorphosis tick upward. Cooking
did not remove the problem: cooked dog meat still increased the Q meter. Mostly
canned flesh was also described as adding Q.


That does not mean I never eat flesh.

I eat it when:

- starvation is the immediate threat;
- the objective is close;
- the exit route is known;
- I have enough reduction supply to absorb the increase;
- I am intentionally preparing for a Baron and need Fullness or regeneration.

I avoid it when:

- ordinary food already covers the route;
- the mission item is secured and I should be leaving;
- the meter is close to its next dangerous state;
- the next floor is still unknown;
- I am eating only to create room for optional loot.

This is a real backpack decision. A common food item can be more valuable than a
rare weapon when it lets me avoid another Q increase.

The same item can reverse value after the objective is complete. If I already
have enough ordinary Calories to reach the elevator, I do not carry extra flesh
deeper into the station merely because it can be cooked later.

<h2 id="eolapse-first-turn">An Ecolapse Gives Me One Setup Turn, Not a Free Kill</h2>

The tutorial describes an **Ecolapse** as a creature being torn apart from the
inside and replaced by a Quasimorph.

The first turn is the most important detail: the newly appeared Quasimorph is
invulnerable while phasing between realities. Shooting it during that turn
wastes ammunition and leaves me in the same position when it becomes active.

    I use the invulnerable turn to improve the next exchange. Firing now spends
    ammunition without changing the threat.
  </figcaption>

My response is:

1. stop firing;
2. check which side of the room it spawned on;
3. identify any surviving humans or other enemies near it;
4. reload if the weapon is not ready;
5. move to cover or increase range;
6. let the Quasimorph choose its first target;
7. kill it after the phase ends if it remains my problem.

An Ecolapse can temporarily help.

When the Quasimorph appears beside hostile humans, it may attack them first. That
gives me time to reload, move left or right, close a door or let both sides spend
their attacks on each other.

I do not assume it will remain friendly. Positioning decides whether it is a
useful distraction or a new melee threat. Once it starts moving toward my route,
I clear it before continuing deeper.

### When I leave it fighting

- the other enemies are closer to it;
- I have a safe door or corner;
- the objective route goes away from the fight;
- ammunition is limited;
- watching one turn will reveal which side it targets.

### When I kill it immediately after the phase

- it spawned between me and the elevator;
- the room contains no other target;
- it has enough Action Points to close the distance quickly;
- leaving it alive would put it behind me during extraction;
- a second Ecolapse would create an uncontrolled crossfire.

The first turn is free preparation. It is not permission to ignore the spawn.

<h2 id="reduction-items">Spend Reduction Items to Protect a Route, Not to Delay Greed</h2>

I decide before launch whether the mission is a **control run** or an **avoidance
run**.

On an avoidance run, the operator does not have a credible Baron-killing weapon.
The loadout is built to keep Q under control long enough to finish the contract
and extract.

One early RealWare preparation made that decision explicitly:

- there was no reliable Baron-killing weapon;
- a Cyborg Head was considered to avoid or control the Baron risk;
- Cigarettes were added as insurance;
- losing the best equipment would have set the campaign back badly.

A later loadout calculation treated Morphine and NOX as a combined **-400**
Quasimorphosis package. I use the exact current tooltip before publishing an
individual item value, because balance and uses can change. The reliable decision
is the total reduction I can still deploy during the route.

I spend a reduction use when:

- it keeps the meter below the maximum state long enough to reach the objective;
- the return path is longer than the path forward;
- I am carrying a story item, chip or Magnum component;
- an event or unexpectedly large floor consumed the planned margin;
- the remaining charge is worth less than the cargo already secured.

I do not spend it when:

- the objective is complete and the elevator is close;
- I am using it only to open optional rooms;
- the current location has no active Quasimorphosis pressure;
- the operator still has a clean route out without crossing another floor;
- saving it will make the next mandatory mission possible.

The mistake is using one Cigarette, feeling safe and then adding enough optional
combat to rebuild the same danger.

<h2 id="maximum-q">At 1000, the Mission Becomes a Baron Decision</h2>

The tested run treated **1000** as the point where the situation would become
extreme. The current screenshot set shows the meter rising through Grasp rather
than the exact 1000 frame, so I use 1000 as the observed run threshold without
pretending the available image proves the final state. By the time the meter
reached maximum, the correct question was no longer “what else can I loot?”

It was:

> Is the mission complete, and do I leave or deliberately prepare the floor for
> a Baron?

In the Baron run, the operator reached maximum Q after the floor was mostly
cleared. The mission objective was then completed before the fight decision was
made.

That order is important:

1. clear the route around the objective;
2. finish the objective;
3. identify the elevator and escape path;
4. confirm ammunition, food, Health and Pain;
5. decide whether the Baron offers enough value to justify the remaining risk.

that must always be completed.

<h2 id="fight-or-extract">Should I Fight the Baron or Extract?</h2>

Most early operators should extract.

I fight only when the run has changed from an accidental crisis into a prepared
encounter.

### I extract when

- the mission objective is complete;
- a permanent unlock or rare component is already secured;
- I do not know which damage type the Baron resists;
- the main weapon has too little ammunition for a long target;
- my reduction supply can still create a safe exit;
- Pain, wounds or low Calories threaten Action Points;
- dying would remove the only working loadout from the Magnum.

### I consider fighting when

- the floor around the chosen position is cleared;
- the objective is already complete;
- the route to the elevator remains open;
- I have enough food and treatment to survive a long exchange;
- the weapon can damage the Baron instead of showing zero repeatedly;
- smoke, doors, fire or another terrain tool can control sight lines;
- I accept that the remaining Quasimorphs may still kill the operator afterward.

The tested fight is a good warning.

The operator had food, ammunition and a mostly controlled floor, but the original
damage plan still failed. Nobilis showed several very high resistances, including
**81% plasma resistance** in the inspection screen. Throwing weapons and fire did
not automatically solve the encounter simply because they used a different damage
type.

This is why I inspect the Baron, test one controlled attack and abandon a plan
that is producing negligible damage. A backpack full of ammunition is not a
Baron-killing loadout when the damage type is wrong.

<h2 id="prepare-baron-room">Prepare the Room Before You Trigger the Baron</h2>

If I choose to fight, I do not summon the Baron in the middle of an unexplored
floor.

The tested route first:

- checked every approach vector;
- confirmed the nearby rooms were clear;
- created a stockpile;
- moved spare gear out of the active inventory;
- selected a room with doors and a barrel;
- spread fuel;
- carried food, water and healing;
- blocked the approach;
- switched out of the original weapon plan when it failed.

My preparation order is:

1. finish the contract;
2. clear the immediate rooms;
3. choose a position with one or two controlled entrances;
4. place spare loot where it will not block inventory actions;
5. load every weapon;
6. prepare food, treatment and Pain control;
7. keep an escape square open;
8. trigger maximum Q only after all of that is ready.

Pain deserves special attention.

In the tested fight, healing one turn earlier would likely have kept Pain lower
and preserved the three actions needed to stay ahead of the remaining threats.
The Baron was killed, but the delayed treatment removed the escape margin.

<h2 id="after-baron">Killing the Baron Does Not Make the Floor Safe</h2>

This is the 1.0 lesson I would not ignore.

After the Baron died, the run did not reset into a calm extraction. A surviving
Quasimorph remained active, could still attack and forced another treatment and
movement decision. The operator had already spent ammunition, healing and Pain
margin on the Baron.

The operator eventually died, although the completed objective still counted as
a mission win in that run.

I do not build a general strategy around that outcome. It worked because the
objective had already been completed and the campaign settings allowed the
result to survive the operator's death.

Before fighting, I therefore reserve:

- ammunition for at least one additional threat;
- one treatment or regeneration option;
- enough Calories for the return;
- an open line to the elevator;
- the discipline to leave immediately instead of checking another room.

A Baron kill is not the moment to start looting again.

<h2 id="absentia">Absentia Changes What I Pack</h2>

Not every location creates the same Q pressure.

In the tested normal-start route, Mars was treated as having no active
Quasimorphosis threat. That is why eating flesh there did not create the same
Baron planning problem as it did on an active Q-meter mission.

I still check the actual mission UI rather than assuming every contract near a
planet behaves identically.

When the mission is in Absentia or otherwise shows no active Q pressure:

- I do not spend scarce inventory slots on Cigarettes only from habit;
- flesh becomes a Calories and infection decision rather than a Baron timer;
- I can prioritise ammunition, medicine or mission cargo;
- long floor count remains a normal combat and food problem.

When Quasimorphosis is active:

- the same flesh has an additional cost;
- long maps consume reduction supply;
- allied humans can become new Quasimorph threats through Ecolapse;
- I prepare either a controlled exit or a real Baron weapon.

The location changes the loadout before the operator lands.

<h2 id="take-control-turn-back">My Take, Control or Turn Back Rules</h2>

### I take the mission

I take it when the floor count, objective and reduction supply leave a real
margin. The operator either has a credible Baron answer or enough control items
to avoid reaching that fight.

### I control the meter

I spend reduction when it protects the objective, the return route or valuable
cargo. I use the first Ecolapse turn to reposition and I stop using flesh as
routine food.

### I turn back

I turn back when the objective is complete, the meter is near maximum and the
next room offers no named progression item.

I also turn back when my only Baron plan is hope:

- an untested damage type;
- low ammunition;
- no Pain control;
- no cleared fight area;
- no reserve for the enemies after the Baron.

Quasimorphosis does not require me to clear every horror it creates.

Most successful runs end because I recognise the point where the contract is
already won and stop asking the operator to win a second, unnecessary mission on
the same floor.
