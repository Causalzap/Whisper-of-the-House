The hardest loot decision in Quasimorph is not identifying the rarest item.

It is deciding which item deserves one inventory slot **right now**.

A stack of Rags can save a run before the Magnum has medical production. The
same stack becomes dead weight after the current recipe is covered. Water can be
essential while the elevator is unknown, then become the first item I leave when
the objective is complete and a permanent Magnum component needs the space.

I judge loot by three questions:

1. What does this item unlock?
2. What current supply or equipment does it replace?
3. Can I still extract safely after keeping it?

The barter number comes later. Mission items, permanent unlocks, the next Magnum
component and the supplies required to reach the elevator all come first.

> Tested in Quasimorph 1.0 on fresh Normal-difficulty campaigns. Loot pools,
> recipes, station ownership and contract layouts vary between saves. These are
> backpack decisions, not fixed item locations.

<h2 id="reserve-objective-slot">Reserve the Objective Slot Before Optional Loot</h2>

I read the contract objective before entering the station.

If the mission requires a PCPU, prototype, data device or another retrieve-and-
return item, I keep at least one slot empty until the item is secured.

My route is:

1. identify what must return to the Magnum;
2. reserve the required space;
3. move toward the target before clearing every side room;
4. secure the item;
5. reopen the map and identify the shortest known return;
6. compare every optional room with the risk of losing the objective.

Killing a commander or reaching a device may only be one step. The contract is
not complete when the required item is left on the floor because the backpack
was filled with duplicate weapons.

After the objective is secured, ordinary loot loses priority.

A second common shotgun is not worth repeating the entire contract.

<h2 id="permanent-unlocks">Permanent Unlocks Beat Ordinary Equipment</h2>

A permanent unlock changes future missions. A normal weapon changes one
loadout.

Production chips can add recipes to Magnum production. Mercenary mind chips can
expand the roster. Other rare unlocks can open a class, operator or ship system
that remains useful after the current firearm is replaced.

I extract instead of continuing when:

- the chip unlocks production I do not have;
- it adds an operator, class or permanent system;
- the mission objective is already complete;
- the operator is wounded, hungry or low on ammunition;
- the next unexplored room has no specific progression target.

I continue only when the route is still mandatory or the operator has enough
margin to reach another named objective.

I do not keep exploring merely because the backpack still has space.

<h2 id="upgrade-components">Keep Components for the Next Upgrade, Not Every Future Upgrade</h2>

I do not try to carry the entire technology tree home in one backpack.

Before launch, I check the Magnum node I am actually pursuing and note the
missing components. That turns “might be useful later” into a short target list.

My priorities depend on the current bottleneck:

- Monitoring components when useful contracts are missing;
- Scanner components when elevator searches consume food and time;
- Hangar components when rare items keep dying with the operator;
- Supply components when ammunition and medicine repeatedly collapse;
- Cloning components when the roster has no Health margin.

A component that completes the next node beats several common materials for an
unknown future recipe.

    return supplies were secure.
  </figcaption>

This does not make Water, food or Springs universally disposable.

I keep survival supplies when:

- the elevator is still unknown;
- the operator is far from the return route;
- additional mandatory floors remain;
- Calories, Health or fire risk can still change the mission;
- the objective has not been secured.

I replace them when:

- the objective is complete;
- the return route is known;
- the operator has enough Calories, Health and ammunition to leave;
- the new item has a confirmed permanent use.

A common item's value falls after its job is finished.

<h2 id="material-caps">Cap Common Materials Before They Fill the Backpack</h2>

Rags and Plastic are useful because they support early medical and repair
crafting. They are dangerous because they are common enough to occupy every
available slot without changing the next run.

### One working stack of Rags is often enough

On an early mission without established ship crafting, one stack of Rags already
covered the immediate material shortage.

### Plastic follows the same rule

Once Plastic reaches a full stack or covers the intended recipe, I stop treating
every corpse and container as a mandatory source.

    priority until another recipe or repair creates a new need.
  </figcaption>

I take more common material when:

- a Fabricator is already on the current route;
- I know the recipe and am short by one component;
- the result repairs equipment required for extraction;
- the Magnum has no reserve;
- no rare item is competing for the slot.

I stop when:

- the stack is full;
- the Conveyor can replace the finished consumable;
- no current recipe or Magnum node needs it;
- keeping it requires dropping ammunition or medicine;
- collecting more means reopening cleared floors or increasing Q pressure.

The goal is not to leave every floor clean. It is to return with a cargo hold
that changes the next decision.

<h2 id="weapons-and-armor">Keep One Clear Equipment Upgrade and Strip the Rest</h2>

A weapon consumes more than the slot shown by its icon. It also creates an
ammunition requirement.

Before keeping a dropped firearm, I check:

- condition;
- compatible ammunition already available;
- effective range and damage type;
- whether the current class develops with that category;
- whether it is clearly better than the equipped weapon;
- whether the Magnum already has another copy.

I keep the weapon when it creates a new practical loadout or is an obvious
condition and performance upgrade.

I do not keep it only because the model is different.

### Unload ammunition before comparing or dismantling

Duplicate weapons often contain the ammunition I actually need.

My order is:

1. unload the weapon;
2. merge the ammunition into a carried stack;
3. compare condition, range and damage profile;
4. keep the clear upgrade;
5. dismantle only when the possible materials serve a current need;
6. leave it when dismantling adds risk without a useful result.

Dismantling is not a guaranteed shopping list. I do not remain on a dangerous
floor because another damaged starter gun might produce one specific component.

<h2 id="craft-on-site">Craft on Site Only When the Result Solves the Current Run</h2>

An on-site Fabricator can convert several scattered materials into one useful
item, but the crafting button is not a reason to spend a scarce component.

The tested Gunsmith Firearm Repair Kit showed:

- **Repair +40**
- **Maximum condition -10**
- **20/20 capacity**
- **0.80 kg item weight**

    width={1280}
    height={720}

  />
  <figcaption className="mx-auto mt-3 max-w-4xl text-sm text-slate-400">
    The kit restores 40 condition but reduces the weapon's maximum condition by
    10. I craft or keep it when the current firearm needs that repair enough to
    justify the permanent cost.
  </figcaption>

I craft on site when:

- the floor around the Fabricator is controlled;
- the machine is already on the route;
- the result repairs the main weapon or completes a current need;
- the missing common materials are nearby;
- Calories, ammunition and Quasimorphosis still leave a safe margin.

I leave when:

- the scarce component is more valuable unspent;
- the finished item does not solve the current loadout;
- crafting requires backtracking through uncontrolled rooms;
- the objective or permanent unlock is already secured;
- the repair's maximum-condition loss is worse than using another weapon.

I protect the bottleneck. I do not consume it merely because a recipe is
available.

<h2 id="trade-or-keep">Trade Only After Protecting Progression Items</h2>

Quasimorph barter is selective. A station does not assign useful value to every
item in the backpack.

In one early exchange, Guardsman Clothing produced **+168**, while another
offered item produced no useful exchange value.

My trade order is:

1. reserve mission and story items;
2. reserve production chips and permanent unlocks;
3. reserve the exact Magnum components I need;
4. keep one practical loadout and its ammunition;
5. offer accepted clothing, corporate goods and genuine surplus;
6. stop when the station has nothing useful to return.

I do not sell an item simply because it displays a number.

A Data Pad or another component can be more valuable installed on the Magnum
than converted into a replaceable weapon.

The reverse is also true. When one item has blocked Monitoring or Scanner for
several missions, I use barter to target it rather than waiting indefinitely for
the correct random container.

<h2 id="full-backpack">What I Drop When the Backpack Is Full</h2>

I do not use one permanent drop order because the return route changes item
value.

I ask what each slot still does.

### I usually consider dropping

- Rags beyond the working stack;
- Plastic beyond the current recipe;
- a second common melee weapon;
- duplicate low-condition armour;
- ammunition for a weapon I am not carrying;
- ordinary food after return Calories are covered;
- Water after Calories, fire and route risk are controlled;
- barter goods rejected by the current station.

### I protect

- the mission objective;
- production and mercenary mind chips;
- rare Magnum modules and components;
- the only ammunition for the main weapon;
- medicine required for an active wound or Infection;
- one useful cutting tool when the route still needs it;
- equipment that is a clear loadout improvement.

The correct decision can look inconsistent.

I may drop Springs and Plastic for a strange module in one mission, then protect
those same materials in the next because they complete a repair kit.

The difference is not rarity alone. It is whether the item has a confirmed job
before the operator reaches the Magnum.

<h2 id="when-to-leave">Leave When the Loot Has Already Won the Run</h2>

A successful loot run can become a failed mission during one unnecessary room.

I leave when:

- the mission item is secured;
- a production or mind chip is secured;
- a rare Magnum component has no replacement;
- Calories threaten regeneration, Health or movement;
- ammunition only covers the known return route;
- a wound can reopen or Infection is growing;
- Quasimorphosis is climbing and the next room has no named target;
- another room cannot solve a progression or survival problem.

I continue when:

- the objective remains incomplete;
- the return route is controlled;
- Health, Calories, ammunition and Q pressure leave a real margin;
- the next room contains a specific target such as a Medical Unit or server rack;
- the expected item is worth risking the current haul.

I stop measuring success by empty slots.

A backpack with a permanent unlock and three unused spaces can be a better run
than a completely full backpack on a dead clone.

<h2 id="sort-after-extraction">Sort the Haul Before the Next Contract</h2>

After returning to the Magnum, I unload everything and sort it by the next
action.

### Next loadout

I group the practical firearm, compatible ammunition, medicine, food, Water and
one melee backup.

### Progression reserve

I separate chips, story items and Magnum components from trade goods so one fast
barter or dismantle action cannot remove them.

### Barter surplus

I group goods accepted by useful nearby stations. I do not cross the system only
to sell low-value leftovers.

### Dismantling candidates

I unload ammunition, keep the best-condition copy and dismantle only duplicates
whose possible materials support a current recipe or Magnum node.

This reset gives the next contract a purpose.

I am no longer entering a station to “get loot.” I am entering to find one
Scanner component, one Medical Unit, one ammunition reserve or one accepted
trade good.

Once that purpose is complete, the backpack has done its job—even when several
slots are still empty.
