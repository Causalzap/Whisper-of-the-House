The contract with the largest Reward is not always the contract I take.

A profitable-looking job can sit on the wrong side of the system, add another
floor, damage a faction relationship I still need and send me into a station
that contains none of the components blocking my next Magnum upgrade.

I read contracts in a fixed order:

> side → objective → travel → time remaining → Difficulty → Floors → station
> type → Power Concentration → Reward.

That order keeps Reward from hiding the real cost of the run.

> Tested in Quasimorph 1.0 on Normal difficulty. Contract availability, faction
> control, station ownership and mission events change between campaigns. The
> screen-reading rules remain useful even when the exact jobs do not repeat.

<h2 id="read-contract-card">Read the Whole Contract Card Before Choosing a Side</h2>

A single location can offer two very different versions of the same conflict.

At Siren's Song, the Sunlight Coven side showed:

- **Espionage**
- **1,080 Power Concentration**
- **2,511 Reward**
- **four floors**

The opposing Church of Revelation side showed:

- **Infiltration**
- **810 Power Concentration**
- **1,509 Reward**
- **three floors**

The contract had **31 days 20 hours** remaining.

  <figcaption className="mx-auto mt-3 max-w-4xl text-sm text-slate-400">

    highlighted side is automatically better.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

I am not choosing between **2,511** and **1,509** in isolation.

I am choosing:

- which faction receives the result;
- which faction loses position;
- which objective I must complete;
- how many floors the operator may cross;
- how long the operator remains exposed;
- what station and container types the mission can generate;
- whether the completion payout justifies the supplies and risk.

The extra floor can mean another elevator search, another food check and another
chance for a wound or Quasimorphosis increase to change the route.

I decide whether I want the **side and objective** first. Reward is the final
comparison, not the first one.

<h2 id="beneficiary-victim">Beneficiary and Victim Decide the Political Cost</h2>

The Beneficiary wants the contract completed. The Victim loses the asset,
personnel or position being attacked.

That means every contract is also a faction decision.

Before confirming, I ask:

1. Am I building reputation with this Beneficiary for a real reason?
2. Do I still need a station, reward pool or story route controlled by the Victim?
3. Is this ordinary work or a story choice?
4. Does Change Side create a reputation loss I cannot easily repair?
5. Will helping this faction remove useful rival contracts from the system?

The Orphan contract shows the cost clearly. Changing to the Civil Resistance
side displayed **Reputation -10**.

I do not try to keep every faction happy. I protect relationships that serve a
concrete campaign need:

- a station that offers a missing Magnum component;
- a faction whose reward pool matches my weapon route;
- a story branch I have deliberately chosen;
- a local power that keeps useful work available near the Magnum.

If neither side helps the current plan, I close the contract and wait for better
work.

<h2 id="travel-and-expiration">Compare Travel Time with Time Remaining</h2>

A safe contract can still be a bad contract when it is too far away.

In one fresh campaign, travel from Phobos to Jupiter required **110 days 20
hours**. That trip was not worth making for ordinary one-skull work while useful
contracts remained near the starting area.

  <Image

    alt="Quasimorph System Map showing travel from Phobos to Jupiter taking 110 days and 20 hours"
    width={1280}
    height={720}
    className="mx-auto h-auto w-full max-w-4xl rounded-xl"
  />
  <figcaption className="mx-auto mt-3 max-w-4xl text-sm text-slate-400">
    A low Difficulty marker does not make a 110-day trip efficient. I build the
    first cargo reserve near the Magnum instead of crossing the system for
    routine work.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

My travel check is:

1. read **Time remains**;
2. select the destination on the System Map;
3. read the full travel duration;
4. leave time for loadout changes and operator recovery;
5. check which local contracts will expire while I am gone.

I do not depart when travel consumes most of the contract window. Arriving with
almost no time remaining removes my ability to fix a bad loadout or wait for a
wounded operator.

### I make the long trip when

- the contract advances a story route I deliberately chose;
- the destination can provide a component unavailable nearby;
- several useful contracts are clustered around the destination;
- the operator and equipment are already prepared;
- Time remains comfortably exceeds the journey.

### I stay local when

- the distant job is ordinary repeatable work;
- the larger Reward mainly compensates for a longer route;
- nearby jobs can build the same ammunition or medical reserve;
- I still lack a replaceable loadout;
- the trip would allow several useful local contracts to expire.

The System Map is also a campaign clock. Travelling means choosing what I will
not be doing during those days.

<h2 id="difficulty-and-floors">Read Difficulty, Floors and Objective Together</h2>

Difficulty estimates danger. Floors estimate how long that danger can continue.
The objective tells me what must still be possible at the end.

A four-floor Espionage mission can require:

- more Calories;
- more ammunition;
- more elevator searches;
- more wound and Infection exposure;
- more Quasimorphosis control;
- enough empty inventory to bring the mission item back.

I do not turn the skull line into a universal formula. Faction Technology,
special targets, mission events and the objective can make a short contract more
dangerous than its floor count suggests.

In one Elimination mission, a scientist target received a **+3 Technology**
adjustment and carried a **Tech 4** weapon that ordinary early enemies would not
normally provide.

I therefore prepare for the marked target, not only the surrounding guards.

### Control and defence objectives change where I fight

When interacting with the objective starts enemy waves, I avoid defending from
inside the target room.

A grenade can destroy the device even when the operator survives.

My route is:

1. identify the objective room;
2. clear a nearby corridor or doorway;
3. choose a position with limited approach angles;
4. activate the objective;
5. fight outside the target's blast radius when the layout allows it.

The mission label gives me the category. The Briefing tells me what actually has
to survive.

<h2 id="power-concentration">Use Station Type and Power Concentration to Predict Floor Loot</h2>

Power Concentration is not Reward and it is not a substitute for Difficulty.

I use station type to decide **what** I am hunting. I use Power Concentration to
judge how large the floor-loot opportunity may be.

A Network Center is a strong target when I need server racks, data-related items
or Magnum upgrade components. In a direct comparison, the higher-Power version
was preferred because it offered a larger item budget for the floors.

    to collect random weapons.
  </figcaption>

### Network Center

I target Network Centers for server racks and technology-related components.

### Farm

A high-Power farm can produce large amounts of food such as watermelons and
cabbage. I choose it when food is the bottleneck, not when the Magnum already
has several contracts of Calories.

### University or science facility

I search these for medical and laboratory components. A Medical Unit is a target,
not a guaranteed spawn.

### Military site or prison

I use these for weapons, ammunition and military equipment. I prepare for the
same equipment being used against me.

Station type narrows the likely containers. Power Concentration improves the
opportunity. Faction Technology and random generation still affect the result.

### Reward and floor loot are separate

Reward is the completion payout.

Floor loot is what I physically find, carry and extract.

A lower-Reward contract can create more campaign progress when it:

- finishes quickly;
- improves reputation with a useful faction;
- contains the station type that can produce the missing component;
- lets me use replaceable equipment;
- ends before another valuable contract expires.

I never add the listed Reward to an imaginary perfect backpack. Most floor loot
stays behind because of inventory space, damage, Calories or the need to leave.

<h2 id="read-objective">Read the Briefing and Evacuation Condition</h2>

I treat the mission label as a category and the Briefing as the real contract.

The Orphan Espionage objective requires me to:

- enter the mining base;
- locate the colnode commander;
- take the **PCPU**;
- return with it to the Magnum.

    className="mx-auto h-auto w-full max-w-4xl rounded-xl"
  />
  <figcaption className="mx-auto mt-3 max-w-4xl text-sm text-slate-400">
    Killing the commander is only one step. The PCPU must survive the return to
    the Magnum, so I reserve an inventory slot before entering the station.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

The useful preparation comes from the verbs:

- **take and return:** reserve a slot and protect the exit;
- **hack:** reach the device with enough supplies for the return route;
- **eliminate:** bring reliable damage for the marked target, then evacuate;
- **protect or hold:** clear a defensible position before triggering waves;
- **retrieve:** do not discard the mission item while reorganising loot.

I reread the objective before launch. That catches problems the loadout screen
cannot:

- no safe slot for the device;
- a melee-only build against a specialist target;
- too little food for the listed Floors;
- premium equipment risked on a short theft;
- no position prepared for a post-objective wave.

Mission events can remove the safety net I expected from the station.

One Orphan run announced after deployment that the level contained no Bandages,
First Aid Kits or other medical supplies.

Other events can alter food, ammunition, medicine or the enemies entering the

I cannot prepare for every random event. I can avoid making the contract depend
on a lucky drop.

Before launch I carry:

- a way to stabilize a wound;
- treatment for the most likely injury;
- an Infection answer when available;
- enough food and water for the Floors;
- enough ammunition to complete the objective without enemy drops.

<h2 id="bad-contract-board">When the Current Contract Board Is Bad</h2>

I have three options when every local contract is wrong:

1. wait and complete useful preparation aboard the Magnum;
2. travel to a system that already has several worthwhile jobs;
3. use Monitoring to create another random mission in the current system.

I use it when another random local mission would be useful. I save it when I am
about to travel, already have a good contract or need a specific story trigger
that random work cannot promise.

<h2 id="take-wait-turn-back">My Take, Wait or Turn Back Rules</h2>

### I take the contract when

- the Beneficiary serves the current campaign plan;
- harming the Victim does not remove access I still need;
- travel fits comfortably inside Time remains;
- the operator can survive the Difficulty and Floors;
- the station type can produce a needed item;
- the objective matches the weapon and inventory;
- the completion Reward justifies the supplies and risk.

### I wait when

- the route is useful but the operator is injured;
- the target can receive equipment above the normal faction level;
- one more ammunition stack or treatment would create a safe margin;
- the long trip becomes worthwhile only after several local jobs;
- another random contract would be useful;
- the relevant faction relationship needs repair.

### I turn back before launch when

- I cannot explain the completion condition;
- Change Side creates a loss I did not intend;
- travel consumes nearly all of Time remains;
- the larger Reward is the only reason I am accepting;
- Power Concentration is high but the station contains nothing I need;
- the objective requires a return item and the loadout has no safe slot;
- success depends on finding medicine or ammunition inside the mission.
