My first Quasimorph campaign did not fail because I misunderstood one weapon.

It failed because I stacked several small mistakes together: I left useful
ammunition inside a weapon on the Magnum, chose a contract before checking the
route, paired a class with the wrong firearm and kept exploring after the mission
had already given me a safe reason to leave.

I now treat the opening as one repeatable loop:

> unload the tutorial gear → secure basic production → stay near the Magnum →
> match the operator, class and weapon → complete the objective → extract before
> the safety margin disappears.

This page covers that first loop. Detailed contract math, loot priority, wound
treatment, Quasimorphosis control and Magnum upgrade order stay in their own
guides so the beginner route remains clear.

> Tested in Quasimorph 1.0 on fresh Normal-difficulty campaigns. Available
> contracts, starting operators, faction control and equipment vary between
> saves, so I use decision rules rather than fixed mission locations.

<h2 id="finish-tutorial">Finish the Tutorial and Empty the Operator</h2>

The tutorial is not a harmless practice room. I can take wounds, lose equipment
and die there, so I use the same habits I want in a real contract:

- reload before moving into another room;
- do not open an unknown door with my final Action Point;
- check the objective before descending;
- protect ammunition and medicine instead of treating them as tutorial items.

Once the boarding fight is over, I use **Unload All** before finishing the
mission.

I also unload ammunition from weapons I am not taking. A shotgun stored with the
only compatible shells still inside it is not helping the operator I actually
send.

### I keep irreplaceable gear aboard at first

The first available contract is not automatically worth my strongest weapon,
largest backpack or rarest medicine.

I begin with equipment I can replace while I learn:

- how long the elevator search takes;
- which damage types the enemy faction is using;
- how quickly the selected operator consumes Calories;
- whether the class actually develops with the firearm I brought;
- how much ammunition the mission length requires.

I risk better gear after the mission has a purpose, not simply because the item
has a higher damage number.

### I install the Conveyor when the opening allows it

From the Magnum compartment screen, I open the Supply branch and check the first
Conveyor node. When the starting cargo includes the required Welder, this is the
first practical ship action I take.

The installed base Conveyor starts with **one production slot** and **one queue
slot**. That is enough to begin producing licensed basic supplies instead of
depending entirely on random floor loot.

<h2 id="where-to-go-first">Where I Go After the Tutorial</h2>

I stay near the Magnum until I can replace a normal loadout.

The early System Map may show story markers, distant planets and ordinary
contracts at the same time. I do not treat the most dramatic marker as the next
mandatory destination.

My first route is local:

1. open the nearby systems and stations;
2. look for a contract at the low end of the available Difficulty;
3. check that the travel time leaves room before the contract expires;
4. read the floor count and objective;
5. confirm that the operator can complete it without relying on loot found
   inside the station.

I make a long trip only when it solves a specific problem:

- a story step I have deliberately chosen;
- a station that can provide a missing Magnum component;
- several useful contracts clustered around the destination;
- a faction relationship I am actively building.

I stay local when the distant job is ordinary repeatable work.

### I accept the first contract when

- it is nearby or already in the current system;
- its Difficulty is manageable for the available roster;
- I understand the completion condition;
- the floor count fits the food, medicine and ammunition I can carry;
- failure will not remove my only strong operator or irreplaceable weapon.

### I wait when

- the route requires a long trip for an ordinary reward;
- the story mission is several skulls above the local work;
- the only suitable firearm has no replacement ammunition;
- the operator is already injured;
- I cannot explain what must be hacked, killed, protected or carried back.

The first goal is not to reach a famous planet. It is to build a repeatable
contract-and-extraction loop.

<h2 id="prepare-operator">Choose the Operator, Class and Weapon Together</h2>

I do not select an operator and then independently equip my favourite gun.

The operator supplies base parameters and a unique talent. The class develops a
specific combat loop. The weapon determines whether those class skills can gain
experience during the mission.

A CQC-Specialist example shows:

- **Scatter -25%**
- **Shooting Accuracy +10%**
- support for **SMG, Shotgun and Assault Rifle**
- experience from firing the supported weapon

My preparation order is:

1. read the mission objective and expected enemies;
2. choose an operator whose Health and talent fit the risk;
3. choose a class that supports the available firearm;
4. equip armour for the expected damage types;
5. load the weapon before launch;
6. place compatible ammunition in the inventory;
7. add medicine, food and water;
8. leave room for the mission item and useful loot.

### My conservative first-contract loadout

For a short early mission, I usually start with:

- one loaded ranged weapon;
- one compatible ammunition stack;
- one melee backup;
- two Bandages;
- two Splints;
- one food item;
- one water item;
- Sorbent or an Antibiotic when available;
- one empty slot for the objective.

This is not a universal template. A larger map, a low-Calorie operator or a
weapon with high ammunition consumption needs more.

The purpose is to cover the common failure points without filling every slot
with fear.

<h2 id="movement-modes">Use Stealth, Walk and Run for Different Jobs</h2>

The current 1.0 UI calls the three modes **Stealth**, **Walk** and **Run**.
Community guides may also call them Sneak, Normal and Sprint.

I use the UI names throughout this guide.

### Stealth is my information mode

Stealth gives:

- **1 Action Point**
- **-1 kcal/AP**
- hidden-enemy detection
- free inventory and interaction actions

I use it before checking containers, corpses and uncertain doors. The low
movement output is the price I pay for information and free item management.

### Walk is my default combat mode

Walk gives:

- **2 Action Points**
- **-2 kcal/AP**

This is my normal fighting rhythm:

- move and shoot;
- shoot twice;
- reload and reposition;
- open inventory without Run restrictions.

I return to Walk before crossing an unexplored doorway.

### Run is for a checked route

Run gives:

- **3 Action Points**
- **-10 kcal/AP**
- **75% Overall accuracy**
- no normal inventory or interaction actions

I Run when:

- the corridor is already cleared;
- I need to break line of sight;
- the elevator is known;
- low Calories or another enemy group makes distance the immediate problem.

I do not Run into an unopened room. Three Action Points do not help when the next
tile is a closed door or the medicine I need is trapped inside an inaccessible
backpack.

<h2 id="check-quasimorphosis">Check Quasimorphosis Before Launch</h2>

I check whether the destination has active Quasimorphosis before filling the
last inventory slots.

When Q pressure is active:

- long contracts become more dangerous than the skull count alone suggests;
- flesh can solve Calories while increasing the meter;
- Ecolapse can create a new enemy during an otherwise controlled fight;
- Cigarettes or another reduction option may be more useful than extra trade
  goods;
- I need either a controlled exit or a credible Baron plan.

When the mission has no active Q pressure, I do not carry reduction items only
from habit. Those slots can support ammunition, medicine or the objective
instead.

<h2 id="treat-wounds">Stabilize the Wound, Then Recalculate the Mission</h2>

A wound changes the route before it kills the operator.

My immediate order is:

1. finish the active exchange;
2. leave Run if I need the normal inventory;
3. open the Health Monitor;
4. stabilize the wound with the cleanest available treatment;
5. decide whether the objective still justifies the remaining rooms.

I do not spend the best medicine merely because it has the best number. I use
premium healing when Health, several wounds, Pain or movement loss threatens the
next turn.

If the room is safe and the operator is stable, I use the targeted item and
reassess.

<h2 id="objective-first">Finish the Objective Before Optional Loot</h2>

I identify the completion condition before entering the first combat room.

The objective may ask me to:

- kill a marked target;
- hack a device;
- retrieve an item and return with it;
- protect a target;
- survive a defence phase.

I reserve one inventory slot when the mission requires a return item.

My floor route is:

1. identify the elevator or target direction;
2. use short detours only for ammunition, medicine or a named component;
3. complete the objective;
4. reassess Health, wounds, Calories, ammunition and Q pressure;
5. return by the shortest controlled route;
6. loot only when the next room solves a specific problem.

### I continue when

- the objective is secure;
- the elevator route is known;
- the main weapon still has a safe ammunition reserve;
- the operator has no uncontrolled wound;
- the next room contains a specific target such as medicine or a server rack.

### I leave when

- the objective or permanent unlock is already secured;
- Calories are threatening regeneration or movement;
- ammunition only covers the known return;
- the mission item occupies a slot I cannot protect;
- another fight would consume the last serious treatment;
- I am considering one more room only because the previous room was profitable.

Quasimorph usually punishes the room after the room where I should have left.

<h2 id="after-extraction">Reset the Magnum Before the Next Contract</h2>

After extraction, I use Unload All again.

I separate the haul into four decisions:

1. **next loadout:** compatible ammunition, medicine, food and practical gear;
2. **progression reserve:** chips, story items and Magnum components;
3. **barter:** surplus goods wanted by a useful station;
4. **dismantle:** duplicate or damaged equipment after unloading its ammunition.

Then I ask what actually limited the previous run.

- If no useful local work existed, I need a contract solution.
- If the elevator search consumed the run, I need a navigation solution.
- If a rare component almost died with the operator, I need an extraction
  solution.
- If supplies collapsed after one failure, I need production.
- If correct play still left no Health margin, I need stronger operators.

I do not buy a random node because the tree is open.
