There are **27 achievements** in Bills Must Be Paid, but I do not try to
complete them in menu order.

The cleanest route is to finish the accuracy challenges while the Hammer is
still slow, let the basic money and bill achievements unlock naturally, handle
the risky gambling and loan achievements with small amounts, and leave the
largest collections for a profitable late-game cycle.

My three-cycle route paid the main bill sequence in **Cycle 3**, stayed in
post-game to finish the Skill Tree, and entered **Cycle 4** only to purchase
the remaining Rings and Bracelets.

That is a useful route target rather than a requirement. A weaker or less lucky
build can take another cycle without making any achievement missable.

<h2 id="accuracy-achievements">
  Finish Accuracy and First-Hit Achievements Before Buying Speed
</h2>

`Not a Single Miss` and `Twice as Precise` become harder once the Hammer starts
swinging faster than I can comfortably move between targets.

I handle both at the beginning of a save.

My setup is:

1. keep the starting Hammer;
2. avoid Attack Speed upgrades;
3. aim slightly through the center of a Piggy instead of stopping at its near
   edge;
4. wait for the result screen before beginning the next run;
5. repeat the same controlled approach for the second consecutive run.

I do not chase a Piggy that has reached the extreme edge of the desk.

One safe hit is better than dragging the Hammer across empty space and
recording a miss.

Once both achievements are complete, accuracy becomes a source of bonus Coins
rather than a completion requirement. I stop abandoning profitable runs merely
because the percentage is no longer perfect.

### Strong Start needs Damage, Radius, and positioning

`Strong Start` is not simply a multi-hit achievement.

The **first swing of the run** must destroy two Piggies.

I wait until I have:

- at least two extra Starting Piggies;
- enough Base Damage to one-hit the opening Normalitos;
- enough Hit Radius to cover two overlapping targets;
- no automatic effect that removes one target before my swing lands.

At the start of the run, I do not swing immediately.

I move over the closest pair, wait until their bodies overlap, and let the
first hit cover both.

### Multismasher is easier with weak clustered Piggies

For `Multismasher`, three Piggies must be destroyed by the same hit or effect.

I do not attempt it against three high-HP Piggies.

I wait for several weaker targets, then use:

- a larger Hit Radius;
- enough Base Damage to finish all three;
- controlled Attack Speed so I can place the hit accurately;
- a naturally tight cluster rather than chasing targets across the desk.

A common failure is damaging three Piggies at once but killing only one or two.

When that happens, I return after another Damage or Hammer breakpoint instead
of repeating the same underpowered setup.

<h2 id="gambling-achievements">
  Do the Gambling Achievements With Disposable Money
</h2>

The gambling achievements are not difficult because of their written
requirements.

They are dangerous because a bad attempt can consume the cash reserved for the
next bill.

I complete `50/50` when Double or Nothing first appears and the stake is still
small.

For `All or Nothing`, winning is not required. I only need to wager the entire
current wallet, so I wait until:

- the next bill is already paid or fully reserved;
- I have spent most of the wallet on useful upgrades;
- losing the remaining amount will not end the cycle.

That turns an achievement with a large theoretical risk into inexpensive
cleanup.

### How I handle Eyes on the Piggy

Piggy Shuffle asks me to follow one selected Piggy through the shuffle and
smash the correct one.

I focus on the selected Piggy from the moment it is shown.

I do not try to read all three positions at once.

The early movement is trackable, but the final acceleration can become too fast
to follow reliably. My successful attempt ended in the middle position, but
that does **not** make the middle a reusable answer.

<h2 id="loan-achievements">
  Take and Repay One Big Toni Loan
</h2>

`Take a Loan` unlocks as soon as I accept Big Toni's offer.

`Pay Back a Loan` requires me to repay the displayed balance separately.

The dangerous detail is that Toni's daily earnings cut does **not** reduce the
principal.

In one offer, I borrowed **$410** at **10% interest** and owed **$451**.

A later profitable run lost **$1,560** to Toni's 5% daily cut, while the loan
balance still needed to be repaid separately.

<h2 id="cycle-achievements">
  Let the Bill and Money Achievements Unlock Across Cycles
</h2>

I do not protect a weak first cycle merely to avoid bankruptcy.

The cumulative achievement counters continue progressing, while bankruptcy
converts the paid bill value into Legacy Points for the next cycle.

My first route reached bankruptcy after:

- **5 bills paid**;
- **$2,015 total**;
- **40 Legacy Points awarded**.

That reset was more useful than spending several extra days delaying an
unavoidable failure.

The cumulative achievements need no separate farming route:

- `One Step at a Time` requires 5 paid bills;
- `Making Progress` requires 10;
- `Keeping Up` requires 20;
- `Rookie Smasher` requires 10 destroyed Piggies;
- `Serial Smasher` requires 100.

I focus on keeping each cycle productive rather than keeping one weak cycle
alive at any cost.

### Single-run earnings and current cash are different

`Better than Nothing`, `High Roller`, and `Five Figures` measure the money
earned during one run.

`The Richest Broke Person` checks whether I own **$1,000,000 at once**.

That distinction matters.

A run can earn more than $1 million and still fail to unlock The Richest Broke
Person if I immediately spend the wallet.

Once late Piggies produce six-figure payouts, I stop buying marginal nodes for
one or two runs and let the current balance cross the threshold.

<h2 id="super-jackpot">
  Buy the Super Jackpot Node Before Farming the Achievement
</h2>

The Super Jackpot achievement is not tied to Piggy Shuffle or Double or
Nothing.

I first need the Skill Tree node that gives each destroyed Piggy a small chance
to drop twice its best possible Loot.

The initial node costs **$2,000,000** and starts at **0.5% per destroyed
Piggy**, or roughly one trigger per 200 kills over a large sample.

I do not rush it while normal bills still consume most of my income.

I buy it when:

- the current Hammer can clear the newest Piggy tier;
- the next bill is already protected;
- $2 million no longer represents several full days of income.

After that, I continue normal farming.

A dry streak is possible at 0.5%. Raising the chance helps, but increasing the
number of Piggies I actually destroy per run is equally important.

<h2 id="collection-achievements">
  Finish Piggies, Coins, and Shop Items in the Winning Cycle
</h2>

`Piggy Bank Collector`, `Coin Collector`, and `Bought It All` are progression
achievements, but each has a different bottleneck.

### Piggy Bank Collector

New Piggies unlock as their progression fills and the bill sequence advances.

A progress bar can reach 100% before the next Piggy becomes usable. When that
happens, I pay the required bill and check the collection again instead of
assuming the unlock is broken.

Late Piggies have much higher HP.

If they fill the desk faster than I can destroy them, I stop buying Spawn Rate
and return to:

- a stronger Hammer;
- Base Damage;
- Crit Damage;
- Stamina;
- effects that remove the strongest high-HP blocker.

The achievement requires every Piggy type to be **unlocked**. I do not need to
maximize every Piggy level.

### Bought It All

I buy inexpensive Shop items when their cost is small relative to the next
bill.

I delay an expensive Hammer while the current one still clears the desk.

Near the end, that decision reverses. Once a new Piggy survives through most of
the run, another small Skill Tree node may not replace the raw stat jump from a
stronger Hammer.

The final Shop cleanup is easiest during a cycle already producing millions per
run.

### Coin Collector

Coins persist across cycles, so I do not abandon a strong run to chase one
missing effect.

I continue unlocking and destroying higher-tier Piggies.

The second-to-last Coin was the Duo Coin, which adds two Piggies at the start
of each run.

The final Coin restores **2% of maximum Stamina every tenth Hammer hit**.

<h2 id="freedom-ending">
  Freedom Is Not the End of the 100% Route
</h2>

The last bill sequence leads to King Piggy.

This is where a build that looked comfortable can suddenly fail a sustained
Damage check.

Before entering the final stage, I confirm:

1. the newest normal Piggies die fast enough not to block the desk;
2. the current Hammer has enough Stamina and Attack Speed to maintain a long
   combo;
3. I have purchased the large raw-Damage nodes rather than only Loot and Spawn.

My first King Piggy attempt did not finish the fight.

I returned to the normal upgrade loop, bought more Damage, Crit, Attack Speed,
and Stamina, then entered the encounter again.

During the successful attempt, maintaining the combo increased my swing speed
and sustained Damage.

Once I maintained that pressure long enough, King Piggy went down and the final
Taxes sequence began.

After the boss falls, the game initially says there are no bills left, then
reveals the final payment: **Taxes**.

Paying it reduces the displayed net worth to **$1** and unlocks `Freedom`.

<h2 id="post-game-achievements">
  Complete Maxed Out Before the Final Prestige Reset
</h2>

Post-game removes the pressure of another normal bill sequence, so I use the
profitable late-game build to finish the expensive Skill Tree branches.

The main trap is assuming the visible pre-boss tree is the complete tree.

The final King Piggy progression reveals additional high-cost Damage, Crit,
Attack Speed, Super Jackpot, and late-game nodes. They all count toward
`Maxed Out`.

When the achievement did not unlock after the visible branches looked complete,
I checked every side and lower branch again and found additional King Piggy
upgrades that still required cash.

I buy income-producing late nodes early enough to repay their cost, then finish
the remaining raw-Damage branches.

### Prestige Jewelry comes last

Once the Skill Tree is complete, I declare bankruptcy again and spend the new
Legacy Points on the remaining Rings and Bracelets.

The final expensive purchase in my route was the **Double Loot Ring**, costing
**1,000,000 Legacy Points**. It makes Piggies destroyed in one hit drop **100%
extra Loot**.

This order prevents a costly mistake:

- Skill Tree upgrades use current-cycle cash;
- Rings and Bracelets use permanent Legacy Points;
- declaring bankruptcy too early removes the profitable build needed for
  `Maxed Out`.

My final sequence is:

1. defeat King Piggy;
2. pay Taxes and choose Keep Playing;
3. complete every Skill Tree branch;
4. confirm `Maxed Out`;
5. declare bankruptcy;
6. unlock the remaining Rings and Bracelets;
7. confirm `Prestige Jewelry` and all 27 achievements.

<h2 id="faq">
  Bills Must Be Paid Achievements FAQ
</h2>

### Do achievement counters continue across cycles?

Yes.

Cumulative goals such as paid bills and destroyed Piggies continue progressing
after bankruptcy.

Coins and permanent jewelry also remain, while cash, Hammers, normal Skill
Tree upgrades, gadgets, and current Piggy progression reset for the new cycle.

### Does All or Nothing require me to win the gamble?

No.

The requirement is to go all in.

I use a small remaining wallet after the next bill has been protected, so a
loss does not destroy a productive cycle.

### Can I finish achievements after paying all the bills?

Yes.

After paying Taxes, I select Keep Playing.

This lets me continue earning cash, complete the remaining Skill Tree branches,
collect anything still missing, and prepare the final bankruptcy for Prestige
Jewelry.

### Should I abandon a run after losing perfect accuracy?

Only while working on `Not a Single Miss` and `Twice as Precise`.

After those achievements unlock, a missed swing does not block the remaining
completion route. I prioritize income, bill safety, and destroying the newest
Piggy tier instead of sacrificing a profitable run for a cleaner percentage.
