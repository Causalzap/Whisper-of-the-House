This Bills Must Be Paid guide is the starting point I use when a run stops
making sense.

I do not repeat all 27 achievements, every Skill Tree node, every Ring effect,
or the full completion route here.

Instead, this hub explains the decisions that affect every cycle:

- what I buy first;
- how I protect the next bill;
- how I diagnose the desk;
- when I stop investing in a failing cycle;
- what bankruptcy removes and preserves;
- and which detailed guide solves the current problem.

<h2 id="what-is-the-game">
  What Kind of Game Is Bills Must Be Paid?
</h2>

Bills Must Be Paid combines short Piggy-smashing runs with bill deadlines,
temporary upgrades, permanent Prestige progression, and a growing collection
of Piggies, Hammers, Coins, Rings, and Bracelets.

Each run gives me money, but that money has two competing jobs:

- pay the next required bill;
- strengthen the current build before later Piggies become harder to destroy.

That conflict drives most of the important decisions.

Buying every available upgrade can leave me unable to pay the deadline.

Saving every dollar can also fail because the build stops improving while the
bills and Piggy HP continue rising.

The goal is not simply to earn the largest possible wallet.

The goal is to spend enough to improve the next run while keeping the required
payment protected.

<h2 id="core-loop">
  How Progression Works
</h2>

The basic loop is:

1. begin a run;
2. destroy Piggies;
3. collect money;
4. identify what limited the run;
5. protect the next bill;
6. buy an upgrade only when it solves the active bottleneck;
7. pay the bill and unlock stronger Piggies, Hammers, and systems.

The buttons are easy to understand.

The difficult part is deciding which purchase will produce more money before
the next deadline.

### Current cash is temporary power

Cash purchases the current Hammer, gadgets, and normal Skill Tree upgrades.

Those purchases can make the present cycle much stronger, but they are not the
same as permanent Prestige progression.

That means I judge an expensive purchase by two questions:

1. Will it materially improve the next few runs?
2. Will the next bill remain safe after I buy it?

A powerful upgrade can still be the wrong purchase when it consumes money that
must be paid tomorrow.

### Bankruptcy is part of progression

Bankruptcy is not automatically a failed save.

Paid bills convert into Legacy Points, which fund permanent Rings and
Bracelets for future cycles.

<h2 id="first-cycle">
  My First-Cycle Plan
</h2>

Cycle 1 does not need to become the winning cycle.

Its job is to teach the basic systems, complete tasks that are easier with a
slow Hammer, pay several bills, and create the first useful Legacy Point total.

### Finish accuracy before buying much speed

I complete `Not a Single Miss` and `Twice as Precise` near the beginning.

The slow starting Hammer gives me more control over each swing.

Once Attack Speed increases, the Hammer becomes harder to place without
crossing empty space or recording an unnecessary miss.

After both achievements unlock, I stop sacrificing profitable runs for perfect
accuracy.

### Buy one solution at a time

My first purchases usually begin with:

1. enough Base Damage to make the opening Piggies manageable;
2. one useful Stamina upgrade;
3. Starting Piggies if the desk becomes empty;
4. Hit Radius or Collateral Damage when several targets overlap;
5. Attack Speed only after enough targets remain available;
6. Loot only after the build destroys Piggies consistently.

This is not a universal node order.

It is a starting sequence that changes as soon as the desk reveals a different
problem.

I do not buy every inexpensive node simply because it is available.

A cheap upgrade is still wasted money when it improves a resource that is not
currently limiting the run.

### Do not force Cycle 1 to survive forever

When the next bill requires income far beyond my recent normal runs, I stop
treating the cycle as recoverable.

Before resetting, I still try to:

- preserve the money required for one reachable bill;
- avoid unnecessary gambling;
- repay a manageable Big Toni balance when practical;
- convert the largest realistic paid-bill total into Legacy Points.

Dragging out a dead cycle delays the permanent upgrades that make the next one
stronger.

<h2 id="bill-reserve">
  Protect the Next Bill Before Buying Optional Upgrades
</h2>

The wallet total is not my real upgrade budget.

I first remove the amount required for the next payment.

    Current cash − protected bill amount = money available for upgrades

Before making an expensive purchase, I ask:

1. Is the next bill already protected?
2. Does this purchase solve the bottleneck visible in recent runs?
3. Can it repay its cost before the deadline?
4. Would a cheaper upgrade reach the same kill, Spawn, or Stamina breakpoint?

When the first answer is no, the purchase must create an immediate and
dependable improvement.

A small random chance is not enough.

### Paying early is not automatically better

Early payments can activate permanent Ring bonuses, but paying ahead also
removes days that could have been used to buy Hammers or Skill Tree upgrades.

I pay early when:

- a normal run already covers the current bill;
- the next essential Hammer remains affordable;
- the current desk can use the bonus;
- losing the remaining upgrade days will not create a new bottleneck.

I do not pay four days early for another Piggy when the existing high-HP
Piggies already survive through the entire run.

### Big Toni is a temporary bridge

I use Big Toni to close a small immediate gap rather than fund a speculative
upgrade plan.

His daily earnings cut does not reduce the displayed principal automatically.

The balance still needs to be repaid separately.

That makes an active loan increasingly expensive once the build begins
producing larger payouts.

<h2 id="bankruptcy">
  Use Bankruptcy to Improve the Next Cycle
</h2>

The first bankruptcy can feel like losing everything, but the useful question
is whether the resulting Legacy Points repair the previous failure.

Bankruptcy removes current-cycle progress, including:

- current cash;
- the current Hammer;
- gadgets;
- normal Skill Tree upgrades;
- current Piggy progression.

The bankruptcy interface confirms that Rings and the Coin Collection remain.

I therefore separate temporary power from permanent progression.

### What I buy after a failed cycle

I use the previous desk as the purchase guide:

- if Piggies survived too long, I buy permanent Damage;
- if the desk became empty, I buy Starting Piggies;
- if Stamina ended with targets remaining, I buy Stamina;
- if the build killed consistently but earned too little, I buy permanent
  Loot.

I do not divide a small Legacy Point total evenly across every category.

The next cycle becomes stronger when one real bottleneck is removed, not when
five unrelated values increase slightly.

### Manual bankruptcy is a planning tool

After bankruptcy has been introduced, later cycles can be ended manually.

Before using that option, I check whether another reachable bill would
meaningfully improve the next Legacy Point total.

Unused wallet cash does not replace paid-bill value.

When the cycle cannot recover, paying one more safe bill can be better than
buying one final upgrade that disappears during the reset.

<h2 id="ending-post-game">
  Do Not Reset Immediately After the Ending
</h2>

The main bill sequence is not the end of the 100% route.

My final progression order is:

1. reach King Piggy;
2. complete the boss sequence;
3. pay Taxes;
4. choose **Keep Playing**;
5. finish the newly available late Skill Tree branches;
6. confirm `Maxed Out`;
7. declare the final bankruptcy;
8. unlock the remaining Rings and Bracelets;
9. confirm `Prestige Jewelry`.

The important trap is resetting before `Maxed Out`.

The visible Skill Tree can appear complete before every late King Piggy branch
has been purchased.

Bankruptcy removes the current Skill Tree and the profitable build funding
those expensive nodes.

I therefore finish every cash-based upgrade before converting the final paid
bill total into Legacy Points.

<h2 id="faq">
  Bills Must Be Paid Beginner FAQ
</h2>

### Is there one best fixed Skill Tree build?

No.

The correct purchase changes when the desk changes.

I use Damage when Piggies stop dying, Spawn when the desk becomes empty,
Stamina when the run ends with targets remaining, and Loot when the build kills
consistently but the bills still grow faster than its income.

The main exception is the opening accuracy route: I delay substantial Attack
Speed until `Not a Single Miss` and `Twice as Precise` are complete.

### Is bankruptcy a failed run?

Not necessarily.

Bankruptcy converts the value of paid bills into permanent Legacy Points.

A controlled reset can be better than using several more days inside a cycle
that cannot reach its next payment.

The important question is whether I paid the last safely reachable bill before
resetting.

### Should I pay every bill as early as possible?

No.

Early payments become useful when permanent Rings reward the number of days
paid early and the current build is already ahead.

During a struggling cycle, keeping several upgrade days can be more valuable
than gaining a small Attack Speed bonus or one additional Piggy.

### Can I continue playing after paying Taxes?

Yes.

I choose **Keep Playing** so I can finish the late Skill Tree branches required
for `Maxed Out`.

I declare the final bankruptcy only after that achievement is complete.

### Do I have to finish the route in three cycles?

No.

Three cycles was one completed main-game route, but another full completion
took five cycles.

An additional bankruptcy does not invalidate the save. I continue from the
equivalent progression stage instead of restarting solely because the cycle
number is higher.
