Bills Must Be Paid becomes much easier when I stop treating every dollar as
upgrade money.

My wallet has three different jobs:

1. protect the next bill;
2. buy an upgrade that changes the current run;
3. create permanent progress through bills paid before bankruptcy.

Most of my failed cycles came from mixing those jobs together.

I spent bill money on an attractive Hammer, called Big Toni to repair the gap,
then discovered that his daily cut could cost several times more than the
original loan.

My reliable rule is simple: I protect the next bill first, buy only upgrades
that can repay themselves before the deadline, and declare bankruptcy once the
required income is clearly beyond my current build.

<h2 id="protect-the-next-bill">
  Protect the Next Bill Before Buying Another Upgrade
</h2>

Having enough cash for an upgrade does not mean I can afford it.

I first subtract the next bill from the wallet.

When a **$200 bill** is due tomorrow and I have **$320**, I treat the wallet as
only **$120 of spendable cash**.

I do not buy a $200 upgrade and hope the next run repairs the gap.

### With one day remaining

I reserve the complete bill amount.

I only spend below that reserve when the purchase creates an immediate,
measurable breakpoint, such as:

- a Hammer that lets me destroy the newest high-HP Piggy;
- enough Base Damage to turn a three-hit target into a one-hit target;
- a Loot upgrade applied to a desk I already clear consistently;
- enough Stamina to finish targets that are still alive when the run ends.

A small Luck node or another minor Attack Speed increase is not worth risking
the cycle.

### With two days remaining

Two days gives me more flexibility, but I still compare the purchase with a
normal run rather than a Jackpot.

I buy the upgrade only when:

1. one ordinary run can replace most or all of its cost;
2. the remaining day can still cover the bill;
3. the upgrade attacks the current bottleneck.

I skip it when repayment depends on Piggy Shuffle, Double or Nothing, Midas
Touch, or a rare Super Jackpot.

Those can rescue a run, but they are not reliable budgeting tools.

### With three or more days remaining

This is the best window for a major breakpoint.

A new Hammer, strong Damage node, additional Starting Piggy, or Stamina
improvement has several runs to repay itself.

I still avoid buying every affordable node.

Each unnecessary purchase increases the size of the rescue I may need later.

<h2 id="when-to-pay-early">
  Pay Bills Early Only After the Build Is Safe
</h2>

Early payment becomes powerful after permanent jewelry unlocks the related
bonuses.

Two important effects are:

- every **four full days paid early** adds **one Piggy** to the desk for the
  rest of the current cycle;
- each day paid early adds **2% Attack Speed** for the rest of the current
  cycle, up to a displayed maximum of **25%**.

These bonuses are useful, but paying early also removes days that could have
been used to buy Damage, Loot, Stamina, or a better Hammer.

That trade is the real decision.

### When I pay early

I pay early when:

- the current bill is trivial compared with one normal run;
- the next important upgrade is still several runs away;
- the desk becomes empty and another Piggy will create more kills;
- the Hammer has enough Damage and Stamina to benefit from more Attack Speed;
- I am deliberately accelerating toward a later bill or final sequence.

At that point, early payment converts surplus cash into immediate cycle power.

### When I keep the remaining days

I do not pay early when:

- a new Hammer is affordable within one or two runs;
- the current Piggy tier survives too many hits;
- I still need Damage, Crit, Loot, or Stamina;
- another Piggy would only add another high-HP blocker;
- paying now would leave almost no reserve for the following bill.

The extra Piggy bonus is not automatically good.

If my desk is already full of targets I cannot destroy, another Piggy makes the
problem worse.

### When I reconsider early payment

I check again after the bottleneck changes.

A cycle can begin with weak Damage, making early payment unattractive, then
reach a Hammer breakpoint that clears the desk too quickly.

That is when the extra Piggy and Attack Speed bonuses begin producing money
instead of pressure.

Each checkpoint asks a different question:

### The first bill is a spending-discipline check

The opening Water Bill is only **$20**, but it establishes the rule that causes
many later failures: cash shown in the wallet is not automatically upgrade
money.

When the bill is already due, I pay it before buying another small Damage,
Speed, Stamina, or Loot node.

The amount is too small for gambling, borrowing, or delaying to provide any
strategic value.

### Five paid bills produced the first permanent reset

My first completed cycle checkpoint was:

- **5 bills paid**;
- **$2,015 total**;
- **40 Legacy Points awarded**.

This is a cycle summary rather than one individual bill.

The game awards one Legacy Point for every complete $50 paid in bills. The
remaining $15 did not create a forty-first point.

I use that first reset to purchase immediate Cycle 2 strength rather than
trying to force a weak opening build through a much larger bill.

The Bail Bond required **$250,000 in five days**.

I keep:

I lose:

- current cash;
- the current Hammer;
- gadgets;

<h2 id="when-to-declare-bankruptcy">
  Declare Bankruptcy When the Math Stops Working
</h2>

I do not reset because one run was unlucky.

I reset when the remaining requirement no longer matches the build.

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-xl border border-slate-200 bg-slate-50 px-5 py-4 dark:border-slate-700 dark:bg-slate-900/50">
  <p className="m-0 text-xs font-semibold uppercase tracking-wide text-slate-500 dark:text-slate-400">
    Remaining bill gap ÷ days left = required income per day
  </p>
</div>

I compare that result with my last two or three ordinary runs.

I ignore the best Jackpot run because I cannot schedule another one.

### A cycle I continue

Suppose I still need **$8,000** with two days left and recent runs produce
around **$4,000 to $6,000**.

That cycle is still playable.

A modest Damage, Loot, or Hammer improvement can close the gap, and an ordinary
pair of runs may already be enough.

### A cycle I abandon

The clearest example is the $250,000 Bail Bond covered in the checkpoints
above.

Once I confirmed that ordinary runs were far below the required $50,000 per
day, Big Toni was unavailable, and no affordable upgrade created a meaningful
income breakpoint, I stopped playing out the remaining days and reset.

### My practical reset signals

I declare bankruptcy when several of these are true:

- required daily income is more than roughly twice my recent ordinary run;
- the newest Piggy survives for most of each run;
- I cannot afford the Hammer or Damage breakpoint needed to destroy it;
- Big Toni is unavailable or would only delay the same failure;
- gambling is the only remaining route;
- paying one more bill is no longer realistic;
- the available Legacy Points can buy meaningful permanent Damage, Loot,
  Stamina, or Starting Piggies.

I continue when one major upgrade can still change the calculation.

A doomed-looking cycle can recover after a Hammer breakpoint, a high-value
Piggy unlock, or a strong Loot node. I do not reset merely because the wallet
looks bad before checking those options.

### When I take the loan

I accept it only when all three are true:

1. the loan closes a small immediate bill gap;
2. the next normal run should cover the bill and the $451 repayment;
3. surviving this bill keeps a genuinely strong cycle alive.

A $410 bridge can make sense when I am only around $400 short and the next run
normally earns several thousand dollars.

### When I refuse the loan

I refuse when:

- the loan only delays a bill already far beyond my income;
- I need several days to repay it;
- current runs are profitable enough to create a large daily cut;
- I expect to need another loan during the five-day cooldown;
- bankruptcy already offers enough Legacy Points for a meaningful permanent
  upgrade.

Toni is not a source of general upgrade money.

I do not borrow to buy optional Loot, Luck, Spawn, or Attack Speed nodes.

### Why I repay immediately

The principal is fixed and visible.

The daily cut scales with earnings.

That creates an inverted penalty: the stronger my next run becomes, the more
expensive it is to leave Toni unpaid.

My repayment order is:

1. pay an immediate bill when it is due;
2. repay Toni;
3. rebuild the reserve for the next bill;
4. buy upgrades with whatever remains.

<h2 id="losing-cycle-route">

When a cycle starts falling behind, I use the same recovery order.

### Step 1: stop buying marginal upgrades

I stop buying small Luck, Attack Speed, and Loot nodes that do not change the
next run.

I save for one real breakpoint.

### Step 2: identify the blocker

I check whether the failure is:

- insufficient Damage;
- too few Piggies;
- insufficient Stamina;
- weak Loot;

<h2 id="faq">
  Bills, Loans, and Bankruptcy FAQ
</h2>

### Are all bill amounts and deadlines fixed?

I have not confirmed that across multiple fresh saves.

The listed checkpoints are verified from a completed Steam full-release route,
but a second clean run is still needed to confirm whether every intermediate
bill name, amount, deadline, and order is identical.

Until then, I use the list as a progression reference rather than a guaranteed
master sequence.

### Does paying a bill early create more Legacy Points?

Not by itself.

Legacy Points are calculated from the dollar value of bills paid. Paying the
same bill several days early does not increase its listed amount.

The early-payment benefit comes from enabled cycle bonuses such as additional
Attack Speed or another desk Piggy.

### Can I manually declare bankruptcy before missing a bill?

After the first forced bankruptcy introduces the system, a manual declaration
option becomes available.

I use it when the required daily income is clearly unreachable rather than
playing several more days with no realistic path forward.

### Should I spend all remaining cash before bankruptcy?

Only when the purchase can help me pay another reachable bill.

Cash does not carry over, but buying random current-cycle upgrades is also
pointless when no additional bill can be paid before the reset.

### Can Big Toni rescue a cycle that is already far behind?

Usually not.

Toni is effective when I am slightly short of an immediate bill and the next
run can repay him. He is poor at rescuing a build whose ordinary income is far
below every remaining deadline.
