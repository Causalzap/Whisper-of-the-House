Prestige in Bills Must Be Paid is not about buying every affordable Ring from
left to right.

I use Legacy Points to repair whatever made the previous cycle fail.

If high-HP Piggies blocked the desk, I buy permanent Damage.

If the desk became empty while Stamina remained, I buy Starting Piggies.

If the run ended with profitable targets still alive, I buy Stamina or a
first-kill refund.

If I already killed consistently but the bills still outscaled my income, I
buy permanent Loot.

That approach gives the next cycle an immediate advantage instead of spreading
Legacy Points across effects that do not solve the actual problem.

<h2 id="legacy-points">
  How Legacy Points Work
</h2>

Legacy Points are calculated from the total dollar value of bills paid before
bankruptcy.

The conversion shown by the game is:

    Legacy Point conversion
  </p>
  <p className="mb-0 mt-1 text-base font-semibold leading-7 text-slate-900 dark:text-slate-100">
    1 Legacy Point for every $50 paid in bills
  </p>

One cycle ended after I had paid **five bills totaling $2,015**.

The bankruptcy summary awarded **40 Legacy Points**.

I plan Prestige purchases using complete $50 blocks.

I do not count a partial final block as another available Legacy Point until
the bankruptcy screen actually awards it.

Paid bills matter.

Cash still sitting in the wallet does not receive the same conversion.

When a cycle is clearly doomed, I still try to pay one more reachable bill
before resetting. I do not gamble away guaranteed bill money merely to finish
the cycle with a larger wallet.

Legacy Points remain available across future cycles and are spent on permanent
Rings and Bracelets.

<h2 id="first-prestige">
  What I Buy After the First Bankruptcy
</h2>

My first Prestige is about fixing the opening minutes of Cycle 2.

I do not spend the first small Legacy Point total on an effect that requires a
late-game build or several early bill payments before it becomes useful.

My usual first-reset priority is:

1. permanent Damage;
2. Starting Piggies;
3. Stamina;
4. permanent Loot.

The exact order depends on what ended the previous cycle.

### Permanent Damage comes first when every Piggy is too slow

I buy permanent Damage first when one Piggy consumed too much of each run.

It helps from the first swing of the new cycle.

A small permanent Damage increase can:

- reduce the number of hits needed for an opening Normalito;
- make first-kill Stamina restoration arrive sooner;
- improve Strong Start and Multismasher setups;
- allow Loot upgrades to apply to more completed kills.

Damage is less urgent when the previous desk was already becoming empty.

That build did not need harder hits.

It needed more available targets.

### Starting Piggies repair a weak opening

Starting Piggies are one of my strongest early permanent purchases.

They improve the beginning of every run without waiting for Spawn Rate to
create the next target.

I prioritize them when:

- the previous desk regularly became empty;
- Stamina remained after every available target was destroyed;
- I wanted more opportunities for Collateral Damage or multi-hit effects;
- the first run of the new cycle rebuilt money too slowly.

I do not keep adding Starting Piggies when the desk is already full of targets
that survive through most of the run.

That situation requires Damage first.

### Stamina is useful only when time creates more kills

More Stamina is valuable when the previous run ended with killable Piggies
still available.

A first-kill restoration effect becomes especially strong once the opening
Piggy dies quickly.

One permanent option restores **15 Stamina after the first Piggy destroyed**.

I delay that effect when the first kill takes most of the run.

A refund that arrives near the end of the Stamina bar cannot repair weak
opening Damage.

### Permanent Loot multiplies a working build

Permanent Loot is one of the strongest long-term purchases, but I do not use
it to repair a build that cannot destroy its targets.

My order is:

1. make the Piggy die;
2. make another Piggy available;
3. keep the Hammer active long enough;
4. increase the payout from each completed kill.

That makes permanent Loot an income multiplier instead of a percentage attached
to unfinished targets.

<h2 id="early-speed-ring">
  Early Speed Ring Is Strong Only When I Can Pay Early
</h2>

The **Early Speed Ring costs 75 Legacy Points**.

Its effect is:

- gain **2% Attack Speed per day paid early**;
- keep the bonus for the rest of the current cycle;
- build toward a displayed maximum of **25%**.

This Ring creates no bonus when I wait until every bill reaches its due date.

That makes it less useful than always-active Damage, Stamina, or Loot during a
cycle where money is still tight.

### When I buy Early Speed Ring

I buy it when:

- one ordinary run can already cover the current bill;
- I can pay several days early without delaying a Hammer;
- the desk remains populated;
- Stamina can support additional swings;
- Crit and other on-hit effects benefit from more attacks.

Paying two days early produced a visible **4% Attack Speed** bonus in one
cycle.

Four days early would provide 8%, but I still compare that bonus with the value
of keeping four additional upgrade days.

### When I delay it

I delay Early Speed Ring when:

- the desk repeatedly becomes empty;
- the newest Piggy is an HP wall;
- Attack Speed already drains Stamina too quickly;
- the next important Hammer is only one or two runs away;
- I cannot safely pay bills ahead of schedule.

A permanent effect tied to early payment should not take priority over an
always-active upgrade when the current cycle is still struggling.

  Early Piggy Ring Fixes an Empty Desk, Not Weak Damage
</h2>

The **Early Piggy Ring costs 150 Legacy Points**.

Every **four full days paid early** adds **one Piggy to the desk** for the rest
of the current cycle.

This is not automatically better than permanent Damage.

I buy it after a cycle where:

- the desk repeatedly emptied before Stamina ran out;
- the Hammer already destroyed the current Piggy tier quickly;
- target availability was the clear income bottleneck;
- another Piggy would improve multi-hit and chain effects.

I do not buy it to repair a desk already packed with high-HP Piggies.

Adding another target in that situation only creates another body I cannot
finish.

### Early Speed or Early Piggy first?

Early Speed Ring costs half as much: **75 instead of 150 Legacy Points**.

When the desk already stays populated, additional speed can produce an
immediate return while I continue saving for Early Piggy Ring.

When the desk empties constantly, I reverse that logic.

I save for Early Piggy Ring instead of buying speed that has no target.

<h2 id="midgame-prestige">
  My Mid-Game Prestige Priorities
</h2>

A later bankruptcy provides enough Legacy Points to combine several permanent
systems instead of buying only one basic stat.

One route entered the Prestige shop with roughly **1,130 Legacy Points** and
could consider effects including:

- 20% more Damage;
- 20% more Loot;
- 15 Stamina after the first kill;
- +3 Base Damage;
- Early Piggy and Early Speed effects;
- 10% Crit Chance and 50% Crit Damage;
- 10% higher Piggy Loot;
- two additional Starting Piggies.

I still do not purchase those effects blindly from left to right.

My general mid-game order is:

1. flat Base Damage;
2. percentage Damage;
3. Starting Piggies;
4. one reliable Stamina effect;
5. permanent Loot;
6. Crit Chance and Crit Damage;
7. early-payment effects when the cycle can use them.

Flat Damage is especially useful at the beginning of a new cycle because the
starting values are still small.

Percentage Damage becomes stronger after Hammers and Skill Tree upgrades have
raised the base.

Crit becomes more dependable after I have enough Crit Chance to trigger it
regularly.

### Crit Ring is an inexpensive way to activate Crit scaling

The Crit Ring provides:

- **10% Crit Chance**;
- **50% Crit Damage**;
- a cost of **50 Legacy Points**.

<h2 id="do-not-buy-in-order">
  I Do Not Buy Rings in Menu Order
</h2>

Prestige prices increase sharply toward the late rows.

Verified examples already range from:

- **50 Legacy Points** for Crit Ring;
- **75 Legacy Points** for Early Speed Ring;
- **150 Legacy Points** for Early Piggy Ring;
- **1,000 Legacy Points** for Damage Ring III;
- **1,000,000 Legacy Points** for Double Loot Ring.

That price curve changes how I spend.

I do not delay several useful permanent upgrades merely to buy one expensive
Ring whose condition rarely appears in the current build.

### I save for an expensive Ring when it changes the cycle

An expensive permanent purchase is worth saving for when it:

- removes the high-HP Piggy blocking the desk;
- provides a large always-active Damage increase;
- adds enough Loot to shorten several future bill sequences;
- supports the final Skill Tree money grind;
- completes the remaining Prestige Jewelry requirement.

### I buy several cheaper effects when the build lacks balance

Several cheaper purchases can be stronger when the new cycle still needs:

- more Damage;
- more Stamina;
- more Starting Piggies;
- more Loot;
- some Crit Chance.

A balanced opening is more useful than one powerful effect attached to a build
that still lacks targets, Stamina, or reliable kills.

<h2 id="final-rings">
  Late Rings Solve Specific Problems and Finish the Collection
</h2>

The most expensive Rings are not required to reach the main ending.

One completed route paid the final bills during Cycle 3, continued through the
post-game Skill Tree, and entered Cycle 4 only to unlock the remaining
permanent jewelry.

That distinction matters:

- Rings needed to stabilize earlier cycles should be purchased earlier;
- extremely expensive Rings can remain until post-game;
- the million-point purchase is primarily a completion target after the main
  build already works.

### Orb Ring turns direct Crits into additional hits

Orb Ring makes a direct Critical Hit launch a bouncing orb.

Each orb hit deals **50% of current Hammer Damage**.

### The strongest-target effect solves a blocked desk

Another late permanent effect marks the strongest Piggy every five seconds for
30 seconds.

Hitting the marked target destroys it immediately.

This solves a specific late-game failure: one durable Piggy occupying valuable
desk space while weaker profitable targets remain difficult to reach.

I value that effect when the strongest Piggy is the actual bottleneck.

I do not prioritize it during early cycles where every target already dies
quickly.

The current screenshots include Orb Ring and Double Loot Ring, but not the

evidence for this separate effect.

Other late permanent effects include:

- 12% faster Hammer swings;

- a first-hit shockwave dealing 100% of current Damage;
- 10% slower Stamina drain;
- 10% higher Piggy Loot.

These effects make later cycles easier, but the main bill sequence can be
completed without owning every one of them.

<h2 id="final-reset-order">
  Finish the Skill Tree Before the Final Prestige Reset
</h2>

The final order matters because Skill Tree upgrades use current-cycle cash,
while Rings and Bracelets use permanent Legacy Points.

Bankruptcy removes the current cash and normal Skill Tree progress.

I therefore use this order:

1. defeat King Piggy;
2. pay the final Taxes bill;
3. choose **Keep Playing**;
4. use the existing late-game income to complete every Skill Tree branch;
5. confirm `Maxed Out`;
6. declare bankruptcy;
7. unlock every remaining Ring and Bracelet;
8. confirm `Prestige Jewelry`.

I do not reset immediately after the ending.

Doing so would remove the profitable late-game build before it has funded the
most expensive Skill Tree nodes.

<h2 id="faq">
  Bills Must Be Paid Prestige FAQ
</h2>

### Does $2,015 produce 41 Legacy Points?

No.

The bankruptcy summary awarded 40 Legacy Points for $2,015 in paid bills.

I plan around complete $50 blocks and do not count the remaining $15 as another
available point.

### Are Early Speed Ring and Early Piggy Ring always worth buying first?

No.

Both require early bill payments before they provide value.

I buy always-active Damage, Starting Piggies, Stamina, or Loot first when the
new cycle cannot safely pay several days ahead of schedule.

### Is Double Loot Ring required to pay all bills?

No.

The main bill sequence was completed before the million-point Double Loot Ring
was purchased.

It was part of the post-game route used to unlock every Ring and Bracelet.

### Should I prestige immediately after paying Taxes?

Not when `Maxed Out` is still incomplete.

I choose Keep Playing, finish every late Skill Tree branch with the existing
profitable build, and only then reset for the remaining permanent jewelry.
