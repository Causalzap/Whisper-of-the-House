The best upgrade in Bills Must Be Paid changes whenever the bottleneck moves.

I do not follow one fixed Skill Tree path from the first bill to King Piggy.

Instead, I watch what prevents the next dollar from reaching my wallet.

If the desk is empty, I need more targets.

If the desk is full, I need more Damage.

If the run ends while killable Piggies remain, I need more Stamina.

If I already clear the desk but the next bill is still outgrowing me, I need
better Loot rather than another small combat upgrade.

That approach carried one completed route through the main bill sequence in
three cycles. I then used the post-game build to finish the remaining Skill
Tree branches before the final permanent reset.

<h2 id="best-upgrade-order">
  Bills Must Be Paid Best Upgrade Order
</h2>

My opening order is usually:

1. Base Damage;
2. one useful Stamina upgrade;
3. Starting Piggies;
4. Hit Radius or Collateral Damage;
5. another Damage breakpoint;
6. Attack Speed;
7. Loot and Luck;
8. another Spawn upgrade only when the desk begins emptying again.

This is not a rigid sequence.

The first cycle starts with so few targets that Attack Speed can become a trap.

I can swing faster, but that does not create another Piggy to hit.

Early in one route, the desk held only **four Piggies**. Buying more speed at
that point would have made the Hammer wait for targets rather than increase the
number of kills.

My first goal is therefore not maximum speed.

It is a balanced loop where:

- another Piggy is normally available;
- the current Piggy dies in a reasonable number of hits;
- Stamina lasts long enough to use the available targets;
- each destroyed Piggy pays enough to keep the next bill safe.

Once all four conditions are true, I can buy upgrades for scaling rather than
basic survival.

<h2 id="diagnose-bottleneck">
  I Choose Upgrades by Reading the Desk
</h2>

The Skill Tree is easier to understand when I treat the desk as a diagnostic
screen.

I do not begin by asking which node has the largest percentage.

I ask which resource is currently being wasted.

I judge the build after a normal run rather than one unusually lucky payout.

A Jackpot can hide a weak setup.

Before spending most of the bill reserve on a major upgrade, I prefer to see
the same bottleneck appear in at least two ordinary runs.

<h2 id="damage-before-speed">
  Damage Comes Before Attack Speed When Piggies Stop Dying
</h2>

Attack Speed feels powerful because the Hammer immediately becomes more active.

It only creates meaningful value when those extra swings land on targets I can
actually destroy.

When a new Piggy tier appears and each target suddenly requires many more hits,
I return to:

- Base Damage;
- Crit Chance;
- Crit Damage;
- first-hit Damage;
- Collateral Damage;
- a stronger Hammer.

I delay additional Attack Speed when the desk is already full of high-HP
Piggies.

Faster weak hits consume Stamina and make the desk busier without removing the
target that is blocking the rest of the run.

### When I buy Attack Speed

I buy Attack Speed when:

- most current Piggies already die quickly;
- the desk stays populated for most of the run;
- Stamina lasts long enough to support more swings;
- Crit and other on-hit effects benefit from additional attacks.

At that point, speed becomes more than an animation upgrade.

Every additional swing creates another opportunity to finish a target or
trigger the rest of the build.

### When I stop buying Attack Speed

I stop when:

- the desk regularly becomes empty;
- accuracy drops because the Hammer moves faster than I can place it;
- Stamina ends much earlier than before;
- the strongest Piggy still survives through most of the run.

Those signs mean the build needs Spawn, Stamina, or Damage instead.

<h2 id="spawn-bottleneck">
  Starting Piggies and Spawn Rate Fix an Empty Desk
</h2>

The clearest Spawn bottleneck appears when I finish every available target and
then wait with Stamina remaining.

At that moment, more Damage has little immediate value.

The existing targets are already dying.

I buy:

- additional Starting Piggies;
- Spawn Rate;
- effects that create another Piggy;
- effects that help one kill reach another available target.

Starting Piggies are especially useful at the beginning of a cycle.

They improve the opening seconds of every run, make multi-hit setups easier,
and help create the starting groups required for Strong Start and
Multismasher.

Spawn Rate becomes more important after the opening group is already large
enough.

### When Spawn Rate becomes harmful

I stop buying Spawn when newly unlocked high-tier Piggies remain alive for most
of the run.

More Spawn can make that situation worse:

1. another high-HP Piggy enters;
2. the desk becomes crowded;
3. weaker profitable targets become harder to reach;
4. the run ends with several unfinished Piggies;
5. the additional targets produce no money.

When that happens, I return to Damage or buy a stronger Hammer.

<h2 id="stamina-upgrades">
  Stamina Is Valuable Only When More Time Creates More Kills
</h2>

I buy Stamina after confirming that the desk still contains killable targets
when the current run ends.

The most useful Stamina upgrades are not always the largest percentage nodes.

I value effects that solve a specific failure:

- more Max Stamina extends every run;
- lower drain supports a faster Hammer;
- restoring Stamina after a kill rewards a build that already kills reliably;
- every-tenth-hit restoration scales with long late-game runs;
- first-kill restoration improves the beginning of each run.

I do not prioritize Stamina when the desk becomes empty before the bar runs
out.

That is a Spawn problem.

I also do not use Stamina to compensate for a Piggy that consumes the entire
run without dying.

That is a Damage problem.

### The first-kill restoration check

A first-kill restoration effect is excellent when the opening Piggy dies
quickly.

It is much weaker when the first target is a high-HP Piggy that consumes a
large part of the Stamina bar before the refund arrives.

When that happens, I improve opening Damage first and return to restoration
after the first kill becomes reliable.

<h2 id="hammer-upgrades">
  I Buy a New Hammer at an HP Breakpoint
</h2>

A new Hammer is one of the few purchases that can change several bottlenecks at
the same time.

The **$1,000 Toy Hammer** raised the displayed Base Damage from **7 to 9**,
Attack Speed from **1.20/s to 1.76/s**, and Hit Radius from **2 to 2.30**.

That was a real breakpoint rather than a cosmetic purchase. It improved how
hard, how quickly, and how widely each swing landed at the same time.

I choose the Hammer when:

- the newest Piggy consumes most of the run;
- several cheap Damage nodes still do not reach the next hit breakpoint;
- the Hammer improves Base Damage and another stat I currently need;
- the next bill remains protected after the purchase.

I delay it when:

- the current Hammer still clears the desk;
- the real problem is a lack of targets;
- the purchase would consume money reserved for the next bill;
- several inexpensive Skill Tree nodes solve active bottlenecks for less.

In the middle game, four or five useful upgrades around **$3,000** each can
outperform one **$15,000** node.

That works when the smaller purchases improve different resources such as
Damage, Stamina, Loot, and Spawn.

It does not work when one stronger Hammer is the only purchase capable of
destroying the newest Piggy.

<h2 id="loot-luck-midas">
  I Buy Loot and Luck After the Build Can Already Kill
</h2>

Loot upgrades multiply completed kills.

They do not repair failed kills.

I begin moving toward Loot, Luck, Glowing Chance, and Midas Touch when:

- the desk remains populated;
- the current Hammer kills the newest normal Piggies;
- Stamina lasts through most of the available targets;
- the next bill requires a larger income jump than another small Damage node
  can provide.

Before that point, an extra Loot percentage may look efficient while applying
to too few destroyed Piggies.

### Damage creates the base; Loot multiplies it

My order is:

1. kill the target;
2. increase how many targets appear;
3. increase how long I can keep attacking;
4. multiply the value of each completed kill.

Reversing that order creates a build with strong payout effects attached to
Piggies that remain alive.

### Is Midas Touch mandatory?

No.

I completed the main route without relying on every Midas-related upgrade.

The cost was a longer post-game money grind.

That makes Midas Touch a farming accelerator rather than a mandatory
progression gate.

I prioritize it when the build already destroys high-value Piggies and many
expensive Skill Tree branches remain.

I delay it when the current cycle is failing because those high-value Piggies
do not die.

  Late-Game Skill Tree Priorities Shift Toward High-HP Piggies
</h2>

The late game usually does not fail because an early Normalito requires one
extra hit.

It fails when King Piggy or another high-HP late-game target occupies valuable
desk space for most of the run.

At that point, I prioritize:

- large Base Damage nodes;
- Crit Chance and Crit Damage;
- the next Hammer breakpoint;
- enough Stamina to maintain a long combo;
- Super Jackpot improvements only after the combat bottleneck is solved.

I stop buying general Spawn Rate when the desk is already blocked.

Adding another durable target does not improve income when the current one
cannot be removed.

Some permanent Rings and Bracelets can later mark, weaken, or remove the

The missing progress was not a random opening node.

Advancing the final bill sequence revealed additional King Piggy upgrades,

<h2 id="faq">
  Bills Must Be Paid Upgrade FAQ
</h2>

### Does Maxed Out require every Piggy level?

No.

`Maxed Out` checks Skill Tree upgrades.

Piggy levels matter because they unlock stronger targets and income, but they
are not part of the Skill Tree achievement condition.

### Do Skill Tree upgrades survive bankruptcy?

No.

Current cash, Hammers, normal Skill Tree progress, gadgets, and current-cycle
Piggy progression reset.

Coins and purchased Rings and Bracelets remain, which is why I complete the
late Skill Tree before the final bankruptcy.

### Does a 0.5% Super Jackpot chance guarantee one payout every 200 Piggies?

No.

Each destroyed Piggy receives an independent roll.

Two hundred kills describe a long-run expectation at 0.5%, not a guaranteed
trigger point. A dry streak can last longer, while two payouts can also occur
close together.

### Should I buy every cheap node before saving for a major upgrade?

Only when the inexpensive nodes solve different active bottlenecks.

Several small upgrades can outperform one expensive node when they improve
Damage, Stamina, Spawn, and Loot together.

I save for the major purchase when it creates a clear Hammer breakpoint,
removes the strongest Piggy, or unlocks an income mechanic the current build is
ready to use.
