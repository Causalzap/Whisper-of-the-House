Capturing enemies in Pax Autocratica is easy to misunderstand because pressing
the capture button is only the first half of the system.

The battlefield part is simple:

**carry Capture Rounds → weaken the target → press F to attempt the capture.**

The base part is where I initially got confused.

A successful captive does not immediately become one of my soldiers. Captives
travel back with the expedition, need Prison capacity, and still have to go
through the Prison's conversion process before they become useful labor.

The two rules that matter most are:

**lower target health = better capture chance**

and

**higher-quality Capture Rounds = better capture chance.**

I have also seen the interface reach **100% Capture Rate**, so I do not waste a
valuable round while the chance is still unnecessarily low if I can safely
weaken the target further.

<h2 id="how-to-capture-enemies">How to Capture Enemies in Pax Autocratica</h2>

<AdUnit placement="article_after_first_h2" />

The game introduces Capture Rounds during the early Elysia expedition.

Once I have Capture Rounds in my inventory, the controls are:

- **press F** to use a Capture Round;
- **hold F** to switch Capture Round types;
- lower the enemy's health to increase the chance of success;
- use a higher-quality Capture Round to increase the chance further.

That means I normally **do not open the fight with the Capture Round**.

I use my normal weapon first.

Once the target has been weakened enough, I stop firing before I accidentally
kill them and switch to the capture attempt.

This sounds simple, but it becomes harder when:

- my weapon deals large bursts of damage;
- another soldier is still shooting the same enemy;
- several enemies are pressuring me at once;
- I am trying to preserve one target while still surviving the fight.

When I know I want a specific enemy, I try to isolate that target and stop
treating the encounter like a normal DPS race.

<h2 id="capture-rate">How Capture Rate Works</h2>

The capture system does not require me to guess whether lowering health is
helping.

The game displays a **Capture Rate** for the attempt.

In one early capture, I was able to push that value all the way to **100%**.

That does not mean every enemy should always be reduced until the screen says
100%.

It means I have useful feedback before spending the round.

My decision is:

**if I can safely lower the enemy further, I improve the odds first.**

**if keeping the enemy alive is making the fight dangerous, I stop chasing a
perfect percentage.**

I do not invent one universal health threshold such as:

> always capture below 20% HP

because the game itself already gives me a better tool: the displayed Capture
Rate changes with the target and the Capture Round I am using.

<h2 id="which-enemies-to-capture">Which Enemies I Spend Capture Rounds On</h2>

A successful capture is not automatically a good use of a Capture Round.

That distinction matters early, when I do not have enough special ammo to take
every enemy home.

I save my rounds for targets I have a reason to want.

During one early expedition, for example, I held onto my remaining Capture
Rounds until I found a more interesting enemy carrying a sniper rifle.

I prioritize a capture when:

- the target looks more useful than the basic units around it;
- I do not already have plenty of similar soldiers;
- I can lower its health without turning the encounter into a survival problem;
- I still have enough health and ammunition to finish the actual expedition objective.

I stop forcing captures when those conditions disappear.

A prisoner is useful.

A failed expedition caused by refusing to kill a dangerous enemy is not.

<h2 id="can-you-capture-strong-enemies">Can You Capture Larger or Stronger Enemies?</h2>

I do not assume that a large enemy is automatically immune to Capture Rounds.

I successfully captured a much heavier-looking target during the early
expedition.

The problem I hit more often was not body size.

It was **running out of Capture Rounds before another useful target appeared**.

That is why I avoid spending the final round simply because an enemy is
capturable.

I would rather preserve one attempt for something I actually want.

The important limitation is that I only claim what I have actually captured.

I do not assume that every boss, elite or special enemy in the game can be taken
alive just because one large normal enemy worked.

<h2 id="where-captives-go">Where Captured Enemies Go</h2>

After a successful capture, the enemy does not immediately appear as a worker at
the base.

During the expedition, captured prisoners are stored aboard the ship with the
other supplies.

When I return to the colony, the transfer depends on Prison capacity.

The game explains it directly:

**if an available Prison exists, captives are automatically transferred into
it.**

**if no Prison space is available, the captives are temporarily stored in my
inventory.**

That second rule is important because it prevents one of the easiest
misdiagnoses in this system.

If I return and cannot find the prisoner inside a cell, I do not immediately
assume:

> my capture failed

or

> the captive disappeared.

I check whether I actually have available Prison space.

The battlefield capture has already succeeded.

The current bottleneck is now the base.

useful base step is straightforward.

I go to the **Command Center** and research the **Prison construction

I prefer to solve that bottleneck before launching another expedition where I

There is little value in filling temporary storage while I still cannot process
the prisoners I already have.

The route is:

**capture enemy → return to base → automatic Prison transfer if space exists →
detention and conversion → new soldier.**

That is the sequence I keep in mind.

<h2 id="prison-capacity">What Happens When the Prison Is Full?</h2>

A full Prison does not undo a successful capture.

If no Prison space is available when I return, the prisoner can remain
temporarily stored in my inventory.

So Prison capacity changes what I build next.

If I have empty cells, capacity is not my problem.

If captives are repeatedly waiting because every cell is occupied, then the
Prison has become the bottleneck.

This is when an upgrade starts making sense.

In one early setup, upgrading the Prison gave me **two usable cells**.

I do not turn that into a universal build-order rule.

I simply use capacity to answer the question:

**is this upgrade solving a problem I actually have right now?**

If there is still empty Prison space, I usually spend the resources somewhere
else.

<h2 id="convert-prisoners">How Prisoner Conversion Works</h2>

Putting someone in Prison is still not the same thing as recruiting them.

The Prison interface tracks the captive's resistance through values including
**Willpower** and **Convictions**.

This is why I think of the Prison as a process rather than a recruitment button.

The captive enters detention.

Their resistance is worked down.

Only after that process succeeds do I get the workforce benefit I originally
captured them for.

That also means bringing home five prisoners at once is not automatically five
new workers.

Prison capacity and conversion speed still matter.

on detention.

The two actions I used were:

In my early Prison, these prisoner operations worked on a **once-per-day**
cadence.

So my normal Prison loop is not:

**open Prison → click until recruitment completes.**

It is:

1. check which captives are being held;
2. use the available action when appropriate;
3. return to the rest of the colony;
4. let time progress;
5. come back when the next Prison operation is available.

That keeps the Prison from turning into something I have to babysit constantly.

<h2 id="capture-round-management">How I Manage My Last Capture Rounds</h2>

The most frustrating capture failure I had was not losing a percentage roll.

It was finding a target I wanted after I had already spent the special ammo.

Capture Rounds can appear again during an expedition, so the first tutorial
supply is not necessarily all I will ever see.

But I still do not assume:

> I can spend everything because the next location will replace it.

Sometimes I find more.

Sometimes I reach the next valuable enemy with none left.

So when my supply is low, I ask four questions:

- is this enemy actually different enough to be worth the round?
- can I lower its health safely?
- do I already have a captive waiting for Prison space?
- am I more likely to regret spending this round now or saving it for the next objective?

That gives me a much better rule than trying to maximize the number of prisoners
per expedition.

**Capture the enemies I have a reason to use.**

<h2 id="after-conversion">What Changes After Prisoners Join the Colony</h2>

Once the first prisoners actually join the base, the Capture system has done its
job.

The next problem is no longer:

**how do I get more people?**

It becomes:

**what do I do with the people I already have?**

New soldiers give me more labor, but they also create more assignments and more
base-management pressure.

If important jobs still cannot be staffed, capturing another useful enemy may
make sense.

If everyone is already assigned but several soldiers are becoming Exhausted or
Overworked, that is no longer a Capture problem.

<h2 id="my-capture-rule">My Capture and Prisoner Rule</h2>

I do not use Capture Rounds as a collectible mechanic where every enemy has to
come home.

I use them to solve a workforce problem.

My decision path is:

**valuable target → lower health → check Capture Rate → press F**

**successful capture → finish the expedition instead of risking everything for
more prisoners**

**return with Prison space → captive transfers into the Prison**

**no Prison space → check temporary inventory storage**

**Prison occupied → reduce Willpower and Convictions over time and use available
conversion actions**

**workers already sufficient → stop capturing randomly**

That is the part of the system that made everything much clearer for me:

**the goal is not to capture the most enemies. It is to turn the right captures
into useful colony growth without ruining the expedition or overloading the
Prison.**
