Core Fragments are where my Pax Autocratica expeditions start turning into
actual builds.

A single choice can change how my weapon behaves. A duplicate can turn that
effect into a much stronger direction. A general upgrade such as Extended
Magazine can become more valuable than a flashy damage proc if it fits the gun
I am already using.

The important thing I had to learn is that there are **two different layers of
progression** here.

The Core Fragments I collect during an expedition belong to that run and are
reset when I return to base.

But the game also gives me **Core Seeds**, which can unlock additional Core
Fragments for future expeditions.

So I do not treat every fragment as permanent power, but I also do not treat
the entire Core system as disposable.

My rule is:

**build for the expedition I am in, strengthen effects that are already working,
and stop overinvesting when I know I am about to return.**

<h2 id="what-core-fragments-do">What Core Fragments Do in Pax Autocratica</h2>

<AdUnit placement="article_after_first_h2" />

During an expedition, I find **Core Fragment Storage Pods** that let me choose
between several upgrades.

The collection screen can contain both broad combat effects and
weapon-specific choices.

That is why I do not think of the system as a simple passive-stat tree.

One pod may offer something that changes my magazine or adds an automatic
damage effect, while another can modify a specific weapon such as the Laser
Rifle.

The game also makes one important rule clear:

**getting the same Core again can enhance it.**

That means my first choice matters twice.

I am not only choosing what helps right now.

I may also be choosing the effect that later duplicate upgrades will build
around.

So before I select anything, I ask:

**what problem is my current weapon actually having?**

Is it:

- not enough sustained fire;
- weak multi-target pressure;
- poor damage against tougher enemies;
- too much downtime from reloading;
- or simply no strong effect tying the build together yet?

That answer usually gives me a better Core choice than chasing rarity or
assuming there is one universal best fragment.

<h2 id="core-terminology">Core Fragments vs Weapon Cores and Combination Cores</h2>

The terminology around the Core system can be confusing.

The expedition interface I actually interact with calls the selectable upgrades
**Core Fragments**, and the collection screen tracks them as part of my current
run.

Inside that system, I can also see different kinds of fragments.

Some are broad **General** effects.

Others are explicitly weapon-specific. For example, one of my choices is shown
as:

**Weapon · Laser Rifle**

with **Nebula Assault Rifle** as the effect.

That is the distinction I use on this page:

- **Core Fragment** = the selectable run upgrade I collect during an expedition;
- **weapon-specific Core effect** = a fragment tied to a particular weapon;
- **general Core effect** = an upgrade that changes a wider combat rule such as magazine size or reload damage.

You may also see broader references to **Weapon Cores** or **Combination Cores**
when reading about Pax Autocratica's Core system.

I do not treat those words as a reason to invent a second inventory or a
separate permanent build system.

For the practical choices on this page, I follow the names and categories
actually shown by the expedition Core Fragment UI.

<h2 id="early-core-choices">How I Choose Between Early Core Fragments</h2>

The early choices that taught me the most were:

- **Nebula Assault Rifle**
- **Electric Bouncer Core**
- **Missile Core**

They solve different problems, so I do not rank them as a universal
first-to-third tier list.

### Nebula Assault Rifle for sustained single-target pressure

The **Nebula Assault Rifle** is a Laser Rifle effect.

Its beam burns the target for **60%–80% Weapon Damage**, and the damage rises
the longer I keep the beam on the same enemy.

  </figcaption>
</figure>

I like it when I already have the Laser Rifle and expect fights where one tough
enemy matters more than clearing a group instantly.

The key phrase there is:

**already have the Laser Rifle.**

I do not take a weapon-specific effect just because I like the description if
my current loadout cannot benefit from it.

### Electric Bouncer when groups are the bigger problem

The **Electric Bouncer Core** gives attacks against enemies within **50 meters**
a **20% chance** to release an electric bouncer.

That bouncer can jump between targets up to **two times**.

This is the choice I prefer when ordinary encounters are crowded enough that
extra automatic chain pressure is more useful than concentrating everything
into one target.

It is also one of the Core lines that became much more interesting once
duplicates started appearing.

### Missile Core when I am already fighting inside 50 meters

The **Missile Core** works at the same **50-meter** range but behaves
differently.

Attacks have a **50% chance** to launch **two homing missiles**, and their
damage scales with my weapon strength.

That is strong when my normal combat rhythm already keeps me inside the
activation range.

I value it less when I am constantly trying to fight farther away or when my
existing Core build has already developed a stronger identity.

So instead of asking:

> Which of these three is the best Core?

I ask:

- **Laser Rifle already equipped and I can hold aim?** Nebula becomes attractive.
- **Groups are slowing the run down?** Electric Bouncer makes more sense.
- **I am consistently fighting inside 50m?** Missile Core gets much easier to justify.

<h2 id="choose-for-current-weapon">Pick for the Weapon I Already Have</h2>

This is the mistake I try hardest not to make:

**building around a weapon I do not actually have.**

Once the Core selection screen is open, I want to know what my current gun needs
before I start comparing descriptions.

If my weapon already has strong damage but spends too much time reloading, I look
for magazine or reload synergy.

If I am firing continuously and staying on one target, I value upgrades that
reward sustained fire.

If groups are the problem, I value chain or multi-target effects.

I do not select a Core because I am imagining some perfect weapon that might
drop three encounters later.

The gun keeping me alive right now gets priority.

That rule also makes later duplicate choices easier because the run starts
developing around one coherent direction instead of five unrelated bonuses.

<h2 id="duplicate-core-upgrades">How Duplicate Core Fragments Strengthen a Build</h2>

Electric Bouncer gave me the clearest example of why duplicate Cores matter.

After the basic effect was already active, later Core choices started offering
upgrades to the same mechanic rather than forcing me to begin a completely new
line.

One option allowed an Electric Bouncer to **split into three additional
bouncers after the first hit**.

If Electric Bouncer is already triggering often and helping me clear groups,

unrelated mechanic.

My usual rule is:

**early expedition → I am willing to start a new direction.**

**mid or late expedition → I prefer strengthening the direction that has already
proved itself.**

There are exceptions, but this prevents the build from becoming a collection of
half-developed ideas.

<h2 id="extended-magazine-power-reload">Extended Magazine vs Power Reload</h2>

Two of the most useful general upgrades are also a good example of why I do not
rank Core Fragments without looking at the weapon.

**Power Reload** gives me **+5% Weapon Damage for 10 seconds** every time I
reload.

**Extended Magazine** increases magazine capacity by **100%**, except on weapons
with a one-round magazine.

  <Image

    width={1280}

    className="mx-auto h-auto w-full max-w-4xl rounded-xl"
  />
  <figcaption className="mx-auto mt-3 max-w-4xl text-sm text-slate-400">
    Power Reload rewards frequent reloads with +5% Weapon Damage for 10 seconds,
    while Extended Magazine doubles magazine capacity on eligible weapons.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

Neither one is universally better.

### I choose Extended Magazine when

- sustained fire is already important;
- stopping to reload is breaking my damage rhythm;
- a weapon-specific effect benefits from staying on target;
- doubling the magazine lets me exploit an effect for noticeably longer.

This became particularly valuable with a laser-focused build because I could
hold pressure on the same target for much longer.

### I choose Power Reload when

- the weapon naturally reloads often;
- I can reliably trigger the buff without changing how I play;
- the 10-second damage window will stay active through most of the next exchange.

So the decision is not:

> magazine good, reload bad

or the reverse.

It is:

**does my weapon want longer firing windows or more value from frequent reload
cycles?**

<h2 id="what-resets">What Resets When I Return to Base</h2>

This is the most important persistence rule in the Core system.

When I choose **Return to Base**, the game warns that:

**Expedition Progress and the Core Fragments collected during the expedition
will be reset.**

<h2 id="core-seeds">What Core Seeds Do Differently</h2>

The reset warning also reveals the part of the Core system that I keep separate
from the current run.

**Core Seeds can be used to unlock more Core Fragments.**

Those newly unlocked fragments can then expand the pool of choices available in
future expeditions.

There is one important limitation:

**a Core Fragment newly unlocked with Core Seeds does not start appearing in the
expedition I am already running.**

That means I do not confuse:

**collecting a Core Fragment now**

with

**unlocking more possible Core Fragments for later runs.**

This distinction fixes the biggest misconception I had about the system.

The exact Core build I am carrying is temporary.

The wider set of Core options I can unlock is part of the longer progression
layer.

So returning to base does not make Core progression meaningless.

It resets the **current build**, not the entire idea of expanding what future
runs can roll.

<h2 id="when-to-commit">When I Commit to One Core Build</h2>

I commit harder to a Core line when three things are true:

- the effect is already contributing to fights;
- I expect the expedition to continue for a while;
- the next choice clearly strengthens the same combat plan.

Electric Bouncer is the easiest example.

If the basic bouncer is already useful, then additional split projectiles,
greater range and more jump damage all improve something I know is working.

That is a much easier investment to justify than starting a completely unrelated
effect late in the expedition.

I commit less when:

- I am close to returning;
- the offered effect does not fit my current weapon;
- the new Core would need several more upgrades before becoming useful;
- ordinary combat is already comfortable and the real problem is survival,
  healing or another part of the loadout.

This keeps me from building Cores just because the game offered another card.

Every fragment still has to solve a problem.

<h2 id="before-boss">How I Treat Core Fragments Before a Major Fight</h2>

Before a Decisive Battle or sector boss, I care more about **coherence** than
about having a specific named meta build.

If Electric Bouncer has already become the main source of extra group pressure,
I keep supporting it.

If my Laser Rifle already has a weapon-specific effect and enough magazine
capacity to maintain the beam, I do not throw that synergy away just to start a
new Core line.

I also do not tell myself that one exact Core combination is required for
MEC-KONG 7X.

The successful boss run still depended on health management, weapon performance
and using the combat tools available to me.

<h2 id="my-core-rule">My Core Fragment Rule</h2>

I no longer try to solve Core Fragments with a universal tier list.

I use a much simpler sequence.

**First useful pod:**  
I choose the effect that best fits the weapon already in my hands.

**Mid-run:**  
If that effect is working, I prioritize upgrades that make the same build more
reliable.

**Weapon-specific choice:**  
I only take it when I am actually using the weapon it modifies.

**Near the end of the expedition:**  
I judge the fragment by how many fights are left, not by how strong it would be
in an imaginary longer run.

**Back at the base:**  
I accept that the collected Core Fragments have reset, then use the longer-term
Core Seed system to expand what future expeditions can offer.

That is the distinction that finally made the system click for me:

**Core Fragments build the current expedition. Core Seeds expand the
possibilities of future expeditions.**
