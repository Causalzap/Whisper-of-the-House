The first time several of my soldiers became exhausted in Pax Autocratica, I
assumed the colony simply needed more workers.

That was the wrong diagnosis.

I already had soldiers mining, logging, researching, transporting resources and
staffing buildings. The real problem was that I had pushed the workday too far.

The confusing part is that the colony-wide schedule is **not** changed from the
normal soldier assignment screen.

The route I actually use is:

**repair Victory Square → research Unified Labor → open Directives → select
Unified Labor → change Daily working hours → Issue Directive.**

If one soldier is already exhausted, I handle that separately with **24 hours
of Paid Time Off**.

That gives me two different tools:

**Unified Labor fixes the schedule.**

**Paid Time Off recovers an individual soldier who has already been pushed too
far.**

<h2 id="why-soldiers-get-overworked">Why My Soldiers Became Overworked</h2>

The problem did not really show up while the base was tiny.

It appeared once the same workforce was supporting several systems at the same
time.

I had soldiers assigned to things such as:

- mining;
- logging;
- research;
- construction;
- resource transport;
- food and other staffed facilities.

At that point, keeping every available soldier busy looked efficient.

It was not.

Once I started seeing **Exhausted** and **Overworked** across several different
jobs, I stopped treating those states as isolated worker problems.

<h2 id="change-work-schedule">How I Actually Change Daily Working Hours</h2>

Once Unified Labor is researched, I go to the **Directives** tab.

Then I select:

**Unified Labor**

This opens the schedule control where I can change **Daily working hours**.

The screen shows the actual active work period, so a 20-hour day should read:

**0:00–20:00**

not 0:00–24:00.

**Victory Square → Directives → Unified Labor → Daily working hours → Issue

That is the route I use whenever the entire colony needs a schedule adjustment.

<h2 id="20-hour-workday">Why a 20-Hour Workday Became Too Aggressive for My Base</h2>

I used a **20-hour workday** while trying to keep production moving.

At first, the extra hours looked useful.

There was always something unfinished:

research, construction, resource production, logistics.

The problem appeared when several workers started showing **Overworked** rather
than recovering normally.

That changed the calculation.

Twenty working hours leaves only four hours outside the scheduled work period.

Once the colony was busy enough, I was getting more nominal working time but
creating a workforce that repeatedly needed recovery.

That is not free production.

I keep a longer schedule when:

- most soldiers are still in good condition;
- only one worker is tired after an unusual assignment;
- I am making a short push to finish something important;
- the base is not showing repeated Overworked states across unrelated jobs.

I shorten it when:

- multiple workers are becoming Exhausted;
- different facilities are showing the same problem;
- soldiers become Overworked before their scheduled work period is finished;
- I keep needing individual Time Off just to maintain normal production.

The number itself is not the goal.

**A sustainable workforce is.**

<h2 id="paid-time-off">Use 24h Paid Time Off for a Soldier Who Is Already Exhausted</h2>

Changing Unified Labor fixes the next work cycle.

It does not instantly repair a soldier who is already exhausted.

For that, I use **Paid Time Off**.

The Assign Work interface includes a **24h Paid Time Off** option once the
corresponding policy has been researched at Victory Square.

**One soldier exhausted after a demanding assignment?**

Paid Time Off may be enough.

**Several soldiers across several jobs repeatedly exhausted?**

I change Unified Labor.

If I find myself handing out 24-hour leave to half the colony, the problem is
not the individual workers anymore.

It is the schedule.

<h2 id="15-hour-workday">Why I Temporarily Dropped the Workday to 15 Hours</h2>

Later, the warning became much clearer.

Workers were becoming overworked while the workday was still running.

At that point, I considered a shorter schedule and eventually set the colony to
a **15-hour workday**.

That means the schedule becomes:

**0:00–15:00**

I want to be careful with that number.

**15 hours is not the proven best Pax Autocratica work schedule.**

It is the value I used when a 20-hour day had become too aggressive for that
colony.

I even considered going lower before settling on 15.

That is why I would not tell every player:

> always set 15 hours

Instead, I use the change as a test.

**20 hours → repeated overwork → reduce to 15 → let another work cycle run →
reassess.**

If exhaustion improves and essential jobs remain staffed, then the problem was
at least partly the schedule.

If important facilities are still constantly unstaffed after the workforce has
had enough rest, then I start looking at labor capacity.

<h2 id="schedule-or-worker-shortage">Schedule Problem or Real Worker Shortage?</h2>

This is the decision that stops me from capturing more people for the wrong
reason.

### I treat it as a schedule problem when

The jobs already have soldiers assigned, but those soldiers repeatedly become
Exhausted or Overworked.

In that case, adding another worker can hide the symptom without fixing the
cause.

I first:

**reduce working hours → recover anyone already exhausted → run another cycle.**

### I treat it as a labor shortage when

Important facilities regularly cannot be staffed because every available
soldier already has another essential assignment.

That is different.

If the schedule is sustainable but I simply do not have enough bodies to cover
the jobs I need, additional labor has an obvious purpose.

This is when capturing and converting another useful soldier makes sense.

The distinction I use is:

**staffed but exhausted = check schedule.**

**important job cannot be staffed = check workforce size.**

That is much more useful than reacting to every production slowdown by recruiting
again.

<h2 id="before-expedition">What I Check Before Leaving the Base</h2>

I care about working hours most when I am about to leave on another long
expedition.

I do not want to launch while the base is already showing a colony-wide
Overworked pattern.

Before departure, I check:

1. whether the important facilities I currently care about are staffed;
2. whether several soldiers are already Exhausted or Overworked;
3. whether the active Unified Labor schedule is still sustainable;
4. whether one critical worker should receive Paid Time Off before I leave.

If those are under control, I stop micromanaging and start the expedition.

I do not delay Elysia just because one optional facility is missing a worker.

<h2 id="my-work-schedule-rule">My Work Schedule Rule</h2>

I do not think Pax Autocratica has one magic working-hours number that every
colony should copy.

What matters is whether the current workforce can sustain the schedule I have
given it.

My decision path is:

**one exhausted soldier → Paid Time Off**

**several exhausted soldiers → check Unified Labor**

**20 hours producing repeated Overworked states → shorten the day**

**15 hours stabilizes the workforce → keep testing instead of immediately
raising it again**

**sustainable schedule but important jobs remain unstaffed → now consider more
workers**

That is the distinction that fixed the problem for me.

The question is not:

**how many hours can I force the colony to work?**

It is:

**how many hours can this workforce work before the extra production starts
creating a bigger recovery problem than it solves?**
