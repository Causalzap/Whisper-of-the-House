Elysia is where Pax Autocratica first stops feeling like a guided opening and
starts asking me to judge how much risk one expedition is actually worth.

The base and the sector feed each other:

**I push Elysia until the run stops being efficient → return with something
useful → turn temporary gains into permanent strength → leave again with a
specific progression goal.**

The mistake I made early was treating Elysia like one long mission that I was
supposed to finish in a single trip.

It is not.

This walkthrough starts once my **Command Center and Barracks are already
online, my first weapon is equipped, and I am ready to board the shuttle**.

From here, the route is:

blockade → defeat MEC-KONG 7X → secure Elysia.**

<h2 id="first-expedition">Start the First Elysia Expedition</h2>

<AdUnit placement="article_after_first_h2" />

Once the opening base is ready, I equip my weapon and head for the shuttle near
the Command Center.

Before departure, I can assign an accompanying soldier.

I take one on the first trip.

At this point I still do not know how expensive a normal Scourgeorn fight will
be, how quickly my current weapon clears enemies, or which locations are worth
pushing. An extra gun is more useful to me here than squeezing one more worker
out of the base.

The first important thing Elysia teaches me is that an expedition is not one
continuous battlefield.

I move through the sector map, collect space resources and reveal destinations
that I can choose to explore.

After the first space-resource collection, the nearby **Scourgeorn outpost** is
marked and the sector begins opening up.

This is where I stop treating every new icon as an instruction.

Some destinations give me resources.

Some lead into useful combat rewards.

Some are worth delaying until I have returned with better permanent progress.

My first Elysia run is successful if it gives me a better base and a clearer
next objective.

It does not need to finish the sector.

### Capture only when the target is worth the risk

Early Elysia also introduces Capture Rounds.

I use them when I see an enemy that is actually worth bringing home, but I do
not turn every encounter into a capture attempt.

Trying to preserve an enemy at low health can make a simple fight much more
dangerous than just finishing it.

If I am already losing too much health, I stop forcing the capture.

<h2 id="when-to-return">When I Return to Base Instead of Pushing Further</h2>

This is the first Elysia decision that actually punishes me for being greedy.

**Expedition Progress and Enemy Strength are different measurements.**

Expedition Progress tells me how far I have pushed the current sector cycle.

Enemy Strength tells me how dangerous the expedition is becoming as I explore
more space.

I watch both.

  <figcaption className="mx-auto mt-3 max-w-4xl text-sm text-slate-400">
    Expedition Progress tells me how far I have advanced. Enemy Strength tells
    me whether continuing at that moment is becoming too expensive.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

The trap is looking only at the progress bar.

Seeing the percentage climb makes one more location feel automatically correct.

It is not.

I start thinking seriously about returning when several warning signs arrive
together:

- normal encounters are consuming healing instead of only ammunition;
- my accompanying soldiers are taking enough damage that the next difficult
  fight may remove the advantage they provide;
- I am already carrying prisoners or resources that will create permanent base
  progress;
- Enemy Strength has climbed while the next destination is optional;
- I am continuing mainly because I do not want to leave something unexplored.

That last one is usually greed.

I do not need to wait until the run kills me.

If I am defeated, I can be sent back to the base and lose part of what I
collected during the expedition.

When I already have something useful to bank, voluntary return is often the
better trade.

<h2 id="100-percent-progress">What I Do at 100% Expedition Progress</h2>

This is one of the easiest places to think the game is waiting for a hidden
“finish expedition” button.

It is not.

Reaching **100% Expedition Progress does not prevent me from returning to the
base**.

In one Elysia run, the sector screen showed:

- **Expedition Progress: 100%**
- **Enemy Strength: Lv.5**
- **Return to Base** still available

at the same time.

throw away useful resources because the progress bar made me feel obligated to
keep going.

<h2 id="stabilize-base">Turn the Return Trip Into Permanent Progress</h2>

Returning to the colony is not downtime.

It is where temporary expedition success becomes permanent strength.

I do not try to fix every base system before leaving again.

I ask one question:

**what is the current bottleneck preventing the next Elysia push from being
better than the last one?**

If I brought captives home, that may be Prison capacity.

If the workforce has expanded but several soldiers are exhausted, that may be
the work schedule.

If my combat performance is the weak point, I spend the base phase preparing
the next expedition instead of expanding the colony sideways.

<h2 id="reach-first-decisive-battle">Reach the First Decisive Battle</h2>

After the first return-and-upgrade cycle, I keep pushing Elysia objectives until
Expedition Progress reaches 100%.

I still watch Enemy Strength while doing it.

There is no bonus for making the road to the first major battle unnecessarily
hard.

If ordinary enemies are already consuming too many resources, I return and
reset before pushing the final percentage.

Once the progress cycle completes, the coordinates for Elysia's first
**Decisive Battle** are locked.

A normal combat node can be worth taking because the reward is useful.

A Decisive Battle is part of the route required to move the sector forward.

Once it appears, I stop spending health and consumables simply to clear
low-priority destinations.

I start preparing for the progression gate.

<h2 id="first-decisive-battle">Clear the First Decisive Battle and Use the New Unlock</h2>

Winning the first Decisive Battle does not finish Elysia.

It changes the preparation options for the next phase.

The important unlock here is **Second Chance, State-Issued**, which can be
produced through the Armor Workshop.

I value this much more than another optional base expansion because the next
part of the sector is harder.

After the first decisive victory, I normally:

1. return to the base if the run is already worn down;
2. check whether my current weapon setup is still competitive;
3. restock the resources I actually consume;
4. deal with any colony bottleneck that developed during the expedition;
5. prepare the newly unlocked Second Chance option;
6. then begin the next Expedition Progress cycle.

I do not automatically spend every resource simply because a major victory
unlocked more choices.

I spend on what increases the odds of surviving the next progression gate.

<h2 id="sector-overlord">The Second Decisive Battle Is Not the End</h2>

After the second Decisive Battle, the objective changes.

I am no longer simply filling another Expedition Progress bar.

The route to the next sector remains blocked, and Elysia reveals its sector
overlord.

The requirement is direct:

**defeat the overlord and the sector is ours.**

<h2 id="after-elysia">What I Do After Elysia Is Secured</h2>

This is where I stop looking around the sector for another hidden progression
requirement.

The game immediately warns that the next sector will be harder and introduces
the **Soldier Training Center**.

That changes my priority again.

I do **not** clear Elysia and immediately launch into the next sector with the
same army just because the route has opened.

I first:

- return to the base;
- inspect the soldiers I actually want to keep;
- research the Soldier Training Center;
- use surplus soldiers to strengthen the core squad I plan to keep using;
- fix any food, work or logistics problem I ignored during the final Elysia push;
- then prepare for the next sector.

This final transition reinforces the rule Elysia taught me from the beginning:

**push while the expedition is efficient → return before greed turns the run
into a bad trade → convert temporary gains into permanent strength → leave
again with a specific objective.**

That rhythm is much more reliable than trying to clear every visible activity
in one expedition.
