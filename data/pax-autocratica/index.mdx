Pax Autocratica became much easier for me once I stopped trying to finish the
entire colony before leaving the base.

The opening only needs one short chain:

**get 4 Titanite → repair the Command Center → research the Barracks → build it
→ equip the first weapon → start the first expedition.**

Everything after that becomes easier to prioritize because the expedition starts
creating real problems for the colony to solve.

Captives give me a reason to build a Prison.

Scattered production eventually gives me a reason to automate resource
transport.

Repeated exhaustion gives me a reason to change working hours.

Harder Elysia fights give me a reason to improve the next expedition instead of
expanding the base sideways.

That is the beginner loop I use:

**unlock the next useful system → test it in the field → come home with a real
bottleneck → solve that bottleneck → leave again.**

<h2 id="first-priority">First Priority: Get 4 Titanite and Repair the Command Center</h2>

<AdUnit placement="article_after_first_h2" />

When I first reach the Aurorian Frontier Base, I do not start by trying to
repair every damaged structure I can see.

The first resource target is much smaller:

**4 Titanite.**

I can issue the early mining order and I can also help collect Titanite myself.

For this first repair, I prefer speed over automation.

Four pieces are enough to get the Command Center moving, so I collect what I
need and return instead of spending the opening stockpiling ore for future
projects.

<h2 id="do-not-finish-base-first">Do I Need to Finish the Base Before the First Expedition?</h2>

No.

This was one of the biggest early traps for me.

The opening colony already shows enough damaged buildings and future systems to
make it feel as though I am supposed to repair everything before leaving.

I am not.

Once these three conditions are true:

1. the Barracks is online;
2. the first weapon is equipped;
3. the expedition shuttle is available;

I have enough to learn more from going out than from another round of optional
base construction.

The first expedition is not something I unlock **after** finishing the base.

**It is part of building the base.**

The expedition starts revealing why I will eventually need systems such as:

- Prison capacity;
- larger labor pools;
- resource transport;
- working-hour policies;
- better expedition builds.

I let those needs appear before spending heavily on their solutions.

### If resource collection is becoming annoying

This is when I start thinking about the **Resource Transport Station**.

Workers assigned to resource transport can collect produced materials around

<h2 id="what-to-research-next">How I Choose What to Research Next</h2>

After the opening Barracks research, I do not use one fixed technology order.

I ask:

**what is stopping the next useful action?**

If captives cannot be processed, Prison capacity matters.

If resource collection is wasting my time, logistics matters.

If the workforce keeps collapsing into exhaustion, Unified Labor matters.

If none of those problems is blocking me and the next Elysia objective is
waiting, another optional research project may be less valuable than simply
leaving.

That gives me two different kinds of upgrades.

**Current-bottleneck upgrade:**  
solves a problem already slowing the colony or expedition.

**Future-problem upgrade:**  
might become useful later but does not change anything important right now.

I prioritize the first type.

That keeps resources available for problems I can actually see instead of
building toward an imaginary perfect colony.

<h2 id="when-to-leave-again">When I Stop Building and Go Back Out</h2>

Pax Autocratica almost always gives me another building I could repair or
another technology I could research.

That does not mean the colony should keep me at the base.

I leave again when:

1. the current bottleneck is under control;
2. my expedition setup is usable;
3. there is a clear Elysia objective worth pursuing.

I do not wait until:

- every research option is finished;
- every building is repaired;
- every worker has a theoretically perfect assignment;
- every resource pile has been collected;
- the base looks finished.

The colony is supposed to support the expedition.

The expedition is supposed to create the next reason to improve the colony.

That gives me the rhythm I want:

**base problem → solve it → expedition → new problem → solve that.**

<h2 id="beginner-rule">The Beginner Rule I Keep Using</h2>

The opening taught me one rule that continues working after the tutorial:

**solve the problem that is blocking the next useful loop, not every problem the
game might eventually give me.**

At first, that blocker is the **Command Center**.

Then it is the **Barracks**.

Then it is starting the first expedition.

After that, the answer depends on what actually happened:

**captives → Prison**

**manual collection becoming tedious → Resource Transport Station**

**repeated Overworked soldiers → Unified Labor**

**sector progression unclear → Elysia**

That is much more efficient than trying to predict the perfect colony from the
first ten minutes.

I build just enough to unlock the next useful loop, test it, and let the result
tell me where the next resources should go.
