MEC-KONG 7X is the fight where my Elysia expedition finally stops being about
progress percentage and becomes a straight survival check.

The boss appears as **MEC-KONG 7X [Lv.10]**.

What nearly killed my successful run was not a complicated puzzle or a hidden
phase.

It was letting sustained Gatling fire turn a manageable fight into a panic
fight.

At the worst point, I dropped to **4/314 HP** and hit Critical Health.

I still cleared MEC-KONG 7X on that attempt.

The difference was not finding some perfect secret build. I healed before the
next mistake could end the run, kept using the Laser Rifle that had already been
working well, and threw grenades instead of saving them for a fight that did not
exist after this one.

One grenade gave me an especially useful opening because MEC-KONG stopped
shooting briefly after it landed.

That is the fight plan I would use again.

<h2 id="should-i-fight-now">When I Fight MEC-KONG Now and When I Return First</h2>

The boss objective unlocking is not the same thing as my current run being
healthy enough to win it.

That distinction matters because I may reach the end of Elysia with a strong
temporary build but very little margin for error.

I take MEC-KONG immediately when:

- normal enemies are still dying cleanly;
- my current weapon has already proved itself against tougher encounters;
- I still have a realistic way to recover health;
- my Core setup has a coherent purpose instead of several weak unrelated effects;
- I am not entering the arena already in a desperate state.

I return first when:

- ordinary fights are already forcing emergency healing;
- I am barely surviving the encounters immediately before the boss;
- my weapon is no longer keeping up with enemy pressure;
- I have reached the boss mostly because I kept pushing out of greed;
- one mistake would probably end the expedition before I learn anything useful
  about the fight.

I do not use MEC-KONG's **Lv.10** label as a required player-level target.

That is the level displayed on the boss.

It is not evidence that my Leader must be Level 10 before I can win.

Likewise, I do not turn the current Enemy Strength into a fixed boss
requirement.

should bank a run belongs in the{" "}

My biggest mistake is trying to treat every firing window as a damage race.

If MEC-KONG is already pouring sustained fire into me, I do not need to prove
that I can keep shooting for another second.

I need to preserve the run.

The basic rhythm I use is:

**deal damage while the exchange is favorable → break away when the pressure
turns bad → recover or reposition → start the next exchange from a safer
position.**

I am deliberately not describing a complicated phase rotation because I did
not need one to beat the fight.

The practical problem was much simpler:

**if I stay exposed too long, the Gatling pressure can erase my margin for
error very quickly.**

<h2 id="heal-before-critical">Heal Before Critical Health Becomes a Death Screen</h2>

This is the most useful mistake from my successful attempt.

I waited too long.

At one point the HUD showed:

**Critical Health: 4/314 HP.**

I survived, but I would not deliberately repeat that timing.

Once I am at 4 HP, the decision is no longer interesting.

I heal.

The better lesson is to make that decision earlier.

When my health reaches the point where another clean Gatling burst could end the
fight, I stop asking whether I can sneak in a little more damage.

I create space and recover.

Against ordinary enemies, I sometimes finish the last target before spending a
healing resource.

Against MEC-KONG, I value survival differently because there is no benefit in
dying while holding the item that could have kept the attempt alive.

My rule is:

**if I am thinking “I can probably survive one more burst,” I am already close
to the point where healing has more value than greed.**

<h2 id="use-grenades">I Use Grenades Instead of Saving Them</h2>

Immediately before MEC-KONG, I realized I had been too conservative with
grenades.

They were becoming available again during the run, which meant I did not need to
treat every grenade like a one-time resource that had to survive until some
future emergency.

There is no more important future fight in Elysia than this one.

So I started using them.

That produced one of the clearest openings in the successful attempt.

After I threw a grenade at MEC-KONG, **the boss stopped shooting briefly**.

  </figcaption>

I would not turn one successful attempt into a claim that every grenade is a
guaranteed hard stagger with a fixed duration.

I did not measure that.

What I did see was enough to change my decision-making:

**grenade lands → MEC-KONG stops firing briefly → I get a useful window.**

What I do with that window depends on the state of the fight.

If health is bad:

**heal.**

If health is safe but positioning is bad:

**move.**

If both are under control:

**push damage.**

The mistake would be getting the opening and then continuing the same panicked
exchange without using it.

<h2 id="core-build">I Do Not Chase a Perfect Core Build Before the Boss</h2>

I do not need one exact Core combination to justify attempting MEC-KONG 7X.

What I want is a build that has already proven itself during the expedition.

If one Core line is carrying ordinary fights, I would rather strengthen or keep
that identity than start assembling a theoretical boss build at the last
minute.

That is especially important because Core Fragments belong to the current
expedition.

If I abandon a coherent run chasing a perfect roll, I may spend more resources
getting ready for MEC-KONG than the improvement is worth.

<h2 id="second-chance">Second Chance Is Useful Insurance, Not a Requirement</h2>

By this stage of Elysia, **Second Chance, State-Issued** can already be part of
my preparation options.

I like having it before the final push because MEC-KONG is exactly the type of
fight where one bad exchange can otherwise end a long expedition.

But I would not describe Second Chance as mandatory.

The successful attempt still came down to:

- keeping useful damage on the boss;
- recognizing when the health trade had become bad;
- recovering instead of continuing to greed;
- using grenades to create better windows.

Second Chance is insurance.

<h2 id="how-i-finish">How I Finish the Fight</h2>

Once I stopped trying to win every second of the encounter, MEC-KONG became much
more manageable.

The successful attempt went through exactly the kind of sequence I would expect
to kill a worse run:

**heavy pressure → health collapses → heal → create another opening → grenade
breaks the firing rhythm → keep damage going → boss dies.**

That distinction matters to me.

A first-attempt clear does not mean the boss did no damage or that the loadout
was overpowered.

I reached Critical Health and genuinely came close to losing the attempt.

The successful part was recovering from that mistake instead of continuing to
make it.

So if I am struggling with MEC-KONG, I do not immediately assume I need a
completely different build.

I first ask:

- am I staying exposed during Gatling fire for too long?
- am I healing after the dangerous moment instead of before it?
- am I carrying grenades instead of using them?
- am I abandoning a weapon that was already performing well?
- am I entering the boss with an expedition that should have returned earlier?

Those are the first things I correct.
