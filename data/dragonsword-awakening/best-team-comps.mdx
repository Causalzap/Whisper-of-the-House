DragonSword Awakening teams are not three independent damage dealers. A lineup
works when one hero creates a readable condition, the next hero can answer it,
and the third hero either extends the sequence or restores a safe position.

The four options below are the teams I actually used as practical starting
points. I do not treat them as a universal tier list for every possible roster.

<h3 id="ranged-team-roles">
  The job of each hero
</h3>

- **Cerese or Dana:** stays active at range, uses the safe action, and preserves
  a lateral exit.
- **Othello:** enters for the close-range transition, completes the immediate
  job, and creates time to reopen the distance.
- **The second ranged hero:** returns after the arena is safe instead of forcing
  another melee exchange.

I do not force one universal Cerese-first or Dana-first script. I start with the
ranged hero who has the better position, stronger current build, and more useful
skills available.

<h3 id="ranged-team-rotation">
  My Cerese / Othello / Dana rotation
</h3>

1. Open with the better-positioned ranged hero.
2. Use one safe ranged action while preserving a side exit.
3. Take the active teammate input before the boss pushes me against the wall.
4. Bring Othello into the close-range phase.
5. Finish Othello&apos;s immediate job instead of extending the melee exchange.
6. Move sideways and reopen the arena.
7. Return to Cerese or Dana when the ranged lane is available again.

The important resource is **space**. When I keep retreating directly backward,
the team eventually reaches a wall, loses the wider camera, and becomes a bad
close-range lineup.

<h3 id="ranged-team-investment">
  Who I build first
</h3>

I complete whichever of Cerese or Dana:

- starts most encounters;
- stays active between Othello entries;
- and already has the stronger usable set and Karma.

Othello receives the second investment because he handles the most dangerous
handoff. The other ranged hero only needs enough gear to survive and preserve
the rotation before I spend premium materials on expensive reforges.

<h3 id="charlotte-team-roles">
  Charlotte drives, Johnny grounds, Reina repositions
</h3>

**Charlotte** receives the first investment. I use her to open, test whether the
target can be launched, and maintain pressure while the next teammate input is
readable.

I stop Charlotte&apos;s aerial route when:

- the target resists launch;
- the camera loses the boss;
- a persistent hazard covers the landing area;
- or the next active hitbox begins.

**Johnny** takes over when I need a shorter grounded continuation. His job is
not to copy Charlotte; it is to return the fight to the floor before the aerial
route becomes unsafe.

**Reina** is the mobile bridge. Her sprint-style movement and separate dodge
let me cross space without spending the shorter defensive action too early.

<h2 id="reina-replacement-team">
  Best Reina replacement team: Cerese / Othello / Reina
</h2>

This was the most practical Dana replacement I tested.

Cerese keeps the ranged field role. Othello remains the close-range pivot.
Reina replaces part of Dana&apos;s ranged rhythm with ground movement and
recovery.

I use this version when:

- Dana is missing or underbuilt;
- Cerese and Othello already form the working core;
- the fight needs more ground movement;
- or the original ranged team keeps losing its exit lane.

I do not call it an identical replacement. The team has less ranged continuity,
and Reina&apos;s entry timing matters more.

My sequence is:

1. Let Cerese control the safe ranged phase.
2. Use Othello for the unavoidable close-range transition.
3. Bring Reina in when the team needs to cross space or reset position.
4. Return to Cerese after the lane is open.

I build Cerese first, Othello second, and bring Reina only to the functional
level required for the movement job before committing premium materials.

<h2 id="lute-team">
  Best Lute team: when healing is worth the third slot
</h2>

I do not build Lute before the primary damage core.

I add him when:

- the other two heroes already trigger their important Signal opportunities;
- the team has enough damage but cannot survive the full encounter;
- an outgoing hero repeatedly leaves the field at low health;
- or learning the boss safely matters more than maximum burst.

In my tested setup, the outgoing hero remained on the field briefly after the
new hero entered. That overlap gave Lute a window to heal the outgoing hero
before the character disappeared.

I remove Lute when the team survives comfortably and the lower damage makes the
fight unnecessarily long.

Healing does not repair an incompatible Signal chain. When the teammate input
never appears, I fix the missing condition before adding Lute.

<h2 id="beginner-team">
  Which team should beginners build first?
</h2>

I start with **Cerese, Othello, and Dana** when all three are available and the
main goal is learning bosses.

The ranged setup gives me more time to see:

- the boss wind-up;
- the arena edge;
- persistent hazards;
- and the next safe direction.

I move to Charlotte, Johnny, and Reina after I can answer three questions:

1. Can I stop Charlotte&apos;s air sequence before the boss ignores the launch?
2. Can I keep the boss visible while moving above or around it?
3. Can Reina restore position without consuming both movement options at once?

When the answer is no, the action team may deal more theoretical damage but
produce fewer successful clears.

My resource rule is simple: finish one field driver, build the transition hero
to survive, and keep the third hero functional. I do not split premium gear,
Karma, and reforges evenly across six characters before one complete team works.

<h2 id="team-synergy">
  How Signal Skills and replacement choices work
</h2>

A complete team needs three jobs:

1. **Field driver:** stays active and creates the next repeatable condition.
2. **Signal answer:** enters when that condition activates the teammate input.
3. **Bridge:** extends pressure or restores a safe position.

My normal sequence is:

1. spend one safe Active Skill;
2. continue a short recovery string;
3. create Break or the required condition;
4. confirm that the teammate input is active;
5. use the Signal Skill;
6. leave before the follow-up becomes unsafe;
7. return to the field driver after the important actions recover.

<h3 id="replacement-rule">
  My replacement rule for a missing character
</h3>

I do not start with individual damage. I use this order:

1. identify the condition the remaining two heroes create consistently;
2. find a hero whose Signal requirement answers that condition;
3. check whether that hero fills the missing field, transition, or recovery job;
4. compare survival and movement;
5. compare personal damage last.

**Condition compatibility first, role fit second, personal damage third.**

A strong hero is a weak replacement when the remaining pair never activates
that hero&apos;s entry condition.

<h2 id="defensive-switching">
  Defensive switching and Super Armor
</h2>

A switch gives me a brief protected entry window. I use it to cross one clearly
timed impact and then move.

I do not switch into:

- a lingering damage field;
- overlapping projectiles;
- a multi-hit spin;
- or an attack that remains active after the entry animation.

Some enemies show a white bar beneath the health bar. I still test the actual
reaction before extending an aerial sequence.

1. stop the long aerial route;
2. leave the counterattack area;
3. use shorter Active Skill windows;
4. build Break with safer hits;
5. use the teammate input only after it becomes active.

<h2 id="troubleshooting">
  Common team problems and fixes
</h2>

### The teammate input never becomes active

I reproduce one condition at a time and watch the enemy state and teammate
icon. I do not replace the entire team until I know which condition is missing.

### The team spends too long waiting for skills

I continue safe attacks after using an Active Skill. Hits shorten the downtime;
standing outside the fight does not.

### Charlotte keeps getting hit in the air

I stop extending when the target resists launch, the camera loses the boss, or
the next active hitbox begins.

### The Cerese team keeps getting trapped

I stop retreating directly backward. I preserve a side exit, let Othello handle
the close-range phase, and reopen the distance before returning to Cerese or
Dana.

### Switching still takes damage

I probably used the switch against a lingering or multi-hit attack, or stayed
inside the hazard after the brief protected entry ended.

### My favorite-character team clears story but fails harder content

I separate a synergy problem from a build problem. More gear does not repair a
Signal chain that never starts, and a roster change does not repair unfinished
gear, Karma, or skill investment.

<h2 id="faq">
  DragonSword Awakening team comps FAQ
</h2>

### What is the best team for learning bosses?

I start with Cerese, Othello, and Dana. The ranged setup keeps more of the boss
and arena visible, while Othello covers the dangerous close-range transition.

### What is the best Charlotte team?

My tested team is Charlotte, Johnny, and Reina. Charlotte drives the pressure,
Johnny returns the sequence to the ground, and Reina restores position.

### Can Reina replace Dana?

Yes. Cerese, Othello, and Reina was my most practical tested replacement. It is
less purely ranged than the Dana version but gives the team more ground
movement.

### When should I add Lute?

After the other two heroes already reach their important Signal opportunities.
I add Lute when healing is the real bottleneck and remove him when survival is
stable.

### Who receives the first full build?

Charlotte in the action team. In the ranged team, I finish whichever of Cerese
or Dana carries most of the field time, then build Othello for the transition.
